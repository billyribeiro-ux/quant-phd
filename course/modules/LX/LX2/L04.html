Fiduciary Duty, Conflicts and Model-Risk Governance

A CFA Institute member or candidate owes clients a duty of loyalty, prudence and care regardless of whether a legal fiduciary duty applies, and a matter that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity must be avoided or disclosed. A model that sets a planning figure carries the model risk that Definition 3.1 defines. The Federal Reserve’s 2026 letter on model risk management lists outcomes analysis, defined in Definition 3.3, among the components of model validation. This lesson defines the three concepts from the cited texts, states what each text says and what it does not say, and computes both outcomes measures on a SIMULATED forecast path in exact arithmetic.

This lesson is an educational reading of the cited sources. It is not legal advice. The professional standards are quoted from the CFA Institute Code, and the supervisory letter from its attachment, each with a locator. Statutes and court decisions on the duties of an investment adviser are not read for this lesson, so their content is not stated here.

1The duty of loyalty in the CFA Code

The CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (key cfandcode) is a professional standard. It is not a statute. Its duty of loyalty is stated in Standard III(A):

“Members and candidates have a duty of loyalty to their clients and must act with reasonable care and exercise prudent judgment. Members and candidates must act for the benefit of their clients and place their clients’ interests before their employer’s or their own interests.”

Definition 1.1 (fiduciary duty, as the Code uses the term)

A fiduciary duty is a duty of the kind the Code describes as “often imposed by law or regulation when an individual or institution is charged with the duty of acting for the benefit of another party, such as managing investment assets.” The quoted sentence is the Code’s own description, and it describes a legal source of the duty rather than setting one.

Standard III(A) “establishes a minimum benchmark for the duties of loyalty, prudence, and care that are required of all members and candidates regardless of whether a legal fiduciary duty applies. Although fiduciary duty often encompasses the principles of loyalty, prudence, and care, Standard III(A) does not render all members and candidates fiduciaries.”

The Code states that “the Code and Standards neither impose such a legal responsibility nor require all members or candidates to act as fiduciaries.” The same guidance adds that members “must comply with any legally imposed fiduciary duty.” The two statements together mean that the Code sets a professional floor of loyalty, prudence and care for all members and candidates, and that a legal fiduciary duty, where one exists, is a separate source.

“Standard III(A), however, is not a substitute for a member’s or candidate’s legal or regulatory obligations.”

Which statute or decision makes a particular adviser a fiduciary is not read for this lesson, and that content is not stated here. The duty of loyalty in the Code is the only fiduciary-duty text this lesson quotes.

2Disclosure under Standard VI(A)

Definition 2.1 (conflict of interest, as the Code defines it)

A conflict of interest is, in the Code’s words, “any matter that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity or raise a question about whether actions, judgment, or decision making is free from bias.”

Standard VI(A) sets the rule that follows from the definition:

“Members and candidates must avoid or make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity or interfere with respective duties to their clients, prospective clients, and employer. Members and candidates must ensure that such disclosures are prominent, are delivered in plain language, and communicate the relevant information effectively.”

“Best practice is to avoid actual conflicts of interest and the appearance of them. When it is not reasonable for members and candidates to avoid a conflict, clear and complete disclosure of the conflict is necessary.” The rule therefore has two parts. Disclosure is the required fallback, and avoidance is the preferred course.

The Code’s Example 3 applies the rule to a conflict that arrived by inheritance. The analyst’s spouse inherits shares in a company the analyst covers. “At minimum, Fargmon must disclose his wife’s ownership of Kincaid stock to his employer and in his follow-up report. Best practice is to avoid the conflict by asking his employer to assign another analyst to draft the follow-up report.” The example shows the two levels. The minimum is disclosure, and the preferred course is to remove the conflict.

Personal trading is a separate conflict under the Code. “Investment transactions for clients and employers must have priority over investment transactions in which a member or candidate is the beneficial owner.”

3Model risk in the 2026 supervisory letter

SR 26-2 (key frb2026fed) is a supervisory letter of the Federal Reserve. Its body names the three agencies that issue the attached guidance. The Board of Governors, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation are issuing the attached Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management.

The letter “supersedes and replaces SR letter 11-7” and “SR letter 21-8”. Neither earlier letter is read for this lesson, and neither has a registry record, so neither is cited.

“This letter is expected to be most relevant to banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets regulated by the Federal Reserve.”

Definition 3.1 (model and model risk, as the attachment defines them)

A model is, for the guidance, “a complex quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, or financial theories to process input data into quantitative estimates”. The term excludes “simple arithmetic calculations, such as those found within spreadsheets”. Model risk is “the potential for adverse financial consequences associated with models, which may result from decisions made based on model output”.

The attachment states: “This guidance does not set forth enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements; accordingly, non-compliance with this guidance will not result in supervisory criticism against a banking organization.” Its footnote 1 adds: “However, supervisory action may result for any violations of law or unsafe or unsound practices stemming from insufficient management of model risk.” The two statements are read together. The guidance sets no enforceable standard, and footnote 1 keeps supervisory action open for violations of law and for unsafe or unsound practices.

Definition 3.2 (model-risk management)

The model-risk management that the attachment describes is the set of practices a banking organization adopts for its models. The attachment says each organization is “responsible for adopting model risk management practices that are appropriate and effective for managing the specific risks the banking organization faces or is likely to face.” The quoted sentence runs from page 1 to page 2.

The validation section of the attachment has three named components. Under Model Validation and Monitoring the attachment lists Conceptual Soundness (page 8), Outcomes Analysis (page 9) and Ongoing Model Monitoring (page 9). It has no heading named Limitations. Limitations appear inside the validation discussion and in the use discussion, as the next paragraphs show.

“Validating conceptual soundness involves assessing and documenting model design (including key modeling choices, assumptions, qualitative judgments, and data selection), construction, and developmental testing.”

Definition 3.3 (outcomes analysis, as the attachment describes it)

Outcomes analysis is, in the attachment’s words, “compares model outputs to corresponding real-world outcomes to assess model performance relative to model objectives and business use.”

The attachment adds that outcomes analysis “can take many forms, including testing conducted during model development, reports or analysis performed as part of ongoing monitoring, or standalone activities such as back-testing or outlier analysis.” It also refers to “persistent deviations outside of the banking organization’s established performance thresholds”, and it does not give those thresholds.

“Ongoing model monitoring involves an evaluation of the extent to which a model is performing as expected given potential changes in products, exposures, activities, clients, data relevance, or market conditions.”

“Model validation evaluates whether models perform as expected and includes an assessment of a model’s reliability and its limitations.” “Effective model use depends on a clear understanding of a model’s limitations.” “Users of model output benefit from understanding and communicating limitations, monitoring performance, periodically reviewing relevance, and supplementing model output with complementary analysis and information.”

A search of the attachment’s text for the words “hit” and “bias” returns no match, so the attachment defines neither a hit rate nor a bias. The two measures in Section 4 are therefore definitions chosen for this lesson, not the regulator’s definitions.

4Outcomes analysis in exact arithmetic

Let \(\hat{y}_t\) be the forecast for period \(t\) and \(y_t\) the realised value, both in index units. The forecast error is \(e_t = \hat{y}_t - y_t\). A period is a hit when the error is inside a band, and the hit rate is the share of hits in a window of \(H\) periods. The bias is the mean error. These are the definitions used in the lab, and they are fixed before any number is computed.

Definition 4.1 (the two measures on a window of H periods)

With the hit indicator \(h_t = \indic\{|e_t| \le 2\}\), the hit rate is \(\frac{1}{H}\sum_{t} h_t\), and the bias is \(\operatorname{bias} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{t} e_t\). The band is 2 index units and is inclusive. Both measures are computed as exact fractions, and each is shown rounded to four decimal places. A tie is rounded away from zero, so the displayed value is \(\operatorname{sign}(x)\lfloor |x|\cdot 10^4 + \tfrac12\rfloor / 10^4\).

Assumptions

A1 (generator). In the lab, each realised value is \(y_t = 100 + U_t\) and each forecast is \(\hat{y}_t = 101 + V_t\), where \(U_t\) and \(V_t\) are independent and uniform on the integers \(-6,\dots,6\) and \(-4,\dots,4\). The lab takes this from its own generator, and the generator is not tested against any real series. A2 (independence). The periods are independent. A2 is used in Problem E3 and in Section 5, and no lab output depends on it.

Theorem 4.2 (the hit rate is monotone in the band)

Let a window of \(n \ge 1\) periods be given, and let \(0 \le b_1 \le b_2\) be two bands. The number of hits at \(b_1\) is at most the number of hits at \(b_2\), so the hit rate at \(b_1\) is at most the hit rate at \(b_2\).

Proof

For every period, \(|e_t| \le b_1\) implies \(|e_t| \le b_2\), because \(b_1 \le b_2\). So \(\indic\{|e_t| \le b_1\} \le \indic\{|e_t| \le b_2\}\) for every \(t\). Summing over \(t\) and dividing by the same \(n\) gives the claim. The proof uses no hypothesis beyond the two bands being ordered. \(\blacksquare\)

Theorem 4.3 (the bias is bounded by the mean absolute error)

For every window of \(n \ge 1\) periods, \(|\operatorname{bias}| \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t}|e_t|\). Equality holds if and only if all the errors are \(\ge 0\) or all the errors are \(\le 0\).

Proof

The triangle inequality for real numbers gives \(|\sum_t e_t| \le \sum_t |e_t|\). Dividing by \(n\) gives the bound. For equality, let \(P\) be the sum of the positive errors and \(N\) the sum of the absolute values of the negative errors. Then \(|P - N| = P + N\) holds if and only if \(\min(P, N) = 0\). A sum of strictly positive terms is zero only when it has no terms, so one of the two groups is empty. That is the stated condition. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 4.4 (exact generator expectations, under A1)

Under Assumption A1, the expected error of one period is \(\E[e_t] = 1\), and the probability that one period is a hit is \(44/117\).

Proof

Write \(e_t = (101 + V_t) - (100 + U_t) = 1 + V_t - U_t\) in the notation of A1, with the forecast draw written \(V_t\) and the realised draw \(U_t\). Both draws are uniform on symmetric integer ranges, so \(\E[V_t] = \E[U_t] = 0\), and \(\E[e_t] = 1\). For the hit probability, \(|e_t| \le 2\) is the event \(-3 \le V_t - U_t \le 1\). The pair \((V_t, U_t)\) takes 9 times 13 equally likely values by A1. For a fixed \(V_t = v\) in \(\{-4, \dots, 4\}\), the realised draw \(U_t = u\) must lie in \([v - 1, v + 3] \cap [-6, 6]\). That interval holds 5 integers for each \(v\) in \(\{-4, \dots, 3\}\) and 4 integers for \(v = 4\). The count is \(8 \cdot 5 + 4 = 44\), so the probability is \(44/117\). \(\blacksquare\)

The lab computes each of these quantities for one path, and for the generator, by enumeration.

Lab 1 · Outcomes analysis of a SIMULATED forecast path

The lab draws one SIMULATED path of 24 periods from seed 20261103 and computes the hit rate and the bias with the definitions above, in exact fractions rounded by the stated rule. The printed bias is positive and differs from the generator’s expectation of 1 index unit (Proposition 4.4), so this one path estimates that expectation with error. The window changes both measures (Part B). A bias of zero is compatible with large errors (Part C). The lab also prints the exact generator probability of a hit (Part D).

"""LX2 L04 lab 1: outcomes analysis of a SIMULATED forecast path, with both measures in exact arithmetic.

SIMULATED. The path of Parts A and B is drawn from the generator below with a fixed seed. Part D enumerates the
generator's distribution, and the path of Part C is hand-built, with fixed values. No value is observed data, and no
value describes a real firm, a real planning figure or a real model.

Standard library only. Seed 20261103, with random.Random(seed) and no other source of randomness. No network.

The generator: for each period t = 1, ..., H with H = 24, the realised value is y_t = 100 + U_t, where U_t is
uniform on the integers -6 to 6. The forecast is yhat_t = 101 + V_t, where V_t is uniform on the integers -4 to 4.
The draws are made in the order U_1, V_1, U_2, V_2, ... All values are integers in index units.

The two measures, defined before any number is computed:
* forecast error e_t = yhat_t - y_t, in index units;
* hit indicator h_t = 1 if |e_t| <= 2 (a hit within an inclusive band of 2 index units), and h_t = 0 otherwise;
* hit rate = (sum over t of h_t) / H, a share of periods;
* bias = (1/H) * sum over t of e_t, in index units; a positive bias means the forecasts sit above the realised
  values on average.

Rounding rule: both measures are computed as exact Fractions. A value is shown rounded to four decimal places,
with ties rounded away from zero, which is floor(|x| * 10^4 + 1/2) / 10^4 with the sign of x. The exact Fraction is
printed next to every rounded value, in lowest terms.

Part A: the two measures over the full 24-period path, with the path printed period by period.
Part B: the same two measures over the first 12 and the last 12 periods, to show that the window is part of the
definition.
Part C: a hand-built path with errors +3, -3, +3, -3, to show that a bias of zero does not mean small errors.
Part D: exact expectations under the generator, by enumerating all 9 x 13 pairs (V, U), with V the forecast draw
and U the realised draw, printed as Fractions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from collections.abc import Sequence
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261103
HORIZON = 24
BAND = 2
PLACES = 4
SCALE = 10**PLACES
REALISED_CENTRE = 100
FORECAST_CENTRE = 101
REALISED_SPREAD = 6
FORECAST_SPREAD = 4


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Period:
    """One period of a path: t is the period index from 1, both values are integers in index units."""

    t: int
    forecast: int
    realised: int


def simulate(seed: int, horizon: int) -> list[Period]:
    """SIMULATED path of horizon periods from random.Random(seed), in the draw order stated in the module docstring."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    path: list[Period] = []
    for t in range(1, horizon + 1):
        realised = REALISED_CENTRE + rng.randint(-REALISED_SPREAD, REALISED_SPREAD)
        forecast = FORECAST_CENTRE + rng.randint(-FORECAST_SPREAD, FORECAST_SPREAD)
        path.append(Period(t, forecast, realised))
    return path


def error(p: Period) -> int:
    """Forecast error yhat_t - y_t in index units. Positive when the forecast is above the realised value."""
    return p.forecast - p.realised


def is_hit(p: Period, band: int = BAND) -> bool:
    """True when |yhat_t - y_t| <= band, with band in index units. The inequality is inclusive."""
    return abs(error(p)) <= band


def hit_rate(periods: Sequence[Period]) -> Fraction:
    """Share of the periods in the window that are hits. The denominator is the number of periods in the window."""
    return Fraction(sum(is_hit(p) for p in periods), len(periods))


def bias(periods: Sequence[Period]) -> Fraction:
    """Mean forecast error over the window, in index units. The denominator is the number of periods in the window."""
    return Fraction(sum(error(p) for p in periods), len(periods))


def round_half_away(x: Fraction) -> str:
    """Round x to PLACES decimal places, ties away from zero, and return the decimal string. Exact: no float is used."""
    sign = "-" if x < 0 else ""
    scaled = abs(x) * SCALE
    units = math.floor(scaled + Fraction(1, 2))
    return f"{sign}{units // SCALE}.{units % SCALE:0{PLACES}d}"


def show(x: Fraction) -> str:
    """The exact Fraction in lowest terms, then the rounded decimal in parentheses."""
    return f"{x} (rounded {round_half_away(x)})"


def part_a(path: Sequence[Period]) -> None:
    """Print the path period by period, then both measures over the whole path."""
    print("Part A: outcomes analysis over the full path")
    print(f"generator seed {SEED}, horizon H = {len(path)}, band = {BAND} index units (inclusive)")
    print("t  forecast  realised  error  hit")
    for p in path:
        print(f"{p.t:>2} {p.forecast:>9} {p.realised:>9} {error(p):>6} {int(is_hit(p)):>4}")
    print(f"hits = {sum(is_hit(p) for p in path)} of {len(path)}")
    print(f"hit rate = {show(hit_rate(path))}")
    print(f"bias = {show(bias(path))}")
    total = sum(error(p) for p in path)
    print(f"sum of errors = {total}; sum / H = {Fraction(total, len(path))}")
    mae = Fraction(sum(abs(error(p)) for p in path), len(path))
    print(f"mean absolute error = {mae} (not one of the two measures)")


def part_b(path: Sequence[Period]) -> None:
    """Print both measures for the first 12 and the last 12 periods, each over its own denominator of 12."""
    print("Part B: the same measures over two windows of 12 periods")
    half = len(path) // 2
    for label, window in (("first 12", path[:half]), ("last 12", path[half:])):
        print(f"{label}: periods {window[0].t} to {window[-1].t}, n = {len(window)}")
        print(f"  hit rate = {show(hit_rate(window))}")
        print(f"  bias = {show(bias(window))}")


def part_c() -> None:
    """A hand-built path with errors +3, -3, +3, -3 against realised values of 100."""
    print("Part C: a bias of zero beside large errors")
    errors = (3, -3, 3, -3)
    path = [Period(t, REALISED_CENTRE + e, REALISED_CENTRE) for t, e in enumerate(errors, start=1)]
    print("errors " + " ".join(f"{e:+d}" for e in errors) + f"; band = {BAND}")
    print(f"hits = {sum(is_hit(p) for p in path)} of {len(path)}")
    print(f"hit rate = {show(hit_rate(path))}")
    print(f"bias = {show(bias(path))}")


def part_d() -> None:
    """Exact expectations under the generator, by enumerating all 9 x 13 equally likely pairs (V, U).

    V is the forecast draw, over the 9 values -4 to 4, and U is the realised draw, over the 13 values -6 to 6.
    """
    print("Part D: exact expectations under the generator, by enumeration")
    pairs = [
        (FORECAST_CENTRE + v - (REALISED_CENTRE + u))
        for v in range(-FORECAST_SPREAD, FORECAST_SPREAD + 1)
        for u in range(-REALISED_SPREAD, REALISED_SPREAD + 1)
    ]
    count = len(pairs)
    hits = sum(1 for e in pairs if abs(e) <= BAND)
    print(f"pairs = {count} (9 forecast draws times 13 realised draws, each pair with probability 1/{count})")
    print(f"P(hit) = {show(Fraction(hits, count))}")
    expected_error = Fraction(sum(pairs), count)
    print(f"E[error] = {expected_error} index units")


def main() -> None:
    """Run Parts A to D. Every printed number is an exact Fraction, an integer count or a rounded exact value."""
    path = simulate(SEED, HORIZON)
    part_a(path)
    part_b(path)
    part_c()
    part_d()


main()

5Where the canon breaks

The Code is a professional standard. Meeting it does not settle a legal duty, because the Code states that it does not impose one (Section 1). The legal duty of an adviser is therefore a separate question, and its content is not stated here.

The letter is a guidance document, and the attachment says it sets no enforceable standards. Its validation components are descriptions of practice. A reviewer who reads “outcomes analysis” as a fixed test with a fixed threshold reads more into the attachment than it states, because it names thresholds without giving them (Definition 3.3). The two measures in Section 4 are the lesson’s own definitions. They are not in the letter, and a different band, a different window or a different denominator can give a different number.

The generator has no trend, no regime change and no serial dependence, and it is not fitted to any series. Real forecast errors may show any of the three. If errors are serially dependent, the periods are not independent, Assumption A2 fails, and the standard error in Problem E3, which is computed under A2, is no longer justified as the sampling error of the mean. Neither case is tested in this lesson.

The lab has no threshold for a bias that is too large. Part A shows a bias of 41/12 index units, which is not decided here as acceptable or unacceptable, because the attachment gives no threshold for this case.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A bias of zero means the forecasts are accurate.”

    Errors can cancel. Part C of the lab gives errors of +3, −3, +3 and −3 index units and prints a bias of 0 and a hit rate of 0. The mean absolute error of these four errors is 3 index units, computed from the printed errors, and Part C does not print it. Theorem 4.3 bounds the bias by the mean absolute error and states when the two are equal.

  2. “Disclosing a conflict is always enough under Standard VI(A).”

    The Code says the rule is to avoid or disclose, and that “best practice is to avoid actual conflicts of interest and the appearance of them”. Example 3 says disclosure is the minimum and that best practice is to remove the conflict. The quotations are in Section 2.

  3. “Every member of the CFA Institute who gives investment advice is a fiduciary under the Code.”

    The Code states that Standard III(A) “does not render all members and candidates fiduciaries”, and that the Code and Standards “neither impose such a legal responsibility nor require all members or candidates to act as fiduciaries”. The evidence is the two quotations in Section 1.

  4. “SR 26-2 is an enforceable standard, so a model that falls short of it breaks a rule.”

    The attachment says the guidance “does not set forth enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements”, and that non-compliance “will not result in supervisory criticism”. Its footnote 1 adds that supervisory action may follow violations of law or unsafe or unsound practices. Both sentences are on page 2, and both are quoted in Section 3.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Hit rate and bias from four errors

Four periods have forecast errors \(+2\), \(-1\), \(+4\) and \(-3\) index units. With the band of 2 index units, find the hit rate and the bias as exact fractions.

Solution

\(|2| \le 2\) and \(|-1| \le 2\) are hits, while \(|4| > 2\) and \(|-3| > 2\) are not. So the hit rate is \(2/4 = 1/2\). The sum of errors is \(2 - 1 + 4 - 3 = 2\), so the bias is \(2/4 = 1/2\). Both measures are \(1/2\), which is their exact value and needs no rounding.

M2 · The rounding rule on a negative value

Display \(-7/3\) to four decimal places by the rule in Definition 4.1.

Solution

\(|-7/3| \cdot 10^4 = 23333.33\ldots\). Adding one half gives \(23333.83\ldots\), and the floor is \(23333\). The sign is negative, so the displayed value is \(-2.3333\).

M3 · Bias of a twelve-period window

A window of 12 periods has errors that sum to 44 index units. Give the bias as an exact fraction and as the rounded display.

Solution

The bias is \(44/12 = 11/3\). Its decimal is \(3.666\ldots\), and \(3.666\ldots \cdot 10^4 = 36666.67\ldots\). Adding one half gives \(36667.17\ldots\), and the floor is \(36667\), so the display is \(3.6667\). This is the value Part B of the lab prints for the first 12 periods.

Proof

P1 · A perfect hit rate with a bias of one

Give a window with hit rate 1 and bias 1 under the band of 2 index units, and state why the two measures are not linked.

Solution

Take the errors \(2, 2, 0, 0\). Every absolute error is at most 2, so all four periods are hits and the hit rate is \(4/4 = 1\). The sum is 4, so the bias is \(4/4 = 1\). The two measures are not linked because the hit indicator depends on each \(|e_t|\), while the bias depends on the signed sum. Different sign patterns can give the same bias with different hit counts.

P2 · The rounding error is at most one half of the last place

Show that for every rational \(x\), the value \(r(x)\) produced by the rule in Definition 4.1 satisfies \(|r(x) - x| \le 1/20000\).

Solution

Let \(y = |x| \cdot 10^4\) and \(n = \lfloor y + 1/2 \rfloor\). The floor satisfies \(y + 1/2 - 1 < n \le y + 1/2\), so \(|n - y| \le 1/2\). Dividing by \(10^4\) gives \(\big| n/10^4 - |x| \big| \le 1/20000\). The displayed value is \(\operatorname{sign}(x)\, n/10^4\), and the sign does not change the distance, so \(|r(x) - x| \le 1/20000\). \(\blacksquare\)

P3 · Two windows with one bias and different hit rates

Give two windows with the same bias and different hit rates, using the band of 2 index units.

Solution

Window A has errors \(0, 0, 0, 0\). Its bias is 0 and its hit rate is \(4/4 = 1\). Window B has errors \(+3, -3, +3, -3\). Its bias is 0 and its hit rate is 0, because every \(|e_t| = 3 > 2\). The bias does not determine the hit rate. Part C of the lab prints window B.

E3 · The full-path bias against the generator expectation

The full-path bias is 41/12. Under A1 and A2, compute the standard error of the mean error over the 24 periods and state how many standard errors the bias lies above the expectation of Proposition 4.4.

Solution

Under A1, \(\Var(e_t) = \Var(V_t) + \Var(U_t)\), because the two draws are independent. The variance of a uniform draw on \(n\) consecutive integers is \((n^2 - 1)/12\). So the forecast draw \(V_t\), which takes 9 values, has variance \((81 - 1)/12 = 20/3\), and the realised draw \(U_t\), which takes 13 values, has variance \((169 - 1)/12 = 14\). Hence \(\Var(e_t) = 20/3 + 14 = 62/3\). Under A2 the standard error of the mean over 24 periods is \(\se = \sqrt{(62/3)/24} = \sqrt{62/72} \approx 0.9280\). The gap is \(41/12 - 1 = 29/12 \approx 2.4167\), which is about \(2.60\) standard errors. This is a calculation under A1 and A2 only. The approximation of the distribution of the mean by a normal law is not checked in this lesson.

Research

R1 · Counting the generator’s hits without enumeration

Count the pairs in Proposition 4.4 with \(-3 \le V_t - U_t \le 1\) by a method that does not list all 117 pairs.

Solution

Fix \(v\) in \(\{-4, \dots, 3\}\). The admissible \(u\) are \(v - 1\) to \(v + 3\), which is 5 integers, and all of them lie in \([-6, 6]\). For \(v = 4\), the admissible \(u\) run from 3 to 7, and only 3 to 6 lie in \([-6, 6]\), which is 4 integers. The total is \(8 \cdot 5 + 4 = 44\), and \(44/117\) is the probability.

R2 · The smallest band with a generator hit probability of one half

Find the smallest integer band \(b\) for which the generator’s one-period hit probability is at least \(1/2\).

Solution

A hit at band \(b\) is the event \(|1 + V_t - U_t| \le b\), that is \(-1 - b \le V_t - U_t \le b - 1\). The counts of the 117 pairs are: \(b = 0\), 9 pairs; \(b = 1\), 27 pairs; \(b = 2\), 44 pairs, which is \(44/117 \approx 0.3761\); \(b = 3\), 60 pairs. For \(b = 3\), each of the 7 values \(v\) in \(\{-4, \dots, 2\}\) has 7 admissible \(u\), \(v = 3\) has 6 and \(v = 4\) has 5, which gives \(7 \cdot 7 + 6 + 5 = 60\). So \(60/117 = 20/39 \approx 0.5128\) is the first value at or above one half, and the answer is \(b = 3\).

R3 · A bias of zero with a positive mean absolute error

Find the smallest window size \(n\) for which a window of integer errors has bias 0 and mean absolute error 1 index unit, and show that no smaller window exists.

Solution

With \(n = 1\), the single integer error \(e\) must satisfy \(e = 0\) for the bias to be zero, and then the mean absolute error is 0, not 1. So \(n = 1\) fails. With \(n = 2\), the errors \(+1\) and \(-1\) give bias \((1 - 1)/2 = 0\) and mean absolute error \((1 + 1)/2 = 1\). So the smallest window size is \(n = 2\).
