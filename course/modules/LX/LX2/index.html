Securities Law, Regulation & Professional Conduct

This module reads the statutes, the cases and the professional standards that govern securities offerings, trading and the advice given about them, and the governance of the models that a firm uses to forecast. Each quoted statute or holding carries its section or page locator and is graded C, and each statement that the module cannot source is marked “not stated here”. The module is an educational reading of cited sources. It is not legal advice. You will be able to quote the registration and reporting sentences of the two Acts and say which duty each one triggers, classify a hypothetical fact pattern under the elements the cited opinions state, compute a manipulation-pattern ratio exactly on simulated order events, and write a model-risk validation report that cites every regulatory statement and computes its outcome measures from unrounded values.

  • Prerequisites: FC1 The Financial System, Markets & Instruments, which pulls in E1 Microeconomics, M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning, M2 Single & Multivariable Calculus and P0.1 How to Study This Program. P0.1 is not yet released in this program, so its concepts link to entries that read “not yet released” (see Prerequisites).
  • Project: “Model-risk validation report”, a four-section report graded by a rubric with two exemplars. No autograder.
  • Data: none embedded. The labs use a hypothetical fact pattern list (L02) and seeded simulated data (L03, L04). See Data status.
  • Atlas milestone: none. Debate brief: none.
  • Unlocks: SR4 Regulation's Market Footprint, which lists LX2 among its prerequisites.

36 h lessons · 14 h project (28% of 50 h)

Objectives

By the end of LX2 you can:

  1. Quote the filing, effectiveness, stop-order and reporting sentences of the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 with their section locators, and state which duty each sentence triggers (Lesson 1).
  2. Trace a registration statement through its statuses and state the offer and sale conditions that apply at each status, under the readings the lesson states (Lesson 1).
  3. Classify a hypothetical fact pattern under the elements of the insider-trading and tipping doctrine that the cited opinions state, and name the element that decides it (Lesson 2).
  4. Compute a cancel-to-fill ratio exactly, from a simulated order-event stream, and state why a demonstration threshold is not a regulatory threshold (Lesson 3).
  5. Compute a hit rate and a bias from unrounded values, state the rounding rule, and explain what each measure does and does not show about a forecasting model (Lesson 4).
  6. Write a validation report to the four-section structure of the project, in which every regulatory or doctrinal statement carries a registry key and a locator (project).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on the prerequisites and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder (below) teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which is eight correct answers out of ten. Seven of ten is 70 percent, and that is below the bar. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar on its item is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. Which evidence grade applies to a result that is proved on the page?

    Options

    Grade T is the theorem grade (P0.1, evidence grade).

  2. In comparative statics, what is compared?

    Options

    Comparative statics compares equilibria across parameter values (E1, comparative statics).

  3. A payment of 100 cents arrives one period from now. The discount rate is 10 percent per period. What is its present value, in cents, to two decimal places?

    The present value is \(100/1.1 = 90.909\ldots\) cents, which is 90.91 to two decimal places (M1, present value and discounting).

  4. Which statement is the negation of “for every \(x\) in \(S\), \(P(x)\) holds”?

    Options

    The negation of a universal statement is an existential statement with the negated property (M1, quantifiers and logical statements).

  5. What is the derivative of \(x^2\) at \(x = 3\)?

    The derivative of \(x^2\) is \(2x\), which equals 6 at \(x = 3\) (M2, derivative).

  6. What is \(\partial f / \partial x\) for \(f(x, y) = x^2 y\) at \((x, y) = (2, 3)\)?

    The partial derivative is \(2xy\), which equals \(2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 12\) at \((2, 3)\) (M2, partial derivative).

  7. Dealers quote prices directly to their customers, with no central order book on an exchange. Which market is this?

    Options

    An over-the-counter market is traded through dealers, not through a central exchange order book (FC1, exchange-traded vs. over-the-counter markets).

  8. A quote shows a bid of 99.50 and an ask of 100.10. What is the quoted spread, as a decimal?

    The quoted spread is the ask minus the bid, \(100.10 - 99.50 = 0.60\) (FC1, bid–ask spread).

  9. Skill check (rung 1): a sample has 8 forecast and realised pairs, and 6 of them are hits. The hit rate is the number of hits divided by the number of pairs. What is the hit rate as a decimal?

    The hit rate is \(6/8 = 0.75\). Lesson 4 defines the hit rate for the project's model case.

  10. Skill check (rung 2): forecasts are 10, 12 and 9, and the realised values are 11, 13 and 8. The bias is the mean of forecast minus realised over the three pairs. What is the bias, as a decimal to four places?

    The differences are \(10 - 11 = -1\), \(12 - 13 = -1\) and \(9 - 8 = 1\). Their sum is \(-1\), so the bias is \(-1/3 = -0.3333\) (Lesson 4).

The project ladder

The module ends in the Model-risk validation report, which applies the four validation elements to one model case. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the outcomes analysis. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    One fully solved and annotated validation of a simulated forecasting model, with the reason for every step. Every number is from simulated data.

  2. Completion problem

    The same validation with the hit-rate line and the bias line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Write the validation report with four sections: conceptual soundness, outcomes analysis, limitations and ongoing monitoring. Every regulatory or doctrinal statement carries a registry key and a locator. Graded by the rubric and its exemplars. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    Optional and not graded. Validate a second simulated model case, or compare the limitations sections of two reports, with each claim cited.

Data status

No dataset is embedded or read in LX2. The labs use three kinds of input, each labelled on its page. The insider-trading classifier in Lesson 2 uses eight HYPOTHETICAL fact patterns, authored for the lab; they are not case records. The manipulation lab in Lesson 3 and the outcomes lab in Lesson 4 use SIMULATED data generated from a fixed seed. The project's model case and its forecast file are SIMULATED and labelled as such.

Two things are not in the module, and the module says so where they arise. The text of the Dodd–Frank Act and of MiFID II is not in the registry, so Lesson 3 names each instrument and states “content not stated here”. The registry has no statute or case on conflicts of interest, so Lesson 4 teaches conflicts from the CFA Code and marks the legal content “not stated here”.
