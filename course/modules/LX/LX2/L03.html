Market manipulation, defined in Definition 1.3, carries a legal meaning that this lesson does not state. The lesson therefore defines a measurable order pattern, the cancel-to-fill ratio, and shows how the share identity of a limit order book changes when traders can cancel their own resting orders. The Dodd–Frank Act and MiFID II are named in Definitions 3.1 and 3.2, and their content is not stated. Lab 1 computes the ratio for six traders on a SIMULATED order flow in exact fractions. At the demonstration threshold it flags no trader.

1Two definitions, and what they do not say

This is an educational reading of order-book mechanics and of the program's concept list. It is not legal advice. The lesson states no legal provision, so it cites no registry key.

Definition 1.1 (cancel-to-fill ratio)

Fix a trader and a run of the order-book model. A resting order is a limit order that has not fully filled and remains in the book. The trader's cancels are the number of times it removes one of its own resting orders.

Its fills are the number of executions in which one of its orders takes part, counted once for each of its orders that takes part. An execution between two orders of the same trader therefore adds 2 to its fills. The cancel-to-fill ratio is cancels divided by fills. It is defined when fills is at least 1, and a trader with no fills has no ratio.

Remark 1.2 (a market cancel is not a trader cancel)

When a market order cannot fill entirely, the book cancels its unfilled remainder at the end of its arrival. This is a market cancel. It is not a trader cancel, which is a trader's removal of one of its own resting orders, the event that Definition 1.1 counts as a cancel. A market cancel does not count toward cancels. A trader cancel acts only on a resting order.

Definition 1.3 (market manipulation, a working label)

In this lesson, market manipulation is a label for the pattern that Definition 1.1 measures: a cancel-to-fill ratio above a threshold that the reader chooses. The label names a pattern in order data. It does not name a violation. The legal definition of manipulation is not stated here. This lesson does not read the provision that defines it, so it makes no statement about that provision's content.

2The share identity with trader cancels

The limit order book of the FC1 lesson on order types has no trader cancels, so its share identity has no term for them. The model in this lesson adds them.

Assumption A1 (the model)

Each order is a distinct object. Each execution of \(q\) shares removes \(q\) shares from one buy order and \(q\) shares from one sell order. The two orders are distinct objects even when one trader owns both. A trader cancel removes the whole remaining quantity of one of that trader's resting orders. A limit order that is not fully filled rests in the book, and a market order never rests.

Proposition 2.2 (share identity with trader cancels)

Under A1, fix any moment of a run that follows an event. Let \(G\) be the shares of all orders that have arrived by that moment, each at its arrival quantity, \(R\) the shares resting in the book at that moment, \(X\) the shares traded up to that moment, \(C_m\) the shares removed by market cancels up to that moment, and \(C_t\) the shares removed by trader cancels up to that moment. Then \[ G = R + 2X + C_m + C_t. \]

Proof

Take one order \(o\) that has arrived by the moment, and let \(g_o\) be its arrival quantity. Each of its shares leaves the order by exactly one route by that moment: it is filled, it is removed by a market cancel, or it is removed by a trader cancel. A share that is none of these is still resting at that moment. The routes are disjoint because a market order never rests, so only a limit order can be trader-cancelled, and a trader cancel acts only on a resting order. Let \(f_o\), \(k_o\) and \(r_o\) be the shares of \(o\) that are filled, removed by a cancel of either kind, and resting at that moment. Then \(g_o = f_o + k_o + r_o\). Summing over all orders that have arrived by that moment gives \(G = \sum_o f_o + \sum_o k_o + \sum_o r_o\). The last sum is \(R\), and the middle sum is \(C_m + C_t\), because each cancelled share is counted in exactly one of the two cancel totals up to that moment. For the first sum, each execution up to that moment of \(q\) shares adds \(q\) to the filled shares of exactly two distinct orders, by A1. The executions' shares up to that moment add up to \(X\), so \(\sum_o f_o = 2X\). Substituting gives the identity at that moment. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (what the missing term costs)

Without the term \(C_t\), the identity fails whenever a trader cancels a resting order. The left side then exceeds \(R + 2X + C_m\) by exactly the trader-cancelled shares. Lab 1 prints the trader-cancelled shares at the end of its run, 2020, and by Proposition 2.2 dropping that term leaves a gap of the same size at that point.

Remark 2.4 (the identity counts shares, the ratio counts cancels)

The cancel-to-fill ratio counts cancel events, not cancelled shares. A trader that cancels one share and a trader that cancels one thousand shares each add 1 to cancels. Proposition 2.2 is an identity in shares, so it does not check the ratio.

Lab 1 · Cancel-to-fill ratios on a seeded SIMULATED order flow

A book is crossed when its best bid is at or above its best ask. A tally is the per-trader count of orders and cancels that the lab rebuilds from its event log. Before the run, the expected results are zero crossed-book events, zero identity failures and zero tally disagreements. The crossed-book count is zero because an arriving order trades against each resting order it crosses before any limit remainder rests, and a cancel only removes an order, so no event can leave a bid at or above an ask. The identity of Proposition 2.2 should hold after every event, and the tally counts the same events the book counts. The flow is SIMULATED: 400 events from seed 2026, six traders, and a cancel rate of 5, 10, 25, 40, 60 and 85 percent for the six traders. A trader's cancel rate applies when the generator selects that trader and it has a resting order. A cancel removes the whole remaining quantity of one of that trader's resting orders, chosen with equal probability from the trader's resting orders in this order: its bids, highest price first and then earliest arrival, followed by its asks, lowest price first and then earliest arrival. The lab prints each trader's exact cancel-to-fill fraction and flags a trader whose fraction exceeds the demonstration threshold 2. The lab does not predict which traders are flagged. It prints what the seeded flow produces.

"""LX2 lab L03: cancel-to-fill ratios per trader on a seeded SIMULATED order flow, exact in Fraction.

Standard library only. No network and no data. The order flow is generated from a fixed seed, so it is SIMULATED and
every run prints the same numbers. The book follows the rules of the FC1 lesson on order types: price-time priority,
a fill at the resting order's price, limit and market orders, and an unfilled remainder of a market order that is
cancelled. This lab has no stop orders.

Each trader has a cancel rate, a whole percentage that the generator uses as its input. A trader's cancel removes the
remaining quantity of one of that trader's resting orders. A market order's unfilled remainder is cancelled by the
book, and that cancellation is not counted as a trader cancel.

The cancel choice: a trader cancel removes the whole remaining quantity of one of that trader's resting orders, chosen
uniformly from the list of those orders in this order: the trader's bids, highest price first and then earliest arrival,
followed by the trader's asks, lowest price first and then earliest arrival.

The draws are made in this order for each event. First the trader, uniformly over the six traders. If that trader has a
resting order, a draw from 0 to 99 is compared with its cancel rate. A draw below the rate makes the event a cancel, of
the order at a uniform index in the list just described. Otherwise, including when the trader has no resting order, the
event is a submission, and its side, quantity, market flag and limit price (ignored for a market order) are drawn in
that order.

For each trader the lab prints the cancel count, the fill count and the exact ratio cancels / fills. A fill is one
execution in which one of the trader's orders takes part. An execution between two orders of the same trader counts
twice for that trader. A trader with no fills has no ratio, and the lab prints "none" for it.

The threshold DEMO_THRESHOLD = 2 is a demonstration value, chosen before the run. It is not a regulatory threshold, and
the lab takes it from no source. The flag says only that the ratio exceeds 2 on this flow.
After every event the lab checks two things: the book is not crossed, and the share identity
entered = resting + 2 * traded + market cancelled + trader cancelled holds, where the traded term counts each executed
share once for each of its two orders. It also checks that a tally of the event log reproduces the book's order and
cancel counts per trader. The lab does not decide whether any trader's behaviour is lawful, and it does not model
latency, brokers or several venues.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction
from typing import Final

SEED: Final[int] = 2026
EVENTS: Final[int] = 400
TRADERS: Final[int] = 6
REFERENCE_PRICE: Final[int] = 10000  # cents
PRICE_BAND: Final[int] = 20  # limit prices lie within this many cents of the reference price
QTY_UNIT: Final[int] = 10  # shares; an order has 1 to 5 units
QTY_STEPS: Final[int] = 5
MARKET_PCT: Final[int] = 10  # percent of submissions that are market orders
CANCEL_PCT: Final[tuple[int, ...]] = (5, 10, 25, 40, 60, 85)  # generator input, one per trader
DEMO_THRESHOLD: Final[Fraction] = Fraction(2)


@dataclass
class Order:
    """One order. Price is in whole cents and is ignored for a market order. Shares are whole numbers."""

    trader: int
    side: int  # +1 for a buy, -1 for a sell
    price: int
    market: bool
    remaining: int
    seq: int  # arrival number; a lower number arrived earlier


def bid_priority(order: Order) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """Sort key for resting bids: the highest price first, then the earliest arrival."""
    return (-order.price, order.seq)


def ask_priority(order: Order) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """Sort key for resting asks: the lowest price first, then the earliest arrival."""
    return (order.price, order.seq)


def crosses(incoming: Order, resting: Order) -> bool:
    """Whether the incoming order can trade with the resting order at the resting order's price."""
    if incoming.market:
        return True
    if incoming.side > 0:
        return resting.price <= incoming.price
    return resting.price >= incoming.price


class Book:
    """A limit order book with price-time priority, and the counts the lab reports."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.bids: list[Order] = []
        self.asks: list[Order] = []
        self.orders = [0] * TRADERS
        self.fills = [0] * TRADERS
        self.cancels = [0] * TRADERS
        self.entered = 0
        self.executions = 0
        self.traded = 0
        self.market_cancelled = 0
        self.trader_cancelled = 0

    def resting(self) -> int:
        """Shares resting in the book."""
        return sum(o.remaining for o in self.bids) + sum(o.remaining for o in self.asks)

    def submit(self, order: Order) -> None:
        """Match an arriving order against the opposite side, then rest any limit remainder."""
        self.orders[order.trader] += 1
        self.entered += order.remaining
        opposite = self.asks if order.side > 0 else self.bids
        while order.remaining > 0 and opposite and crosses(order, opposite[0]):
            best = opposite[0]
            q = min(order.remaining, best.remaining)
            order.remaining -= q
            best.remaining -= q
            self.traded += q
            self.executions += 1
            self.fills[order.trader] += 1
            self.fills[best.trader] += 1
            if best.remaining == 0:
                opposite.pop(0)
        if order.remaining > 0:
            if order.market:
                self.market_cancelled += order.remaining
                order.remaining = 0
            elif order.side > 0:
                self.bids.append(order)
                self.bids.sort(key=bid_priority)
            else:
                self.asks.append(order)
                self.asks.sort(key=ask_priority)

    def cancel(self, target: Order) -> None:
        """Remove a resting order of its own trader and count the cancel against that trader."""
        if target.side > 0:
            self.bids = [o for o in self.bids if o is not target]
        else:
            self.asks = [o for o in self.asks if o is not target]
        self.trader_cancelled += target.remaining
        self.cancels[target.trader] += 1
        target.remaining = 0

    def best_bid(self) -> int | None:
        """The highest resting bid price, or None when no bid rests."""
        return self.bids[0].price if self.bids else None

    def best_ask(self) -> int | None:
        """The lowest resting ask price, or None when no ask rests."""
        return self.asks[0].price if self.asks else None


def run() -> tuple[Book, list[tuple[str, int]], int, int]:
    """Generate the seeded flow and apply it to a book. Returns the book, the event log, and the two failure counts."""
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    book = Book()
    log: list[tuple[str, int]] = []
    crossed = 0
    identity_failures = 0
    for seq in range(EVENTS):
        trader = rng.randrange(TRADERS)
        own = [o for o in book.bids + book.asks if o.trader == trader]
        if own and rng.randrange(100) < CANCEL_PCT[trader]:
            book.cancel(own[rng.randrange(len(own))])
            log.append(("cancel", trader))
        else:
            side = 1 if rng.randrange(2) == 0 else -1
            qty = QTY_UNIT * rng.randrange(1, QTY_STEPS + 1)
            market = rng.randrange(100) < MARKET_PCT
            price = REFERENCE_PRICE + rng.randrange(-PRICE_BAND, PRICE_BAND + 1)
            book.submit(Order(trader, side, price, market, qty, seq))
            log.append(("market" if market else "limit", trader))
        bid, ask = book.best_bid(), book.best_ask()
        if bid is not None and ask is not None and bid >= ask:
            crossed += 1
        if book.entered != book.resting() + 2 * book.traded + book.market_cancelled + book.trader_cancelled:
            identity_failures += 1
    return book, log, crossed, identity_failures


def tally_mismatches(book: Book, log: list[tuple[str, int]]) -> int:
    """Trader counts that the event log does not reproduce: orders submitted and cancels, per trader."""
    orders = [0] * TRADERS
    cancels = [0] * TRADERS
    for kind, trader in log:
        if kind == "cancel":
            cancels[trader] += 1
        else:
            orders[trader] += 1
    mismatches = sum(orders[t] != book.orders[t] for t in range(TRADERS))
    return mismatches + sum(cancels[t] != book.cancels[t] for t in range(TRADERS))


def main() -> None:
    book, log, crossed, identity_failures = run()
    total_orders = sum(book.orders)
    total_cancels = sum(book.cancels)
    print(
        f"SIMULATED flow: {EVENTS} events from seed {SEED}, {TRADERS} traders, reference price {REFERENCE_PRICE} "
        f"cents, limit prices within {PRICE_BAND} cents, quantities of {QTY_UNIT} to {QTY_UNIT * QTY_STEPS} shares"
    )
    print(
        f"orders {total_orders}, executions {book.executions}, shares traded {book.traded}, "
        f"trader cancel events {total_cancels}"
    )
    print(f"events with a crossed book: {crossed}")
    print(f"events where entered != resting + 2 * traded + market cancelled + trader cancelled: {identity_failures}")
    print(
        f"shares resting {book.resting()}, market shares cancelled {book.market_cancelled}, "
        f"trader shares cancelled {book.trader_cancelled}"
    )
    print(f"fills total {sum(book.fills)}, which equals 2 * executions: {sum(book.fills) == 2 * book.executions}")
    print(f"tally from the event log disagrees with the book in {tally_mismatches(book, log)} trader counts")
    print(
        f"demonstration threshold {DEMO_THRESHOLD} (not a regulatory threshold): "
        f"flag when cancels / fills > {DEMO_THRESHOLD}"
    )
    print("trader  cancel_pct  orders  cancels  fills  ratio  flag")
    flagged = 0
    ratios: list[Fraction] = []
    for t in range(TRADERS):
        fills = book.fills[t]
        if fills == 0:
            ratio_text, flag = "none", "undefined"
        else:
            ratio = Fraction(book.cancels[t], fills)
            ratios.append(ratio)
            ratio_text = str(ratio)
            is_flagged = ratio > DEMO_THRESHOLD
            flagged += is_flagged
            flag = "yes" if is_flagged else "no"
        print(f"{t:<7} {CANCEL_PCT[t]:<11} {book.orders[t]:<7} {book.cancels[t]:<8} {fills:<6} {ratio_text:<6} {flag}")
    print(f"traders flagged: {flagged} of {TRADERS}")
    print(f"largest ratio {max(ratios)}: a threshold at or above it flags no trader on this flow")

4Where the canon breaks

The model leaves out features of real order books. Each limit below names one feature the lab does not model and what the lesson cannot show without it.

  • No broker. A broker here is a firm that routes a customer's orders to a venue, meaning a market where orders meet. Every order in the model enters one book directly. The model cannot ask whether one firm's cancels and another firm's fills belong to the same customer.
  • No latency. Latency is the delay between sending an order and its arrival at the book. Each event in the model takes effect at once and in sequence, so the order in which a real cancel and a real fill reach a venue is not modelled.
  • No cross-venue routing. The model has one book, so a pattern that spans venues is outside the lab, and routing an order to a second venue is not modelled.
  • The ratio counts events. It records neither the trader's intent nor the reason for a cancel. A cancel that reprices an order and a cancel that withdraws a resting order add the same 1 to cancels.
  • The threshold is a demonstration value. The threshold 2 is not a rule. The flags in Lab 1 are facts about that threshold and this flow.
  • The flow is SIMULATED. The cancel rates are the generator's inputs. A flag, or the absence of one, comes from those inputs and the seed, so it is not evidence about any trader.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A high cancel-to-fill ratio shows that a trader manipulated the market.”

    The ratio divides two counts, cancels and fills, and it records neither intent nor any legal test. Lab 1 shows that the ratio does not track the generator's cancel rates exactly. Trader 1 has the higher cancel rate, 10 against 5, but the lower ratio, 3/104 against 6/97. The comparison is exact: 3 × 97 = 291 is less than 6 × 104 = 624. The ratio measures the counts of this flow, and nothing more.

  2. “The share identity holds in any book, with or without trader cancels.”

    Proposition 2.2 needs the term \(C_t\). Without it, the right side is short by the shares that traders cancelled. Lab 1 prints 2020 trader-cancelled shares for its run, so the identity without that term would be off by 2020 shares when the run ends. The proof of Proposition 2.2 sums the removals of every order, so a sum that leaves out the trader cancels does not equal \(G\).

  3. “Naming the Dodd–Frank Act or MiFID II in a lesson states what either one requires.”

    Definitions 3.1 and 3.2 name the two texts and state no provision of either. A duty that a reader finds in this lesson would be an error in the lesson, not a provision of either text.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the printed output of Lab 1.

Mechanics

M1 · Ratio from counts

A trader has 3 cancels. Its orders take part in 4 executions as the buyer and 2 executions as the seller, and no execution pairs two of its own orders. Find its fills and its cancel-to-fill ratio.

Solution

Each execution in which one of its orders takes part adds one fill, so the fills are \(4 + 2 = 6\). The ratio is cancels over fills, \(3/6 = 1/2\).

M2 · A self-trade

A trader has one execution in which its buy order and its sell order both take part, and no other execution. It has no cancels. Find its fills and its ratio.

Solution

Under Definition 1.1, the execution adds 1 to the fills for the buy order and 1 for the sell order, so the fills are \(2\). The cancels are \(0\), so the ratio is \(0/2 = 0\). The ratio is defined, because the fills are at least 1.

M3 · Solving the identity for trader cancels

A run has entered shares \(G = 1000\), resting shares \(R = 120\), traded shares \(X = 300\) and market cancels \(C_m = 40\). Its only other removals are trader cancels. Find \(C_t\).

Solution

Proposition 2.2 gives \(C_t = G - R - 2X - C_m\). Here \(2X = 600\), so \(C_t = 1000 - 120 - 600 - 40 = 240\) shares.

Proof

P1 · The flag rule in integers

Let a trader have fills \(f \ge 1\) and cancels \(c\), and let \(D\) be a whole number. Prove that the trader is flagged, meaning \(c/f > D\), if and only if \(c > Df\).

Solution

Because \(f \ge 1\), \(f\) is positive. Multiplying both sides of \(c/f > D\) by \(f\) keeps the inequality, which gives \(c > Df\). Reading the same step backwards gives the converse. So a whole-number threshold can be applied exactly with integers.

P2 · A market cancel never enters \(C_t\)

Under A1, prove that a market cancel is never counted in \(C_t\).

Solution

A market cancel removes the unfilled remainder of a market order, at the end of that order's arrival. A market order never rests, by A1. A trader cancel acts only on a resting order, by A1. So a market cancel is never the object of a trader cancel, and the two totals are disjoint. Each cancelled share lies in exactly one of \(C_m\) and \(C_t\).

P3 · The ratio falls as fills rise

Fix cancels \(c \ge 0\). Prove that \(c/f_1 \ge c/f_2\) whenever \(1 \le f_1 \le f_2\).

Solution

The difference is \(c/f_1 - c/f_2 = c(f_2 - f_1)/(f_1 f_2)\). Here \(c \ge 0\), \(f_2 - f_1 \ge 0\) and \(f_1 f_2 > 0\), so the difference is non-negative. Hence \(c/f_1 \ge c/f_2\).

Empirical

E1 · The largest ratio

Using the table of Lab 1, which trader has the largest cancel-to-fill ratio, and what is the exact fraction? Compare it with the next largest.

Solution

Trader 5 has the largest ratio, 21/37. The next largest is trader 4, with 17/50. Cross-multiplying gives \(21 \times 50 = 1050\) and \(17 \times 37 = 629\). Since 1050 is greater than 629, \(21/37 > 17/50\). Lab 1 prints the largest ratio as 21/37.

E2 · The smallest whole-number threshold that flags no trader

What is the smallest whole-number threshold at which Lab 1 flags no trader? Why does the threshold 0 not work?

Solution

A threshold \(D\) flags a trader when its ratio exceeds \(D\). At \(D = 0\), every trader with a positive ratio is flagged. Every trader in Lab 1 has at least 3 cancels, so every ratio is positive and all six traders are flagged. At \(D = 1\), no trader is flagged, because the largest ratio, 21/37, is below 1. So the answer is 1.

E3 · Two traders with close cancel rates

Trader 2 has cancel rate 25 and ratio 5/34. Trader 3 has cancel rate 40 and ratio 6/29. Which ratio is larger, and by how much, as an exact fraction?

Solution

Cross-multiplying gives \(5 \times 29 = 145\) and \(6 \times 34 = 204\), so \(5/34 < 6/29\), and trader 3's ratio is larger. The difference is \(6/29 - 5/34 = (6 \cdot 34 - 5 \cdot 29)/(29 \cdot 34) = (204 - 145)/986 = 59/986\).

Research

R1 · Two reasons for the same count

Name two different reasons a trader might cancel a resting order that add the same amount to the cancel-to-fill ratio. State which observable data would separate them, and say why the ratio cannot separate them.

Solution

One reason is to reprice an order to a new price. The other is to withdraw a resting order to change the depth that other traders see. Both add 1 to cancels, so both add the same amount to the ratio. Separating them needs order-level data that this lesson's model does not hold: the time of each cancel relative to the orders around it, the resting depth before and after the cancel, and any replacement order the trader enters. The ratio cannot separate them, because it counts events and not reasons.

R2 · What the ratio estimates

State what the cancel-to-fill ratio estimates in Lab 1, and name one reason it does not estimate the generator's cancel rate.

Solution

The ratio is cancels per fill. The generator's cancel rate is a probability per selected turn, applied only when the selected trader has a resting order. These are different quantities. The fill count depends on the state of the book, which depends on the resting orders of the other traders as well as on the trader's own orders. Lab 1 shows the difference: trader 1 has the higher cancel rate, 10, and the lower ratio, 3/104 against 6/97 for trader 0 at rate 5.

R3 · What would settle the question

What evidence would settle whether a cancel-to-fill threshold separates behaviour that the program would call manipulation from ordinary market making? Say which part of that evidence this lesson cannot supply.

Solution

The question needs the legal definition of manipulation, which this lesson does not state. It also needs labelled order data from a real venue, with each trader's role, the times of its cancels and fills, and a comparison of the ratio distributions for traders whose roles differ.
