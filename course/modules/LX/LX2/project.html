Model-risk validation report

A model-risk reviewer must validate a forecasting model that sets a monthly planning figure. You write the validation report in four sections, conceptual soundness, outcomes analysis, limitations and ongoing monitoring, on one SIMULATED model case (generated for teaching, not taken from any firm) that this page documents in full. Three labs compute the outcomes measures, and the third prints the case table the report quotes. The report is graded by a rubric against two annotated exemplars, which are sample reports written to this specification.

The ladder

If you reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks on the module page, skip rungs 1 and 2 and start at rung 3.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Lab 1 below: the outcomes analysis (the second section of the report, defined under the conventions) of an eight-month SIMULATED window. The worked steps after the lab give the reason for each step and work the other three sections on the same window.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Lab 2 below: the same analysis on a six-month SIMULATED window, with two lines blanked. Fill both blanks. The check is instant and compares the printed output only, so it does not grade how a blank was filled.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The validation report in the specification, graded by the rubric against two exemplars and your own self-assessment. Each of its four sections is held to the attachment expectation listed there, which quotes the attachment to SR 26-2 described in the source note. Lab 3 below prints the case table (defined under the conventions) that the report must quote.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Optional and not graded: open-ended extensions in the stretch section, for your portfolio.

Conventions and definitions

The labs, the case and the rubric use these definitions. Each measure below states its unit.

Windows
The calibration window is months 1 to 12. The validation window is months 13 to 36, so \(H = 24\), where \(H\) is the number of months in the window a measure is taken over (defined under Hit rate below). Lab 3 reports the calibration-window bias separately, as a check of the fit.
The case model
The model forecasts a monthly planning figure in whole units. For month index \(t\), its forecast is \(\hat{y}_t = 100 + 2t\). Assumption M1: the trend of 2 units per month holds over the validation window. Assumption M2: the forecast for month \(t\) is issued before month \(t\) starts and uses no information from month \(t\). The case is SIMULATED. It describes no firm and no real planning figure.
Error and sign
The error of month \(t\) is \(e_t = \hat{y}_t - y_t\), in units, where \(y_t\) is the realised value. A positive error is an over-forecast.
Hit
A month is a hit when \(|e_t| \le 2\) units. The band is inclusive: a month with \(|e_t| = 2\) is a hit at the edge of the band. Lesson 4, section 4 uses the same band and the same inclusive rule.
Hit rate
The number of hits divided by \(H\), the number of months in the window the measure is taken over. The result is a share between 0 and 1. On the validation window \(H = 24\). Lab 1 takes its measures over months 13 to 20, so \(H = 8\), and Lab 2 over months 13 to 18, so \(H = 6\).
Bias
\(\operatorname{bias} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_t e_t\), summed over the same window with the same \(H\), in units per month. Its sign is the sign of the error sum, which is the sign of the average error: a positive bias is an over-forecast on average.
Rounding
Every measure is computed from exact fractions, and it is rounded once, when printed: half up to four decimal places, so a tie goes away from zero. Lesson 4, section 4 uses the same rule. Your report states the rule where the first number appears.
Outcomes analysis
The second section of the report: the hit rate and the bias over a window, with the rounding rule and the window stated.
Case table
The validation table that Lab 3 prints in its Part A: one row for each month from 13 to 36, with the forecast, the realised value, the error and the hit. The realised values come from the seed and the generator stated in Lab 3.
Basis of a threshold
The number, taken from the case, the conventions or a Part row, that a threshold is set against, with its source named.
Registry key and locator
A registry key is the identifier of a source in the course's reference list. A locator is the place in that source where a statement is found, such as a cover letter, a paragraph or a section.

Lab 1 · Worked example: the outcomes analysis of an eight-month window

SIMULATED. Stated before running: the eight months 13 to 20 give 4 hits of 8, a hit rate of 0.5000, a bias of 0.2500 units per month, and two months at the edge of the band, 15 and 19, where \(|e_t| = 2\) exactly. The lab prints the forecast, realised value, error and hit of each month, then the hit count and hit rate, the bias, and the months at the edge of the band.

"""LX2 project lab 1 (rung 1, worked example): the outcomes analysis of a SIMULATED forecasting model.

SIMULATED. The realised values below are illustrative numbers written into this file. They are not data. No random
draws are used, so every run prints the same lines.

The case model. The model forecasts a monthly planning figure in whole units. For month index t its forecast is
forecast(t) = 100 + 2 * t (assumption M1: the trend of 2 units per month holds over the validation window). The
forecast for month t is issued before month t starts and uses no information from month t (assumption M2). The
eight-month window is months 13 to 20, so H = 8 months.

Error and hit. The error of month t is e = forecast - realised, in units. A month is a hit when |e| <= 2 (a band of
2 units, inclusive). A month with |e| = 2 exactly is a hit at the edge of the band.

Hit rate. The hit rate is the number of hits divided by H.

Bias. The bias is the mean error over the eight-month window, (1 / H) times the sum of e, in units per month. A
positive bias means the model forecasts too high on average.

Part A: the forecast, realised value, error and hit of each month.
Part B: the number of hits and the hit rate, to four decimals.
Part C: the bias, in units per month, to four decimals.
Part D: the months with |e| = 2 exactly, which are hits at the edge of the band.

The hit rate and the bias are exact Fractions. A value is rounded once, when it is printed: half up (a tie goes away
from zero).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

START_MONTH = 13
HIT_BAND = 2
REALISED = (125, 131, 128, 132, 138, 133, 140, 135)  # y_t for months 13 to 20, illustrative values


def forecast(t: int) -> int:
    """The case model's forecast for month index t, in units: 100 + 2 * t (assumption M1)."""
    return 100 + 2 * t


def is_hit(predicted: int, realised: int) -> bool:
    """True when the absolute error is at most the band of 2 units: |forecast - realised| <= 2."""
    if realised <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"the realised value must be a positive count of units, got {realised}")
    return abs(predicted - realised) <= HIT_BAND


def decimal_text(value: Fraction, places: int) -> str:
    """The value rounded half up (a tie goes away from zero) to places decimals, with exactly places digits."""
    scale = 10**places
    magnitude = math.floor(abs(value) * scale + Fraction(1, 2))
    sign = "-" if value < 0 and magnitude != 0 else ""
    whole, part = divmod(magnitude, scale)
    if places == 0:
        return f"{sign}{whole}"
    return f"{sign}{whole}.{part:0{places}d}"


def main() -> None:
    print(
        "SIMULATED case model: forecast(t) = 100 + 2t units; eight-month window, months 13 to 20, H = 8; band 2 units"
    )
    print("Part A: month, forecast, realised, error (forecast - realised), hit")
    errors: list[int] = []
    hits = 0
    for offset, realised in enumerate(REALISED):
        t = START_MONTH + offset
        predicted = forecast(t)
        error = predicted - realised
        errors.append(error)
        hit = is_hit(predicted, realised)
        if hit:
            hits += 1
        print(f"  t={t} forecast={predicted} realised={realised} error={error:+d} hit={'yes' if hit else 'no'}")
    horizon = len(REALISED)
    hit_rate = Fraction(hits, horizon)
    print(f"Part B: hits {hits} of {horizon}; hit rate {decimal_text(hit_rate, 4)}")
    bias = Fraction(sum(errors), horizon)
    print(f"Part C: bias {decimal_text(bias, 4)} units per month")
    edge = [START_MONTH + i for i, e in enumerate(errors) if abs(e) == HIT_BAND]
    print(f"Part D: months with |error| = 2 exactly (hits at the edge): {edge}")

The worked validation, element by element, with the reason for each step

Lab 1 computes element 2. The other three elements are worked here on the same eight months, from the case documentation and the Part A and Part C lines that Lab 1 prints, in the order of the specification.

Element 1 · Conceptual soundness

  1. State both assumptions as the case documents them. The reason: a validation tests the assumptions it names, so an unnamed assumption cannot be tested. M1 is the trend of 2 units per month. M2 is that the forecast for month \(t\) is issued before month \(t\) starts and uses no information from month \(t\).
  2. Test M2, or say why it is untested. The reason: a test of M2 needs the dates on which the forecasts were issued, and the case does not document them (the case section, item (b)). M2 is therefore untested here, and the worked example says so.
  3. Test M1 over the window, not over a single month. The reason: M1 is a claim about the realised trend, and one month cannot show a trend. Lab 1 Part C gives a bias of 0.2500 units per month, and the errors in Part A have both signs (+1, −3, +2, 0, −4, +3, −2, +5). Those signs and this bias describe the window. They do not test M1, because eight months of illustrative values cannot separate a trend from noise.

Element 2 · Outcomes analysis (Lab 1)

  1. Compute each forecast from the equation, never from the realised value. The reason: the forecast is the model's output. A forecast read off the realised value has zero error by construction and hides the misses.
  2. Take the error as forecast minus realised, keeping its sign. The reason: the bias is the average of these signed errors, so each sign counts. Month 13 gives \(e_{13} = 126 - 125 = +1\), an over-forecast, and month 14 gives \(e_{14} = 128 - 131 = -3\), an under-forecast.
  3. Test \(|e_t| \le 2\), with the equality counted. The reason: months 15 and 19 have \(|e_t| = 2\) exactly. Under a strict test they would be misses, and the hit rate would fall from 0.5000 (4 of 8) to 0.2500 (2 of 8). The inclusive rule is the one stated in the conventions.
  4. Divide the hit count and the error sum by \(H = 8\), from exact values. The reason: the hit rate is \(4/8 = 0.5000\). The errors sum to \(2\), so the bias is \(2/8 = 0.2500\). No intermediate value is rounded.
  5. Apply the rounding rule once, at print time. The reason: for this window both measures are exact at four places, so the rule changes no printed digit here. Half up and half even, the two usual tie rules, differ only at an exact tie, a value whose fifth decimal place is a 5 with nothing after it. None of the three labs has one: Lab 3's hit rate \(13/24\), which is 0.5416 followed by a repeating 6, rounds to 0.5417 under either tie rule.

Element 3 · Limitations

  1. Name the window length as a limit. The reason: \(H = 8\), so each hit moves the hit rate by \(1/8 = 0.1250\) (Part B: 4 hits of 8). The eight months are one window's count and say nothing about other months. Removing the limit means a longer window; the measures would then be taken over that window and stated again.
  2. Name the illustrative realised values as a limit. The reason: the values Lab 1 prints are numbers written into the lab's file, not data, and they agree with the case series only at month 18 (the case section, item (e)). So Parts B and C describe these values, not the case table. Removing the limit, by measuring against the case series, could change Parts B and C.

Element 4 · Ongoing monitoring

  1. Name one metric and define it. The reason: a monitoring metric must be computable from the rows the report already has. Here the metric is the bias of Part C, recomputed each month over the trailing eight months with the same definition as Lab 1.
  2. Set a threshold with its basis. The reason: a threshold without a basis cannot be checked. This worked example sets an absolute bias above 2 units per month as the review trigger. The basis is the band of 2 units in the conventions: a bias larger than the band means the average error lies outside the band that defines a hit. The threshold is this example's choice, and a report must state its own basis.
  3. Apply the threshold, and state the frequency and the reviewer. The reason: a threshold that is never applied monitors nothing. Part C's bias of 0.2500 units per month is below 2, so this window starts no review. The example names a monthly frequency and, as reviewer, the person who signs the monthly planning figure. The case names no reviewer, so the frequency and the reviewer are choices that the report must state with its own reason.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A model-risk reviewer must validate PF-SIM, the case model of the case section. The model sets a monthly planning figure, and its forecasts are compared with the realised values for months 13 to 36. You write the validation report in four sections, one for each element the project names. Every claim is either computed from the case table or sourced to a registry key with a locator, or it is marked not stated here.

(1) The case documentation in the case section, which is complete. (2) The case table printed by Lab 3 below, seed 20261103, 24 validation months. (3) The regulatory source described in the source note.

validation.md, with four sections in this order: Conceptual soundness, Outcomes analysis, Limitations, Ongoing monitoring. Without JavaScript, write self_assessment.md by hand: your five criterion scores, the scores you give each exemplar, and the criteria where they differ from the official scores.
Constraints
Every computed number comes from exact values and states the rounding rule. The report claims nothing about PF-SIM beyond its documentation and the Lab 3 table. The report does not describe the case as a real model, a real firm or a real planning figure. Each statement about a regulator's text carries its registry key and a locator. Each of the four sections is held to the expectation listed under attachment expectations. Standard library or any tool you choose; the rubric does not depend on the tool.
Attachment expectations
Each expectation is quoted from the attachment with the registry key frb2026fed and a locator. The sentence after each quote says what the section does with it. The attachment states sound practice, not requirements (see the source note).
  1. Conceptual soundness. "Validating conceptual soundness involves assessing and documenting model design (including key modeling choices, assumptions, qualitative judgments, and data selection), construction, and developmental testing." The section states each assumption of PF-SIM and what the documentation shows about the design. Item (a) of the case's not-documented list covers how the intercept and the slope were obtained.
  2. Outcomes analysis. "Outcomes analysis compares model outputs to corresponding real-world outcomes to assess model performance relative to model objectives and business use."   3. Limitations. The attachment has no heading for this element, so the element is the course's list.   4. Ongoing monitoring. "Ongoing model monitoring involves an evaluation of the extent to which a model is performing as expected given potential changes in products, exposures, activities, clients, data relevance, or market conditions." Grading mode
Rubric only. There is no autograder. The report is scored against the five criteria of the rubric, each with four observable levels. Part F: the bias over the calibration window, in units per month, to four decimals. It checks the calibration fit. It is
not part of the outcomes measures.

The hit rate and both biases are exact Fractions. A value is rounded once, when it is printed: half up (a tie goes away
from zero).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261103
LAST_MONTH = 36
CALIBRATION_END = 12
HIT_BAND = 2
SHIFT_FROM = 28
SHIFT_UNITS = -6


def forecast(t: int) -> int:
    """The case model's forecast for month index t, in units: 100 + 2 * t (assumption M1)."""
    return 100 + 2 * t


def is_hit(predicted: int, realised: int) -> bool:
    """True when the absolute error is at most the band of 2 units: |forecast - realised| <= 2."""
    if realised <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"the realised value must be a positive count of units, got {realised}")
    return abs(predicted - realised) <= HIT_BAND


def simulate_realised(seed: int) -> dict[int, int]:
    """The realised values of months 1 to 36, in units: 100 + 2 * t + noise, less 6 units from month 28 on."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    realised: dict[int, int] = {}
    for t in range(1, LAST_MONTH + 1):
        noise = rng.randint(-3, 3)
        shift = SHIFT_UNITS if t >= SHIFT_FROM else 0
        realised[t] = 100 + 2 * t + noise + shift
    return realised


def decimal_text(value: Fraction, places: int) -> str:
    """The value rounded half up (a tie goes away from zero) to places decimals, with exactly places digits."""
    scale = 10**places
    magnitude = math.floor(abs(value) * scale + Fraction(1, 2))
    sign = "-" if value < 0 and magnitude != 0 else ""
    whole, part = divmod(magnitude, scale)
    if places == 0:
        return f"{sign}{whole}"
    return f"{sign}{whole}.{part:0{places}d}"


def main() -> None:
    realised = simulate_realised(SEED)
    print(f"SIMULATED case, seed {SEED}: forecast(t) = 100 + 2t units; validation months 13 to 36, H = 24; band 2")
    print("Part A: month, forecast, realised, error (forecast - realised), hit")
    errors: dict[int, int] = {}
    hits = 0
    for t in range(CALIBRATION_END + 1, LAST_MONTH + 1):
        predicted = forecast(t)
        error = predicted - realised[t]
        errors[t] = error
        hit = is_hit(predicted, realised[t])
        if hit:
            hits += 1
        print(f"  t={t} forecast={predicted} realised={realised[t]} error={error:+d} hit={'yes' if hit else 'no'}")
    horizon = len(errors)
    hit_rate = Fraction(hits, horizon)
    print(f"Part B: hits {hits} of {horizon}; hit rate {decimal_text(hit_rate, 4)}")
    bias = Fraction(sum(errors.values()), horizon)
    print(f"Part C: bias {decimal_text(bias, 4)} units per month")
    edge = [t for t, e in errors.items() if abs(e) == HIT_BAND]
    print(f"Part D: months with |error| = 2 exactly (hits at the edge): {edge}")
    worst = max(errors, key=lambda t: (abs(errors[t]), -t))
    print(f"Part E: largest absolute error {abs(errors[worst])} units, month {worst}")
    calibration = [forecast(t) - realised[t] for t in range(1, CALIBRATION_END + 1)]
    calibration_bias = Fraction(sum(calibration), len(calibration))
    print(f"Part F: calibration-window bias {decimal_text(calibration_bias, 4)} units per month (months 1 to 12)")

Exemplar A

Conceptual soundness

PF-SIM is \(\hat{y}_t = 100 + 2t\), a model whose only input is the month index (case documentation). Its assumptions are M1, the trend of 2 units per month holds over the validation window, and M2, the forecast for month \(t\) is issued before month \(t\) starts and uses no information from month \(t\). The documentation does not say how the intercept 100 or the slope 2 were obtained, so their origin is not tested here.

M1 is tested on the validation window, months 13 to 36, which is the window the assumption is stated over. Lab 3 Part C gives a bias of 2.1250 units per month over that window, and the errors of months 28 to 36 are all positive, from 3 to 8 units (Part A). M1 is a claim about the trend, and a bias is a claim about the level: the errors are positive from month 28, so the forecast runs above the realised level from that month, and the window shows a level error. It does not by itself show whether the trend holds. The case does not say why the forecasts run high from month 28. The report does not test whether 2.1250 differs from zero: the case documents no sampling model for the realised values, so no range for the bias can be stated. Lab 3 Part F gives the calibration-window bias as 0.9167 units per month over months 1 to 12. That is a check of the fit, not a test of M1. Its errors sum to 11, the only integer sum that prints as 0.9167 over 12 months, and Part A does not print those months. M2 is not tested. The case table records the equation's values and no issue dates, so the timing of information cannot be checked from it.

  • Meets C1 level 4: lists both assumptions; gives the test for M1 on the validation window with its source (Part C and the Part A rows for months 28 to 36); names M2 as untested and gives the reason.
Outcomes analysis

A month is a hit when \(|e_t| \le 2\) units, with the equality counted, the same band and rule as Lesson 4. The hit rate is the hit count divided by \(H\). The bias is \(\frac{1}{H}\sum_t e_t\). The validation window is months 13 to 36, so \(H = 24\). Every measure is computed from exact values and rounded once, half up to four decimal places, when printed.

Lab 3 Part B gives 13 hits of 24 months, a hit rate of \(13/24 = 0.5417\). Part C gives a bias of 2.1250 units per month. The errors of the 24 months sum to 51, and \(51/24 = 2.125\), which prints as 2.1250.

The window divides at month 28, the month from which the lab's docstring lowers the realised value by 6 units. The sums of the two parts are computed from the Part A rows, and the lab does not print them. Months 13 to 27 (15 months): the errors sum to \(-3\), a bias of \(-3/15 = -0.2000\) units per month, with 13 hits, a hit rate of \(13/15 = 0.8667\). Months 28 to 36 (9 months): the errors sum to 54, a bias of \(54/9 = 6.0000\) units per month, with no hits, a hit rate of \(0/9 = 0.0000\). The two sums give the total of 51.

Part D lists seven months with \(|e_t| = 2\) exactly: 14, 15, 16, 19, 22, 24 and 26. Under a strict band they would be misses, and the hit count would fall from 13 to 6, a hit rate of 6 of 24, or 0.2500.

  • Meets C2 level 4: states both definitions, the band rule, the window and the rounding rule; computes both measures from exact sums; cites the Part of Lab 3 for each figure; derives the split from the rows.
Limitations

Limit one: PF-SIM has no input except the month index (case documentation). From month 28 every forecast misses, with errors from 3 to 8 units (Part A). The forecast cannot respond to the change from month 28, because it has no input that moves with it. A second model with such an input would show whether the misses could have been reduced; this case contains none, so the report cannot test that. If the limit were removed by an input that moves with the change, the misses from month 28 on could be checked against that input; the report cannot say whether they would have been avoidable.

Limit two: the validation window is one window of 24 months, and 9 of its months, 28 to 36, are the months of the change from month 28. The window cannot separate a persistent change from a single episode. A later window of months after 36 would show whether the misses persist. The case has no such window, so the report states the result from month 28 on as the result of one window only.

  • Meets C3 level 4: names two limits; gives the evidence for each (the documentation and the Part A rows); says what would change if each were removed.
Ongoing monitoring

Metric: the rolling 12-month bias, the sum of the last twelve errors divided by 12, computed at each month end. Threshold: an absolute rolling bias above 2.00 units per month starts a review. Basis: the band of 2 units in the conventions. A rolling bias above 2 units means the average error over the last twelve months lies outside the band that defines a hit. The Part F calibration-window bias of 0.9167 units per month (months 1 to 12) and the Part C validation-window bias of 2.1250 units per month (months 13 to 36) are whole-window averages, not rolling values, so they show where this case sits against the threshold and are not its basis. One case cannot justify a permanent threshold, so the threshold is set for the first review cycle and is to be re-derived on the next validation window. Frequency: monthly, at month end. Role: the person who signs the monthly planning figure, who receives the rolling bias. The case names no reviewer, so the role is this report's choice.

Model risk reviews commonly set thresholds on a rolling window. At month 36 the rolling bias is \(56/12 = 4.6667\) units per month, the sum of the errors of months 25 to 36 in Part A. That is above the threshold, so the first review starts now.

  • Meets C4 level 4: names one metric, the threshold with its basis, the frequency and the reviewing role.
  • Misses C5 level 4: the sentence "Model risk reviews commonly set thresholds on a rolling window" has no source and is not marked not stated here.
