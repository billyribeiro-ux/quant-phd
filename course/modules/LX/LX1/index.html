Financial History

This module covers four topics in financial history: bubbles and panics, the gold standard and debt deflation, the credit channel and the lender of last resort in the Great Depression, and the Bretton Woods system and its end. Each topic is taught as a mechanism for explaining an episode. Each mechanism is either a derivation with its assumptions named, a lab on seeded numbers labelled SIMULATED, or, in Lesson 4 and the project's rung-3 check (the independent project, see the project ladder), a computation on one embedded public-domain series. The module narrates no episode beyond what its sources state, and it gives no date, price or outcome for an episode that the sources read in this module do not establish. You will be able to state a benchmark and its assumptions, derive the real burden of a fixed debt, trace a mechanism through a stated model, and write a comparison of one episode and one current condition that cites every claim.

  • Prerequisites: FC1 The Financial System, Markets & Instruments and E5 Macroeconomics I: Aggregates, Cycles & Policy, which together pull in E1 Microeconomics, M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning, M2 Single & Multivariable Calculus and P0.1 How to Study This Program. P0.1 is not yet released in this program, so its concepts link to entries that read "not yet released" (see Prerequisites).
  • Project: "Then vs. now", a two-page essay graded by a rubric with exemplars. No autograder.
  • Data: one embedded series, the Federal Reserve H.10 monthly spot rates (fx_monthly), used in Lesson 4 and in the project's rung-3 check. The labs of Lessons 1 to 3 and the project's rungs 1 and 2 run on SIMULATED numbers. See Data status.
  • Atlas milestone: none. Debate brief: none.

40 h lessons · 10 h project (20% of 50 h)

Objectives

By the end of LX1 you can:

  1. State the constant-growth fundamental value, prove its closed form under the stated assumptions, and show that a price can be above the benchmark under one growth assumption and below it under another (Lesson 1).
  2. Define a speculative bubble as a gap relative to a named benchmark and a financial panic by its mechanism, and state the assumption the bubble definition depends on, while the panic definition rests on its mechanism alone (Lesson 1).
  3. Derive the real value of a fixed nominal debt at a given price level, compute its change over a price path in integer cents, and explain why a fall in prices raises the real burden of a fixed debt (Lesson 2).
  4. Trace a stated two-economy model of parity adherence and a credit-channel premium to its output depth, and state which printed orderings come from the model's assumptions and which do not describe any economy (Lesson 3).
  5. Compute the dispersion of monthly log changes in one embedded spot rate over two windows, with the rounding rule stated, and state what the series does not show (Lesson 4).
  6. Write a two-page comparison of one historical episode and one current market condition, with the mechanism at each step and a citation for every claim (project).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on the prerequisites (one on P0.1, one on E1, two on M1, one on M2, two on E5 and one on FC1) and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. For ten items that is eight correct: seven of ten is 70 percent, and eight of ten is 80 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar on its item is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. Which evidence grade applies to a result that is proved on the page?

    Options

    Grade T is the theorem grade (P0.1, evidence grade).

  2. In comparative statics, what is compared?

    Options

    Comparative statics compares equilibria across parameter values (E1, comparative statics).

  3. A perpetuity pays 100 cents at the end of each period, starting one period from now, and the discount rate is 10 percent per period. What is its present value, in cents?

    The present value is \(\sum_{k \ge 1} 100/1.1^{k} = 100/0.10 = 1000\) cents (M1, perpetuity).

  4. What is the discount factor for a payment two periods ahead at a per-period rate \(r\)?

    Options

    A payment two periods ahead is discounted by \(1/(1 + r)\) twice (M1, present value and discounting).

  5. What is the derivative of \(\ln x\) for \(x > 0\)?

    Options

    The derivative of the natural logarithm is \(1/x\) on \(x > 0\) (M2, derivative).

  6. A price index rises from 100 to 110 between two periods. Give the simple measured inflation over the period as a decimal.

    Simple inflation is \(P_t/P_{t-1} - 1 = 110/100 - 1 = 0.1\) (E5, price indices and measured inflation).

  7. In a closed economy, what does total saving equal?

    Options

    Proposition 1.7 of E5 shows that private plus public saving equals investment in a closed economy.

  8. A dealer's quote has a bid of 9950 cents and an ask of 10050 cents. What is the quoted spread, in cents?

    The quoted spread is the ask minus the bid, \(10050 - 9950 = 100\) cents (FC1, bid–ask spread).

  9. Skill check (rung 1): a fixed nominal debt of 1000 cents. The price level rises from 1.00 to 1.25. What is the real value of the debt, in cents?

    The real value is the nominal debt divided by the price level, \(1000/1.25 = 800\) cents. Lesson 2 derives the identity.

  10. Skill check (rung 2): in the real-debt identity \(D/P_t\), which quantity is held fixed over the path?

    Options

    The completion problem of rung 2 removes the lines that compute the real debt and the burden change, with the nominal debt fixed as the input.

Lessons

Four lessons of ten study hours each, which sum to the 40 lesson hours. Each lesson page shares one structure: graded claims, runnable labs placed where they belong in the argument, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 10 hBubbles and Panics: A Fundamental Benchmark and Its LimitsThe constant-growth fundamental value and its closed form, the speculative gap above a benchmark and why its sign depends on the assumed growth rate, and a financial panic defined by its mechanism. L02 · 10 hThe Gold Standard and Debt DeflationConvertibility and parity, the real value of a fixed nominal debt over a price path in integer cents, and the rise in the real value of a fixed nominal debt that a fall in prices implies. L03 · 10 hThe Great Depression: The Credit Channel and the Lender of Last ResortA stylised two-economy model of parity adherence and output depth, the credit-channel premium as a mechanism in that model, and the lender of last resort defined by its function. L04 · 10 hBretton Woods and Its EndSpot-rate dispersion from the embedded Federal Reserve H.10 series over two windows, with what the series does and does not show.

The project ladder

The module ends in Then vs. now, a two-page comparison. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    One fully solved and annotated computation of the real burden of a fixed nominal debt over two SIMULATED price paths, periods 0 to 5, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same task with the real-debt line and the burden-change line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Write a two-page essay on one historical episode and one current market condition. The essay gives the mechanism as a chain of at least three steps for each, names the step where the analogy fails, and cites every claim. Graded by the rubric and its exemplars. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a third episode, or a test of one claim in your essay on the embedded spot-rate series, with the window and its limitation stated.

Data status

One embedded series is used in LX1: the Federal Reserve H.10 spot rates in the snapshot fx_monthly, read in Lesson 4 and in the project's rung-3 check. The Board's licence statement reads: "Unless otherwise indicated, information on Board's website is in the public domain and may be copied and distributed without permission." The licence page is federalreserve.gov/disclaimer.htm. The series is spot-only, as its note states: each value is the last noon rate of its month, and the euro column starts in January 1999. Lesson 4 uses the GBP column, which has no blank cells in the file.

No historical series for gold reserves, price levels, output, unemployment or bank failures is embedded. The curriculum names embedded data for the Lesson 3 lab. No redistributable historical series for that lab is embedded in this program yet, so Lesson 3 runs a stylised model labelled SIMULATED, and its page says so.
