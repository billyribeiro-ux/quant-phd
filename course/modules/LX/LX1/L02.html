The Gold Standard and Debt Deflation

  • Uses: the price index of E5 (price indices and measured inflation), and the algebra of positive numbers
  • Introduces: gold standard (defined in §1), debt deflation (defined in §3)
  • Ttheorem, proved on the page
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or lab result
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A debt fixed in cents does not stay fixed in what it can buy. When the price index (defined in Section 2) falls, the same nominal payment buys more goods, so the real burden of the debt rises. This lesson defines the gold standard as a commitment to a fixed parity, proves the identity that links the real value of a fixed nominal debt to the price index, and shows that the real value rises in exactly the periods in which the index falls. The results are algebra on positive numbers. They say nothing about which economies had falling prices, or why.

1Convertibility at a fixed parity

Definition 1.1 (gold standard)

A gold standard is a monetary regime in which the issuer fixes a parity \(\kappa > 0\), a quantity of gold in grams for one unit of the currency, and makes the currency convertible at that parity: the issuer stands ready to buy and sell gold at the parity on demand.

The definition fixes one price, the price of gold in the currency, and nothing else. Proposition 1.2 states what it implies, and only under an explicit assumption about the issuer's conduct.

Proposition 1.2 (the currency price of gold)
  1. (A1) The issuer keeps the parity at every date \(t\): one unit of the currency is convertible into \(\kappa\) grams at every \(t\), with \(\kappa\) as in Definition 1.1.

Conclusion: at every date \(t\), one gram of gold costs \(1/\kappa\) units of the currency.

Proof

Fix a date \(t\). Under (A1), one unit of the currency buys \(\kappa\) grams at the parity. So \(\kappa\) grams cost one unit, and one gram costs \(1/\kappa\) units. The argument uses only the parity at date \(t\), and (A1) gives the parity at every date, so the price is \(1/\kappa\) at every date. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.3 (what Proposition 1.2 does not say)

Proposition 1.2 is a statement about one price, the currency price of gold. It says nothing about the prices of goods, and so nothing about the price index \(P_t\) of Section 2. The hypothesis (A1) does not involve \(P_t\), so the conclusion holds whatever path \(P_t\) follows.

2Nominal debt, real value and the price index

A nominal debt is an amount of currency that the debtor owes at each date of a contract, stated in the currency's own units and not indexed to prices. Here the amount is \(D\) cents. The price index is \(P_t\) at date \(t\), with base date \(0\). Its values are positive numbers, and \(P_0\) is the base value. The real value of the debt at date \(t\), in base-date cents, is the nominal amount divided by the index relative to its base:

\[ V_t = \frac{D\,P_0}{P_t}. \]

Proposition 2.1 uses two assumptions, numbered (A2) and (A3) and used throughout Sections 2 and 3.

Proposition 2.1 (the real-value identity)
  1. (A2) \(D\) is a fixed nominal debt: \(D\) is a positive integer number of cents, the same at every date, and the debtor pays it in full. The contract is not renegotiated.
  2. (A3) \(P_t\) is a positive real number for every date \(t\), and \(P_0\) is the base value.

Conclusion: (a) for every date \(t \ge 1\), \(V_t / V_{t-1} = P_{t-1} / P_t\); (b) for every \(H \ge 1\), \(V_H / V_0 = P_0 / P_H\).

Proof

(a) By (A2) and (A3), \(D\,P_0\) is a positive number, and \(V_t\) and \(V_{t-1}\) are defined and positive. Then \(V_t / V_{t-1} = (D\,P_0 / P_t) / (D\,P_0 / P_{t-1}) = P_{t-1} / P_t\), because the factor \(D\,P_0\) cancels.

(b) Multiply the equalities of (a) for \(t = 1, \dots, H\). The left side telescopes: \(\prod_{t=1}^{H} V_t / V_{t-1} = V_H / V_0\). The right side telescopes the same way: \(\prod_{t=1}^{H} P_{t-1} / P_t = P_0 / P_H\). Both products are equal, so \(V_H / V_0 = P_0 / P_H\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.2 (the index can be any positive path)

For a debt that satisfies (A2), Proposition 2.1 holds for every index path that satisfies (A3). The conclusion does not depend on how the index moves, or on what caused its movement. The positivity of \(D\) is needed: with \(D = 0\), the ratio in (a) is \(0/0\), and the identity is not defined.

3Debt deflation

Definition 3.1 (debt deflation)

Debt deflation is the rise in the real value \(V_t\) of a fixed nominal debt that a fall in the price index \(P_t\) implies by the identity of Proposition 2.1, with \(D\) held fixed. It is an arithmetic consequence, not a claim about the cause of any fall.

Corollary 3.2 (a fall in the index raises the real value)
  1. (A2) and (A3) of Proposition 2.1.

Conclusion: for every date \(t \ge 1\), \(V_t > V_{t-1}\) if and only if \(P_t < P_{t-1}\).

Proof

By Proposition 2.1(a), \(V_t / V_{t-1} = P_{t-1} / P_t\). Since \(V_{t-1} > 0\), the inequality \(V_t > V_{t-1}\) holds exactly when \(V_t / V_{t-1} > 1\). Since \(P_t > 0\), the inequality \(P_{t-1} / P_t > 1\) holds exactly when \(P_{t-1} > P_t\), that is, when \(P_t < P_{t-1}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.3 (what the corollary does not give)

Corollary 3.2 gives the direction of the change in \(V_t\), not its size. For a fall from \(P_{t-1}\) to \(P_t\), the real value is multiplied by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\), which is larger than one. The corollary says nothing about the cause of a fall, and nothing about whether any economy had one.

Lab 1 checks Proposition 2.1 and Corollary 3.2 on one simulated path, and checks the identity in part (b) as well.

Lab 1 · SIMULATED: the real value of a fixed nominal debt on an index path

Predicted before running. The identity \(V_t / V_{t-1} = P_{t-1} / P_t\) holds exactly at every date of the simulated path, so the printed check is True. The real value rises in exactly the periods in which the index falls, so the two counts of periods are equal and the set comparison is True. The path is SIMULATED: its index changes are seeded draws, and the nominal debt is an illustrative 1,000,000 cents (10,000.00 currency units). Part E checks the formula \(1/\kappa\) of Proposition 1.2 at an illustrative parity of 2/5 gram per currency unit. No historical series is used, and no data file is read.

"""LX1 L02 lab 1: the real value of a fixed nominal debt over a SIMULATED price-index path.

SIMULATED. The price index is generated from seeded draws of integer percentage changes, one per period, so
every run prints the same lines. No economy is described by these numbers. The nominal debt is an illustrative
amount of 10,000.00 currency units, which is 1,000,000 cents, and the base-date index is P_0 = 100 index points.

All money is in integer cents. The price index is an exact Fraction. The real value of the fixed nominal debt at date t,
in base-date cents, is V_t = D * P_0 / P_t, computed exactly and rounded once, half up, to a whole cent. The index is
shown to four decimal places, rounded half up, for display only.

Part A: the simulated price index P_t for t = 0 to 12, from the seeded draws.
Part B: the real value V_t in whole cents, and the exact identity V_t / V_(t-1) = P_(t-1) / P_t for every period.
Part C: the periods in which the price index falls, the periods in which the real value rises, and whether the two sets
are equal.
Part D: the change in real value over the whole horizon, exactly and in whole cents.
Part E: Proposition 1.2 at an illustrative parity: the currency price of one gram is 1 / kappa, and kappa times that
price is 1 exactly.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261104
HORIZON = 12
DEBT_CENTS = 1_000_000
BASE_INDEX = Fraction(100)
CHANGE_LOW_PERCENT = -4
CHANGE_HIGH_PERCENT = 3
KAPPA_GRAMS_PER_UNIT = Fraction(2, 5)


def round_half_up(amount: Fraction) -> int:
    """Round a Fraction to a whole number, with a tie going toward plus infinity."""
    return math.floor(amount + Fraction(1, 2))


def fixed_decimal(amount: Fraction, places: int) -> str:
    """Show a non-negative Fraction with a fixed number of decimal places, rounded half up, for display only."""
    scaled = round_half_up(amount * 10**places)
    whole, frac = divmod(scaled, 10**places)
    return f"{whole}.{frac:0{places}d}"


def price_path(seed: int, horizon: int) -> tuple[list[Fraction], list[int]]:
    """Return (P_0 .. P_horizon, percentage changes c_1 .. c_horizon) for a seeded simulated index.

    The index P_t = P_(t-1) * (1 + c_t / 100) is exact. Each change c_t is an integer percent drawn uniformly from
    CHANGE_LOW_PERCENT to CHANGE_HIGH_PERCENT, inclusive.
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    prices = [BASE_INDEX]
    changes: list[int] = []
    for _ in range(horizon):
        change = rng.randint(CHANGE_LOW_PERCENT, CHANGE_HIGH_PERCENT)
        changes.append(change)
        prices.append(prices[-1] * (1 + Fraction(change, 100)))
    return prices, changes


def real_value(debt_cents: int, base: Fraction, price: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Real value of a fixed nominal debt, in base-date cents: D * P_0 / P_t.

    Raises ValueError when the price index is not positive, because the real value is then undefined.
    """
    if price <= 0:
        raise ValueError("the price index must be positive")
    return Fraction(debt_cents) * base / price


def gram_price(kappa: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Currency units per gram at a parity of kappa grams per currency unit: 1 / kappa, exact.

    Raises ValueError when the parity is not positive, because the price is then undefined.
    """
    if kappa <= 0:
        raise ValueError("the parity must be positive")
    return 1 / kappa


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to E in a fixed order."""
    prices, changes = price_path(SEED, HORIZON)

    print("Part A: SIMULATED price index, base P_0 = 100 index points, shown to four decimals, rounded half up")
    print(f"t =  0: index {fixed_decimal(prices[0], 4)}")
    for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1):
        print(f"t = {t:2d}: change {changes[t - 1]:+d} percent; index {fixed_decimal(prices[t], 4)}")

    values = [real_value(DEBT_CENTS, BASE_INDEX, p) for p in prices]
    cents = [round_half_up(v) for v in values]
    print(f"Part B: real value of the fixed nominal debt of {DEBT_CENTS} cents, in base-date cents")
    for t in range(HORIZON + 1):
        print(f"t = {t:2d}: real value {cents[t]} cents")
    identity = all(values[t] / values[t - 1] == prices[t - 1] / prices[t] for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1))
    print(f"identity V_t / V_(t-1) = P_(t-1) / P_t holds exactly for t = 1 to {HORIZON}: {identity}")

    falls = [t for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1) if prices[t] < prices[t - 1]]
    rises = [t for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1) if values[t] > values[t - 1]]
    print("Part C: falls in the price index and rises in the real value")
    print(f"periods with a falling price index: {len(falls)} of {HORIZON}")
    print(f"periods with a rising real value: {len(rises)} of {HORIZON}")
    print(f"real value rises exactly in the periods where the index falls: {falls == rises}")
    steps = [cents[t] - cents[t - 1] for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1)]
    top = max(range(HORIZON), key=lambda i: steps[i]) + 1
    print(f"largest one-period rise in real value: {steps[top - 1]} cents at t = {top}")

    print("Part D: change over the whole horizon")
    print(f"real value at t = {HORIZON}: {cents[HORIZON]} cents, against {cents[0]} cents at t = 0")
    telescoped = values[HORIZON] / values[0] == prices[0] / prices[HORIZON]
    print(f"V_{HORIZON} / V_0 = P_0 / P_{HORIZON} holds exactly: {telescoped}")
    print(f"ratio V_{HORIZON} / V_0 to six decimals: {fixed_decimal(values[HORIZON] / values[0], 6)}")

    kappa = KAPPA_GRAMS_PER_UNIT
    price_per_gram = gram_price(kappa)
    print(f"Part E: Proposition 1.2 at an illustrative parity of {kappa} gram per currency unit")
    print(f"currency price of one gram = 1 / kappa = {price_per_gram} currency units")
    print(f"kappa times the gram price equals 1 exactly: {kappa * price_per_gram == 1}")

4Where the canon breaks

The identity of Proposition 2.1 is algebra. Each of the following shows what the algebra does not cover.

  • The real value depends on the index. The formula uses one index, \(P_t\). A second index, built from a different basket of goods, gives a different real value, and Proposition 2.1 holds for each index separately. Nothing in this lesson relates the two. The index is the quantity linked in the header to the E5 concept of measured prices. Its choice is an input to this lesson, not a result of it.
  • A debtor who does not pay in full. Assumption (A2) requires full payment and no renegotiation. Without it, the formula still gives the contract's real value \(V_t\), but the real value of the payment received is a different quantity, and Corollary 3.2 does not describe it.
  • An indexed payment. A contract with payments indexed to prices has \(D\) changing with \(P_t\). Under that contract the real value is constant, and Corollary 3.2 does not apply. Problem R2 works this case.
  • The cause of the index path. Proposition 2.1 takes the path \(P_t\) as given. It does not say why the index fell, and the lesson takes no position on the causal role of the gold standard in any historical fall in prices. This lesson cites no source for a causal claim, so none is made here.

5Misconceptions

  1. “When prices fall, a fixed debt becomes cheaper to repay.”

    Corollary 3.2 gives the opposite direction for the real value. A fall in \(P_t\) raises \(V_t\), because the same nominal payment buys more goods. Lab 1, Part C, prints six periods with a falling index and six with a rising real value, and the two sets of periods are equal.

  2. “Fixing the gold price of the currency fixes the price level.”

    Proposition 1.2 gives one price, the currency price of gold, and Remark 1.3 says that it implies nothing about \(P_t\). The simulated index in Lab 1, Part A, changes in each of its twelve periods, while the index path of Parts A to D is not linked to the gold price that Part E computes, so the lab does not link the two prices.

  3. “A fixed nominal debt has a fixed real value.”

    The nominal amount \(D\) is fixed, but the real value \(V_t = D\,P_0 / P_t\) changes whenever \(P_t\) changes. Lab 1, Part B, prints a real value that differs from its predecessor in each of the twelve periods. For a fixed nominal debt, the real value is constant exactly when the index is constant, because \(V_t = D\,P_0 / P_t\) equals \(V_0 = D\) only when \(P_t = P_0\). An index-linked payment (Problem R2) is not a fixed nominal debt, so its real value can be constant while the index moves.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1.

Mechanics

M1 · The real value after one fall in the index

A fixed nominal debt is \(D = 2{,}000\) cents, the base index is \(P_0 = 100\), and the index falls to \(P_1 = 80\). Compute \(V_1\) in base-date cents.

Solution

\(V_1 = D\,P_0 / P_1 = 2000 \cdot 100 / 80 = 200000 / 80 = 2500\) cents. The division is exact, so no rounding is needed. The real value rose from 2000 to 2500 cents, a factor of \(100/80 = 1.25\), as Corollary 3.2 requires for a fall in the index.

M2 · A one-period ratio with the debt cancelled

With \(P_0 = 100\), \(P_1 = 90\) and \(P_2 = 85\), find \(V_2 / V_1\) as an exact fraction, without computing \(D\).

Solution

By Proposition 2.1(a), \(V_2 / V_1 = P_1 / P_2 = 90 / 85 = 18 / 17\). The debt \(D\) cancels, so the answer does not depend on it. As a check, \(V_1 = D \cdot 100 / 90\) and \(V_2 = D \cdot 100 / 85\), and their ratio is \((100 / 85) / (100 / 90) = 90 / 85\).

M3 · A rounded real value

A fixed nominal debt is \(D = 2000\) cents, the base index is \(P_0 = 100\), and the index at date 1 is \(P_1 = 95\). Compute \(V_1\) to the nearest cent, with ties going up.

Solution

\(V_1 = 200000 / 95 = 2105.2631\ldots\) cents. The unrounded value is kept until the last step, and the fractional part \(0.263\ldots\) is below one half, so the nearest cent, with ties up, is 2105 cents.

Proof

P1 · The direction of the real-value change

Under (A2) and (A3), show that \(P_t = P_{t-1}\) implies \(V_t = V_{t-1}\), and that \(P_t > P_{t-1}\) implies \(V_t < V_{t-1}\).

Solution

If \(P_t = P_{t-1}\), then \(V_t = D\,P_0 / P_t = D\,P_0 / P_{t-1} = V_{t-1}\) directly from the definition. If \(P_t > P_{t-1}\), both are positive, so \(1 / P_t < 1 / P_{t-1}\). Multiplying by the positive number \(D\,P_0\) gives \(V_t < V_{t-1}\).

P2 · The telescoped identity by induction

Prove Proposition 2.1(b) for every \(H \ge 1\) by induction on \(H\), using only Proposition 2.1(a) and (A2), (A3).

Solution

For \(H = 1\), (a) gives \(V_1 / V_0 = P_0 / P_1\), which is (b). Suppose \(V_H / V_0 = P_0 / P_H\). By (a) at \(H + 1\), \(V_{H+1} / V_H = P_H / P_{H+1}\). Multiplying the two equalities gives \(V_{H+1} / V_0 = (P_0 / P_H)(P_H / P_{H+1}) = P_0 / P_{H+1}\), which is (b) at \(H + 1\). The factors \(P_H\) and \(V_H\) are positive, so the divisions are defined.

P3 · Rescaling the index leaves the real value unchanged

Let \(c > 0\) and replace every index value \(P_t\) by \(c P_t\), keeping \(D\) fixed. Show that every real value \(V_t\) is unchanged.

Solution

The new base value is \(c P_0\), and the new index at date \(t\) is \(c P_t\). The new real value is \(D \cdot (c P_0) / (c P_t) = D \cdot P_0 / P_t = V_t\), because the factor \(c\) cancels. So the real value depends on the index only through the ratio \(P_0 / P_t\), and not on the units in which the index is quoted.

Empirical

E1 · The count of falling periods

Lab 1, Part C prints six periods with a falling price index out of twelve. Count the negative percentage changes in Part A, and say which periods they are.

Solution

The negative changes in Part A are at \(t = 1\) (\(-1\) percent), \(t = 5\) (\(-4\)), \(t = 6\) (\(-1\)), \(t = 8\) (\(-4\)), \(t = 9\) (\(-3\)) and \(t = 12\) (\(-4\)). That is six, so the printed count of six agrees. The positive changes are at \(t = 2, 3, 4, 7, 10, 11\), which are also six, so the twelve changes are all non-zero.

E2 · The first real value by hand

Lab 1, Part B prints \(V_1 = 1010101\) cents. Compute \(V_1\) from \(D = 1{,}000{,}000\) cents and the index \(P_1 = 99\), and state the rounding rule you use.

Solution

\(V_1 = 1000000 \cdot 100 / 99 = 1010101.0101\ldots\) cents. Rounded to the nearest cent, with ties up, this is 1010101 cents, which agrees with the printed value. The index \(P_1 = 99\) is exact, because \(100 \cdot (1 - 0.01) = 99\), and the rule is applied once, at the last step.

E3 · The horizon ratio to six decimals

Lab 1, Part D prints \(V_{12} / V_0\) to six decimals as 1.055909, and Part A prints \(P_{12}\) as 94.7051. Compute \(P_0 / P_{12}\) from the printed index, and say why it agrees with the printed ratio to six decimals.

Solution

From the printed index, \(100 / 94.7051 = 1.0559093\ldots\), which is 1.055909 to six decimals. The printed index is itself a four-decimal display. The lab uses the unrounded index, which is \(94.70509914\ldots\), and \(100 / 94.70509914\ldots = 1.05590935\ldots\), which is also 1.055909 to six decimals. The two agree to six decimals because the index rounding changes the ratio by less than one part in a million.

Research

R1 · What a finite check cannot show

Lab 1 checks the identity on twelve periods of one path. Explain why this check cannot establish Proposition 2.1 for all paths, and identify the step of this lesson that does.

Solution

The lab checks finitely many dates on one path, so it covers a finite set of cases. Proposition 2.1 is a statement about every path that satisfies (A3). Its proof uses only the cancellation of the positive factor \(D\,P_0\) in the ratio, which holds for any positive values. That algebra, in Proposition 2.1(a), covers all paths at once, and the lab serves as a numerical check of it.

R2 · An index-linked payment

Suppose the payment at date \(t\) is \(D_t = D\,P_t / P_0\), so the nominal payment moves with the index. Compute the real value \(D_t \cdot P_0 / P_t\), and say which assumption of Proposition 2.1 fails.

Solution

The real value is \(D_t \cdot P_0 / P_t = (D P_t / P_0) \cdot P_0 / P_t = D\), the same at every date. Assumption (A2) fails, because the nominal amount \(D_t\) is no longer the same at every date. Corollary 3.2 does not apply: the real value does not rise when the index falls, since it does not change at all. When the index falls, the nominal payment falls with it.

R3 · Two indices and one debt

Let \(Q_t\) be a second positive index, built from a different basket, and let \(W_t = D\,Q_0 / Q_t\) be the real value measured with it. Suppose \(P\) rises in a period in which \(Q\) falls. State what Corollary 3.2 says about each real value, and say whether the lesson's results relate the two.

Solution

Corollary 3.2 applies to each index separately, given (A2) and (A3) for that index. For \(P\), the real value falls in that period, because the index rose. For \(Q\), the real value rises, because the index fell. Both statements are true, and they concern different measures of the same nominal debt. The lesson's proofs use only the one index that the definition names, so they establish no relation between \(V_t\) and \(W_t\). Such a relation would need a separate assumption about the two baskets.

  2. Under (A2) and (A3), when does the real value of a fixed nominal debt rise from one date to the next?

    Options

    Corollary 3.2: \(V_t > V_{t-1}\) if and only if \(P_t < P_{t-1}\). A positive nominal debt alone does not decide the direction.

  3. With \(P_0 = 100\) and \(P_1 = 80\), what is \(V_1 / V_0\)?

    \(V_1 / V_0 = P_0 / P_1 = 100 / 80 = 1.25\), by Proposition 2.1(a).

  4. In Lab 1, what is the real value \(V_1\) of the debt of 1,000,000 cents, in whole cents?

    \(V_1 = 1000000 \cdot 100 / 99 = 1010101.0101\ldots\), which is 1010101 cents to the nearest cent (Problem E2). Lab 1, Part B, prints the same value.

  5. Does Proposition 2.1 hold for every positive index path?

    Options

    The proof in §2 cancels the positive factor \(D\,P_0\) and uses only (A2) and (A3). Lab 1 checks the identity on one path, which is a finite check (Problem R1).

  6. In Lab 1, Part C, how many of the twelve periods have a falling price index?

    Six: the changes at \(t = 1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12\) are negative (Problem E1). Lab 1, Part C, prints 6 of 12.

  7. Does the gold standard of Definition 1.1, by itself, imply a constant price index \(P_t\)?

    Options

    Remark 1.3: the gold price is one price. The index \(P_t\) is a different measure, and the definition does not constrain it.

  8. With \(P_1 = 90\) and \(P_2 = 85\), what is \(V_2 / V_1\) to ten decimals?

    \(V_2 / V_1 = P_1 / P_2 = 90 / 85 = 18/17 = 1.0588235294\ldots\) (Problem M2). The debt cancels.

  9. Lab 1 is labelled SIMULATED. What does that label tell a reader about its numbers?

    Options

    The index path is generated from a seeded generator (Lab 1 docstring). No historical series is read, so the lab supports the identity and nothing about history (Remark 3.3).

  10. If the debt is indexed so that \(D_t = D\,P_t / P_0\), what happens to its real value over time?

    Options

    Problem R2: \(D_t \cdot P_0 / P_t = D\). Assumption (A2) fails, because the nominal amount is not fixed, so Corollary 3.2 does not apply.
