LX1 project · 10 hours · rubric-graded essay

Then vs. now

A reader of financial history asks whether an old episode tells them anything about today. You answer in two printed pages: one historical episode and one current market condition, the mechanism at each step, the step where the analogy breaks, and a citation for every claim. The essay is graded by a rubric against two annotated model essays (the exemplars).

The ladder

Rungs 1 and 2 use illustrative price paths, labelled SIMULATED (written into the lab code, not data). They read no file. If you already have the skill they teach, and you reached the mastery bar of the placement diagnostic (80 percent) and answered both skill checks, skip to rung 3.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Open-ended extensions in the stretch section, for your portfolio.

Conventions and definitions

The labs and the rubric use these definitions. Each rate is in the unit stated with it.

Nominal debt and real value
The nominal debt \(D\) is a fixed number of cents in every period (lab assumption A1). The price level \(P_t\) is a positive ratio with \(P_0 = 1\) (lab assumption A2). The real value of the debt is \(V_t = D / P_t\), which is Lesson 2's \(V_t = D P_0 / P_t\) with \(P_0 = 1\). The labs call it real debt. Lesson 2 states its assumptions as (A2) and (A3); the labs' A1 and A2 are their own and are not Lesson 2's.
Burden change
The burden change from period 0 to period \(t\) is \(100\,(P_0 / P_t - 1)\), in percent of the period-0 real value. A positive number is a rise in the real burden. Because \(V_t / V_0 = P_0 / P_t\), the change needs no rounded real value.
Monthly log change and dispersion
For a monthly exchange rate \(S_m\), the monthly log change is \(\ln(S_m / S_{m-1})\), per month and not annualised. The dispersion of a window is the sample standard deviation of its monthly log changes, with denominator \(n - 1\), reported in percent per month. Each standard deviation is computed twice, by the two-pass formula (the mean, then the squared deviations from it) and with the standard library's statistics.stdev; the lab raises an error if the two differ by more than 1e-9 percent per month. An exact zero change is a month whose rate equals the previous month's rate, so its log change is zero.
The three windows
The windows are for the GBP column that Lab 3 reads. Window one holds every monthly change of that column, 1971-02 to 2026-09 (668 changes). Window two holds the changes dated 2000-01 to 2026-09 (321 changes); its first change uses the rate of 1999-12. Windows one and two are the windows of Lesson 4. Window three holds the changes dated 2025-10 to 2026-09 (12 changes), the twelve months to the snapshot's last month, and is the current window of the brief (section The brief, below).
Evidence grade and counter-evidence
You grade each factual claim in your essay on the site's evidence scale, which criterion 3 of the rubric uses: T, a theorem proved on the page or with a precisely cited proof; A, replicated out of sample by independent teams, across periods and/or markets; B, published in a top peer-reviewed venue but not robustly replicated or actively contested; C, a working paper, practitioner research or single-sample evidence; D, practitioner lore with no systematic evidence; X, refuted, failed to replicate or retracted. Counter-evidence is published evidence that points the other way.
Rounding
Every computed number is computed from unrounded values. It is rounded once, half up (a tie goes away from zero), when it is printed, and the rounding rule is stated where the number first appears in your essay.

Lab 1 · Worked example: the real burden on two price paths

A fixed nominal debt of 100000.00 dollars (A1) is held against two SIMULATED price paths, falling and rising, each over five periods from \(P_0 = 1\). The lab prints the real value in each period, the burden change from period 0 to period 5, and the period-5 difference between the paths. Expected: the falling path raises the real value to 117647.06 dollars by period 5, a burden change of 17.65 percent; the rising path lowers it to 86956.52 dollars, a change of -13.04 percent; the difference is 30690.54 dollars.

"""LX1 project lab 1 (rung 1, worked example): the real burden of a fixed nominal debt on two price paths.

SIMULATED. Both price paths are illustrative values written into this file. They are not data, and no random draws
are used, so every run prints the same lines.

Assumptions. A1: the nominal debt is fixed at 10,000,000 cents (100000.00 dollars) in every period. A2: the price
level P_t is a positive ratio with P_0 = 1, so the real debt in period t is the nominal debt divided by P_t.

Part A: the real debt of each period of each path, in dollars to the cent.
Part B: the burden change of each path from period 0 to period 5, in percent of the period-0 real debt. It is
100 * (P_0 / P_5 - 1), a ratio of price levels, so no real debt has to be rounded before it is computed.
Part C: one comparison, the period-5 real debt of the falling path minus that of the rising path, in dollars.

Every value is an exact Fraction. A value is rounded once, when it is printed: half up (a tie goes away from zero),
to the whole cent for dollar amounts and to two decimals for percentages.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

NOMINAL_DEBT_CENTS = 10_000_000  # 100000.00 dollars, fixed in every period (A1)
PATHS: dict[str, tuple[Fraction, ...]] = {
    "falling": tuple(Fraction(p, 100) for p in (100, 97, 94, 91, 88, 85)),
    "rising": tuple(Fraction(p, 100) for p in (100, 103, 106, 109, 112, 115)),
}


def real_debt_cents(nominal_cents: int, price_level: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The real debt in cents: the nominal debt divided by the price level, which must be positive (A2)."""
    if price_level <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"price level must be positive, got {price_level}")
    return Fraction(nominal_cents) / price_level


def burden_change_percent(start_price: Fraction, end_price: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The change in real debt from the start to the end of a path, in percent of the start: 100 * (start / end - 1)."""
    return 100 * (start_price / end_price - 1)


def round_half_up(value: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest integer to a value of either sign, a tie going away from zero."""
    magnitude = math.floor(abs(value) + Fraction(1, 2))
    return magnitude if value >= 0 else -magnitude


def fixed_two(hundredths: int) -> str:
    """An integer count of hundredths as a decimal with two places, for example -1304 as -13.04."""
    sign = "-" if hundredths < 0 else ""
    whole, part = divmod(abs(hundredths), 100)
    return f"{sign}{whole}.{part:02d}"


def main() -> None:
    print("SIMULATED price paths, P_0 = 1.00; nominal debt 100000.00 dollars, fixed (A1)")
    for name, path in PATHS.items():
        print(f"Part A, path {name}, real debt in dollars by period:")
        for t, price in enumerate(path):
            cents = round_half_up(real_debt_cents(NOMINAL_DEBT_CENTS, price))
            print(f"  t={t} price={fixed_two(int(price * 100))} real debt={fixed_two(cents)}")
    print("Part B, burden change from t=0 to t=5, percent of the period-0 real debt:")
    for name, path in PATHS.items():
        change = burden_change_percent(path[0], path[-1])
        print(f"  path {name}: {fixed_two(round_half_up(change * 100))}")
    falling_end = real_debt_cents(NOMINAL_DEBT_CENTS, PATHS["falling"][-1])
    rising_end = real_debt_cents(NOMINAL_DEBT_CENTS, PATHS["rising"][-1])
    print("Part C, period 5, falling path minus rising path, real debt in dollars:")
    print(f"  difference: {fixed_two(round_half_up(falling_end - rising_end))}")

Each printed real value is the nominal debt divided by the price level, rounded half up to the cent after the division. The burden change is a ratio of two price levels, so it is computed from exact values. The printed numbers describe the two SIMULATED paths only. They say nothing about any economy, and nothing about why either path moved. A falling price level raises the real value of a fixed nominal debt; that is the arithmetic of debt deflation as Lesson 2 defines it, shown here on two paths.

The worked steps, with the reason for each

  1. Write the price paths with \(P_0 = 1\). The reason: the ratio \(P_0 / P_t\) then equals the price level's inverse, and the real value at period 0 equals the nominal debt. Both paths start from the same debt.
  2. Divide the nominal debt by each price level. The reason: a debt fixed in cents buys less when prices are higher, so the real value is \(D / P_t\). On the falling path the divisor shrinks and the real value grows, from 100000.00 to 117647.06 dollars.
  3. Round once, at print time. The reason: each printed figure is a function of exact values, so rounding happens once, at print time. The real value at period 5 is the exact value 10000000 / 0.85 cents, about 11764705.882 cents, which prints as 117647.06 dollars.
  4. Take the burden change as a ratio of price levels. The reason: \(100\,(P_0 / P_5 - 1)\) gives 17.65 percent on the falling path, computed from exact values. Its base is the period-0 real value, and its sign gives the direction.
  5. Compare the paths on unrounded values. The reason: the difference of the two period-5 real values is the exact value 10000000 / 0.85 - 10000000 / 1.15 cents, about 3069053.708 cents, which prints as 30690.54 dollars. The difference of the two printed values would give the same figure here, but the rule is the unrounded one.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A reader asks whether an old episode of financial history says anything about a condition in the market today. You write a two-page comparison of one episode and one current condition. For each, you show the mechanism as a chain of at least three steps and name the step at which the analogy fails. Every claim is cited, with a locator, to a source you have read, or is marked as your own inference.
Inputs
(1) Your episode and your current condition. You choose both; the page supplies no list. (2) A source for each claim, with a DOI, an ISBN or the issuer's URL, and a locator (page, table, section or title field). (3) Optionally, the embedded snapshot fx_monthly (its id in the site's MANIFEST, the dataset record that states each column's source and transform), described in the data section. (4) Labs 1 and 2 use SIMULATED paths only. Lab 3 reads the embedded snapshot.
Required outputs
essay.md, or a printed PDF of it: two printed pages at the default type size of the page you print to. It states the episode, the condition, the mechanism chain of each with its failure step and that step's evidence, each computed number with its formula and rounding rule, and two limits of the analogy, with the test that would settle at least one of them. self_assessment.md is the summary that the form in the self-assessment section produces.
Constraints
Every computed number comes from unrounded values, and its rounding rule is stated. Every claim about a dataset stays within its MANIFEST entry: fx_monthly holds one value per month for each currency column, the last value of its month (MANIFEST transform field), blank before a column's first observation (the euro column is blank before January 1999), and no interest-rate column. No claim about a source goes beyond its locator. No claim about an episode's date or outcome appears without a locator in a source you have read. A claim graded B or C names its counter-evidence, or the essay states that it makes no such claim. Use the standard library or any tool you choose; the rubric does not depend on the tool.
Grading mode
Rubric only. The essay is scored against the five criteria of the rubric, each with four observable levels. You score your own essay with the self-assessment form, then score the two exemplars with their annotations hidden, and the form shows where your scores differ from the official ones. No adversarial review is used for this project.

Lab 3 · The dispersion of the GBP exchange rate, in three windows

The lab reads the GBP column of data/fx_monthly.csv, from Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, G.5/H.10 Foreign Exchange Rates (noon buying rates in New York), retrieved 2026-10-19 (MANIFEST). It computes the sample standard deviation of monthly log changes in window one, 1971-02 to 2026-09, window two, 2000-01 to 2026-09, and window three, 2025-10 to 2026-09. Expected: window one has 668 changes and a dispersion of 2.79 percent per month, with 3 exact zero changes; window two has 321 changes and a dispersion of 2.47 percent per month, with 2 exact zero changes; window three has 12 changes and a dispersion of 1.90 percent per month, with no exact zero change; the ratio of window two to window one is 0.88; the difference of window one minus window two is 0.32 percent per month; the two methods of computing the standard deviation agree.

"""LX1 project lab 3 (rung 3 check): the dispersion of the GBP exchange rate in the embedded H.10 snapshot.

Data. The lab reads data/fx_monthly.csv, the month-end exchange rates of the Board of Governors of the Federal
Reserve System, G.5/H.10 Foreign Exchange Rates (noon buying rates in New York). The snapshot was retrieved 2026-10-19
and holds 669 months, 1971-01 to 2026-09. Each value is the column's last value in its month, as the MANIFEST
transform field states. The column read is GBP. The MANIFEST title gives US dollars per unit of foreign currency,
and the transform inverts only CAD, CHF, DKK, JPY, NOK and SEK, so GBP is in US dollars per pound sterling.
The file has no interest-rate column.

Windows. Windows one and two are the two windows of LX1 Lesson 4. That lesson's lab reads the same column with the same
windows and prints the same change counts (668 and 321); its standard deviations are decimals (0.027935 and 0.024700),
which are 2.79 and 2.47 percent per month here. Window one holds every monthly change of the GBP column, dated 1971-02
to 2026-09 (668 changes). Window two holds the changes dated 2000-01 to 2026-09 (321 changes); its first change uses the
rate of 1999-12. Window three holds the changes dated 2025-10 to 2026-09 (12 changes), the twelve months to the
snapshot's last month; it is the current window of this project's brief.

Conventions. A monthly log change at month m is ln(S_m / S_(m-1)), per month, with no annualization. A window's
dispersion is the sample standard deviation of its changes, with denominator n - 1, reported in percent per month as
100 times that standard deviation.

Check. Each standard deviation is computed twice: by the two-pass formula, and with statistics.stdev. The two must agree
to 1e-9 percent per month, or the lab raises. A blank or non-decimal cell in the GBP column raises ValueError, and so
does a month that does not follow the previous one. No row is skipped or filled.

Rounding. Every printed value is the exact binary value of its float, rounded half up (a tie away from zero) to two
decimals. The ratio of the two standard deviations, and their difference, window one minus window two, are taken from
their unrounded values.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import csv
import itertools
import math
import re
import statistics
from collections.abc import Sequence
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal
from fractions import Fraction
from pathlib import Path

DATA_PATH = Path("data") / "fx_monthly.csv"
COLUMN = "GBP"
WINDOW_TWO_FROM = "2000-01"
WINDOW_THREE_FROM = "2025-10"
AGREEMENT = 1e-9  # percent per month
MONTH_PATTERN = re.compile(r"(\d{4})-(\d{2})")
DECIMAL_PATTERN = re.compile(r"\d+(\.\d+)?")


def month_index(month: str) -> int:
    """The month as a count of months from year 0, so that consecutive months differ by one."""
    found = MONTH_PATTERN.fullmatch(month)
    if found is None:
        raise ValueError(f"not a month written YYYY-MM: {month!r}")
    return int(found.group(1)) * 12 + int(found.group(2)) - 1


def load_column(path: Path, column: str) -> list[tuple[str, Fraction]]:
    """Every row of one column as an exact Fraction, in file order. Fails on a blank cell or a gap in the months."""
    with path.open(encoding="utf-8", newline="") as handle:
        reader = csv.DictReader(handle)
        if reader.fieldnames is None or column not in reader.fieldnames:
            raise ValueError(f"column {column} is missing from {path}")
        rows: list[tuple[str, Fraction]] = []
        for row in reader:
            month = row["month"]
            cell = row.get(column) or ""
            if DECIMAL_PATTERN.fullmatch(cell) is None:
                raise ValueError(f"{month}: {column} cell {cell!r} is blank or not a decimal number")
            rows.append((month, Fraction(Decimal(cell))))
    for (previous, _), (current, _) in itertools.pairwise(rows):
        if month_index(current) != month_index(previous) + 1:
            raise ValueError(f"month {current} does not follow {previous}")
    return rows


def log_changes(rows: Sequence[tuple[str, Fraction]]) -> list[tuple[str, float]]:
    """The monthly log change ln(S_m / S_(m-1)), labelled by its month m, for every month after the first."""
    return [(month, math.log(float(rate / previous))) for (_, previous), (month, rate) in itertools.pairwise(rows)]


def sample_sd_two_pass(values: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """The sample standard deviation with denominator n - 1, from the mean and the squared deviations."""
    n = len(values)
    mean = math.fsum(values) / n
    return math.sqrt(math.fsum((value - mean) ** 2 for value in values) / (n - 1))


def show(value: float, places: int) -> str:
    """The float rounded half up (a tie away from zero) to the given number of decimal places."""
    return str(Decimal(value).quantize(Decimal(1).scaleb(-places), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP))


def window_report(window: Sequence[tuple[str, float]]) -> float:
    """Prints the figures of one window and returns its sample standard deviation, in unrounded form."""
    values = [change for _, change in window]
    sd = sample_sd_two_pass(values)
    if abs(100 * (sd - statistics.stdev(values))) > AGREEMENT:
        raise ValueError("the two-pass and statistics.stdev standard deviations disagree")
    zeros = sum(1 for value in values if value == 0.0)
    biggest_month, biggest = max(window, key=lambda item: abs(item[1]))
    print(f"  changes: {len(values)}")
    print(f"  exact zero changes: {zeros}")
    print(f"  sample sd, percent per month: {show(100 * sd, 2)}")
    print(f"  largest monthly log change by size, percent, with its sign: {show(100 * biggest, 2)} ({biggest_month})")
    return sd


def main() -> None:
    rows = load_column(DATA_PATH, COLUMN)
    print(f"{COLUMN} exchange rate, US dollars per pound, H.10 snapshot retrieved 2026-10-19")
    print(f"months in file: {len(rows)}, {rows[0][0]} to {rows[-1][0]}")
    changes = log_changes(rows)
    window_one = changes
    window_two = [item for item in changes if item[0] >= WINDOW_TWO_FROM]
    window_three = [item for item in changes if item[0] >= WINDOW_THREE_FROM]
    print(f"Window one, {window_one[0][0]} to {window_one[-1][0]}:")
    sd_one = window_report(window_one)
    print(f"Window two, {window_two[0][0]} to {window_two[-1][0]}:")
    sd_two = window_report(window_two)
    print(f"Window three, {window_three[0][0]} to {window_three[-1][0]}:")
    window_report(window_three)
    print(f"ratio of window two to window one sd, two decimals: {show(sd_two / sd_one, 2)}")
    difference = show(100 * (sd_one - sd_two), 2)
    print(f"difference of window one minus window two sd, percent per month, two decimals: {difference}")
    print("two-pass and statistics.stdev agree in all three windows: True")

The figures describe the GBP column in the three windows. Each measures how far the rate moved from one month to the next, and none of them says why it moved. The largest monthly log change in window one, 13.58 percent in 1985-03, is outside window two, and the two windows have different dispersions: window one minus window two is 0.32 percent per month, the difference line of this lab, computed from unrounded dispersions. Window three has 12 changes, so its 1.90 percent per month is estimated from all 12 changes, with 11 degrees of freedom (n - 1). The two zero changes in window two are months whose last value equals the value of the month before. A parity, as the fixed-parity model of Lesson 4 (Definition 2.1) uses the word, would give a zero change in every month, so a parity held over window two would show 321 zero changes, against the 2 that window two shows.

The brief

  1. Name one historical episode and one current condition, each with its date and a locator for the date. A current condition is dated within the twelve months ending at the last month of the data you use. For the embedded snapshot, that is 2025-10 to 2026-09, window three of Lab 3.
  2. For each, state the mechanism as a chain of at least three steps. Name the first step at which the analogy fails, and give the evidence for the failure: a lab line, a locator, or a derivation.
  3. Give every computed number with its formula, its source line (the lab and its printed row, or a derivation), and its rounding rule.
  4. Give every factual claim a citation with a locator, or mark it as your own inference. A claim graded B or C names its counter-evidence, or the essay states that it makes no such claim.
  5. Name two limits of the analogy. For at least one of them, state the evidence that would test it.
  6. Keep the essay to two printed pages. The tools do not measure the length; the rubric judges the content.

Rubric

The essay is scored on five criteria. Each criterion has four levels, and each level is observable: a reader can check whether the essay shows the element the cell names. A criterion scores the highest level whose every element the essay shows. An essay that reaches level 3 or above on every criterion meets the bar.

Exemplar A

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 3. The essay gives both dates with locators, a chain for each mechanism with its failure step and evidence, and every number with its rule. C5 falls short of level 4: limit one has a test, but limits two and three name none.

Episode and condition

Episode one is the interwar gold-standard episode. Its period is the period in the title of Eichengreen (1992), Golden fetters: the gold standard and the Great Depression, 1919-1939 (registry key eichengreen1992golden, locator: the title field). The condition is the exchange rate of sterling in US dollars, from the G.5/H.10 snapshot, over window three of Lab 3, 2025-10 to 2026-09, the twelve months to the snapshot's last month. That last month is 2026-09 (MANIFEST, field last; retrieved 2026-10-19). The essay describes no other period and no other condition.

  • Meets C1 level 4: names the episode and the condition with dates, and a locator for each date (the title field, the MANIFEST field, and the window of Lab 3).
Mechanism, episode one

In the model of Lesson 2, the real value of a fixed nominal debt \(D\) is \(V_t = D P_0 / P_t\), and a fall in the price level from \(P_{t-1}\) to \(P_t\) multiplies it by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\), which exceeds one (Proposition 2.1(a) of Lesson 2, under its assumptions (A2) and (A3)). The chain has three steps: (1) the nominal debt is fixed, which is the model's assumption and not a claim about the episode; (2) the price level falls; (3) the real value rises by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\). The analogy fails at step 2. Remark 3.3 of Lesson 2 says the corollary gives no cause for a fall, so the model cannot say why the price level fell in the episode. The essay asserts no price path for the episode. The chain applies only if a fall occurred, and the essay does not claim one.

  • Meets C2 level 4: states the mechanism as three steps and names step 2 as the first failure, with its evidence (Remark 3.3 of Lesson 2).
Mechanism, condition

Step 1: the rate \(S_m\) is the last value of month \(m\) in the snapshot (MANIFEST transform field). Step 2: the monthly log change \(\ln(S_m / S_{m-1})\) measures the move. Step 3: the dispersion of window three is the sample standard deviation of its 12 changes, 1.90 percent per month (Lab 3). The analogy fails at step 2. Under a fixed parity every log change would be zero, because a constant rate has \(\ln 1 = 0\) as its change. Window three has 0 exact zero changes in 12 (Lab 3). The file therefore rules out a parity held over all of window three. The essay makes no claim about any exchange-rate regime.

  • Meets C2 level 4 for the condition: three steps, with the failure step at step 2 and its evidence (the 0 exact zero changes in window three of Lab 3).
Numbers

Lab 1 is SIMULATED and is not data. A fall in the price level from 1.00 to 0.85 raises the real value by \(100\,(1/0.85 - 1)\), which is 17.65 percent of the period-0 real value, computed from unrounded values and rounded half up to two decimals (Lab 1, Part B). Lab 3 gives 2.47 percent per month for window two, 2.79 percent per month for window one and 1.90 percent per month for window three, each from an unrounded standard deviation rounded half up to two decimals. Their ratio, window two over window one, is taken from the unrounded values and printed as 0.88 (Lab 3). The essay does not divide the printed figures.

  • Meets C4 level 4: each number comes from unrounded values, its rounding rule is stated, and its lab line is cited.
Limits

Limit one: the snapshot fx_monthly holds exchange rates only, with no interest-rate column (MANIFEST columns), so it cannot test the relation between interest rates and exchange rates, such as covered interest parity, which Lesson 4 names but does not state or test (Lesson 4, Section 4, item "Exchange rates only"). A test would need the short rates of both countries over the same months. The essay names no source for them, and so it runs no such test. Limit two: the start month is a choice. Window one, from 1971-02, contains the largest absolute monthly log change of the GBP column, 13.58 percent in 1985-03 (Lab 3, window one: the largest-change line, rounded half up to two decimals from the unrounded change), and window two does not. The difference between the dispersions, window one minus window two, is 0.32 percent per month (Lab 3, difference line, rounded half up to two decimals from the unrounded dispersions). Limit three: the essay does not test the causal role of the gold standard, and it makes no causal claim about it, so it needs no counter-evidence for one. The essay makes no claim graded B or C, so no claim needs counter-evidence, and every factual claim above carries its locator or is the essay's own inference.

  • Meets C3 level 4: every factual claim carries a locator or is marked as inference, and the essay states that it makes no claim graded B or C.
  • Meets C5 level 3: names three limits, and the first one gives the test that would settle it.
  • Misses C5 level 4: limits two (the window start, with the difference between the two windows' dispersions) and three name no test that would settle them.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 2 · C2 2 · C3 1 · C4 2 · C5 1. The essay names the episode and the condition and dates only the episode. It cites one source with a locator, names the H.10 data without one, and leaves at least three of its factual claims uncited. It states the volatility in the wrong unit, computes its ratio from rounded inputs, and names no specific limit.

Summary

Sterling today is nothing like the gold standard. The pound has been volatile, which the gold standard was meant to stop, so the analogy holds for the system as a whole.

  • Misses C3 level 3: the claim that the gold standard was meant to stop volatility has no source.
Data

The episode is the gold standard, 1919-1939, as the title field of Eichengreen (1992) names it. The condition is sterling against the dollar in the Federal Reserve's H.10 data, which is monthly. The window is recent.

  • Meets C1 level 2: names both the episode and the condition, and a date is missing for the condition only.
  • Meets C3 level 1: lists at least one source, the Eichengreen title field for the episode and the H.10 data for the condition, the latter without a locator.
  • Misses C3 level 2: at least three factual claims are uncited, namely the Summary's claims that the pound has been volatile and that the gold standard was meant to stop volatility, and the Mechanism's claim that both periods show falls in prices.
  • Misses C1 level 3: the condition has no date, so the essay does not name both with dates.
Numbers

The monthly volatility is 2.47 percent per year. The later window's volatility divided by the earlier window's is 2.47 divided by 2.79, which is 0.89, so volatility fell by about 11 percent.

  • Meets C4 level 2: rounds the inputs before computing the ratio. From the unrounded values Lab 3 prints 0.88, not 0.89.
  • Misses C4 level 3: the ratio is computed from rounded inputs, and no number in this passage states its rounding rule or cites its lab line.
Mechanism

Both periods have a currency tied to a standard, and both show falls in prices. One difference: the gold standard fixed the price of gold, and the spot rate does not. The analogy is therefore weak.

  • Meets C2 level 2: lists similarities and one difference, with no chain of steps.
  • Misses C3 level 3: the claim that both periods show falls in prices has no source.
Limits

The data have caveats, and the essay could be wrong about some of them.

  • Meets C5 level 1: notes that caveats exist, with no specific limit named.
