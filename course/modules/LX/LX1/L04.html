1Monthly log changes and dispersion

Lab 1 measures a recorded series, so it needs a measure of how far the series moves from month to month. This section defines that measure and proves the identity that Lab 1 checks on the data.

Definition 1.1 (monthly log change, dispersion)

Let \(x_t > 0\) be a rate at each month \(t\). The monthly log change at month \(t\) is \(\ell_t = \ln(x_t / x_{t-1})\), the natural logarithm of the ratio of two consecutive rates. It is a change per month and is not annualized. A window of \(n\) monthly log changes has dispersion \(s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k}(\ell_k - \overline{\ell})^{2}}\), the sample standard deviation of the \(n\) changes with denominator \(n-1\), where \(\overline{\ell}\) is their mean. The dispersion is defined only when \(n \ge 2\).

The simple change in a month is \(x_t / x_{t-1} - 1\). Log changes add across months, and simple changes do not add in general. A rise of 10 percent followed by a rise of 10 percent has simple changes that sum to 20 percent, but the total change is 21 percent, because \(1.1 \times 1.1 = 1.21\). Proposition 1.2 gives the additive property of log changes.

Proposition 1.2 (telescoping of log changes)

Assuming (A1): the rates \(x_t\) are positive for every month \(t\) from \(a\) to \(b\), where \(a < b\). Then \(\displaystyle\sum_{t=a+1}^{b} \ell_t = \ln\frac{x_b}{x_a}\). The sum has \(b-a\) terms.

Proof

By (A1), each ratio \(x_t / x_{t-1}\) is positive, and the logarithm of a quotient is the difference of the logarithms. So \(\ell_t = \ln x_t - \ln x_{t-1}\) for each \(t\) with \(a < t \le b\). Summing over \(t = a+1, \dots, b\), each interior term \(\ln x_t\) with \(a < t < b\) appears once with a plus sign, in \(\ell_t\), and once with a minus sign, in \(\ell_{t+1}\), so these terms cancel. What remains is \(\ln x_b - \ln x_a = \ln(x_b / x_a)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.3 (exact in real numbers, checked to a tolerance)

Proposition 1.2 is an exact identity in real numbers. Lab 1 checks it in floating-point arithmetic, to a tolerance of \(10^{-12}\). That check confirms the floating-point computation to that tolerance, and nothing more.

2A fixed-exchange-rate model

The lesson models a fixed-exchange-rate arrangement. Definition 2.1 states what the model contains. It does not state what any historical arrangement had: its fixed exchange rates, its participants or its end. No source for that arrangement is in the course's reference list, so this lesson states none, and Section 4 lists what is left open.

Definition 2.1 (fixed-exchange-rate model)

The model has an anchor currency, in which every rate is quoted, and one foreign currency. Its fixed exchange rate is a number \(\bar{x} > 0\) of anchor units per unit of the foreign currency. This is a different object from the gold parity of Lesson 2, which is a quantity of gold in grams. Its market rate at each month \(t\) is \(x_t > 0\), in the same units. A fixed-exchange-rate model is this model together with Assumption (A2), fixed rate held: at every month \(t\) from the month before the window's first change to its last change, \(x_t = \bar{x}\).

Bretton Woods system: definition pending. The concept's definition and any date for its end wait for a source from the course's reference list (Section 4). The fixed-exchange-rate model above is not a definition of the historical system.

Proposition 2.2 (zero dispersion in the model)
  1. (A2) as stated above, for the model of Definition 2.1, with \(\bar{x} > 0\), and with a window of \(n \ge 2\) monthly changes, so that \(x_t = \bar{x}\) at the month before the window's first change and at every later month of the window.

Assuming (A2): (a) every monthly log change in the window is \(0\); (b) the dispersion of the window is \(0\).

Proof

(a) By (A2), \(x_t = \bar{x}\) and \(x_{t-1} = \bar{x}\) for each change in the window. So \(\ell_t = \ln(\bar{x} / \bar{x}) = \ln 1 = 0\), using \(\ln 1 = 0\).

(b) Every change is \(0\), so their mean \(\overline{\ell}\) is \(0\), each deviation \(\ell_k - \overline{\ell}\) is \(0\), and the dispersion is \(\sqrt{0 / (n-1)} = 0\). The denominator \(n-1\) is at least \(1\), because \(n \ge 2\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (what Proposition 2.2 does not say)

Proposition 2.2 is a statement about the model. It says nothing about a recorded rate, which need not equal its fixed exchange rate at any month. Section 3 measures the dispersion of a recorded column, and Lab 1, Part A runs the model beside it. The lab does not derive the column from the model and does not test the two against each other. The fixed exchange rate here plays the part that the gold parity plays in the gold standard of L02. This model contains no gold and no metal, and nothing in Proposition 2.2 depends on either.

"""LX1 L04 lab 1: dispersion of one embedded H.10 column in two windows, against a fixed-exchange-rate model.

Data. The lab reads data/fx_monthly.csv, the exchange rates of the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve
System, G.5/H.10 Foreign Exchange Rates (noon buying rates in New York), one per month. Each value is the column's
last value in its month, as the MANIFEST transform states (month-end, each column's last value in the month). The
snapshot was retrieved 2026-10-19 and holds 669 months, 1971-01 to 2026-09. The column read is GBP, in US dollars per
pound sterling. The MANIFEST transform inverts CAD, CHF, DKK, JPY, NOK and SEK only, so GBP is a direct quote. The
file holds exchange-rate columns only: it has no interest-rate column and no forward-rate column (a forward rate is an
exchange rate agreed now for exchange at a later date).

Conventions. A monthly log change at month m is ln(x_m / x_(m-1)), with x_m the rate of month m, the natural
logarithm, per month, and no annualization. A window's dispersion is the sample standard deviation of its monthly log
changes, with denominator n - 1. Window one holds every change in the file, dated 1971-02 to 2026-09 (668 changes).
Window two holds the changes dated 2000-01 to 2026-09; its first change uses the rate of 1999-12. The window two start
is the first month from which the other two embedded snapshots, fx_daily and ust_daily, have data (both start
2000-01-03 in the MANIFEST).

Every rate is an exact Fraction parsed from its text. A blank cell, a non-decimal cell, a month that does not follow
the previous one, or a missing column raises ValueError. No row is skipped or filled.

Rounding. Log changes are floats computed from exact ratios. Printed values are the exact binary value of each float,
rounded half away from zero (Decimal ROUND_HALF_UP) to the number of places stated on the line.

Part A is a SIMULATED model check, not data: a fixed exchange rate of 2.0000 dollars per pound held for 12 months (11
monthly changes) gives zero monthly log changes and zero dispersion, which is the model's claim in Proposition 2.2 of
the lesson. Part B reads the GBP column and counts its changes and its exactly zero changes. Part C prints the count,
the dispersion and the largest absolute change for each window, and checks the telescoping identity of Proposition 1.2
of the lesson: the sum of a window's log changes equals the log of its last rate over the rate before the window. Part
D prints the difference and the ratio of the two windows' dispersions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import csv
import itertools
import math
import re
from collections.abc import Sequence
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal
from fractions import Fraction
from pathlib import Path

DATA_PATH = Path("data") / "fx_monthly.csv"
COLUMN = "GBP"
WINDOW_TWO_FROM = "2000-01"
MODEL_RATE = Fraction(2)
MODEL_MONTHS = 12
SUM_TOLERANCE = 1e-12

MONTH_PATTERN = re.compile(r"(\d{4})-(\d{2})")
DECIMAL_PATTERN = re.compile(r"\d+(\.\d+)?")


def month_index(month: str) -> int:
    """Return a serial number for a 'YYYY-MM' label, so that consecutive months differ by one.

    Raises ValueError when the label is not a YYYY-MM month with a month number from 1 to 12.
    """
    match = MONTH_PATTERN.fullmatch(month)
    if match is None:
        raise ValueError(f"not a YYYY-MM month: {month!r}")
    year, number = int(match.group(1)), int(match.group(2))
    if not 1 <= number <= 12:
        raise ValueError(f"month number out of range: {month!r}")
    return 12 * year + (number - 1)


def month_label(index: int) -> str:
    """Return the 'YYYY-MM' label of a serial number from month_index."""
    year, offset = divmod(index, 12)
    return f"{year:04d}-{offset + 1:02d}"


def parse_rate(text: str, month: str) -> Fraction:
    """Parse one published rate, a decimal written without sign or exponent, as an exact positive Fraction.

    Raises ValueError for a blank cell, any other text, or a zero rate. The lab never fills a cell.
    """
    if not DECIMAL_PATTERN.fullmatch(text):
        raise ValueError(f"{month}: {COLUMN} cell is {text!r}, not a decimal rate")
    rate = Fraction(text)
    if rate <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"{month}: {COLUMN} rate must be positive")
    return rate


def load_column(path: Path, column: str) -> list[tuple[str, Fraction]]:
    """Read one column of the snapshot as (month, rate) pairs in file order.

    Raises ValueError when the header lacks the column, when a month label is not YYYY-MM, when two months are not
    consecutive, or when a cell is not a decimal rate.
    """
    rows: list[tuple[str, Fraction]] = []
    previous: int | None = None
    with path.open(encoding="utf-8", newline="") as handle:
        reader = csv.DictReader(handle)
        if reader.fieldnames is None or column not in reader.fieldnames:
            raise ValueError(f"{path.name} has no column {column!r}")
        for record in reader:
            month = str(record["month"])
            index = month_index(month)
            if previous is not None and index != previous + 1:
                raise ValueError(f"{month} does not follow the previous month in {path.name}")
            previous = index
            rows.append((month, parse_rate(str(record[column]), month)))
    return rows


def log_changes(rows: Sequence[tuple[str, Fraction]]) -> list[tuple[str, float]]:
    """Return (month, ln(x_m / x_(m-1))) for every month after the first, in file order.

    The ratio is an exact Fraction, converted once to a float before the logarithm.
    Each change is dated by the later month.
    """
    return [(month, math.log(float(rate / previous))) for (_, previous), (month, rate) in itertools.pairwise(rows)]


def sample_sd(values: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """Sample standard deviation with denominator n - 1, in the units of the values.

    Raises ValueError with fewer than two values, because the n - 1 denominator is then zero.
    """
    n = len(values)
    if n < 2:
        raise ValueError("a sample standard deviation needs at least two values")
    mean = math.fsum(values) / n
    return math.sqrt(math.fsum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in values) / (n - 1))


def show(value: float, places: int) -> str:
    """Show a float with a fixed number of decimal places, rounded half away from zero, for display only."""
    return str(Decimal(value).quantize(Decimal(f"1e-{places}"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP))


def largest_move(changes: Sequence[tuple[str, float]]) -> tuple[str, float]:
    """Return the change with the largest absolute value; on a tie, the earliest month."""
    return max(changes, key=lambda item: abs(item[1]))


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to D in a fixed order."""
    # Part A: a SIMULATED model check, not data.
    model_rows = [(month_label(month_index("2000-01") + i), MODEL_RATE) for i in range(MODEL_MONTHS)]
    model = log_changes(model_rows)
    model_values = [c for _, c in model]
    print(
        "Part A: SIMULATED model check, not data: a fixed exchange rate held at "
        f"{show(float(MODEL_RATE), 4)} dollars per pound"
    )
    print(f"model monthly log changes: {len(model)}; all exactly zero: {all(c == 0.0 for c in model_values)}")
    print(f"model sample standard deviation: {show(sample_sd(model_values), 6)}")

    # Part B: the embedded column.
    rows = load_column(DATA_PATH, COLUMN)
    changes = log_changes(rows)
    zero = [month for (_, previous), (month, rate) in itertools.pairwise(rows) if rate == previous]
    print(f"Part B: {DATA_PATH.name}, column {COLUMN} (USD per pound sterling), exchange rates")
    print(f"months read: {len(rows)}, from {rows[0][0]} to {rows[-1][0]}; cells read as decimal rates: {len(rows)}")
    print(f"monthly log changes: {len(changes)}, dated {changes[0][0]} to {changes[-1][0]}")
    print(f"months with an exactly zero change: {len(zero)}: {', '.join(zero)}")

    # Part C: the two windows.
    before_window_two = month_label(month_index(WINDOW_TWO_FROM) - 1)
    rate_before_two = dict(rows)[before_window_two]
    name_one = "window one (1971-02 to 2026-09)"
    name_two = f"window two ({WINDOW_TWO_FROM} to 2026-09)"
    windows = {
        name_one: (changes, rows[0][1]),
        name_two: ([item for item in changes if item[0] >= WINDOW_TWO_FROM], rate_before_two),
    }
    print("Part C: dispersion of monthly log changes, sample standard deviation with n - 1")
    sds: dict[str, float] = {}
    for name, (window, start_rate) in windows.items():
        values = [c for _, c in window]
        sd = sample_sd(values)
        sds[name] = sd
        biggest_month, biggest = largest_move(window)
        print(f"{name}: changes {len(values)}; standard deviation {show(sd, 6)}")
        print(f"  largest absolute change {show(abs(biggest), 6)} at {biggest_month} (signed {show(biggest, 6)})")
        last_rate = rows[-1][1]
        identity = abs(math.fsum(values) - math.log(float(last_rate / start_rate))) <= SUM_TOLERANCE
        print(f"  sum of the changes equals ln(last rate / rate before the window) within 1e-12: {identity}")

    # Part D: the difference between the windows.
    one, two = sds[name_one], sds[name_two]
    print("Part D: window two minus window one")
    print(f"difference in standard deviation: {show(two - one, 6)}")
    print(f"ratio of window two to window one standard deviation, four decimals: {show(two / one, 4)}")

4Where the canon breaks

Proposition 2.2 holds in the model whatever the data show. The comparison in Lab 1 is between that model and one recorded column, and the following limits apply to it.

  • One foreign currency, one anchor. The model has one foreign currency. Lab 1 reads one column. The dispersion of one column does not describe how several currencies move together, and no statement here covers a second currency.
  • Exchange rates only. The snapshot holds exchange rates and no interest-rate column. The course's data register note for fx_monthly states that the series is spot-only, so each value is a spot rate (a rate for exchange at once) and the series holds no forward rate (a rate for exchange at a later date). The lab cannot test any relation between interest rates and exchange rates, because the file has no interest-rate column, and this lesson states none.
  • Monthly sampling. Each value is the column's last value in its month, not the monthly average that the Board publishes in its own monthly series (the data register note for fx_monthly states this). A monthly dispersion does not show the path inside a month, and the lab computes no daily figure.
  • The window start is a convention. Window two starts where the other embedded snapshots begin. A different start can give a different dispersion, and the lab computes no other start. The lab does not test whether the difference between the two windows survives another start.
  • No lender of last resort. Proposition 2.2 contains no central bank. A lender of last resort lends against collateral to institutions that are solvent but illiquid. The model has no such lender and no lending, so nothing in this lesson measures a lender's effect on any rate.
  • The historical arrangement. The lesson states no fact about the fixed exchange rates, participants, dates or end of the Bretton Woods arrangement. The course's reference list has no source for them yet. Any dated account of the system's end needs a source that is admitted to that list first.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A currency with a fixed exchange rate has no monthly change in its recorded rate.”

    Proposition 2.2 gives zero change under (A2), and (A2) describes the model, not a recorded series. Lab 1, Part B, counts three exactly zero changes among 668 in window one, so 665 changes are nonzero in the recorded GBP column.

  2. “A smaller dispersion in window two shows that the currency became calmer because of a regime change.”

    Part D prints a difference of -0.003236 between two sample standard deviations of one column. The lab computes no test and attaches no inference to the difference. The window start is a convention, and the lab names no cause for any change.

  3. “The monthly log change and the simple percent change are the same number.”

    For a rise from 1.00 to 1.10, the simple change is 0.10 exactly, and the log change is \(\ln 1.1 = 0.095310\) to six decimals. The two differ by 0.004690 (Problem M1 gives the calculation).

  4. “The fixed-exchange-rate model describes what the historical Bretton Woods arrangement was.”

    Definition 2.1 defines a model with an anchor currency, a fixed exchange rate and a market rate. This lesson states no historical fact about the arrangement's fixed exchange rates, participants or end, and Section 4 lists what is still open.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · A log change and a simple change

A rate moves from 1.2500 to 1.3750 dollars per pound in one month. Compute the monthly log change and the simple percent change, each to six decimals, and state their difference.

Solution

The ratio is \(1.3750 / 1.2500 = 1.1\). The log change is \(\ell = \ln 1.1 = 0.0953101798\ldots\), which is 0.095310 to six decimals. The simple change is \(1.1 - 1 = 0.100000\) exactly. The difference, computed from the unrounded values, is \(0.1 - 0.0953101798\ldots = 0.0046898\ldots\), which is 0.004690 to six decimals.

M2 · The dispersion of three changes

A window holds three monthly log changes, 0.01, 0.03 and -0.02. Compute their mean and their dispersion with denominator \(n-1\), each to six decimals.

Solution

The mean is \(0.02 / 3 = 1/150 = 0.006667\) to six decimals. The sum of squares of the changes is \(0.0001 + 0.0009 + 0.0004 = 0.0014\). The sum of squared deviations is \(0.0014 - 3 (1/150)^{2} = 0.0014 - 0.000133\ldots = 19/15000\). With \(n - 1 = 2\), the dispersion is \(\sqrt{19/30000} = 0.025166\) to six decimals.

M3 · Counting the changes in a window

How many monthly log changes does a window from 2000-01 to 2026-09 contain, and which rate does its first change use?

Solution

Each month from 2000-01 to 2026-09 carries one change, dated by that month. The months from 2000-01 to 2025-12 are 26 full years, which is \(26 \times 12 = 312\) months. The months from 2026-01 to 2026-09 are 9 more, so the window has \(312 + 9 = 321\) changes. The first change is dated 2000-01 and uses the rate of 1999-12, the month before the window. Lab 1, Part C, prints 321 changes for window two.

Proof

P1 · Telescoping by induction

Prove Proposition 1.2 for a fixed starting month \(a\) by induction on \(b\), and state the step where (A1) is used.

Solution

Base case \(b = a + 1\): the sum has one term, \(\ell_{a+1} = \ln x_{a+1} - \ln x_a = \ln(x_{a+1} / x_a)\). Inductive step: assume \(\sum_{t=a+1}^{b} \ell_t = \ln(x_b / x_a)\) for some \(b \ge a + 1\). Adding \(\ell_{b+1} = \ln x_{b+1} - \ln x_b\) gives \(\ln x_b - \ln x_a + \ln x_{b+1} - \ln x_b = \ln(x_{b+1} / x_a)\), since the \(\ln x_b\) terms cancel. Assumption (A1) is used at every step, because the logarithms of \(x_t\) exist only for positive rates.

P2 · A band bound on the log change

Assume (A3): for some \(\bar{x} > 0\) and some \(h\) with \(0 \le h < 1\), the rate satisfies \(\bar{x}(1-h) \le x_t \le \bar{x}(1+h)\) at every month \(t\) from the month before the window's first change to its last change. Prove that \(\lvert \ell_t \rvert \le \ln\frac{1+h}{1-h}\) for each change in the window, and state the bound when \(h = 0\).

Solution

Take a change dated \(t\). Upper bound on the ratio: \(x_t \le \bar{x}(1+h)\) and \(x_{t-1} \ge \bar{x}(1-h) > 0\), since \(h < 1\). So \(x_t / x_{t-1} \le (1+h)/(1-h)\). Lower bound: \(x_t \ge \bar{x}(1-h)\) and \(x_{t-1} \le \bar{x}(1+h)\), so \(x_t / x_{t-1} \ge (1-h)/(1+h)\). The logarithm is increasing, so \(\ln\frac{1-h}{1+h} \le \ell_t \le \ln\frac{1+h}{1-h}\). Since \(\ln\frac{1-h}{1+h} = -\ln\frac{1+h}{1-h}\), this is \(\lvert \ell_t \rvert \le \ln\frac{1+h}{1-h}\). When \(h = 0\) the bound is \(\ln 1 = 0\), which is the conclusion of Proposition 2.2.

P3 · The log change is at most the simple change

Prove that \(\ln(1+u) \le u\) for every \(u > -1\), with equality only at \(u = 0\). Deduce that \(\ell_t \le x_t / x_{t-1} - 1\) for every positive pair of consecutive rates.

Solution

Let \(f(u) = \ln(1+u) - u\) on \((-1, \infty)\). Then \(f(0) = 0\) and \(f'(u) = \frac{1}{1+u} - 1 = -\frac{u}{1+u}\). On \((-1, 0)\) the derivative is positive, so \(f\) is strictly increasing there, and \(f(u) < f(0) = 0\). On \((0, \infty)\) the derivative is negative, so \(f\) is strictly decreasing there, and \(f(u) < f(0) = 0\). Hence \(f(u) < 0\) for \(u \ne 0\) and \(f(0) = 0\). For the deduction, put \(u = x_t / x_{t-1} - 1 > -1\), so that \(\ell_t = \ln(1+u) \le u\).

Research

R1 · A test and its assumptions

State a test of whether the dispersion of window two differs from that of window one. Name the statistic, name every assumption it needs, and say which of those assumptions Lab 1 checks.

Solution

A natural statistic is the ratio of the two sample variances, \(s_2^{2} / s_1^{2}\), with the window-two variance in the numerator. A null distribution for this ratio depends on assumptions about the changes, such as their distribution and their independence across months. This lesson does not derive that null distribution, and no p-value is reported here. Lab 1 checks none of these assumptions. It checks the telescoping identity, and Part A's exact-zero result; Part B only counts the exact zeros. A test that reports a p-value needs the null distribution derived or cited from a source admitted to the reference list first.

R2 · The band implied by the largest move

Window one has a largest absolute monthly log change of 0.135846 (Lab 1, Part C). Under (A3), find the smallest half-width \(h\) for which the bound of Problem P2 allows a change of that size. Give \(h\) in closed form and evaluate it to four decimals.

Solution

Set \(\ln\frac{1+h}{1-h} = z\), where \(z = 0.135846\ldots\) is the unrounded change. Then \(\frac{1+h}{1-h} = e^{z}\), so \(1 + h = e^{z}(1 - h)\), which gives \(h = \frac{e^{z}-1}{e^{z}+1} = \tanh\frac{z}{2}\). The function \(h \mapsto \ln\frac{1+h}{1-h}\) is strictly increasing on \([0, 1)\), because its derivative is \(\frac{2}{1-h^{2}} > 0\), so this \(h\) is the smallest one whose bound reaches \(z\). Evaluating, \(\tanh(0.0679228\ldots) = 0.067819\ldots\), so the exact value is 0.067819 to six decimals. Rounded to four decimals it is 0.0678, which lies slightly below the exact value, so a band of half-width 0.0678 gives a bound of 0.135808 and does not quite reach 0.135846. The value is derived here and is not printed by the lab.

R3 · Why the window start is a convention

Explain why the start of window two is a convention, and list what a finding about the difference between the windows would need before it could be stated.

Solution

The start 2000-01 is the first month from which the other embedded snapshots have data. That reason concerns the data files, not the GBP rate, so the choice of 2000-01 is a convention and not a property of the rate. A finding about the difference would need four things. It needs a stated test of the difference, which Problem E3 shows the lab does not supply. It needs a sensitivity check over several starts, because the lab computes one start and so does not show whether the printed difference changes with it. It needs a source for any cause of a difference. And it needs the claim limited to this column, because a second column is a second sample. This solution states no number for a new start.
