In the model below, a fall in the price level raises the real value of a fixed nominal debt, and that rise weakens the borrower's balance sheet and raises the external-finance premium on further credit (Definition 2.1). This lesson states that chain as a model, with three assumptions, and proves its closed form. The model has no data behind it and describes no historical economy. The lesson also defines the lender of last resort (Definition 4.1), which the model does not contain. Lab 1 prints the output depth (Assumption 1.3) of two model economies, one on parity for longer than the other. Its ordering follows from Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3 (Corollary 3.3) and is not evidence about any economy.

1A stylised model of parity, debt and output

The model has one good, one borrower and a nominal debt. It includes a fixed-parity regime, the gold standard of Lesson 2 in its simplest form. A period on parity is a period under that regime, and in the model the regime lowers the price index while it lasts, by assumption. The regime is an assumption of the model and not a description of any actual regime. Section 3 proves its results under the three assumptions below. Sections 2 and 4 give definitions.

Assumption 1.1 (parity and deflation, A1)

Let \(0 < \delta < 1\) be the fraction by which the price index falls in each period on parity. Let \(H \ge 1\) be an integer horizon and let \(m\) be an integer with \(0 \le m \le H\), the number of periods on parity. Set \(P_0 = 1\) and

\[ P_t = (1 - \delta)^{\min(t, m)}, \qquad t = 0, 1, \ldots, H. \]

The economy deflates in periods 1 to \(m\) and its price index is flat in the periods after \(m\).

Assumption 1.2 (fixed nominal debt, net worth and no default, A2)

The nominal debt \(D > 0\) is a whole number of cents, fixed for the whole horizon. Its real value is \(V_t = D / P_t\), so \(V_0 = D\). Borrower net worth is \(N_t = N_0 - (V_t - V_0)\), where \(N_0 > 0\) is the baseline net worth in cents. The whole rise in the real debt falls on net worth. There is no default, no repricing of assets and no other change to the balance sheet. Assume

\[ V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m} < N_0 + D. \]

Proposition 3.2 (Section 3) shows that \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\), so this one inequality keeps \(N_t > 0\) for every period.

Assumption 1.3 (premium and output, A3)

The slope \(\varphi > 0\) and the output coefficient \(\beta > 0\) are fixed. Leverage (Definition 2.1) is \(L_t = V_t / N_t\), with baseline \(L_0 = D / N_0\). The external-finance premium (Definition 2.1) above its baseline is \(\psi_t = \varphi\,(L_t - L_0)\), and the fall in output as a fraction of baseline output is \(f_t = \beta\,\psi_t\). The depth for parity duration \(m\) is

\[ \operatorname{depth}(m) = \max_{0 \le t \le H} f_t. \]

Honest-pending note. The course's curriculum names embedded data for this lab. No redistributable historical series for gold-standard adherence or for output depth is embedded, and the lab reads no historical series. The lab therefore runs the stylised model above and is labelled SIMULATED. Its numbers are the model's, and no claim about any historical economy follows from them. The lab stays SIMULATED until a redistributable series for these quantities is embedded.

2Definitions

Definition 2.1 (leverage and the external-finance premium)

Under Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, the leverage at period \(t\) is \(L_t = V_t / N_t\), the ratio of the real debt to the borrower's net worth. The external-finance premium is the extra cost a borrower pays for outside funds over the cost of its own funds. In the model it is the rise in that extra cost above its baseline, \(\psi_t\).

Definition 2.2 (credit channel of a depression, in the model)

In the model of Section 1, the credit channel of a depression is the following chain. A fall in the price index raises the real value of the fixed nominal debt (A1 and A2). The rise in the real value lowers borrower net worth one for one (A2). The rise in leverage raises the external-finance premium (A3). The premium lowers output by \(f_t\) as a fraction of baseline output (A3). The chain describes the model. It makes no claim about any economy outside it.

The first step of the chain is the real-debt mechanism of Lesson 2, the debt deflation defined there. The model adds the balance-sheet and premium steps. Its balance sheet is the borrower's. The model has no intermediary whose net worth is modelled, so a claim about banks is outside it.

3Closed form, ordering and the ratio of depths

Lemma 3.1 (leverage is strictly increasing in real debt)

Assume Assumption 1.2, so that \(N_0 > 0\). Let \(C = N_0 + D\), so \(C > 0\), and, for real \(x\) with \(D \le x < C\), let \(\lambda(x) = x / (C - x)\). Then \(\lambda\) is strictly increasing on \([D, C)\). Under Assumptions 1.2 and 1.3, \(L_t = \lambda(V_t)\) whenever \(V_t < C\), and \(L_0 = \lambda(D) = D / N_0\).

Proof

Let \(D \le x < y < C\). Then both \(C - x\) and \(C - y\) are positive, and

\[ \lambda(y) - \lambda(x) = \frac{y}{C - y} - \frac{x}{C - x} = \frac{y(C - x) - x(C - y)}{(C - x)(C - y)} = \frac{C\,(y - x)}{(C - x)(C - y)} > 0. \]

Since \(N_t = N_0 - (V_t - D) = C - V_t\), the leverage is \(L_t = V_t / (C - V_t) = \lambda(V_t)\) whenever \(N_t > 0\). At \(t = 0\), \(V_0 = D\) and \(C - D = N_0\), so \(L_0 = D / N_0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3.2 (closed form of the depth)

Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3. Let \(V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m}\). Then

(a) \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\) with \(0 \le t \le H\). For \(t \le m\), \(V_t = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-t}\), which is strictly increasing when \(m \ge 1\). For \(t \ge m\), \(V_t = V_m\).

(b) \(\operatorname{depth}(m) = \beta\varphi\,\bigl(\lambda(V_m) - \lambda(D)\bigr) = \beta\varphi\Bigl(\dfrac{V_m}{N_0 + D - V_m} - \dfrac{D}{N_0}\Bigr)\).

(c) The maximum defining the depth is first attained at \(t = m\). In particular \(\operatorname{depth}(0) = 0\).

Proof

(a) For \(t \le m\), Assumption 1.1 gives \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^{t}\), so \(V_t = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-t}\). Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\), the factor \((1 - \delta)^{-1}\) exceeds 1, so \(t \mapsto V_t\) is strictly increasing on \(0, \ldots, m\) when \(m \ge 1\). For \(t \ge m\), \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^{m}\) and \(V_t = V_m\). Together these give \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\).

(b) By Assumption 1.2, \(V_m < C\), so \(V_t \le V_m < C\) for every \(t\). Lemma 3.1 then gives \(L_t = \lambda(V_t)\) for every \(t\), and \(f_t = \beta\varphi\,(\lambda(V_t) - \lambda(D))\). Because \(\lambda\) is strictly increasing on \([D, C)\), \(\lambda(V_t) \le \lambda(V_m)\) for every \(t\), and \(\beta\varphi > 0\), so \(f_t \le f_m\) for every \(t\). Since \(m\) is one of the periods \(0, \ldots, H\), the maximum equals \(f_m\), which is the displayed formula.

(c) If \(m = 0\), then \(V_t = D\) for every \(t\) by (a), so \(f_t = 0\) for every \(t\) and the first maximising period is \(t = 0 = m\). If \(m \ge 1\), then for every \(t < m\), (a) gives \(V_t < V_m\), so \(f_t < f_m\) by the strict monotonicity of \(\lambda\). No period before \(m\) attains the maximum, and \(t = m\) does. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3.2 is an identity about the model. Part (b) is the closed form of the depth. At \(t = m\) it equals the output fall that Lab 1 computes from the price path, which is the maximum, and the lab does not evaluate part (b) itself. Part (c) says where the depth occurs. Under Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3 the depth rises with the number of periods on parity, which is the next result.

Corollary 3.3 (in the model, a longer parity duration gives a deeper fall)

Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 for two parity durations \(m'\) and \(m\) with \(0 \le m' < m \le H\). Then \(\operatorname{depth}(m') < \operatorname{depth}(m)\). Economy X in Lab 1 has \(m = 12\) and economy Y has \(m' = 4\), so under these assumptions X is deeper than Y.

Proof

Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\) and \(m' < m\), we have \(V_{m'} = D(1 - \delta)^{-m'} < D(1 - \delta)^{-m} = V_m\). Also \(V_m < C\) by Assumption 1.2, so \(D \le V_{m'} < V_m < C\). Lemma 3.1 gives \(\lambda(V_{m'}) < \lambda(V_m)\). Proposition 3.2(b) applies to both durations with the same \(\beta\varphi > 0\), so \(\operatorname{depth}(m') = \beta\varphi\,(\lambda(V_{m'}) - \lambda(D)) < \beta\varphi\,(\lambda(V_m) - \lambda(D)) = \operatorname{depth}(m)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 3.4 (the ratio of depths does not depend on the product \(\beta\varphi\))

Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 for two parity durations \(m'\) and \(m\) with \(1 \le m' < m \le H\). Then

\[ \frac{\operatorname{depth}(m)}{\operatorname{depth}(m')} = \frac{\lambda(V_m) - \lambda(D)}{\lambda(V_{m'}) - \lambda(D)} \]

and the right-hand side does not depend on \(\beta\) or \(\varphi\).

Proof

Since \(m' \ge 1\), \(V_{m'} > D\), so \(\lambda(V_{m'}) - \lambda(D) > 0\) by Lemma 3.1, and \(\operatorname{depth}(m') > 0\) by Proposition 3.2(b). Divide the two formulas of Proposition 3.2(b). The common factor \(\beta\varphi\) cancels, which gives the displayed ratio. \(\blacksquare\)

Lab 1 computes each depth from the model's price path on the parameters of Part A. Part B prints whether X is deeper than Y. Part C prints the depth for each parity duration from 0 to 12, and prints whether the depth never falls as the duration rises and whether it rises at every step, which are the adjacent-duration orderings of Corollary 3.3. Part D prints the ratio of the two depths for three products of the slope and the output coefficient, and makes no equality test of those three ratios.

Lab 1 · SIMULATED · The two-economy model: paths, depths and the ratio of depths

The hypothesis, stated from Proposition 3.2 before the lab is run. With \(D = 1{,}000{,}000\) cents, \(N_0 = 4{,}000{,}000\) cents, a deflation of \(1/50\) per period on parity, \(H = 12\), \(\varphi = \beta = 1\), economy X on parity for 12 periods and economy Y for 4 periods, the depth of X is reached at \(t = 12\) and the depth of Y at \(t = 4\). The printed depths are 9.20 and 2.69 percent of baseline output, rounded half up from the exact values. Corollary 3.3 predicts that X is deeper and that the depth never falls as the parity duration rises. Corollary 3.4 predicts that the ratio of the two depths is the same for every product \(\beta\varphi\), and Part D prints that ratio. Every value is exact and is rounded once, only when printed.

"""LX1 L03 lab 1: a stylised two-economy model of parity, real debt, the external-finance premium and output depth.

SIMULATED. Every number comes from a deterministic model with the parameters printed in Part A. The model uses no data
and describes no historical economy. Economy X keeps its parity for the whole horizon. Economy Y leaves parity after a
stated number of periods. The printed depths are the model's own values, so they answer a question about the
assumptions and not about any country.

Units and conventions. The nominal debt D is fixed in integer cents. The price index P_t is dimensionless with P_0 = 1,
so the real value of the debt, V_t = D / P_t, is in cents. Leverage L_t = V_t / N_t is a dimensionless ratio. The
premium psi_t and the output fall f_t are dimensionless fractions; a percentage is a fraction times 100. Every quantity
is an exact Fraction. Each value is rounded once, half up, only when it is printed.

Model, for t = 0, 1, ..., H with H = 12 periods:
    P_t = (1 - delta) ** min(t, m)     the price index: it falls by the fraction delta in each period on parity,
                                       and is flat after the economy leaves parity; m is the number of periods on parity
    V_t = D / P_t                      the real value of the fixed nominal debt, in cents
    N_t = N_0 - (V_t - V_0)            borrower net worth, in cents: it falls one for one with the rise in V_t
    L_t = V_t / N_t                    leverage, a dimensionless ratio
    psi_t = phi * (L_t - L_0)          the external-finance premium above its baseline, a dimensionless fraction
    f_t = beta * psi_t                the fall in output, as a fraction of baseline output
    depth(m) = max over t of f_t       the output depth for a parity duration m

Part A prints the parameters and the two paths. Part B prints the depth of each economy, the period where it is first
reached, whether X is deeper than Y, and the difference of the two depths. Part C prints the depth for every parity
duration m from 0 to H, and checks that it never falls as m rises and that it rises at every step from m to m + 1.
Part D prints the ratio of the two depths for three values of the product beta * phi.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

DEBT_CENTS = 1_000_000
NET_WORTH_CENTS = 4_000_000
DELTA = Fraction(1, 50)
HORIZON = 12
PARITY_X = 12
PARITY_Y = 4
PHI = Fraction(1)
BETA = Fraction(1)


def round_half_up(amount: Fraction) -> int:
    """Round a Fraction to a whole number, with a tie going toward plus infinity."""
    return math.floor(amount + Fraction(1, 2))


def fixed_decimal(amount: Fraction, places: int) -> str:
    """Show a non-negative Fraction with a fixed number of decimal places, rounded half up, for display only.

    Raises ValueError for a negative amount, because the display rule here is written for magnitudes only.
    """
    if amount < 0:
        raise ValueError("fixed_decimal shows non-negative amounts only")
    scaled = round_half_up(amount * 10**places)
    whole, frac = divmod(scaled, 10**places)
    return f"{whole}.{frac:0{places}d}"


def price_index(parity_periods: int, t: int) -> Fraction:
    """Price index P_t = (1 - delta) ** min(t, m), with P_0 = 1.

    Raises ValueError when t is negative or the parity duration m is outside 0 to HORIZON.
    """
    if t < 0:
        raise ValueError("the period t must be non-negative")
    if not 0 <= parity_periods <= HORIZON:
        raise ValueError("the parity duration must be between 0 and HORIZON periods")
    return (1 - DELTA) ** min(t, parity_periods)


def real_debt(parity_periods: int, t: int) -> Fraction:
    """Real value of the fixed nominal debt at period t, in cents: V_t = D / P_t."""
    return Fraction(DEBT_CENTS) / price_index(parity_periods, t)


def leverage(real: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Leverage L = V / N for a real debt V in cents, with N = N_0 - (V - V_0) the borrower's net worth in cents.

    Raises ValueError when the net worth is not positive, because the leverage ratio is then undefined.
    """
    net_worth = Fraction(NET_WORTH_CENTS) - (real - Fraction(DEBT_CENTS))
    if net_worth <= 0:
        raise ValueError("borrower net worth must stay positive")
    return real / net_worth


def baseline_leverage() -> Fraction:
    """Leverage at t = 0, where V_0 = D and N_0 is the baseline net worth."""
    return leverage(Fraction(DEBT_CENTS))


def premium(parity_periods: int, t: int, phi: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """External-finance premium above its baseline: psi_t = phi * (L_t - L_0)."""
    return phi * (leverage(real_debt(parity_periods, t)) - baseline_leverage())


def output_fall(parity_periods: int, t: int, phi: Fraction, beta: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Fall in output as a fraction of baseline output: f_t = beta * psi_t."""
    return beta * premium(parity_periods, t, phi)


def depth_path(parity_periods: int, phi: Fraction, beta: Fraction) -> list[Fraction]:
    """The output fall f_0, ..., f_H for a parity duration m."""
    return [output_fall(parity_periods, t, phi, beta) for t in range(HORIZON + 1)]


def depth(parity_periods: int, phi: Fraction, beta: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Output depth: the largest output fall over the horizon, a fraction of baseline output."""
    return max(depth_path(parity_periods, phi, beta))


def first_period_at_depth(parity_periods: int, phi: Fraction, beta: Fraction) -> int:
    """The earliest period t at which the output fall reaches its depth."""
    path = depth_path(parity_periods, phi, beta)
    top = max(path)
    return next(t for t, value in enumerate(path) if value == top)


def print_economy(label: str, parity_periods: int) -> None:
    """Print the price index, real debt, leverage, premium and output fall for each period."""
    print(f"economy {label}, parity held for {parity_periods} of {HORIZON} periods:")
    for t in range(HORIZON + 1):
        price = price_index(parity_periods, t)
        real = real_debt(parity_periods, t)
        lev = leverage(real)
        psi = premium(parity_periods, t, PHI)
        fall = output_fall(parity_periods, t, PHI, BETA)
        print(
            f"  t = {t:2d}: index {fixed_decimal(price, 4)}; real debt {round_half_up(real)} cents; "
            f"leverage {fixed_decimal(lev, 4)}; premium {fixed_decimal(psi, 4)}; "
            f"output fall {fixed_decimal(100 * fall, 2)} percent"
        )


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to D in a fixed order."""
    print("Part A: SIMULATED model, parameters and paths")
    print(
        f"D = {DEBT_CENTS} cents; N_0 = {NET_WORTH_CENTS} cents; deflation per period on parity = 1/50; "
        f"H = {HORIZON} periods; phi = {PHI}; beta = {BETA}"
    )
    print(f"baseline leverage L_0 = V_0 / N_0 = {fixed_decimal(baseline_leverage(), 4)}")
    print_economy("X", PARITY_X)
    print_economy("Y", PARITY_Y)

    depth_x = depth(PARITY_X, PHI, BETA)
    depth_y = depth(PARITY_Y, PHI, BETA)
    print("Part B: output depth of each economy")
    print(
        f"economy X depth {fixed_decimal(100 * depth_x, 2)} percent of baseline output, "
        f"first reached at t = {first_period_at_depth(PARITY_X, PHI, BETA)}"
    )
    print(
        f"economy Y depth {fixed_decimal(100 * depth_y, 2)} percent of baseline output, "
        f"first reached at t = {first_period_at_depth(PARITY_Y, PHI, BETA)}"
    )
    print(f"X is deeper than Y: {depth_x > depth_y}")
    print(f"difference X minus Y: {fixed_decimal(100 * (depth_x - depth_y), 2)} percentage points")

    depths = [depth(m, PHI, BETA) for m in range(HORIZON + 1)]
    print("Part C: depth by parity duration m, in percent of baseline output")
    for m, value in enumerate(depths):
        print(f"m = {m:2d}: depth {fixed_decimal(100 * value, 2)} percent")
    never_falls = all(depths[m] <= depths[m + 1] for m in range(HORIZON))
    strict_steps = all(depths[m] < depths[m + 1] for m in range(HORIZON))
    print(f"depth never falls as the parity duration rises: {never_falls}")
    print(f"depth rises at every step from m to m + 1 for m = 0 to {HORIZON - 1}: {strict_steps}")

    print("Part D: the ratio of the two depths for three values of the product beta * phi")
    for phi, beta in ((Fraction(1, 2), Fraction(1)), (Fraction(1), Fraction(1)), (Fraction(3), Fraction(1))):
        ratio = depth(PARITY_X, phi, beta) / depth(PARITY_Y, phi, beta)
        print(f"phi = {phi}, beta = {beta}, product {phi * beta}: ratio of depths X to Y {fixed_decimal(ratio, 4)}")


main()

4The lender of last resort

Definition 4.1 (lender of last resort)

A central bank is the public institution that issues the currency. A lender of last resort is a central bank that lends against collateral to institutions that are solvent but illiquid. Solvent means that the institution's assets, valued as the lender values them, cover its liabilities. Illiquid means that the institution cannot meet its payments from its own resources in the time available.

Remark 4.2 (the model has no lender of last resort)

Assumption 1.2 has no term for lending, and Assumption 1.3 has no term through which a lending facility changes the premium of a solvent borrower. Proposition 3.2 therefore says nothing about what such a facility would do. This lesson makes no claim about its effect. The model's net-worth condition \(N_t > 0\) (Assumption 1.2) is a solvency condition in the balance-sheet sense. Assumption 1.2 assumes \(V_m < N_0 + D\), and Proposition 3.2(a) shows that \(V_t \le V_m\) in every period, so \(V_t < N_0 + D\) and \(N_t > 0\) in every period. What the model lacks is any illiquidity, collateral or lending term, so the lender's role in Definition 4.1 has no counterpart.

5Where the canon breaks

The model is exact inside its assumptions and silent outside them. Four places where the reader must act:

  • One good, no default, no fiscal response. Assumption 1.2 sets the change in net worth equal to minus the change in the real debt. A default, a bailout or a change in the value of the assets would change that identity, and Proposition 3.2 would no longer apply.
  • The parity duration is an input. Assumption 1.1 fixes \(m\) for each economy. In the model, when an economy leaves parity is a choice of the modeller. The lesson gives no rule for that choice, and the ordering in Corollary 3.3 depends on the durations being different.
  • The functional form is a choice. The premium is linear in leverage with slope \(\varphi\), and the output fall is linear in the premium. Corollary 3.3 is proved for the linear premium, and Problem R2 shows that the ordering needs only that the premium is strictly increasing in the real debt. Corollary 3.4's ratio depends on the shape of the premium, not on its scale. A different shape can change the ratio: if the premium is \(\varphi\) times the square of \((L_t - L_0)\), each depth is \(\beta\varphi\) times the square of the linear bracket in Corollary 3.4, so the ratio is the square of the ratio of Corollary 3.4, which differs from it whenever that ratio is not 1. A change of slope cannot change the ratio.
  • The credit channel is not measured here. The model makes no empirical claim about the credit channel and no claim about its size. This lesson cites no source for an empirical credit-channel claim, so a reader who wants one needs a source with a locator, and a counter-account, which this lesson does not supply. The course's reference list holds the monetary history cited in Section 10. This lesson attributes no account to it, because no passage of that book has been read for this page, and it does not identify the model's chain with any account in it.

6Misconceptions

  1. “The model shows that bank failures caused the depth.”

    The model has no bank and no failure. Assumption 1.2 excludes default and any other change to the balance sheet. Proposition 3.2 is proved from Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, and the proof uses none of these events. A failure would change the balance-sheet identity of Assumption 1.2, so Proposition 3.2 would not apply to it.

  2. “Economy X's larger depth shows that holding parity makes output fall further.”

    The ordering follows from the parity durations of 12 and 4 that the lab sets under Assumption 1.1. The proof of Corollary 3.3 uses those durations, the closed form \(V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m}\) from Assumption 1.1, the bound \(V_m < N_0 + D\) of Assumption 1.2, Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2(b), and none of the bank failures or defaults that the first misconception rules out. The model cannot say whether holding parity causes a deeper fall. Lab 1 prints the ordering the assumptions imply.

  3. “A lender of last resort lends to any firm that asks, so panics end.”

    Definition 4.1 requires collateral, and it limits the lending to institutions that are solvent but illiquid. A lender that lent to insolvent firms would not fit the definition. Remark 4.2 adds that the model contains no such facility, so no panic result follows from it.

  4. “The 9.20 percent depth in Lab 1 is a measured fall in output.”

    Lab 1 is SIMULATED. Its depth is the model's value under Assumption 1.3 with \(\beta = 1\), and no output series enters the lab. The printed figure is a property of the model's parameters, and changing \(\beta\) changes it in proportion, as the factor \(\beta\varphi\) in Proposition 3.2(b) shows.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The real debt in period 4 of economy Y

Under Assumption 1.1 with \(m = 4\), find \(P_4\) exactly, and the real debt \(V_4\) in whole cents. State the rounding rule.

Solution

Since \(m = 4\), \(P_4 = (49/50)^4 = 5\,764\,801 / 6\,250\,000 = 0.92236816\) exactly. Then \(V_4 = 1\,000\,000 / 0.92236816 = 1\,084\,165.7847\ldots\) cents. Rounding half up to a whole cent gives 1,084,166 cents, the value printed in Lab 1, Part A.

M2 · Net worth in period 4 of economy Y

Using the exact value of \(V_4\) from M1, find \(N_4\) in whole cents, from Assumption 1.2.

Solution

\(N_4 = N_0 - (V_4 - D) = 4\,000\,000 - (1\,084\,165.7847 - 1\,000\,000) = 3\,915\,834.2153\) cents, which rounds to 3,915,834 cents. Computing \(N_0 - (V_4 - D)\) with the rounded \(V_4\) gives the same whole number here: \(4\,000\,000 - (1\,084\,166 - 1\,000\,000) = 3\,915\,834\), so the rounding order does not change the answer in this case.

M3 · Leverage in period 4 of economy Y

Find \(L_4 = V_4 / N_4\) from the exact values, to four decimal places. Check the printed value of Lab 1, Part A.

Solution

From the exact values, \(L_4 = 1\,084\,165.7847 / 3\,915\,834.2153 = 0.2768671\ldots\), which rounds to 0.2769 half up. Lab 1, Part A prints leverage 0.2769 for economy Y at \(t = 4\). Rounding \(V_4\) and \(N_4\) first gives the same four decimals here.

Proof

P1 · Monotone prices and real debt

Under Assumption 1.1, show that \(P_t\) is non-increasing in \(t\) and that \(V_t\) is non-decreasing in \(t\).

Solution

Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\), the map \(k \mapsto (1 - \delta)^{k}\) is strictly decreasing on the integers. The exponent \(\min(t, m)\) is non-decreasing in \(t\), so \(P_t\) is non-increasing in \(t\). Because \(D > 0\), \(V_t = D / P_t\) is then non-decreasing in \(t\). This is Proposition 3.2(a) with the same argument.

P2 · No depth without a parity duration

Show that \(\operatorname{depth}(0) = 0\) for every \(\varphi > 0\) and \(\beta > 0\).

Solution

If \(m = 0\), then \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^0 = 1\) for every \(t\), so \(V_t = D\) and \(N_t = N_0\). Hence \(L_t = L_0\) and \(\psi_t = \varphi(L_t - L_0) = 0\), and \(f_t = \beta \cdot 0 = 0\) for every \(t\). The maximum of a constant zero sequence is zero.

P3 · The strict step in Lemma 3.1

Prove that \(\lambda(y) - \lambda(x) = C(y - x) / ((C - x)(C - y))\) for \(x, y\) with \(x, y < C\), and deduce Lemma 3.1 for \(D \le x < y < C\).

Solution

Put the two fractions over the common denominator: \(\frac{y}{C-y} - \frac{x}{C-x} = \frac{y(C-x) - x(C-y)}{(C-x)(C-y)}\). The numerator expands to \(yC - xy - xC + xy = C(y - x)\), which gives the identity. If \(D \le x < y < C\), then \(y - x > 0\), \(C - x > 0\) and \(C - y > 0\), so the difference is positive and \(\lambda\) is strictly increasing.

Empirical

E1 · The depth of economy Y

Using only the printed output of Lab 1, Part B, give the depth of economy Y and the period at which it is first reached.

Solution

Part B prints a depth of 2.69 percent of baseline output for economy Y, first reached at \(t = 4\). The exact depth is 2.6867 percent, which rounds to 2.69. The period is 4 because the premium rises until period 4 and is flat after it, so the first period at the maximum is \(t = 4\).

E2 · The difference of the two depths

Lab 1, Part B prints a difference of 6.52 percentage points between X and Y. Subtract the two printed depths, and say which figure is correct and why.

Solution

The printed depths give \(9.20 - 2.69 = 6.51\). The printed difference is 6.52, which is correct. Part B computes the difference from the unrounded depths: \(9.204597 - 2.686713 = 6.517884\), which rounds half up to 6.52. The difference of the rounded figures is 6.51 and is not the answer to the stated question.

E3 · Unchanged prices in economy Y

Using only the printed output of Lab 1, Part A for economy Y, count the periods in which the price index is the same as in the period before.

Solution

The index for economy Y is 0.9800, 0.9604, 0.9412 and 0.9224 for \(t = 1\) to 4, and then 0.9224 for every period from 5 to 12. The index is unchanged from \(t - 1\) to \(t\) for \(t = 5, \ldots, 12\), which is 8 periods.

Research

R1 · A lending facility in the model

Describe the change to the model that a lender of last resort would require, and name the assumption it extends or replaces.

Solution

A facility would have to act on the premium of a solvent borrower, and it would need a case in which some borrower is insolvent. Assumption 1.2 excludes that case, since its inequality keeps \(N_t > 0\) in every period (Remark 4.2). The extension would also need a term in Assumption 1.3 for the facility's effect on \(\psi_t\). Proposition 3.2 is proved for the model without that term, so its statements do not carry over to the extended model without a new proof. Lemma 3.1, a fact about the function \(\lambda\) alone, is unaffected.

R2 · The ordering under any strictly increasing premium

Under Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, let \(G\) be any strictly increasing function on \([D, C)\), and define \(\operatorname{depth}_G(m) = \beta \max_{0 \le t \le H} G(V_t)\). Show that \(\operatorname{depth}_G(m') < \operatorname{depth}_G(m)\) for \(0 \le m' < m \le H\).

Solution

By Proposition 3.2(a), the largest real debt on the horizon is \(V_m\), attained at \(t = m\), and the largest real debt for \(m'\) is \(V_{m'}\). Since \(G\) is strictly increasing, \(\max_t G(V_t) = G(V_m)\) for duration \(m\), and \(G(V_{m'})\) for \(m'\). Corollary 3.3's proof gives \(V_{m'} < V_m\), so \(G(V_{m'}) < G(V_m)\), and multiplying by \(\beta > 0\) gives the claim. The ordering needs only that the premium is a strictly increasing function of the real debt.

R3 · The data that a test of Corollary 3.3 would need

Name the series a test of Corollary 3.3 against an economy would need, and say which of them this course embeds.

Solution

A test needs a price index path with the dates on which an economy left parity, a measured external-finance premium, a measured output series with the baseline it is compared with, and a measure of borrower net worth or leverage. The course embeds none of these for this lesson. The test therefore cannot be run here, and the lab is a model check only.
