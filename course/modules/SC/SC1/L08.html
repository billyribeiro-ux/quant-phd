First-Passage Times

  • Introduces: first-passage time (defined in §1)

  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or lab result
  • Dlore

The first-passage time \(\tau_a\) is the first time a path reaches the level \(a\). Its distribution is the running-maximum tail of Lesson 7 read in time: \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=\Prob(M_t\ge a)\) on every continuous path, and the closed form \(\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{2t})\) under Assumptions A3 and A4. This lesson shows that \(\tau_a\) is finite with probability one. It does not evaluate \(\E[\tau_a]\). Lab 1 checks the walk version of the identity by enumeration, computes exact truncated means, and compares a seeded simulation with them.

1The first-passage time of Brownian motion

Let \(B\) be a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, as in Lesson 2. The closed forms below use Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5 through Lesson 7. Every result that uses them says so.

Definition 1.1 (first-passage time)

For \(a>0\), the first-passage time of \(B\) at \(a\) is \(\tau_a=\inf\{t\ge 0: B_t=a\}\), with \(\inf\varnothing=\infty\). Lesson 6 uses the same time under the same name.

Proposition 1.2 (running maximum and first passage on each path)
  1. (H1) For a fixed outcome, \(s\mapsto B_s\) is continuous and \(B_0=0\).
  2. (H2) \(a>0\) and \(t\ge 0\) are fixed.

Conclusion: \(\{\tau_a\le t\}=\{M_t\ge a\}\), where \(M_t=\max_{0\le s\le t}B_s\).

Proof

Suppose \(\tau_a\le t\). The set \(\{s: B_s=a\}\) is closed by continuity and nonempty, so it contains \(\tau_a\), and \(B_{\tau_a}=a\). Since \(\tau_a\in[0,t]\), \(M_t\ge B_{\tau_a}=a\). Conversely, suppose \(M_t\ge a\). The continuous function \(s\mapsto B_s\) attains its maximum on \([0,t]\) at some \(s^\ast\le t\), so \(B_{s^\ast}\ge a>0=B_0\). By the intermediate value theorem there is \(u\in(0,s^\ast]\) with \(B_u=a\). Then \(\tau_a\le u\le t\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 1.3 (distribution function of the first passage)

Under H1 and H2 of Proposition 1.2, and assuming A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, for \(t>0\), \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=\Prob(M_t\ge a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt t))=\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{2t})\).

Proof

The first equality is Proposition 1.2 and holds without any probability assumption. The second is Theorem 1.3 of Lesson 7, which assumes A3 and A4 through Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6. The erfc form is Corollary 2.6 of Lesson 6. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 1.4 (finite with probability one)

Under the assumptions of Corollary 1.3, \(\Prob(\tau_a<\infty)=1\).

Proof

The event \(\{\tau_a<\infty\}\) is the union of the increasing events \(\{\tau_a\le n\}\) over integers \(n\ge 1\), because a finite \(\tau_a\) is at most some integer \(n\). By continuity of probability from below and Corollary 1.3, \(\Prob(\tau_a<\infty)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prob(\tau_a\le n)=\lim_{n\to\infty}2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt n))\). Since \(a/\sqrt n\to 0\) and \(\Phi\) is continuous, the limit is \(2(1-\tfrac12)=1\). The argument is a limit of probabilities, so it establishes finiteness with probability one and nothing about any single outcome. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 1.5 (density of the first passage)

Under the assumptions of Corollary 1.3, the law of \(\tau_a\) restricted to \((0,\infty)\) has the density \(f_a(t)=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,t^{3/2}}\,e^{-a^2/(2t)}\) for \(t>0\).

Proof

Let \(F(t)=2(1-\Phi(a\,t^{-1/2}))\) for \(t>0\), so \(F(t)=\Prob(\tau_a\le t)\) by Corollary 1.3. Differentiating with \(\Phi'=\varphi\), where \(\varphi(u)=e^{-u^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\), gives \(F'(t)=2\varphi(a t^{-1/2})\cdot\tfrac{a}{2}t^{-3/2}=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,t^{3/2}}e^{-a^2/(2t)}=f_a(t)\). For \(0<\varepsilon<t\), \(F(t)-F(\varepsilon)=\int_\varepsilon^t f_a(s)\,ds\), because \(F\) is continuously differentiable on \((0,\infty)\). As \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\), \(a/\sqrt\varepsilon\to\infty\), so \(F(\varepsilon)\to 0\). Hence \(F(t)=\int_0^t f_a(s)\,ds\), which says that \(f_a\) is the density of the law of \(\tau_a\) on \((0,\infty)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.6 (what is not evaluated)

Corollary 1.4 gives the probability of finiteness, and Corollary 1.5 gives the law. This page does not compute \(\E[\tau_a]\), and it states no value or bound for it. The density of Corollary 1.5 behaves like \(a\,t^{-3/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\) as \(t\to\infty\). The walk truncated means in Lab 1, Part B are sums of probabilities up to a fixed horizon for the walk, and they are not a value of \(\E[\tau_a]\).

2First passage of the simple random walk

The lab works with a simple random walk, the sum \(S_k=e_1+\dots+e_k\) with \(S_0=0\) and each step \(e_i\) equal to \(+1\) or \(-1\). Its counts are exact, so it gives a check of the same events that the Brownian results use.

Definition 2.1 (first passage of the walk)

For an integer \(a\ge 1\), let \(T_a=\min\{k\ge 1: S_k\ge a\}\), with \(T_a=\infty\) if no such \(k\) exists. Then \(\{T_a\le N\}\) is the event that the walk is at or above \(a\) by step \(N\).

Proposition 2.2 (walk identity for first passage)

For integers \(a\ge 1\) and \(N\ge 1\), on every step sequence, \(\{T_a\le N\}=\{\max_{0\le k\le N}S_k\ge a\}\). Hence \(\#\{T_a\le N\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\#\{S_N>a\}\) over the \(2^N\) step sequences.

Proof

If \(T_a\le N\), then \(S_{T_a}\ge a\) with \(T_a\le N\), so the maximum is at least \(a\). Conversely, let \(\max_{k\le N}S_k\ge a\), and let \(k^\ast\) be the first index with \(S_{k^\ast}\ge a\). Since \(S_0=0<a\), \(k^\ast\ge 1\), and \(S_{k^\ast-1}\le a-1\). The step is \(\pm 1\), so \(S_{k^\ast}\le a\), which gives \(S_{k^\ast}=a\) and \(T_a=k^\ast\le N\). Since the two events coincide, their counts coincide, and the count of \(\{\max_{k\le N}S_k\ge a\}\) is \(\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\#\{S_N>a\}\) by the discrete tail identity of Corollary 1.4 of Lesson 6. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.3 (exact truncated mean of the walk)

For integers \(a\ge 1\) and \(N\ge 1\), \(\E[\min(T_a,N)]=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Prob(T_a>k)\), and \(\Prob(T_a>k)=1-\Prob(\max_{0\le j\le k}S_j\ge a)\). Both sums are finite, and the second is an exact fraction of \(2^k\).

Proof

For each outcome, \(\min(T_a,N)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\indic\{T_a>k\}\). If \(T_a\le N\), the terms with \(k\ge T_a\) vanish and the sum counts \(k=0,\dots,T_a-1\), which is \(T_a\). If \(T_a>N\), every term equals 1 and the sum is \(N\). Taking expectations of this finite sum gives the first identity. The second follows from Proposition 2.2 with \(N\) replaced by \(k\), since \(\{T_a>k\}\) is the complement of \(\{T_a\le k\}\). \(\blacksquare\)

"""SC1 lab L08: first-passage counts of the simple random walk, truncated means, and the Brownian first-passage law.

Standard library only. Parts A, B and D use no randomness. Part C is SIMULATED: it draws PATHS seeded simple random
walk paths from random.Random(SEED), so every run prints the same lines.

The walk is S_0 = 0 and S_k is the sum of the steps e_1 through e_k, each e_i equal to +1 or -1.
For an integer level a >= 1, the first passage index T_a is the smallest k with S_k >= a, and it is None
when no k <= n has this property.

Part A enumerates all 2**n step sequences for n = 1 to 12 and every level a from 1 to n. For each sequence it compares
the event {T_a <= n} with the event {running maximum over S_0 .. S_n is at least a}. Because the walk moves by one step
at a time, the two events agree on every sequence, and the lab checks this for every sequence in the enumeration. It
also checks the count of such sequences against the walk identity of Lesson 6, and prints the first-passage counts
for n = 12 and a = 3, and two probabilities for that case.

Part B computes the truncated mean E[min(T_1, N)] exactly, from the identity E[min(T, N)] = sum over k < N of
P(T > k), with P(T_1 > k) = 1 - P(max over S_0 .. S_k is at least 1). The values are exact fractions printed to six
decimals.

Part C is SIMULATED. It draws SIM_PATHS seeded walks of length SIM_STEPS, records min(T_1, SIM_STEPS) on each, and
compares the sample mean of min(T_1, N) with the exact value from Part B for N = 16, 64 and 256.

Part D evaluates the first-passage distribution function of Brownian motion, erfc(a / sqrt(2 t)), and its density,
a / (sqrt(2 pi) t^(3/2)) exp(-a^2 / (2 t)), at a = 1 and t = 1 and t = 2. The density is compared with a central
difference of the distribution function. Part D evaluates formulas; it does not check the assumption that the lesson
states for them.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import product

SEED = 20260508
MAX_ENUM = 12
EXACT_HORIZONS = (16, 64, 256)
SIM_PATHS = 1000
SIM_STEPS = 256
DIFFERENCE_STEP = 1e-5


def end_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
    """The number of length-n step sequences whose endpoint S_n is at least m, as an exact integer."""
    return sum(math.comb(n, (n + s) // 2) for s in range(-n, n + 1, 2) if s >= m)


def max_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
    """The number of length-n step sequences whose running maximum over S_0 to S_n is at least m (walk identity)."""
    return end_at_least(n, m) + end_at_least(n, m + 1)


def enumerated_paths(n: int, a: int) -> list[tuple[int | None, int]]:
    """For each step sequence of length n: (first k with S_k >= a, or None, and the running maximum over S_0 to S_n)."""
    results: list[tuple[int | None, int]] = []
    for steps in product((1, -1), repeat=n):
        position = 0
        highest = 0
        hit: int | None = None
        for k, step in enumerate(steps, start=1):
            position += step
            highest = max(highest, position)
            if hit is None and position >= a:
                hit = k
        results.append((hit, highest))
    return results


def part_a() -> None:
    """Compare the first-passage event with the maximum event on every sequence, for n <= 12 and a <= n."""
    sequences = 0
    pathwise_agree = 0
    counts_agree = 0
    pairs = 0
    for n in range(1, MAX_ENUM + 1):
        for a in range(1, n + 1):
            paths = enumerated_paths(n, a)
            pairs += 1
            sequences += len(paths)
            hits_by_n = sum(1 for hit, _ in paths if hit is not None and hit <= n)
            pathwise_agree += sum(1 for hit, top in paths if (hit is not None) == (top >= a))
            counts_agree += hits_by_n == max_at_least(n, a)
    print(f"Part A: pairs (n, a) checked: {pairs}; counts agree with the walk identity for {counts_agree} of {pairs}")
    print(f"Part A: sequences checked: {sequences}; {{T_a <= n}} equals {{maximum >= a}} on {pathwise_agree} of them")
    print(f"Part A: pathwise agreement holds for every sequence: {pathwise_agree == sequences}")

    n, a = MAX_ENUM, 3
    paths = enumerated_paths(n, a)
    by_step = [sum(1 for hit, _ in paths if hit == k) for k in range(a, n + 1)]
    print(f"Part A: counts of first passage to {a} at step k, for k = {a} to {n}: {by_step}")
    hits = sum(by_step)
    walk_count = max_at_least(n, a)
    print(f"Part A: sum of the counts equals the walk identity count {walk_count}: {hits == walk_count}")
    print(f"Part A: P(T_{a} <= {n}) = {hits} / {2**n} = {hits / 2**n:.6f}")
    print(f"Part A: P(S_{n} >= {a}) = {end_at_least(n, a)} / {2**n} = {end_at_least(n, a) / 2**n:.6f}")


def part_b() -> None:
    """Print the exact truncated means E[min(T_1, N)] for the horizons in EXACT_HORIZONS."""
    largest = max(EXACT_HORIZONS)
    tail_above = [Fraction(max_at_least(k, 1), 2**k) for k in range(largest)]
    survive = [1 - tail for tail in tail_above]
    running = Fraction(0)
    truncated: dict[int, Fraction] = {}
    for k in range(largest):
        running += survive[k]
        if k + 1 in EXACT_HORIZONS:
            truncated[k + 1] = running
    print("Part B: exact truncated means E[min(T_1, N)] = sum over k < N of P(T_1 > k) for the simple random walk")
    for n in EXACT_HORIZONS:
        print(f"Part B: N = {n:4d}: E[min(T_1, N)] = {float(truncated[n]):.6f}")


def simulated_first_passage(rng: random.Random) -> list[int]:
    """For each of SIM_PATHS seeded walks of length SIM_STEPS, the value min(T_1, SIM_STEPS), in steps."""
    capped: list[int] = []
    for _ in range(SIM_PATHS):
        position = 0
        first = SIM_STEPS
        for k in range(1, SIM_STEPS + 1):
            position += 1 if rng.random() < 0.5 else -1
            if position >= 1:
                first = k
                break
        capped.append(first)
    return capped


def mean_and_standard_error(values: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """The sample mean, and the standard error sd / sqrt(n) with sd computed by divisor n - 1."""
    n = len(values)
    mean = sum(values) / n
    variance = sum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in values) / (n - 1)
    return mean, math.sqrt(variance / n)


def part_c() -> None:
    """Compare the seeded sample mean of min(T_1, N) with the exact truncated mean from Part B."""
    capped = simulated_first_passage(random.Random(SEED))
    print(f"Part C: {SIM_PATHS} seeded simple random walk paths (SIMULATED), each followed for {SIM_STEPS} steps")
    largest = max(EXACT_HORIZONS)
    tails = [Fraction(max_at_least(k, 1), 2**k) for k in range(largest)]
    running = Fraction(0)
    exact: dict[int, Fraction] = {}
    for k in range(largest):
        running += 1 - tails[k]
        if k + 1 in EXACT_HORIZONS:
            exact[k + 1] = running
    for n in EXACT_HORIZONS:
        values = [float(min(c, n)) for c in capped]
        mean, se = mean_and_standard_error(values)
        target = float(exact[n])
        print(
            f"Part C: N = {n:4d}: sample mean {mean:.4f}, se {se:.4f}; exact {target:.4f}; "
            f"z = {(mean - target) / se:+.2f}"
        )


def first_passage_cdf(a: float, t: float) -> float:
    """P(tau_a <= t) = erfc(a / sqrt(2 t)) = 2 (1 - Phi(a / sqrt(t))), for a > 0 and t > 0."""
    return math.erfc(a / math.sqrt(2.0 * t))


def first_passage_density(a: float, t: float) -> float:
    """The density a / (sqrt(2 pi) t^(3/2)) exp(-a^2 / (2 t)) per unit of time, for a > 0 and t > 0."""
    return a / (math.sqrt(2.0 * math.pi) * t * math.sqrt(t)) * math.exp(-(a * a) / (2.0 * t))


def part_d() -> None:
    """Evaluate the first-passage distribution and compare its density with a central difference."""
    level = 1.0
    for t in (1.0, 2.0):
        cdf = first_passage_cdf(level, t)
        upper = first_passage_cdf(level, t + DIFFERENCE_STEP)
        lower = first_passage_cdf(level, t - DIFFERENCE_STEP)
        numeric = (upper - lower) / (2.0 * DIFFERENCE_STEP)
        closed = first_passage_density(level, t)
        print(f"Part D: a = 1, t = {t:g}: P(tau_a <= t) = {cdf:.6f}")
        print(
            f"Part D: a = 1, t = {t:g}: density {closed:.6f}; central difference {numeric:.6f}; "
            f"agree to 1e-8: {abs(closed - numeric) < 1e-8}"
        )


def main() -> None:
    part_a()
    part_b()
    part_c()
    part_d()

3Where the canon breaks

  • The identity needs continuous paths. Proposition 1.2 uses the intermediate value theorem on a continuous path. Let the path be \(0\) on \([0,\tfrac12)\), jump to \(2\) at \(\tfrac12\), and stay at 2. Then \(M_1=2\ge 1\), but the path never equals 1, so \(\tau_1=\infty\) and the event \(\{\tau_1\le 1\}\) fails. The identity is therefore not asserted for paths with jumps.
  • The closed form is conditional. Corollary 1.3 and everything after it assume A3 and A4 of Lesson 5. Corollary 1.4 is a statement about probability, so it does not say that the set of outcomes with \(\tau_a=\infty\) is empty.
  • Finite does not mean integrable. Corollary 1.4 gives finiteness with probability one. It gives no bound on \(\E[\tau_a]\). This page does not evaluate it.

4Misconceptions

  1. “The first-passage time \(\tau_a\) is finite on every path.”

    Corollary 1.4 gives \(\Prob(\tau_a<\infty)=1\). That is a statement about the probability of the event \(\{\tau_a=\infty\}\), which is zero. It does not say that the event is empty. The proof passes to the limit in probabilities and examines no single outcome.

  2. “The first passage to \(a\) has the same probability as the terminal value: \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=\Prob(B_t\ge a)\).”

    Corollary 1.3 gives \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a)\). At \(t=1\) and \(a=1\), Lab 1, Part D prints \(\Prob(\tau_1\le 1)=0.317311\), while \(\Prob(B_1\ge 1)=0.1586552539\) (Lesson 7, Part D). The walk gives the same pattern: Lab 1, Part A prints 0.387695 for the first passage by step 12 and 0.193848 for the endpoint.

  3. “Finiteness with probability one implies that the mean of \(\tau_a\) is finite.”

    Finiteness does not bound a mean. The density of Corollary 1.5 behaves like a constant times \(t^{-3/2}\) for large \(t\), and this page evaluates no moment of \(\tau_a\). Lab 1, Part B prints truncated means that increase with the horizon, and the page states no limit for them.

5Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1. Problems that use the closed form assume A3 and A4.

Mechanics

M1 · A first passage at horizon one

Let \(a=1\) and \(t=1\). Compute \(\Prob(\tau_1\le 1)\), assuming A3 and A4.

Solution

By Corollary 1.3, \(\Prob(\tau_1\le 1)=2(1-\Phi(1))=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)\). Lab 1, Part D prints \(\Prob(\tau_1\le 1)=0.317311\), which is twice the value \(0.1586552539\) of \(1-\Phi(1)\) printed in Lesson 7.

M2 · The density at a point

Let \(a=1\). Evaluate \(f_1(1)\) from Corollary 1.5 and compare it with Lab 1, Part D.

Solution

Here \(f_1(1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-1/2}\). Since \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=0.398942\) and \(e^{-1/2}=0.606531\), the product is \(0.241971\). Part D prints the density 0.241971 at \(t=1\), and the central difference of the distribution function gives the same value.

M3 · A walk of three steps

For the simple random walk of 3 steps, find \(\Prob(T_1\le 3)\) by listing the sequences, and check it with the walk identity.

Solution

Sequences starting with \(+1\) reach 1 at step 1, which gives 4 sequences. Sequences starting with \(-1\) reach 1 only through \((-1,+1,+1)\), which gives 1 more. The total is 5 of \(2^3=8\), so \(\Prob(T_1\le 3)=5/8=0.625\). The walk identity gives \(\#\{S_3\ge 1\}+\#\{S_3>1\}=(3+1)+1=5\), which agrees.

P1 · Proposition 1.2 on one path

Prove that on a continuous path with \(B_0=0\), \(\{\tau_a\le t\}=\{M_t\ge a\}\) for \(a>0\). State each hypothesis used.

Solution

If \(\tau_a\le t\), the set \(\{s:B_s=a\}\) is closed and nonempty by continuity, so \(B_{\tau_a}=a\) with \(\tau_a\in[0,t]\), giving \(M_t\ge a\). If \(M_t\ge a\), the maximum over \([0,t]\) is attained at \(s^\ast\le t\) with \(B_{s^\ast}\ge a>0=B_0\), and the intermediate value theorem gives \(u\in(0,s^\ast]\) with \(B_u=a\), so \(\tau_a\le u\le t\). Hypotheses used: continuity of the path, \(B_0=0\), and \(a>0\).

P2 · The walk identity

Prove that for integers \(a\ge 1\) and \(N\ge 1\), the events \(\{T_a\le N\}\) and \(\{\max_{k\le N}S_k\ge a\}\) coincide on every step sequence.

Solution

If \(T_a\le N\), then \(S_{T_a}\ge a\), so the maximum is at least \(a\). If the maximum is at least \(a\), let \(k^\ast\) be the first index with \(S_{k^\ast}\ge a\). Then \(k^\ast\ge 1\), since \(S_0=0<a\). The previous value satisfies \(S_{k^\ast-1}\le a-1\), and the step is \(\pm 1\), so \(S_{k^\ast}=a\). Hence \(T_a=k^\ast\le N\).

P3 · The truncated mean

Prove that \(\E[\min(T_a,N)]=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Prob(T_a>k)\) for integers \(a\ge 1\) and \(N\ge 1\).

Solution

For each outcome, \(\min(T_a,N)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\indic\{T_a>k\}\). If \(T_a\le N\), the sum counts \(k=0,\dots,T_a-1\), which is \(T_a\). If \(T_a>N\), each of the \(N\) terms is 1. Taking expectations of a finite sum of indicators gives \(\Prob(T_a>k)\) for each \(k\), and linearity of expectation gives the identity.

Empirical

E1 · Parity of the first-passage counts

Lab 1, Part A prints counts of 512, 0, 384, 0, 288, 0, 224, 0, 180 and 0 for steps 3 to 12 when \(a=3\) and \(n=12\). Explain the zeros and check the total.

Solution

\(S_k\) has the parity of \(k\), because each step changes the sum by \(\pm1\). Reaching level 3 at step \(k\) needs \(S_k=3\), so \(k\) must be odd, and the even steps have count 0. The odd counts are 512 at step 3, 384 at step 5, 288 at step 7, 224 at step 9 and 180 at step 11. Their sum is 1588, which is the walk count the lab prints.

E2 · Reading the seeded means

Lab 1, Part C prints sample means 5.2850, 11.3840 and 23.1240, against exact values 5.4806, 11.8157 and 24.5572. State the z values the lab prints and what one seed shows.

Solution

The lab prints z values of \(-1.05\), \(-0.69\) and \(-0.77\), so each sample mean lies below its exact value by less than 1.1 standard errors. The walks are the same for all three horizons, so the three comparisons are correlated. One seed of 1000 walks cannot test the exact values separately, and it cannot show a bias either way.

E3 · The density at a second point

Lab 1, Part D prints a density of 0.109848 at \(a=1\) and \(t=2\). Recompute it from Corollary 1.5.

Solution

The formula is \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,t^{-3/2}e^{-1/(2t)}\) with \(a=1\). At \(t=2\), \(t^{-3/2}=2^{-3/2}=0.353553\) and \(e^{-1/4}=0.778801\). The product with \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=0.398942\) is \(0.109848\), which matches the printed value.

Research

R1 · Why the walk ratio is exactly two here

Show that \(\#\{T_a\le N\}=2\,\#\{S_N\ge a\}-\#\{S_N=a\}\) for integers \(a\ge 1\) and \(N\ge 1\). Use it to explain why Lab 1 gives \(\Prob(T_3\le 12)=2\,\Prob(S_{12}\ge 3)\) exactly.

Solution

By Proposition 2.2, \(\#\{T_a\le N\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\#\{S_N>a\}\). Since \(\#\{S_N>a\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}-\#\{S_N=a\}\), the identity follows. For \(N=12\) and \(a=3\), \(S_{12}\) is even, so \(\#\{S_{12}=3\}=0\). Hence \(\#\{T_3\le 12\}=2\,\#\{S_{12}\ge 3\}\), which gives 1588 = 2 × 794, matching the printed probabilities 0.387695 and 0.193848.

R2 · Increments of the truncated mean

Show that \(\E[\min(T_a,N+1)]-\E[\min(T_a,N)]=\Prob(T_a>N)\), and use it to show that the truncated mean is nondecreasing in \(N\).

Solution

For each outcome, \(\min(T_a,N+1)-\min(T_a,N)=\indic\{T_a>N\}\). Taking expectations gives the increment \(\Prob(T_a>N)\), which is nonnegative. So the truncated mean is nondecreasing in \(N\).

R3 · The mode of the first-passage density

For fixed \(a>0\), find the time \(t\) at which \(f_a\) is largest, assuming A3 and A4.

Solution

Write \(\log f_a(t)=\text{const}-\tfrac32\log t-\dfrac{a^2}{2t}\). Its derivative in \(t\) is \(-\dfrac{3}{2t}+\dfrac{a^2}{2t^2}=\dfrac{a^2-3t}{2t^2}\). This is positive for \(t<a^2/3\) and negative for \(t>a^2/3\), because the denominator is positive. So \(\log f_a\), and hence \(f_a\), increases up to \(t=a^2/3\) and decreases after it, and \(f_a\) is largest at \(t=a^2/3\). The result uses Corollary 1.5, and so it assumes A3 and A4.
