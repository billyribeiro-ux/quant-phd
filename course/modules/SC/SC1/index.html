Stochastic Processes

Many models of prices and noise describe a quantity that moves randomly through time, and the questions that matter about such a process are about its paths: how far it can move in a short time, how its sums of squared moves behave, when it first reaches a level, and how its running maximum is distributed. This module builds the continuous-time theory those questions need, starting from Brownian motion. It defines the process by its increments, constructs it with continuous paths, computes its quadratic variation, and develops the martingale, Markov and stopping-time properties. It ends with the reflection principle, which gives the distribution of the running maximum and of the first-passage time in closed form under Assumption A3. Its project proves those results and checks them with a module that runs on generated instances.

  • Prerequisites: M6 Measure-Theoretic Probability. Its closure is M1, M2, M4, M5, M6 and P0.1. The concepts this module uses are linked from the section below.
  • Project: Reflection principle verified: a proof set and an engine, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. The labs and the grader use generated paths, which are labelled SIMULATED, exact finite walks, and closed forms. No real market data is used.

72 h lessons · 28 h project (28% of the 100 h module)

Objectives

By the end of SC1 you can:

  1. Define standard Brownian motion by its three conditions on increments, and derive its covariance \(\min(s,t)\) (Lesson 1).
  2. Construct a Brownian motion on \([0,1]\) with continuous paths from independent normal coefficients, and state the assumption that its identification at all times needs (Lesson 2).
  3. Prove that the sums of squared increments of a Brownian motion converge in \(L^2\) to the length of the interval along partitions whose mesh tends to zero, and show that the variation of \(t\mapsto t^2\) over the same partitions vanishes (Lesson 3).
  4. Verify that \(B_t\) and \(B_t^2-t\) are continuous-time martingales, and check the exponential martingale by quadrature (Lesson 4).
  5. State the Markov property and the strong Markov property with its assumption, and prove the Chapman–Kolmogorov identity for the Gaussian transition density (Lesson 5).
  6. Prove the reflection principle, derive the tail of the running maximum and the first-passage identity, and name every assumption each one uses (Lessons 6 to 8).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions. Seven test the prerequisites, from P0.1, M1, M2, M4, M5 and M6. Two are skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. With nine items the bar needs eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. What does grade T mean in this program?

    Options

    T is the theorem grade (P0.1, evidence grade).

  2. Which technique proves a claim by assuming its negation and deriving a contradiction?

    Options

    Proof by contradiction assumes the negation of the claim (M1, proof by contradiction).

  3. What is \(\int_0^1 2x\,dx\)?

    An antiderivative of \(2x\) is \(x^2\), and \(1^2-0^2=1\) (M2, definite integral).

  4. A random variable \(X\) takes the value 1 with probability \(1/2\) and the value 3 with probability \(1/2\). What is \(\E[X]\)?

    \(\E[X]=\tfrac12\cdot 1+\tfrac12\cdot 3=2\) (M4, expectation).

  5. For independent integrable random variables \(X\) and \(Y\), which statement holds?

    Options

    Independence gives the product rule for expectations (M4, independence). It is Assumption A1(a) in Lesson 1.

  6. Which property of the real numbers makes every Cauchy sequence of reals converge?

    Options

    Completeness of the reals is the property that gives convergence of Cauchy sequences (M5, completeness of the reals).

  7. In a martingale \((X_n)\) with respect to a filtration \((\mathcal F_n)\), what is \(\E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\)?

    Options

    The martingale property is \(\E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=X_n\) (M6, martingale).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): for a standard Brownian motion and \(a>0\), which expression equals \(\Prob(\max_{s\le t}B_s\ge a)\)?

    Options

    This is the reflection tail, which the reflection principle of Lesson 6 gives under Assumption A3. Rung 1 works an instance of it.

  9. Skill check (rung 2): a simple random walk takes two steps of \(+1\) or \(-1\), each with probability \(1/2\). How many of its four two-step paths reach level 1 at some step?

    The paths \(++\) and \(+-\) reach 1. The paths \(-+\) and \(--\) do not. The count is the completion line of rung 2.

Lessons

Eight lessons. Each declares 9 study hours in its data-hours attribute, so the lesson hours sum to 72. Lessons 1 and 2 define and construct Brownian motion. Lesson 3 computes its quadratic variation. Lesson 4 covers continuous-time martingales. Lesson 5 covers the Markov property. Lessons 6 to 8 cover the reflection principle, the running maximum and first-passage times. Every lesson has labs inline, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 9 hBrownian Motion as a Gaussian ProcessThe definition of standard Brownian motion by its increments, the covariance \(\min(s,t)\) derived from the increments, the scale of an increment, and what the definition does not decide about the paths. L02 · 9 hConstructing Brownian MotionA construction on \([0,1]\) from independent normal coefficients and hat functions, with uniform convergence and continuous paths, and the identification of the laws at all times stated as Assumption A2. L03 · 9 hQuadratic VariationSums of squared increments over partitions, their \(L^2\) limit along partitions with mesh tending to zero, and the vanishing variation of \(t\mapsto t^2\). L04 · 9 hContinuous-Time MartingalesThe filtration of Brownian motion, the martingales \(B_t\), \(B_t^2-t\) and the exponential martingale, and the optional stopping theorem for a bounded stopping time. L05 · 9 hThe Markov PropertyThe Gaussian transition density, the Chapman–Kolmogorov identity proved by Gaussian convolution, and the strong Markov property stated as Assumption A3. L06 · 9 hThe Reflection PrincipleReflection at the first passage time under Assumption A3, the tail \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=2\Prob(B_t\ge a)\), and the discrete reflection identity for the simple random walk. L07 · 9 hThe Distribution of the Running MaximumThe closed-form tail of the running maximum through the standard normal distribution function, and the discretely monitored maximum with its inequality. L08 · 9 hFirst-Passage TimesThe identity between the first-passage probability and the tail of the running maximum, finiteness of the first-passage time with probability one, and the walk count that checks the identity exactly.

The project ladder

The module ends in the reflection principle project. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks. The hours of each rung are on the project page.

  1. Worked example

    One reflection tail computed for a generated instance, annotated line by line with the reason for each step. The instance is labelled SIMULATED.

  2. Completion problem

    The discrete reflection count for the simple random walk, with the cap line and the final comparison line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Prove the reflection principle, the running-maximum distribution and the first-passage identity, and implement the checking module bm_checks.py. Graded by the autograder against generated instances and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example the same checks for a second level, or a measured discretization bias over a family of grids, suitable for your portfolio.

Data status

No real data is used in SC1. The labs and the grader compute from closed forms, exact finite walks and generated paths. Every generated path is labelled SIMULATED on the page where it appears.
