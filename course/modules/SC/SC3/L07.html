Quanto Adjustments and Cross-Currency Derivatives

  • Uses: Girsanov's theorem, change of numeraire, exchange option, geometric Brownian motion, Itô's lemma, continuous-time martingale
  • Introduces: quanto adjustment
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

  • Dlore

Global investment portfolios routinely trade cross-currency contracts whose payoffs are tied to foreign assets but settled in domestic currency at a fixed exchange rate (quanto derivatives). Because the foreign asset and the exchange rate fluctuate simultaneously, the domestic investor is exposed to currency covariance risk. Under the domestic risk-neutral measure, the growth rate of the foreign asset acquires an exact drift correction proportional to the asset-currency correlation, known as the quanto adjustment.

1Cross-Currency Setup and Market Diffusions

Consider two currencies: a domestic currency (USD) with constant risk-free rate \(r_d\) and money-market numeraire \(B_t^d = e^{r_d t}\), and a foreign currency (EUR) with risk-free rate \(r_f\) and money-market numeraire \(B_t^f = e^{r_f t}\). Let \(X_t\) denote the spot exchange rate quoted as units of domestic currency per unit of foreign currency (e.g. USD per EUR). Let \(S_t^f\) denote the price of a foreign asset (e.g. European equity) denominated in foreign currency (EUR).

Theorem 1.1 (Exchange Rate Dynamics under Domestic Measure)

Assumptions: (A1) Domestic and foreign risk-free rates \(r_d, r_f\) are constant; (A2) Holding foreign cash yields domestic dividends at foreign rate \(r_f\).

Statement: Under the domestic risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), the exchange rate \(X_t\) satisfies:

\[ \frac{dX_t}{X_t} = (r_d - r_f) dt + \sigma_X dW_t^{d, X}, \]

where \(W_t^{d, X}\) is a standard Brownian motion under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) and \(\sigma_X > 0\) is the FX volatility.

Proof

An investment of 1 foreign currency unit in the foreign bank account grows to \(B_t^f = e^{r_f t}\) foreign units. Converted into domestic currency, its domestic value is \(Y_t \coloneqq X_t e^{r_f t}\). Because \(Y_t\) is a tradable security in the domestic market, its discounted domestic value \(e^{-r_d t} Y_t = e^{-(r_d - r_f)t} X_t\) must be an \((\mathcal{F}_t, \mathbb{Q}^d)\)-martingale. Applying Itô's lemma: \(d(e^{-(r_d - r_f)t} X_t) = e^{-(r_d - r_f)t}[dX_t - (r_d - r_f)X_t dt]\). For this to be a driftless martingale, the drift of \(dX_t\) must be \((r_d - r_f)X_t dt\). Thus \(dX_t / X_t = (r_d - r_f) dt + \sigma_X dW_t^{d, X}\).

2The Quanto Drift Adjustment

Now consider a domestic contract whose terminal payoff at maturity \(H\) is determined by the foreign asset price \(S_H^f\) without currency conversion: for example, a fixed-rate quanto European call delivering:

\[ V_H = \max(S_H^f - K, 0) \quad \text{dollars}. \]

Because the payout is in domestic currency, pricing must take place under the domestic risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\):

\[ V_0 = e^{-r_d H} \E^{\mathbb{Q}^d}[(S_H^f - K)^+]. \]

What are the dynamics of the foreign asset \(S_t^f\) under the domestic measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\)?

Theorem 1.2 (Quanto Drift Adjustment Theorem)

Assumptions: (A1) Under the foreign risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^f\), \(dS_t^f / S_t^f = r_f dt + \sigma_S dW_t^{f, S}\); (A2) The foreign asset volatility is \(\sigma_S\) and the exchange rate volatility is \(\sigma_X\); (A3) The instantaneous correlation between the foreign asset and the exchange rate is \(d\langle W^S, W^X \rangle_t = \rho dt\).

Statement: Under the domestic risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), the foreign asset \(S_t^f\) follows geometric Brownian motion with drift shifted by \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\):

\[ \frac{dS_t^f}{S_t^f} = (r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X) dt + \sigma_S dW_t^{d, S}. \]

The quantity \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\) is the quanto adjustment (or quanto drift adjustment).

Proof

The domestic investor can trade the foreign asset directly by converting domestic currency to foreign, purchasing \(S_t^f\), and converting back. The domestic currency value of one share of the foreign asset is \(U_t \coloneqq X_t S_t^f\). Because \(U_t\) is a tradable asset in the domestic market, its discounted domestic value \(e^{-r_d t} U_t\) must be a continuous \(\mathbb{Q}^d\)-martingale, having drift \(r_d U_t dt\).

Let the dynamics of \(S_t^f\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) be \(dS_t^f / S_t^f = \mu_S^d dt + \sigma_S dW_t^{d, S}\). By Itô's product rule:

\[ dU_t = d(X_t S_t^f) = S_t^f dX_t + X_t dS_t^f + d\langle X, S^f \rangle_t. \]

Dividing by \(U_t = X_t S_t^f\):

\[ \frac{dU_t}{U_t} = \frac{dX_t}{X_t} + \frac{dS_t^f}{S_t^f} + \frac{d\langle X, S^f \rangle_t}{X_t S_t^f}. \]

From Theorem 1.1, the drift of \(dX_t / X_t\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) is \((r_d - r_f) dt\). The quadratic covariation is:

\[ \frac{d\langle X, S^f \rangle_t}{X_t S_t^f} = \sigma_X \sigma_S d\langle W^{d, X}, W^{d, S} \rangle_t = \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X dt. \]

Combining the drift contributions of \(U_t\):

\[ \text{Drift}\left(\frac{dU_t}{U_t}\right) = (r_d - r_f) + \mu_S^d + \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X. \]

For \(e^{-r_d t} U_t\) to be a \(\mathbb{Q}^d\)-martingale, this total drift must equal \(r_d\):

\[ (r_d - r_f) + \mu_S^d + \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = r_d \implies \mu_S^d = r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X. \]

Thus the drift of \(S_t^f\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) is \(r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\).

Theorem 1.3 (Quanto European Call Option Formula)

Assumptions: (A1) \(S_t^f\) satisfies Theorem 1.2 under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\); (A2) Payoff at maturity \(H\) is \(\max(S_H^f - K, 0)\) domestic dollars.

Statement: The domestic price of the quanto call option at \(t = 0\) is:

\[ V_0 = e^{-r_d H} [F^{\text{quanto}}(0, H) \Phi(d_1) - K \Phi(d_2)], \]

where the quanto forward price is:

\[ F^{\text{quanto}}(0, H) \coloneqq S_0^f \exp((r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X)H), \]

and

\[ d_1 \coloneqq \frac{\log(F^{\text{quanto}}(0, H) / K) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_S^2 H}{\sigma_S \sqrt{H}}, \quad d_2 \coloneqq d_1 - \sigma_S \sqrt{H}. \]
Proof

Under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), \(\log(S_H^f / S_0^f) \sim \Normal((r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_S^2)H, \sigma_S^2 H)\). Evaluating \(e^{-r_d H}\E^{\mathbb{Q}^d}[(S_H^f - K)^+]\) via standard Black-Scholes lognormal integration yields the result directly.

3Curriculum Lab: Girsanov Importance Sampling for Deep-OTM Options

Pricing rare-event derivatives (such as deep out-of-the-money options, barrier hits, or far-tail credit triggers) presents a severe challenge for standard Monte Carlo simulation. When strike \(K \gg S_0\), only a tiny fraction of simulated paths (e.g. 1 in 10,000) terminate in the money. Standard Monte Carlo produces high relative standard error. By Girsanov's theorem, we can shift the Brownian drift so that simulated trajectories are centered directly in the payoff region, weighting each payoff by the Radon–Nikodym likelihood ratio to preserve unbiasedness while drastically reducing estimator variance.

Lab 1 · Curriculum lab: Importance sampling via Girsanov for deep-OTM options

For deep out-of-the-money options, standard Monte Carlo simulation suffers from severe variance because the payoff event {S_T > K} is extremely rare. By Girsanov's theorem, we can change the drift of the underlying Brownian motion so that paths frequently terminate in the money, and weight each simulated payoff by the Radon-Nikodym likelihood ratio: L = exp(-theta * W_T - (1/2) * theta^2 * T). Choosing the drift shift theta* = (log(K / S_0) - (r - sigma^2/2) * T) / (sigma * T) centers the terminal distribution at the strike K. This lab simulates 20,000 paths (SIMULATED) under standard Monte Carlo and Girsanov importance sampling for a deep-OTM call (S_0 = 100, K = 220, r = 0.05, sigma = 0.20, T = 1.0), verifying that Girsanov importance sampling yields an unbiased estimate with an empirical variance reduction exceeding 50x.

"""Lab 1 · Curriculum lab: Importance sampling via Girsanov for deep-OTM options.

For deep out-of-the-money options, standard Monte Carlo simulation suffers from severe variance
because the payoff event {S_T > K} is extremely rare.
By Girsanov's theorem, we can change the drift of the underlying Brownian motion so that paths
frequently terminate in the money, and weight each simulated payoff by the Radon-Nikodym likelihood ratio:
    L = exp(-theta * W_T - (1/2) * theta^2 * T).
Choosing the drift shift theta* = (log(K / S_0) - (r - sigma^2/2) * T) / (sigma * T) centers the terminal
distribution at the strike K.
This lab simulates 20,000 paths (SIMULATED) under standard Monte Carlo and Girsanov importance sampling
for a deep-OTM call (S_0 = 100, K = 220, r = 0.05, sigma = 0.20, T = 1.0), verifying that Girsanov importance
sampling yields an unbiased estimate with an empirical variance reduction exceeding 50x.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def bs_call_price(s0: float, k: float, r: float, sigma: float, t: float) -> float:
    sq_t = math.sqrt(t)
    d1 = (math.log(s0 / k) + (r + 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t) / (sigma * sq_t)
    d2 = d1 - sigma * sq_t
    return s0 * norm_cdf(d1) - k * math.exp(-r * t) * norm_cdf(d2)


def main() -> None:
    seed = 42
    s0 = 100.0
    k = 220.0
    r = 0.05
    sigma = 0.20
    t = 1.0
    n_paths = 20000

    exact_price = bs_call_price(s0, k, r, sigma, t)
    sq_t = math.sqrt(t)

    # 1. Standard Monte Carlo
    rng_std = random.Random(seed)
    drift_std = (r - 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t
    vol = sigma * sq_t
    df = math.exp(-r * t)

    vals_std: list[float] = []
    hits_std = 0
    for _ in range(n_paths):
        z = rng_std.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        st = s0 * math.exp(drift_std + vol * z)
        payoff = max(st - k, 0.0)
        if payoff > 0.0:
            hits_std += 1
        vals_std.append(df * payoff)

    mean_std = sum(vals_std) / n_paths
    var_std = sum((x - mean_std) ** 2 for x in vals_std) / (n_paths - 1)
    se_std = math.sqrt(var_std / n_paths)

    # 2. Girsanov Importance Sampling
    # Optimal drift shift theta to center S_T at K
    theta_star = (math.log(k / s0) - (r - 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t) / (sigma * t)

    rng_is = random.Random(seed)
    vals_is: list[float] = []
    hits_is = 0

    for _ in range(n_paths):
        z_tilde = rng_is.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z_orig = z_tilde + theta_star * sq_t
        st = s0 * math.exp(drift_std + vol * z_orig)
        payoff = max(st - k, 0.0)
        if payoff > 0.0:
            hits_is += 1
        # Likelihood ratio L = exp(-theta* * W_T - 0.5 * (theta*)^2 * T)
        # where W_T = z_tilde * sqrt(T) + theta* * T
        lr = math.exp(-theta_star * sq_t * z_tilde - 0.5 * (theta_star**2) * t)
        vals_is.append(df * payoff * lr)

    mean_is = sum(vals_is) / n_paths
    var_is = sum((x - mean_is) ** 2 for x in vals_is) / (n_paths - 1)
    se_is = math.sqrt(var_is / n_paths)
    var_reduction_ratio = var_std / var_is if var_is > 0.0 else float("inf")

    print(f"exact analytical deep-OTM call price: {exact_price:.6e}")
    print(f"standard MC estimate: {mean_std:.6e} (se {se_std:.6e}, hits {hits_std}/{n_paths})")
    print(f"Girsanov IS estimate: {mean_is:.6e} (se {se_is:.6e}, hits {hits_is}/{n_paths})")
    print(f"empirical variance reduction ratio: {var_reduction_ratio:.1f}x")
    print("Girsanov IS inside 2*se of exact:", abs(mean_is - exact_price) <= 2.0 * se_is)
    print("variance reduction exceeds 50x:", var_reduction_ratio > 50.0)

For the deep-OTM option (\(K = 220\) on \(S_0 = 100\)), the exact Black-Scholes price is \(7.709169 \times 10^{-4}\). Standard Monte Carlo observed only 1 in-the-money path out of 20,000, producing a noisy estimate of \(8.015798 \times 10^{-5}\) with 100% relative standard error. Girsanov importance sampling centered the distribution at \(K\), recording 9,968 in-the-money paths. Its estimate \(7.705927 \times 10^{-4}\) (standard error \(7.724660 \times 10^{-6}\)) matches the analytical truth within 0.04 standard errors, achieving a 107.7x empirical variance reduction.

4Where the canon breaks

The analytical quanto drift adjustment \(r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\) assumes that the covariance between the asset and the exchange rate is continuous and deterministic. In reality, exchange rate distributions exhibit fat tails, stochastic volatility, and discrete jump devaluations (e.g. currency peg abandonments such as the Swiss franc decoupling from the euro on January 15, 2015). Under jump diffusions, the quanto adjustment acquires an additional jump-covariance expectation term \(\lambda \E[(\Delta S^f / S^f)(\Delta X / X)]\). If an analyst uses the continuous Brownian formula during currency crises, the quanto adjustment misses the correlation of simultaneous crash jumps, severely mispricing foreign asset exposure.

5Misconceptions

  1. “Because the payoff is in domestic dollars, the foreign asset grows at the domestic risk-free rate \(r_d\).”

    Under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), it is the domestic-currency value of the asset \(X_t S_t^f\) that grows at rate \(r_d\), not the foreign price \(S_t^f\). The foreign asset price grows at \(r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\), reflecting both the foreign rate \(r_f\) and the currency covariance.

  2. “A positive correlation between the foreign asset and the exchange rate increases the quanto call price.”

    When \(\rho > 0\), the quanto adjustment is negative (\(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X < 0\)), which lowers the expected terminal price \(F^{\text{quanto}}\) and strictly decreases the call option value.

  3. “Importance sampling changes the expected value of the option.”

    Multiplying each simulated payoff by the Radon–Nikodym likelihood ratio \(L = d\mathbb{Q} / d\mathbb{Q}^\theta\) exactly offsets the artificial drift shift, guaranteeing that the expectation remains mathematically unbiased.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Quanto drift calculation

A Japanese equity asset has volatility \(\sigma_S = 0.22\). The USD/JPY exchange rate has volatility \(\sigma_X = 0.12\). The correlation is \(\rho = -0.40\). If the Japanese risk-free rate is \(r_f = 0.005\) (0.5%), compute the quanto drift under the USD risk-neutral measure.

Solution

The quanto adjustment is \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = -(-0.40)(0.22)(0.12) = +0.01056\) (105.6 bps). The net drift under the domestic measure is: \(\mu_S^d = r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = 0.005 + 0.01056 = 0.01556\) (1.556%). Negative correlation increases the domestic drift of the foreign asset.

M2 · Quanto forward price evaluation

Let \(S_0^f = 100\), \(r_f = 0.03\), \(\rho = 0.50\), \(\sigma_S = 0.20\), \(\sigma_X = 0.15\), and horizon \(H = 2.0\) years. Compute the quanto forward price \(F^{\text{quanto}}(0, 2)\).

Solution

The covariance term is \(\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = (0.50)(0.20)(0.15) = 0.015\). The adjusted drift is \(r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = 0.03 - 0.015 = 0.015\). The quanto forward price is: \(F^{\text{quanto}}(0, 2) = S_0^f e^{(0.015)(2.0)} = 100 e^{0.03} \approx 100(1.03045) = 103.05\).

M3 · Girsanov likelihood ratio computation

In importance sampling with shift \(\theta = 1.20\) and \(H = 1.0\), what is the likelihood ratio weight \(L\) for a path where the shifted Brownian increment is \(\widetilde{W}_H = 0.5\)?

Solution

Formula: \(L = \exp(-\theta \widetilde{W}_H - \tfrac{1}{2}\theta^2 H)\). Substituting \(\theta = 1.20\), \(\widetilde{W}_H = 0.5\), and \(H = 1.0\): \(-\theta \widetilde{W}_H - \tfrac{1}{2}\theta^2 H = -(1.20)(0.5) - 0.5(1.44)(1.0) = -0.60 - 0.72 = -1.32\). The weight is \(L = e^{-1.32} \approx 0.2671\).

P1 · Invariance of foreign asset volatility

Prove that the volatility of \(S_t^f\) under the domestic risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) is identical to its volatility \(\sigma_S\) under the foreign measure \(\mathbb{Q}^f\).

Solution

The measure change from \(\mathbb{Q}^f\) to \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) is an equivalent change of measure with density process \(Z_t = \frac{d\mathbb{Q}^d}{d\mathbb{Q}^f}\big|_{\mathcal{F}_t} = \frac{e^{r_d t} / 1}{(X_t e^{r_f t}) / X_0}\). By Girsanov's theorem and the pathwise property of quadratic variation (Problem P2 in Lesson 1), \([S^f, S^f]_t\) is identical under both measures: \(d\langle \log S^f, \log S^f \rangle_t = \sigma_S^2 dt\). Because quadratic variation is invariant under equivalent probability measures, the diffusion volatility of \(S_t^f\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) is identically \(\sigma_S\).

P2 · Zero-correlation quanto limit

Prove that when \(\rho = 0\), the quanto call price equals a standard Black-Scholes call discounted at the domestic rate \(r_d\) with forward price growing at the foreign rate \(r_f\).

Solution

When \(\rho = 0\), the quanto adjustment \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = 0\). The quanto forward price simplifies to \(F^{\text{quanto}}(0, H) = S_0^f e^{r_f H}\). Substituting into Theorem 1.3: \(V_0 = e^{-r_d H}[S_0^f e^{r_f H}\Phi(d_1) - K \Phi(d_2)]\), where \(d_1 = \frac{\log(S_0^f / K) + (r_f + \sigma_S^2/2)H}{\sigma_S \sqrt{H}}\). This is exactly the Garman–Kohlhagen / Black-Scholes formula with underlying growth \(r_f\) and discounting \(r_d\).

P3 · Optimal Girsanov shift for importance sampling

Prove that for a linear payoff \(\indic_{B_H > a}\), the variance of the Girsanov importance sampling estimator is strictly minimized by choosing \(\theta = a / H\).

Solution

Under measure \(\mathbb{Q}^\theta\) with shift \(\theta\), the estimator is \(Y = \indic_{\widetilde{B}_H + \theta H > a} e^{-\theta \widetilde{B}_H - \frac{1}{2}\theta^2 H}\). Its second moment under \(\mathbb{Q}^\theta\) is: \(\E^{\mathbb{Q}^\theta}[Y^2] = \E^\Prob[\indic_{B_H > a} e^{-\theta B_H - \frac{1}{2}\theta^2 H}]\). Evaluating the Gaussian integral: \(\int_a^\infty e^{-\theta x - \frac{1}{2}\theta^2 H} \frac{e^{-x^2 / (2H)}}{\sqrt{2\pi H}} dx = \int_a^\infty \frac{e^{-(x + \theta H)^2 / (2H)}}{\sqrt{2\pi H}} dx \cdot e^{\theta^2 H / 2}\). Differentiating with respect to \(\theta\) and setting to zero yields \(\theta^* = a / H\), centering the shifted distribution mode exactly at the threshold \(a\).

Empirical

E1 · Nikkei quanto futures adjustment

The Nikkei 225 USD-denominated futures contract trades on the CME. If Japanese rates are 0.1%, US rates are 4.5%, Nikkei volatility is 20%, USD/JPY volatility is 10%, and correlation is \(-0.35\), calculate the annual percentage drift difference between the domestic Nikkei index and the USD quanto future.

Solution

The quanto adjustment is \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = -(-0.35)(0.20)(0.10) = +0.0070\) (+70 bps). The domestic Japanese index forward grows at \(r_f = 0.1\%\), while the quanto futures forward grows at \(r_f - \rho \sigma_S \sigma_X = 0.1\% + 0.70\% = 0.80\%\). The difference is +70 bps per year due to the negative correlation between the Japanese stock market and the dollar/yen exchange rate.

E2 · Importance sampling efficiency vs path count

In Lab 1, standard Monte Carlo with 20,000 paths recorded 1 hit, giving an estimated standard error of \(8.0 \times 10^{-5}\). How many standard Monte Carlo paths would be required to match the Girsanov importance sampling standard error of \(7.7 \times 10^{-6}\)?

Solution

Monte Carlo standard error scales as \(1 / \sqrt{N}\), so variance scales as \(1 / N\). The ratio of standard errors is \((8.0 \times 10^{-5}) / (7.7 \times 10^{-6}) \approx 10.39\). The required increase in path count is \((10.39)^2 \approx 108\). Standard Monte Carlo would require approximately \(20{,}000 \times 108 \approx 2{,}160{,}000\) paths to achieve the precision that Girsanov importance sampling delivers in 20,000 paths.

E3 · Quanto put option sensitivity

If correlation \(\rho\) increases from \(-0.4\) to \(+0.4\), explain how a quanto European put option price behaves.

Solution

As \(\rho\) increases, the quanto adjustment \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\) decreases, shifting the expected foreign asset price \(F^{\text{quanto}}\) lower. For a put option with payoff \((K - S_H^f)^+\), a lower underlying price increases the probability and payoff of expiring in the money. Therefore, unlike a call, a quanto put option price increases as correlation \(\rho\) increases.

Research

R1 · Siegel's paradox and exchange rate symmetry

Explain Siegel's paradox regarding expected exchange rate movements and how change of measure resolves it.

Solution

Siegel's (1972) paradox observes that if the exchange rate \(X_t\) is a martingale under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), then by Jensen's inequality, \(\E^{\mathbb{Q}^d}[1 / X_t] > 1 / X_0\), seemingly creating an arbitrage for foreign investors. The paradox is resolved by recognizing that the foreign investor operates under a different probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}^f\). By Girsanov's theorem with density \(d\mathbb{Q}^f / d\mathbb{Q}^d = (X_t / X_0)e^{-(r_d - r_f)t}\), under \(\mathbb{Q}^f\) the inverted exchange rate \(1/X_t\) is indeed a martingale with growth rate \(r_f - r_d\).

R2 · Composite FX options vs Quanto options

Contrast a quanto option with a composite (or flex-currency) foreign equity option whose payoff is converted at the prevailing spot rate \(X_H (S_H^f - K)^+\).

Solution

A composite option converts the payoff at the terminal exchange rate \(X_H\), so the domestic payoff is \((X_H S_H^f - K X_H)^+\). By defining the domestic stock price \(S_t^d = X_t S_t^f\), this is simply an exchange option between the domestic stock \(S^d\) and \(K\) units of foreign currency \(K X_t\), priced using Margrabe's formula without quanto drift shifts. A quanto option fixes the exchange rate, decoupling the payout currency from the asset currency.

R3 · Cross-currency Libor market model with quanto spreads

How do quanto adjustments appear in cross-currency interest rate swaptions where forward Libor/SOFR in one currency is paid in another?

Solution

In a cross-currency Libor market model, foreign forward rate \(L_k^f(t)\) is a martingale under the foreign forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{f, T_{k+1}}\). To price a quanto swaption settled in domestic currency, the measure is transformed to the domestic forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{d, T_{k+1}}\). The drift of \(L_k^f(t)\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) acquires a quanto covariance term \(-\rho_{L, X} \sigma_{L_k^f} \sigma_X\), altering forward swap rate dynamics.

  6. Under the domestic risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^d\), what is the drift of the exchange rate \(X_t\)?

    Options

    Theorem 1.1: Uncovered interest parity under \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) requires the exchange rate to drift at \(r_d - r_f\).

  7. What happens to the quanto call price when the correlation \(\rho\) between asset and FX rate is zero?

    Options

    Problem P2: When \(\rho = 0\), the quanto adjustment vanishes, reducing to standard Garman–Kohlhagen valuation.

  8. If the correlation \(\rho = 0\), what is the quanto drift adjustment \(-\rho \sigma_S \sigma_X\)?

    When \(\rho = 0\), the adjustment is \(-0 \cdot \sigma_S \sigma_X = 0\).
