Numeraire Toolkit

In multi-asset derivative pricing and cross-currency risk management, choosing the traditional money-market account as numeraire frequently creates intractable pricing problems involving coupled diffusions or multi-dimensional numerical integration. By selecting an appropriate tradable asset as numeraire—such as the underlying deliverable asset in an exchange option or the domestic money market in a quanto—the pricing equation simplifies to a univariate expectation. This project develops a production-grade software toolkit implementing closed-form solutions and Monte Carlo engines alongside a rigorous mathematical derivations workbook.

Lab 1 · Worked example: Margrabe exchange option pricing and Monte Carlo verification on seed 0

Under asset 2 as numeraire, the exchange option price is given by the Margrabe (1978) closed form V_0 = S_1(0) e^{-q_1 T} \Phi(d_1) - S_2(0) e^{-q_2 T} \Phi(d_2). On the seed 0 SIMULATED instance, the analytical price is 15.538676. A 2D Monte Carlo simulation across 5,000 paths produces 15.637391 (standard error 0.305782), agreeing within two standard errors.

"""Lab 1 · Worked example: Margrabe exchange option pricing and Monte Carlo verification on seed 0.

An exchange option gives the holder the right to exchange asset 2 for asset 1, payoff max(S_1(T) - S_2(T), 0).
On seed 0 (SIMULATED), parameters are:
    S_1 = 113.777, S_2 = 110.318, sigma_1 = 0.234, sigma_2 = 0.202, rho = 0.014,
    q_1 = 0.026, q_2 = 0.041, T = 0.955, r = 0.039.
By Margrabe (1978), using asset 2 as numeraire yields relative volatility:
    sigma_M = sqrt(sigma_1^2 - 2 * rho * sigma_1 * sigma_2 + sigma_2^2) = 0.306917
and closed-form price V_0 = 15.538676.
This lab computes the exact closed-form price, simulates 5,000 bivariate paths under the risk-neutral measure Q,
and confirms that the simulated price 15.637391 matches the analytical price within 2 standard errors (se = 0.305782).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def main() -> None:
    # Seed 0 instance parameters (SIMULATED)
    s1 = 113.777
    s2 = 110.318
    sig1 = 0.234
    sig2 = 0.202
    rho = 0.014
    q1 = 0.026
    q2 = 0.041
    t = 0.955
    r = 0.039
    n_paths = 5000
    seed = 0

    # Margrabe closed-form price
    sig_m = math.sqrt(sig1**2 - 2.0 * rho * sig1 * sig2 + sig2**2)
    f1 = s1 * math.exp(-q1 * t)
    f2 = s2 * math.exp(-q2 * t)
    sq_t = math.sqrt(t)
    d1 = (math.log(f1 / f2) + 0.5 * (sig_m**2) * t) / (sig_m * sq_t)
    d2 = d1 - sig_m * sq_t
    exact_price = f1 * norm_cdf(d1) - f2 * norm_cdf(d2)

    # 2D Monte Carlo simulation under Q
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    drift1 = (r - q1 - 0.5 * (sig1**2)) * t
    vol1 = sig1 * sq_t
    drift2 = (r - q2 - 0.5 * (sig2**2)) * t
    vol2 = sig2 * sq_t
    df = math.exp(-r * t)

    payoffs: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(n_paths):
        z1 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z2 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        w1 = z1
        w2 = rho * z1 + math.sqrt(1.0 - rho**2) * z2

        s1_t = s1 * math.exp(drift1 + vol1 * w1)
        s2_t = s2 * math.exp(drift2 + vol2 * w2)
        payoffs.append(df * max(s1_t - s2_t, 0.0))

    mc_price = sum(payoffs) / n_paths
    var_mc = sum((x - mc_price) ** 2 for x in payoffs) / (n_paths - 1)
    se_mc = math.sqrt(var_mc / n_paths)

    print(f"seed 0 parameters: S1={s1:.3f}, S2={s2:.3f}, sig1={sig1:.3f}, sig2={sig2:.3f}, rho={rho:.3f}")
    print(f"Margrabe volatility sigma_M: {sig_m:.6f}")
    print(f"analytical Margrabe price: {exact_price:.6f}")
    print(f"Monte Carlo price: {mc_price:.6f} (se {se_mc:.6f})")
    print(f"absolute difference: {abs(mc_price - exact_price):.6f}")
    print("simulation within 2*se of analytical price:", abs(mc_price - exact_price) <= 2.0 * se_mc)

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
In exotic equity and foreign exchange derivatives desks, structuring and pricing complex multi-asset contracts requires moving fluently between probability measures. An exchange option gives the holder the right to swap one risky asset for another at maturity, while a quanto call provides exposure to a foreign asset with payoffs settled in domestic currency at a pre-determined exchange rate. Solving these pricing equations under the standard domestic money-market measure requires evaluating multi-dimensional stochastic integrals. By changing to the appropriate asset numeraire, both models collapse to transparent, analytical Black-Scholes-type formulas. You must mathematically derive the pricing formulas and build a verified computational engine numeraire_toolkit.py.
Inputs
A deterministic instance dictionary generated by make_instance(seed) carrying parameters s1, s2, sig1, sig2, rho, q1, q2, t, r, s_f, x0, k_quanto, r_d, r_f, sig_s, sig_x, rho_sx, n_paths, and seed. All instance data is labeled SIMULATED.

Margrabe Exchange Option Derivation via Change of Numeraire

Consider two assets \(S_1, S_2\) following geometric Brownian motion under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\): \(dS_i(t) = (r - q_i) S_i(t) dt + \sigma_i S_i(t) dW_i(t)\) with correlation \(d\langle W_1, W_2 \rangle_t = \rho dt\). The terminal payoff at maturity \(T\) is \((S_1(T) - S_2(T))^+\). We choose the dividend-reinvested second asset \(N_t = S_2(t) e^{q_2 t}\) as numeraire. Under the numeraire measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}\), the Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to \(\mathbb{Q}\) is \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}}{d\mathbb{Q}}\big|_{\mathcal{F}_t} = \frac{S_2(t) e^{q_2 t} / S_2(0)}{e^{rt}}\). By Geman, El Karoui & Rochet (1995), the relative price \(Z_t = \frac{S_1(t) e^{q_1 t}}{S_2(t) e^{q_2 t}}\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}\)-martingale. Applying Itô's formula to \(Z_t\), its diffusion is \(\sigma_M = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2}\). The pricing formula evaluates to \(V_0 = S_2(0) e^{-q_2 T} \E^{\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}}[(Z_T - 1)^+] = S_1(0) e^{-q_1 T} \Phi(d_1) - S_2(0) e^{-q_2 T} \Phi(d_2)\), where \(d_1 = \frac{\log(F_1/F_2) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_M^2 T}{\sigma_M \sqrt{T}}\) and \(d_2 = d_1 - \sigma_M \sqrt{T}\).

  • Meets C1 level 4: explicitly constructs the numeraire measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}\) with its Radon-Nikodym derivative and states the martingality of relative prices.
  • Meets C2 level 4: details the Itô expansion of the relative price process and proves the effective volatility formula.
  • Meets C3 level 4: provides exact closed-form numbers and standard errors on seed 0.
  • Meets C4 level 4: proves homogeneity of degree 1 and put-call exchange symmetry analytically.
  • Meets C5 level 4: adheres strictly to program notation and full typing.

Exchange Option and Quanto Work

For Margrabe, we divide by \(S_2\) so the strike becomes 1, and the volatility is \(\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\). Putting this into Black-Scholes gives the answer 15.54. Running Monte Carlo gave around 15.6, which matches.

For quanto, foreign growth is \(r_f - \rho\sigma_S\sigma_X\). We plug this into the call formula and discount at \(r_d\), getting 15.81.
