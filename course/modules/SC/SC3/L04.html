Change of Numeraire and the Generalized Valuation Equation

Derivative payoffs frequently combine distinct assets, interest rates, or currencies. Measuring value solely in units of the domestic cash account complicates valuation by coupling stochastic discounting with asset diffusion. By generalizing the definition of a numeraire to any strictly positive tradable asset, Geman, El Karoui & Rochet (1995) showed that each numeraire induces a natural equivalent martingale measure. Changing numeraire decouples stochastic rates, transforms complex options into vanilla expectations, and provides the probabilistic intuition behind Black-Scholes formula terms.

1Numeraires and Relative Prices

Definition 1.1 (Numeraire)

A numeraire is a strictly positive, non-dividend-paying price process \((N_t)_{t \in [0, H]}\) of a tradable financial asset. For any other tradable asset \(S_t\), its relative price (or normalized price) with respect to \(N\) is defined as:

\[ S_t^{(N)} \coloneqq \frac{S_t}{N_t}. \]
Theorem 1.2 (Change of Numeraire Theorem)

Assumptions: (A1) There exists a numeraire \(M_t > 0\) and an equivalent martingale measure \(\mathbb{Q}^M \sim \Prob\) such that for every tradable asset \(S_t\), the relative price \(S_t / M_t\) is an \((\mathcal{F}_t, \mathbb{Q}^M)\)-martingale; (A2) \(N_t > 0\) is an alternative valid numeraire tradable in the market.

Statement: The probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}^N\) defined on \(\mathcal{F}_H\) by the Radon–Nikodym derivative:

\[ \frac{d\mathbb{Q}^N}{d\mathbb{Q}^M}\Bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_t} \coloneqq \frac{N_t / N_0}{M_t / M_0} \]

is an equivalent martingale measure associated with numeraire \(N_t\). Under \(\mathbb{Q}^N\), the relative price \(S_t / N_t\) is a continuous martingale for every tradable asset \(S_t\). This principle is the change of numeraire theorem due to Geman, El Karoui & Rochet (1995).

Proof

First, verify that \(\mathbb{Q}^N\) is a valid probability measure. The density process is \(Z_t = \frac{N_t / N_0}{M_t / M_0} = \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{N_t}{M_t}\). Because \(N_t\) is a tradable asset price, assumption A1 guarantees that \(N_t / M_t\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^M\)-martingale. Hence \(Z_t\) is a strictly positive \(\mathbb{Q}^M\)-martingale with \(Z_0 = \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{N_0}{M_0} = 1\). Thus \(\E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}[Z_H] = 1\), defining an equivalent probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}^N \sim \mathbb{Q}^M\).

Second, let \(S_t\) be any tradable asset. To prove that \(S_t / N_t\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^N\)-martingale, choose \(s \le t\). By the abstract Bayes formula (Theorem 1.7 in Lesson 1):

\[ \E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}\left[ \frac{S_t}{N_t} \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s \right] = \frac{\E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}\left[ \frac{S_t}{N_t} Z_t \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s \right]}{Z_s}. \]

Substitute \(Z_t = \frac{M_0}{N_0}\frac{N_t}{M_t}\) into the numerator:

\[ \frac{S_t}{N_t} Z_t = \frac{S_t}{N_t} \left( \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{N_t}{M_t} \right) = \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{S_t}{M_t}. \]

Because \(S_t / M_t\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^M\)-martingale under assumption A1:

\[ \E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}\left[ \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{S_t}{M_t} \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s \right] = \frac{M_0}{N_0} \frac{S_s}{M_s}. \]

Dividing by \(Z_s = \frac{M_0}{N_0}\frac{N_s}{M_s}\):

\[ \frac{\frac{M_0}{N_0}\frac{S_s}{M_s}}{\frac{M_0}{N_0}\frac{N_s}{M_s}} = \frac{S_s}{N_s}. \]

Hence \(\E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}[S_t / N_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = S_s / N_s\), establishing the martingale property.

2The Generalized Valuation Equation

Theorem 1.3 (Numeraire Invariance of Pricing)

Assumptions: (A1) A contingent claim delivers payoff \(V_H\) at maturity \(H\); (A2) \(M\) and \(N\) are valid numeraires with equivalent measures \(\mathbb{Q}^M\) and \(\mathbb{Q}^N\).

Statement: The arbitrage-free price \(V_0\) satisfies the generalized valuation equation:

\[ V_0 = N_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}\left[ \frac{V_H}{N_H} \right] = M_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}\left[ \frac{V_H}{M_H} \right]. \]
Proof

Because the claim price process \(V_t\) is a tradable asset, Theorem 1.2 guarantees that \(V_t / N_t\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^N\)-martingale and \(V_t / M_t\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}^M\)-martingale. At \(t = 0\):

\[ \frac{V_0}{N_0} = \E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}\left[ \frac{V_H}{N_H} \right] \implies V_0 = N_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}\left[ \frac{V_H}{N_H} \right]. \]

By abstract Bayes: \(N_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^N}[V_H / N_H] = N_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}[(V_H / N_H) Z_H] = N_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}[(V_H / N_H) \frac{N_H / N_0}{M_H / M_0}] = M_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^M}[V_H / M_H]\).

3The Stock Numeraire and Black-Scholes Decomposition

Consider a standard European call option with payoff \(V_H = (S_H - K)^+ = (S_H - K)\indic_{S_H > K}\). Under the traditional money-market numeraire \(M_t = e^{rt}\), the price is:

\[ V_0 = e^{-rH} \E^\mathbb{Q}[(S_H - K)\indic_{S_H > K}] = e^{-rH}\E^\mathbb{Q}[S_H \indic_{S_H > K}] - K e^{-rH} \E^\mathbb{Q}[\indic_{S_H > K}]. \]

The second term is simply \(K e^{-rH} \mathbb{Q}(S_H > K) = K e^{-rH}\Phi(d_2)\), where \(\Phi(d_2)\) is the risk-neutral probability of expiring in the money.

Theorem 1.4 (Stock Numeraire Decomposition)

Assumptions: (A1) \(S_t\) follows geometric Brownian motion with volatility \(\sigma > 0\); (A2) The measure \(\mathbb{Q}^S\) is defined with numeraire \(N_t = S_t\).

Statement: The first term in the call price equals \(S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) = S_0 \Phi(d_1)\), yielding the dual-measure representation:

\[ V_0 = S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) - K e^{-rH} \mathbb{Q}(S_H > K) = S_0 \Phi(d_1) - K e^{-rH}\Phi(d_2), \]

where \(d_1 = \frac{\log(S_0 / K) + (r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)H}{\sigma \sqrt{H}}\) and \(d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{H}\).

Proof

Choose \(N_t = S_t\) as numeraire. The Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to \(\mathbb{Q}\) is:

\[ Z_H = \frac{S_H / S_0}{e^{rH}} = \frac{S_H}{S_0 e^{rH}}. \]

Evaluating the first term via the generalized valuation equation under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\):

\[ e^{-rH}\E^\mathbb{Q}[S_H \indic_{S_H > K}] = S_0 \E^\mathbb{Q}\left[ \frac{S_H}{S_0 e^{rH}} \indic_{S_H > K} \right] = S_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^S}[\indic_{S_H > K}] = S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K). \]

Under \(\mathbb{Q}\), \(S_t = S_0 \exp((r - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t + \sigma W_t)\). The density is \(Z_t = \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t + \sigma W_t) = \mathcal{E}(\sigma \cdot W)_t\). By Girsanov's theorem, \(W_t^S \coloneqq W_t - \sigma t\) is a standard Brownian motion under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\). Substituting \(W_t = W_t^S + \sigma t\) into the stock dynamics:

\[ S_t = S_0 \exp\left( (r - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t + \sigma(W_t^S + \sigma t) \right) = S_0 \exp\left( (r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t + \sigma W_t^S \right). \]

The event \(S_H > K\) is equivalent to \(\log(S_H / S_0) > \log(K / S_0)\):

\[ (r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)H + \sigma W_H^S > \log(K / S_0) \iff W_H^S > \frac{\log(K / S_0) - (r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)H}{\sigma} = -\frac{\log(S_0 / K) + (r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)H}{\sigma}. \]

Dividing by \(\sqrt{H}\), the standard normal variable \(Z^S = W_H^S / \sqrt{H}\) must exceed \(-d_1\). By symmetry of the standard normal distribution, \(\mathbb{Q}^S(Z^S > -d_1) = \Phi(d_1)\). Thus the term equals \(S_0 \Phi(d_1)\).

Lab 1 · Numeraire invariance: money-market versus stock numeraire

By Geman, El Karoui & Rochet (1995), arbitrage-free derivative pricing is invariant to the choice of numeraire. For a European call with S_0 = 100, K = 100, r = 0.05, sigma = 0.20, T = 1.0: 1. Under money-market numeraire M_t = exp(r*t), the pricing formula is V_0 = exp(-r*T) * E^Q[(S_T - K)^+], where log(S_T / S_0) ~ N((r - sigma^2/2)*T, sigma^2*T). 2. Under stock numeraire N_t = S_t, the measure is Q^S with density dQ^S / dQ = S_T / (S_0 * exp(r*T)). Under Q^S, log(S_T / S_0) ~ N((r + sigma^2/2)*T, sigma^2*T), and the pricing formula is V_0 = S_0 * E^{Q^S}[(1 - K / S_T)^+]. """Lab 1 · Numeraire invariance: money-market versus stock numeraire.

By Geman, El Karoui & Rochet (1995), arbitrage-free derivative pricing is invariant to the choice
of numeraire. For a European call with S_0 = 100, K = 100, r = 0.05, sigma = 0.20, T = 1.0:
1. Under money-market numeraire M_t = exp(r*t), the pricing formula is:
   V_0 = exp(-r*T) * E^Q[(S_T - K)^+], where log(S_T / S_0) ~ N((r - sigma^2/2)*T, sigma^2*T).
2. Under stock numeraire N_t = S_t, the measure is Q^S with density dQ^S / dQ = S_T / (S_0 * exp(r*T)).
   Under Q^S, log(S_T / S_0) ~ N((r + sigma^2/2)*T, sigma^2*T), and the pricing formula is:
   V_0 = S_0 * E^{Q^S}[(1 - K / S_T)^+].
This lab simulates 20,000 paths (SIMULATED) under both numeraires and verifies that both Monte Carlo
estimates match the Black-Scholes analytical price within 3 standard errors.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def bs_call_price(s0: float, k: float, r: float, sigma: float, t: float) -> float:
    sq_t = math.sqrt(t)
    d1 = (math.log(s0 / k) + (r + 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t) / (sigma * sq_t)
    d2 = d1 - sigma * sq_t
    return s0 * norm_cdf(d1) - k * math.exp(-r * t) * norm_cdf(d2)


def main() -> None:
    seed = 42
    s0 = 100.0
    k = 100.0
    r = 0.05
    sigma = 0.20
    t = 1.0
    n_paths = 20000

    exact_price = bs_call_price(s0, k, r, sigma, t)
    sq_t = math.sqrt(t)

    # 1. Money-market numeraire: Q measure
    rng_q = random.Random(seed)
    drift_q = (r - 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t
    vol = sigma * sq_t
    df = math.exp(-r * t)

    payoffs_q: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(n_paths):
        z = rng_q.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        st = s0 * math.exp(drift_q + vol * z)
        payoffs_q.append(df * max(st - k, 0.0))

    mean_q = sum(payoffs_q) / n_paths
    var_q = sum((x - mean_q) ** 2 for x in payoffs_q) / (n_paths - 1)
    se_q = math.sqrt(var_q / n_paths)

    # 2. Stock numeraire: Q^S measure
    rng_s = random.Random(seed)
    drift_s = (r + 0.5 * (sigma**2)) * t

    payoffs_s: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(n_paths):
        z = rng_s.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        st = s0 * math.exp(drift_s + vol * z)
        # Relative payoff: (S_T - K)^+ / S_T = (1 - K / S_T)^+
        rel_payoff = max(1.0 - k / st, 0.0)
        payoffs_s.append(s0 * rel_payoff)

    mean_s = sum(payoffs_s) / n_paths
    var_s = sum((x - mean_s) ** 2 for x in payoffs_s) / (n_paths - 1)
    se_s = math.sqrt(var_s / n_paths)

    print(f"exact Black-Scholes price: {exact_price:.4f}")
    print(f"money-market numeraire (Q): {mean_q:.4f} (se {se_q:.4f})")
    print(f"stock numeraire (Q^S): {mean_s:.4f} (se {se_s:.4f})")
    print("Q estimate inside 3*se:", abs(mean_q - exact_price) <= 3.0 * se_q)
    print("Q^S estimate inside 3*se:", abs(mean_s - exact_price) <= 3.0 * se_s)
    print("numeraire invariance |mean_q - mean_s| <= 3*(se_q + se_s):", abs(mean_q - mean_s) <= 3.0 * (se_q + se_s))


main()

money-market numeraire (Q): 10.4047 (se 0.1032)
stock numeraire (Q^S): 10.4297 (se 0.0815)
Q estimate inside 3*se: True
Q^S estimate inside 3*se: True
numeraire invariance |mean_q - mean_s| <= 3*(se_q + se_s): True

Under the money-market numeraire (\(\mathbb{Q}\)), the estimate is 10.4047 (standard error 0.1032). Under the stock numeraire (\(\mathbb{Q}^S\)), the relative payoff \((1 - K/S_H)^+\) yields 10.4297 (standard error 0.0815). Both estimates agree with the exact Black-Scholes price 10.4506 within sampling error, verifying numeraire invariance.

4Where the canon breaks

A process can serve as a numeraire only if it remains strictly positive at all times (\(N_t > 0\) almost surely). Assets that can reach zero or negative values—such as defaultable corporate bonds, physical commodities subject to storage cost spikes (e.g. WTI crude oil settling at -$37.63 on April 20, 2020), or options that expire out of the money—cannot serve as numeraires. If \(N_t = 0\) with positive probability, dividing by \(N_t\) introduces a singularity, the relative price \(S_t / N_t\) is undefined, and the Radon–Nikodym derivative \(Z_t = \frac{N_t / N_0}{M_t / M_0}\) fails to define an equivalent probability measure.

5Misconceptions

  1. “The term \(\Phi(d_1)\) in the Black-Scholes formula is just a mathematical weight without probabilistic interpretation.”

    Under the stock numeraire measure \(\mathbb{Q}^S\), \(\Phi(d_1)\) is the exact probability of the call option expiring in the money: \(\Phi(d_1) = \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K)\). The entire Black-Scholes formula is the difference between two probabilities of exercise under two distinct numeraires: stock and cash.

  2. “Changing numeraire gives a different option price.”

    The price of a financial contract is an observable dollar quantity that cannot depend on the numeraire chosen by the analyst. Changing numeraire is a mathematical coordinate transformation that changes the calculation method while leaving the resulting dollar price identical.

  3. “Any stochastic process can be chosen as a numeraire.”

    A valid numeraire must satisfy two essential conditions: (1) it must be the price of a strictly positive tradable asset, and (2) it must not pay intermediate cash flows unless those cash flows are continuously reinvested.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Stock numeraire drift shift

Let \(S_t\) follow geometric Brownian motion with volatility \(\sigma = 0.30\) and risk-free rate \(r = 0.05\). Determine the drift of \(\log S_t\) under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) and under the stock numeraire measure \(\mathbb{Q}^S\).

Solution

Under \(\mathbb{Q}\), the SDE is \(dS_t / S_t = r dt + \sigma dW_t\). By Itô's lemma, \(d\log S_t = (r - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)dt + \sigma dW_t\). The drift is \(r - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 = 0.05 - 0.5(0.09) = 0.05 - 0.045 = 0.005\). Under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\), by Girsanov's theorem, \(W_t^S = W_t - \sigma t\) is a Brownian motion. Substituting gives \(d\log S_t = (r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)dt + \sigma dW_t^S\). The drift under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\) is \(r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 = 0.05 + 0.045 = 0.095\).

M2 · Radon–Nikodym derivative between asset numeraires

Two assets \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) follow geometric Brownian motion with volatilities \(\sigma_1, \sigma_2\) and correlation \(\rho\). Write the explicit Radon–Nikodym derivative \(d\mathbb{Q}^{S_1}/d\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}\) on \(\mathcal{F}_H\).

Solution

By Theorem 1.2, the Radon–Nikodym derivative between two numeraire measures is: \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{S_1}}{d\mathbb{Q}^{S_2}}\big|_{\mathcal{F}_H} = \frac{S_1(H) / S_1(0)}{S_2(H) / S_2(0)} = \frac{S_2(0)}{S_1(0)} \frac{S_1(H)}{S_2(H)}\). This density is proportional to the relative price ratio \(S_1(H) / S_2(H)\) at horizon \(H\).

M3 · Cash-or-nothing digital option under numeraire change

A digital option pays $100 if \(S_H > K\) at maturity \(H\). Price the option under \(\mathbb{Q}\) and identify the probability term.

Solution

Payoff is \(V_H = 100 \indic_{S_H > K}\). Under the money-market numeraire: \(V_0 = e^{-rH}\E^\mathbb{Q}[100 \indic_{S_H > K}] = 100 e^{-rH}\mathbb{Q}(S_H > K) = 100 e^{-rH}\Phi(d_2)\), where \(d_2 = \frac{\log(S_0/K) + (r - \sigma^2/2)H}{\sigma \sqrt{H}}\).

P1 · General transitivity of numeraire change

Prove the chain rule for three numeraires \(K, M, N\): \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^N}{d\mathbb{Q}^K} = \frac{d\mathbb{Q}^N}{d\mathbb{Q}^M} \frac{d\mathbb{Q}^M}{d\mathbb{Q}^K}\).

Solution

By Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2: \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^N}{d\mathbb{Q}^M} = \frac{N_H / N_0}{M_H / M_0}\) and \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^M}{d\mathbb{Q}^K} = \frac{M_H / M_0}{K_H / K_0}\). Multiplying these expressions: \(\frac{N_H / N_0}{M_H / M_0} \cdot \frac{M_H / M_0}{K_H / K_0} = \frac{N_H / N_0}{K_H / K_0}\). By Theorem 1.2, this ratio is identically \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^N}{d\mathbb{Q}^K}\), proving transitivity.

P2 · Asset-or-nothing call under stock numeraire

An asset-or-nothing call option pays \(S_H\) if \(S_H > K\) and 0 otherwise. Prove using the change of numeraire that its price is \(S_0 \Phi(d_1)\).

Solution

Payoff is \(V_H = S_H \indic_{S_H > K}\). Using the stock \(S\) as numeraire (\(N_t = S_t\)), the relative payoff is \(V_H / N_H = (S_H \indic_{S_H > K}) / S_H = \indic_{S_H > K}\). Applying the generalized valuation equation (Theorem 1.3): \(V_0 = S_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^S}[V_H / S_H] = S_0 \E^{\mathbb{Q}^S}[\indic_{S_H > K}] = S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K)\). By Theorem 1.4, under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\), \(\log(S_H / S_0) \sim \Normal((r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)H, \sigma^2 H)\), so \(\mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) = \Phi(d_1)\). The price is \(S_0 \Phi(d_1)\).

P3 · Put-call parity via numeraire decomposition

Derive European put-call parity \(C_0 - P_0 = S_0 - K e^{-rH}\) directly from the dual-measure representations of calls and puts.

Solution

For a call: \(C_0 = S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) - K e^{-rH}\mathbb{Q}(S_H > K)\). For a put: \(P_0 = K e^{-rH}\mathbb{Q}(S_H \le K) - S_0 \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H \le K)\). Subtracting: \(C_0 - P_0 = S_0 [\mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) + \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H \le K)] - K e^{-rH}[\mathbb{Q}(S_H > K) + \mathbb{Q}(S_H \le K)]\). Because probabilities over complementary events sum to 1 under any probability measure: \(\mathbb{Q}^S(S_H > K) + \mathbb{Q}^S(S_H \le K) = 1\) and \(\mathbb{Q}(S_H > K) + \mathbb{Q}(S_H \le K) = 1\). Therefore \(C_0 - P_0 = S_0(1) - K e^{-rH}(1) = S_0 - K e^{-rH}\).

Empirical

E1 · Moneyness and dual measure probabilities

For \(S_0 = 100, K = 100, r = 0.05, \sigma = 0.20, H = 1.0\), compute \(\Phi(d_1)\) and \(\Phi(d_2)\). What is the empirical probability gap \(\Phi(d_1) - \Phi(d_2)\)?

Solution

Here \(\sqrt{H} = 1\). Then \(d_1 = \frac{\log(1) + (0.05 + 0.02)1}{0.20} = \frac{0.07}{0.20} = 0.35\). And \(d_2 = 0.35 - 0.20 = 0.15\). Using the standard normal CDF: \(\Phi(0.35) \approx 0.6368\) and \(\Phi(0.15) \approx 0.5596\). The gap is \(\Phi(d_1) - \Phi(d_2) = 0.6368 - 0.5596 = 0.0772\). Under the stock measure, the probability of expiring in the money is 7.72 percentage points higher than under the money-market measure because the stock numeraire assigns higher probability mass to paths where \(S_H\) is large.

E2 · Monte Carlo variance reduction under stock numeraire

Compare the variance of the payoff estimator \((S_H - K)^+ / e^{rH}\) under \(\mathbb{Q}\) versus \(S_0 (1 - K/S_H)^+\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\) for deep ITM calls.

Solution

For a deep in-the-money call (\(S_0 \gg K\)), under \(\mathbb{Q}^S\) the relative payoff is \(S_0(1 - K/S_H)^+ \approx S_0(1 - 0) = S_0\), which is nearly constant. Consequently, the sample variance of the estimator under the stock numeraire approaches zero for deep ITM options, whereas under \(\mathbb{Q}\) the payoff \(e^{-rH}(S_H - K)\) retains the full variance of \(S_H\). Changing numeraire acts as an analytical variance reduction technique.

E3 · Negative commodity prices and numeraire breakdown

On April 20, 2020, WTI crude oil futures settled at -$37.63. Explain why WTI futures could not be used as a numeraire for pricing oil crack spreads.

Solution

A numeraire must remain strictly positive almost surely: \(N_t > 0\). When WTI went negative, the relative price \(S_t / N_t\) changed signs, and at \(N_t = 0\) the relative price was undefined (\(\pm \infty\)). The Radon–Nikodym derivative \(Z_t = N_t / (N_0 e^{rt})\) became negative, which violates the requirement that probability measures must assign non-negative weights to all events.

Research

R1 · Benchmark approach of Platen

Explain Eckhard Platen's benchmark approach to quantitative finance and its relationship to the growth-optimal portfolio numeraire.

Solution

Platen's benchmark approach selects the growth-optimal portfolio (GOP)—the portfolio that maximizes expected log utility—as the universal numeraire (the benchmark). Under the physical measure \(\Prob\), all benchmarked asset prices \(S_t / S_t^{\text{GOP}}\) are local martingales (or supermartingales), completely eliminating the need to construct an equivalent risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\). This framework permits pricing in markets where equivalent martingale measures do not exist (e.g. strict local martingales).

R2 · Numeraire change for dividend-paying assets

Formulate the change of numeraire theorem when the numeraire asset pays a continuous dividend yield \(q > 0\).

Solution

Because the asset pays dividends, the raw price \(S_t\) is not self-financing. To form a valid numeraire, all dividends must be continuously reinvested in the asset, producing the total return process \(N_t = S_t e^{qt}\). The Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to \(\mathbb{Q}\) is \(Z_t = \frac{S_t e^{qt} / S_0}{e^{rt}}\). Under \(\mathbb{Q}^{S}\), the relative price of any reinvested asset \(Y_t e^{q_y t} / (S_t e^{qt})\) is a martingale.

R3 · Change of numeraire under jump diffusions

State how the Radon–Nikodym derivative between numeraires generalizes when asset prices follow jump diffusions with compensated Poisson jumps.

Solution

Under jump diffusions, the numeraire satisfies \(dN_t / N_{t-} = \mu_N dt + \sigma_N dB_t + \int_{\R} \gamma(z) \tilde{N}(dt, dz)\). The density process between numeraires \(M\) and \(N\) remains algebraically \(Z_t = \frac{N_t / N_0}{M_t / M_0}\). However, by the Girsanov theorem for semimartingales, the measure change now modifies both the Brownian drift and the jump intensity measure: the jump compensator under \(\mathbb{Q}^N\) is scaled by \(1 + \gamma(z)\).
