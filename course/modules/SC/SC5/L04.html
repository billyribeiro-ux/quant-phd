The Variance Gamma Model

  • Uses: continuous-time martingale, characteristic function, random variable, Lévy process, Lévy–Khintchine representation
  • Introduces: Variance Gamma model
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

Merton's jump-diffusion models price discontinuities as rare, large cataclysms superimposed on continuous Gaussian noise. In reality, financial order flow generates an infinite cascade of small price revisions alongside occasional substantial adjustments. Introduced by Dilip Madan, Peter Carr, and Eric Chang (1998), the Variance Gamma (VG) model discards continuous diffusion altogether, replacing it with a pure-jump Lévy process of infinite activity and finite variation. By evaluating Brownian motion along an independent stochastic Gamma time clock, VG provides an analytically tractable four-parameter framework that captures heavy tails, negative skewness, and short-dated implied volatility smiles without requiring a continuous Brownian driver.

1Subordination of Brownian Motion

The economic intuition behind the Variance Gamma model rests on the concept of business time versus calendar time. Rather than information arriving continuously and uniformly across calendar days, market activity accelerates during corporate announcements and slows during quiet trading sessions [madan1998variance].

Definition 1.1 (The Gamma Process) A non-decreasing Lévy process \(G = (G_t)_{t \ge 0}\) with \(G_0 = 0\) is called a Gamma process with mean rate \(1\) and variance rate \(\nu > 0\) if for each \(t > 0\), \(G_t\) follows a Gamma distribution with shape parameter \(a = t / \nu\) and scale parameter \(b = \nu\):

\[ f_{G_t}(g) = \frac{g^{\frac{t}{\nu} - 1} e^{-\frac{g}{\nu}}}{\nu^{\frac{t}{\nu}} \Gamma\left(\frac{t}{\nu}\right)}, \quad g > 0. \]

The expectation and variance of the stochastic time clock are \(\E[G_t] = t\) and \(\Var(G_t) = \nu t\). The parameter \(\nu\) measures the variance of the economic time clock: as \(\nu \to 0\), \(G_t\) degenerates to deterministic calendar time \(t\).

Definition 1.2 (Variance Gamma via Subordination) Let \(B = (B_s)_{s \ge 0}\) be a standard Brownian motion with drift \(\theta \in \R\) and volatility \(\sigma > 0\), defined as \(W_s^{(\theta, \sigma)} = \theta s + \sigma B_s\). Let \(G = (G_t)_{t \ge 0}\) be an independent Gamma process with mean rate 1 and variance rate \(\nu > 0\). The Variance Gamma process \(X = (X_t)_{t \ge 0}\) is defined by evaluating the arithmetic Brownian motion at the random time \(G_t\):

\[ X_t = \theta G_t + \sigma B(G_t). \]

Theorem 1.1 (Lévy Property of Subordinated Brownian Motion) The subordinated process \(X_t = \theta G_t + \sigma B(G_t)\) is an autonomous Lévy process with stationary and independent increments.

Proof Since \(G_0 = 0\), \(X_0 = \theta G_0 + \sigma B(0) = 0\). Let \(s < t\). Conditioning on the independent increment \(G_t - G_s = \Delta G\), the increment of the subordinated process is \(X_t - X_s = \theta (G_t - G_s) + \sigma (B(G_t) - B(G_s))\). By the strong Markov property and stationary increments of Brownian motion, \((B(G_t) - B(G_s)) \mid (G_t - G_s = \Delta G) \stackrel{d}{=} B(\Delta G)\). Because the increments of \(G\) are stationary and independent, \(X_t - X_s\) depends only on \(t - s\) and is independent of \(\mathcal{F}_s\). Stochastic continuity follows directly from the stochastic continuity of both \(B\) and \(G\) [cont2003financial].

2Characteristic Function & Moments

Because the conditional distribution of \(X_t\) given the time clock \(G_t\) is Gaussian, the unconditional characteristic function admits a simple, closed-form algebraic expression [madan1998variance].

Theorem 2.1 (Variance Gamma Characteristic Function) For any \(u \in \R\) such that \(1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu u^2 \ne 0\), the characteristic function of the Variance Gamma process \(X_t\) is given by:

\[ \phi_{X_t}(u) = \E\left[e^{i u X_t}\right] = \left( 1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu u^2 \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}}. \]

Equivalently, the characteristic exponent \(\psi_{VG}(u) = \frac{1}{t} \ln \phi_{X_t}(u)\) is:

\[ \psi_{VG}(u) = -\frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left( 1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2 \right). \]

Proof Condition on the random time variable \(G_t = g\). Because \(B\) is standard Brownian motion independent of \(G\):

\[ \E\left[e^{i u X_t} \mid G_t = g\right] = \E\left[e^{i u (\theta g + \sigma B_g)}\right] = e^{i u \theta g} \E\left[e^{i u \sigma B_g}\right] = e^{i u \theta g} e^{-\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 u^2 g} = \exp\left( \left( i \theta u - \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 u^2 \right) g \right). \]

By the law of total expectation, \(\phi_{X_t}(u) = \E\left[\E\left[e^{i u X_t} \mid G_t\right]\right] = \E\left[ e^{- z G_t} \right]\), where \(z = - i \theta u + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 u^2\). For a Gamma random variable with shape \(a = t / \nu\) and scale \(b = \nu\), its Laplace transform is \(\E[e^{- z G_t}] = (1 + b z)^{-a} = (1 + \nu z)^{-t / \nu}\). Substituting \(z\) yields:

\[ \phi_{X_t}(u) = \left( 1 + \nu \left( -i \theta u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 u^2 \right) \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}} = \left( 1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu u^2 \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}}. \]

Theorem 2.2 (Analytical Moments of Variance Gamma) The mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis of \(X_t\) are given in closed form by:

\[ \E[X_t] = \theta t, \] \[ \Var(X_t) = \left( \sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2 \right) t, \] \[ \operatorname{Skew}(X_t) = \frac{\theta \nu \left( 3 \sigma^2 + 2 \nu \theta^2 \right)}{\left( \sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2 \right)^{3/2} \sqrt{t}}, \] \[ \operatorname{Kurt}(X_t) = 3 \left( 1 + \nu \, \frac{\sigma^4 + 2 \nu \theta^2 \sigma^2 + \nu^2 \theta^4}{\left(\sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2\right)^2 t} \right) = 3 + \frac{3 \nu \left( \sigma^4 + 2 \nu \theta^2 \sigma^2 + \nu^2 \theta^4 \right)}{\left( \sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2 \right)^2 t}. \]

In particular, when \(\theta < 0\), the distribution is negatively skewed (capturing the equity market crash skew). When \(\theta = 0\), skewness is exactly 0 and excess kurtosis simplifies to \(\frac{3 \nu}{t}\).

Proof Outline Compute the cumulant generating function \(\Psi(u) = \ln \phi_{X_t}(-i u) = - \frac{t}{\nu} \ln\left(1 - \theta \nu u - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2\right)\). Taylor expanding the logarithm around \(u = 0\) via \(\ln(1 - y) = - y - \frac{y^2}{2} - \frac{y^3}{3} - \frac{y^4}{4} - \dots\) with \(y = \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2\) and reading off the coefficients of \(u^k / k!\) yields the cumulants \(\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \kappa_3, \kappa_4\) directly [madan1998variance].

3Representation as Difference of Two Gamma Processes

A remarkable algebraic feature of the Variance Gamma model is that any subordinated Brownian motion can be uniquely decomposed into the difference of two independent, monotonic Gamma processes representing upward and downward price adjustments [madan1998variance].

Theorem 3.1 (Difference of Gammas Decomposition) Let \(X_t\) be a Variance Gamma process with parameters \((\sigma, \nu, \theta)\). There exist two independent standard Gamma processes \(\Gamma_t^{(p)}\) (positive jumps) and \(\Gamma_t^{(n)}\) (negative jumps) such that:

\[ X_t \stackrel{d}{=} \Gamma_t^{(p)} - \Gamma_t^{(n)}, \]

where the marginal densities of \(\Gamma_t^{(p)}\) and \(\Gamma_t^{(n)}\) have common shape parameter \(t / \nu\) and distinct scale parameters \(\eta_p\) and \(\eta_n\) given by:

\[ \eta_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\theta^2 \nu^2 + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu} + \frac{1}{2}\theta \nu, \qquad \eta_n = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\theta^2 \nu^2 + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu} - \frac{1}{2}\theta \nu. \]

Defining \(\lambda_p = 1 / \eta_p\) and \(\lambda_n = 1 / \eta_n\), the characteristic function factorizes as:

\[ \phi_{X_t}(u) = \left( 1 - i u \eta_p \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}} \left( 1 + i u \eta_n \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}} = \left( \frac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_p - i u} \right)^{\frac{t}{\nu}} \left( \frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_n + i u} \right)^{\frac{t}{\nu}}. \]

Proof Consider the quadratic polynomial in the characteristic function denominator: \(P(u) = 1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2\). Notice that \(\eta_p \eta_n = \left(\frac{1}{4}\theta^2 \nu^2 + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu\right) - \frac{1}{4}\theta^2 \nu^2 = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu\), and \(\eta_p - \eta_n = \theta \nu\). Multiplying the linear binomials:

\[ (1 - i u \eta_p)(1 + i u \eta_n) = 1 - i u (\eta_p - \eta_n) + u^2 \eta_p \eta_n = 1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2 = P(u). \]

Raising both sides to the power \(-t / \nu\) decomposes \(\phi_{X_t}(u)\) into the product of two characteristic functions of independent Gamma random variables with parameters \((t/\nu, \eta_p)\) and \(-(t/\nu, \eta_n)\).

4The Variance Gamma Lévy Measure

By inverting the Lévy–Khintchine representation for \(\psi_{VG}(u)\), we obtain the exact distribution of jump arrivals across sizes.

Theorem 4.1 (Lévy Density of Variance Gamma) The Variance Gamma process is a pure-jump Lévy process with Gaussian component \(\sigma_{diff} = 0\). Its Lévy measure has density \(k_{VG}(x) = \frac{\nu(dx)}{dx}\) given by:

\[ k_{VG}(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{C}{x} e^{- M x}, & x > 0 \\ \dfrac{C}{|x|} e^{- G |x|}, & x < 0 \end{cases} \]

where \(C = \frac{1}{\nu}\), \(M = \lambda_p = \frac{1}{\eta_p} > 0\), and \(G = \lambda_n = \frac{1}{\eta_n} > 0\).

Proof Outline Using the Frullani integral identity \(\ln(1 + z) = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x} - e^{-(1+z)x}}{x} dx\), the logarithm of the factorized characteristic function can be written as:

\[ \ln \phi_{X_1}(u) = -\frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left(1 - \frac{i u}{M}\right) - \frac{1}{\nu}\ln\left(1 + \frac{i u}{G}\right) = \int_0^\infty \left(e^{i u x} - 1\right) \frac{e^{-M x}}{\nu x} dx + \int_{-\infty}^0 \left(e^{i u x} - 1\right) \frac{e^{-G |x|}}{\nu |x|} dx. \]

Comparing this with the un-truncated finite-variation Lévy–Khintchine formula \(\psi(u) = i b_0 u + \int (e^{i u x} - 1) k(x) dx\) confirms \(k_{VG}(x) = \frac{C}{|x|} e^{-M x \mathbf{1}_{x>0} - G |x| \mathbf{1}_{x<0}}\) with \(b_0 = 0\) [cont2003financial].

Remark 4.1 (Jump Activity and Variation) Because \(k_{VG}(x) \sim \frac{C}{|x|}\) as \(x \to 0\), the total arrival rate diverges: \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty k_{VG}(x) dx = \infty\). Thus, Variance Gamma exhibits infinite jump activity. However, \(\int_{-1}^1 |x| k_{VG}(x) dx = C \int_{-1}^1 e^{- \dots} dx < \infty\), proving that Variance Gamma has paths of finite total variation.

5Risk-Neutral Dynamics & Martingale Convexity Correction

In derivative pricing under a risk-neutral measure \(\Prob^*\), the discounted asset price \(e^{-(r - q) t} S_t\) must be a continuous-time martingale. We model the underlying asset price as:

\[ S_t = S_0 \exp\left( (r - q + \omega) t + X_t \right), \]

where \(r\) is the risk-free rate, \(q\) is the dividend yield, and \(\omega\) is the deterministic convexity adjustment [madan1998variance].

Theorem 5.1 (Variance Gamma Martingale Correction) The discounted stock price \(e^{-(r - q) t} S_t\) is a \(\Prob^*\)-martingale if and only if the drift correction \(\omega\) is chosen as:

\[ \omega = \frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left( 1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu \right). \]

A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of this equivalent martingale measure is that the argument of the logarithm remains strictly positive:

\[ \theta \nu + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu < 1 \iff 1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu > 0. \]

Proof The martingale condition requires \(\E^*[S_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = S_s e^{(r - q)(t - s)}\), which reduces to \(\E^*[e^{X_t}] = e^{- \omega t}\). Evaluating the characteristic function at \(u = -i\):

\[ \E^*\left[e^{X_t}\right] = \phi_{X_t}(-i) = \left( 1 - i \theta \nu (-i) + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu (-i)^2 \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}} = \left( 1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu \right)^{-\frac{t}{\nu}}. \]

Equating this to \(e^{- \omega t} = \exp(-\omega t)\) and taking the natural logarithm:

\[ - \omega t = - \frac{t}{\nu} \ln\left( 1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu \right) \implies \omega = \frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left( 1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu \right). \]

For \(\phi_{X_t}(-i)\) to be real and finite, the argument \(1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu\) must be strictly positive.

7Canon Breaks & Market Realities

The transition from Merton jump-diffusion to Variance Gamma overturns established modeling paradigms:

  • Abandonment of Diffusion: Classical models assume that Brownian motion is an indispensable base component of asset dynamics. Variance Gamma demonstrates that setting \(\sigma_{diff} = 0\) produces an exceptionally rich market model with infinite micro-jumps that emulate continuous diffusion at coarse scales while correctly pricing heavy tails.
  • Path Monotonicity Decomposition: Rather than viewing asset log-returns as a single fluctuating trajectory, the difference-of-Gammas representation reveals that any Variance Gamma path can be viewed as the net balance of two accumulated order-flow streams: buying volume (\(\Gamma^{(p)}\)) and selling volume (\(\Gamma^{(n)}\)).
  • Time Dilation as Risk: In Black–Scholes, volatility is constant in calendar time. In Variance Gamma, risk is twofold: Gaussian randomness conditional on time, and stochastic dilation of the time clock itself (\(\nu\)). When \(\nu\) is high, the market experiences severe clustering of information shocks.

8Common Pitfalls & Misconceptions

Misconception 1: "Because Variance Gamma has infinite jump activity, simulating its sample paths requires drawing an infinite number of random numbers."

Correction: While the sample path exhibits infinitely many jumps in any non-empty time window, the marginal distribution over any discrete interval \(\Delta t\) is exactly subordinated: \(X_{t+\Delta t} - X_t = \theta \Delta G + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta G} Z\), where \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) and \(\Delta G \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(\Delta t / \nu, \nu)\). Exact path simulation at discrete observation dates requires exactly two random draws per time step.

Misconception 2: "Variance Gamma is a stochastic volatility model like Heston."

Correction: Variance Gamma is a Lévy process with stationary and independent increments. In contrast, Heston (1993) is a continuous-path diffusion with a mean-reverting instantaneous variance process that has memory and dependent increments. VG generates volatility clustering across business time, but its increments over disjoint calendar intervals remain statistically independent.

Misconception 3: "The parameter \(\theta\) represents the expected annual return of the underlying stock."

Correction: The parameter \(\theta\) governs the drift of the subordinated Brownian motion, controlling the skewness of returns. In derivative pricing under \(\Prob^*\), the expected return of the stock is strictly constrained to the risk-free rate \(r - q\) by the martingale correction \(\omega\); \(\theta\) does not affect the risk-neutral mean return of the stock.

9Comprehensive Problem Set

Tier 1: Mechanics

Problem 1.1: Given Variance Gamma parameters \(\sigma = 0.25\), \(\nu = 0.40\), and \(\theta = -0.15\), calculate the exact numerical value of the risk-neutral martingale correction \(\omega\).

Solution

From Theorem 5.1, \(\omega = \frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left(1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu\right)\). First compute the inner terms: \(\theta \nu = -0.15 \times 0.40 = -0.06\). \(\sigma^2 = (0.25)^2 = 0.0625\), so \(\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \nu = 0.5 \times 0.0625 \times 0.40 = 0.0125\). Thus, the argument of the logarithm is: \(1 - (-0.06) - 0.0125 = 1 + 0.06 - 0.0125 = 1.0475\). Evaluating the natural logarithm: \(\ln(1.0475) \approx 0.0464115\). Dividing by \(\nu = 0.40\): \(\omega = \frac{0.0464115}{0.40} \approx 0.116029\).

Problem 1.2: For a symmetric Variance Gamma process with \(\theta = 0\), \(\sigma = 0.20\), and \(\nu = 0.50\), compute the annualized variance \(\Var(X_1)\) and the excess kurtosis \(\operatorname{Kurt}(X_1) - 3\).

Solution

From Theorem 2.2 with \(\theta = 0\) and \(t = 1\): \(\Var(X_1) = (\sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2) = \sigma^2 = (0.20)^2 = 0.040\). The excess kurtosis formula simplifies to: \(\operatorname{Kurt}(X_1) - 3 = \frac{3 \nu}{t} = \frac{3 \times 0.50}{1} = 1.50\).

Problem 1.3: Compute the parameters \(\eta_p\) and \(\eta_n\) of the difference-of-Gammas representation for \(\sigma = 0.20\), \(\nu = 0.20\), and \(\theta = -0.10\).

Solution

From Theorem 3.1: \(\theta \nu = -0.10 \times 0.20 = -0.02\). \(\frac{1}{2}\theta \nu = -0.01\). \(\frac{1}{4}\theta^2 \nu^2 = \frac{1}{4}(-0.02)^2 = 0.0001\). \(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu = \frac{1}{2}(0.04)(0.20) = 0.0040\). The radical term is \(\sqrt{0.0001 + 0.0040} = \sqrt{0.0041} \approx 0.064031\). Then: \(\eta_p = 0.064031 + (-0.01) = 0.054031\), and \(\eta_n = 0.064031 - (-0.01) = 0.074031\).

Tier 2: Proof & Theory

Problem 2.1: Prove that in the limit as the time variance parameter \(\nu \downarrow 0\), the Variance Gamma characteristic exponent \(\psi_{VG}(u)\) converges pointwise to the characteristic exponent of an arithmetic Brownian motion with drift \(\theta\) and volatility \(\sigma\).

Solution

Recall that \(\psi_{VG}(u) = - \frac{1}{\nu} \ln\left(1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2\right)\). Let \(z = - i \theta u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 u^2\), so the expression becomes \(- \frac{\ln(1 + \nu z)}{\nu}\). By L'Hôpital's rule or the first-order Taylor expansion \(\ln(1 + \nu z) = \nu z + O(\nu^2)\) as \(\nu \to 0\): \(\lim_{\nu \to 0} \left( - \frac{\ln(1 + \nu z)}{\nu} \right) = - z = - \left( - i \theta u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 u^2 \right) = i \theta u - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 u^2\). This is precisely the characteristic exponent of Brownian motion with drift \(\theta\) and volatility \(\sigma\).

Problem 2.2: Derive the third central moment \(\mu_3 = \E[(X_t - \E[X_t])^3]\) of the Variance Gamma process using conditioning on the subordinator \(G_t\).

Solution

Recall \(X_t = \theta G_t + \sigma B(G_t)\) and \(\E[X_t] = \theta t\). Thus \(X_t - \E[X_t] = \theta (G_t - t) + \sigma B(G_t)\). Cube both sides: \((X_t - \E[X_t])^3 = \theta^3 (G_t - t)^3 + 3 \theta^2 (G_t - t)^2 \sigma B(G_t) + 3 \theta (G_t - t) \sigma^2 B(G_t)^2 + \sigma^3 B(G_t)^3\). Take conditional expectation given \(G_t\): since \(B(G_t)\) is Gaussian with mean 0, all odd powers of \(B\) vanish: \(\E[B(G_t) \mid G_t] = 0\) and \(\E[B(G_t)^3 \mid G_t] = 0\). For the even power, \(\E[B(G_t)^2 \mid G_t] = G_t\). Thus: \(\E[(X_t - \E[X_t])^3 \mid G_t] = \theta^3 (G_t - t)^3 + 3 \theta \sigma^2 (G_t - t) G_t\). Now take unconditional expectation: \(\E[(G_t - t)^3]\) is the third central moment of \(\operatorname{Gamma}(t/\nu, \nu)\), which is \(2 \nu^2 t\). For the cross term: \(\E[(G_t - t) G_t] = \E[G_t^2] - t \E[G_t] = \Var(G_t) = \nu t\). Hence: \(\E[(X_t - \E[X_t])^3] = \theta^3 (2 \nu^2 t) + 3 \theta \sigma^2 (\nu t) = \theta \nu t (3 \sigma^2 + 2 \nu \theta^2)\).

Problem 2.3: Prove that the Variance Gamma process has finite total variation almost surely by evaluating the integral \(\int_{-1}^1 |x| k_{VG}(x) dx\).

Solution

From Theorem 4.1, the Lévy density on \([-1, 1] \setminus \{0\}\) is \(k_{VG}(x) = \frac{C}{x} e^{-M x}\) for \(x > 0\) and \(k_{VG}(x) = \frac{C}{|x|} e^{-G |x|}\) for \(x < 0\), where \(C = 1/\nu\). Evaluating the integral: \(\int_{-1}^1 |x| k_{VG}(x) dx = \int_0^1 x \left( \frac{C}{x} e^{-M x} \right) dx + \int_{-1}^0 |x| \left( \frac{C}{|x|} e^{-G |x|} \right) dx = C \int_0^1 e^{-M x} dx + C \int_{-1}^0 e^{G x} dx = C \left[ \frac{1 - e^{-M}}{M} + \frac{1 - e^{-G}}{G} \right]\). Since \(M > 0\) and \(G > 0\), this quantity is strictly positive and finite. Combined with \(\sigma_{diff} = 0\), this proves that paths of Variance Gamma have bounded variation on any finite interval almost surely.

Tier 3: Empirical & Numerical

Problem 3.1: In calibrating Variance Gamma to equity index options, an analyst observes that the implied volatility skew is steepest at short tenors (\(\tau = 1\) month) and flattens out at long tenors (\(\tau = 2\) years). Explain this phenomenon using the analytical skewness and excess kurtosis decay formulas.

Solution

By Theorem 2.2, the skewness of Variance Gamma decays as \(\operatorname{Skew}(X_\tau) \propto 1 / \sqrt{\tau}\), and excess kurtosis decays as \(\operatorname{Kurt}(X_\tau) - 3 \propto 1 / \tau\). In option pricing, implied volatility skew is directly proportional to skewness of the underlying return distribution, while implied volatility smile curvature (wings) is driven by excess kurtosis. At short maturities (\(\tau \to 0\)), \(1 / \sqrt{\tau}\) and \(1 / \tau\) diverge, creating an extremely pronounced, steep skew and sharp wings. As maturity increases (\(\tau \to \infty\)), the central limit theorem takes over, skewness and kurtosis vanish, and the implied volatility surface flattens toward Black–Scholes constant volatility.

Problem 3.2: Consider the parameter admissibility constraint \(\theta \nu + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu < 1\). If \(\sigma = 0.30\) and \(\nu = 0.50\), determine the maximum permissible value of the drift parameter \(\theta\) for a valid risk-neutral Variance Gamma model.

Solution

First compute \(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu = 0.5 \times (0.30)^2 \times 0.50 = 0.5 \times 0.09 \times 0.50 = 0.0225\). The constraint requires \(\theta \nu < 1 - 0.0225 = 0.9775\). Dividing by \(\nu = 0.50\): \(\theta < \frac{0.9775}{0.50} = 1.955\). Any value of \(\theta \ge 1.955\) produces infinite stock price expectations (\(\E^*[S_T] = \infty\)), violating absence of arbitrage.

Problem 3.3: In a Monte Carlo pricing simulation of European call options under Variance Gamma, why does Gamma subordination yield orders of magnitude lower execution time than Euler discretization of a jump SDE?

Solution

Euler discretization of a jump SDE requires discretizing time into small steps \(\Delta t\) to capture jump arrivals, requiring \(M = T / \Delta t\) evaluations per path (often thousands of steps per trajectory) and suffering from discretization bias. In contrast, under Gamma subordination, the increment \(X_T\) over the entire maturity \(T\) can be sampled exactly in just two random draws: draw \(\Delta G \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(T/\nu, \nu)\), then draw \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), and set \(X_T = \theta \Delta G + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta G} Z\). This provides zero time-discretization error and evaluates each path in \(O(1)\) computational operations.

Tier 4: Research & Extensions

Problem 4.1: Dilip Madan and Marti Subrahmanyam (2000) introduced the Variance Gamma with Stochastic Arrival (VGSA) model. How does VGSA generalize the Gamma subordinator to capture persistent volatility clustering?

Solution

In standard Variance Gamma, the time clock \(G_t\) has independent increments, precluding volatility clustering across time. In VGSA, the business time clock is modeled as the integral of a mean-reverting square-root (CIR / Heston-type) process \(y_t\): \(T_t = \int_0^t y_s ds\), where \(d y_t = \kappa (\eta - y_t) dt + \lambda \sqrt{y_t} d W_t\). Evaluating the base Variance Gamma process along this continuous rate clock creates intertemporal dependency in volatility: periods of high trading intensity persist over time, matching empirical autoregressive volatility clustering while preserving analytical characteristic functions via the affine transform method.

Problem 4.2: Contrast the Variance Gamma model with the Normal Inverse Gaussian (NIG) model of Barndorff-Nielsen (1997). What subordinator replaces the Gamma process in NIG, and how does the small-jump behavior differ?

Solution

In the Normal Inverse Gaussian (NIG) model, Brownian motion is subordinated by an Inverse Gaussian process \(I_t\) rather than a Gamma process. The Inverse Gaussian subordinator has Lévy density \(k_{IG}(x) \propto x^{-3/2} e^{-\gamma x}\), which has a higher singularity at the origin than the Gamma subordinator's \(k_{Gamma}(x) \propto x^{-1} e^{-\lambda x}\). Consequently, the NIG process has Lévy density \(k_{NIG}(x) \propto |x|^{-1} K_1(\alpha |x|) e^{\beta x} \sim |x|^{-2}\) near zero, which is a process of infinite activity and infinite variation, whereas Variance Gamma has finite variation. NIG paths are thus significantly rougher at microscopic scales.

Problem 4.3: In high-frequency electronic trading, order flow is often modeled as two competing Poisson or Hawkes processes. How does the difference-of-Gammas representation provide a continuous-time fluid limit for limit order book queue dynamics?

Solution

Consider limit order book queues where buy market orders arrive with intensity \(\lambda_b\) and sell market orders arrive with intensity \(\lambda_s\). Under high trading volume and infinitesimal tick sizes, scaling the arrivals via functional central limit theorems leads to subordinated jump processes. Since \(\Gamma_t^{(p)}\) and \(\Gamma_t^{(n)}\) are pure-jump subordinators with dense small jumps, they represent the continuous fluid accumulation of buying pressure and selling pressure, respectively. The price \(X_t = \Gamma_t^{(p)} - \Gamma_t^{(n)}\) reflects the net inventory imbalance, providing an analytical bridge between microscopic order matching and macroscopic heavy-tailed asset pricing.

11Formative Quiz

  1. What type of stochastic process is the Variance Gamma model?

    Options

    Variance Gamma has \(\sigma_{diff} = 0\), \(\int k(x) dx = \infty\) (infinite activity), and \(\int_{-1}^1 |x| k(x) dx < \infty\) (finite variation).

  2. For a Variance Gamma process with \(\sigma = 0.20\), \(\nu = 0.60\), and \(\theta = -0.10\), what is the total annualized variance \(\Var(X_1)\)?

    \(\Var(X_1) = \sigma^2 + \nu \theta^2 = (0.20)^2 + 0.60 \times (-0.10)^2 = 0.040 + 0.60 \times 0.010 = 0.040 + 0.006 = 0.046 \approx 0.046\).

  3. What role does the drift parameter \(\theta\) play in the shape of the Variance Gamma return distribution?

    Options

    By Theorem 2.2, the skewness is proportional to \(\theta\); negative \(\theta\) tilts probability density to the left tail.

  4. Under what mathematical condition is the risk-neutral martingale correction \(\omega = \frac{1}{\nu}\ln(1 - \theta \nu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu)\) well-defined and real?

    Options

    The argument of the natural logarithm must be strictly positive to ensure finite real expectations \(\E^*[e^{X_t}]\).

  5. What is the excess kurtosis \(\operatorname{Kurt}(X_1) - 3\) of a symmetric Variance Gamma process (\(\theta = 0\)) with \(\nu = 1.0\) at horizon \(t = 1\)?

    For \(\theta = 0\), excess kurtosis is \(\frac{3 \nu}{t} = \frac{3 \times 1.0}{1} = 3.0\).

  6. How does Variance Gamma decompose into the difference of two independent Gamma processes \(\Gamma_t^{(p)} - \Gamma_t^{(n)}\)?

    Options

    By Theorem 3.1, \(1 - i \theta \nu u + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \nu u^2 = (1 - i u \eta_p)(1 + i u \eta_n)\), which corresponds to the difference of two independent Gammas.

  7. Why can an increment of a Variance Gamma process be simulated exactly in a single time step without time-discretization error?

    Options

    By Definition 1.2, \(X_t \mid G_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta G_t, \sigma^2 G_t)\), allowing exact two-stage sampling regardless of step size.

  8. What is the continuous Brownian diffusion volatility \(\sigma_{diff}\) of the Variance Gamma model in its characteristic triplet?

    Variance Gamma is a pure-jump process: its continuous Gaussian diffusion parameter is identically zero (\(\sigma_{diff} = 0\)); all variance originates from jumps.
