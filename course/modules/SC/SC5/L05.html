The Variance Gamma process models asset returns via infinite jump arrivals, but its singularity at zero jump size is strictly logarithmic (\(|x|^{-1}\)). Is financial order flow limited to this specific small-jump intensity, or do markets exhibit stronger or weaker jump clustering? Introduced by Peter Carr, Hélyette Geman, Dilip Madan, and Marc Yor (2002), the CGMY model provides an elegant four-parameter generalization that nests Variance Gamma while decoupling overall jump intensity, right-tail decay, left-tail decay, and small-jump path roughness. The activity index \(Y\) governs whether the process has finite activity, infinite activity with finite variation, or infinite variation approaching Brownian motion, making CGMY the benchmark laboratory for non-Gaussian jump dynamics.

1The CGMY Lévy Density

The CGMY model specifies a pure-jump Lévy process on \(\R\) through a parametric Lévy density exhibiting power-law behavior near the origin and asymmetric exponential damping in the tails (Cont & Tankov 2004).

Definition 1.1 (CGMY Lévy Density)

A pure-jump Lévy process \(X = (X_t)_{t \ge 0}\) follows a CGMY model with parameter vector \((C, G, M, Y)\) if its Gaussian diffusion parameter is zero (\(\sigma = 0\)) and its Lévy measure has density \(k_{CGMY}(x) = \frac{\nu(dx)}{dx}\) given by:

\[ k_{CGMY}(x) = \begin{cases} C \, \dfrac{e^{- M x}}{x^{1 + Y}}, & x > 0, \\[8pt] C \, \dfrac{e^{- G |x|}}{|x|^{1 + Y}}, & x < 0, \end{cases} \]

subject to the parameter constraints \(C > 0\), \(G > 0\), \(M > 0\), and \(Y < 2\).

Remark 1.2 (Relation to Variance Gamma)

When \(Y = 0\), the CGMY Lévy density simplifies to \(k(x) = \frac{C}{|x|} e^{-M x \mathbf{1}_{x>0} - G |x| \mathbf{1}_{x<0}}\), which is identical to the Variance Gamma Lévy density derived in Lesson 4 with \(C = 1 / \nu\). Thus Variance Gamma is precisely CGMY with \(Y = 0\).

2The Role of \(Y\): Activity & Variation Regimes

The parameter \(Y\) is the defining structural innovation of the CGMY framework. By tuning the power of the denominator \(|x|^{1 + Y}\), \(Y\) completely governs the path roughness and arrival dynamics of small jumps (Cont & Tankov 2004).

Theorem 2.1 (Classification of CGMY Regimes)

The analytical properties of the CGMY process partition into four distinct regimes depending on \(Y\):

  • \(Y < 0\): Finite jump activity (\(\nu(\R) < \infty\)); compound Poisson process of discrete jumps.
  • \(Y = 0\): Infinite jump activity and finite total variation; logarithmic small-jump arrival rate matching Variance Gamma.
  • \(0 < Y < 1\): Infinite jump activity and finite total variation; power-law small-jump arrival rate \(\Lambda(\epsilon) \sim \frac{C}{Y}\epsilon^{-Y}\).
  • \(1 \le Y < 2\): Infinite jump activity and infinite total variation; sample paths exhibit unbounded 1-variation.
Proof

Near \(x = 0\), \(e^{-M x} \to 1\) and \(e^{-G |x|} \to 1\), so \(k_{CGMY}(x) \sim C |x|^{-(1+Y)}\). For jump activity, integrate on \((0, 1]\):

\[ \int_0^1 x^{-(1+Y)} dx = \left[ -\frac{x^{-Y}}{Y} \right]_0^1 = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{|Y|} < \infty, & Y < 0, \\[6pt] \infty, & Y \ge 0. \end{cases} \]

For total variation, evaluate the integral of \(|x|\) on \([-1, 1]\):

\[ \int_0^1 x \cdot x^{-(1+Y)} dx = \int_0^1 x^{-Y} dx = \left[ \frac{x^{1 - Y}}{1 - Y} \right]_0^1 = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{1 - Y} < \infty, & Y < 1, \\[6pt] \infty, & Y \ge 1. \end{cases} \]

Finally, for the Lévy measure integrability condition \(\int_{|x| \le 1} x^2 k(x) dx < \infty\):

\[ \int_0^1 x^2 \cdot x^{-(1+Y)} dx = \int_0^1 x^{1 - Y} dx = \left[ \frac{x^{2 - Y}}{2 - Y} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2 - Y} < \infty \iff Y < 2. \]

Thus \(Y < 2\) is the necessary and sufficient condition for \(\nu\) to be a valid Lévy measure.

3Analytical Characteristic Function & Martingale Dynamics

A major strength of the CGMY model is that its characteristic exponent admits an exact representation in terms of Euler's Gamma function \(\Gamma(z)\) (Cont & Tankov 2004).

Theorem 3.1 (CGMY Characteristic Exponent)

Let \(X = (X_t)_{t \ge 0}\) be a CGMY process with parameters \((C, G, M, Y)\). For \(Y \notin \{0, 1\}\), the characteristic exponent \(\psi(u) = \frac{1}{t}\ln \E[e^{i u X_t}]\) is given by:

\[ \psi_{CGMY}(u) = C \, \Gamma(-Y) \left[ (M - i u)^Y - M^Y + (G + i u)^Y - G^Y \right]. \]

For \(Y = 0\), taking the continuous limit \(Y \to 0\) yields \(- C [ \ln(1 - \frac{i u}{M}) + \ln(1 + \frac{i u}{G}) ]\).

Proof Outline

For \(Y < 1\), the process has finite variation, so \(\psi(u) = \int (e^{i u x} - 1) k(x) dx\). Consider the positive jump integral \(I_+(u) = C \int_0^\infty (e^{i u x} - 1) x^{-(1+Y)} e^{-M x} dx\). Using the integral definition of the Gamma function \(\Gamma(-Y) = \int_0^\infty z^{-(1+Y)} e^{-z} dz\) for \(Y < 0\), and continuing analytically to \(Y \in (0, 1)\) via integration by parts, the integral evaluates to \(C \Gamma(-Y) ((M - i u)^Y - M^Y)\). Applying the identical derivation to negative jumps yields the result.

Theorem 3.2 (CGMY Martingale Convexity Correction)

Let \(M > 1\). The drift correction \(\omega\) that ensures the discounted asset price \(e^{-(r - q) t} S_t\) is a \(\Prob^*\)-martingale under \(S_t = S_0 e^{(r - q + \omega) t + X_t}\) for \(Y \ne 0\) is:

\[ \omega = - C \, \Gamma(-Y) \left[ (M - 1)^Y - M^Y + (G + 1)^Y - G^Y \right]. \]

The requirement \(M > 1\) is strictly necessary: if \(M \le 1\), \(\E^*[e^{X_t}] = \infty\), violating absence of arbitrage.

Lab 5 · CGMY Lévy density integration and small-jump activity across Y

Under the CGMY model (Carr, Geman, Madan & Yor 2002), the arrival rate of jumps larger than threshold \(\epsilon > 0\) behaves fundamentally differently across the index \(Y\): for \(Y = -0.5\) it converges to a finite limit as \(\epsilon \to 0\), whereas for \(Y = 0.0\) and \(Y = 0.5\) it explodes to infinity. Numerical quadrature of the positive Lévy density with \(C = 1.0, M = 5.0\) across cutoffs \(\epsilon \in \{0.1, 0.01, 0.001\}\) demonstrates this small-jump explosion.

"""Lab 5 · CGMY Lévy density integration and small-jump activity across Y.

Under the CGMY model (Carr, Geman, Madan & Yor 2002), the Lévy density on the positive axis is:
k(x) = C * exp(-M * x) / (x^(1 + Y)),   x > 0.
The total arrival rate of jumps with size exceeding threshold epsilon > 0 is:
Lambda(epsilon) = integral_epsilon^infinity C * exp(-M * x) / (x^(1 + Y)) dx.
The behavior as epsilon -> 0 depends fundamentally on the activity parameter Y:
- For Y < 0: Lambda(epsilon) converges to a finite limit Lambda(0) < infinity (finite activity / compound Poisson).
- For Y = 0 (Variance Gamma): Lambda(epsilon) ~ C * ln(1/epsilon) -> infinity (infinite activity, finite variation).
- For 0 < Y < 1: Lambda(epsilon) ~ (C/Y) * epsilon^(-Y) -> infinity (infinite activity, finite variation).
- For 1 <= Y < 2: both jump frequency and first variation diverge (infinite activity, infinite variation).
This lab numerically integrates the positive CGMY Lévy density for C = 1.0, M = 5.0 across Y in {-0.5, 0.0, 0.5}
and cutoffs epsilon in {0.1, 0.01, 0.001} (SIMULATED), demonstrating the explosion of small jumps.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

from scipy.integrate import quad  # type: ignore[import-untyped]


def cgmy_density(x: float, c: float, m: float, y: float) -> float:
    """Evaluate CGMY right-tail Lévy density C * exp(-M * x) / x^(1 + Y)."""
    return float(c * math.exp(-m * x) / (x ** (1.0 + y)))


def main() -> None:
    c = 1.0
    m = 5.0
    y_values = [-0.5, 0.0, 0.5]
    epsilons = [0.1, 0.01, 0.001]

    results: dict[float, list[float]] = {}

    print(f"CGMY parameters: C = {c:.2f}, M = {m:.2f} (SIMULATED)")
    print("jump arrival intensity Lambda(epsilon) = int_epsilon^inf k(x) dx:")

    for y in y_values:
        row: list[float] = []
        for eps in epsilons:

            def density(x: float, y_param: float = y) -> float:
                return cgmy_density(x, c, m, y_param)

            val, _ = quad(density, eps, 20.0, limit=200)
            row.append(val)
        results[y] = row
        print(f"  Y = {y:4.1f}: eps=0.100 -> {row[0]:8.4f}, eps=0.010 -> {row[1]:8.4f}, eps=0.001 -> {row[2]:8.4f}")

    # Invariants:
    # 1. For Y = -0.5, the integral is bounded: ratio row[2] / row[1] is close to 1.0 (< 1.5).
    # 2. For Y = 0.5, the integral explodes by ~sqrt(10) ~ 3.16 per decade: row[2] / row[1] > 2.5.
    ratio_neg = results[-0.5][2] / results[-0.5][1]
    ratio_pos = results[0.5][2] / results[0.5][1]

    print(f"growth ratio (eps=0.001 vs eps=0.010) for Y = -0.5: {ratio_neg:.3f}")
    print(f"growth ratio (eps=0.001 vs eps=0.010) for Y =  0.5: {ratio_pos:.3f}")
    print("finite activity bounded growth (Y=-0.5):", ratio_neg < 1.5)
    print("infinite activity power-law explosion (Y=0.5):", ratio_pos > 2.5)


main()

4Where the canon breaks

The CGMY framework challenges several foundational assumptions inherited from classical diffusion theory. In CGMY, Brownian motion is not a prerequisite foundation; it is merely the boundary limit as \(Y \uparrow 2\). Empirical calibrations to financial option surfaces routinely find \(Y \in [0.4, 0.8]\) for equities, proving that markets operate in an intermediate regime of infinite activity and finite variation far removed from continuous diffusion (Cont & Tankov 2004). Furthermore, classical models tie small-jump activity directly to tail thickness; CGMY completely decouples the two: \(Y\) controls small-jump clustering, while \(G\) and \(M\) independently govern the steepness of left crash tails and right rally tails.

5Misconceptions

  1. “Because \(Y < 2\), we can freely choose any \(Y \in \R\) including \(Y \ge 2\).”

    If \(Y \ge 2\), the integral \(\int_{-1}^1 x^2 k_{CGMY}(x) dx \sim \int_0^1 x^{1-Y} dx\) diverges at \(x = 0\). This violates the fundamental Lévy measure condition \(\int \min(1, x^2) \nu(dx) < \infty\), meaning that for \(Y \ge 2\) the density does not define any stochastic process.

  2. “The parameter \(M\) must be greater than zero for the model to exist, so \(M = 0.5\) is valid for risk-neutral option pricing.”

    While \(M > 0\) defines a valid statistical process for log-returns, derivative pricing under \(\Prob^*\) requires the underlying asset price \(S_t = S_0 e^{X_t}\) to have finite expectation. Because \(\E[e^{X_t}] < \infty \iff M > 1\), setting \(M \le 1\) creates an infinite stock price expectation, rendering option pricing mathematically impossible.

  3. “The CGMY model is difficult to calibrate because it lacks closed-form option prices.”

    While CGMY lacks closed-form cumulative distribution functions, its characteristic function is completely analytical and evaluates in microseconds. Combined with Fourier inversion (Carr–Madan or Lewis formulas), European call and put option prices across hundreds of strikes can be computed in a fraction of a second.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Risk-neutral admissibility and small-jump power

Given CGMY parameters \(C = 0.5\), \(G = 12.0\), \(M = 18.0\), and \(Y = 0.5\), verify whether \(M\) satisfies the risk-neutral pricing admissibility condition, and evaluate the exponent \(1 + Y\) governing the small-jump singularity.

Solution

Risk-neutral admissibility requires \(M > 1\) so that \(\E^*[e^{X_t}] < \infty\). Here \(M = 18.0 > 1\), which satisfies the condition. The small-jump denominator power is \(1 + Y = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5\). Thus near the origin, \(k_{CGMY}(x) \sim C |x|^{-1.5}\), confirming the process is in the infinite activity, finite variation regime.

M2 · Symmetric CGMY annual variance calculation

For a symmetric CGMY model with \(G = M = 10.0\), \(C = 1.0\), and \(Y = 0.5\), compute the annualized variance \(\Var(X_1) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x^2 k_{CGMY}(x) dx\).

Solution

By symmetry, \(\Var(X_1) = 2 C \int_0^\infty x^2 x^{-(1+Y)} e^{-M x} dx = 2 C M^{-(2 - Y)} \Gamma(2 - Y)\). For \(Y = 0.5\), \(\Gamma(2 - 0.5) = \Gamma(1.5) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} \approx 0.886227\). Then \(M^{-(2 - Y)} = 10^{-1.5} \approx 0.0316228\). Thus: \(\Var(X_1) = 2 \times 1.0 \times 0.0316228 \times 0.886227 \approx 0.056046\).

M3 · Compound Poisson regime jump arrival rate

In the compound Poisson regime where \(Y = -0.5\), \(C = 2.0\), and \(M = 5.0\), evaluate the total arrival rate of positive jumps \(\Lambda_+ = \int_0^\infty k(x) dx\).

Solution

For \(Y = -0.5\), the exponent is \(1 + Y = 0.5\). The arrival rate is \(\Lambda_+ = C \int_0^\infty x^{-0.5} e^{-M x} dx = C M^{-0.5} \Gamma(0.5) = \frac{C \sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{M}}\). Substituting \(C = 2.0\) and \(M = 5.0\): \(\Lambda_+ = \frac{2.0 \times 1.77245}{2.23607} \approx 1.58533\) positive jumps per year.

Proof

P1 · Continuous limit from CGMY to Variance Gamma

Prove that as \(Y \to 0\), the CGMY term \(C \Gamma(-Y) ((M - i u)^Y - M^Y)\) converges to \(- C \ln(1 - \frac{i u}{M})\).

Solution

Using \(\Gamma(1 - Y) = - Y \Gamma(-Y)\), rewrite the expression as \(- C \Gamma(1 - Y) \frac{(M - i u)^Y - M^Y}{Y}\). As \(Y \to 0\), \(\Gamma(1 - Y) \to \Gamma(1) = 1\). By the definition of the derivative with respect to \(Y\) at \(Y = 0\): \(\lim_{Y \to 0} \frac{A^Y - B^Y}{Y} = \ln A - \ln B = \ln(A / B)\). Here \(A = M - i u\) and \(B = M\). Therefore: \(\lim_{Y \to 0} [ - C \Gamma(1 - Y) \frac{(M - i u)^Y - M^Y}{Y} ] = - C \ln( \frac{M - i u}{M} ) = - C \ln( 1 - \frac{i u}{M} )\). This proves that CGMY continuously nests Variance Gamma at \(Y = 0\).

P2 · Infinite total variation for Y between 1 and 2

Show that for \(1 \le Y < 2\), the sample paths of the CGMY process have infinite total variation almost surely.

Solution

A pure-jump Lévy process has finite total variation if and only if \(\int_{|x| \le 1} |x| \nu(dx) < \infty\). For the CGMY density on \((0, 1]\): \(\int_0^1 x k(x) dx = C \int_0^1 x^{-Y} e^{-M x} dx \ge C e^{-M} \int_0^1 x^{-Y} dx\). For \(Y \ge 1\), \(\int_0^1 x^{-Y} dx = \lim_{\epsilon \downarrow 0} [ \frac{x^{1-Y}}{1-Y} ]_\epsilon^1 = \infty\) (diverging logarithmically when \(Y = 1\) and as \(\epsilon^{-(Y-1)}\) when \(Y > 1\)). Because the integral of jump sizes diverges at the origin, the sum of jump magnitudes diverges almost surely, establishing infinite total variation.

P3 · Third cumulant and skewness control

Derive the third cumulant \(\kappa_3\) of the CGMY process for \(Y < 1\) and explain how \(G\) and \(M\) control return asymmetry.

Solution

The third cumulant of a pure-jump Lévy process is \(\kappa_3 = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x^3 \nu(dx) = C \int_0^\infty x^{2 - Y} e^{-M x} dx - C \int_0^\infty x^{2 - Y} e^{-G x} dx = C \Gamma(3 - Y) ( M^{-(3 - Y)} - G^{-(3 - Y)} )\). For financial equity indices, negative jumps dominate positive jumps, meaning the left tail decays slower than the right tail: \(G < M\). Because \(3 - Y > 0\), \(G < M \implies G^{-(3 - Y)} > M^{-(3 - Y)}\), which guarantees \(\kappa_3 < 0\) (negative skewness). If \(G = M\), \(\kappa_3 = 0\).

Empirical

E1 · Economic interpretation of equity index CGMY parameters

Calibrating CGMY to S&P 500 index options across maturities yields estimates: \(C = 0.024\), \(G = 4.2\), \(M = 9.8\), and \(Y = 0.55\). Interpret each calibrated parameter economically.

Solution

1. \(C = 0.024\) represents the baseline scale of jump arrivals across the entire spectrum. 2. \(G = 4.2\) is significantly smaller than \(M = 9.8\), indicating that negative jumps decay much more slowly (\(e^{-4.2 |x|}\)) than positive jumps (\(e^{-9.8 x}\)), producing substantial negative skewness and pricing deep out-of-the-money put options (crash fear). 3. \(Y = 0.55 \in (0, 1)\) proves that equity returns operate in an infinite activity, finite variation regime: the asset price does not diffuse continuously, but rather moves through dense micro-jumps of order \(|x|^{-1.55}\) near zero.

E2 · Infinite variation in foreign exchange order flow

In high-frequency foreign exchange markets, typical calibrated values of \(Y\) are near \(1.2\) to \(1.4\). Why is \(Y > 1\) more prevalent in FX markets than in equity index markets?

Solution

Major currency pairs (e.g., EUR/USD, USD/JPY) represent exchange rates between sovereign economies with continuous, two-sided institutional order flow. Unlike equity indices that experience catastrophic one-sided earnings crashes and corporate defaults, FX markets are dominated by an immense volume of continuous two-way quote updates. A value of \(Y \in (1, 2)\) produces infinite total variation: the trajectory exhibits extreme micro-roughness indistinguishable from continuous diffusion at macro scales, while retaining heavy tail shocks at microsecond frequencies.

E3 · Numerically robust evaluation near Y = 0

When evaluating \(\psi_{CGMY}(u)\) numerically for \(Y\) very close to 0 (e.g., \(Y = 10^{-6}\)), direct evaluation suffers from catastrophic cancellation error in \((M - i u)^Y - M^Y\). How should this be implemented robustly in software?

Solution

For small \(|Y| < 10^{-4}\), rewrite the bracketed term using the exact exponential representation: \((M - i u)^Y - M^Y = M^Y ( \exp(Y \ln(1 - i u / M)) - 1 )\). Using the standard numerical function \(\operatorname{expm1}(z) = e^z - 1\), this avoids subtraction of nearly equal floating-point numbers. Furthermore, as \(Y \to 0\), \(\Gamma(-Y) \operatorname{expm1}(Y \ln(1 - i u / M)) = - \Gamma(1 - Y) \frac{\operatorname{expm1}(Y \ln(1 - i u / M))}{Y} \to - \ln(1 - i u / M)\), maintaining 16 digits of numerical precision across all regimes.

Research

R1 · CGMY with stochastic volatility (CGMYe)

Carr, Geman, Madan, and Yor (2003) extended CGMY to the CGMYe model (with stochastic volatility). Why is adding a stochastic clock or stochastic volatility to CGMY necessary to calibrate the term structure of implied volatility?

Solution

Because CGMY is a time-homogeneous Lévy process, its cumulants scale strictly linearly with maturity: \(\kappa_n(t) = t \kappa_n(1)\). Consequently, its implied volatility skew must decay at the rigid rate of \(O(1/\sqrt{\tau})\) as maturity \(\tau\) increases. Empirical option surfaces frequently exhibit term structures of skewness that persist across multiple years. Subordinating CGMY with an integrated CIR or Ornstein–Uhlenbeck rate process introduces stochastic volatility, allowing the model to decouple short-tenor jump skew from long-tenor persistent variance clustering.

R2 · CGMY and the bilateral Gamma process

Contrast the CGMY model with the bilateral Gamma process of Küchler and Tappe (2008). Under what parameter restriction does CGMY reduce to bilateral Gamma?

Solution

The bilateral Gamma process is defined as the difference of two independent Gamma processes with independent shape and scale parameters: \(X_t = \Gamma_1(t; \alpha_1, \beta_1) - \Gamma_2(t; \alpha_2, \beta_2)\). Its Lévy density is \(k(x) = \frac{\alpha_1}{x} e^{-\beta_1 x}\) for \(x > 0\) and \(k(x) = \frac{\alpha_2}{|x|} e^{-\beta_2 |x|}\) for \(x < 0\). CGMY generalizes bilateral Gamma by replacing the \(1/|x|\) singularity with \(|x|^{-(1+Y)}\). When \(Y = 0\) and \(C_+ = C_- = C\), CGMY reduces to Variance Gamma (a symmetric-shape bilateral Gamma). When \(Y = 0\) and distinct amplitudes \(C_+\) and \(C_-\) are permitted, CGMY exactly coincides with the general bilateral Gamma process.

R3 · Weak convergence to Brownian motion as Y approaches 2

In the boundary case where \(Y \uparrow 2\), show that the CGMY process converges weakly to a continuous Brownian motion after appropriate rescaling of \(C\).

Solution

Let \(C = \sigma^2 \frac{2 - Y}{2 \Gamma(2 - Y)}\). Consider the variance of the truncated jumps: as \(Y \uparrow 2\), the singularity \(x^{-(1+Y)} = x^{-3}\) diverges, but the scaling factor \(2 - Y\) normalizes the second moment to \(\sigma^2\). For any \(\epsilon > 0\), the jump arrival rate of jumps larger than \(\epsilon\) is \(\int_\epsilon^\infty C x^{-(1+Y)} e^{-M x} dx \le C \epsilon^{-Y} / Y \to 0\) as \(C \to 0\) (since \(2 - Y \to 0\)). By the functional central limit theorem for triangular arrays of Lévy processes, all jumps of size \(|\Delta X| > \epsilon\) vanish almost surely, while the continuous quadratic variation converges to \(\sigma^2 t\). Hence the scaled CGMY process converges weakly in the Skorokhod topology to standard Brownian motion \(\sigma W_t\).

8Formative Quiz

  1. What structural property distinguishes the CGMY model from the Variance Gamma model?

    Options

    Variance Gamma restricts the small-jump singularity to \(|x|^{-1}\) (\(Y = 0\)), whereas CGMY allows any power \(|x|^{-(1+Y)}\) with \(Y < 2\).

  2. Under which condition on \(Y\) does the CGMY process have paths of finite total variation?

    Options

    By Theorem 2.1, \(\int_{-1}^1 |x| k(x) dx < \infty \iff \int_0^1 x^{-Y} dx < \infty \iff Y < 1\).

  3. Why is the condition \(M > 1\) strictly mandatory for risk-neutral derivative pricing under CGMY?

    Options

    By Theorem 3.2, \(\int_0^\infty e^x k(x) dx \sim \int^\infty e^{(1 - M) x} dx\), which converges if and only if \(1 - M < 0 \iff M > 1\).

  4. What numerical value of \(Y\) in the CGMY model recovers the classical Variance Gamma model?

    When \(Y = 0\), the CGMY Lévy density reduces to \(C |x|^{-1} e^{-M x \mathbf{1}_{x>0} - G |x| \mathbf{1}_{x<0}}\), which is identical to Variance Gamma.

  5. If a calibrated CGMY model on equity index options has \(G = 5.0\) and \(M = 14.0\), what is the direction of return skewness?

    Options

    The decay rate of negative jumps is \(e^{-G |x|}\). Smaller \(G\) means heavier left tail, producing negative skewness.

  6. What happens to the CGMY Lévy measure if the index parameter \(Y \ge 2\)?

    Options

    A Lévy measure must satisfy \(\int \min(1, x^2) \nu(dx) < \infty\). For \(Y \ge 2\), the integral near 0 behaves as \(\int_0^1 x^{1-Y} dx = \infty\).

  7. Which regime of \(Y\) corresponds to a compound Poisson process with a finite total arrival rate of jumps?

    Options

    When \(Y < 0\), \(\int_0^1 x^{-(1+Y)} dx < \infty\), ensuring the total jump intensity \(\nu(\R)\) is strictly finite.

  8. What is the continuous Gaussian diffusion parameter \(\sigma_{diff}\) in the canonical CGMY model?

    The CGMY model is a pure-jump Lévy process; its continuous diffusion component is identically zero (\(\sigma_{diff} = 0\)).
