Jumps & Lévy Processes

Continuous diffusion models cannot reproduce the steep implied volatility smiles observed at short tenors or the sudden discontinuous price crashes witnessed during market stress. This module builds jump processes and Lévy models from the ground up: Poisson and compound Poisson counting processes, Merton's lognormal jump-diffusion model, the axiomatic foundation of Lévy processes and the Lévy–Khintchine representation, infinite-activity and finite-variation processes including Variance Gamma and CGMY, and analytical option pricing via the Carr–Madan damped Fourier transform method.

  • Prerequisites: SC2 Itô Calculus & SDEs. Prerequisite closure includes SC1, SC2, M1, M2, M4, M5, M6 and P0.1.
  • Project: Lévy model calibration, graded by autograder and rubric (both).
  • Data: SIMULATED option surfaces and paths.

56 h lessons · 24 h project (30% of the 80 h module)

Objectives

By the end of SC5 you can:

  1. Formulate Poisson and compound Poisson counting processes, prove their martingale compensation properties, and derive their characteristic functions (Lesson 1).
  2. Solve Merton's jump-diffusion SDE under the risk-neutral measure, derive the compensated drift, and price European options using Poisson series expansions (Lesson 2).
  3. State and apply the Lévy–Khintchine representation theorem, decompose Lévy paths via the Lévy–Itô theorem, and classify models by jump activity and variation (Lesson 3).
  4. Construct the Variance Gamma model through Gamma subordination of Brownian motion, and calculate its closed-form moments, skewness, and kurtosis (Lesson 4).
  5. Analyze the CGMY model family, demonstrate how the parameter \(Y\) controls the fine structure of small jumps, and characterize its tail decay rates (Lesson 5).
  6. Implement European option pricing via the Carr–Madan damped Fourier transform, and calibrate Merton and Variance Gamma models to market implied volatility surfaces (Lessons 6 & 7).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions. Seven evaluate prerequisites from SC2, SC1, M6, and M4. Two are skill checks, marked as such, testing Fourier pricing and jump drift compensation taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (requiring at least eight correct answers out of nine). Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80 percent mastery bar and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. What is the quadratic variation \([B, B]_H\) of standard Brownian motion on \([0, H]\)?

    Options

    By SC1, standard Brownian motion increments have quadratic variation converging in \(L^2\) to interval length \(H\).

  2. Under Itô's lemma for geometric Brownian motion \(dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dB_t\), what is \(d(\ln S_t)\)?

    Options

    By SC2, the second-order Taylor term \(\tfrac{1}{2} f''(S) (dS)^2 = -\tfrac{1}{2S^2}(\sigma^2 S^2 dt) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 dt\) provides the Itô drift correction.

  3. For a Poisson counter \(N_t\) with constant intensity \(\lambda > 0\), which process is a continuous-time martingale?

    Options

    Because \(\E[N_t - N_s \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = \lambda(t-s)\), subtracting the deterministic compensator \(\lambda t\) leaves a zero-drift martingale.

  4. If \(X \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma = 0.5\), what is \(\Var(X)\)?

    \(\Var(X) = \sigma^2 = 0.5^2 = 0.25\).

  5. What does the martingale property \(\E[M_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = M_s\) for \(s \le t\) imply about expected future increments \(\E[M_t - M_s \mid \mathcal{F}_s]\)?

    Options

    By linearity of conditional expectation, \(\E[M_t - M_s \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = \E[M_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - M_s = M_s - M_s = 0\).

  6. What is the characteristic function \(\phi_Z(u) = \E[e^{i u Z}]\) of a standard normal random variable \(Z \sim \Normal(0, 1)\)?

    Options

    By standard Gaussian Fourier transform, \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i u z} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2} dz = e^{-u^2/2}\).

  7. For any valid probability distribution, what is the value of its characteristic function evaluated at zero, \(\phi(0) = \E[e^{i \cdot 0 \cdot X}]\)?

    \(\E[e^{0}] = \E[1] = 1\) by the total probability axiom.

  8. Skill check 1 · Jump drift compensation: Under Merton's jump-diffusion model with arrival intensity \(\lambda\) and lognormal jump amplitude \(Y = e^J\), what must the drift adjustment \(\kappa = \E[e^J - 1]\) satisfy to guarantee that discounted stock price \(e^{-rt} S_t\) is a martingale under \(\mathbb{Q}\)?

    Options

    Because \(\E[e^J] = \exp(\mu_j + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_j^2)\), the expected percentage price jump is \(\kappa = \exp(\mu_j + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_j^2) - 1\). To maintain risk-neutral drift \(r\), the drift of \(\ln S_t\) must be compensated by \(-\lambda \kappa\).

  9. Skill check 2 · Carr–Madan Fourier pricing: Why does the Carr–Madan (1999) method introduce a positive damping factor \(\alpha > 0\) into the option payoff \(e^{\alpha k} C_T(k)\) before applying the Fourier transform?

    Options

    As \(k = \ln K \to -\infty\), call price \(C_T(k) \to S_0\), so \(C_T(k) \notin L^1(\R)\). Multiplying by \(e^{\alpha k}\) for \(\alpha > 0\) ensures integrability as \(k \to -\infty\), enabling Fourier inversion.

Lesson map

Seven lessons totaling 56 study hours:

  1. L01 · Poisson & Compound Poisson Processes

    Counting processes, exponential inter-arrival times, jump intensity, compensated Poisson martingales, and compound Poisson characteristic functions. 8 hours.

  2. L02 · Merton's Jump-Diffusion Model

    Lognormal jumps, risk-neutral drift compensation, analytical series option pricing, and the short-tenor implied volatility smile. 8 hours.

  3. L03 · Lévy Processes & the Lévy–Khintchine Representation

    Infinite divisibility, stationary independent increments, characteristic triplets, Lévy measures, activity versus variation, and the Lévy–Itô decomposition. 8 hours.

  4. L04 · The Variance Gamma Model

    Subordination of Brownian motion by a Gamma process, difference of Gammas, closed-form moments, skewness, kurtosis, and risk-neutral drift correction. 8 hours.

  5. L05 · The CGMY Model & Extended Lévy Dynamics

    Four-parameter tempered stable Lévy density, transition across activity regimes via parameter \(Y\), power-law small jumps, and exponential tail decay. 8 hours.

  6. L06 · Option Pricing via Characteristic Functions

    Carr–Madan damped Fourier transform, damping parameter selection, numerical quadrature, Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) grid pricing, and put-call parity. 8 hours.

  7. L07 · Implied Volatility Smiles & Calibration Principles

    Jump-induced smile asymptotics, short-tenor skew blowup, nonlinear least-squares formulation, ill-posedness, and parameter identification. 8 hours.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Calibrate Merton and Variance Gamma models to the embedded option surface, and produce a formal calibration code and comparative report. See project specification. Graded by autograder and rubric (both).

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the calibration framework to the four-parameter CGMY model, optimizing the activity parameter \(Y\) and analyzing parameter identification and surface fit across tenors.
