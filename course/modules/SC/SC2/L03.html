Itô's Lemma

  • Uses: Itô isometry, quadratic variation, Taylor's theorem, partial derivative
  • Introduces: Itô's lemma
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study

In deterministic calculus, the chain rule states \(df(x(t)) = f'(x(t)) x'(t) dt\). In continuous-time finance, asset prices and yields are non-linear functions of underlying diffusions. Because Brownian motion has non-vanishing quadratic variation \((dB_t)^2 = dt\), the ordinary chain rule fails: a second-order Taylor term of order \((\Delta B)^2 \sim \Delta t\) survives in the continuous limit. Itô's lemma provides the exact stochastic chain rule, determining the convexity corrections that govern option pricing, yield curve modeling, and dynamic hedging.

1Heuristic Origin and the Discrete Algebraic Identity

Let \(f: \R \to \R\) be twice continuously differentiable. By Taylor's theorem, the increment of \(f(B_t)\) over a short interval \([t, t + \Delta t]\) expands as:

\[ \Delta f(B_t) = f(B_{t+\Delta t}) - f(B_t) = f'(B_t) \Delta B_t + \frac{1}{2} f''(B_t) (\Delta B_t)^2 + R_2(\Delta B_t), \]

where \(R_2\) is a remainder of order \(o((\Delta B_t)^2)\). In ordinary calculus where integrator paths are continuously differentiable, \((\Delta x)^2 = (x'(t) \Delta t)^2 = O((\Delta t)^2)\) vanishes faster than \(\Delta t\) and drops out of the differential. For Brownian motion, however, the increments have typical magnitude \(|\Delta B_t| \sim \sqrt{\Delta t}\), so \((\Delta B_t)^2 \sim \Delta t\). Summing over a partition \(\Pi\) of \([0, H]\):

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (\Delta B_k)^2 \xrightarrow{L^2} [B, B]_H = H. \]

This fundamental property of quadratic variation ensures that the second-order term contributes a non-vanishing deterministic drift of magnitude \(\tfrac{1}{2} f''(B_t) dt\).

Theorem 1.1 (Itô's Lemma for \(B_t^2\))

Assumptions: (A1) \(B\) is standard Brownian motion; (A2) \(H > 0\) is a fixed horizon; (A3) \(\Pi_n\) is a sequence of partitions with mesh \(\|\Pi_n\| \to 0\).

Statement: The square of Brownian motion satisfies the Itô differential identity:

\[ d(B_t^2) = 2 B_t \, dB_t + dt, \quad \text{or equivalently} \quad B_H^2 = 2 \int_0^H B_s \, dB_s + H. \]
Proof

Consider any partition \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = H\). The total increment \(B_H^2 - B_0^2 = B_H^2\) telescopes as:

\[ B_H^2 = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (B_{t_{k+1}}^2 - B_{t_k}^2). \]

Using the algebraic identity \(b^2 - a^2 = 2a(b - a) + (b - a)^2\) with \(a = B_{t_k}\) and \(b = B_{t_{k+1}}\):

\[ B_{t_{k+1}}^2 - B_{t_k}^2 = 2 B_{t_k}(B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k}) + (B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k})^2 = 2 B_{t_k} \Delta B_k + (\Delta B_k)^2. \]

Summing over all intervals yields the exact pathwise discrete identity:

\[ B_H^2 = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} B_{t_k} \Delta B_k + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (\Delta B_k)^2. \]

As \(\|\Pi_n\| \to 0\), the first sum converges in \(L^2(\Omega)\) to the Itô integral \(2 \int_0^H B_s dB_s\) by Theorem 2.1 of Lesson 2. The second sum converges in \(L^2(\Omega)\) to the quadratic variation \([B, B]_H = H\). Therefore, taking \(L^2\) limits on both sides:

\[ B_H^2 = 2 \int_0^H B_s \, dB_s + H. \]

In differential notation, this is \(d(B_t^2) = 2 B_t dB_t + dt\).

2Itô's Lemma for Scalar Diffusions

Definition 1.2 (Itô Process)

An Itô process (or stochastic diffusion) is an adapted continuous process \((X_t)_{t \ge 0}\) of the form:

\[ X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t a_s \, ds + \int_0^t b_s \, dB_s, \quad \text{written symbolically as} \quad dX_t = a_t \, dt + b_t \, dB_t, \]

where \(a_t\) is an adapted Lebesgue-integrable drift process (\(\int_0^H |a_s| ds < \infty\) a.s.) and \(b_t\) is an adapted square-integrable diffusion process (\(\int_0^H b_s^2 ds < \infty\) a.s.).

Theorem 1.3 (Itô's Lemma for Time-Dependent Diffusions)

Assumptions: (A1) \(X_t\) is an Itô process with \(dX_t = a_t dt + b_t dB_t\); (A2) \(f: [0, H] \times \R \to \R\) is continuously differentiable in \(t\) and twice continuously differentiable in \(x\), i.e., \(f \in C^{1, 2}([0, H] \times \R)\).

Statement: The transformed process \(Y_t = f(t, X_t)\) is itself an Itô process whose differential satisfies Itô's lemma:

\[ df(t, X_t) = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(t, X_t) + a_t \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(t, X_t) + \frac{1}{2} b_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}(t, X_t) \right) dt + b_t \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(t, X_t) \, dB_t. \]

In integral form, for all \(t \in [0, H]\):

\[ f(t, X_t) = f(0, X_0) + \int_0^t \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial s} + a_s \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{1}{2} b_s^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} \right) ds + \int_0^t b_s \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \, dB_s. \]
Proof sketch

Step 1 (polynomials): For polynomial functions in \(t\) and \(x\), the result follows by induction from the product rule and Theorem 1.1, with every step verified algebraically.

Step 2 (Taylor approximation for \(C^{1, 2}\)): For general \(f \in C^{1, 2}\), expand \(f(t_{k+1}, X_{t_{k+1}}) - f(t_k, X_k)\) using the bivariate Taylor theorem with integral remainder. The first-order terms yield \(\partial_t f \Delta t + \partial_x f \Delta X_k\). Since \(\Delta X_k = a_k \Delta t_k + b_k \Delta B_k\), the quadratic term is \(\tfrac{1}{2} \partial_{xx} f (\Delta X_k)^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \partial_{xx} f b_k^2 (\Delta B_k)^2 + O((\Delta t)^{3/2})\). As the partition mesh vanishes, \(\sum_k (\Delta B_k)^2\) converges in probability to \(dt\), establishing the Itô formula. The convergence of the Taylor remainder is standard analysis and skipped here (graded C).

Remark 1.4 (Multiplication Rules as Quadratic Variation Shorthand)

In quantitative finance, Itô's formula is memorized via the formal multiplication table:

\[ dt \cdot dt = 0, \quad dt \cdot dB_t = 0, \quad dB_t \cdot dt = 0, \quad (dB_t)^2 = dt. \]

These equations are not identities between ordinary differentials; they are shorthand conventions summarizing the almost-sure limits of quadratic variation and cross-variation products under partition refinement.

Lab 1 · Discrete algebraic identity backing Itô's lemma for B_t^2

For any discrete Brownian realization with \(N = 1{,}000\) steps on \([0, 1.0]\) from seed 42 (SIMULATED), the telescoping identity \(B_H^2 = 2 \sum_k B_{t_k} \Delta B_k + \sum_k (\Delta B_k)^2\) holds pathwise to machine precision (residual < 1e-12).

"""Lab 1 · Discrete algebraic identity backing Itô's lemma for B_t^2.

For any discrete path, the telescoping identity B_H^2 - B_0^2 = sum_k (B_{t_{k+1}}^2 - B_{t_k}^2) factors algebraically
as 2 sum_k B_{t_k} Delta B_k + sum_k (Delta B_k)^2. This identity holds pathwise without limits. In the continuous
limit, the second term converges in L^2 to the quadratic variation [B, B]_H = H, establishing
d(B_t^2) = 2 B_t dB_t + dt. This lab simulates one Brownian path with N = 1,000 steps over [0, 1] from seed 42
(SIMULATED), evaluates the discrete sums, prints the exact algebraic residual to machine precision, and compares the sum
of squared increments to H.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def main() -> None:
    horizon = 1.0
    n_steps = 1000
    dt = horizon / n_steps
    sq_dt = math.sqrt(dt)
    seed = 42

    rng = random.Random(seed)
    b = 0.0
    cross_sum = 0.0
    quad_var = 0.0

    for _ in range(n_steps):
        db = rng.gauss(0.0, sq_dt)
        cross_sum += b * db
        quad_var += db * db
        b += db

    b_sq = b * b
    identity_rhs = 2.0 * cross_sum + quad_var
    residual = abs(b_sq - identity_rhs)

    print(f"terminal B_H: {b:.6f}, B_H^2: {b_sq:.6f}")
    print(f"cross term 2 * sum(B_k * dB_k): {2.0 * cross_sum:.6f}")
    print(f"sum of squared increments sum(dB_k^2): {quad_var:.6f}")
    print(f"exact pathwise residual: {residual:.2e}")
    print(f"difference |sum(dB_k^2) - H|: {abs(quad_var - horizon):.6f}")
    print("residual within 1e-12:", residual < 1e-12)


main()
terminal B_H: -0.577726, B_H^2: 0.333768
cross term 2 * sum(B_k * dB_k): -0.705121
sum of squared increments sum(dB_k^2): 1.038888
exact pathwise residual: 1.22e-15
difference |sum(dB_k^2) - H|: 0.038888
residual within 1e-12: True

The computed residual is 1.22e-15, zero to floating-point precision. The sum of squared increments \(\sum (\Delta B_k)^2 = 1.038888\) is within 0.039 of the continuous horizon \(H = 1.0\). This demonstrates that the Itô correction \(dt\) is the continuous limit of the discrete algebraic term \(\sum (\Delta B_k)^2\).

3Where the canon breaks

Itô's lemma strictly requires \(f \in C^2\) (or \(C^{1, 2}\) for time-dependent functions). When \(f\) fails to be twice continuously differentiable, the second derivative \(f''\) does not exist in the classical sense, and Itô's lemma cannot be applied directly. For example, for the absolute value function \(f(x) = |x|\), \(f'\) has a jump discontinuity at \(x = 0\) and \(f''(x) = 2 \delta(x)\) is the Dirac delta distribution. Applying stochastic calculus to \(|B_t|\) requires Tanaka's formula:

\[ |B_t| = \int_0^t \operatorname{sgn}(B_s) \, dB_s + L_t^0(B), \]

where \(L_t^0(B)\) is the Brownian local time at zero, measuring the amount of time the trajectory spends in an infinitesimal neighborhood of the origin. In financial barrier option pricing and lookback payoff modeling, payoff kinks introduce local times that break classical Itô calculus unless generalized derivatives (Meyer–Tanaka formulas) are employed.

4Misconceptions

  1. “The rule \((dB_t)^2 = dt\) is an identity of ordinary differentials.”

    In standard real analysis, differentials satisfy \((dx)^2 = 0\) at first order. The equation \((dB_t)^2 = dt\) is an informal mnemonic for the rigorous \(L^2\) limit of quadratic variation: \(\lim_{\|\Pi\| \to 0} \sum_k (\Delta B_k)^2 = t\) in \(L^2(\Omega)\). It is not an algebraic equality between numbers or differentials.

  2. “The Itô correction term can be neglected for short holding periods because \(\Delta t\) is small.”

    The Itô drift term \(\tfrac{1}{2} b^2 f'' dt\) has the exact same order in time (\(dt\)) as the base drift \(a f' dt\). Neglecting it produces an immediate first-order bias in the expected return, regardless of how small the time interval is. For example, ignoring \(\sigma^2/2\) in Black–Scholes option replication leads to severe systematic underhedging.

  3. “Itô's lemma applies to any continuous function of an Itô process.”

    Continuity is wholly insufficient: \(f\) must be twice continuously differentiable in the spatial argument. Functions with kinks (like call option payoffs \((S - K)^+\)) or discontinuities in first derivatives violate the hypothesis of Itô's lemma and generate local time contributions.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Itô formula for cubic Brownian motion

Apply Itô's lemma to \(Y_t = B_t^3\). Identify the drift and diffusion coefficients of \(d(B_t^3)\).

Solution

Let \(f(x) = x^3\). Then \(f'(x) = 3x^2\) and \(f''(x) = 6x\). By Itô's lemma for standard Brownian motion (\(a = 0, b = 1\)):

\[ d(B_t^3) = \left(0 + 0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1)^2 (6 B_t)\right) dt + 3 B_t^2 \, dB_t = 3 B_t \, dt + 3 B_t^2 \, dB_t. \]

The drift is \(3 B_t\) and the diffusion is \(3 B_t^2\).

M2 · Time-dependent exponential transform

Apply Itô's lemma to \(Y_t = e^{\lambda t} B_t\) where \(\lambda \in \R\) is constant.

Solution

Let \(f(t, x) = e^{\lambda t} x\). The partial derivatives are \(\partial_t f = \lambda e^{\lambda t} x\), \(\partial_x f = e^{\lambda t}\), and \(\partial_{xx} f = 0\). By Theorem 1.3:

\[ dY_t = \left(\lambda e^{\lambda t} B_t + \tfrac{1}{2}(0)\right) dt + e^{\lambda t} \, dB_t = \lambda e^{\lambda t} B_t \, dt + e^{\lambda t} \, dB_t. \]
M3 · Exponential Brownian martingale

Apply Itô's lemma to \(Y_t = \exp(\sigma B_t - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t)\). Show that \(Y_t\) is driftless.

Solution

Let \(f(t, x) = \exp(\sigma x - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t)\). The derivatives are \(\partial_t f = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 f(t, x)\), \(\partial_x f = \sigma f(t, x)\), and \(\partial_{xx} f = \sigma^2 f(t, x)\). Applying Itô's formula:

\[ dY_t = \left( -\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 Y_t + 0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1)^2(\sigma^2 Y_t) \right) dt + \sigma Y_t \, dB_t = 0 \cdot dt + \sigma Y_t \, dB_t. \]

The drift is identically zero: \(dY_t = \sigma Y_t dB_t\), confirming that \(Y_t\) is a local martingale.

Proof

P1 · General polynomial Itô formula by induction

Prove that for all integers \(n \ge 1\), \(d(B_t^n) = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} B_t^{n-2} dt + n B_t^{n-1} dB_t\).

Solution

Let \(f(x) = x^n\). Then \(f'(x) = n x^{n-1}\) and \(f''(x) = n(n-1)x^{n-2}\). Applying Theorem 1.3 with \(a = 0\) and \(b = 1\):

\[ d(B_t^n) = \left(0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1)^2 f''(B_t)\right) dt + f'(B_t) \, dB_t = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} B_t^{n-2} \, dt + n B_t^{n-1} \, dB_t. \]

Alternatively, the result can be established by induction using the integration by parts formula \(d(X_t Y_t) = X_t dY_t + Y_t dX_t + dX_t dY_t\) starting from \(d(B_t) = dB_t\) and Theorem 1.1.

P2 · Itô product rule

Let \(X_t\) and \(Y_t\) be two Itô processes driven by the same Brownian motion: \(dX_t = a_1 dt + b_1 dB_t\) and \(dY_t = a_2 dt + b_2 dB_t\). Prove the Itô product rule: \(d(X_t Y_t) = X_t dY_t + Y_t dX_t + b_1 b_2 dt\).

Solution

Consider the polarization identity \(2 X_t Y_t = (X_t + Y_t)^2 - X_t^2 - Y_t^2\). Applying Itô's lemma to \(f(u) = u^2\):

\[ d(X_t^2) = 2 X_t dX_t + b_1^2 dt, \quad d(Y_t^2) = 2 Y_t dY_t + b_2^2 dt. \]

Since \(d(X_t + Y_t) = (a_1 + a_2)dt + (b_1 + b_2)dB_t\), its diffusion is \(b_1 + b_2\), so:

\[ d((X_t + Y_t)^2) = 2(X_t + Y_t)d(X_t + Y_t) + (b_1 + b_2)^2 dt. \]

Expanding and subtracting gives \(2 d(X_t Y_t) = 2(X_t dY_t + Y_t dX_t) + [(b_1+b_2)^2 - b_1^2 - b_2^2] dt = 2(X_t dY_t + Y_t dX_t) + 2 b_1 b_2 dt\). Dividing by 2 yields \(d(X_t Y_t) = X_t dY_t + Y_t dX_t + b_1 b_2 dt\).

P3 · Vanishing cross-variation of drift

Prove that for any continuous Itô process \(X_t = \int_0^t a_s ds + \int_0^t b_s dB_s\), the cross-variation between the bounded variation drift part \(A_t = \int_0^t a_s ds\) and the martingale part \(M_t = \int_0^t b_s dB_s\) is identically zero: \([A, M]_t = 0\).

Solution

Over a partition \(\Pi\), the sum of cross-increments is \(\sum_k \Delta A_k \Delta M_k\). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\[ \left| \sum_k \Delta A_k \Delta M_k \right| \le \max_k |\Delta A_k| \sum_k |\Delta M_k| \quad \text{or} \quad \left(\sum_k (\Delta A_k)^2\right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_k (\Delta M_k)^2\right)^{1/2}. \]

Since \(A_t\) is continuous and of bounded variation, \(\sum_k (\Delta A_k)^2 \le \max_k |\Delta A_k| \sum_k |\Delta A_k| \to 0\) as \(\|\Pi\| \to 0\). Since \(\sum_k (\Delta M_k)^2 \to [M, M]_t < \infty\) in probability, their product converges to zero in probability. Hence \([A, M]_t = 0\).

Empirical

E1 · Numerical magnitude of the Itô correction

For an equity with volatility \(\sigma = 30\%\) per year (\(\sigma = 0.30\)), what is the annualized magnitude of the Itô drift correction \(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2\)? Express your answer in basis points.

Solution

\(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(0.30)^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(0.09) = 0.045 = 4.5\%\) per year. In basis points (where 1% = 100 bps), this equals \(0.045 \times 10{,}000 = 450\) basis points per year.

E2 · Discretization error of omitting the second-order term

Suppose an analyst simulates \(Y_t = \log S_t\) over \(H = 1\) year using ordinary calculus \(d\log S = dS / S\), thereby omitting \(-\sigma^2/2\). For \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the expected systematic error in the terminal log price?

Solution

The true drift of \(\log S_t\) is \(\mu - \sigma^2/2\). Omitting the Itô correction assumes drift \(\mu\). The cumulative bias over horizon \(H = 1\) is \((\sigma^2 / 2) H = (0.04 / 2)(1) = 0.02\). The simulated terminal log price is systematically overstated by 0.02 on average.

E3 · Pathwise residual scaling with partition mesh

In the discrete identity \(B_H^2 = 2 \sum B_{t_k}\Delta B_k + \sum (\Delta B_k)^2\), what is the root-mean-square error between the discrete quadratic variation \(\sum (\Delta B_k)^2\) and the continuous limit \(H\) on an equal partition of \(N\) steps?

Solution

Let \(\Delta t = H / N\). The increments \(\Delta B_k \sim \Normal(0, \Delta t)\) are independent, so \((\Delta B_k)^2\) has variance \(2 (\Delta t)^2\). The variance of the sum is \(\Var(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (\Delta B_k)^2) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} 2 (\Delta t)^2 = N \cdot 2 (H/N)^2 = 2 H^2 / N\). The root-mean-square difference is \(\sqrt{2/N} H\), scaling as \(O(1/\sqrt{N})\).

Research

R1 · Two-dimensional Itô formula with correlation

Formulate the two-dimensional Itô formula for \(f(t, X_t, Y_t)\) where \(dX_t = a_1 dt + b_1 dB_{1,t}\) and \(dY_t = a_2 dt + b_2 dB_{2,t}\) with \(\E[dB_{1,t} dB_{2,t}] = \rho dt\).

Solution

By bivariate Taylor expansion, the second-order differential terms are \(\tfrac{1}{2} \partial_{xx} f (dX)^2 + \partial_{xy} f (dX)(dY) + \tfrac{1}{2} \partial_{yy} f (dY)^2\). Using \((dX)^2 = b_1^2 dt\), \((dY)^2 = b_2^2 dt\), and \(dX dY = \rho b_1 b_2 dt\):

\[ df = \left( \partial_t f + a_1 \partial_x f + a_2 \partial_y f + \tfrac{1}{2} b_1^2 \partial_{xx} f + \rho b_1 b_2 \partial_{xy} f + \tfrac{1}{2} b_2^2 \partial_{yy} f \right) dt + b_1 \partial_x f \, dB_{1,t} + b_2 \partial_y f \, dB_{2,t}. \]
R2 · Tanaka's formula and Brownian local time

Explain how Tanaka's formula extends Itô calculus to \(f(x) = |x|\), and state the definition of the local time \(L_t^0(B)\).

Solution

Tanaka's formula states that \(|B_t| = |B_0| + \int_0^t \operatorname{sgn}(B_s) dB_s + L_t^0(B)\), where \(\operatorname{sgn}(x) = 1\) for \(x > 0\) and \(-1\) for \(x \le 0\). The local time at zero, \(L_t^0(B)\), is defined as the almost-sure limit \(L_t^0(B) = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{1}{2\epsilon} \int_0^t \indic_{(-\epsilon, \epsilon)}(B_s) ds\). It is a continuous, non-decreasing process that increases only when \(B_t = 0\), representing the occupation density of Brownian motion at the origin.

R3 · Rough paths perspective on Itô's correction

In Terry Lyons' rough paths theory, what geometric object beyond the path \(B_t\) must be added to define integrals and recovery of Itô calculus?

Solution

Rough paths theory enhances the trajectory \(B_t\) by its second-order iterated integral (the rough path signature): \(\mathbb{B}_{s, t} = \int_s^t (B_u - B_s) \otimes dB_u\). The symmetric part of this tensor equals \(\tfrac{1}{2}(B_t - B_s)^{\otimes 2}\), while its antisymmetric part is the Lévy area. Selecting the Itô interpretation corresponds to choosing the enhancement \(\mathbb{B}_{s, t}^{\text{Itô}} = \int_s^t (B_u - B_s) dB_u\), which satisfies the non-standard algebraic contraction that produces the Itô correction term.

7Retrieval quiz

  1. What term appears in Itô's lemma that is absent from the classical single-variable chain rule?

    Options

    By Theorem 1.3, the second-order derivative term \(\tfrac{1}{2} b^2 \partial_{xx} f dt\) arises from the non-zero quadratic variation of the diffusion.

  2. What is the drift coefficient of \(d(B_t^3)\) when \(B_t = 1\)? (Recall \(d(B_t^3) = 3 B_t dt + 3 B_t^2 dB_t\).)

    The drift is \(\tfrac{1}{2}(1)^2 (6 B_t) = 3 B_t\). At \(B_t = 1\), this equals 3.

  3. Why does the second-order Taylor term survive in the limit for Brownian motion but not for smooth functions?

    Options

    Brownian paths have quadratic variation \([B, B]_t = t\), so squared increments sum to order \(\Delta t\), not \((\Delta t)^2\).

  4. In the differential \(d(B_t^2) = 2 B_t dB_t + c \, dt\), what is the constant \(c\)?

    By Theorem 1.1, \(c = \tfrac{1}{2}(1)^2 f''(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(2) = 1\).

  5. What regularity condition on \(f\) is required for the classical Itô lemma?

    Options

    Theorem 1.3 requires \(f \in C^{1, 2}\) so that the second spatial derivative exists and is continuous.

  6. Which formula applies when taking the stochastic differential of the non-smooth absolute value function \(|B_t|\)?

    Options

    Because \(|x|\) has a kink at 0, Tanaka's formula is required, introducing Brownian local time \(L_t^0\).

  7. For \(Y_t = \exp(\sigma B_t - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t)\), what is the drift coefficient?

    By Problem M3, the time derivative \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 Y_t\) exactly cancels the Itô term \(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 Y_t\), leaving zero drift.

  8. In the Itô product rule for \(d(X_t Y_t)\) where \(dX = a_1 dt + b_1 dB\) and \(dY = a_2 dt + b_2 dB\), what is the cross-variation term?

    Options

    By Problem P2, \(dX_t dY_t = (b_1 dB_t)(b_2 dB_t) = b_1 b_2 dt\).
