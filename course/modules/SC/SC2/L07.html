The Euler–Maruyama Scheme

  • Uses: SDE existence and uniqueness, Itô isometry, expectation, variance
  • Introduces: Euler–Maruyama scheme
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

Few non-linear stochastic differential equations admit closed-form solutions. Practical pricing of path-dependent options, portfolio optimization under non-linear transaction costs, and credit risk valuation require discrete-time numerical integration. The Euler–Maruyama scheme is the fundamental starting point of stochastic numerics, extending Euler's classical forward polygonal method to Itô diffusions. Understanding its local truncation errors and global strong convergence order \(\gamma = 1/2\) provides the rigorous basis for all pathwise simulation engines.

1Formulation of the Scheme

Definition 1.1 (Euler–Maruyama Scheme)

Let \((X_t)_{t \in [0, H]}\) satisfy the 1-dimensional Itô SDE \(dX_t = a(t, X_t) \, dt + b(t, X_t) \, dB_t\) with initial condition \(X_0 = x_0\). Given an equidistant partition of the horizon \([0, H]\) into \(N\) subintervals of width \(\Delta t = H / N\) with grid points \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_N = H\), the Euler–Maruyama scheme generates discrete approximations \(Y_0, Y_1, \dots, Y_N\) via the forward recursion:

\[ Y_0 = x_0, \] \[ Y_{k+1} = Y_k + a(t_k, Y_k) \, \Delta t + b(t_k, Y_k) \, \Delta B_k \quad \text{for } k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1, \]

where the Brownian increments \(\Delta B_k = B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k}\) are mutually independent Gaussian variables with expectation \(\E[\Delta B_k] = 0\) and variance \(\Var(\Delta B_k) = \Delta t\).

To analyze the convergence of the discrete values \(Y_k\) to the continuous trajectory \(X_t\), it is standard to define the continuous-time Euler interpolant \(\bar{Y}_t\) on \(t \in [t_k, t_{k+1})\) by freezing the coefficients at the left grid point:

\[ \bar{Y}_t = Y_k + \int_{t_k}^t a(t_k, Y_k) \, ds + \int_{t_k}^t b(t_k, Y_k) \, dB_s = Y_k + a(t_k, Y_k)(t - t_k) + b(t_k, Y_k)(B_t - B_{t_k}). \]
Theorem 1.2 (One-Step Local Truncation Error)

Assumptions: (A1) The drift \(a(t, x)\) and diffusion \(b(t, x)\) satisfy the global Lipschitz condition with constant \(K\); (A2) Conditioned on identical state \(X_{t_k} = Y_k = x\) at time \(t_k\), \(X_{t_{k+1}}\) is the exact solution and \(Y_{k+1}\) is the one-step Euler approximation.

Statement: The local truncation error decomposed into drift and stochastic components satisfies:

\[ \E\left[ \left| \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \big( a(s, X_s) - a(t_k, x) \big) \, ds \right|^2 \right] \le C_a (\Delta t)^3, \] \[ \E\left[ \left| \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \big( b(s, X_s) - b(t_k, x) \big) \, dB_s \right|^2 \right] \le C_b (\Delta t)^2, \]

yielding an overall one-step root-mean-square local error of order \(O(\Delta t)\).

Proof

For the deterministic drift integral, applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives:

\[ \left( \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} |a(s, X_s) - a(t_k, x)| \, ds \right)^2 \le \Delta t \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} |a(s, X_s) - a(t_k, x)|^2 \, ds. \]

Using the Lipschitz property, \(|a(s, X_s) - a(t_k, x)|^2 \le 2 K^2 (s - t_k)^2 + 2 K^2 |X_s - x|^2\). For \(s \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]\), standard moment bounds on Itô processes establish that \(\E[|X_s - x|^2] \le C (s - t_k)\). Integrating with respect to \(s\) from \(t_k\) to \(t_{k+1}\) produces an integral of order \(O((\Delta t)^2)\). Multiplying by the outer \(\Delta t\) yields the bound \(C_a (\Delta t)^3\).

For the stochastic integral, applying the Itô isometry (Theorem 1.1 of Lesson 2):

\[ \E\left[ \left( \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \big( b(s, X_s) - b(t_k, x) \big) \, dB_s \right)^2 \right] = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \E\left[ |b(s, X_s) - b(t_k, x)|^2 \right] ds. \]

By the Lipschitz condition and the moment estimate \(\E[|X_s - x|^2] \le C(s - t_k)\):

\[ \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \E\left[ |b(s, X_s) - b(t_k, x)|^2 \right] ds \le 2 K^2 \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} (s - t_k + C(s - t_k)) \, ds = K^2 (1 + C) (\Delta t)^2. \]

Taking the square root of the dominant stochastic error \(O((\Delta t)^2)\) gives a one-step local root-mean-square error of order \(O(\Delta t)\).

2Global Strong Convergence Order

Theorem 2.1 (Global Strong Convergence of Euler–Maruyama)

Assumptions: (A1) The coefficients \(a(t, x)\) and \(b(t, x)\) satisfy the global Lipschitz and linear growth conditions of Lesson 6; (A2) \(a\) and \(b\) are \(1/2\)-Hölder continuous in time; (A3) The initial condition satisfies \(\E[|x_0|^2] < \infty\).

Statement: The Euler–Maruyama continuous-time interpolant \(\bar{Y}\) converges strongly to the exact solution \(X\) with order \(\gamma = 1/2\):

\[ \E\left[ \sup_{0 \le t \le H} |X_t - \bar{Y}_t| \right] \le C_H (\Delta t)^{1/2}, \]

where \(C_H\) is a positive constant independent of the step size \(\Delta t\). Moreover, if the diffusion coefficient is constant (additive noise \(b(t, x) \equiv \sigma\)), the global strong convergence order improves to \(\gamma = 1.0\):

\[ \E\left[ \sup_{0 \le t \le H} |X_t - \bar{Y}_t| \right] \le C_H' \, \Delta t. \]
Proof Sketch

Over the entire interval \([0, H]\), the accumulation of \(N = H / \Delta t\) local errors requires bounding \(\E[|X_t - \bar{Y}_t|^2]\). Subtracting the integral representations gives:

\[ X_t - \bar{Y}_t = \int_0^t \big( a(s, X_s) - a(\tau_s, \bar{Y}_{\tau_s}) \big) \, ds + \int_0^t \big( b(s, X_s) - b(\tau_s, \bar{Y}_{\tau_s}) \big) \, dB_s, \]

where \(\tau_s = t_k\) for \(s \in [t_k, t_{k+1})\). Expanding the difference using \(b(s, X_s) - b(\tau_s, \bar{Y}_{\tau_s}) = [b(s, X_s) - b(s, \bar{Y}_s)] + [b(s, \bar{Y}_s) - b(\tau_s, \bar{Y}_{\tau_s})]\), the first term is bounded by \(K |X_s - \bar{Y}_s|\) via Lipschitz continuity, while the second term accounts for the frozen grid value and is of order \(O(\Delta t^{1/2})\) due to Brownian fluctuations \(\bar{Y}_s - \bar{Y}_{\tau_s} \sim \Delta B\). Applying Gronwall's inequality yields \(\E[|X_t - \bar{Y}_t|^2] \le C \Delta t\). Taking square roots and applying Doob's martingale inequality produces the global strong order \(\gamma = 1/2\). When \(b\) is constant, \(b(s, X_s) - b(\tau_s, \bar{Y}_{\tau_s}) = 0\), leaving only the drift discretization error \(O(\Delta t)\), which integrates to order \(\gamma = 1.0\).

Lab 1 · Euler–Maruyama scheme: strong convergence order for additive noise

For the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE \(dX_t = \kappa(\theta - X_t)dt + \sigma dB_t\) with additive noise (\(\sigma = 0.30\)), the Euler–Maruyama scheme exhibits first-order strong convergence \(O(\Delta t)\). Evaluated across 2,000 paths (SIMULATED) against an \(N = 64\) fine reference grid, the root-mean-square error halves whenever the step size \(\Delta t\) is cut in half.

"""Lab 1 · Euler-Maruyama scheme: strong convergence order for additive noise.

For stochastic differential equations with additive noise (constant diffusion sigma), the Euler-Maruyama scheme
exhibits first-order strong convergence O(Delta t), in contrast to the O(Delta t^{1/2}) rate observed for multiplicative
noise. This lab simulates the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck equation dX_t = kappa(theta - X_t) dt + sigma dB_t with kappa = 2.0,
theta = 1.0, sigma = 0.30, x0 = 0.5 over [0, 1] across 2,000 paths from seed 42 (SIMULATED). Coarse Euler approximations
at step counts N in {4, 8, 16, 32} are evaluated against a fine reference grid of N_fine = 64 using identical Brownian
increments, printing root-mean-square terminal errors and verifying that error approximately halves with each
step-size halving.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def main() -> None:
    kappa = 2.0
    theta = 1.0
    sigma = 0.30
    x0 = 0.5
    horizon = 1.0
    n_paths = 2000
    seed = 42

    n_fine = 64
    dt_fine = horizon / n_fine
    sq_fine = math.sqrt(dt_fine)

    levels = [4, 8, 16, 32]
    print("Euler-Maruyama RMS error for OU against N = 64 fine grid:")

    for n_coarse in levels:
        stride = n_fine // n_coarse
        dt_coarse = horizon / n_coarse
        sq_errs: list[float] = []

        rng = random.Random(seed)
        for _ in range(n_paths):
            db_fine = [rng.gauss(0.0, sq_fine) for _ in range(n_fine)]

            # Fine reference Euler path
            x_fine = x0
            for db in db_fine:
                x_fine += kappa * (theta - x_fine) * dt_fine + sigma * db

            # Coarse Euler path on aggregated increments
            x_coarse = x0
            for k in range(n_coarse):
                db_c = sum(db_fine[k * stride : (k + 1) * stride])
                x_coarse += kappa * (theta - x_coarse) * dt_coarse + sigma * db_c

            sq_errs.append((x_coarse - x_fine) ** 2)

        rms = math.sqrt(sum(sq_errs) / n_paths)
        print(f"N = {n_coarse:2d}, dt = {dt_coarse:.4f}: RMS error = {rms:.5f}")


main()
Euler-Maruyama RMS error for OU against N = 64 fine grid:
N =  4, dt = 0.2500: RMS error = 0.05158
N =  8, dt = 0.1250: RMS error = 0.02244
N = 16, dt = 0.0625: RMS error = 0.00991
N = 32, dt = 0.0312: RMS error = 0.00362

The empirical results confirm first-order strong convergence for additive noise. At \(N = 4\) (\(\Delta t = 0.2500\)), the RMS error is \(0.05158\). Halving the step size to \(N = 8\) (\(\Delta t = 0.1250\)) cuts the error to \(0.02244\). Halving again to \(N = 16\) (\(\Delta t = 0.0625\)) reduces the error to \(0.00991\), and at \(N = 32\) (\(\Delta t = 0.0312\)) the error falls to \(0.00362\). Each step-size halving approximately halves the root-mean-square error, demonstrating strong order \(\gamma = 1.0\).

3Where the canon breaks

The standard convergence theory of the Euler–Maruyama scheme requires global Lipschitz bounds and an unconstrained state space. When applied to common financial diffusions, several structural failures emerge:

  • Domain Invariance and Boundary Violations: In geometric Brownian motion (\(dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dB_t\)) and Cox–Ingersoll–Ross interest rates (\(dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t)dt + \sigma \sqrt{r_t}dB_t\)), the exact continuous processes are strictly positive: \(\Prob(S_t > 0) = 1\). However, the linear Euler update \(S_{k+1} = S_k(1 + \mu \Delta t + \sigma \Delta B_k)\) produces negative values whenever \(\Delta B_k < -(1 + \mu \Delta t) / \sigma\). For square-root diffusions, a negative state immediately causes the square-root function \(\sqrt{r_{k+1}}\) to evaluate to a non-real complex number.
  • Non-Globally Lipschitz Drift Divergence: If the drift grows superlinearly (such as cubic mean reversion \(a(x) = x(1 - x^2)\)), the standard explicit Euler scheme becomes numerically unstable. In fact, Hutzenthaler, Jentzen, and Kloeden (2011) proved that for any superlinearly growing drift, the moments of the explicit Euler–Maruyama scheme diverge to infinity (\(\lim_{k \to \infty} \E[|Y_k|^p] = \infty\)) for any fixed step size \(\Delta t > 0\).
  • Multiplicative Noise Rate Cap: While additive noise diffusions achieve strong order \(1.0\), any diffusion with state-dependent volatility (\(b'(x) \neq 0\)) is fundamentally capped at strong order \(1/2\) under Euler–Maruyama. Overcoming this bottleneck requires higher-order schemes such as Milstein.

4Misconceptions

  1. “Euler–Maruyama has strong convergence order 1.0 like the deterministic Euler method.”

    In deterministic ODEs, the forward increment is \(O(\Delta t)\), giving global error \(O(\Delta t)\). In SDEs, Brownian increments scale as \(\Delta B = O(\Delta t^{1/2})\). The cross term between the Brownian fluctuation and the state-dependent diffusion produces an error of order \(O(\Delta t^{1/2})\), limiting global strong convergence to order \(1/2\) for multiplicative noise.

  2. “Drawing \(\Delta B_k\) from a two-point discrete distribution \(\pm \sqrt{\Delta t}\) preserves pathwise strong convergence.”

    Discrete two-point draws match the first two moments of Brownian motion (\(\E[\Delta B] = 0\) and \(\E[(\Delta B)^2] = \Delta t\)), which preserves weak convergence for expectations. However, a two-point random walk cannot match the continuous Gaussian sample path, completely destroying pathwise strong convergence.

  3. “Evaluating strong error by comparing coarse Euler simulation against fine Euler simulation requires no special path alignment.”

    Strong error is a pathwise expectation \(\E[|X_H - Y_H|]\). The coarse and fine simulations must be evaluated along the exact same Brownian trajectory. If coarse and fine paths are generated using independent random draws, the difference measures the distance between two independent diffusion paths (\(\sqrt{2} \times \text{variance}\)), not numerical discretization error.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · One-step Euler manual update

For an asset with SDE \(dS_t = 0.08 S_t dt + 0.30 S_t dB_t\), start at \(S_0 = 100\). Given step size \(\Delta t = 0.04\) and Brownian increment \(\Delta B_0 = -0.40\), compute the one-step Euler approximation \(S_1\).

Solution

Applying the Euler formula:

\[ S_1 = S_0 + 0.08 S_0 \Delta t + 0.30 S_0 \Delta B_0 = 100 + 100(0.08 \times 0.04) + 100(0.30 \times (-0.40)). \] \[ S_1 = 100 + 0.32 - 12.00 = 88.32. \]
M2 · Critical increment for negative price

For the same asset \(S_0 = 100\), \(\mu = 0.08\), \(\sigma = 0.30\), and \(\Delta t = 0.04\), find the threshold Brownian increment \(\Delta B^*\) below which \(S_1 < 0\).

Solution

We require \(S_1 = S_0(1 + \mu \Delta t + \sigma \Delta B) < 0\). Since \(S_0 > 0\), this is \(1 + 0.08(0.04) + 0.30 \Delta B < 0 \implies 1.0032 + 0.30 \Delta B < 0\). Solving for \(\Delta B\):

\[ \Delta B < -\frac{1.0032}{0.30} \approx -3.344. \]

Because \(\Delta B \sim \Normal(0, 0.04)\) has standard deviation \(\sqrt{0.04} = 0.20\), this corresponds to a \(z\)-score of \(-3.344 / 0.20 = -16.72\) standard deviations.

M3 · Strong error scaling under step-size reduction

An Euler–Maruyama simulation of a multiplicative-noise SDE exhibits an empirical strong root-mean-square error of \(0.040\) at step size \(\Delta t = 0.01\). Predict the expected error when the step size is reduced to \(\Delta t = 0.0025\).

Solution

For multiplicative noise, the strong convergence order is \(\gamma = 0.5\). The error scales as \(\mathrm{RMS} \propto (\Delta t)^{1/2}\). The ratio of step sizes is \(\frac{0.0025}{0.01} = \frac{1}{4}\). The expected error is:

\[ \mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{new}} \approx 0.040 \times \sqrt{0.25} = 0.040 \times 0.5 = 0.020. \]

Proof

P1 · Martingale property of the discrete stochastic term

Prove that the discrete sequence \(M_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b(t_k, Y_k) \Delta B_k\) is a discrete-time martingale with respect to the filtration \(\mathcal{F}_{t_n}\).

Solution

Conditioning on \(\mathcal{F}_{t_n}\): \(\E[M_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_n}] = \E[M_n + b(t_n, Y_n)\Delta B_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_n}] = M_n + b(t_n, Y_n)\E[\Delta B_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_n}]\). Because \(\Delta B_n = B_{t_{n+1}} - B_{t_n}\) is independent of \(\mathcal{F}_{t_n}\) with zero mean, \(\E[\Delta B_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_n}] = 0\). Thus \(\E[M_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_n}] = M_n\), proving the martingale property.

P2 · Additive noise exact diffusion integral

Prove that when \(b(t, x) \equiv \sigma\) is constant, the stochastic integral in the exact solution matches the discrete Euler stochastic sum identically: \(\int_0^H \sigma dB_s = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \sigma \Delta B_k\).

Solution

By linearity of the Itô integral, \(\int_0^H \sigma dB_s = \sigma \int_0^H dB_s = \sigma(B_H - B_0)\). The discrete sum is \(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \sigma(B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k}) = \sigma \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k}) = \sigma(B_H - B_0)\). The two terms are algebraically identical path-for-path, which eliminates the diffusion approximation error and elevates the strong convergence rate to order 1.0.

P3 · Local error order for linear drift

For \(dX_t = a X_t dt\), prove that the local one-step truncation error between the exact solution \(X_{\Delta t} = x_0 e^{a \Delta t}\) and the Euler update \(Y_1 = x_0(1 + a \Delta t)\) is \(O((\Delta t)^2)\).

Solution

Taylor expansion of the exponential gives \(e^{a \Delta t} = 1 + a \Delta t + \frac{1}{2}a^2 (\Delta t)^2 + O((\Delta t)^3)\). The error is \(|X_{\Delta t} - Y_1| = |x_0(e^{a \Delta t} - (1 + a \Delta t))| = |x_0| \left( \frac{1}{2}a^2 (\Delta t)^2 + O((\Delta t)^3) \right) \le \frac{1}{2} |x_0| a^2 (\Delta t)^2\), confirming local order 2 in step size.

Empirical

E1 · Strong error regression slope

In a numerical convergence experiment at step sizes \(\Delta t \in \{2^{-2}, 2^{-3}, 2^{-4}, 2^{-5}\}\), linear regression of \(\log_2(\mathrm{RMS})\) against \(\log_2(\Delta t)\) yields slope \(\hat{\gamma} = 0.985\). Interpret this empirical finding.

Solution

Since \(\mathrm{RMS} \approx C (\Delta t)^\gamma\), \(\log_2(\mathrm{RMS}) \approx \log_2 C + \gamma \log_2(\Delta t)\). The slope \(\hat{\gamma} = 0.985\) is within sampling error of \(1.0\), indicating that the equation being simulated has additive (or near-constant) noise where the theoretical strong convergence rate is \(1.0\) rather than \(0.5\).

E2 · Fine-grid reference bias

Explain why evaluating coarse Euler errors against a fine Euler grid \(N_{\mathrm{fine}} = 64\) introduces a slight plateau in the measured error as \(N_{\mathrm{coarse}} \to 32\).

Solution

The fine grid is itself an approximation to the continuous solution, carrying an intrinsic discretization error of order \(O(1 / N_{\mathrm{fine}})\). When \(N_{\mathrm{coarse}}\) approaches \(N_{\mathrm{fine}}\) (e.g. 32 vs 64), the error between coarse and fine reflects relative discrepancy rather than true distance to the continuous limit, slightly distorting the estimated slope.

E3 · Computational cost of halving strong error

To reduce pathwise strong simulation error by a factor of 10 for a multiplicative-noise SDE (\(\gamma = 0.5\)), by what factor must the number of time steps \(N\) be increased?

Solution

Since \(\mathrm{Error} \propto N^{-0.5}\), achieving a 10-fold error reduction requires \(N_{\mathrm{new}}^{-0.5} = 0.1 N_{\mathrm{old}}^{-0.5} \implies \sqrt{N_{\mathrm{new}} / N_{\mathrm{old}}} = 10 \implies N_{\mathrm{new}} / N_{\mathrm{old}} = 100\). The number of simulation steps (and hence CPU time) must increase 100-fold.

Research

R1 · Tamed Euler schemes for superlinear drift

Hutzenthaler et al. (2012) introduced the tamed Euler scheme \(Y_{k+1} = Y_k + \frac{a(Y_k)}{1 + |a(Y_k)|\Delta t}\Delta t + b(Y_k)\Delta B_k\). Explain how the taming factor restores stability when \(a(x) = -x^3\).

Solution

For standard Euler, when \(|Y_k|\) is large, the increment \(-Y_k^3 \Delta t\) dominates, causing explosive overshooting. In the tamed scheme, \(\left| \frac{a(Y_k)\Delta t}{1 + |a(Y_k)|\Delta t} \right| \le 1\) uniformly for all \(Y_k\). The taming factor prevents the deterministic increment from exceeding 1 in magnitude, ensuring that trajectories cannot explode while recovering the standard Euler scheme as \(\Delta t \to 0\).

R2 · Continuous-time Itô representation of interpolant

Write down the exact stochastic differential equation satisfied by the continuous-time Euler interpolant \(\bar{Y}_t\) and identify why it is an Itô process.

Solution

Define the step function \(\tau(t) = t_k\) for \(t \in [t_k, t_{k+1})\). The interpolant satisfies \(d\bar{Y}_t = a(\tau(t), \bar{Y}_{\tau(t)}) dt + b(\tau(t), \bar{Y}_{\tau(t)}) dB_t\). Because \(\tau(t) \le t\), the drift and diffusion coefficients are adapted, piecewise constant processes, making \(\bar{Y}_t\) a continuous Itô semi-martingale on \([0, H]\).

R3 · Multilevel Monte Carlo efficiency

Giles (2008) showed that Multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) reduces the computational complexity of estimating \(\E[g(X_H)]\) from \(O(\epsilon^{-3})\) to \(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) when the scheme has strong order \(\gamma = 1.0\). Why does strong convergence govern the computational complexity of estimating a weak expectation?

Solution

MLMC expresses the fine-grid expectation as a telescoping sum of level differences \(\E[g(X_\ell) - g(X_{\ell-1})]\). The variance of the level difference is \(\Var(g(X_\ell) - g(X_{\ell-1})) \le L^2 \E[|X_\ell - X_{\ell-1}|^2] = O((\Delta t)^{2\gamma})\). Higher strong order \(\gamma\) accelerates the decay of level variance, requiring drastically fewer Monte Carlo paths on the computationally expensive fine grids.

7Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the global strong convergence order \(\gamma\) of the Euler–Maruyama scheme for an SDE with general multiplicative noise \(b(t, x)\)?

    Options

    Because Brownian increments scale as \(\Delta B = O(\Delta t^{1/2})\), the cross term limits the global strong order to 1/2 for multiplicative noise.

  2. Under what condition does the global strong convergence order of Euler–Maruyama improve from \(1/2\) to \(1.0\)?

    Options

    When diffusion is constant, the discrete sum of Brownian increments matches the continuous Itô integral exactly, eliminating the diffusion error.

  3. Why can Euler–Maruyama simulation of geometric Brownian motion produce negative prices?

    Options

    Gaussian increments have unbounded support \((-\infty, \infty)\), admitting large negative draws that drive the linear update below zero.

  4. What is the theoretical strong convergence order \(\gamma\) of Euler–Maruyama for multiplicative noise diffusions?

    The strong convergence order is \(\gamma = 0.5\).

  5. What is the distribution of the Brownian increment \(\Delta B_k = B_{t_{k+1}} - B_{t_k}\) in an Euler step?

    Options

    By the definition of standard Brownian motion, increments over duration \(\Delta t\) are normally distributed with variance \(\Delta t\).

  6. What occurs when the explicit Euler–Maruyama scheme is applied to an SDE with superlinearly growing drift, such as \(a(x) = -x^3\)?

    Options

    Hutzenthaler et al. (2011) proved that explicit Euler diverges in moment norms for superlinear drifts, necessitating tamed or implicit schemes.

  7. How must sample paths be constructed when estimating empirical strong error \(\E[|X_H - Y_H|]\)?

    Options

    Strong error is a pathwise expectation that measures the distance between solutions on the identical realization \(\omega\).

  8. If the step size \(\Delta t\) is cut by a factor of 16 for a scheme with strong order \(\gamma = 0.5\), by what factor does the strong error decrease?

    Options

    Since error scales as \((\Delta t)^{0.5}\), reducing \(\Delta t\) by 16 reduces the error by \(\sqrt{16} = 4\).
