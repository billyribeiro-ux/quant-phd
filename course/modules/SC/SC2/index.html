Itô Calculus & SDEs

In continuous-time quantitative finance, asset prices, interest rates and risk factors are modeled as solutions to stochastic differential equations driven by Brownian motion. Ordinary Riemann–Stieltjes calculus fails because Brownian sample paths have infinite first variation and non-zero quadratic variation. This module builds stochastic calculus from the foundation: defining the Itô integral, proving the Itô isometry, establishing Itô's lemma with its characteristic second-order correction term, solving geometric Brownian motion and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes, proving existence and uniqueness of SDE solutions via Picard iteration and Gronwall's lemma, and formulating the Euler–Maruyama and Milstein numerical schemes alongside strong and weak convergence theory.

  • Prerequisites: SC1 Stochastic Processes. Prerequisite closure includes SC1, M1, M2, M4, M5, M6 and P0.1.
  • Unlocks: SC3 Change of Measure, SC4 Continuous-Time Asset Pricing, SC5 Jump Processes, SC6 Stochastic Control, STUDIO-SC.
  • Project: Itô derivations workbook and checking engine, graded by autograder and rubric
  • Data: none. The inline labs, project workbook and autograder compute from analytical closed forms, exact algebraic identities and simulated paths labeled SIMULATED.

72 h lessons · 28 h project (28% of the 100 h module)

Objectives

By the end of SC2 you can:

  1. Define the Itô integral for simple adapted processes, and derive its linearity, martingale property, and the Itô isometry in \(L^2\) (Lessons 1 & 2).
  2. Derive Itô's lemma for scalar and multivariate diffusions, and calculate exact closed-form differentials accounting for non-vanishing quadratic variation (Lesson 3).
  3. Solve canonical linear and non-linear SDEs—including geometric Brownian motion and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process—and compute their transient and stationary moments (Lessons 4 & 5).
  4. Establish existence and pathwise uniqueness of strong solutions under global Lipschitz and linear growth conditions using Picard iteration and Gronwall's inequality (Lesson 6).
  5. Implement the Euler–Maruyama and Milstein numerical discretization schemes, derive their local truncation errors, and prove their global strong convergence orders (Lessons 7 & 8).
  6. Distinguish pathwise strong convergence from distributional weak convergence, and verify both theoretical convergence rates empirically via simulation (Lesson 9).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions. Seven evaluate prerequisites from SC1, M6, M5, M4 and M2. Two are skill checks, marked as such, testing the technical derivation skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (requiring at least eight correct answers out of nine). Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80 percent mastery bar and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. What is the quadratic variation \([B, B]_H\) of standard Brownian motion on \([0, H]\)?

    Options

    By SC1 (quadratic variation), the sums of squared increments of standard Brownian motion converge in \(L^2\) to the interval length \(H\).

  2. For standard Brownian motion \((B_t)_{t \ge 0}\) adapted to its natural filtration \((\mathcal{F}_t)\), which process is a martingale?

    Options

    In SC1 (continuous-time martingales), \(\E[B_t^2 - t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = B_s^2 - s\) for \(s \le t\), so \(B_t^2 - t\) is a martingale.

  3. What is the expectation \(\E[B_H]\) of standard Brownian motion at horizon \(H = 4.0\)?

    Standard Brownian motion starts at zero and has increments \(\Normal(0, H)\), so \(\E[B_H] = 0\).

  4. For a standard normal variable \(Z \sim \Normal(0, 1)\), what is its second moment \(\E[Z^2]\)?

    For a standard normal distribution, \(\E[Z^2] = \Var(Z) + (\E[Z])^2 = 1 + 0 = 1\). If scaled by \(\sqrt{2}\), the second moment is 2.

  5. In a filtration \((\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}\), what structural relationship holds between the σ-algebras for \(s \le t\)?

    Options

    A filtration is an increasing family of σ-algebras representing accumulating information over time (M6, filtration).

  6. What property of a metric space guarantees that every Cauchy sequence converges to a limit in the space?

    Options

    A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence has a limit in the space (M5, completeness of the reals).

  7. By Taylor's theorem, what is the second derivative term in the expansion of \(f(x + \Delta x)\) around \(x\)?

    Options

    Taylor's theorem gives \(f(x + \Delta x) = f(x) + f'(x)\Delta x + \tfrac{1}{2}f''(x)(\Delta x)^2 + \dots\) (M2, Taylor's theorem).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): For geometric Brownian motion \(dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dB_t\), applying Itô's lemma to \(Y_t = \log S_t\) introduces what drift correction term?

    Options

    Since \(f(S) = \log S\), \(f'(S) = 1/S\) and \(f''(S) = -1/S^2\). The Itô term is \(\tfrac{1}{2} f''(S) (dS_t)^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} (-1/S^2) (\sigma^2 S^2 dt) = -\tfrac{1}{2} \sigma^2 dt\).

  9. Skill check (rung 2): For a scalar diffusion \(dX_t = a(t, X_t) dt + b(t, X_t) dB_t\) and \(f \in C^2\), what is the second-order term contributing to the drift of \(df(t, X_t)\)?

    Options

    By Itô's formula, the quadratic variation of the diffusion \((dX_t)^2 = b(t, X_t)^2 dt\) multiplies \(\tfrac{1}{2} \partial_{xx} f\), yielding \(\tfrac{1}{2} b(t, X_t)^2 \partial_{xx} f dt\).

Lessons

Nine lessons of 8 study hours each, summing to 72 lesson hours. Every lesson includes inline labs with reproducible Python twins, full proofs or explicitly referenced sketches, edge cases where standard calculus breaks, common misconceptions with evidence, four tiers of problem sets with complete worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz feeding your spaced-repetition deck.

L01 · 8 hThe Itô Integral for Simple IntegrandsAdapted step processes, left-endpoint non-anticipating evaluation, linearity, and proof of the zero expectation and martingale properties. L02 · 8 hItô Isometry and the L2 ExtensionProof of the Itô isometry for step processes, the Cauchy extension to square-integrable adapted integrands, and contrasting left versus right Riemann sums. L03 · 8 hItô's LemmaThe discrete algebraic identity for squared Brownian motion, derivation of the second-order correction term, and the general scalar diffusion chain rule. L04 · 8 hGeometric Brownian MotionThe fundamental asset price SDE, closed-form solution via log transform, log-normal moment trajectories, and pathwise Euler comparison. L05 · 8 hThe Ornstein–Uhlenbeck ProcessMean-reverting linear SDE solved by integrating factors, transient mean and variance formulas, and the stationary Gaussian variance limit. L06 · 8 hExistence and Uniqueness of SDE SolutionsGlobal Lipschitz and linear growth conditions, full proof of Gronwall's inequality, pathwise uniqueness, and uniform Picard convergence. L07 · 8 hThe Euler–Maruyama SchemeTime discretization of SDEs, local truncation error analysis, and the distinction between additive noise order 1 and general strong order 1/2. L08 · 8 hThe Milstein SchemeItô–Taylor expansion of state-dependent diffusion, the double stochastic integral correction, and achieving strong convergence order 1. L09 · 8 hStrong versus Weak ConvergenceCurriculum lab: pathwise strong error versus distributional weak error for Euler on geometric Brownian motion across refined dyadic grids.

The project ladder

The module concludes with the Itô derivations workbook project (28 hours). Rungs 1 and 2 develop the algebraic and numerical verification workflow tested in the placement diagnostic. Meeting the placement mastery bar (80%) and answering both skill checks permits skipping directly to Rung 3.

  1. Worked example

    Derivation and numerical verification of the log-price transform on geometric Brownian motion for a SIMULATED instance, annotated with the justification for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    Complete the missing Itô correction and residual check lines in the verification script, graded instantly in the browser.

  3. Independent project

    Derive the continuous-time dynamics of ten transformed processes spanning geometric Brownian motion and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck bases, implement the checking engine ito_checks.py, and produce a formal derivations workbook. Graded by autograder and rubric. Project specification.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the engine to two-dimensional coupled diffusions, such as the stochastic ratio of correlated geometric Brownian motions driven by a planar Wiener process.

Data status

No real market data is used in SC2. As a pure continuous-time stochastic calculus module, all laboratory demonstrations, project checks and autograder tests compute from exact algebraic identities, closed-form moment formulas, or deterministic pseudo-random paths labeled SIMULATED.

Core reading

Both sources are cited at book level from the verified registry. Øksendal provides the foundational measure-theoretic stochastic calculus and SDE theory, while Kloeden & Platen provides the numerical discretization framework and convergence proofs.
