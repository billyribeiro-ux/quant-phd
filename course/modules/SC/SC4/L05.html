Explicit Finite-Difference Schemes & CFL Stability

Finite difference methods replace continuous differential operators with algebraic difference quotients on discrete grids. The explicit forward-time central-space (FTCS) scheme is conceptually the simplest numerical algorithm: it updates each grid point at the next time step directly from neighboring values at the current step without solving a linear system. However, this algebraic simplicity comes at a steep price: explicit schemes suffer from a rigid stability restriction (the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy / CFL condition), where violating the time step bound \(\Delta t \le \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta x^2\) triggers catastrophic, exponential blow-up of high-frequency roundoff noise.

1The Forward-Time Central-Space (FTCS) Discretization

Consider the diffusion equation for \(u(x, \tau)\) in time-to-maturity \(\tau \in [0, T]\) and spatial coordinate \(x \in [x_{\min}, x_{\max}]\):

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial \tau} = D \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}, \quad D > 0. \]

Discretize the space-time domain into a uniform Cartesian grid:

\[ x_j = x_{\min} + j \Delta x, \quad j = 0, 1, \dots, N; \qquad \tau_n = n \Delta t, \quad n = 0, 1, \dots, M, \]

where \(\Delta x = (x_{\max} - x_{\min}) / N\) and \(\Delta t = T / M\). Denote the discrete numerical approximation by \(u_j^n \approx u(x_j, \tau_n)\).

Definition 5.1 (Explicit finite-difference scheme)

An explicit finite-difference scheme is a time-stepping scheme in which the solution at the advanced time level \(n+1\) at any interior node \(j\) is given as an explicit function solely of known values at the current time level \(n\):

\[ u_j^{n+1} = \mathcal{H}\left( u_{j-1}^n, u_j^n, u_{j+1}^n \right). \]

For the diffusion equation, the Forward-Time Central-Space (FTCS) scheme approximates the temporal derivative with a forward difference and the spatial derivative with a central second difference:

\[ \frac{u_j^{n+1} - u_j^n}{\Delta t} = D \frac{u_{j+1}^n - 2u_j^n + u_{j-1}^n}{\Delta x^2}. \]
Theorem 5.2 (Local Truncation Error of FTCS)

Statement: For a smooth solution \(u \in C^{4,2}\), the local truncation error of the FTCS scheme is first-order in time and second-order in space: \(O(\Delta t + \Delta x^2)\).

Proof

Expanding the discrete operators via Taylor's theorem about \((x_j, \tau_n)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{u(x_j, \tau_{n+1}) - u(x_j, \tau_n)}{\Delta t} &= \frac{\partial u}{\partial \tau} + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \tau^2} + O(\Delta t^2), \\ \frac{u(x_{j+1}, \tau_n) - 2u(x_j, \tau_n) + u(x_{j-1}, \tau_n)}{\Delta x^2} &= \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\Delta x^2}{12}\frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} + O(\Delta x^4). \end{aligned} \]

Subtracting the PDE \(\frac{\partial u}{\partial \tau} - D \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} = 0\) leaves the truncation residual:

\[ T_j^n = \frac{\Delta t}{2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \tau^2} - D \frac{\Delta x^2}{12}\frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} + O(\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^4) = O(\Delta t + \Delta x^2). \]

2Probabilistic Interpretation: Equivalence to Trinomial Trees

Rearranging the FTCS formula to isolate \(u_j^{n+1}\):

\[ u_j^{n+1} = \lambda u_{j-1}^n + (1 - 2\lambda) u_j^n + \lambda u_{j+1}^n, \]

where the dimensionless parameter \(\lambda \coloneqq \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2}\) is the mesh ratio.

Define the coefficients \(p_d \coloneqq \lambda\), \(p_m \coloneqq 1 - 2\lambda\), and \(p_u \coloneqq \lambda\). Then:

\[ u_j^{n+1} = p_d u_{j-1}^n + p_m u_j^n + p_u u_{j+1}^n. \]

Observe that the sum of the weights is identically 1: \(p_d + p_m + p_u = \lambda + (1 - 2\lambda) + \lambda = 1\). This establishes that the FTCS update is mathematically identical to a single backward step on an explicit trinomial tree (Hull 2021).

Remark 5.3 (Positivity and Discrete Maximum Principle)

For the weights \((p_d, p_m, p_u)\) to represent valid transition probabilities, they must be non-negative. This requires:

\[ p_m = 1 - 2\lambda \ge 0 \iff \lambda \le \frac{1}{2}. \]

When \(\lambda \le 1/2\), the update \(u_j^{n+1}\) is a convex combination of \(\{u_{j-1}^n, u_j^n, u_{j+1}^n\}\). Consequently, \(\min_k u_k^n \le u_j^{n+1} \le \max_k u_k^n\). This guarantees the discrete maximum principle: no spurious extrema can be generated, and the numerical scheme is strictly monotone and contractive in the \(L^\infty\) norm.

3The Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) Stability Constraint

Theorem 5.4 (FTCS Stability Criterion)

Statement: The explicit FTCS scheme for the diffusion equation \(u_\tau = D u_{xx}\) is stable in the \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) norms if and only if:

\[ \lambda = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} \le \frac{1}{2} \iff \Delta t \le \frac{\Delta x^2}{2D}. \]

If \(\lambda > 1/2\), high-frequency spatial modes explode exponentially as \(\left| 1 - 4\lambda \right|^n \to \infty\).

Proof

Consider a Fourier mode perturbation \(u_j^n = G^n e^{i k x_j}\), where \(k\) is the wavenumber and \(G\) is the amplification factor per time step. Substituting into the FTCS scheme:

\[ \begin{aligned} G^{n+1} e^{i k x_j} &= G^n e^{i k x_j} + \lambda G^n \left( e^{i k (x_j + \Delta x)} - 2 e^{i k x_j} + e^{i k (x_j - \Delta x)} \right) \\ G &= 1 + \lambda \left( e^{i k \Delta x} - 2 + e^{-i k \Delta x} \right) = 1 + 2\lambda (\cos(k \Delta x) - 1) \\ &= 1 - 4\lambda \sin^2\left(\frac{k \Delta x}{2}\right). \end{aligned} \]

For numerical stability, the magnitude of the amplification factor must satisfy \(|G| \le 1\) for all Fourier frequencies \(\xi = k \Delta x \in [0, \pi]\). Since \(\sin^2(\xi / 2) \in [0, 1]\):

\[ -1 \le 1 - 4\lambda \sin^2\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right) \le 1. \]

The upper bound \(1 - 4\lambda \sin^2(\xi/2) \le 1\) holds automatically because \(\lambda > 0\). The lower bound requires \(1 - 4\lambda \sin^2(\xi/2) \ge -1 \implies 4\lambda \sin^2(\xi/2) \le 2\). The most restrictive mode is the highest grid frequency (the Nyquist mode \(\xi = \pi\), where adjacent nodes alternate signs), yielding \(\sin^2(\pi/2) = 1\):

\[ 4\lambda \le 2 \implies \lambda \le \frac{1}{2}. \]

If \(\lambda > 1/2\), the Nyquist mode has amplification factor \(G(\pi) = 1 - 4\lambda < -1\). Its amplitude grows as \(|G(\pi)|^n = (4\lambda - 1)^n > 1\), doubling or tripling at each time step and causing instantaneous explosive blow-up.

4The Full Black–Scholes FTCS Scheme & Computational Penalty

For the full Black–Scholes PDE in asset coordinates \(S\):

\[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial \tau} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + r S \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - r V, \]

the FTCS discretization on node \((S_j, \tau_n)\) with central spatial differences is:

\[ \frac{V_j^{n+1} - V_j^n}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_j^2 \frac{V_{j+1}^n - 2V_j^n + V_{j-1}^n}{\Delta S^2} + r S_j \frac{V_{j+1}^n - V_{j-1}^n}{2\Delta S} - r V_j^n. \]

Collecting terms produces the explicit update:

\[ V_j^{n+1} = a_j V_{j-1}^n + b_j V_j^n + c_j V_{j+1}^n, \]

where:

\[ \begin{aligned} a_j &= \frac{1}{2}\Delta t \left( \frac{\sigma^2 S_j^2}{\Delta S^2} - \frac{r S_j}{\Delta S} \right), \\ b_j &= 1 - \Delta t \left( \frac{\sigma^2 S_j^2}{\Delta S^2} + r \right), \\ c_j &= \frac{1}{2}\Delta t \left( \frac{\sigma^2 S_j^2}{\Delta S^2} + \frac{r S_j}{\Delta S} \right). \end{aligned} \]

Positivity of \(b_j\) requires \(\Delta t \le \frac{\Delta S^2}{\sigma^2 S_{\max}^2 + r \Delta S^2}\). Because \(S_{\max}\) is large, this restriction forces \(\Delta t\) to be extraordinarily small, scaling as \(O(\Delta S^2 / S_{\max}^2)\).

Remark 5.5 (The Quadratic Computational Penalty)

Suppose an analyst halves the spatial step \(\Delta S \to \Delta S / 2\) to achieve higher spatial accuracy. To maintain CFL stability, \(\Delta t\) must be reduced by a factor of 4: \(\Delta t \to \Delta t / 4\). The total number of space-time grid updates scales as \(N \times M = N \times O(N^2) = O(N^3)\). A \(10\times\) refinement in spatial resolution requires a \(1000\times\) increase in computational wall-clock time. This severe quadratic scaling penalty motivates the transition to implicit methods.

Lab 1 · CFL Stability Threshold and Explosive Instability in Explicit FTCS Scheme

An explicit FTCS scheme on the diffusion equation remains bounded and accurate when the mesh ratio lambda = dt / dx^2 <= 0.50, but explodes exponentially when lambda = 0.55 > 0.50 (SIMULATED).

"""Lab SC4-L05-1 · CFL Stability Threshold and Explosive Instability in Explicit FTCS Scheme.

Demonstrates the sharp stability transition for the explicit Forward Time, Central Space (FTCS)
finite difference scheme on the diffusion equation u_t = u_xx. High-frequency spatial modes
(excited by machine roundoff or non-smooth data) explode exponentially when the mesh ratio
lambda = dt / dx^2 exceeds 0.50 (SIMULATED).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

import numpy as np


def run_explicit_ftcs(lambda_mesh: float, n_space: int, n_steps: int) -> tuple[float, float, bool]:
    """Run explicit FTCS scheme on u_t = u_xx with initial fundamental and high-frequency modes.

    Initial condition: u(x, 0) = sin(pi * x) + 1e-4 * sin((N - 1) * pi * x).
    Returns (max_numerical_value, max_error_to_fundamental, is_bounded).
    """
    dx = 1.0 / n_space
    dt = lambda_mesh * (dx**2)
    x = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n_space + 1)

    # Initial condition: fundamental mode plus tiny high-frequency Nyquist perturbation
    u = np.sin(math.pi * x) + 1e-4 * np.sin((n_space - 1) * math.pi * x)

    # Advance n_steps
    for _ in range(n_steps):
        u_next = np.zeros_like(u)
        u_next[1:-1] = u[1:-1] + lambda_mesh * (u[2:] - 2.0 * u[1:-1] + u[:-2])
        u = u_next
        if np.any(np.isnan(u)) or np.max(np.abs(u)) > 1e12:
            break

    t_final = n_steps * dt
    exact = math.exp(-(math.pi**2) * t_final) * np.sin(math.pi * x)
    max_val = float(np.max(np.abs(u)))
    max_err = float(np.max(np.abs(u - exact)))
    is_bounded = max_val < 10.0

    return max_val, max_err, is_bounded


def main() -> None:
    n_space = 40
    n_steps = 150

    lambda_stable = 0.48
    lambda_unstable = 0.55

    max_val_s, max_err_s, is_b_s = run_explicit_ftcs(lambda_stable, n_space, n_steps)
    max_val_u, _, is_b_u = run_explicit_ftcs(lambda_unstable, n_space, n_steps)

    print(f"Explicit FTCS on u_t = u_xx with {n_space} grid intervals over {n_steps} steps:")
    print(f"  Stable case (lambda = {lambda_stable:.2f} <= 0.50):")
    print(f"    Maximum solution amplitude: {max_val_s:.6f}")
    print(f"    Maximum error vs exact:     {max_err_s:.6e}")
    print(f"    Stable and bounded:         {is_b_s}")
    print(f"  Unstable case (lambda = {lambda_unstable:.2f} > 0.50):")
    print(f"    Maximum solution amplitude: {max_val_u:.4e}")
    print(f"    Explosive blow-up observed: {not is_b_u}")


main()

5Where the canon breaks

The explicit FTCS method breaks down completely in several practical quantitative settings:

  • Convection-Dominated PDEs (Peclet Number Instability): When drift dominates diffusion (e.g., low volatility \(\sigma \to 0\) or deep out-of-the-money regimes), the cell Peclet number \(Pe \coloneqq \frac{|r S| \Delta S}{\sigma^2 S^2} = \frac{r \Delta S}{\sigma^2 S}\) can exceed 2. When \(Pe > 2\), the down-node coefficient \(a_j\) becomes negative: \[ a_j = \frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta S}\left( \frac{\sigma^2 S}{\Delta S} - r \right) < 0. \] Negative weights destroy monotonicity and produce severe spurious non-physical spatial oscillations even when the temporal CFL condition \(\lambda \le 1/2\) is fully satisfied. Fixing this requires upwind differencing or artificial diffusion.
  • Multi-Dimensional Basket PDEs: In \(d\) spatial dimensions with cross-diffusion terms \(\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j S_i S_j\), explicit stability requires \(\Delta t \le \frac{1}{2d} \frac{\Delta x^2}{\sigma^2}\). For \(d = 3\), this forces \(\Delta t\) to be so microscopic that explicit stepping is virtually unusable.
  • Stiff Boundary Conditions: At absorbing barrier boundaries \(V(B) = 0\), steep payoff gradients excite high-frequency Fourier modes that push the numerical solution past the stability boundary if \(\lambda\) is even marginally near \(0.50\).

6Misconceptions

  1. “Refining the spatial grid automatically improves the accuracy of an explicit FTCS solver.”

    Refining the spatial grid without simultaneously reducing the time step violates the CFL condition. If \(\Delta x\) is halved while \(\Delta t\) remains fixed, the mesh ratio \(\lambda = \Delta t / \Delta x^2\) quadruples, instantly transforming a stable simulation into an explosive exponential divergence.

  2. “Instability in FTCS can be prevented by numerically clamping option prices between 0 and \(S\).”

    Artificial clipping or clamping merely masks the underlying numerical pathology without correcting it. An unstable scheme has amplification factor \(|G| > 1\), distorting gradients, Delta, and Gamma. Clamping produces a flattened, unphysical solution with completely bogus Greeks.

  3. “Small local truncation error \(O(\Delta t + \Delta x^2)\) guarantees that the global numerical solution will converge.”

    By the Lax Equivalence Theorem, consistency (small local truncation error) guarantees convergence if and only if the scheme is stable. Without stability (\(\lambda \le 1/2\)), the local truncation error is overwhelmed by exponentially growing roundoff errors, regardless of how small \(\Delta t\) and \(\Delta x\) are locally.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Maximum stable time step calculation

For the pure diffusion equation \(u_\tau = 0.04 u_{xx}\) on a spatial grid with \(\Delta x = 0.02\), compute the maximum permissible time step \(\Delta t_{\max}\) under explicit FTCS.

Solution

The CFL stability condition is \(\lambda = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} \le \frac{1}{2}\). Here \(D = 0.04\) and \(\Delta x = 0.02\):

\[ \Delta t_{\max} = \frac{\Delta x^2}{2 D} = \frac{(0.02)^2}{2 \times 0.04} = \frac{0.0004}{0.08} = 0.005. \]

Any time step \(\Delta t > 0.005\) will cause explosive exponential instability.

M2 · Trinomial transition probabilities

Given \(\Delta x = 0.1\), \(D = 0.5\), and \(\Delta t = 0.008\), calculate the trinomial weights \(p_d, p_m, p_u\) in the FTCS update and verify that they form a valid probability distribution.

Solution

Compute the mesh ratio:

\[ \lambda = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} = \frac{0.5 \times 0.008}{(0.1)^2} = \frac{0.004}{0.01} = 0.40. \]

The weights are:

\[ p_d = \lambda = 0.40, \quad p_u = \lambda = 0.40, \quad p_m = 1 - 2\lambda = 1 - 2(0.40) = 0.20. \]

Since \(p_d, p_m, p_u \ge 0\) and \(p_d + p_m + p_u = 0.40 + 0.20 + 0.40 = 1.0\), they form a valid non-negative discrete probability distribution.

M3 · Amplification factor evaluation

Compute the Von Neumann amplification factor \(G(\xi)\) for the FTCS scheme with \(\lambda = 0.60\) at the Nyquist frequency \(\xi = \pi\), and calculate the amplification after 20 time steps.

Solution

The amplification factor is \(G(\xi) = 1 - 4\lambda \sin^2(\xi/2)\). At \(\xi = \pi\):

\[ G(\pi) = 1 - 4(0.60) \sin^2(\pi/2) = 1 - 2.40(1) = -1.40. \]

Since \(|G(\pi)| = 1.40 > 1\), the mode grows exponentially. After 20 steps, the amplitude factor is:

\[ G(\pi)^{20} = (-1.40)^{20} \approx 836.68. \]

The Nyquist noise is magnified by over 800 times in just 20 steps.

P1 · Monotonicity and maximum principle

Prove that if \(\lambda \le 1/2\), the FTCS scheme is strictly monotone: if \(u_j^n \ge v_j^n\) for all \(j\), then \(u_j^{n+1} \ge v_j^{n+1}\) for all \(j\).

Solution

The update difference is:

\[ u_j^{n+1} - v_j^{n+1} = \lambda (u_{j-1}^n - v_{j-1}^n) + (1 - 2\lambda)(u_j^n - v_j^n) + \lambda (u_{j+1}^n - v_{j+1}^n). \]

When \(\lambda \le 1/2\), all three coefficients \(\lambda, 1 - 2\lambda, \lambda\) are non-negative. Because each difference \(u_k^n - v_k^n \ge 0\) by hypothesis, the linear combination of non-negative quantities with non-negative weights is non-negative: \(u_j^{n+1} - v_j^{n+1} \ge 0\). This proves strict monotonicity.

P2 · Cell Peclet number condition for positivity

For the advection-diffusion PDE \(u_\tau = D u_{xx} - c u_x\), discretize using central differences in space. Prove that the lower-neighbor coefficient \(p_d\) is positive if and only if the cell Peclet number \(Pe \coloneqq \frac{|c| \Delta x}{D} < 2\).

Solution

Discretizing the advection-diffusion operator explicitly:

\[ u_j^{n+1} = u_j^n + \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2}(u_{j+1}^n - 2u_j^n + u_{j-1}^n) - \frac{c \Delta t}{2\Delta x}(u_{j+1}^n - u_{j-1}^n). \]

Collecting the coefficient of \(u_{j-1}^n\):

\[ p_d = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} + \frac{c \Delta t}{2\Delta x} = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2}\left( 1 + \frac{c \Delta x}{2D} \right). \]

Similarly, the coefficient of \(u_{j+1}^n\) is \(p_u = \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2}(1 - \frac{c \Delta x}{2D})\). For both coefficients to remain non-negative regardless of the sign of \(c\), we must have \(1 - \frac{|c|\Delta x}{2D} > 0 \implies \frac{|c|\Delta x}{D} < 2\), which is precisely \(Pe < 2\).

P3 · Discrete L2 energy dissipation under CFL

Define the discrete energy \(E_n \coloneqq \sum_{j} (u_j^n)^2 \Delta x\). Prove via Parseval's identity that \(E_{n+1} \le E_n\) for all \(n\) under periodic boundary conditions if and only if \(\lambda \le 1/2\).

Solution

By Parseval's theorem for the discrete Fourier transform, \(E_n = \sum_j (u_j^n)^2 \Delta x = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi |\hat{u}^n(\xi)|^2 d\xi\). Under the FTCS scheme, each Fourier component evolves as \(\hat{u}^{n+1}(\xi) = G(\xi) \hat{u}^n(\xi)\), so:

\[ E_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi |G(\xi)|^2 |\hat{u}^n(\xi)|^2 d\xi. \]

If \(\lambda \le 1/2\), \(|G(\xi)| \le 1\) for all \(\xi \in [-\pi, \pi]\), so \(|G(\xi)|^2 \le 1\), implying \(E_{n+1} \le E_n\) (strict energy dissipation). Conversely, if \(\lambda > 1/2\), \(|G(\pi)| = |1 - 4\lambda| > 1\), so for an initial condition containing the Nyquist mode, \(E_{n+1} > E_n\), proving necessity.

Empirical

E1 · Scaling of compute time with grid refinement

A quant prices an option using explicit FTCS with \(N = 100\) spatial steps and \(M = 2000\) time steps, which takes 0.05 seconds. If the spatial grid is refined to \(N = 500\) to reduce pricing error, estimate the required number of time steps \(M_{\text{new}}\) and the new execution time.

Solution

Refining from \(N = 100\) to \(N = 500\) reduces \(\Delta x\) by a factor of 5 (\(\Delta x_{\text{new}} = \Delta x / 5\)). To maintain the same stability ratio \(\lambda = \Delta t / \Delta x^2\), \(\Delta t\) must decrease by \(5^2 = 25\), so \(M_{\text{new}} = 25 \times M = 25 \times 2000 = 50,000\) time steps.

The total number of cell updates increases by \(5 \times 25 = 125\) times. The new execution time scales to \(0.05 \times 125 = 6.25\) seconds.

E2 · Upwind differencing for advection stabilization

Suppose in a low-volatility contract \(Pe = 5.0 > 2\). Formulate the first-order upwind difference approximation for \(c u_x\) when \(c > 0\) and show why it eliminates negative coefficients.

Solution

When \(c > 0\), information propagates from left to right. The backward (upwind) difference is \(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \approx \frac{u_j^n - u_{j-1}^n}{\Delta x}\). Substituting into the explicit scheme:

\[ u_j^{n+1} = u_j^n + \frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2}(u_{j+1}^n - 2u_j^n + u_{j-1}^n) - \frac{c \Delta t}{\Delta x}(u_j^n - u_{j-1}^n). \]

The coefficient of \(u_{j-1}^n\) is \(\frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} + \frac{c \Delta t}{\Delta x} > 0\), and the coefficient of \(u_{j+1}^n\) is \(\frac{D \Delta t}{\Delta x^2} > 0\). Both spatial neighbor weights are strictly positive for all \(c > 0\) regardless of \(Pe\), completely eliminating spurious spatial oscillations at the cost of introducing \(O(\Delta x)\) numerical diffusion.

E3 · Roundoff noise seed explosion

In double precision floating-point arithmetic, machine epsilon is \(\epsilon_{\text{mach}} \approx 10^{-16}\). If \(\lambda = 0.60\), so \(G(\pi) = -1.40\), estimate how many time steps are required for a roundoff perturbation of \(10^{-16}\) to grow to \(O(1)\).

Solution

The roundoff error grows as \(\epsilon_n = \epsilon_{\text{mach}} |G(\pi)|^n = 10^{-16} (1.40)^n\). Setting \(\epsilon_n = 1\):

\[ (1.40)^n = 10^{16} \implies n \ln(1.40) = 16 \ln(10) \implies n = \frac{16 \times 2.3026}{0.33647} \approx \frac{36.84}{0.33647} \approx 109.5. \]

In only 110 time steps, invisible 64-bit roundoff noise explodes into full order-unity error, completely destroying the calculation.

Research

R1 · Modified equation analysis and artificial diffusion

Perform modified equation analysis on the upwind advection scheme \(u_\tau + c u_x = D u_{xx}\) to find the effective numerical diffusion coefficient \(D_{\text{num}}\).

Solution

Expanding the upwind differencing \(\frac{u_j - u_{j-1}}{\Delta x} = u_x - \frac{\Delta x}{2} u_{xx} + O(\Delta x^2)\) and forward time \(\frac{u^{n+1} - u^n}{\Delta t} = u_\tau + \frac{\Delta t}{2} u_{\tau\tau} + O(\Delta t^2)\). Since \(u_\tau \approx -c u_x\), \(u_{\tau\tau} \approx c^2 u_{xx}\). Substituting into the discrete scheme:

\[ u_\tau + c u_x = \left( D + \frac{c \Delta x}{2} - \frac{c^2 \Delta t}{2} \right) u_{xx} + O(\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^2). \]

The effective numerical diffusion is \(D_{\text{num}} = D + \frac{c \Delta x}{2}(1 - Cr)\), where \(Cr = c \Delta t / \Delta x\) is the Courant number. Upwinding introduces positive artificial viscosity \(\frac{c \Delta x}{2}\), which stabilizes oscillations but smears sharp option payoffs (such as digital strikes).

R2 · Godunov's barrier theorem

State Godunov's theorem regarding linear numerical schemes for convection equations and explain its implications for option pricing with non-smooth payoffs.

Solution

Godunov's Theorem: Linear numerical schemes for solving partial differential equations, having the property of not generating new extrema (monotone / Total Variation Diminishing), can be at most first-order accurate. Implication for finance: any linear scheme that is second-order accurate (such as central FTCS or Crank–Nicolson) must necessarily produce spurious oscillations near non-smooth payoff kinks (digital call discontinuity or barrier knockout). To achieve second-order accuracy without oscillations, one must employ non-linear TVD flux limiters or specialized damping such as Rannacher smoothing.

R3 · Runge–Kutta Chebyshev (RKC) explicit stabilization

Explain how Runge–Kutta Chebyshev (RKC) high-order explicit methods extend the CFL stability boundary from \(\Delta t \le O(\Delta x^2)\) to \(\Delta t \le O(s^2 \Delta x^2)\) using \(s\) internal stages.

Solution

Standard explicit Euler uses a stability polynomial \(R(z) = 1 + z\), whose stability interval along the negative real axis is \([-2, 0]\). An \(s\)-stage Runge–Kutta Chebyshev method constructs the stability polynomial from shifted Chebyshev polynomials \(T_s(x)\), which possess the minimax optimal property of staying bounded by 1 over the largest possible negative interval. The stability boundary scales as \(\beta_s \approx 0.65 s^2\). For \(s = 10\) stages, the stable time step is enlarged by \(\approx 65\times\), enabling explicit stepping without tridiagonal linear solvers while maintaining large time steps.

9Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the CFL stability threshold for the explicit FTCS scheme on the diffusion equation \(u_\tau = D u_{xx}\)?

    Options

    By Von Neumann stability analysis, the Nyquist amplification factor is \(1 - 4\lambda\). Requiring \(|1 - 4\lambda| \le 1\) gives \(\lambda \le 1/2\).

  2. What is the local truncation error of the standard FTCS scheme?

    Options

    Forward difference in time has \(O(\Delta t)\) error; central difference in space has \(O(\Delta x^2)\) error.

  3. Under the trinomial tree interpretation of FTCS, what is the middle probability \(p_m\)?

    Options

    The FTCS update is \(u_j^{n+1} = \lambda u_{j-1}^n + (1 - 2\lambda)u_j^n + \lambda u_{j+1}^n\), so \(p_m = 1 - 2\lambda\).

  4. What is the maximum value of the mesh ratio \(\lambda = D \Delta t / \Delta x^2\) for which FTCS preserves the discrete maximum principle?

    When \(\lambda \le 0.5\), all trinomial weights are non-negative, preserving the maximum principle.

  5. If the spatial grid spacing \(\Delta x\) is halved, how must \(\Delta t\) be adjusted to maintain CFL stability?

    Options

    Because \(\Delta t \le \lambda \Delta x^2\), halving \(\Delta x\) reduces \(\Delta x^2\) by 4, forcing a 4-fold reduction in \(\Delta t\).

  6. What happens to high-frequency spatial noise when \(\lambda > 1/2\) in FTCS?

    Options

    At \(\xi = \pi\), \(G(\pi) = 1 - 4\lambda < -1\). The sign alternates and amplitude compounds exponentially as \(|G|^n\).

  7. What condition on the cell Peclet number \(Pe = |c| \Delta x / D\) prevents negative coefficients in central-differenced advection?

    Options

    Central differencing of advection gives weights \(1 \pm Pe / 2\). For non-negativity, \(Pe < 2\).

  8. What is the total computational complexity of solving a 1D parabolic PDE with explicit FTCS to time \(T\) on a grid of \(N\) spatial points?

    Options

    There are \(N\) spatial points and \(M = O(N^2)\) time steps to satisfy CFL, leading to \(N \times O(N^2) = O(N^3)\) total operations.
