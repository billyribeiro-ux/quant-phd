PDEs & Feynman–Kac

Continuous-time derivative pricing and term structure modeling stand on a deep mathematical duality: conditional expectations under risk-neutral diffusion measures equal classical solutions to linear parabolic partial differential equations. This module builds the theoretical bridge and numerical machinery: deriving the infinitesimal generator and Kolmogorov backward and forward (Fokker–Planck) equations, proving the Feynman–Kac representation theorem, establishing physical and far-field boundary conditions, constructing explicit, fully implicit, and Crank–Nicolson finite-difference schemes, conducting rigorous Von Neumann Fourier stability analysis, and implementing Rannacher smoothing to guarantee quadratic convergence on non-smooth and discontinuous option payoffs.

  • Prerequisites: SC2 Itô Calculus & SDEs, M8 Numerical Methods & Scientific Computing. Prerequisite closure includes SC2, SC1, M8, M6, M5, M4, M3, M2, M1, FC2, FC1, and P0.1.
  • Unlocks: CS6 Scientific Computing in Practice, DV4 Numerical Pricing (C++), STUDIO-SC.
  • Project: PDE pricer: Build a Crank–Nicolson pricer with Rannacher smoothing for European and barrier options, and certify its convergence order (autograded).
  • Data: none. The inline labs, project workbook and autograder compute from analytical closed forms, exact differential identities, and simulated grids labeled SIMULATED.

64 h lessons · 16 h project (20% of the 80 h module)

Objectives

By the end of SC4 you can:

  1. Formulate the infinitesimal generator \(\mathcal{A}\) of a multi-dimensional diffusion, derive Dynkin's formula, and prove the Kolmogorov backward equation for transition densities and terminal value problems (Lesson 1).
  2. Construct the formal \(L^2\) adjoint operator \(\mathcal{A}^*\), derive the Kolmogorov forward (Fokker–Planck) equation, and verify the conservation of probability mass and stationary distributions (Lesson 2).
  3. Prove the classical Feynman–Kac theorem using Itô's formula and martingale representations, and connect risk-neutral conditional expectations to the Black–Scholes parabolic PDE (Lesson 3).
  4. Formulate Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and absorbing barrier boundary conditions, and transform degenerate asset-coordinate PDEs into regular constant-coefficient heat equations via log-moneyness (Lesson 4).
  5. Discretize parabolic pricing PDEs with the explicit forward-time central-space (FTCS) scheme, interpret grid weights as transition probabilities, and derive the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) stability limit \(\Delta t \le \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta x^2\) (Lesson 5).
  6. Implement the fully implicit backward-time central-space (BTCS) scheme, and solve the resulting tridiagonal system in \(O(N)\) operations using the Thomas algorithm (Lesson 6).
  7. Conduct Von Neumann Fourier stability analysis on the Crank–Nicolson \(\theta\)-family, derive the complex amplification factor \(G(\xi)\), and prove unconditional \(A\)-stability and second-order convergence \(O(\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^2)\) (Lesson 7).
  8. Diagnose spurious high-frequency numerical oscillations induced by non-smooth payoff initial conditions, and apply Rannacher smoothing to restore strict monotonicity and quadratic convergence (Lesson 8).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions. Eight evaluate prerequisites from SC2, M8, SC1, M6, and FC2. Two are skill checks, marked as such, testing the core numerical PDE stepping and tridiagonal matrix skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least eight correct answers). Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80 percent mastery bar and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. What is the infinitesimal generator \(\mathcal{A}\) applied to \(f \in C^2(\R)\) for the geometric Brownian motion \(dX_t = \mu X_t dt + \sigma X_t dB_t\)?

    Options

    By Itô's lemma in SC2, the drift of \(f(X_t)\) is \(\mu X_t f'(X_t) + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 X_t^2 f''(X_t)\), which defines the generator.

  2. Under the Black–Scholes model, what risk-neutral drift replaces the physical drift \(\mu\) in the pricing PDE?

    Options

    In FC2 and SC2, the no-arbitrage replication argument eliminates the expected return \(\mu\) in favor of the risk-free rate \(r\).

  3. In the Thomas algorithm for solving a tridiagonal system \(Ax = d\) with main diagonal \(b_i\), subdiagonal \(a_i\), and superdiagonal \(c_i\), what is the forward elimination denominator update for modified diagonal \(b'_i\)?

    Options

    Gaussian elimination without pivoting on a tridiagonal matrix subtracts the multiple \(a_i / b'_{i-1}\) times row \(i-1\), modifying the diagonal to \(b_i - a_i c_{i-1} / b'_{i-1} = b_i - a_i c'_{i-1}\).

  4. What weighting parameter \(\theta\) corresponds to the second-order Crank–Nicolson scheme in the general two-level \(\theta\)-method \((I - \theta \Delta t \mathcal{L}) u^{n+1} = (I + (1-\theta)\Delta t \mathcal{L}) u^n\)?

    Options

    \(\theta = 1/2\) centers the time difference at the midpoint \(t_{n+1/2}\), canceling the first-order truncation error and achieving \(O(\Delta t^2)\) accuracy.

  5. What is the maximum stable mesh ratio \(\lambda = \Delta t / \Delta x^2\) for the explicit FTCS scheme applied to the standard heat equation \(u_t = u_{xx}\)?

    By the CFL stability criterion and maximum principle, \(\lambda \le 1/2 = 0.5\) is required for stability and non-negative discrete transition probabilities.

  6. For an up-and-out call option with knock-out barrier \(B > K\), what Dirichlet boundary condition holds on the barrier \(S = B\) for all \(t < H\)?

    Options

    The contract instantly terminates with zero payout the moment the underlying touches the barrier, imposing the absorbing Dirichlet boundary \(V(t, B) = 0\).

  7. Which mathematical property allows the Feynman–Kac formula to represent discounted conditional expectations as PDE solutions?

    Options

    Applying Itô's formula to \(e^{-rs} V(s, X_s)\) sets the drift coefficient to zero, which produces the parabolic pricing PDE.

  8. What computational complexity per time step does the Thomas algorithm achieve for a tridiagonal linear system with \(N\) interior spatial nodes?

    Options

    Because the matrix is tridiagonal, forward elimination and backward substitution each require only a single linear sweep of \(O(N)\) floating-point operations.

  9. By put–call parity, what is the asymptotic value \(V(t, S) - (S - K e^{-r(H-t)})\) of a European call option as \(S \to \infty\)?

    As \(S \to \infty\), the put value approaches 0, so \(C(t, S) - (S - K e^{-r(H-t)}) \to 0\). (Answer 0).

  10. Why does the raw Crank–Nicolson scheme produce spurious numerical oscillations when applied to digital options or barrier payoffs?

    Options

    While Crank–Nicolson is \(A\)-stable (\(|G| \le 1\)), it is not \(L\)-stable: \(G(\pi) \to -1\) causes high-frequency Fourier components to alternate signs without decaying.

Lesson map

Eight lessons of 8 study hours each (64 hours total), developing the continuum from stochastic generators to production finite-difference solvers:

  1. L01 · The Infinitesimal Generator & Kolmogorov Backward Equation 8 h

    From Itô's formula to the differential generator \(\mathcal{A}\), Dynkin's formula, transition densities, and the derivation of the backward parabolic equation. Introduces Kolmogorov backward equation.

  2. L02 · The Kolmogorov Forward (Fokker–Planck) Equation 8 h

    The \(L^2\) adjoint operator \(\mathcal{A}^*\), forward evolution of probability distributions, mass conservation, and invariant measures. Introduces Kolmogorov forward (Fokker–Planck) equation.

  3. L03 · The Feynman–Kac Formula & Parabolic PDEs 8 h

    The stochastic representation bridge connecting conditional expectations of discounted terminal payoffs to terminal-boundary PDE problems. Introduces Feynman–Kac formula.

  4. L04 · Boundary Conditions for Pricing PDEs 8 h

    Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin conditions, absorbing barrier options, far-field asymptotic domain truncation, and the dimensionless log-moneyness heat transformation. Introduces Boundary conditions for pricing PDEs.

  5. L05 · Explicit Finite-Difference Schemes & CFL Stability 8 h

    Forward Time, Central Space (FTCS) discretization, probabilistic trinomial tree interpretation, and the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy stability barrier. Introduces Explicit finite-difference scheme.

  6. L06 · Implicit Finite-Difference Schemes & Tridiagonal Solvers 8 h

    Backward Time, Central Space (BTCS) formulation, unconditional stability, and the \(O(N)\) Thomas algorithm for tridiagonal systems. Introduces Implicit finite-difference scheme.

  7. L07 · The Crank–Nicolson Scheme & Von Neumann Stability 8 h

    The \(\theta = 1/2\) midpoint scheme, Fourier mode decomposition, complex amplification factor \(G(\xi)\), and quadratic convergence \(O(\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^2)\). Introduces Crank–Nicolson scheme and Von Neumann stability analysis.

  8. L08 · Non-Smooth Payoffs & Rannacher Smoothing 8 h

    Failure of \(L\)-stability on discontinuous initial data, high-frequency spurious oscillations, and Rannacher implicit damping to restore quadratic convergence. Introduces Rannacher smoothing.

The project ladder

The four-rung project ladder leads from a guided worked example to an industrial PDE option pricer and research stretch:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance of the Crank–Nicolson scheme with the Thomas algorithm applied to a European call option on seed 0, tracing grid construction, boundary conditions, and linear solver steps.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    The same task with the tridiagonal forward elimination denominator and theta-weighting step blanked with ___, graded instantly in the browser.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Build a production Crank–Nicolson pricer with Rannacher smoothing for European and barrier options, and certify its second-order convergence order against Black–Scholes analytical benchmarks across 50 seeded instances. See project specification.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the finite-difference solver to free-boundary early exercise problems (American options via Projected SOR) or two-dimensional coupled diffusions (Heston stochastic volatility or multi-asset basket options via ADI splitting).
