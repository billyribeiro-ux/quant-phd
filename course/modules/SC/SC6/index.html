Stochastic Control

Continuous-time stochastic control provides the mathematical foundation for optimal dynamic decision-making under uncertainty in quantitative finance. While discrete-time dynamic programming recurses backward across finite epochs, continuous-time control optimizes over diffusion paths, collapsing the Bellman recursion into the non-linear Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) partial differential equation. This module develops the rigorous analytical and numerical machinery of stochastic control: formulating controlled stochastic differential equations, establishing the Dynamic Programming Principle, deriving the HJB equation from Itô's formula, proving classical verification theorems, solving the foundational Merton portfolio and consumption problem in closed form, and building numerical finite-difference policy iteration solvers robust to portfolio borrowing constraints.

  • Prerequisites: SC2 Itô Calculus & SDEs and M7 Optimization & Convexity. Prerequisite closure includes SC1, SC2, M1, M2, M3, M4, M5, M6, M7, and P0.1.
  • Introduces: Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation, verification theorem, Merton portfolio and consumption problem, Merton fraction, policy iteration.
  • Project: HJB solver for constrained Merton portfolio selection, graded by autograder (24 hours).
  • Data: none. Laboratory benchmarks, project test suites, and autograder instances compute from exact analytical closed forms and deterministic pseudo-random paths labeled SIMULATED.

Objectives

By the end of SC6 you can:

  1. Formulate controlled stochastic differential equations with progressive measurability constraints, and state the continuous-time Dynamic Programming Principle (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) non-linear partial differential equation from the Dynamic Programming Principle and Itô's formula (Lesson 2).
  3. Prove classical verification theorems for smooth candidate value functions, establishing the optimality of feedback controls and transversality conditions (Lesson 3).
  4. Solve the continuous-time Merton portfolio and consumption problem for CRRA power and logarithmic utility via algebraic ansatz reduction (Lesson 4).
  5. Calculate the closed-form Merton fraction \(\pi^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\), and verify continuous-time two-fund separation and horizon invariance (Lesson 5).
  6. Discretize continuous-time HJB equations using monotone upwind finite differences that preserve the M-matrix maximum principle (Lesson 6).
  7. Implement Howard's policy iteration algorithm with borrowing constraints, and prove its quadratic convergence to the constrained viscosity solution (Lesson 7).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions evaluating prerequisites from SC2, M7, M6, M5, M2, and testing practical numerical control skills. Mastery bar is 80% (requiring at least 8 correct answers). Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. For a scalar diffusion \(dX_t = b(X_t) dt + \sigma(X_t) dB_t\) and a function \(v \in C^2\), what is the second-order diffusion term in the differential \(dv(X_t)\) under Itô's lemma?

    Options

    By Itô's formula (SC2), \((dX_t)^2 = \sigma(X_t)^2 dt\), contributing the characteristic half-variance second derivative term.

  2. In discrete dynamic programming (M7), what fundamental property guarantees that a policy optimal from time \(t\) onward is optimal regardless of the prior history?

    Options

    Bellman's principle of optimality states that an optimal policy has the property that whatever the initial state and initial decision, the remaining decisions must constitute an optimal policy with regard to the state resulting from the first decision.

  3. If a continuous-time process \((M_t)_{t \ge 0}\) satisfies \(\E[M_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] \le M_s\) for all \(s \le t\), what class of process is \(M\)?

    Options

    A process whose conditional expectation decreases over time is a supermartingale (M6, martingale).

  4. For an investor with CRRA relative risk aversion \(\gamma = 2.0\), risk-free rate \(r = 0.02\), and a stock with expected return \(\mu = 0.06\) and volatility \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the unconstrained Merton fraction \(\pi^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\)?

    \(\pi^* = (0.06 - 0.02) / (2.0 \times 0.20^2) = 0.04 / 0.08 = 0.50\).

  5. What fixed-point result establishes that a contraction mapping on a complete metric space possesses a unique fixed point?

    Options

    By M5 (Banach fixed-point theorem), every contraction mapping on a complete metric space has a unique fixed point reached by Picard iteration.

  6. What condition on the gradient and Hessian of an objective function \(f(w)\) characterizes an unconstrained local maximum of a concave function?

    Options

    A critical point of a smooth function is a local maximum if the gradient vanishes and the Hessian is negative semidefinite (M2, Hessian).

  7. In infinite-horizon discounted utility maximization with subjective discount rate \(\rho\), what condition prevents explosive expected utility (transversality)?

    Options

    Transversality requires the discounted continuation value to vanish at infinity, ruling out explosive infinite utility.

  8. For an M-matrix \(A\), what property does its inverse \(A^{-1}\) possess?

    Options

    By definition of an M-matrix, the off-diagonals are non-positive and the inverse is element-wise non-negative, preserving the maximum principle.

  9. Skill check (rung 1): In finite-difference discretization of the drift term \(b(w) V'(w)\), why does an upwind scheme use a forward difference when \(b(w) \ge 0\) and a backward difference when \(b(w) < 0\)?

    Options

    Upwinding aligns the difference stencil with the characteristic flow of the drift, ensuring all off-diagonal entries have the correct sign to guarantee M-matrix stability and satisfy the discrete maximum principle.

  10. Skill check (rung 2): When maximizing the quadratic Hamiltonian in portfolio fraction \(\pi\) subject to borrowing constraint \(\pi \le \pi_{\max}\), how is the constrained optimal allocation \(\pi^*\) obtained from unconstrained \(\pi_{\text{unc}} = -\frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2} \frac{V'(w)}{w V''(w)}\)?

    Options

    Because the objective is strictly concave in \(\pi\), the constrained maximizer over the convex interval \([0, \pi_{\max}]\) is simply the projection (clamping) of the unconstrained stationary point onto the constraint set.

Lessons

Seven lessons of 8 study hours each, summing to 56 lesson hours. Every lesson contains inline Python labs, complete mathematical proofs with explicit hypotheses, edge cases where standard models fail, common misconceptions grounded in literature, four tiers of problem sets with complete worked solutions, and an interactive retrieval quiz.

L01 · 8 h Controlled Diffusions & Dynamic Programming Controlled SDEs, admissible feedback controls, the continuous-time Dynamic Programming Principle, and proof of the martingale optimality property of the value process. L02 · 8 h The Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman Equation Infinitesimal generator of controlled diffusions, rigorous derivation of the non-linear HJB partial differential equation, and definition of the continuous-time Hamiltonian. L03 · 8 h Verification Theorems & Classical Solutions Classical verification theorem, localization with stopping times, sufficiency of HJB solutions, and infinite-horizon transversality conditions. L04 · 8 h The Merton Portfolio & Consumption Problem Intertemporal portfolio and consumption choice under uncertainty, wealth dynamics, CRRA power utility ansatz, and reduction of the HJB PDE to an algebraic root equation. L05 · 8 h The Merton Fraction & Closed-Form Allocation Curriculum lab: closed-form Merton fraction \(\pi^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\), verification against simulated expected utility, two-fund separation, and horizon invariance. L06 · 8 h Finite Difference Discretization & Monotonicity Upwind finite-difference stencils, preservation of the M-matrix maximum principle, failure of central differences under high Peclet numbers, and viscosity solution convergence. L07 · 8 h Policy Iteration & Constrained Stochastic Control Howard's policy iteration algorithm, interpretation as Newton's method on the Bellman operator, quadratic convergence, borrowing constraints, and exact constrained benchmarks.

The project ladder

The module culminates in the HJB solver project (24 hours). The four-rung ladder develops the theoretical, numerical and algorithmic architecture tested in the placement diagnostic:

  1. Worked example

    Fully solved continuous-time Merton problem on a 91-node wealth grid on a SIMULATED instance, demonstrating upwind stencil assembly, policy iteration, and closed-form comparison.

  2. Completion problem

    Complete the missing policy improvement and borrowing constraint projection routines in the verification lab, evaluated and graded instantly in the browser.

  3. Independent project

    Build the full HJB policy iteration solver hjb_solver.py for the constrained Merton problem, passing all 10 acceptance tests across 50 independent pseudorandom seeds. Graded by autograder. Project specification.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the policy iteration engine to state-dependent stochastic volatility (Heston or Stein–Stein dynamics) or non-linear transaction costs.

Core reading

Merton (1969, 1971) introduces the continuous-time consumption and portfolio problem and derives the closed-form Merton fraction. Pham (2009) provides the modern rigorous measure-theoretic treatment of continuous-time stochastic control, verification theorems, viscosity solutions, and numerical discretization.
