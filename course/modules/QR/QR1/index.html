Research Data Engineering

Quantitative research models fail in production primarily due to information contamination: historical backtests that observe future data revisions, unadjusted corporate action price breaks, or microsecond tick arrival order artifacts that could not have been traded in live markets. QR1 establishes the mathematical foundations and production architecture of research data engineering. You will formalize timestamp semantics across observation, publication, and intake instants; construct bitemporal data models that store immutable real-time data vintages; derive corporate action adjustment factors that preserve economic returns under capital restructuring; resolve prevailing market microstructure states from asynchronous tick feeds; and conduct rigorous alternative-data due diligence to eliminate survivorship and backfill biases. The module culminates in building a vintage-aware ingestion engine proving that historical queries cannot observe revisions prior to their publication timestamps.

  • Prerequisites: CS3 Data, Databases & SQL, CS4 Software Engineering & Architecture, S0 Data Quality, Cleaning & Preprocessing.
  • Project: Vintage-Aware Ingestion Engine, graded by autograder.
  • Hours: 60 study hours (48 h across 6 lessons, 12 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formalize the three fundamental instants of a datum—event time, publication time, and intake time—and prove the Information Set Invariance Theorem guaranteeing zero look-ahead bias under contemporaneous publication filtrations.
  2. Implement the bitemporal data model separating valid event date from transaction publication time, proving Proposition 2.1 (point-in-time visibility invariance under future data revisions).
  3. Construct deterministic temporal ASOF joins between asynchronous trade streams and prevailing information records using total lexicographical ordering on timestamp and sequence counters.
  4. Derive the split return invariance identity and compute backward cumulative adjustment factors eliminating artificial price breaks while preserving true economic returns.
  5. Evaluate market microstructure tick streams across exchange matching engine and feed handler intake timestamps, resolving out-of-order execution records without arrival skew.
  6. Audit alternative and vendor datasets using the six-pillar due diligence framework to identify and quantify survivorship, restatement, and backfill biases.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite CS3, CS4, and S0 foundations, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In a bitemporal store, an advance GDP estimate for 2025-Q4 is published at 2026-01-29T13:30:00Z with value 2.1%. A second revised estimate is published at 2026-02-26T13:30:00Z with value 2.4%. What value must a point-in-time query for 2025-Q4 return at decision cut-off instant 2026-02-15T00:00:00Z?

    Options

    At cut-off 2026-02-15, the second estimate (published 2026-02-26) was unknown to the market. Querying 2.4% would constitute look-ahead bias. The visible value is 2.1%. Rung 1 skill check.

  2. During the US spring Daylight Saving Time transition on the second Sunday of March in New York, what occurs when a parser receives the local wall-clock timestamp "2026-03-08 02:15"?

    Options

    At 02:00:00 on the second Sunday of March, the wall clock skips immediately to 03:00:00. The interval [02:00, 03:00) does not exist and must raise a ValueError. Rung 2 skill check.

  3. Under the Atlas A2 data layer contract from CS3, how are ties broken when two observations for the same series share identical observation dates and retrieval timestamps?

    Options

    Atlas A2 enforces a deterministic tie-breaking rule: when timestamps are identical, the later ingested row (higher observations.seq) prevails.

  4. A stock trades at \$120 on day \(t-1\). On ex-date \(t\), a pure 2-for-1 stock split occurs, and the stock opens at \$60. What is the backward-adjusted simple return \(\tilde{R}_t = \frac{\tilde{P}_t}{\tilde{P}_{t-1}} - 1\)?

    Under backward adjustment, \(\tilde{P}_t = 60 / 1.0 = 60\), while \(\tilde{P}_{t-1} = 120 / 2.0 = 60\). The adjusted return is \(60/60 - 1 = 0.0\).

  5. What distinguishes the matching engine timestamp \(t_{\mathrm{ex}}\) from the feed handler intake timestamp \(t_{\mathrm{intake}}\) in microstructure tick data?

    Options

    \(t_{\mathrm{ex}}\) reflects matching engine chronological order, while \(t_{\mathrm{intake}}\) incorporates variable network delivery jitter and reordering.

  6. Why does evaluating an equity momentum strategy on current S&P 500 index constituents over historical data generate survivorship bias?

    Options

    Conditioning constituent membership on survival to the present eliminates poorly performing, distressed, and bankrupt firms from the historical sample, severely overstating backtest performance.

  7. In CS4 software engineering principles, what core property distinguishes property-based testing with randomized seeds from example-based unit testing?

    Options

    Property-based testing verifies mathematical invariants (e.g. split return invariance, monotonicity, idempotence) across diverse synthesized inputs.

  8. What is a corporate action break as defined in S0 data cleaning?

    Options

    A corporate action break represents an unadjusted price jump resulting from capital events rather than market trading supply and demand.

  9. If a stock experiences a 2-for-1 split on date \(e_1\) and a 2-for-1 split on date \(e_2\) with \(e_2 > e_1\), what is the cumulative backward adjustment factor for a date \(d < e_1\)?

    The cumulative factor is \(F_d = \prod_{k: e_k > d} r_k = 2.0 \times 2.0 = 4.0\).

  10. What does the cryptographic manifest in research data stores provide?

    Options

    A cryptographic manifest records the exact SHA-256 digest of ingested raw payloads, detecting any byte-level mutation or silent data corruption.

Lessons

L01 · 8 h Timestamps, Time Zones & Look-Ahead Bias The three instants of a datum (\(t_E, t_P, t_R\)), UTC normalization, Daylight Saving Time spring gaps and autumn overlaps, information filtrations, and mathematical formalization of look-ahead bias. L02 · 8 h Data Vintages & Bitemporal Data Models Macroeconomic and corporate reporting revisions, Croushore & Stark real-time foundations, valid time vs transaction time, immutable snapshotting, and Proposition 2.1 visibility invariance. L03 · 8 h Point-in-Time Merges & Temporal Joins Asynchronous time series alignment, temporal ASOF joins, non-strict inequalities, deterministic sequence tie-breaking, binary search complexity, and equi-join failure modes. L04 · 8 h Corporate Actions & Adjustment Factors Stock splits, reverse splits, spin-offs, cash dividends, cumulative backward adjustment factor formulation, Derivation 4.1 split return invariance, and volume adjustments. L05 · 8 h Tick Data Architecture & Timestamp Semantics Microstructure trade and quote streams, exchange matching timestamps vs intake timestamps, network jitter and out-of-order resequencing, and total ordering on \((t, seq)\). L06 · 8 h Alternative-Data Due Diligence & Point-in-Time Integrity The six-pillar alternative data audit framework, point-in-time entity mapping, panel survivorship and Shumway delisting bias quantification, vendor restatements, and backfill contamination.
