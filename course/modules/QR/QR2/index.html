Backtesting Architecture

A backtest is not a profit predictor; it is a hypothesis rejection engine designed to prove that a strategy fails under real-world market mechanics. Most published and commercial quantitative strategies fail out-of-sample because backtesting architectures implicitly leak future information, ignore execution latencies, or treat cross-validation folds as independent identically distributed samples. QR2 establishes the rigorous engineering and econometric principles of institutional backtesting. You will compare vectorized array programming against state-machine discrete event simulation; enforce strict causal timestamp ordering and signal-to-execution lags; formulate nonlinear market impact and transaction cost models; conduct walk-forward validation; implement purged and embargoed cross-validation (CPCV); and design adversarial planted-lookahead test suites. The module culminates in building Atlas milestone A9: an institutional event-driven backtesting engine with automatic research integrity reporting.

  • Prerequisites: QR1 Research Data Engineering, S7 Research Integrity & Multiple Testing.
  • Project: Atlas A9: backtester, graded by autograder.
  • Hours: 90 study hours (66 h across 6 lessons, 24 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Analyze structural and mathematical distinctions between vectorized array operations and discrete event-driven backtesters, proving how continuous reinvestment and discrete lot constraints induce path-dependent tracking drift.
  2. Formalize causal timestamp semantics across information arrival, decision generation, and order execution, establishing strictly positive signal-to-execution lags to eliminate the trade-on-close lookahead fallacy.
  3. Formulate nonlinear transaction cost models combining exchange commissions, bid-ask spread crossing, and the square-root law of market impact, calculating strategy capacity ceilings.
  4. Design walk-forward optimization frameworks that measure parameter stability and Walk-Forward Efficiency (WFE) ratios across structural regime shifts.
  5. Implement purged and embargoed cross-validation (CPCV) under overlapping return labels, proving how purging removes boundary leakage and post-test embargoes neutralize autoregressive feature memory.
  6. Construct automated adversarial planted-lookahead test suites that inject forward-looking signals to verify the temporal integrity of backtesting engines.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite QR1 and S7 foundations, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In an event-driven backtesting engine with a declared signal-to-execution lag of \(\tau_{\text{lag}} = 1\) bar, a strategy generates a target allocation signal during bar \(t=10\) based on closing prices at \(t=10\). At which bar index is the resulting order earliest eligible for fill execution?

    Options

    Strict causal ordering dictates that an order submitted at bar \(t\) with lag \(\tau_{\text{lag}} = 1\) can execute no earlier than bar \(t + \tau_{\text{lag}} = t + 1\).

  2. Under the square-root law of market impact (\(\Delta P = Y \cdot \sigma \cdot P \sqrt{Q / V}\)), if an institutional trader increases order execution size \(Q\) by a factor of 4 while daily volume \(V\) and volatility \(\sigma\) remain unchanged, by what factor does the total dollar impact cost (\(Q \cdot \Delta P\)) increase?

    Options

    Price impact per share scales as \(\sqrt{Q}\). Multiplying by the number of executed shares \(Q\) yields total dollar impact cost scaling as \(Q \times Q^{0.5} = Q^{1.5}\). Thus \(4^{1.5} = 8\).

  3. What constitutes look-ahead bias in financial backtesting?

    Options

    Look-ahead bias is the methodological defect of conditioning decision rules on information that was not yet realized or published at the decision timestamp.

  4. In a bitemporal point-in-time database, what distinguishes event time from publication time?

    Options

    Bitemporal modeling explicitly separates the effective reference date of an economic phenomenon from the dissemination instant when market agents could first observe it.

  5. What does the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (\(\DSR\)) adjust for relative to the standard Sharpe ratio?

    Options

    The Deflated Sharpe Ratio penalizes the observed Sharpe ratio for multiple testing across \(N\) trials and adjusts the asymptotic variance for return non-normality.

  6. What does the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (\(\PBO\)) measure in the CSCV framework?

    Options

    In CSCV, \(\PBO\) is the empirical probability that the best-performing in-sample trial configuration ranks in the bottom half of out-of-sample outcomes (relative rank logit \(\le 0\)).

  7. Why must backward corporate action adjustment factors be applied multiplicatively to historical prices rather than subtractively?

    Options

    Capital restructuring splits alter share counts by a multiplicative ratio. Applying multiplicative factors guarantees that percentage returns \(P_t / P_{t-1} - 1\) are invariant across split boundaries.

  8. What does the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure control across multiple hypothesis tests?

    Options

    The Benjamini–Hochberg step-up procedure guarantees \(\E[\text{FDP}] \le q\), controlling the False Discovery Rate under independent or positive-dependent test statistics.

  9. In an ASOF join between an execution stream and a quote book, why must the join condition use an inequality on publication timestamp?

    Options

    An ASOF join matches each event record with the most recent state record where \(\text{state.time} \le \text{event.time}\), enforcing chronological precedence.

  10. Why does serial correlation in asset returns violate the standard independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption of ordinary bootstrap and k-fold cross-validation?

    Options

    When returns or features possess memory, random shuffling mixes future and past, while unpurged boundaries allow overlapping labels to leak across folds.

  1. Lesson 1 Vectorized vs. Event-Driven Backtesting Architectures Array programming models vs. state-machine discrete event simulation. Tracking drift, path-dependent cash constraints, and stop-loss breakdown. 11 hours
  2. Lesson 2 Timestamp Semantics, Information Arrival & Execution Lags Strict chronological timestamp pipelines. The trade-on-close fallacy, signal-to-execution lag enforcement, and alpha decay under latency. 11 hours

  5. Lesson 5 Purged & Embargoed Cross-Validation, CPCV Label overlap leakage in financial ML. Purging overlapping label intervals, post-test embargo buffers, and Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV). 11 hours
  6. Lesson 6 Planted-Lookahead Testing & Backtester Verification Adversarial verification of backtesting systems. Injecting planted future signals, lead-lag cross-correlation diagnostics, and automated CI integrity suites. 11 hours
