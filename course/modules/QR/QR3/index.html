Statistical Learning for Finance

Financial machine learning operates in an extreme statistical environment: signal-to-noise ratios are routinely below 0.05, non-stationarity erodes historical relationships, and high feature collinearity produces spurious correlations. Standard deep learning and statistical tools imported naively from computer vision or natural language processing fail out-of-sample because they are designed for high-SNR regimes with stationary data generating processes. QR3 establishes the rigorous statistical learning foundations required for institutional quantitative research. You will formulate the bias-variance decomposition under massive noise; construct purged and combinatorial cross-validation engines that isolate overlapping label leakage; derive Ridge and LASSO shrinkage estimators for sparse factor selection; build decision trees, random forests, and gradient boosted ensembles tailored to noisy asset pricing panels; architect feedforward neural networks with Bayesian weight decay; calculate exact Shapley values for local model interpretability; and manage point-in-time feature pipelines. The module culminates in building Atlas milestone A17: a comprehensive, lookahead-free machine learning pipeline with automated model registry governance and research integrity auditing.

  • Prerequisites: S2 Linear Regression, M7 Optimization, CS4 Software Engineering & Reproducible Research, QR2 Backtesting Architecture.
  • Project: Atlas A17: ML pipeline, graded by autograder.
  • Hours: 130 study hours (78 h across 8 lessons, 52 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formulate the bias-variance decomposition of mean squared error and analyze its pathology under ultra-low signal-to-noise ratio regimes characteristic of cross-sectional and time-series asset returns.
  2. Implement purged, embargoed, and combinatorial cross-validation schemes that eliminate information leakage arising from overlapping holding-period labels and serial dependence.
  3. Derive and compute regularized linear predictors (Ridge \(L_2\) shrinkage and LASSO \(L_1\) coordinate descent) for high-dimensional factor selection.
  4. Construct recursive binary partitioning trees and analyze how tree depth and leaf regularization govern variance inflation in financial datasets.
  5. Train ensemble models (bagged Random Forests and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to capture nonlinear interaction effects across asset characteristics while penalizing complex splits.
  6. Architect feedforward neural network regressors with regularized backpropagation, early stopping, and dropout tailored to noisy macroeconomic and market panels.
  7. Calculate exact Shapley values and TreeSHAP feature attributions to satisfy local additivity, evaluate feature importance, and audit machine learning black-box predictions.
  8. Design an integrated Atlas A17 ML pipeline incorporating a point-in-time feature store, purged CV, model registry, and SHAP report generation plugged into Atlas A8 integrity audits and A9 backtesting.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite S2, M7, CS4, and QR2 foundations, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In cyclical coordinate descent for LASSO, what is the exact output of the soft-thresholding operator \(\mathcal{S}(z, \gamma) = \operatorname{sign}(z) \max(|z| - \gamma, 0)\) evaluated at \(z = -2.5\) with penalty parameter \(\gamma = 0.5\)?

    Options

    By definition, when \(z < -\gamma\), the soft-thresholding operator shrinks the negative value toward zero: \(\mathcal{S}(z, \gamma) = z + \gamma = -2.5 + 0.5 = -2.0\).

  2. In Purged \(K\)-Fold cross-validation, suppose a test fold spans sample indices \([j_{\text{start}}, j_{\text{end}}] = [60, 99]\) and return labels are computed over forward 5-day windows (\(h=5\) bars). What is the starting index of the left-purge window in the candidate training set?

    Options

    Left-purging removes any training observation \(i\) whose label window \([i, i + h - 1]\) intersects the test window. For \(h=5\), \(i + 5 - 1 \ge 60 \implies i \ge 56\). Thus, purging starts at index 56.

  3. Under the Gauss–Markov theorem, what optimality property does the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator satisfy?

    Options

    The Gauss–Markov theorem establishes that under spherical errors, OLS has the lowest sampling variance among the class of linear unbiased estimators (BLUE).

  4. How does introducing an \(L_2\) Ridge penalty affect the bias and variance of a regression estimator?

    Options

    Shrinkage estimators trade a small amount of bias for a substantial reduction in estimation variance, which lowers overall mean squared error in noisy regimes.

  5. What mathematical condition characterizes a strictly convex optimization problem?

    Options

    Strict convexity with a positive definite Hessian guarantees that the objective has a unique global minimizer without local sub-optima.

  6. What do the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) complementary slackness conditions state regarding inequality constraints \(\lambda_i g_i(x) = 0\)?

    Options

    Complementary slackness dictates that either the constraint is active (\(g_i(x^*) = 0\)) or its multiplier vanishes (\(\lambda_i = 0\)).

  7. Why is bit-for-bit reproducibility via seeded random number generators essential in quantitative machine learning?

    Options

    Seeded pseudo-random number generators provide deterministic reproducibility across hardware architectures and research reviews.

  8. What is the signal-to-execution lag \(\tau_{\text{lag}}\) in backtesting architecture?

    Options

    Enforcing \(\tau_{\text{lag}} \ge 1\) ensures that a decision based on bar \(t\) information can execute no earlier than bar \(t+1\).

  9. What does the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) theorem demonstrate regarding multiple regression?

    Options

    The FWL theorem shows that multivariate regression coefficients represent the relationship between variables after projecting out the linear influence of other covariates.

  10. Why does omitted-variable bias occur when relevant factors are excluded from a return prediction model?

    Options

    When an omitted variable is correlated with included regressors, the regression error violates exogeneity (\(\E[X'\epsilon] \ne 0\)), biasing the estimated coefficients.

Project ladder

The four-rung project ladder bridges conceptual machine learning theory to an institutional production pipeline (Atlas milestone A17). Complete the course project to certify production readiness:

  1. Rung 1 (Worked Example): Walk through point-in-time feature ingestion, purged cross-validation splits, coordinate descent LASSO shrinkage, and TreeSHAP additivity verification on a multi-factor equity panel.
  2. Rung 2 (Completion Problem): Complete the three core algorithmic routines: coordinate descent soft-thresholding, purged boundary calculation, and TreeSHAP additivity verification.
  3. Rung 3 (Independent Project): Build the full atlas.ml library conforming to Atlas milestone A17. Evaluated by automated testing across 50 pseudo-random market seeds.
  4. Rung 4 (Stretch Extensions): Extend the pipeline with KernelSHAP for deep architectures, adaptive online regret minimization under regime drift, and automated pairwise SHAP interaction screening.
