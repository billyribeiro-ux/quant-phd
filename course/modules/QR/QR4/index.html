Text, Sequence Models & LLMs in Finance

Financial markets generate massive volumes of unstructured textual data: SEC corporate filings (10-K and 10-Q), earnings call transcripts, analyst research reports, central bank policy statements, and real-time financial news feeds. Converting qualitative human prose into robust, statistically sound quantitative alpha factors requires specialized econometric and linguistic techniques. Standard natural language processing tools imported naively from general domain text fail because financial language is governed by specialized accounting semantics, high feature sparsity, extreme noise, and severe temporal lookahead risks. QR4 provides the doctoral-level foundations for modern quantitative text analysis: representing text as high-dimensional data, extracting domain-calibrated sentiment via Loughran-McDonald lexicons, mapping dense semantic vector spaces with word embeddings, formulating self-attention mechanisms in transformer architectures, and auditing large language model return predictability claims against temporal pretraining contamination. The module culminates in building an institutional EDGAR filing text factor engine and executing adversarial econometric tests proving that the model could not have accessed the forward outcome period.

  • Prerequisites: QR3 Statistical Learning for Finance, QR2 Backtesting Architecture, QR1 Data Architecture & Corporate Actions.
  • Project: Contamination-safe text signal, graded by autograder and rubric.
  • Hours: 80 study hours (48 h across 6 lessons, 32 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Construct high-dimensional document-term representations and analyze the sparsity and Zipf's law scaling of SEC corporate filings.
  2. Implement sublinear term-frequency inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) feature weighting and evaluate pairwise document cosine similarities.
  3. Apply Loughran & McDonald domain-specific sentiment lexicons to corporate disclosures, diagnosing false-negative distortions from general-purpose dictionaries.
  4. Train and manipulate continuous distributed word embeddings, solving financial analogy arithmetic and evaluating semantic geometry.
  5. Formulate multi-head self-attention mechanisms and implement causal upper-triangular masks to prevent lookahead in autoregressive transformers.
  6. Audit large language model financial return prediction claims across pretraining cutoff boundaries, detecting lookahead memorization via time-permutation and entity perturbation tests.
  7. Construct a point-in-time filing text factor pipeline enforcing strict execution lag and cross-sectional market-neutral standardization.

Prerequisites & closure

QR4 builds directly on foundational statistical learning, backtesting architecture, and data engineering:

  • Learning at low signal-to-noise ratios, bias-variance tradeoff, and feature store from QR3.
  • Signal-to-execution lag and purged cross-validation from QR2.
  • Look-ahead bias, timestamp semantics, and data vintages from QR1.
  • Ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator and coefficient of determination (\(R^2\)) from S2.
  • Singular value decomposition (SVD) and principal component analysis (PCA) from M3.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite statistical learning, backtesting, and linear algebra foundations, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In sublinear Loughran-McDonald sentiment scoring with \(w(t) = 1 + \ln(c(t))\), if a 10-K filing contains 4 occurrences of the positive term 'profitable' and 1 occurrence of the negative term 'loss', what is the unnormalized numerator \(\sum w_{\text{pos}} - \sum w_{\text{neg}}\)?

    Options

    Under sublinear scaling, 4 occurrences receive weight \(1 + \ln(4) \approx 2.386\), while 1 occurrence receives weight \(1 + \ln(1) = 1.0\). The difference is \(2.386 - 1.0 = 1.386\).

  2. Why must a quantitative text factor trading on date \(t_{\text{query}} = \text{2023-04-15}\) exclude filings released on date 2023-04-15 under strict execution lag \(\tau_{\text{lag}} \ge 1\)?

    Options

    Enforcing strictly \(\tau_{\text{lag}} \ge 1\) day (\(t_{\text{file}} < t_{\text{query}}\)) ensures that trading decisions on date \(t\) utilize only disclosures that were physically public and disseminated prior to the trading decision window.

  3. Why does learning at low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR < 0.05) make high-capacity unregularized models prone to catastrophic failure?

    Options

    In low SNR regimes, return variance is overwhelmingly dominated by noise. High-capacity models allocate degrees of freedom to memorizing noise spikes, destroying out-of-sample generalization.

  4. What is the core distinction between timestamp semantics for event generation versus publication in quantitative research?

    Options

    Point-in-time stores must index queries by publication/acceptance timestamp to prevent querying economic events before they were publicly disclosed.

  5. In singular value decomposition \(\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^\top\), what do the columns of \(\mathbf{V}\) represent in textual document-term analysis?

    Options

    In Latent Semantic Analysis via SVD, the right singular vectors in \(\mathbf{V}\) span the term space, identifying orthogonal combinations of words that co-occur across topics.

  6. What is the primary vulnerability of standard K-fold cross-validation when applied to sequential financial time series?

    Options

    Randomly shuffling time series data violates temporal ordering and leaks future information across folds, requiring purged and embargoed cross-validation.

  7. How does cross-sectional factor standardization \(z_i = (s_i - \bar{s}) / \sigma_s\) affect portfolio construction?

    Options

    Demeaning guarantees dollar neutrality (\(\sum w_i = 0\)), while dividing by cross-sectional standard deviation standardizes factor exposures across time.

  8. What is the definition of the coefficient of determination \(R^2\) in a predictive linear regression?

    Options

    \(R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}\) measures the fraction of sample variance accounted for by the predictor.

  9. Why does empirical return predictability in financial markets rarely exceed an \(R^2\) of 2–3% on monthly frequencies?

    Options

    Market efficiency forces asset price changes to behave near martingales, so genuine forecastable variance is tiny relative to unexpected order flow.

  10. What does the Spearman rank Information Coefficient (IC) measure?

    Options

    Rank IC assesses the monotonic sorting ability of a factor signal, evaluating whether higher scored assets systematically outperform lower scored assets.

Project ladder

The four-rung project ladder connects natural language processing theory with production quantitative signal engineering. Complete the course project to certify institutional competence:

  1. Rung 1 (Worked Example): Walk through point-in-time filing ingestion, sublinear Loughran-McDonald sentiment extraction, and chronological boundary evaluation on historical 10-K disclosures.
  2. Rung 2 (Completion Problem): Complete the three core algorithmic routines: sublinear polarity weighting, causality filtering with execution lag, and permutation p-value calculation.
  3. Rung 3 (Independent Project): Build the full text_signal.py engine with point-in-time store, cross-sectional normalization, and contamination auditing, evaluated across 50 pseudo-random market seeds.
  4. Rung 4 (Stretch Extensions): Extend the pipeline with causal autoregressive transformer attention, synthetic counterfactual entity swapping, and dynamic LLM cutoff auditing.
