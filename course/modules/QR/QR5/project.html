Rung 1 · Worked example

In frictionless markets, complete replication guarantees zero terminal tracking error via continuous rebalancing. In the presence of proportional transaction costs \(\epsilon > 0\), continuous rebalancing incurs unbounded total friction: \(\int_0^T \epsilon S_t |\mathrm{d}\delta_t| \to \infty\). As established by Buehler et al. (2019), deep hedging replaces analytical Greeks with a neural policy \(\delta_k = F_\theta(S_k, \delta_{k-1}, \tau_k)\) trained to minimize a convex risk measure of terminal hedging loss.

Consider an at-the-money call option with \(S_0 = 100\), \(K = 100\), \(\sigma = 0.20\), \(T = 0.20\), and proportional cost \(\epsilon = 0.005\) (50 bps). Below is the complete worked implementation of the forward cash flow evaluation:

# Worked Example: Cash flow evaluation of a hedging policy
import numpy as np

def evaluate_hedging_path(paths: np.ndarray, deltas: np.ndarray, strike: float, eps: float):
    # paths: (M, N+1), deltas: (M, N)
    price_diffs = paths[:, 1:] - paths[:, :-1]
    trading_gains = np.sum(deltas * price_diffs, axis=1)
    # Friction on initial position, rebalancing, and terminal liquidation
    costs = eps * paths[:, 0] * np.abs(deltas[:, 0])
    costs += np.sum(eps * paths[:, 1:-1] * np.abs(deltas[:, 1:] - deltas[:, :-1]), axis=1)
    costs += eps * paths[:, -1] * np.abs(deltas[:, -1])
    payoff = np.maximum(paths[:, -1] - strike, 0.0)
    pnl = trading_gains - payoff - costs
    return pnl, costs

Annotated Exemplars

Distinguished Exemplar (Level 4)

Meets Level 4 descriptors across all 5 criteria: rigorous mathematical formulation, causal neural policy with inventory state, exact Whalley-Wilmott asymptotic calibration, and stress testing under Merton jump diffusion.

Our deep hedging architecture parameterizes the hedge ratio \(\delta_k = \pi_\theta(S_k/K, \tau_k, \delta_{k-1}, \Delta_{\mathrm{BS}})\) using a multi-layer feedforward network with bounded sigmoid activation \(\delta_k \in [0, 1]\). We train the policy to minimize the Entropic risk measure \(\rho_\gamma(L) = \frac{1}{\gamma} \ln \E[e^{\gamma L}]\), which upper-bounds expected shortfall while remaining smooth and strictly convex everywhere. On held-out Geometric Brownian Motion paths with \(\epsilon = 50\) bps friction, the Black-Scholes continuous hedger incurs an average transaction cost drag of $1.84 per contract, whereas the Whalley-Wilmott asymptotic band reduces turnover by 64.2%, cutting costs to $0.66 while maintaining a 95% CVaR of $4.12. The trained DeepHedger achieves a 95% CVaR of $3.88 with transaction costs of $0.58, successfully learning a state-dependent no-trade zone that widens when Gamma is elevated. When evaluated out-of-sample on Merton jump-diffusion stress paths (\(\lambda = 2.0, \mu_J = -0.06\)), the DeepHedger maintains robust tracking, demonstrating superior downside protection over delta hedging.

Exhibits Level 2 defects: optimizes sample mean loss rather than a coherent tail risk measure, omits initiation and liquidation transaction costs, and fails to evaluate performance under jump regimes.

We trained a neural network in Python to predict delta. The loss function was the average mean squared error between the option payoff and the stock gains. The network traded every step. The transaction costs were calculated after trading was finished by multiplying turnover by 0.005.
