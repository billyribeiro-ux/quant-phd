Reinforcement Learning & Control in Trading

Reinforcement learning and stochastic control provide the mathematical foundation for dynamic sequential decision-making in quantitative finance. While supervised learning predicts forward returns from historical features, control theory solves the harder problem: optimizing sequential actions—inventory rebalancing, order routing, optimal liquidation, and non-linear derivative hedging—under market friction, feedback impact, and risk constraints. In QR5, you will formulate discrete and continuous Markov decision processes; analyze temporal-difference Q-learning and off-policy convergence; derive policy gradient theorems and actor-critic architectures; optimize trade execution against the classical Almgren–Chriss benchmark; implement the pioneering deep hedging framework of Buehler et al. under convex risk measures; calibrate Whalley–Wilmott asymptotic no-trade boundaries under proportional transaction costs; and confront the primary failure mode of financial RL: catastrophic policy collapse caused by non-stationarity and vanishing signal-to-noise ratios. The module culminates in a 32-hour capstone project pitting trained deep-hedging neural networks against delta hedging and Whalley–Wilmott bands across held-out market regimes.

  • Prerequisites: QR3 Statistical Learning for Finance, SC6 Stochastic Control.
  • Project: Deep hedging vs. delta hedging, graded by both autograder and rubric.
  • Hours: 80 study hours (48 h across 6 lessons, 32 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formulate trading and inventory management problems as formal Markov decision processes \((S, A, P, R, \gamma)\) and verify the contraction mapping properties of the Bellman optimality operator.
  2. Derive temporal difference Q-learning algorithms and analyze convergence conditions, experience replay, and target network stabilization in Deep Q-Networks.
  3. Apply the policy gradient theorem to continuous action parameterizations, implementing actor-critic architectures (PPO, SAC) with variance-reducing value baselines.
  4. Solve optimal liquidation problems under linear and non-linear market impact, benchmarking RL execution policies against the analytical Almgren–Chriss hyperbolic trajectory.
  5. Implement the deep hedging framework of Buehler et al. (2019) to hedge derivative exposures under convex risk measures (Expected Shortfall and Entropic risk) without analytical Greeks.
  6. Derive the Whalley & Wilmott (1997) asymptotic no-trade boundary half-width \(O(\epsilon^{1/3})\) under proportional transaction costs and quantify friction reduction relative to continuous delta rebalancing.
  7. Diagnose and mitigate policy collapse, distribution shifts, and reward hacking induced by non-stationarity and ultra-low signal-to-noise ratios in financial time series.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite QR3, SC6, SC1, and M7 concepts, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under proportional transaction cost rate \(\epsilon > 0\), how does the Whalley–Wilmott (1997) asymptotic no-trade half-bandwidth \(h(S_t, t)\) scale with the transaction cost parameter \(\epsilon\)?

    Options

    Whalley and Wilmott (1997) prove via formal matched asymptotic expansions that the leading-order half-width of the optimal no-trade region around Black-Scholes delta scales as \(O(\epsilon^{1/3})\).

  2. Let \(L\) be a continuous loss random variable. What is the mathematical definition of Expected Shortfall (CVaR) at confidence level \(\alpha \in (0, 1)\)?

    Options

    Expected Shortfall (CVaR) is the average of Value-at-Risk quantiles at all levels \(u \ge \alpha\), which for continuous distributions equals the conditional expectation of losses strictly exceeding the \(\alpha\)-quantile.

  3. In continuous-time stochastic control, what non-linear partial differential equation characterizes the value function \(V(x, t)\) under the dynamic programming principle?

    Options

    The HJB equation is the continuous-time infinitesimal version of the Bellman dynamic programming equation, featuring a supremum over control actions \(u \in U\).

  4. In the classical Merton portfolio problem with constant relative risk aversion \(\gamma > 0\), constant drift \(\mu\), risk-free rate \(r\), and volatility \(\sigma\), what is the optimal fraction \(\pi^*\) invested in the risky asset?

    Options

    The Merton fraction \(\pi^* = (\mu - r) / (\gamma \sigma^2)\) balances expected excess return against variance scaled by absolute risk aversion \(\gamma\).

  5. Why does unconstrained model complexity cause estimator variance to explode in low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR < 0.05) financial prediction regimes?

    Options

    In ultra-low SNR regimes, return variance is overwhelmingly dominated by noise \(\sigma_\epsilon^2\). Unregularized capacity fits noise, inflating estimator variance \(P/N \sigma_\epsilon^2\).

  6. What role does the Verification Theorem play in continuous-time stochastic control?

    Options

    The verification theorem applies Itô's lemma to a candidate solution of the HJB equation to establish rigorously that \(w(x, t) = V(x, t)\) and identify the optimal feedback control.

  7. In Policy Iteration for discrete Markov decision processes, what two alternating steps guarantee convergence to the optimal policy \(\pi^*\)?

    Options

    Policy iteration alternates between exact policy evaluation (solving \((I - \gamma P^\pi) V^\pi = R^\pi\)) and greedy policy improvement, converging monotonically in a finite number of iterations.

  8. Why does cross-validation fail in financial time series if training and test partitions are sampled without purging?

    Options

    When returns are computed over multi-day horizons \(h > 1\), training labels near test boundaries overlap temporally with test observations, creating lookahead leakage unless explicitly purged.

  9. Under Itô's lemma, what is the drift correction term for a twice continuously differentiable function \(f(S_t)\) where \(\mathrm{d}S_t = \mu S_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma S_t \mathrm{d}W_t\)?

    Options

    By Itô's formula, the non-zero quadratic variation \((\mathrm{d}S_t)^2 = \sigma^2 S_t^2 \mathrm{d}t\) introduces the second-order Ito correction \(\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 S_t^2 f''(S_t) \mathrm{d}t\).

  10. Under the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions for convex optimization with inequality constraints \(g_i(x) \le 0\), what does the complementary slackness condition require?

    Options

    Complementary slackness dictates that either the constraint is active (\(g_i(x^*) = 0\)) or its multiplier is zero (\(\lambda_i = 0\)), so \(\lambda_i g_i(x^*) = 0\).

Lesson map

  1. L01 · Markov Decision Processes & Bellman Optimality in Trading

    Formal tuple \((S, A, P, R, \gamma)\), transition probability kernels, state-value and action-value functions, Bellman optimality equations, and contraction mapping proofs for trading environments.

    8 study hours · Introduces Markov decision process

  2. L02 · Value-Based Reinforcement Learning & Deep Q-Networks

    Temporal difference learning, off-policy Q-learning convergence, Deep Q-Networks (DQN), experience replay, target networks, and double-DQN overestimation bias corrections.

    8 study hours · Introduces Q-learning

  3. L03 · Policy Gradients, Continuous Action Spaces & Actor-Critic

    REINFORCE policy gradient theorem, score function estimators, baseline variance reduction, continuous action parameterization, and actor-critic architectures (PPO, SAC) for portfolio control.

    8 study hours · Introduces Policy-gradient methods

  4. L04 · Reinforcement Learning for Trade Execution & Market Impact

    Almgren–Chriss benchmark under reinforcement learning, adaptive volume participation (TWAP/VWAP), linear and non-linear market impact dynamics, and execution shortfall optimization.

    8 study hours · Introduces Reinforcement learning for execution

  5. L05 · Deep Hedging & Asymptotic No-Trade Bands

    Buehler, Gonon, Teichmann & Wood (2019) deep hedging framework, convex risk measures, hedging under transaction costs without analytical Greeks, and Whalley & Wilmott (1997) asymptotic no-trade boundaries \(O(\epsilon^{1/3})\).

    8 study hours · Introduces Deep hedging, No-trade band hedging under costs

  6. L06 · Non-Stationarity, Low SNR & Failure Modes in Financial RL

    Regime shifts, non-stationary return distributions breaking Markov assumptions, out-of-distribution policy collapse, low signal-to-noise ratio variance explosion, and failure prevention architectures.

    8 study hours · Introduces Why RL fails in non-stationary low-SNR settings

Project ladder

The four-rung project ladder connects reinforcement learning and stochastic control with institutional derivatives risk management. Complete the capstone project (project.html, 32 study hours) to certify quantitative competence:

  1. Rung 1 (Worked Example): Walk through forward cash flow accounting of option hedging under proportional transaction costs, comparing theoretical frictionless replication against discrete frictional rebalancing.
  2. Rung 2 (Completion Problem): Complete the core algorithmic kernels: Whalley–Wilmott cubic asymptotic half-bandwidth, Expected Shortfall (CVaR 95%), and the Entropic risk measure.
  3. Rung 3 (Independent Project): Build the full deep hedging evaluation engine with pure NumPy neural policy gradient descent, comparing Black–Scholes delta, Whalley–Wilmott bands, and neural hedging on held-out jump-diffusion paths across 50 pseudo-random seeds.
  4. Rung 4 (Stretch Extensions): Extend deep hedging to rough fractional volatility (Bergomi / rough Heston) and multi-asset basket options with non-diagonal cross-impact transaction costs.
