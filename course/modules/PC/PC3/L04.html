Market-making games and inventory risk

  • Uses: expected-value game (Lesson 3), expectation, market maker and dealer, bid–ask spread, limit order book
  • Introduces: market-making game
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated

A market maker quotes a bid and an ask, and trades with whoever arrives. Its result is the cash it collects plus the value of the units it holds, and that result depends on who trades with it. This lesson states a one-asset market-making game as a model, proves the profit and loss identity, computes the expected change of one turn from inventory zero, and shows that, while the inventory (the units the maker holds) stays below its limit, it adds variance from each move of the fair value (defined in Section 1) and, for a given quote, adds no expected gain. The constants, listed in Remark 1.2 and Example 1.3, are modelling choices, not estimates from any market.

1The game and its rules

The game has \(N\) turns, numbered \(t=1,\dots,N\). Money is an integer number of cents, and the fair value of the asset in cents at turn \(t\) is \(v_t\). The maker starts with cash \(C_0=0\) and inventory \(I_0=0\) in units. The fair value before turn 1 is \(v_0=10000\) cents. The symbols \(N\) (the number of turns), \(t\) (the turn index), \(p\) (the probability that an arrival is informed) and \(L\) (the inventory limit) are local to this lesson. Other symbols are introduced where they first appear.

Definition 1.1 (market-making game)

A market-making game is the repeated decision defined by the following steps for each turn \(t=1,\dots,N\), in this order.

  1. The fair value moves: \(v_t=v_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\), where \(\varepsilon_t\) is \(+5\) or \(-5\) cents with probability \(1/2\) each.
  2. The maker posts a bid \(b_t\) and an ask \(a_t\), integers with \(b_t<a_t\), and a quoted size \(q_t\) in \(\{0,1,\dots,\bar q\}\), with \(\bar q=5\).
  3. One customer arrives, and the arrival is resolved by Assumptions A1, A4 and A5 of the standing assumptions stated below.
  4. If a fill occurs, the maker's cash and inventory change as in Section 2.
Standing assumptions (A1 to A6)
  • A1 (arrivals). Each turn has exactly one arrival. The arrivals of different turns are independent, and each is independent of the fair path and of the quote, so that the arrival distribution is the same whatever is quoted. With probability \(p\) it is informed. With probability \(1-p\) it is uninformed, and then it is a buy or a sell with probability \(1/2\) each. Lab 1 (Section 4) and the unit project use \(p=1/2\), so the three outcomes uninformed buy, uninformed sell and informed have probabilities \(1/4\), \(1/4\) and \(1/2\).
  • A2 (fair path). The moves \(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_N\) are independent, each \(+5\) or \(-5\) with probability \(1/2\), and independent of the arrivals.
  • A3 (information). The quote in turn \(t\) is a function of the public history: the earlier quotes, fills, inventory and cash. It is not a function of \(v_t\).
  • A4 (informed trader). An informed trader buys one unit at the ask when \(v_t>a_t\), sells one unit at the bid when \(v_t<b_t\), and otherwise does not trade.
  • A5 (fills). A trade fills only if \(q_t\ge 1\) and the inventory after the trade satisfies \(\lvert I\rvert\le L\), with \(L=10\). A fill is one unit at the quoted price, whatever the quoted size.
  • A6 (quote form). Each quote has the form \(a_t=m_t+h\) and \(b_t=m_t-h\), with \(m_t\) and \(h\ge 1\) integers. Here \(m_t\) is the mid and \(2h\) the quoted spread, both in cents.
Remark 1.2 (what the constants are)

\(N=20\), \(v_0=10000\) cents, moves of 5 cents, \(\bar q=5\), \(L=10\) and \(p=1/2\) are choices of this model. The lesson uses them so that Lab 1 and the unit project agree. It does not claim that any real asset has them. Under A5 the quoted size only switches a fill on or off, through the test \(q_t\ge 1\), because a fill is always one unit.

Example 1.3 (the quoting rule of Lab 1, Section 4)

The maker quotes on its last filled price \(m_t\), or on \(10000\) cents before any fill, with \(h=10\) cents and \(q_t=1\). Each quote therefore satisfies A6 with \(h=10\), and the quote uses only the fill history, so it satisfies A3. The rule is kept simple so that its arithmetic can be followed by hand.

2Cash, inventory and the profit and loss identity

A fill has a sign \(\sigma_t\in\{-1,0,+1\}\). It is \(+1\) when the maker buys one unit, \(-1\) when it sells one unit, and \(0\) when there is no fill. Let \(f_t\) be the fill price in cents. A customer who buys takes one unit from the maker at the ask, so \(\sigma_t=-1\), \(f_t=a_t\), the maker's cash rises by \(a_t\) and its inventory falls by one. A customer who sells gives the maker one unit at the bid, so \(\sigma_t=+1\), \(f_t=b_t\), the cash falls by \(b_t\) and the inventory rises by one. In both cases the cash change is \(c_t=-\sigma_t f_t\) and the inventory change is \(\sigma_t\). When there is no fill, \(c_t=0\) and \(\sigma_t=0\).

Definition 2.1 (mark-to-market value)

The mark-to-market value after turn \(t\) is \(W_t=C_t+I_t v_t\): the maker's cash plus its units valued at the fair value of the same turn. The game settles at \(v_N\), so the profit and loss of the game is \(W_N=C_N+I_N v_N\).

Proposition 2.2 (the profit and loss identity)

Hypotheses: (H1) \(C_0=0\) and \(I_0=0\); (H2) for each turn the cash change is \(c_t=-\sigma_t f_t\) and the inventory change is \(\sigma_t\), as above, with \(c_t=\sigma_t=0\) when there is no fill. Conclusion: \(W_N=\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_t+\Bigl(\sum_{t=1}^{N}\sigma_t\Bigr)v_N\). In words, the profit and loss equals the cash flow of the fills plus the inventory at the end times the settlement value.

Proof

By H1 and H2, \(C_t=C_{t-1}+c_t\) and \(I_t=I_{t-1}+\sigma_t\) for each \(t\ge 1\). Induction on \(t\) gives \(C_t=\sum_{s=1}^{t}c_s\) and \(I_t=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\sigma_s\). Setting \(t=N\) and substituting into \(W_N=C_N+I_N v_N\) gives the conclusion. \(\square\)

Proposition 2.3 (the turn decomposition)

Hypotheses: (H1) and (H2) of Proposition 2.2, and \(W_0=C_0+I_0v_0=0\). Conclusion: for each \(t\ge 1\), \(W_t-W_{t-1}=\bigl(c_t+\sigma_t v_t\bigr)+I_{t-1}\bigl(v_t-v_{t-1}\bigr)\). The first bracket is the trade term of turn \(t\). The second term is the fair-move term.

Proof

Write \(W_t-W_{t-1}=(C_t-C_{t-1})+I_t v_t-I_{t-1}v_{t-1}\). By H2, \(C_t-C_{t-1}=c_t\) and \(I_t=I_{t-1}+\sigma_t\), so \(I_t v_t=I_{t-1}v_t+\sigma_t v_t\). Substituting gives \(c_t+\sigma_t v_t+I_{t-1}(v_t-v_{t-1})\). \(\square\)

Remark 2.4 (why the settlement and not the quote)

Proposition 2.2 marks the units at \(v_N\), the settlement value. Marking them at the last quote's mid instead gives a different number whenever \(v_N\) differs from that mid and the final inventory is not zero. Problem E3 computes the difference on seed 0, where the final mid is 9990 cents and the settlement is 9980 cents.

3The expected trade term of one turn from inventory zero

Proposition 3.1 (expected trade term from inventory zero)

Hypotheses: (H1) \(I_{t-1}=0\), the inventory before turn \(t\) is zero, and the conditioning event \(\{I_{t-1}=0,\ v_t=v\}\) has positive probability; (H2) the quote is a fixed pair of integers \(a\) and \(b\), with \(b<a\), \(a-b=2h\) for an integer \(h\ge 1\) (A6), the quoted size satisfies \(q_t\ge 1\), and \(L\ge 1\); (H3) A1, A2, A4 and A5 hold, and \(v=v_t\) is a number. Conclusion: \[\E\bigl[c_t+\sigma_t v_t\,\big|\,I_{t-1}=0,\ v_t=v\bigr]=(1-p)\,h-p\bigl[(v-a)^{+}+(b-v)^{+}\bigr].\] The statement is for the quote \((a,b)\) that H2 fixes, and for inventory zero before turn \(t\). It is not claimed for a quote that depends on the public history, because the quote is then random given \(v_t\) and the proof does not cover that case. It is not claimed for a nonzero inventory; Remark 4.2 treats an inventory at the limit.

Proof

The event \(\{I_{t-1}=0,\ v_t=v\}\) is determined by the earlier arrivals and the fair-value moves, because the quote is fixed. By A1 and A2 the arrival at turn \(t\) is independent of those, so under the conditioning its outcomes keep the probabilities of A1. By H2 the quote is fixed, and by H1 and \(L\ge 1\) the inventory test of A5 passes for either fill, since \(\lvert\pm 1\rvert\le L\). So a fill depends only on the arrival, on \(v\) and on the quote. By H1 and H2, a fill changes the inventory by \(\sigma_t\), so the trade term equals \(c_t+\sigma_t v\). Take the three arrival outcomes in turn. The total is the probability-weighted sum of the three contributions.

(i) Uninformed buy, probability \((1-p)/2\). The customer buys at the ask, so \(\sigma_t=-1\) and \(c_t=a\). The contribution is \(a-v\). Because \(q_t\ge 1\) and \(\lvert\pm 1\rvert\le L\), the fill occurs (A5).

(ii) Uninformed sell, probability \((1-p)/2\). The customer sells at the bid, so \(\sigma_t=+1\) and \(c_t=-b\). The contribution is \(v-b\). The fill occurs by the same test.

(iii) Informed arrival, probability \(p\). If \(v>a\), A4 gives a buy at the ask, with contribution \(a-v\). If \(v<b\), A4 gives a sell at the bid, with contribution \(v-b\). If \(b\le v\le a\), there is no trade and the contribution is \(0\). Since \(b<a\), the cases \(v>a\) and \(v<b\) cannot both hold, so the contribution is \(-(v-a)^{+}-(b-v)^{+}\).

Adding the three contributions with their probabilities gives \(\tfrac{1-p}{2}\bigl[(a-v)+(v-b)\bigr]-p\bigl[(v-a)^{+}+(b-v)^{+}\bigr]\). The bracket equals \(a-b=2h\) by A6, which gives \((1-p)h\). \(\square\)

Example 3.2 (the quote of Lab 1 at \(p=1/2\))

Take \(a=10010\), \(b=9990\) and \(h=10\), with \(p=1/2\). Proposition 3.1 gives \(E=5-\tfrac12\bigl[(v-10010)^{+}+(9990-v)^{+}\bigr]\) cents. From inventory zero, at \(v=10000\) the expected trade term is \(5\) cents. At \(v=10020\) it is \(5-\tfrac12\cdot 10=0\) cents. At \(v=9985\) it is \(5-\tfrac12\cdot 5=\tfrac52\) cents. Lab 1, Part 2, evaluates these values by enumeration.

Corollary 3.3 (the zero-edge fair value)

Hypotheses: those of Proposition 3.1 (including H1, inventory zero before turn \(t\), and its fixed quote \((a,b)\)) with \(p=1/2\) and \(v\ge a\). Conclusion: the expected trade term of Proposition 3.1 is zero if and only if \(v=a+h\).

Proof

With \(p=1/2\) and \(v\ge a\), Proposition 3.1 gives \(E=h/2-\tfrac12(v-a)\), because \((b-v)^{+}=0\) when \(v\ge a>b\). This is zero exactly when \(v-a=h\). \(\square\)

Remark 3.4 (reading the two terms)

For a fixed quote, from inventory zero (Proposition 3.1), the uninformed flow earns \((1-p)h\) on average, which is a quarter of the quoted spread \(2h\) when \(p=1/2\). By A4, an informed arrival trades when the fair value lies outside the quote, provided that A5 allows the fill, and each such trade costs the maker the distance by which the fair value lies outside the quote. This cost is adverse selection, the loss a maker takes when its counterparty knows the value better, in its simplest form here. This lesson describes the mechanism and does not develop its theory.

4Inventory risk

Proposition 4.1 (the fair-move term)

Hypotheses: (H1) the inventory before the move is a fixed integer \(I\), given the history; (H2) the move \(\varepsilon_t\) is \(+5\) or \(-5\) with probability \(1/2\) each, and independent of the history (A2). Conclusion: the fair-move term \(I\varepsilon_t\) has mean \(0\) and variance \(25I^{2}\).

Proof

\(\E[I\varepsilon_t]=I\bigl(\tfrac12\cdot 5+\tfrac12\cdot(-5)\bigr)=0\). Also \(\E[(I\varepsilon_t)^{2}]=I^{2}\bigl(\tfrac12\cdot 25+\tfrac12\cdot 25\bigr)=25I^{2}\). Therefore \(\Var(I\varepsilon_t)=\E[(I\varepsilon_t)^{2}]-\bigl(\E[I\varepsilon_t]\bigr)^{2}=25I^{2}\). \(\square\)

Remark 4.2 (the size of the risk)

The mean of the fair-move term is zero for every inventory, so the move adds zero expected amount to the turn's change in mark-to-market value (Proposition 2.3). Its standard deviation is \(\sqrt{25I^{2}}=5\lvert I\rvert\) cents. A maker with 3 units carries a standard deviation of 15 cents from one move, and a maker at the limit of 10 units carries 50 cents. While \(\lvert I\rvert\le L-1\), the inventory test of A5, \(\lvert I\pm 1\rvert\le L\), refuses no fill. For a given quote and fair value \(v_t\), the trade term then has the same distribution as at inventory 0, so its expected value does not depend on the position. At \(\lvert I\rvert=L\) one side is refused and the term changes. At \(I=10\), with the quote held at 9990 and 10010 cents (the quote of Lab 1 at inventory 0; the rule of Example 1.3 would quote differently at inventory 10, and this remark does not use it), \(p=1/2\) and \(v=10000\) cents, the informed arrival does not trade, since \(v\) lies inside the quote, and the uninformed sell is refused because it would take the inventory to 11. Only the uninformed buy contributes, \(10\) cents with probability \(1/4\), so the expected trade term is \(5/2\) cents, against 5 cents at \(I=0\). The variance of the fair-move term grows with the square of the position.

Lab 1 · The game on seed 0, the one-turn expectation, and the variance of a move

"""PC3 lab L04: the market-making game on seed 0, one fixed quoting rule, and two exact checks.

SIMULATED. The fair path and the arrivals are model inputs drawn from random.Random(0). No data file is read and there
is no network access. Standard library only. Quotes, cash flows and inventory marks are integer numbers of cents.
Expectations and variances are fractions.Fraction, so every printed value is exact; a printed fraction such as 5/2 is
2.5 cents.

Part 1 plays the 20-turn game on seed 0 with the lesson's rule: quote symmetrically on the last filled price, or
at 10000 cents before any fill, with a half-spread of 10 cents and a size of 1. It prints the turn log and the
settlement, then checks the identity P&L = cash flow + inventory x settlement exactly for this run. It also prints the
number of informed arrivals on seed 0, and a one-line summary of the same rule played on seed 1.

Part 2 enumerates the three arrival outcomes of one turn, for a grid of fair values, from inventory 0 and with the quote
fixed at 9990 and 10010 cents. Its exact expectation of the one-turn change in cash plus inventory times the fair value
is compared with the closed form h/2 - (1/2)[(v - a)^+ + (b - v)^+], where h is the half-spread,
a the ask and b the bid.

Part 3 computes the exact mean and variance of the mark change caused by one fair-value move, for several inventories.

The checks test this one seed and this one grid. They are not a proof for other seeds or other fair values; the lesson
proves the closed form of Part 2 for every fair value, from inventory zero and with the quote held fixed at 9990 and
10010 cents.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

HORIZON = 20  # turns
START_CENTS = 10_000  # v_0, in cents
MOVE_CENTS = 5  # the fair value moves by +5 or -5 cents each turn
MAX_SIZE = 5  # largest quoted size
LIMIT = 10  # inventory limit: a fill is refused when |inventory| would exceed it
HALF_SPREAD = 10  # half-spread of the quoting rule, in cents
SEED = 0
ARRIVAL_PROBABILITY = {"uninformed-buy": Fraction(1, 4), "uninformed-sell": Fraction(1, 4), "informed": Fraction(1, 2)}


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Turn:
    """One turn of the log. Money is in cents, inventory in units. side is +1 when the maker buys, -1 when it sells."""

    turn: int
    bid_cents: int
    ask_cents: int
    size: int
    arrival: str
    filled: bool
    fill_price_cents: int | None
    side: int
    cash_flow_cents: int
    inventory_after: int
    cash_after_cents: int


def draw_game(seed: int) -> tuple[list[int], list[str]]:
    """The fair path v_0 .. v_HORIZON (cents) and the arrival of each turn, drawn from random.Random(seed).

    Turn t uses two draws in this order. The first sets the move: +5 cents if the draw is below 0.5, otherwise -5 cents.
    The second sets the arrival: uninformed buy below 0.25, uninformed sell below 0.5, otherwise informed.
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    path = [START_CENTS]
    arrivals: list[str] = []
    for _ in range(HORIZON):
        move = MOVE_CENTS if rng.random() < 0.5 else -MOVE_CENTS
        path.append(path[-1] + move)
        draw = rng.random()
        if draw < 0.25:
            arrivals.append("uninformed-buy")
        elif draw < 0.5:
            arrivals.append("uninformed-sell")
        else:
            arrivals.append("informed")
    return path, arrivals


def quote(last_fill_cents: int | None) -> tuple[int, int, int]:
    """The lesson's rule: mid = last filled price, or 10000 before any fill; bid = mid - 10; ask = mid + 10; size 1."""
    mid = START_CENTS if last_fill_cents is None else last_fill_cents
    return mid - HALF_SPREAD, mid + HALF_SPREAD, 1


def customer_side(arrival: str, fair_cents: int, bid_cents: int, ask_cents: int) -> int:
    """Return +1 if the customer sells to the maker at the bid, -1 if the customer buys at the ask, 0 for no trade.

    An uninformed order names its direction; whether it fills is decided in play(). An informed trader buys at the ask
    when the fair value is above the ask, sells at the bid when the fair value is below the bid, and otherwise does not
    trade.
    """
    if arrival == "uninformed-buy":
        return -1
    if arrival == "uninformed-sell":
        return 1
    if fair_cents > ask_cents:
        return -1
    if fair_cents < bid_cents:
        return 1
    return 0


def play(seed: int) -> tuple[list[int], list[Turn]]:
    """Play the 20 turns. A fill needs size >= 1, a trade on the arrival, and |inventory| within the limit after it."""
    path, arrivals = draw_game(seed)
    inventory = 0
    cash = 0
    last_fill: int | None = None
    turns: list[Turn] = []
    for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1):
        bid, ask, size = quote(last_fill)
        arrival = arrivals[t - 1]
        side = customer_side(arrival, path[t], bid, ask)
        filled = side != 0 and size >= 1 and abs(inventory + side) <= LIMIT
        price: int | None = None
        cash_flow = 0
        if filled:
            price = ask if side < 0 else bid
            cash_flow = -side * price
            inventory += side
            cash += cash_flow
            last_fill = price
        turns.append(
            Turn(
                turn=t,
                bid_cents=bid,
                ask_cents=ask,
                size=size,
                arrival=arrival,
                filled=filled,
                fill_price_cents=price,
                side=side if filled else 0,
                cash_flow_cents=cash_flow,
                inventory_after=inventory,
                cash_after_cents=cash,
            )
        )
    return path, turns


def part_one() -> None:
    print("Part 1 · seed 0, 20 turns, SIMULATED fair path and arrivals")
    path, turns = play(SEED)
    print("turn  bid    ask    size  arrival           filled  fill    inv  cash_after")
    for r in turns:
        fill = "-" if r.fill_price_cents is None else str(r.fill_price_cents)
        print(
            f"{r.turn:>4}  {r.bid_cents:>5}  {r.ask_cents:>5}  {r.size:>4}  {r.arrival:<16}  "
            f"{r.filled!s:<6}  {fill:>6}  {r.inventory_after:>3}  {r.cash_after_cents:>10}"
        )
    settlement = path[HORIZON]
    fills = [r for r in turns if r.filled]
    cash_flow = sum(r.cash_flow_cents for r in fills)
    inventory_end = turns[-1].inventory_after
    mark = inventory_end * settlement
    pnl = turns[-1].cash_after_cents + mark
    print(f"fills {len(fills)} of {HORIZON} turns")
    print(f"cash flow, sum of the fill cash flows = {cash_flow} cents")
    print(f"inventory at the end = {inventory_end} units")
    print(f"settlement fair value v_{HORIZON} = {settlement} cents")
    print(f"inventory mark = inventory x settlement = {mark} cents")
    print(f"P&L = cash + inventory mark = {pnl} cents")
    print(f"identity P&L = cash flow + inventory x settlement: {pnl == cash_flow + inventory_end * settlement}")
    print(f"cash after the last turn equals the cash flow sum: {turns[-1].cash_after_cents == cash_flow}")
    print(f"largest |inventory| over the run = {max(abs(r.inventory_after) for r in turns)} (limit {LIMIT})")
    quotes_valid = all(r.bid_cents < r.ask_cents and 0 <= r.size <= MAX_SIZE for r in turns)
    print(f"every quote has bid < ask and 0 <= size <= {MAX_SIZE}: {quotes_valid}")
    fills_at_quote = all(r.fill_price_cents in (r.bid_cents, r.ask_cents) for r in fills)
    print(f"every fill price is the bid or the ask of its own turn: {fills_at_quote}")
    informed = sum(1 for r in turns if r.arrival == "informed")
    print(f"informed arrivals on seed 0 = {informed} of {HORIZON} turns")
    path_one, turns_one = play(1)
    fills_one = [r for r in turns_one if r.filled]
    cash_flow_one = sum(r.cash_flow_cents for r in fills_one)
    inventory_one = turns_one[-1].inventory_after
    settlement_one = path_one[HORIZON]
    pnl_one = turns_one[-1].cash_after_cents + inventory_one * settlement_one
    print(
        f"seed 1, same rule: fills {len(fills_one)} of {HORIZON} turns, cash flow {cash_flow_one} cents, "
        f"inventory at the end {inventory_one} units, settlement {settlement_one} cents, P&L {pnl_one} cents"
    )


def one_turn_change(fair_cents: int, bid_cents: int, ask_cents: int) -> Fraction:
    """Exact expectation of the one-turn change in cash + inventory x fair value, from inventory 0 and size 1."""
    total = Fraction(0)
    for arrival, probability in ARRIVAL_PROBABILITY.items():
        side = customer_side(arrival, fair_cents, bid_cents, ask_cents)
        if side == 0:
            continue
        price = ask_cents if side < 0 else bid_cents
        total += probability * (-side * price + side * fair_cents)
    return total


def closed_form(fair_cents: int, bid_cents: int, ask_cents: int) -> Fraction:
    """h/2 - (1/2)[(v - a)^+ + (b - v)^+] with h = (a - b)/2: the same quantity in closed form."""
    h = Fraction(ask_cents - bid_cents, 2)
    above = max(fair_cents - ask_cents, 0)
    below = max(bid_cents - fair_cents, 0)
    return h / 2 - Fraction(above + below, 2)


def part_two() -> None:
    print("Part 2 · one turn from inventory 0, quote 9990 bid and 10010 ask, exact expectation by enumeration")
    bid, ask = START_CENTS - HALF_SPREAD, START_CENTS + HALF_SPREAD
    agree = True
    for fair in range(9980, 10025, 5):
        exact = one_turn_change(fair, bid, ask)
        formula = closed_form(fair, bid, ask)
        agree = agree and exact == formula
        same = exact == formula
        print(f"fair value v = {fair}: E[change] = {exact} cents, closed form = {formula} cents, equal: {same}")
    print(f"enumeration equals the closed form on every grid point: {agree}")


def fair_move_mark_change(inventory: int) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction]:
    """Mean and variance of the mark change I x (v_t - v_{t-1}) from one fair-value move of +5 or -5 cents."""
    moves = [Fraction(MOVE_CENTS), Fraction(-MOVE_CENTS)]
    changes = [inventory * m for m in moves]
    mean = sum(changes, Fraction(0)) / len(changes)
    variance = sum(((c - mean) ** 2 for c in changes), Fraction(0)) / len(changes)
    return mean, variance


def part_three() -> None:
    print("Part 3 · mark change from one fair-value move, exact mean and variance over the two equally likely moves")
    for inventory in (0, 1, 3, -3, LIMIT):
        mean, variance = fair_move_mark_change(inventory)
        print(
            f"inventory {inventory:>3}: mean = {mean} cents, variance = {variance} cents^2, "
            f"variance equals 25 x inventory^2: {variance == MOVE_CENTS**2 * inventory**2}"
        )


def main() -> None:
    part_one()
    part_two()
    part_three()

5Where the canon breaks

The model has integer-cent quotes, and its fair value moves by 5 cents per turn, up or down. Its results are conditional on A1 to A6, and the lesson makes no claim that a real market maker faces these assumptions.

A market maker in a limit order book also sees the queue of resting orders at each price, and this game does not model that queue. Adverse selection is modelled in its simplest form. The informed trader knows \(v_t\) exactly and trades when \(v_t\) lies outside the quote, provided that A5 allows the fill. The model gives it no information beyond \(v_t\), and it does not vary that information over time. Proposition 3.1 does not cover a trader who learns from the fills, and this lesson does not model learning.

The arrival probabilities do not depend on the quote, by A1. So, for each fixed quote and from inventory zero, Proposition 3.1 gives the expected trade term \((1-p)h\) at a fair value inside the quote, and across quotes this value rises with the half-spread \(h\). The model therefore cannot show that a wider quote costs customers. Any loss of flow from wider quotes is outside the model.

By A5, a fill is one unit at the quote whatever the size, so the quoted size only decides whether a fill occurs. The inventory limit is a refusal. The quoting rule of Example 1.3 takes its mid from the last filled price, and each fill moves that price by 10 cents: a purchase by the maker lowers the mid by 10 cents and a sale raises it by 10 cents. By induction on the fills, the mid at turn \(t\) is \(10000-10I_{t-1}\), so the quote does move with the inventory, through the fill history. A3 allows any rule that uses only the public history. This lesson analyses only the rule of Example 1.3.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A market maker earns the spread on every trade.”

    For a fixed quote, from inventory zero, the uninformed flow earns part of the spread, \((1-p)h\) by Proposition 3.1, which is a quarter of the quoted spread when \(p=1/2\). The informed flow costs the distance by which the fair value lies outside the quote. At \(p=1/2\), from inventory zero, a fair value of \(a+h\) gives an expected trade term of zero (Corollary 3.3). Lab 1, Part 2 prints 0 cents at \(v=10020\), where the quote is 9990 and 10010.

  2. “The profit and loss of the game is the cash flow.”

    Proposition 2.2 adds the units held at the settlement value. After an uninformed buy at 10010 cents, the cash flow is \(+10010\) cents, but the maker holds one unit short, marked at the settlement value of 10000 cents. The profit and loss is \(10010-10000=10\) cents, not 10010.

  3. “Inventory is harmless, because the fair value is as likely to rise as to fall.”

    The mean of the fair-move term is zero, as Proposition 4.1 shows. Its variance is \(25I^{2}\), so a position of 10 units has a standard deviation of 50 cents from one move (Remark 4.2). Lab 1, Part 3 prints a variance of 2500 cents² at inventory 10.

  4. “The maker can quote from the fair value, since the game is a model of a market.”

    A3 excludes it. A quote that depends on \(v_t\) is a different game, and Proposition 3.1 does not apply to it. The rule of Example 1.3 uses the fill history only, so it satisfies A3.

7Problem set

Each problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Empirical problems use the seeded game of Lab 1, labelled SIMULATED.

Mechanics

M1 · A customer buys at the ask

The quote is 9990 bid and 10010 ask, the quoted size is 1, the inventory is 0 and the cash is 0. An uninformed customer buys one unit. Find the fill sign, the cash change, the inventory and the cash after the turn, and the mark-to-market value at a fair value of 10000 cents.

Solution

The customer buys, so the maker sells and \(\sigma_t=-1\). The fill price is the ask, \(f_t=10010\), and the cash change is \(c_t=-\sigma_t f_t=+10010\). The inventory becomes \(0-1=-1\). Since the cash starts at 0, the cash after the turn is 10010 cents. The mark-to-market value is \(W=10010+(-1)(10000)=10\) cents.

M2 · The inventory limit

The inventory before a turn is 10 units, the limit is \(L=10\), and the quoted size is 3. Decide whether an uninformed customer who sells one unit to the maker fills, and whether one who buys from the maker fills.

Solution

A customer who sells to the maker makes the maker buy, so the inventory becomes \(10+1=11\). Since \(\lvert 11\rvert>10\), A5 refuses the fill. A customer who buys from the maker makes the inventory \(10-1=9\). Since \(\lvert 9\rvert\le 10\) and the size is at least 1, the fill occurs. The limit is a test on the inventory after the trade, not before it.

M3 · An informed trader at the boundary

The inventory before the turn is 0, the quote is 9990 bid and 10010 ask, and an informed arrival comes in a turn with fair value 10010. Does the informed trader trade? Repeat with the fair value 10011.

Solution

A4 requires \(v_t>a_t\) for a buy. At \(v_t=10010=a_t\) the condition fails, and the test \(v_t<b_t\) also fails, so there is no trade and the contribution is zero. At \(v_t=10011\) the condition \(10011>10010\) holds, so the trader buys one unit at the ask. The maker sells at 10010 cents while the fair value is 10011, a loss of one cent on the unit.

Proof

P1 · The identity for two turns

Under H1 and H2 of Proposition 2.2, write out \(W_2\) for \(N=2\) in terms of \(c_1\), \(c_2\), \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\), \(v_2\), and check the result against the proposition.

Solution

After turn 1, \(C_1=c_1\) and \(I_1=\sigma_1\). After turn 2, \(C_2=c_1+c_2\) and \(I_2=\sigma_1+\sigma_2\). Hence \(W_2=C_2+I_2 v_2=c_1+c_2+(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)v_2\). This is the sum over \(t=1,2\) of \(c_t\), plus the sum of \(\sigma_t\) times \(v_2\), which is the statement of Proposition 2.2 with \(N=2\).

P2 · The expected change at the ask

Using Proposition 3.1 with \(p=1/2\) and inventory zero, compute the expected trade term at \(v=a\) and at \(v=a+h\) for a quote with \(h\ge 1\), and show that the two values differ by \(h/2\).

Solution

At \(v=a\), \((v-a)^{+}=0\) and \((b-v)^{+}=(b-a)^{+}=0\) since \(b<a\). So \(E=h/2\). At \(v=a+h\), \((v-a)^{+}=h\) and \((b-v)^{+}=0\), so \(E=h/2-\tfrac12 h=0\). The difference is \(h/2-0=h/2\).

P3 · The variance at a negative inventory

Let \(\varepsilon\) take the values \(\pm 5\) with probability \(1/2\) each. Show that \(\Var(I\varepsilon)=25I^{2}\) for every integer \(I\), and compute it at \(I=-4\).

Solution

\(\E[\varepsilon]=0\) and \(\E[\varepsilon^{2}]=25\). Then \(\E[I\varepsilon]=0\), so \(\Var(I\varepsilon)=\E[(I\varepsilon)^{2}]=I^{2}\E[\varepsilon^{2}]=25I^{2}\). The argument uses only \(I^{2}\), so the sign of \(I\) does not matter. At \(I=-4\) the variance is \(25\cdot 16=400\) cents².

Empirical

E1 · Seed 1, SIMULATED

Run the game of Lab 1 with seed 1 and the same rule. Report the number of fills, the cash flow of the fills, the final inventory, the settlement value, the mark and the profit and loss.

Solution

The seed-1 line that Lab 1, Part 1 prints gives 16 fills in 20 turns, a cash flow of 20100 cents, and a final inventory of \(-2\) units. The settlement is 10020 cents, so the mark is \((-2)(10020)=-20040\) cents. The profit and loss is \(20100-20040=60\) cents, which is the identity of Proposition 2.2 on this path. It is one path, so it is not an average.

E2 · Arrivals in seed 0, SIMULATED

The 20 arrivals on seed 0 contain 13 informed arrivals. Assuming A1 with \(p=1/2\), compute the standard error of the sample share of informed arrivals and the standardised distance of 13/20 from 1/2. State whether this path rejects \(p=1/2\) at the 5 percent level under the normal approximation.

Solution

The sample share is \(13/20=0.65\), where the count of 13 informed arrivals is the one that Lab 1, Part 1 prints. Under A1 each turn is informed with probability \(p=1/2\), independently of the other turns, so the share of informed arrivals in 20 turns has variance \(p(1-p)/20\) and standard error \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/20}=\sqrt{0.25/20}\approx 0.1118\). The distance is \(0.65-0.5=0.15\), which is \(0.15/0.1118\approx 1.34\) standard errors. The two-sided normal critical value at 5 percent is 1.96, and 1.34 is below it, so this path does not reject \(p=1/2\). One path of 20 turns cannot establish the value of \(p\), and the lesson does not estimate it.

E3 · The mid instead of the settlement, SIMULATED

On seed 0, the last quote is 9980 bid and 10000 ask, so its mid is 9990. The final inventory is 1 unit, the cash flow is \(-9950\) cents and the settlement is 9980 cents. Compute the profit and loss marked at the settlement and marked at the mid, and state the difference.

Solution

Marked at the settlement, the profit and loss is \(-9950+1\cdot 9980=30\) cents, which is the value Lab 1 prints. Marked at the mid, it is \(-9950+1\cdot 9990=40\) cents. The difference is \(9990-9980=10\) cents, which equals the inventory times the gap between the mid and the settlement. Proposition 2.2 requires the settlement.

Research

R1 · The informed share that makes a quote fair

Criterion: from inventory zero, find the distance \(d\ge 0\) above the ask at which the expected trade term is zero, for a general informed share \(p\) and half-spread \(h\). Evaluate it at \(p=3/5\) and \(h=10\). State what the model does not say about estimating \(p\).

Solution

Take \(v=a+d\) with \(d\ge 0\). Then \((v-a)^{+}=d\) and \((b-v)^{+}=0\), so Proposition 3.1 (from inventory zero, H1) gives \(E=(1-p)h-pd\) for the fixed quote \((a,b)\). This is zero when \(d=(1-p)h/p\). At \(p=3/5\) and \(h=10\), \(d=(2/5)(10)/(3/5)=20/3\), about 6.67 cents. The model does not say how \(p\) can be estimated from fills, and the lesson gives no estimate of it.

R2 · A spread chosen by the model's own criterion

Criterion: from inventory zero, under A1 to A6, choose the half-spread \(h\) that makes the expected trade term zero at a fair value at the mid. Decide whether such an \(h\) exists, and state which assumption would have to change for the criterion to select a finite spread.

Solution

For each fixed half-spread \(h\), with the quote \(m\pm h\) and inventory zero, at \(v=m\) both brackets of Proposition 3.1 are zero, so \(E=(1-p)h\). Under A1, \(p\) does not depend on \(h\). If \(p<1\), \(E>0\) for every \(h\ge 1\), so no spread makes it zero. If \(p=1\), \(E=0\) for every \(h\), so the criterion picks no particular spread. Either way the criterion does not select a unique spread. A unique choice would need the arrival distribution to depend on the quote, which A1 excludes. The model therefore leaves the spread undetermined, and the question needs a model of arrivals that depends on the quote.

R3 · A variance budget for the inventory

Criterion: find the largest inventory magnitude \(\lvert I\rvert\) for which the variance of the fair-move term is strictly below 100 cents².

Solution

By Proposition 4.1 the variance is \(25I^{2}\). The condition \(25I^{2}<100\) gives \(I^{2}<4\), so \(\lvert I\rvert<2\). The largest integer magnitude is 1, which gives a variance of 25 cents². At \(\lvert I\rvert=2\) the variance is exactly 100, which fails the strict criterion.
