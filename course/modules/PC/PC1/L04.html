Delivering a research presentation to an expert audience is an exercise in managing adversarial scrutiny under strict time constraints. Unlike an introductory lecture where an instructor guides students through foundational theorems, a departmental finance seminar or conference workshop brings together hostile referees and domain experts who actively test whether the empirical design identifies the causal effect. Presenting forty cluttered slides in twenty minutes guarantees failure. An expert presentation delivers the headline empirical finding within the first two minutes, budgets an uncompromised reserve for audience interruptions, and directly confronts the strongest counter-argument through rigorous steelmanning. This lesson formalizes slot budgeting, slide pacing, and adversarial Q&A defense protocols.

1The Expert Audience Model and Results-First Slides

An expert audience in quantitative finance possesses deep knowledge of baseline econometric tools, data limitations, and institutional asset mechanics. Speakers who spend ten minutes reviewing textbook asset pricing formulas before stating their empirical model provoke frustration and early adversarial interruptions.

Definition 4.1 (Expert Presentation)

An expert presentation is a structured research talk delivered to an audience of active peer researchers, referees, or institutional practitioners, organized around two central pillars:

  1. Early Result Anchoring: The central research question, headline quantitative magnitude, and identifying mechanism are delivered on Slide 1 (or Slide 2) within the first 120 seconds of the presentation.
  2. Adversarial Objection Alignment: The narrative spine is constructed around the strongest counter-argument or endogeneity threat, demonstrating via pre-computed empirical tests that the core finding survives hostile scrutiny.
Remark 4.2 (Slide Text Density vs. Standalone Exhibits)

While tables must be self-contained in print (standalone table), presentation slides must not reproduce full narrative paragraphs. Slide text serves as visual signposts for spoken remarks. A slide containing three paragraphs of 10-point prose forces the audience to read instead of listen, dividing cognitive attention.

2Slot Budgeting and Slide Pacing Arithmetic

Conference presentations operate under non-negotiable time limits enforced by session chairs. Attempting to present too many slides invariably results in the speaker rushing through primary evidence or being cut off before presenting robustness checks.

Definition 4.3 (Presentation Slot Budget)

Let total allotted session time be \(T_0 \in \mathbb{Q}^+\) minutes. The canonical presentation slot budget partitions \(T_0\) into: \[ T_0 = T_p + T_q \] where:

  • Presentation Delivery Time (\(T_p\)): \(T_p = \frac{3}{4} T_0\) (75% of slot time).
  • Question and Interruption Reserve (\(T_q\)): \(T_q = \frac{1}{4} T_0\) (25% of slot time).

Let \(N_s \in \mathbb{N}\) denote the total number of main slide canvases. The slide pace \(\tau\) is the average delivery time per slide: \[ \tau = \frac{T_p}{N_s} \] The admissible pace constraint requires: \[ 1.5 \le \tau \le 3.0 \quad \text{minutes per slide} \] which determines the admissible slide count bounds: \[ \frac{T_p}{3.0} \le N_s \le \frac{T_p}{1.5} \]

Example 4.4 (Evaluating a 20-Minute Conference Slot)

For a standard \(T_0 = 20\) minute conference presentation:

  • Question reserve: \(T_q = 20 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = 5.0\) minutes (300 seconds).
  • Net delivery time: \(T_p = 20 \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 15.0\) minutes (900 seconds).
  • Admissible slide count bounds: \[ N_{s,\min} = \left\lceil \frac{15.0}{3.0} \right\rceil = 5 \text{ slides}, \quad N_{s,\max} = \left\lfloor \frac{15.0}{1.5} \right\rfloor = 10 \text{ slides} \]

A slide deck containing \(N_s = 6\) slides yields an average pace of \(\tau = \frac{15}{6} = 2.5\) minutes per slide (150 seconds per slide). In contrast, bringing 24 slides yields \(\tau = \frac{15}{24} = 0.625\) minutes (37.5 seconds per slide), which guarantees verbal rushing and structural failure.

3Steelmanning and Adversarial Q&A Protocols

In finance seminars, audience members do not wait until the final slide to ask questions; they interrupt immediately when an identification choice or institutional detail is questionable. Handling questions effectively requires adhering to the steelmanning discipline.

Definition 4.5 (Three-Step Adversarial Q&A Protocol)

When an expert participant raises an objection or questioning challenge, the speaker executes the following three-step protocol:

  1. Step 1 · Restate and Steelman: Restate the objection in its strongest possible form, stripping any hostile framing (e.g. "The concern is that unobserved industry demand shocks simultaneously drive institutional mergers and capital expenditures, confounding our instrument.").
  2. Step 2 · Direct Quantitative Answer: Give an immediate unhedged answer regarding whether the concern is supported by data (e.g. "Yes, that is an endogeneity threat. We address it directly by controlling for granular product-market demand shifts.").
  3. Step 3 · Reference Pre-Computed Evidence: Point immediately to pre-computed sensitivity evidence in the appendix or on a specific exhibit (e.g. "In Appendix Slide 14, we show that including 4-digit industry-by-year fixed effects leaves the coefficient essentially unchanged at 13.8 basis points.").

Lab 1 · Presentation slot budget and question reserve arithmetic

The lab calculates exact fractional and integer-second budgets for a 20-minute presentation slot, verifying a 15-minute delivery budget (75%), a 5-minute question reserve (25%), a 6-slide allocation with average pace 2.5 min/slide, and confirms that all timing constraints pass.

"""PC1 Lab L04-1: Presentation slot budget and question reserve arithmetic.

Illustrative plan for a 20-minute expert finance presentation. Not data.
All calculations use exact fractions and integer seconds. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SlideAllocation:
    slide_number: int
    topic: str
    minutes: Fraction

    @property
    def seconds(self) -> int:
        return int(self.minutes * 60)


def main() -> None:
    print("PC1 Lab L04-1: Presentation Slot Budget and Question Reserve Arithmetic")
    print("Illustrative plan for an expert presentation. Not data.")

    total_slot_minutes = Fraction(20, 1)  # 20 minutes
    question_reserve_fraction = Fraction(1, 4)  # 25% reserve
    question_reserve_minutes = total_slot_minutes * question_reserve_fraction
    presentation_minutes = total_slot_minutes - question_reserve_minutes

    print(f"Total slot duration: {total_slot_minutes} min ({int(total_slot_minutes * 60)} s)")
    print(
        f"Question and buffer reserve: {question_reserve_minutes} min "
        f"({int(question_reserve_minutes * 60)} s, fraction {question_reserve_fraction})"
    )
    print(
        f"Net presentation delivery time: {presentation_minutes} min "
        f"({int(presentation_minutes * 60)} s, fraction {1 - question_reserve_fraction})"
    )

    # 6-slide expert presentation plan
    slides = [
        SlideAllocation(1, "Core Question & Headline Finding", Fraction(2, 1)),
        SlideAllocation(2, "Institutional Setting & Identification", Fraction(3, 1)),
        SlideAllocation(3, "Primary Empirical Result & Standalone Figure", Fraction(4, 1)),
        SlideAllocation(4, "Economic Mechanism & Channels", Fraction(3, 1)),
        SlideAllocation(5, "Steelmanned Strongest Objection & Robustness", Fraction(2, 1)),
        SlideAllocation(6, "Concluding Takeaway & Policy Implications", Fraction(1, 1)),
    ]

    print("\nSlide Schedule:")
    print(f"{'Slide':5s} | {'Topic':45s} | {'Minutes':7s} | {'Seconds':7s} | {'Share of Talk':13s}")
    print("-" * 88)

    sum_slide_minutes = Fraction(0, 1)
    for s in slides:
        sum_slide_minutes += s.minutes
        share = s.minutes / presentation_minutes
        print(
            f"{s.slide_number:5d} | {s.topic:45s} | {float(s.minutes):7.1f} | "
            f"{s.seconds:7d} | {float(share) * 100:6.1f}% ({share})"
        )

    print("-" * 88)
    print(f"Total planned slide time: {sum_slide_minutes} min ({int(sum_slide_minutes * 60)} s)")
    matches_delivery_budget = sum_slide_minutes == presentation_minutes
    print(f"Slide time exactly matches delivery budget: {matches_delivery_budget}")

    num_slides = len(slides)
    avg_minutes_per_slide = presentation_minutes / num_slides
    print(f"Total slide count: {num_slides}")
    print(
        f"Average pace per slide: {avg_minutes_per_slide} min/slide "
        f"({float(avg_minutes_per_slide):.2f} min/slide, {int(avg_minutes_per_slide * 60)} s/slide)"
    )

    # Rules validation
    pace_ok = Fraction(3, 2) <= avg_minutes_per_slide <= Fraction(3, 1)
    reserve_ok = question_reserve_minutes >= Fraction(5, 1)
    objection_included = any("objection" in s.topic.lower() for s in slides)

    print(f"Rule check: Pace between 1.5 and 3.0 min/slide: {pace_ok}")
    print(f"Rule check: Question reserve >= 5 min: {reserve_ok}")
    print(f"Rule check: Explicit objection/robustness slide: {objection_included}")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

The expert presentation model and strict slide pace constraints break in specific academic and industry settings:

  • Non-Expert Policy Testimony: Presenting before regulatory panels or legislative committees requires an entirely different register. Non-expert policymakers do not possess econometric training; technical identification slides are replaced with direct policy implications, aggregate dollar totals, and visual exhibits adhering to figure redesign principles.
  • Early-Stage Chalk Talks and Brainstorms: In internal brown-bag workshops where the empirical paper is not yet written, prepared slide decks often impede open discussion. In this setting, the speaker writes on a whiteboard, sketching the econometric identification problem and soliciting audience feedback on data sources.
  • Job Market Talks: Job market presentations (typically 75 to 90 minutes) feature intense, continuous audience interruptions throughout the first 20 minutes. The question reserve must expand from 25% to at least 40% of the slot, requiring the speaker to prepare modular slide blocks that can be omitted dynamically if early questioning extends beyond schedule.

5Misconceptions

  1. Presentation slides should replicate the paragraphs of the written paper.

    Pasting full prose paragraphs onto slides impairs communication. Audiences cannot read complex text while simultaneously processing spoken exposition. Slides should present high-density standalone figures, concise summary points, and key equations, leaving narrative interpretation to speech.

  2. The Q&A period should be confined entirely to the final five minutes of the slot.

    In professional finance seminars, expert audiences do not wait until the final five minutes. When audience members suspect an identification flaw or data error, they intervene immediately. A speaker who fails to reserve time for spontaneous interruptions will run out of time before reaching their conclusion.

  3. Steelmanning an objection means refuting the critic immediately.

    Steelmanning requires reformulating the critic's objection in its strongest, most persuasive intellectual form before responding. Defensively dismissing a question or arguing with the questioner alienates the audience. Restating the objection with intellectual generosity demonstrates command of the econometric domain.

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 4.1 (Computing Slide Pace and Count Bounds): An academic conference allots a 16-minute slot for an empirical presentation. Compute the net presentation delivery budget \(T_p\), the question reserve \(T_q\), and the minimum and maximum allowable slide count under the admissible pace constraint \(\tau \in [1.5, 3.0]\) minutes per slide.

Solution

Total slot \(T_0 = 16\) minutes. \(T_p = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 16 = 12\) minutes (720 seconds). \(T_q = \frac{1}{4} \cdot 16 = 4\) minutes (240 seconds). Slide count bounds: \(N_{s,\min} = \lceil \frac{T_p}{3.0} \rceil = \lceil \frac{12}{3} \rceil = 4\) slides. \(N_{s,\max} = \lfloor \frac{T_p}{1.5} \rfloor = \lfloor \frac{12}{1.5} \rfloor = \lfloor 8 \rfloor = 8\) slides. The deck should contain between 4 and 8 slides.

Problem 4.2 (Evaluating Slide Text Density): A candidate slide contains 180 words across 8 bullet points. At a standard verbal speaking rate of 140 words per minute, compute how long the speaker would take merely to read the slide verbatim, and evaluate whether this satisfies slide design principles.

Solution

Time to read slide verbatim: \(\frac{180}{140} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.2857\) minutes (approximately 77 seconds). If the slide pace is 2.0 minutes, reading the text consumes over 64% of the slide's entire allocation. This violates slide design craft: slides should contain high-density figures and key point signposts, not verbatim transcripts. Audiences read faster than speakers talk, resulting in cognitive disengagement.

Problem 4.3 (Allocating Time Across Slide Topics): Construct a 5-slide allocation for a 12-minute presentation delivery budget (\(T_p = 12\)), specifying topic and exact minutes for each slide such that total time equals 12 minutes.

Solution

Canonical 5-slide schedule: Slide 1 (Research Question & Headline Result): 2.0 min. Slide 2 (Institutional Setting & Identification Strategy): 2.5 min. Slide 3 (Primary Baseline Regression Exhibit): 3.5 min. Slide 4 (Core Mechanism & Steelmanned Objection): 3.0 min. Slide 5 (Conclusion & Boundary Conditions): 1.0 min. Check sum: \(2.0 + 2.5 + 3.5 + 3.0 + 1.0 = 12.0\) minutes. Average pace: \(\frac{12}{5} = 2.4\) min/slide (falls within [1.5, 3.0]).

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 4.4 (Probability of Completing Presentation under Stochastic Interruptions): Suppose audience questions arrive according to a Poisson process with arrival rate \(\mu\) questions per minute, and each question takes a fixed \(d\) minutes to answer. Let planned delivery time be \(T_p\) and question reserve be \(T_q\). Prove that the probability that total presentation duration does not exceed the allotted slot \(T_0 = T_p + T_q\) is given by \(P(N(T_p) \le \lfloor T_q / d \rfloor)\), where \(N(T_p) \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu T_p)\).

Solution

During the delivery time \(T_p\), the number of questions that arrive is \(N(T_p) \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu T_p)\). Total time spent answering questions is \(d \cdot N(T_p)\). Total session duration is \(T_{\text{total}} = T_p + d \cdot N(T_p)\). The session does not exceed allotted slot \(T_0\) if and only if \(T_{\text{total}} \le T_0\): \[ T_p + d \cdot N(T_p) \le T_p + T_q \iff d \cdot N(T_p) \le T_q \iff N(T_p) \le \frac{T_q}{d} \] Since \(N(T_p)\) is an integer, this condition is equivalent to \(N(T_p) \le \lfloor \frac{T_q}{d} \rfloor\). Hence \(P(T_{\text{total}} \le T_0) = \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor T_q / d \rfloor} \frac{(\mu T_p)^k e^{-\mu T_p}}{k!}\), proving the result.

Problem 4.5 (Optimality of the Three-Step Q&A Protocol): Model the Q&A interaction as a signaling game between a speaker of type \(\theta \in \{\text{Competent}, \text{Defensive}\}\) and an expert audience. Prove that restating the question with steelmanning serves as a separating equilibrium that reliably signals competence.

Solution

Let steelmanning require cognitive effort \(c(\theta)\) to identify the econometric premise behind a hostile query. A competent speaker with full mastery of identification possesses low cost \(c(\text{Competent}) < \kappa\), whereas a defensive speaker lacking deep understanding of identification mechanisms incurs high cost \(c(\text{Defensive}) > \kappa\). If the audience rewards perceived competence with payoff \(V\) satisfying \(c(\text{Competent}) < V < c(\text{Defensive})\), the competent type strictly chooses to steelman while the defensive type hedges or dismisses the question. Thus, execution of Step 1 of Protocol 4.5 constitutes a separating equilibrium reliably signaling technical mastery.

Problem 4.6 (Information Entropy Decay of Deferred Findings): Suppose audience attention level at time \(t \in [0, T_p]\) is \(A(t) = A_0 \exp(-\gamma t)\) for decay parameter \(\gamma > 0\). Prove that placing the primary result at time \(t_1\) versus time \(t_2\) with \(t_1 < t_2\) maximizes total retained audience information.

Solution

Retained information from communicating finding \(F\) at timestamp \(t\) is proportional to attention level \(I(t) = k \cdot A(t) = k A_0 \exp(-\gamma t)\). The derivative with respect to timestamp \(t\) is \(\frac{dI}{dt} = -k \gamma A_0 \exp(-\gamma t) < 0\) for all \(t \ge 0\). Since \(\frac{dI}{dt} < 0\) strictly, \(I(t_1) > I(t_2)\) for any \(t_1 < t_2\). Therefore, presenting the result within the first two minutes (\(t_1 \le 2\)) strictly preserves more information than delaying it to \(t_2 = 12\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 4.7 (Auditing Seminar Slide Decks): An academic candidate brings a 36-slide deck to a 25-minute seminar presentation. The speaker runs out of time on Slide 18 before presenting the instrumental variable estimates. Compute the candidate's planned slide pace versus the realized pace, and diagnose the structural failure.

Solution

Planned allocation: Delivery time \(T_p = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 25 = 18.75\) minutes. Planned pace for 36 slides: \(\frac{18.75}{36} \approx 0.52\) minutes per slide (31 seconds per slide). Realized pace: 18 slides in 25 minutes = \(\frac{25}{18} \approx 1.39\) minutes per slide. Diagnosis: The planned pace of 31 seconds per slide was unachievable in an expert seminar with audience interruptions. Attempting to present 36 slides forced premature termination, preventing the audience from evaluating the core identification strategy.

Problem 4.8 (Restructuring an Adversarial Q&A Exchange): A hostile participant interrupts: "Your finding is purely driven by Apple and Microsoft during the pandemic." Formulate a compliant 3-step response adhering to Protocol 4.5.

Solution

Compliant Response: Step 1 (Restate and Steelman): "The concern is that our asset pricing anomaly is not a broad market phenomenon, but an artifact of mega-cap technology firms experiencing abnormal pandemic demand." Step 2 (Direct Answer): "No, the anomaly is not driven by mega-cap outliers. It persists across the cross-section." Step 3 (Reference Pre-Computed Evidence): "In Table 4 and Appendix Slide 18, we replicate the baseline regression excluding the top 10 firms by market capitalization, as well as excluding the entire 2020–2021 pandemic period. The point estimate remains positive and statistically significant at 18 basis points per month."

Problem 4.9 (Simulating Question Reserve Depletion): In a 20-minute slot (\(T_p = 15\) min, \(T_q = 5\) min), a seminar participant asks a question at minute 6 that takes 3 minutes to address, and another question at minute 11 that takes 2.5 minutes. Compute the remaining question reserve and state whether the speaker must accelerate their slide pace.

Solution

Initial reserve: \(T_q = 5.0\) minutes. Time consumed by questions: \(3.0 + 2.5 = 5.5\) minutes. Remaining reserve: \(5.0 - 5.5 = -0.5\) minutes. The question reserve is fully exhausted with a 30-second deficit. To finish within the 20-minute hard stop, the speaker must shave 30 seconds from remaining delivery time, for example by omitting one secondary mechanism slide and moving directly to the primary robustness check.

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 4.10 (Designing a Modular Slide Deck Architecture): Design a modular slide deck architecture for an 80-minute university job market seminar, partitioning slides into Core (non-negotiable), Intermediate (expandable), and Appendix (backup) tiers to withstand extreme questioning variance.

Solution

Modular Architecture: (1) Core Tier (12 slides, 35 minutes): Research Question, Headline Finding, Institutional Environment, Identification Natural Experiment, Baseline Regression Table, Primary Mechanism Exhibit, Concluding Implications. (2) Intermediate Tier (8 slides, 15 minutes): Data Attrition Details, Secondary Mechanism Tests, Subsample Heterogeneity by Firm Size. These slides are tagged with skip markers and can be bypassed if early questions run long. (3) Appendix Tier (25+ slides, 0 planned delivery minutes): Hyperlinked slides addressing specific referee concerns (alternative clustering, winsorization thresholds, placebo shocks, instrument validity tests).

Problem 4.11 (Formulating the Strongest Counter-Argument Slide): You are presenting an empirical study showing that corporate share repurchases reduce long-term capital investment. Identify the strongest counter-argument from modern corporate finance theory and design a dedicated steelmanned slide confronting it.

Solution

Strongest Counter-Argument (Free Cash Flow Theory): Firms repurchasing shares have depleted positive-NPV physical investment opportunities; capital returned to shareholders is reallocated to high-growth innovators, so reduced firm-level investment represents efficient capital reallocation rather than managerial myopia. Steelmanned Slide Blueprint: Title: Addressing the Free Cash Flow / Efficient Capital Reallocation Hypothesis Content: (1) Formal statement of the objection: Repurchasing firms lack high-NPV projects. (2) Empirical test: Regress capital expenditures on share repurchases partitioned by Tobin's Q and industry investment opportunities. (3) Finding: Even among firms in the top tercile of investment opportunities (high Q) and high product demand growth, repurchases predict reductions in R&D and patent output.

Problem 4.12 (Refactoring an Ineffective Slide Presentation): A presenter opens a talk with 4 consecutive text-heavy slides summarizing 20 papers on credit default swaps, followed by a slide with 12 regression columns in 8-point font. Redesign the first 5 minutes of this presentation to adhere to results-first craft and standalone exhibit principles.

Solution

Redesign Plan: Slide 1 (Title & Question): Clear paper title, author affiliations, and a single bold question: "Do sovereign credit default swaps reflect sovereign default risk or global funding liquidity?" Slide 2 (Headline Finding & Standalone Graphic): An uncrowded figure plotting sovereign CDS spreads against duration-matched bond spreads, with a prominent callout box: "Sovereign CDS spreads price dollar liquidity frictions: 62% of CDS spread variation is explained by U.S. dollar repo rates rather than domestic fiscal debt." Slide 3 (Identification Setting): Clean diagram illustrating the 2014 regulatory change in covered bond repo rules that provides exogenous liquidity variation. Move the 20-paper literature review into spoken narrative and relegate the 12-column table to an appendix slide, showing only the 2 primary columns on Slide 4.
