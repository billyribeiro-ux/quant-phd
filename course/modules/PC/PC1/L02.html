An Introduction That States the Result

  • Uses: research paper structure (Lesson 1), evidence grade, identification, robustness check (module P0.1)
  • Introduces: results-first introduction, defined in Section 1
  • Ttheorem or definition, proved or established on the page
  • Areplicated empirical result
  • Bpublished empirical result, not yet settled
  • Csingle study or lab simulation
  • Dprofessional convention or lore
  • Xrefuted claim

Academic readers evaluate manuscripts under severe time scarcity. When an introduction spends two pages surveying thirty years of background literature before revealing its empirical discovery, referees lose patience and lose track of the core contribution. A results-first introduction leads immediately with the central discovery: the exact estimate, the measurement unit, the economic direction, the institutional setting, and the sample period appear in the very first sentence. This lesson establishes the operational principles of results-first writing, provides an exact five-part anatomical standard for result sentences, and demonstrates why rounding must occur only at display.

1The Results-First Mandate

In empirical finance research, the introduction is not an exploratory mystery novel where the resolution is withheld until the climax. In accordance with the canonical research paper structure, an expert reader must be informed of the quantitative answer before reading why previous authors failed to discover it.

Definition 2.1 (Results-First Introduction)

A results-first introduction is an empirical introduction whose opening paragraph states the primary quantitative empirical finding in sentence 1 (or sentence 2), prior to any review of external literature, satisfying the five-element tuple: \[ \mathcal{R} = (M,\, D,\, U,\, \Delta X,\, \mathcal{S}_0) \] where:

  1. Mechanism / Subject (\(M\)): The economic actor, policy friction, or explanatory asset pricing phenomenon under investigation.
  2. Direction (\(D\)): A signed directional verb (e.g. increases, reduces, predicts, compresses).
  3. Magnitude with Unit (\(U\)): A concrete quantitative point estimate accompanied by its explicit measurement scale (e.g. basis points, percent of assets, dollars per share).
  4. Standardized Shock (\(\Delta X\)): The magnitude of the explanatory shift generating the response (e.g. per standard deviation increase, for an interquartile shift, upon credit rating downgrade).
  5. Sample Universe and Period (\(\mathcal{S}_0\)): The exact boundary of the observational universe, asset classes, and chronological date range (e.g. across United States manufacturing firms from 1990 to 2020).
Example 2.2 (Evaluating Five-Element Sentence Compliance)

Consider the opening sentence:

"We show that institutional cross-ownership (\(M\)) increases (\(D\)) physical investment by 14 basis points (\(U\)) per standard deviation increase in common ownership (\(\Delta X\)) across United States manufacturing firms from 1990 to 2020 (\(\mathcal{S}_0\))."

All five elements are present and observable. In contrast, consider the weak opening sentence:

"Institutional ownership has received considerable scrutiny in the corporate finance literature regarding capital allocation."

This sentence contains zero elements from \(\{D, U, \Delta X, \mathcal{S}_0\}\), deferring all empirical substance.

2Exact Rounding and Discrepancy Arithmetic

Empirical papers report rounded numbers in prose for readability, but computing secondary figures (such as percentage changes, economic multipliers, or aggregate dollar impacts) from pre-rounded values introduces severe compounding errors.

Definition 2.3 (Rounding-at-Display Rule)

Let an econometric model yield an exact unrounded parameter estimate \(\hat{\beta} \in \mathbb{Q}\). Any secondary derived quantity \(g(\hat{\beta})\) must be computed directly from \(\hat{\beta}\): \[ Y_{\text{derived}} = g(\hat{\beta}) \] The display representation \(\text{round}(Y_{\text{derived}}, d)\) to \(d\) decimal places occurs strictly at the final prose rendering. Computing \(\tilde{Y} = g(\text{round}(\hat{\beta}, d))\) is prohibited.

Theorem 2.4 (Compounding Error Bound in Ratio Computation)

Let baseline capital expenditure be \(C_0 = 2.10\) percent of assets, and let the estimated treatment effect be \(\hat{\beta} = 0.1437\) percentage points. If \(\hat{\beta}\) is pre-rounded to \(0.14\) percentage points before computing the relative effect \(R = \frac{\hat{\beta}}{C_0}\), the relative percentage error incurred by pre-rounding is: \[ \begin{aligned} \text{RelError} &= \left|\frac{R_{\text{rounded}} - R_{\text{exact}}}{R_{\text{exact}}}\right| \\ &= \left|\frac{\text{round}(\hat{\beta}) - \hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}}\right| \end{aligned} \] For \(\hat{\beta} = \frac{1437}{10000}\) and \(\text{round}(\hat{\beta}) = \frac{14}{100}\), this relative error is exactly \(\frac{37}{1437} \approx 2.575\%\).

Proof

Let \(R_{\text{exact}} = \frac{\hat{\beta}}{C_0}\) and \(R_{\text{rounded}} = \frac{\text{round}(\hat{\beta})}{C_0}\). The relative error is: \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{|R_{\text{rounded}} - R_{\text{exact}}|}{R_{\text{exact}}} &= \frac{|\text{round}(\hat{\beta})/C_0 - \hat{\beta}/C_0|}{\hat{\beta}/C_0} \\ &= \left|\frac{\text{round}(\hat{\beta}) - \hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}}\right| \end{aligned} \] Substituting \(\hat{\beta} = 0.1437 = \frac{1437}{10\,000}\) and \(\text{round}(\hat{\beta}) = 0.1400\): \[ \left|\frac{1400 - 1437}{1437}\right| = \frac{37}{1437} \] Converting to a percentage yields \(\frac{37}{1437} \times 100\% = 2.5748\dots\%\), bounding the distortion.

3Scope Bounding and Causal Attribution

A rigorous result sentence never over-generalizes beyond its empirical sample. Claims must respect the evidence grade of the underlying design. If an instrumental variable strategy uses financial institution mergers from 1990 to 2020 across U.S. manufacturing firms, the paper must not state that "common ownership increases corporate investment globally."

Definition 2.5 (Claim Scope Bounding)

A claim \(C\) is scope-bounded conditional on empirical specification \(\mathcal{E} = (\text{Sample}, \text{Period}, \text{Estimator})\) if: \[ \text{Domain}(C) \subseteq \text{Support}(\mathcal{E}) \] Any assertion whose stated domain strictly contains \(\text{Support}(\mathcal{E})\) is an ungrounded extrapolation.

Lab 1 · Result sentence formatting and exact rounding check

The lab takes an unrounded corporate finance regression estimate (beta = 1437/10000 percentage points, se = 41/1000), applies round-half-to-even to nearest basis point, assembles the standardized five-element result sentence, and validates that all five elements are present.

"""PC1 Lab L02-1: Result sentence formatting and exact rounding check.

SIMULATED. Illustrative regression estimates and standardized sentence builder.
All arithmetic uses exact fractions and explicit rounding rules. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class RegressionEstimate:
    variable_name: str
    beta_exact: Fraction
    se_exact: Fraction
    unit: str
    sample_universe: str
    sample_period: str


def compute_t_stat(est: RegressionEstimate) -> Fraction:
    """Compute exact t-statistic as beta / se."""
    return est.beta_exact / est.se_exact


def round_half_to_even(val: Fraction, decimal_places: int) -> Fraction:
    """Exact decimal rounding to stated places using round-half-to-even."""
    factor = 10**decimal_places
    scaled = val * factor
    # Floor integer part and remainder
    int_part = int(scaled)
    rem = scaled - int_part
    if rem > Fraction(1, 2):
        rounded_int = int_part + 1
    elif rem < Fraction(1, 2):
        rounded_int = int_part
    else:
        # Halfway: round to even
        rounded_int = int_part if int_part % 2 == 0 else int_part + 1
    return Fraction(rounded_int, factor)


def build_result_sentence(est: RegressionEstimate, rounded_bp: int) -> str:
    """Assemble a standardized results-first sentence from exact fields and rounded magnitude."""
    return (
        f"We find that {est.variable_name} increases physical investment by {rounded_bp} basis points "
        f"per standard deviation increase in common ownership across {est.sample_universe} from {est.sample_period}."
    )


def main() -> None:
    print("PC1 Lab L02-1: Result Sentence Formatting and Exact Rounding Check")
    print("SIMULATED estimates from empirical corporate finance model")

    # Raw estimates in percentage points (1 percentage point = 100 basis points)
    # beta = 0.1437 percentage points = 14.37 basis points = 1437/10000
    # se = 0.0410 percentage points = 41/1000
    estimate = RegressionEstimate(
        variable_name="institutional cross-ownership",
        beta_exact=Fraction(1437, 10000),
        se_exact=Fraction(41, 1000),
        unit="basis points",
        sample_universe="United States manufacturing firms",
        sample_period="1990 to 2020",
    )

    t_stat = compute_t_stat(estimate)
    # Convert beta to basis points (multiply by 100)
    beta_bp = estimate.beta_exact * 100  # 1437/100 = 14.37
    rounded_bp_frac = round_half_to_even(beta_bp, 0)
    rounded_bp = int(rounded_bp_frac)
    t_stat_rounded = round_half_to_even(t_stat, 2)

    rounding_error = abs(beta_bp - rounded_bp)
    max_allowed_error = Fraction(1, 2)  # Half a basis point

    print(f"Raw beta (percentage points): {estimate.beta_exact} ({float(estimate.beta_exact):.4f})")
    print(f"Raw standard error: {estimate.se_exact} ({float(estimate.se_exact):.4f})")
    print(f"Exact t-statistic: {t_stat} ({float(t_stat):.4f})")
    print(f"Rounded t-statistic (2 decimal places): {float(t_stat_rounded):.2f}")
    print(f"Raw effect in basis points: {beta_bp} ({float(beta_bp):.2f} bp)")
    print(f"Rounded effect for prose: {rounded_bp} bp")
    print(f"Rounding error: {rounding_error} bp (<= 0.5 bp: {rounding_error <= max_allowed_error})")

    sentence = build_result_sentence(estimate, rounded_bp)
    print("Standardized result sentence:")
    print(f'  "{sentence}"')

    # Verify presence of the 5 mandatory elements
    elements = {
        "Mechanism/Subject": estimate.variable_name in sentence,
        "Direction (increases)": "increases" in sentence,
        "Magnitude with unit": f"{rounded_bp} basis points" in sentence,
        "Standardized shift": "per standard deviation increase" in sentence,
        "Sample and Period": f"{estimate.sample_universe} from {estimate.sample_period}" in sentence,
    }
    for elem, present in elements.items():
        print(f"Element check: {elem:30s} -> {present}")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

The results-first structure breaks or requires specialized adaptation in the following research domains:

  • Null Results and Falsifications: When a paper's contribution is proving the absence of an effect (e.g. showing that ESG mutual fund inflows do not lower portfolio cost of capital), the sentence must state the statistical precision of the null. Stating "we find no effect" is insufficient; the sentence must state the two-sided 95% confidence interval bound: "we rule out reductions in cost of capital larger than 2 basis points."
  • New Dataset Creation: When the contribution is constructing a novel historical or regulatory dataset (such as digitizing 19th-century bank balance sheets), the primary contribution is the dataset properties rather than a single regression coefficient. In this setting, Sentence 1 reports sample coverage, observation count, and the core structural stylized fact.
  • Pure Methodological Identification: When demonstrating an econometric bias in existing estimators (e.g. staggered difference-in-differences negative weighting), the headline result is the mathematical identification failure rather than a single empirical estimate.

5Misconceptions

  1. A headline estimate without a concrete unit is sufficient if the sign is positive.

    Reporting that an effect is "positive and significant at the 1 percent level" conveys zero economic magnitude. An effect could be statistically significant yet economically trivial (such as an increase of 0.001 basis points). Every reported magnitude must be paired with its measurement unit.

  2. The abstract replaces the need for a results-first opening paragraph in the introduction.

    Abstracts and introductions serve distinct functions. Readers use the abstract to filter search results, but once reading the paper, they read the introduction to verify the exact mechanism, magnitude, and identification assumptions. Deferring the result in the introduction forces the reader back to the abstract.

  3. Percentage changes can be computed from rounded numbers printed in tables.

    Computing percentage changes from rounded numbers introduces substantial truncation error. If a baseline yield of 1.4% rounds down from 1.44% and a new yield of 1.8% rounds up from 1.76%, the apparent change is \(\frac{1.8 - 1.4}{1.4} = 28.6\%\), whereas the exact change is \(\frac{1.76 - 1.44}{1.44} = 22.2\%\), an error of over 6 percentage points.

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 2.1 (Element Audit of Opening Sentences): Identify the missing elements from the five-element tuple \(\mathcal{R} = (M, D, U, \Delta X, \mathcal{S}_0)\) in the following candidate opening sentence: "We find that bank capital requirements reduce commercial real estate lending across OECD economies."

Solution

Audit against \(\mathcal{R}\): (1) Mechanism \(M\): "bank capital requirements" (Present). (2) Direction \(D\): "reduce" (Present). (3) Magnitude with Unit \(U\): Absent. No numerical estimate or unit is reported. (4) Standardized Shock \(\Delta X\): Absent. Does not state whether this is per 100 bp increase in Tier 1 ratio or upon regulatory breach. (5) Sample & Period \(\mathcal{S}_0\): Partially present ("across OECD economies"), but missing exact calendar years. Missing elements: \(U\), \(\Delta X\), and exact period in \(\mathcal{S}_0\).

Problem 2.2 (Exact Rounding Error Computation): An estimated treatment effect is \(\hat{\beta} = 0.0864\) (8.64 percentage points). An author rounds this to 9 percentage points in the prose. Compute the absolute and relative rounding error.

Solution

Exact value: \(\hat{\beta} = \frac{864}{10000}\). Rounded value: \(\frac{900}{10000}\).

Absolute error: \(|0.09 - 0.0864| = 0.0036\) percentage points (or 36 basis points).

Relative error: \(\frac{0.0036}{0.0864} = \frac{36}{864} = \frac{1}{24} \approx 4.167\%\).

The author should round to one decimal place (8.6 percentage points) to keep relative rounding error below 0.5% (\(\frac{0.0004}{0.0864} \approx 0.46\%\)).

Problem 2.3 (Assembling the Five-Element Sentence): Construct a compliant results-first sentence from these parameters: \(M\) = cross-border syndicated lending, \(D\) = declines, point estimate = 42 basis points, shock = per standard deviation increase in geopolitical risk index, universe = 42 emerging market borrowers from 2000 to 2021.

Solution

Compliant sentence: "We show that cross-border syndicated lending declines by 42 basis points per standard deviation increase in the geopolitical risk index across 42 emerging market sovereign and corporate borrowers from 2000 to 2021." Checks: \(M\) = cross-border syndicated lending; \(D\) = declines; \(U\) = 42 basis points; \(\Delta X\) = per standard deviation increase in geopolitical risk index; \(\mathcal{S}_0\) = 42 emerging market borrowers from 2000 to 2021.

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 2.4 (Convexity of Ratio Truncation): Let \(f(x, y) = \frac{x}{y}\) for \(x, y > 0\). If \(x\) and \(y\) are perturbed by rounding errors \(\epsilon_x\) and \(\epsilon_y\) satisfying \(|\epsilon_x| \le \delta_x\) and \(|\epsilon_y| \le \delta_y < y\), prove that the first-order approximation to the maximum relative error in the ratio is bounded by \(\frac{\delta_x}{x} + \frac{\delta_y}{y}\).

Solution

Take the natural logarithm: \(\ln f(x, y) = \ln x - \ln y\).

The total differential is \(d \ln f = \frac{dx}{x} - \frac{dy}{y}\).

By the triangle inequality, the maximum absolute relative differential is: \[ |d \ln f| \le \left|\frac{dx}{x}\right| + \left|\frac{dy}{y}\right| \] Substituting maximum rounding perturbations \(|dx| \le \delta_x\) and \(|dy| \le \delta_y\) gives: \[ \left|\frac{\Delta f}{f}\right| \approx |d \ln f| \le \frac{\delta_x}{x} + \frac{\delta_y}{y} \] This proves that relative rounding errors in numerator and denominator additively compound.

Problem 2.5 (Necessary Condition for Scope Bounding): Let the support of identification variation be \(\mathcal{V} \subset \mathcal{X}\), where \(\mathcal{X}\) is the domain of all corporate entities. Prove that if \(\mathcal{V} \ne \mathcal{X}\), any claim asserting the relationship holds for all \(x \in \mathcal{X}\) is unsupported by the empirical design.

Solution

Empirical identification identifies the local average treatment effect (LATE) or average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) over the support of identifying variation \(\mathcal{V}\). For any \(x \in \mathcal{X} \setminus \mathcal{V}\), the counterfactual distribution is unobserved, and no identifying moment condition holds. Therefore, the assertion \(\forall x \in \mathcal{X}\) relies on an untested structural extrapolation. Under the scientific evidence standard, the claim is unsupported for \(x \notin \mathcal{V}\).

Problem 2.6 (Identification Precedence over Literature Positioning): Prove that an introduction that defines the empirical identifying instrument before discussing competing theoretical models minimizes cognitive ambiguity relative to the reverse ordering.

Solution

Let an empirical result \(R\) depend on identification assumption \(I\). Readers evaluate competing theoretical interpretations \(\mathcal{T} = \{T_1, T_2, \dots\}\) against \(R\). If \(T_i\) is presented before \(R\) and \(I\) are established, the reader evaluates theoretical validity with respect to an unspecified data-generating process. Establishing \(I\) and \(R\) first restricts the admissible theoretical models to those consistent with \(R\), strictly reducing the set of active hypotheses to be evaluated.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 2.7 (Auditing Published Opening Sentences): An author writes: "We find that short sellers predict stock returns." Rewrite this sentence to satisfy the five-element results-first standard using simulated parameters: magnitude = 45 basis points per month, shock = top-decile short interest ratio, sample = CRSP/Compustat common stocks from 1995 to 2022.

Solution

Compliant rewrite: "We show that high short interest predicts a 45 basis point reduction in next-month stock returns for firms in the top short-interest decile relative to the bottom decile across CRSP common stocks from 1995 to 2022." Verification of elements: (1) \(M\) = high short interest; (2) \(D\) = predicts a reduction; (3) \(U\) = 45 basis points per month; (4) \(\Delta X\) = top short-interest decile relative to bottom decile; (5) \(\mathcal{S}_0\) = CRSP common stocks from 1995 to 2022.

Problem 2.8 (Assessing Scope Extrapolation): A study identifies the causal effect of CEO golf club membership on merger outcomes using a weather instrument across 200 firms headquartered in Florida. The introduction concludes: "Our findings demonstrate that executive leisure networks govern corporate governance across modern corporations." Identify the scope violation and propose a bounded revision.

Solution

Scope violation: The empirical sample is restricted to 200 Florida-headquartered firms where weather shocks provide local exogenous variation in golf play. Generalizing to "modern corporations" extrapolates across all industries, geographies, and firm sizes without identifying evidence. Bounded revision: "Our findings demonstrate that executive leisure interactions in local social networks facilitate informal merger negotiations across Florida-headquartered public firms between 2005 and 2018."

Problem 2.9 (Evaluating Compounding Rounding in Multipliers): An empirical paper estimates an investment multiplier \(\hat{m} = \frac{1}{1 - \hat{c}}\), where marginal propensity to invest is \(\hat{c} = 0.8125\). If \(\hat{c}\) is pre-rounded to \(0.81\), compute the exact multiplier versus the pre-rounded multiplier and the resulting error.

Solution

Exact multiplier: \(\hat{c} = \frac{8125}{10000} = \frac{13}{16}\), yielding \(m_{\text{exact}} = \frac{1}{1 - 13/16} = \frac{16}{3} \approx 5.3333\).
Pre-rounded: \(\tilde{c} = 0.81 = \frac{81}{100}\), yielding \(m_{\text{rounded}} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.81} = \frac{100}{19} \approx 5.2632\).
Absolute error: \(\frac{16}{3} - \frac{100}{19} = \frac{4}{57} \approx 0.0702\).
Relative error: \(\frac{4/57}{16/3} = \frac{1}{76} \approx 1.316\%\).
Pre-rounding \(\hat{c}\) by just 0.0025 alters the headline multiplier by over 1.3%.

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 2.10 (Formulating an Introduction for a Null Finding): A researcher examines whether voluntary corporate sustainability disclosures reduce debt issuance costs and estimates \(\hat{\beta} = -0.4\) basis points with standard error \(1.2\) basis points (\(t = -0.33\)). Construct a results-first opening sentence communicating this precise null result without deceptive phrasing.

Solution

Compliant results-first null sentence: "We show that voluntary corporate sustainability disclosures do not materially reduce secondary corporate bond yield spreads, with an estimated point estimate of -0.4 basis points and a 95 percent confidence interval of [-2.8, 2.0] basis points across United States investment-grade corporate issuers from 2010 to 2021. This tight empirical bound rules out yield reductions greater than 3 basis points, indicating that voluntary ESG reporting does not provide material debt pricing benefits."

Problem 2.11 (Deconstructing a Flawed Working Paper Opener): Deconstruct the following draft paragraph and identify four distinct violations of results-first research craft: "Financial markets have witnessed dramatic technological evolution over recent decades. From open-outcry pits to high-frequency algorithms, market structure has fundamentally transformed. In this paper, we explore whether high-frequency trading affects liquidity. We use a novel dataset from Euronext and run panel regressions."

Solution

Violations: (1) Narrative/Historical Filler: Sentences 1 and 2 comprise generic historical exposition conveying zero empirical content. (2) Withholding the Answer: Sentence 3 states an exploratory question ("we explore whether high-frequency trading affects liquidity") without reporting the finding. (3) Missing Magnitude and Unit: No quantitative estimate (e.g. spread change in basis points) is reported anywhere in the paragraph. (4) Vague Empirical Design: Sentence 4 mentions "a novel dataset" and "panel regressions" without stating the sample period, identification strategy, or causal mechanism.

Problem 2.12 (Refactoring an Empirical Contribution Narrative): A corporate finance paper finds that mandatory disclosure of carbon emissions causes heavy emitters to divest high-polluting plants to private equity buyers rather than reducing aggregate emissions. Write a 3-sentence opening paragraph satisfying the results-first standard.

Solution

Compliant opening paragraph: "We show that mandatory facility-level greenhouse gas reporting induces United States industrial public firms to increase emissions-intensive plant divestitures by 22 percent relative to unaffected peers from 2010 to 2020. Rather than reducing operational carbon intensity, treated public firms transfer 68 percent of divested polluting assets to unlisted private equity owners that operate outside disclosure mandates. These results indicate that disclosure regulation reallocates carbon emissions across organizational ownership structures without decreasing aggregate physical greenhouse emissions."

8Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. What are the five mandatory components of a results-first empirical headline sentence?

    Options

    Definition 2.1 specifies the five-element tuple: Mechanism, Direction, Magnitude with explicit Unit, Standardized Shock, and Sample Universe with Period.

  2. When should numerical rounding be executed in empirical research reporting?

    Options

    Definition 2.3 mandates that secondary calculations must operate on exact unrounded estimates to avoid compounding rounding errors; rounding occurs only at final display.

  3. If an unrounded regression estimate is 0.1437 percentage points, what is the rounded magnitude in integer basis points?

    0.1437 percentage points equals 14.37 basis points. Rounding to the nearest integer basis point yields 14 basis points.

  4. How must an empirical introduction communicate a statistical null result?

    Options

    A scientific null result requires stating the statistical precision: the point estimate and the confidence interval that bounds the maximum economically plausible effect.

  5. What constitutes a claim scope violation in empirical finance?

    Options

    Definition 2.5 establishes that a claim must be bounded by the observational support and identifying variation of the study. Generalizing beyond the sample is an ungrounded extrapolation.

  6. Why is reporting that an estimate is "statistically significant at the 1% level" insufficient on its own?

    Options

    Statistical significance demonstrates only that the point estimate is distinguishable from zero; without a concrete magnitude and unit, readers cannot judge economic significance.

  7. Under the first-sentence result rule, what is the maximum allowable sentence index in the introduction for stating the primary empirical finding?

    Definition 1.5 and Definition 2.1 establish that the headline finding must appear in sentence 1, with a maximum allowable threshold of sentence 2.

  8. Which element in the results-first tuple indicates the magnitude of the explanatory shock?

    Options

    \(\Delta X\) defines the standardized shift in the explanatory variable (such as one standard deviation increase or an interquartile range shift) that produces the estimated response.
