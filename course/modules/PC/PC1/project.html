Rewrite a Weak Paper Introduction

You rewrite three supplied weak introductions of hypothetical empirical finance papers to the results-first standard established in this module, annotate every structural sentence revision, and evaluate your work against observable rubric descriptors. Every paper is SIMULATED.

The four-rung project ladder

The project follows the canonical four-rung skill progression. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the core rewrite and annotation tasks; students passing the placement diagnostic on the module hub may skip directly to Rung 3.

  1. Worked example

    Lab 1 below provides a fully solved reference rewrite of hypothetical introduction H1, annotated line by line with explicit rationale for each structural change.

  2. Completion

    Lab 2 below provides an incomplete rewrite of hypothetical introduction H2 with the headline finding and roadmap sentences blanked, graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent

    Rewrite all three hypothetical introductions (H1, H2, H3), compile annotations.md, verify compliance via the structural self-check lab, and score your submission against the rubric.

  4. Stretch

    Open-ended extension: design a 10-minute presentation slide deck for one rewrite using the L04 slot budget, or construct a standalone regression table from simulated panel estimates.

Lab 1 · Worked example: rewrite verification and annotation checker for H1

Evaluates weak introduction H1 and its reference rewrite, verifying that the rewrite moves the first empirical number from sentence 7 to sentence 1, adds explicit measurement units (basis points) and sample boundaries, establishes an informative roadmap, and satisfies the 150-to-250 word budget.

"""PC1 Lab Project-1: Rung 1 worked example: rewrite verification and annotation checker for H1.

SIMULATED. Hypothetical introduction H1 and its reference rewrite.
All numbers and text metrics are deterministic. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import re
from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Annotation:
    original_text: str
    rewritten_text: str
    rule_applied: str


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class IntroMetrics:
    word_count: int
    sentence_count: int
    first_number_sentence: int
    has_unit: bool
    has_sample: bool
    has_roadmap: bool


def check_introduction_structure(text: str) -> IntroMetrics:
    """Evaluate structural properties of an empirical introduction."""
    sentences = [s.strip() for s in re.split(r"\.\s+", text.strip()) if s.strip()]
    first_num_sentence = 0
    for idx, s in enumerate(sentences, start=1):
        if re.search(r"\d", s):
            first_num_sentence = idx
            break

    words = len(text.split())
    has_unit = any(
        u in text.lower() for u in ["basis points", "percent", "%", "dollars", "cents", "standard deviation"]
    )
    has_sample = "1990" in text and "2020" in text and "manufacturing" in text.lower()
    has_roadmap = "section" in text.lower() or "remainder of this paper" in text.lower()

    return IntroMetrics(
        word_count=words,
        sentence_count=len(sentences),
        first_number_sentence=first_num_sentence,
        has_unit=has_unit,
        has_sample=has_sample,
        has_roadmap=has_roadmap,
    )


def main() -> None:
    print("PC1 Lab Project-1: Rung 1 Worked Example (Introduction H1 Rewrite)")
    print("SIMULATED text from hypothetical paper H1")

    # Weak H1: literature opener, late unscaled result, generic roadmap

        "alleviate agency frictions or foster anti-competitive coordination across product markets. "
        "Theoretical models offer conflicting predictions regarding whether common owners encourage managers "
        "to curtail redundant capacity or stimulate aggressive expansion. "
        "Prior empirical literature presents mixed findings across diverse historical samples and industries. "
        "In this paper, we explore the empirical linkage using a broad dataset. "
        "We hypothesize that cross-ownership exerts a material impact on firm capital outlays. "
        "Empirically, our regression analysis shows an increase of 14 across manufacturing firms. "
        "Section 2 describes the data. Section 3 presents the methodology. "
        "Section 4 reports the empirical results. Section 5 concludes."
    )

    # Rewritten H1: result in sentence 1, explicit unit, bounded sample, informative roadmap (162 words)
    rewritten_h1 = (
        "We show that institutional cross-ownership increases five-year physical investment by 14 basis points "
        "per standard deviation increase in common ownership across United States manufacturing firms from 1990 to "
        "2020. "
        "This economic response is concentrated in industries characterized by high product-market overlap and "
        "remains statistically robust when instrumenting for institutional holdings using financial institution "
        "mergers as exogenous ownership shocks. "
        "In contrast to unilateral theories of corporate governance, the investment response reflects coordinated "
        "capital expenditure reductions in duplicate manufacturing facilities rather than broad-based expansion. "
        "Economically, a 14 basis point change represents 6.8 percent of median capital expenditure, suggesting "
        "that common portfolio allocations generate measurable real efficiency gains across overlapping product "
        "markets without depressing firm profitability. "
        "Furthermore, we establish that these physical capital adjustments are not explained by differences in "
        "leverage, firm size, or growth opportunities across treatment cohorts. "
        "The remainder of this paper details the sample selection criteria, the quasi-experimental identification "
        "strategy, primary regression results, extensive robustness checks, and concluding implications for "
        "financial regulation."
    )

    annotations = [
        Annotation(
            original_text="The relationship between corporate ownership structure...",
            rewritten_text="We show that institutional cross-ownership increases...",
            rule_applied="Result First: Replaced literature opener with headline finding and unit in Sentence 1.",
        ),
        Annotation(
            original_text="...shows an increase of 14 across manufacturing firms.",
            rewritten_text="...by 14 basis points per standard deviation increase... 1990 to 2020.",
            rule_applied="Explicit Unit and Sample: Attached basis point unit, standardized shift, and sample dates.",
        ),
        Annotation(
            original_text="We hypothesize that cross-ownership exerts a material impact...",
            rewritten_text="The response reflects coordinated reductions in duplicate facilities...",
            rule_applied="Mechanism and Scope: Clarified economic channel without generalizing beyond manufacturing.",
        ),
        Annotation(
            original_text="Section 2 describes the data. Section 3 presents the methodology...",
            rewritten_text="The remainder of this paper details the sample selection criteria...",
            rule_applied="Informative Roadmap: Replaced bare section numbers with content-descriptive signposts.",
        ),
    ]

    before = check_introduction_structure(weak_h1)
    after = check_introduction_structure(rewritten_h1)

    print("\n--- Structural Comparison ---")

    print(f"{'Explicit unit present':26s} | {before.has_unit!s:12s} | {after.has_unit!s:12s} | {'True':25s}")
    print(f"{'Sample & dates present':26s} | {before.has_sample!s:12s} | {after.has_sample!s:12s} | {'True':25s}")
    print(
        f"{'Roadmap present':26s} | {before.has_roadmap!s:12s} | "

Rung 3 · Project Specification

Context
In empirical financial economics, flawed introductions that bury findings behind expansive literature surveys prevent referee and reader engagement. A researcher must rewrite three supplied weak paper introductions (H1, H2, H3) to the results-first research craft standard, ensuring that every empirical claim is strictly bounded by observational support and that every structural change is documented in an accompanying annotation log.
Inputs
Three supplied weak introductions:
  • H1 (Corporate Governance): Common ownership and corporate investment across U.S. manufacturing firms (1990–2020). Baseline finding: 14 basis points increase in physical investment per standard deviation increase in common ownership.
  • H2 (Sovereign Debt): Sovereign debt restructuring haircuts and secondary bond spreads across 48 emerging market default episodes (1980–2018). Baseline finding: haircuts exceeding 30% reduce spreads by 185 basis points.
  • H3 (Market Microstructure): High-frequency order flow imbalance and 10-year Treasury futures returns (2015–2022). Baseline finding: 5-minute order flow imbalance predicts next-minute yield changes by 0.32 basis points with out-of-sample R-squared of 4.1%.
All papers are hypothetical models; all figures are SIMULATED.
Required outputs
Four markdown files created in your working repository:
  1. rewrite-H1.md: The refactored introduction for paper H1 (150 to 250 words).
  2. rewrite-H2.md: The refactored introduction for paper H2 (150 to 250 words).
  3. rewrite-H3.md: The refactored introduction for paper H3 (150 to 250 words).
  4. annotations.md: A structured log containing at least 4 annotations per rewrite (12 total), recording original text, rewritten text, and the specific research craft rule applied.
Constraints
  • Length: Each rewrite must contain between 150 and 250 words.
  • Result First: Sentence 1 (or sentence 2) must state the primary quantitative finding with units, direction, and sample support.
  • Bounded Scope: Claims must not extrapolate beyond the stated sample period, firm universe, or identifying variation of the hypothetical paper.
  • Exact Rounding: All reported prose magnitudes must match the underlying unrounded estimates within stated half-unit rounding bounds.
  • Citation Integrity: No external unverified sources may be cited; citations within rewrites may only reference works in the program registry.
Grading
Grading is by rubric, evaluated across five criteria with observable performance levels. You self-assess your submission against the rubric and compare your evaluation with the official exemplar scoring.

"""PC1 Lab Project-3: Rung 3 independent project self-check on rewrites of H1, H2, H3 and annotations.

SIMULATED. Evaluates structural properties of three paper introduction rewrites.
Scores structure only, not literary quality. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import re
from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class IntroCheckResult:
    paper_id: str
    word_count: int
    first_num_sent: int
    has_unit: bool
    has_sample: bool
    has_roadmap: bool
    all_structural_ok: bool


def verify_rewrite(paper_id: str, text: str, sample_keywords: list[str]) -> IntroCheckResult:
    """Verify the 5 core structural requirements for an introduction rewrite."""
    words = len(text.split())
    sentences = [s.strip() for s in re.split(r"\.\s+", text.strip()) if s.strip()]

    first_num_sent = 0
    for idx, s in enumerate(sentences, start=1):
        if re.search(r"\d", s):
            first_num_sent = idx
            break

    has_unit = any(
        u in text.lower()
        for u in [
            "basis points",
            "percent",
            "%",
            "dollars",
            "cents",
            "r-squared",
            "standard deviation",
        ]
    )
    has_sample = all(k.lower() in text.lower() for k in sample_keywords)
    has_roadmap = "remainder of this paper" in text.lower() or "section" in text.lower()

    ok = 150 <= words <= 250 and first_num_sent == 1 and has_unit and has_sample and has_roadmap

    return IntroCheckResult(
        paper_id=paper_id,
        word_count=words,
        first_num_sent=first_num_sent,
        has_unit=has_unit,
        has_sample=has_sample,
        has_roadmap=has_roadmap,
        all_structural_ok=ok,
    )

Rubric

The submission is scored across five criteria, each evaluated at four observable performance levels. Meeting the qualification standard requires achieving Level 3 or Level 4 across all five criteria.

Introduction rewrite rubric: five criteria, four observable performance levels each.
Criterion 4 · Exemplary 3 · Proficient 2 · Developing 1 · Beginning
C1 · Result First The first paragraph states the primary quantitative estimate with its explicit measurement unit, sample, and signed direction in sentence 1 (or 2), prior to any external literature citation. The first paragraph states the headline estimate and unit, but defers it past sentence 2 or positions it after preliminary background literature. The headline result appears in the introduction, but lacks an explicit measurement unit or signed direction verb. The headline result is absent from the introduction, or is stated only as an exploratory question without an answer.
C2 · Claim Scope Every empirical claim strictly names its sample universe, calendar period, and identifying variation; zero claims extrapolate beyond observational support. Claims name sample and period, but include one unsupported general assertion regarding broader economic applicability. Claims report empirical estimates without explicitly bounding the calendar period or asset subpopulation. Claims assert universal applicability across all financial markets or corporate firms without empirical support.
C3 · Roadmap and Structure The roadmap names each subsequent section by substantive finding and methodological content in canonical six-section order. The roadmap lists sections in canonical order, but uses generic descriptive phrases for two or more sections. The roadmap lists bare section numbers without substantive content ("Section 2 describes data; Section 3 describes methods"). No roadmap is provided, or sections are presented out of canonical empirical sequence.
C4 · Annotation Every modified sentence is logged with original text, rewritten text, and specific craft rule applied; at least 4 annotations per rewrite (12 total). Annotations provide original and rewritten text with craft rules, but miss one modified passage across the submission. Annotations summarize changes globally without mapping to specific sentences, or rules are stated vaguely ("improved prose"). Changes are unannotated, or annotations state only "improved writing" without explanatory rules.
C5 · Limits States exactly one concrete limitation per rewrite, strictly tied to identifying variation, sample support, or transaction frictions, in one declarative sentence. States a limitation, but relies on a generic caveat ("more data would help") rather than an identification bound. States limitations vaguely without connecting them to empirical findings or parameter sensitivity. Omits limitations entirely or asserts that the empirical design is free of caveats.

Exemplar A · Level 4 (Exemplary)

Fully compliant rewrite of H1 meeting Level 4 across all five criteria.

We show that institutional cross-ownership increases five-year physical investment by 14 basis points per standard deviation increase in common ownership across United States manufacturing firms from 1990 to 2020. This economic response is concentrated in industries characterized by high product-market overlap and remains statistically robust when instrumenting for institutional holdings using financial institution mergers as exogenous ownership shocks. In contrast to unilateral theories of corporate governance, the investment response reflects coordinated capital expenditure reductions in duplicate manufacturing facilities rather than broad-based expansion. Economically, a 14 basis point change represents 6.8 percent of median capital expenditure, suggesting that common portfolio allocations generate measurable real efficiency gains across overlapping product markets without depressing firm profitability. Furthermore, we establish that these physical capital adjustments are not explained by differences in leverage, firm size, or growth opportunities across treatment cohorts. A central limitation is that our instrumental variable identifies local average treatment effects specifically among firms undergoing institutional merger exposure, which may not generalize to privately held enterprises. The remainder of this paper details the sample selection criteria, the quasi-experimental identification strategy, primary regression results, extensive robustness checks, and concluding implications for financial regulation.

  • Meets C1 Level 4: States mechanism, direction (increases), point estimate with unit (14 basis points), standardized shock, and sample in Sentence 1 before any literature.
