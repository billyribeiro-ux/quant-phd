Investment Memos & Trade Pitches

Institutional capital allocation is mediated not by mathematical proofs or 60-page sell-side reports, but by rigorous investment memos, asymmetric one-page trade pitches, and adversarial Investment Committee (IC) defenses. This module trains you to formulate actionable variant perceptions, construct probability-weighted base/bull/bear valuation distributions, identify discrete market catalysts, execute Gary Klein’s prospective hindsight pre-mortems to establish binding falsification tripwires, calculate fractional Kelly sizing under Almgren-Chriss liquidity horizons, and survive hostile committee cross-examination. In the capstone project, you author and defend both a long equity pitch derived from your A16 valuation model and a macro trade pitch derived from your A20 engine.

  • Prerequisites: CF4 Valuation and PC1 Research Writing & Presentation. Prerequisite closure includes AC1, AC2, AP2, CF1, CF2, CS1, E1, E3, M1, M2, M3, M4, M7, P0.1, S0, S1, S2.
  • Project: Two Pitches: Long Equity and Macro Trade, evaluated against a 5-criterion observable rubric with annotated exemplars and a 15-question adversarial bank.
  • Data: SIMULATED financial statements, valuation parameters, yield-curve spreads, and institutional committee interrogation transcripts. No external data redistribution required.

32 h lessons · 8 h project (20%)

Objectives

By the end of PC2 you can:

  1. Deconstruct institutional investment memos into the canonical six-section architecture, and audit draft memos to eliminate descriptive sell-side filler in favor of actionable capital-allocation contracts (Lesson 1).
  2. Synthesize complex multi-asset investment theses into high-conviction one-page trade pitches satisfying the 3-minute executive scan test and meeting the 2.0x return asymmetry hurdle (Lesson 2).
  3. Model probability-weighted payoff distributions across Base, Bull, and Bear scenarios, and quantify the margin of safety and downside skewness for equity and macro instruments (Lesson 1 and Lesson 2).
  4. Execute Gary Klein’s prospective hindsight pre-mortem protocol across five systematic risk categories, and formulate non-negotiable quantitative invalidation tripwires (Lesson 3).
  5. Calculate institutional position sizing under fractional Kelly criteria, single-position mandate risk policies, and Almgren-Chriss liquidation liquidity constraints (Lesson 4).
  6. Defend investment proposals under hostile Investment Committee cross-examination using the canonical three-step defense protocol: acknowledge and bound, present empirical base rates, and state conditional exit tripwires (Lesson 4 and Project).

Prerequisites

PC2 builds upon the valuation mechanics of CF4 and the empirical communication discipline of PC1. It uses these concepts from the prerequisite closure without redefining them:

  • DCF valuation on free cash flow to the firm (CF4): Deriving operating enterprise value by discounting cash flows at the weighted average cost of capital.
  • Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) (CF4): Net operating profit after tax adjusted for depreciation, capital expenditures, and working capital changes.
  • Terminal value (CF4): Capitalizing post-horizon cash flows via the Gordon growth formula or exit EBITDA multiples.
  • Enterprise value to equity value bridge (CF4): Reconciling operating enterprise value to common equity value per share.
  • Beneish M-Score (AC2): Eight-variable probit diagnostic detecting earnings manipulation and accrual inflation.
  • Results-first introduction (PC1): Stating the core quantitative finding, signed direction, and operational mechanism in the opening sentences.
  • Standalone table (PC1): Designing tabular exhibits that communicate entire empirical findings without surrounding prose.
  • Presenting to an expert audience (PC1): Budgeting slide pacing and executing structured Q&A defense against skeptical evaluators.

Placement diagnostic

Pass this diagnostic at 80% mastery (including all items marked with skill badges) to demonstrate working proficiency in valuation mechanics, results-first synthesis, and position sizing, enabling you to skip scaffolding Rungs 1 and 2 directly to Rung 3.

  1. An analyst pitches a long equity position at an entry price of $60.00. The Bear case target price is $45.00 (reflecting multiple compression to 10x EBITDA). Under the canonical institutional rule requiring a minimum 2.0x upside-to-downside asymmetry ratio, what is the minimum required Base case target price?

    Downside risk is $(60.00 - 45.00) = $15.00 (25.0%). A 2.0x asymmetry ratio requires Base case upside potential of at least 2.0 * $15.00 = $30.00 (50.0%). Therefore, the minimum Base case target is $60.00 + $30.00 = $90.00.

  2. In designing a US Treasury 2Y/10Y yield curve steepener trade derived from an A20 macro trade engine, the 2Y note contract has a DV01 of $42.50 per contract, while the 10Y note contract has a DV01 of $85.00 per contract. To construct a duration-neutral (DV01-neutral) steepener with 50 short 10Y contracts, how many long 2Y contracts must be purchased?

    Total 10Y leg DV01 is 50 * $85.00 = $4,250 per basis point. To match this duration risk on the long 2Y leg, the fund requires $4,250 / $42.50 = 100 contracts of 2Y Treasuries (a 2.0x contract hedge ratio).

  3. In a discounted cash flow valuation derived under CF4, what distinguishes the Gordon growth terminal value from the value-driver formulation?

    The value-driver formula replaces arbitrary terminal cash flows with NOPAT * (1 - g/RONIC) / (WACC - g), ensuring that growth only creates value if the return on new invested capital exceeds the cost of capital.

  4. According to the results-first standard established in PC1, which sentence represents a compliant opening for an institutional investment pitch memo?

    An institutional memo opening must immediately deliver the recommendation, position size, current price, target price, signed return, economic mechanism, and risk/reward asymmetry in sentence 1.

  5. Under the discrete Kelly criterion for an investment trade with win probability p = 0.60, win payoff b = 0.35 (+35%), and loss payoff a = 0.15 (-15%), what is the unconstrained full Kelly fraction f*?

    Full Kelly is f* = (p*b - (1-p)*a) / (a*b) = (0.60*0.35 - 0.40*0.15) / (0.15*0.35) = (0.21 - 0.06) / 0.0525 = 0.15 / 0.0525 = 2.857 (285.7% NAV). Institutional funds scale this by 0.25x (71.4% NAV) and bind it by fund mandate caps (e.g. 5.0% NAV).

  6. What forensic accounting diagnostic indicates aggressive revenue recognition and earnings inflation under the Beneish M-Score model?

    A DSRI > 1.0 indicates accounts receivable are outpacing revenues, a primary red flag for accelerated billing. An overall Beneish M-Score > -1.78 flags a high probability of earnings manipulation.

Lesson map

Four lessons totaling 32 study hours build the foundational craft of institutional capital allocation:

  1. Lesson 1 · Anatomy of the Institutional Investment Memo

    8 hours · Introduces investment-memo

    The memo as a binding capital-allocation decision engine vs. descriptive sell-side research. The canonical six-section sequence: Executive Summary, Variant Perception, Business Architecture, Forensic Accounting, Multi-Scenario Valuation (Base/Bull/Bear), and Risk/Sizing. Probability-weighted expected value, downside skewness, and margin of safety calculations.

  2. Lesson 2 · The One-Page Trade Pitch Architecture

    8 hours · Introduces trade-pitch and catalyst

    The economics of executive brevity: passing the 3-minute executive scan test. Constructing the pitch hook, variant perception, and return-to-risk asymmetry. Categorizing scheduled vs. unscheduled and micro vs. macro catalysts. Post-announcement drift, pricing lag, and the impact of financing borrow drag on net alpha.

  3. Lesson 3 · Pre-Mortems & Falsification Criteria

    8 hours · Introduces pre-mortem

    Gary Klein’s prospective hindsight protocol: neutralizing overconfidence, confirmation bias, and escalating commitment by assuming catastrophic failure 12 months forward. Evaluating five systematic failure categories: thesis invalidation, execution blowout, competitive response, balance sheet refinancing, and factor crowding. Formulating non-negotiable quantitative invalidation tripwires.

  4. Lesson 4 · Investment Committee Defense & Position Sizing

    8 hours · Introduces position-sizing-section and ic-adversarial-qa

    Mathematical position sizing: discrete Kelly growth optimization, fractional Kelly (0.25x–0.50x) de-leveraging, and Almgren-Chriss market liquidity horizons. Institutional sociology of the Investment Committee: why ICs attack. Surviving adversarial cross-examination via the three-step defense protocol: acknowledge and bound, present empirical base rates, and state conditional exit rules.

The project ladder

The capstone project develops institutional pitch writing and committee defense capabilities across the canonical four-rung ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Examine a fully solved institutional long equity pitch for Enterprise Infrastructure Cloud (EIC) derived from an A16 valuation model, accompanied by a 5-step scripted IC adversarial defense with line-by-line annotations.

  2. Completion

    Complete a US Treasury 2Y/10Y yield curve steepener macro trade pitch derived from the A20 trade-construction engine, calculating the DV01 hedge ratio, fractional Kelly sizing, and missing IC defense arguments.

  3. Independent

    Author two complete pitches: one long equity pitch (from your A16 valuation) and one macro trade pitch (from your A20 engine). Defend each pitch against 5 hostile attacks selected from the 15-question IC attack bank, evaluated against observable rubrics C1–C5.

  4. Stretch

    Formulate an activist intervention thesis or multi-asset macro cross-market pitch, including credit-default swap (CDS) hedging overlays and dynamic factor attribution stress testing.
