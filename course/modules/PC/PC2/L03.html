Pre-Mortems & Falsification Criteria

High-conviction investment ideas are particularly susceptible to human psychological failure: once an analyst falls in love with a thesis, confirmation bias systematically filters out contradictory data. Traditional risk sections ask, 'What could go wrong?', yielding superficial lists of generic platitudes. Gary Klein's prospective hindsight pre-mortem forces teams to assume the trade has already suffered catastrophic failure, surfacing hidden structural vulnerabilities and establishing binding, quantitative invalidation tripwires before capital is deployed.

1. The Psychology of Prospective Hindsight

In standard investment diligence, analysts draft risk sections under an optimism bias. When asked "What might go wrong?", team members instinctively soften their critiques to avoid appearing unsupportive of colleagues. In 2007, cognitive psychologist Gary Klein formalized an alternative protocol in the Harvard Business Review: the pre-mortem.

Definition 3.1 · Pre-Mortem in Capital Allocation

A pre-mortem is an ex-ante managerial protocol wherein an investment team assumes that a prospective high-conviction trade has suffered catastrophic failure 12 months in the future, and works backward from that established failure state to identify the exact causal mechanisms, leading indicators, and invalidation triggers that led to the collapse.

The psychological power of the pre-mortem rests on prospective hindsight. When individuals are asked to generate reasons why an uncertain event might fail, they generate few reasons. In contrast, when instructed to assume the failure has already occurred with certainty, cognitive search shifts from prospective speculation to retrospective explanation, unlocking hidden doubts and legitimizing dissenting analysis without social friction.

2. Cognitive Biases in Institutional Capital Allocation

A successful investment memo or trade pitch must systematically neutralize four pervasive psychological biases documented in behavioral finance:

Cognitive Bias Manifestation in Investment Teams Institutional Risk Consequence Pre-Mortem Countermeasure
Overconfidence Bias Investors trade excessively and hold unhedged, concentrated positions because they overestimate the precision of their private information. Extreme position sizing without sufficient margin of safety. Forces explicit assignment of prior failure probabilities and bounding of maximum drawdown.
Confirmation Bias & Loss Aversion Loss aversion induces risk-seeking behavior in negative payoff domains: investors refuse to realize losses, holding deteriorating positions. Doubling down on failing positions ("bag-holding" and escalating commitment). Mandates pre-committed liquidation tripwires that remove discretionary exit decisions.
Social Groupthink & Deference Junior analysts withhold dissenting viewpoints to avoid challenging senior portfolio managers. Consensus blindness to emerging structural competitors or accounting red flags. Grants institutional permission to identify failure modes by framing critique as project protection.
Narrative Fallacy Analysts construct compelling, linear qualitative stories that explain past success while ignoring quantitative base rates. Treating cyclical industry peaks as permanent secular transformations. Audits cash flow conversion and tests operational metrics against historical cohort base rates.

3. The Five Failure Vectors & Quantitative Tripwires

An institutional pre-mortem categorizes prospective failure modes into five systematic risk vectors:

  1. Vector 1: Variant Perception Invalidation: The market consensus was right all along; the analyst's perceived "temporary margin dip" was a permanent structural deterioration.
  2. Vector 2: Execution and Cost Management: Capital expenditure overruns, customer acquisition cost (CAC) inflation, or botched international expansion consume operational liquidity.
  3. Vector 3: Competitive Response and Pricing Wars: An incumbent or cloud hyperscaler bundles a competing solution at zero marginal cost, triggering gross margin compression.
  4. Vector 4: Balance Sheet and Refinancing Liquidity: Debt maturities arrive during a credit market freeze; interest expense surges or covenant breaches force dilutive equity issuance.
  5. Vector 5: Macro Factor Rotations and Crowding Shocks: Rising risk-free rates or factor rotation out of growth/momentum triggers correlated institutional selling and liquidity evaporation.
Definition 3.2 · Quantitative Invalidation Tripwire

A falsification tripwire is an observable, pre-committed quantitative metric threshold (e.g. Net Retention Rate \(< 105\%\), Debt/EBITDA \(> 3.5x\), Spread widening \(> 50\text{ bps}\)) whose breach constitutes definitive refutation of the core investment thesis, triggering mandatory, non-discretionary position liquidation within one trading session.

A tripwire transforms vague qualitative anxiety into an automated risk protocol. When an operational metric breaches a tripwire, the portfolio manager does not convene a debate on whether to hold; the position is closed immediately, cutting losses before capital destruction accelerates.

"""PC2 Lab L03-1: Institutional pre-mortem generator and falsification tripwire monitor.

SIMULATED. Implements Gary Klein's (2007) prospective hindsight technique,
generating failure modes across five systematic risk categories and evaluating
observable metric checkpoints against non-negotiable thesis invalidation tripwires.
Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass
from enum import StrEnum


class RiskCategory(StrEnum):
    VARIANT_PERCEPTION = "Variant Perception Invalidation"
    EXECUTION_COSTS = "Execution and Cost Management"
    COMPETITIVE_RESPONSE = "Competitive Response and Pricing"
    BALANCE_SHEET = "Balance Sheet and Liquidity"
    MACRO_CROWDING = "Macro Factor and Crowding Shocks"


class TripwireStatus(StrEnum):
    NORMAL = "NORMAL"
    WARNING = "WARNING"
    BREACHED = "BREACHED"


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FailureMode:
    failure_id: str
    category: RiskCategory
    mechanism: str
    leading_indicator: str
    current_value: float
    warning_threshold: float
    breach_threshold: float
    lower_is_worse: bool

    def evaluate(self, observed_value: float) -> TripwireStatus:
        """Evaluate observed metric against warning and breach thresholds."""
        if self.lower_is_worse:
            if observed_value <= self.breach_threshold:
                return TripwireStatus.BREACHED
            if observed_value <= self.warning_threshold:
                return TripwireStatus.WARNING
            return TripwireStatus.NORMAL
        else:
            if observed_value >= self.breach_threshold:
                return TripwireStatus.BREACHED
            if observed_value >= self.warning_threshold:
                return TripwireStatus.WARNING
            return TripwireStatus.NORMAL


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class PremortemAuditResult:
    total_modes: int
    breached_count: int
    warning_count: int
    normal_count: int
    thesis_status: str
    action_directive: str


def run_premortem_audit(
    modes: list[FailureMode],
    observed_metrics: dict[str, float],
) -> tuple[PremortemAuditResult, list[tuple[FailureMode, float, TripwireStatus]]]:
    """Execute pre-mortem falsification check against current operational metrics."""
    evaluations: list[tuple[FailureMode, float, TripwireStatus]] = []
    breached = 0
    warnings = 0
    normals = 0

    for mode in modes:
        observed = observed_metrics.get(mode.failure_id, mode.current_value)
        status = mode.evaluate(observed)
        if status == TripwireStatus.BREACHED:
            breached += 1
        elif status == TripwireStatus.WARNING:
            warnings += 1
        else:
            normals += 1
        evaluations.append((mode, observed, status))

    if breached > 0:
        thesis_status = "INVALIDATED"
        action_directive = "MANDATORY EXIT: Liquidate position within 1 trading session."
    elif warnings >= 2:
        thesis_status = "UNDER SURVEILLANCE"
        action_directive = "DE-RISK: Cut position size by 50% and demand management explanation."
    else:
        thesis_status = "VALID"
        action_directive = "HOLD / ACCUMULATE: Core thesis metrics intact."

    audit_result = PremortemAuditResult(
        total_modes=len(modes),
        breached_count=breached,
        warning_count=warnings,
        normal_count=normals,
        thesis_status=thesis_status,
        action_directive=action_directive,
    )
    return audit_result, evaluations

4. Pre-Mortem vs. Post-Mortem in Institutional Governance

Institutional investment committees maintain both pre-mortem and post-mortem protocols, each serving distinct governance functions:

Dimension Ex-Ante Pre-Mortem Ex-Post Post-Mortem
Timing Before capital allocation (during memo approval). After position liquidation (at trade completion or stop-loss exit).
Primary Objective Capital preservation: shape position sizing, establish hedges, set tripwires. Skill attribution: separate decision quality from market outcome noise.
Psychological Mode Prospective hindsight: "Imagine we have failed; why?" Root cause analysis: "Did the thesis fail or did exogenous factors dominate?"
Actionable Output Binding liquidation tripwires and fractional Kelly sizing caps. Analyst scoring updates, process improvements, and model recalibrations.

5. Where the Canon Breaks

Pre-mortem implementations fail when firms treat them as a checkbox compliance exercise:

  • Tripwire Renegotiation: When an operational metric hits a hard invalidation tripwire, the analyst invents a new narrative rationalizing why "this quarter is exceptional," moving the stop level lower. A tripwire whose execution is subject to debate is completely useless.
  • Paralysis by Extreme Tail Risk: Listing improbable global catastrophic events (e.g. asteroid strikes, total currency collapse) that distract from realistic operational failure modes like customer churn or margin degradation.
  • Unobservable Metrics: Setting tripwires on unmeasurable internal metrics (e.g. "management enthusiasm drops") rather than audited, third-party verifiable metrics (e.g. quarterly SEC filing data or verified alternate data).

6. Common Misconceptions

  1. A pre-mortem is designed to generate reasons to reject every high-conviction investment idea and promote zero-risk paralysis.

    A pre-mortem is designed to protect and optimize good ideas. By explicitly bounding the downside and establishing automated exit criteria, portfolio managers gain the confidence to size high-conviction positions more aggressively knowing capital is safeguarded.

  2. Setting a strict price-based stop-loss order (e.g. -15%) eliminates the need for an operational pre-mortem and fundamental tripwires.

    Price stops alone suffer from two severe defects: they can be triggered by transient market liquidity noise in high-quality assets, and they fail to trigger when fundamental deterioration occurs slowly without immediate price collapse, exposing the fund to sudden gapping downside.

  3. Once an investment memo is approved by the committee, the analyst's job is to defend the thesis until the target price is achieved.

    An institutional analyst's fiduciary duty is to actively attempt to falsify the thesis continuously. The moment proprietary data indicates an operational tripwire has been breached, the analyst must initiate liquidation rather than defending sunk costs.

7. Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An analyst establishes a pre-mortem tripwire for a subscription SaaS investment: the thesis is invalidated if trailing-twelve-month Net Retention Rate (NRR) falls below 108.0%, or if Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) Payback exceeds 18.0 months. In Q1, NRR is 112.5% and CAC Payback is 15.2 months. In Q2, NRR is 106.8% and CAC Payback is 16.5 months. In Q3, NRR is 109.1% and CAC Payback is 19.4 months. Determine the thesis status and required institutional directive for each quarter.

Solution

Q1: NRR = 112.5% (> 108.0%) and CAC Payback = 15.2 months (< 18.0 months). Both metrics satisfy criteria. Status: VALID. Directive: HOLD / ACCUMULATE.

Q2: NRR = 106.8% (≤ 108.0% threshold). This represents a hard breach of the retention tripwire, even though CAC payback is acceptable. Status: INVALIDATED. Directive: MANDATORY EXIT: Liquidate position within 1 trading session.

Q3: NRR recovered to 109.1%, but CAC Payback reached 19.4 months (> 18.0 months). Even if the position had not been liquidated in Q2, CAC payback now triggers a hard breach. Status: INVALIDATED. Directive: MANDATORY EXIT.

A bank equity pitch has an entry price of $50.00. The pre-mortem identifies commercial real estate loan charge-offs as the primary failure vector. The analyst sets a dual tripwire: (1) Non-Performing Assets (NPA) to Total Loans exceeds 2.50%, or (2) Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio falls below 10.50%. If NPA reaches 2.75% while CET1 is 11.20%, evaluate whether the thesis is falsified.

Solution

Under institutional falsification criteria, tripwires are formulated with disjunctive ("OR") logic: breach of any single core parameter falsifies the thesis. Since NPA / Loans of 2.75% strictly exceeds the 2.50% threshold, the thesis is falsified regardless of current CET1 capital adequacy. The analyst cannot argue that strong CET1 mitigates the credit quality breach; the position must be liquidated.

An analyst configures five pre-mortem failure modes for an equity trade. Historical tracking shows that warning thresholds trigger with probability 0.20 each quarter independently. Calculate the probability that at least two failure modes show warnings in a given quarter.

Solution

Let \(X \sim \text{Binomial}(n=5, p=0.20)\) be the number of warnings. We seek \(\Prob(X \ge 2) = 1 - \Prob(X = 0) - \Prob(X = 1)\).

\[ \Prob(X = 0) = (1 - 0.20)^5 = (0.80)^5 = 0.32768 \] \[ \Prob(X = 1) = \binom{5}{1} (0.20)^1 (0.80)^4 = 5 \times 0.20 \times 0.4096 = 0.40960 \] \[ \Prob(X \ge 2) = 1 - 0.32768 - 0.40960 = 1 - 0.73728 = 0.26272 \quad (26.27\%) \]

There is approximately a 26.3% probability of triggering the multi-warning surveillance review in any quarter under independent baseline noise.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that implementing an automated falsification tripwire with maximum capital loss threshold \(L_{\text{max}}\) guarantees that the portfolio's realized drawdown on the position cannot exceed \(L_{\text{max}}\) in continuous trading without price gapping. What happens under discrete market jumps?

Solution

Let asset price follow a continuous semimartingale \(P_t\). Define the stopping time \(\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0 : (P_0 - P_t)/P_0 \ge L_{\text{max}}\}\). By continuity of sample paths, \(P_\tau = P_0 (1 - L_{\text{max}})\). Since the automated tripwire executes liquidation immediately at \(\tau\), the realized loss is exactly \((P_0 - P_\tau)/P_0 = L_{\text{max}}\), guaranteeing the drawdown bound.

Under discrete jump processes (e.g. jump-diffusion \(dP_t = \mu P_t dt + \sigma P_t dW_t + J_t P_{t-} dN_t\)), the price can jump discontinuously past the tripwire: \(P_\tau < P_0 (1 - L_{\text{max}})\). In this case, realized loss is \((P_0 - P_\tau)/P_0 = L_{\text{max}} + \text{Jump Gap}\), proving that tripwires bound losses exactly only under continuous liquidity, and must be padded with cash reserves or put option hedges to manage overnight jump risk.

Let a fund hold \(N\) independent investment positions, each having an ex-ante pre-mortem failure probability \(q \in (0, 1)\). If each failure mode is detected early with probability \(\beta\) (the detector sensitivity), prove that the expected number of positions liquidated prior to catastrophic loss is \(N q \beta\), and derive the variance.

Solution

For each position \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\), let \(Y_i = 1\) if position \(i\) fails AND the pre-mortem tripwire successfully detects it early, and \(Y_i = 0\) otherwise. By conditional probability, \(\Prob(Y_i = 1) = \Prob(\text{Failed}) \cdot \Prob(\text{Detected} \mid \text{Failed}) = q \cdot \beta\).

Since the \(N\) positions are independent, \(Y_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(q \beta)\), and the total number of early liquidated positions is \(S_N = \sum_{i=1}^N Y_i \sim \text{Binomial}(N, q \beta)\).

The expectation is \(\E[S_N] = N q \beta\). The variance of a binomial random variable is \(\Var(S_N) = N (q \beta) (1 - q \beta)\).

Show that under prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky 1979), the value function \(v(x)\) is concave for gains (\(x > 0\)) and convex for losses (\(x < 0\)). Prove that this implies risk-seeking behavior over losses, explaining why discretionary investors fail to cut losing trades without automated tripwires.

Solution

Kahneman and Tversky (1979) specify the value function as \(v(x) = x^\alpha\) for \(x \ge 0\) and \(v(x) = -\lambda (-x)^\beta\) for \(x < 0\), with \(\alpha, \beta \in (0, 1)\) and loss aversion parameter \(\lambda > 1\) (empirically \(\approx 2.25\)).

For losses (\(x < 0\)), let \(z = -x > 0\), so \(v(x) = -\lambda z^\beta\). Differentiating with respect to \(x\):

\[ \frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{dv}{dz} \frac{dz}{dx} = (-\lambda \beta z^{\beta-1}) (-1) = \lambda \beta (-x)^{\beta-1} > 0 \] \[ \frac{d^2v}{dx^2} = \lambda \beta (\beta-1) (-x)^{\beta-2} (-1) = \lambda \beta (1-\beta) (-x)^{\beta-2} > 0 \]

Since \(v''(x) > 0\) for \(x < 0\), the value function is strictly convex over losses. By Jensen's inequality, for any zero-mean gamble \(\tilde{\epsilon}\), \(\E[v(x + \tilde{\epsilon})] > v(x)\) in the loss domain. Hence, an investor facing a certain realized loss \(x < 0\) strictly prefers taking an unhedged gamble on recovery rather than locking in the loss, proving that human psychology inherently escalates commitment to losing positions unless bound by automated tripwires.

Tier 3 · Empirical

In their study of individual and institutional trading behaviors, Barber & Odean (2000) find that portfolio turnover degrades net performance. Explain how overconfidence leads analysts to omit pre-mortem failure modes, and describe the empirical evidence regarding the performance penalty of unhedged confidence.

Solution

Barber & Odean (2000) demonstrate that the most active traders (highest turnover quintile, over 250% annually) earn an average annual net return of only 11.4%, compared to 18.5% for the least active buy-and-hold quintile—a massive 7.1% annual penalty driven by trading costs and misplaced conviction.

Omitting Pre-Mortem Failure Modes: Overconfidence leads analysts to believe their DCF forecasts are highly accurate, viewing variance as negligible. Consequently, they treat pre-mortems as unnecessary impediments, failing to model competitive responses or balance sheet refinancing risks. When negative surprises occur, their unhedged high-turnover reallocation incurs substantial execution drag and portfolio drawdowns.

A quant hedge fund audits 100 historical equity pitch memos over five years: 60 pitches succeeded (achieved Base or Bull target), while 40 pitches failed (liquidated at or below Bear stop). In the 40 failed pitches, pre-mortem tripwires were formally documented in 25 memos, while 15 memos lacked explicit tripwires. In the 25 pitches with tripwires, average realized loss was 12.4%; in the 15 pitches without tripwires, average realized loss was 34.8%. Calculate the empirical capital preservation benefit of pre-mortem tripwires.

Solution

Average loss reduction: \(34.8\% - 12.4\% = 22.4\) percentage points of preserved capital per failed trade.

Total dollar preservation on a $10M position allocation: On 15 trades without tripwires, average loss was $3.48M; with tripwires, the loss would have been bounded to $1.24M, saving $2.24M per trade. Across 15 trades, this equals \$33.6M in cumulative capital preservation.

Empirical Takeaway: Pre-mortems do not prevent ideas from failing; they prevent failure from becoming catastrophic by cutting losses at pre-committed threshold levels.

An analyst pitches a long thesis in an offshore wind turbine developer based on government subsidies and power purchase agreements. Draft three concrete pre-mortem failure modes covering Vector 1 (Variant Perception), Vector 2 (Execution), and Vector 4 (Balance Sheet), each with an explicit quantitative tripwire.

Solution

(1) Vector 1 (Variant Perception - Wholesale Power Price Decoupling): Failure mechanism: Merchant electricity prices collapse due to regional solar oversupply, invalidating projected uncontracted power margins. Leading indicator: PJM/ERCOT off-peak forward contract price. Quantitative Tripwire: Forward power price drops below $32/MWh on a 30-day moving average.

(2) Vector 2 (Execution - Offshore Installation Vessel Bottleneck): Failure mechanism: Shortage of specialized heavy-lift installation vessels delays wind farm commissioning by 18 months, triggering contractual delay penalties. Leading indicator: Vessel day-rates and charter lead time. Quantitative Tripwire: Installation vessel day-rate exceeds $350,000/day, or installation timeline extends past Q3 schedule by > 90 days.

(3) Vector 4 (Balance Sheet - Floating-Rate Project Debt Spread Blowout): Failure mechanism: Construction debt interest rate swaps expire; floating SOFR debt service consumes operating cash flow. Leading indicator: Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR). Quantitative Tripwire: Project-level DSCR falls below 1.20x.

Tier 4 · Research

Contrast the institutional decision architecture of Gary Klein's pre-mortem with traditional "Red Team" adversarial reviews. In what organizational cultures does a pre-mortem succeed where Red Teams provoke defensive retrenchment?

Solution

In a traditional Red Team review, an external group is tasked with attacking the core team's thesis. This inherently creates an adversarial, defensive dynamic: the original team perceives the Red Team as hostile opponents trying to kill their project, leading to defensive posturing, confirmation bias entrenchment, and information withholding.

In contrast, Gary Klein's pre-mortem enlists the project's own authors and sponsors to imagine the failure. Because the team itself generates the failure mechanisms under the assumption that the collapse has already occurred, critique is reframed as internal diligence and collective protection rather than external attack. In collegial or hierarchical cultures where challenging a senior manager's idea carries career risk, pre-mortems legitimize dissent by making criticism a mandatory procedural duty.

Why do quantitative macro funds establish tripwires on market liquidity metrics (e.g. market depth, bid-ask spread, swap-spread widening) in addition to fundamental economic data? Formulate the transmission mechanism during a liquidity crisis.

Solution

Macro fundamental data (GDP, CPI, employment) is reported with significant time lag (monthly or quarterly) and subject to backward-looking revisions. In contrast, market liquidity metrics (order book depth, 10-year swap spreads, FX basis) reflect real-time financing frictions and dealer balance sheet capacity.

During a market liquidity shock (e.g. March 2020 Treasury market dislocation), primary dealers exhaust risk limits and withdraw market-making capacity. Bid-ask spreads blow out, and margin requirements increase non-linearly. If a macro fund waits for fundamental data to confirm economic distress, the liquidity required to exit large positions evaporates, trapping the fund in catastrophic fire sales (Shleifer & Vishny 1997). Real-time liquidity tripwires trigger de-risking while dealer markets are still functioning.

Develop an institutional policy governing "Tripwire Exceptions." Under what strict governance criteria, if any, may an investment committee grant a temporary waiver to keep a position open after an operational tripwire has been breached?

Solution

To prevent moral hazard and narrative rationalization, an institutional Tripwire Exception Policy must enforce rigorous hurdles:

(1) Absolute Prohibition on Analyst Discretion: The original pitch author cannot unilaterally request or grant an exception.

(2) Unanimous Committee Approval: A waiver requires unanimous approval from the Chief Investment Officer and Chief Risk Officer.

(3) Mandatory Capital De-Risking: Any approved exception automatically mandates an immediate 50% reduction in position size, freeing capital.

(4) Time-Bounded Expiration: The waiver expires strictly in 14 calendar days; if the metric does not recover above the tripwire within this window, liquidation is mandatory with zero further extension permitted.

(5) Real-Time Risk Charge: The position incurs a 2.0x capital charge in fund internal risk budgeting while under waiver.
