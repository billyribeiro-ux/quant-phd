Risk Parity, Vol Targeting & Leverage

Advanced portfolio engineering beyond mean-variance: Euler risk budgeting, convex Equal Risk Contribution (ERC) solvers, the microeconomics of leverage aversion and borrowing constraints, dynamic volatility targeting, the Moreira-Muir volatility-management anomaly, and its rigorous out-of-sample critique under transaction costs.

  • Prerequisites: PR1 Mean-Variance in Practice, AP2 Mean-Variance, CAPM, ICAPM & APT
  • Unlocks: PR3 Factor Investing & Smart Beta, PR4 Quantitative Risk Engine & Extreme Value Theory, STUDIO-PR Multi-Asset Portfolio with Risk Engine
  • Project: Atlas milestone A11 (atlas.portfolio): shrinkage covariance, MV, risk-parity, and vol-target allocators with constraint handling

40 h lessons · 20 h project (33.3%)

Objectives

By the end of PR2 you can:

  1. Derive Euler's homogeneous risk decomposition and compute marginal, component, and percent risk contributions for arbitrary multi-asset portfolios.
  2. Formulate and solve the convex Equal Risk Contribution (ERC) risk parity optimization problem via logarithmic barrier Newton-Raphson methods.
  3. Analyze the theoretical mechanism of leverage aversion and explain why borrowing constraints create the low-beta anomaly and risk parity Sharpe advantage.
  4. Implement time-varying volatility targeting to demonstrate tail-risk compression, excess kurtosis reduction, and maximum drawdown mitigation.
  5. Construct and evaluate Moreira-Muir volatility-managed factor portfolios, estimating spanning regressions, alpha, and mean-variance utility gains.
  6. Critique and test volatility management out-of-sample under expanding-window parameter estimation, realistic execution lags, and turnover transaction costs.

Prerequisites

PR2 builds upon the mean-variance framework and econometric toolkit established in PR1 and AP2. Concepts used without definition include:

  • From PR1: estimation error in mean-variance inputs, optimizer error maximization, the 1/N equal-weight benchmark, covariance shrinkage, and the Black-Litterman model.
  • From AP2: the Capital Asset Pricing Model, the security market line, the tangency portfolio, the minimum-variance portfolio, and the Roll critique.
  • From M7: KKT conditions, convex functions, and quadratic programming.
  • From S1: the Sharpe ratio and Sharpe ratio inference.

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic questions covering prerequisite foundations and two implementation skill checks from project rungs 1 and 2. The placement mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 of 10 correct, including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 2): In a 4-asset Equal Risk Contribution (ERC) portfolio where portfolio volatility is \(\sigma_p = 0.007055\) and total risk contribution is \(\mathrm{TRC}_i = 0.00176375\) for each asset, what is the exact percent risk contribution \(\mathrm{PRC}_i = \mathrm{TRC}_i / \sigma_p\) of asset 1?

    \(\mathrm{PRC}_1 = 0.00176375 / 0.007055 = 0.25\) (exactly 25% of total risk budget).

  2. Skill check (rung 1): Given an unscaled portfolio with volatility \(\sigma_p = 0.007055\) per period, what scaling factor \(\lambda = \sigma_{\text{target}} / \sigma_p\) is required to target an annualized volatility of \(\sigma_{\text{target}} = 0.10\)?

    \(\lambda = 0.10 / 0.007055 \approx 14.1748 \approx 14.17\).

  3. From PR1: In a traditional 60/40 equity/bond portfolio with equity volatility \(\sigma_e = 18\%\), bond volatility \(\sigma_b = 6\%\), and correlation \(\rho = 0.10\), what fraction of total portfolio variance is driven by the equity allocation?

    Options

    Portfolio variance is \(0.6^2(0.18^2) + 0.4^2(0.06^2) + 2(0.6)(0.4)(0.10)(0.18)(0.06) = 0.011664 + 0.000576 + 0.000518 = 0.012758\). Equity variance contribution is \(0.011664 / 0.012758 \approx 91.4\%\).

  4. From AP2: What does the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) predict regarding the relationship between expected return and systematic risk \(\beta\)?

    Options

    Under standard CAPM, \(\E[R_i] - R_f = \beta_i (\E[R_m] - R_f)\). The security market line has an intercept of zero in excess returns.

  5. From M7: In mathematical optimization, what condition guarantees that a local minimizer of an objective function \(f(x)\) over a convex set is the unique global minimizer?

    Options

    Strict convexity of the objective function over a convex feasible domain guarantees that any stationary point is the unique global minimizer.

  6. From PR1: What primary regularization benefit does Ledoit-Wolf linear shrinkage provide over the raw sample covariance matrix in high dimensions?

    Options

    Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage shrinks extreme sample eigenvalues toward a well-conditioned structured target, reducing inversion instability.

  7. From AP2: In the Roll critique, why is empirical testing of the CAPM fundamentally difficult?

    Options

    Roll (1977) proved that testing the CAPM is equivalent to testing whether the true aggregate wealth portfolio is mean-variance efficient, which is unobservable.

  8. From S1: If an asset has an expected annual return of 8% with standard deviation 16% in excess of the risk-free rate, what is its annualized Sharpe ratio?

    \(\SR = 0.08 / 0.16 = 0.50\).

  9. From M4: If a vector of asset returns \(r \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)\), what is the exact distribution of portfolio return \(r_p = w^\top r\)?

    Options

    Any linear combination of multivariate normal random variables is normally distributed with mean \(w^\top \mu\) and variance \(w^\top \Sigma w\).

  10. From PR1: Why does naive sample mean-variance optimization act as an "error maximizer" as demonstrated by Michaud (1989)?

    Options

    The quadratic optimizer takes extreme long positions in assets whose expected returns are overestimated and whose risks are underestimated.

Lesson map

Six lessons totaling 40 study hours:

  1. L01 · Risk Budgeting & Marginal Risk Contributions 7 h

    Euler's homogeneous function theorem, marginal risk contributions (MRC), total component risk contributions (TRC), percent risk contributions (PRC), and the equity-concentration paradox in balanced portfolios.

  2. L02 · Risk Parity & Equal Risk Contribution 7 h

    Equal Risk Contribution (ERC) portfolios, existence and uniqueness via convex log-barrier formulation, Newton-Raphson numerical solvers, scale invariance, and comparison against 1/N and Markowitz frontiers.

  3. L03 · Leverage Aversion, Borrowing Constraints & The Beta Anomaly 7 h

    Microeconomic foundations of leverage aversion (Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen 2012), borrowing constraints, the Betting-Against-Beta anomaly, and the leveraged risk parity Sharpe ratio advantage over equities.

  4. L04 · Volatility Targeting & Time-Varying Exposure 7 h

    Mechanics of dynamic inverse-volatility scaling (Harvey et al. 2018), tail-risk compression, excess kurtosis reduction, volatility drag mitigation, and look-ahead-free implementation lags.

  5. L05 · Volatility-Managed Portfolios & Moreira-Muir Alpha 6 h

    Moreira & Muir (2017) volatility-managed factor portfolios: empirical spanning regressions, alpha generation, variance timing, and economic utility gains across equity factors.

  6. L06 · The Volatility Management Critique & Out-of-Sample Performance 6 h

    The Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020) critique: look-ahead bias in in-sample scaling constants, out-of-sample expanding-window decay, transaction costs on turnover, and the literature debate brief.
