Risk Measurement

Foundations of quantitative market risk measurement: axiomatic coherence, Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall, formal backtesting frameworks (Kupiec POF and Christoffersen conditional coverage), Peaks-Over-Threshold extreme value theory, non-linear copula dependence, macro scenario stress testing, and Basel Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) capital standards.

  • Prerequisites: PR1 Mean-Variance in Practice, S4 Volatility Modeling & ARCH/GARCH
  • Introduces: value-at-risk, expected-shortfall, coherent-risk-measure, kupiec-test, christoffersen-test, extreme-value-theory, copula, tail-dependence, stress-testing, frtb
  • Project: Atlas milestone A12 (atlas.risk): quantitative risk engine implementing historical, parametric, and EVT VaR/ES, Kupiec/Christoffersen backtesting, and stress testing

70 h lessons · 30 h project (30.0%)

Objectives

By the end of PR4 you can:

  1. Formulate the mathematical definitions of Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall and prove the failure of subadditivity for VaR on discrete and asymmetric payoff profiles.
  2. Prove the axiomatic coherence of Expected Shortfall under the four Artzner, Delbaen, Eber & Heath (1999) axioms using dual representations and convex variational principles.
  3. Implement statistical backtesting engines using the Kupiec (1995) Proportion of Failures likelihood ratio test and map model exception counts to Basel regulatory traffic light zones.
  4. Evaluate time-series violation clustering and conditional coverage using the Christoffersen (1998) first-order Markov chain likelihood ratio framework.
  5. Fit Generalized Pareto Distributions to tail exceedances using the Pickands-Balkema-de Haan Peaks-Over-Threshold method and extrapolate extreme out-of-sample quantiles.
  6. Construct and simulate non-linear multivariate distributions via Sklar's theorem, demonstrating why Gaussian copulas underestimate joint tail risk relative to Student-t copulas.
  7. Execute multidimensional historical scenario replay and reverse stress testing to identify minimum insolvency thresholds and quantify model risk capital buffers.

Prerequisites

PR4 requires mastery of portfolio construction and econometric time-series modeling established in PR1 and S4. Concepts usable without definition include:

  • From PR1: covariance shrinkage, estimation error, and the 1/N benchmark.
  • From S4: volatility clustering, ARCH models, GARCH(1,1) processes, and realized volatility.
  • From AP2: the Capital Asset Pricing Model and the minimum-variance portfolio.
  • From S1 and S2: the Sharpe ratio, maximum likelihood estimation, and likelihood ratio tests.
  • From M4 and M7: quantile functions, random variables, convex functions, and KKT optimality conditions.

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic questions testing prerequisite foundations and core implementation skills from project rungs 1 and 2. Mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 correct, including both marked skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 1): Given \(T = 250\) trading days and a 99% 1-day VaR model (\(p = 0.01\)), if exactly \(x = 2\) exceptions are observed, what is the Kupiec Proportion of Failures likelihood ratio test statistic \(LR_{\text{POF}} = -2 \ln(L(p) / L(\hat{p}))\) to four decimal places?

    With \(\hat{p} = 2/250 = 0.008\) and \(p = 0.01\), \(LR_{\text{POF}} = -2 [248 \ln(0.99) + 2 \ln(0.01) - 248 \ln(0.992) - 2 \ln(0.008)] \approx 0.1084\).

  2. Skill check (rung 2): In a 250-day exception sequence with transition counts \(n_{00} = 245\), \(n_{01} = 1\), \(n_{10} = 1\), \(n_{11} = 2\), what is the Christoffersen independence test statistic \(LR_{\text{ind}}\) to two decimal places?

    With \(\pi_{01} = 1/246 \approx 0.004065\), \(\pi_{11} = 2/3 \approx 0.66667\), and \(\pi = 3/249 \approx 0.012048\), \(LR_{\text{ind}} = -2 (\ln L_0 - \ln L_1) \approx 15.6511 \approx 15.65\).

  3. From S4: What empirical feature of asset returns makes constant-volatility risk models systematically understate tail risk during financial market turmoil?

    Options

    Conditional heteroskedasticity concentrates large negative returns into consecutive periods, violating independence and dramatically swelling conditional tail risk.

  4. From PR1: Why does sample covariance estimation perform poorly in high dimensions without shrinkage regularization?

    Options

    Marchenko-Pastur dispersion causes sample eigenvalues to spread, creating artificial zero or near-zero eigenvalues that destabilize portfolio risk minimization.

  5. From S2: Under standard regularity conditions, what is the asymptotic distribution of a likelihood ratio test statistic comparing a nested model of dimension \(k_0\) to an unrestricted model of dimension \(k_1\)?

    Options

    By Wilks' theorem, \(-2 \ln(L_0 / L_1) \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2(k_1 - k_0)\) under the null hypothesis as sample size \(T \to \infty\).

  6. From M4: What is the 99th percentile quantile \(\Phi^{-1}(0.99)\) of the standard normal distribution to three decimal places?

    \(\Phi^{-1}(0.99) \approx 2.32635 \approx 2.326\).

  7. From M7: What mathematical property guarantees that an inequality-constrained optimization problem possesses no spurious local minima?

    Options

    In convex programming, any local optimum on a convex feasible domain is necessarily a global optimum.

  8. From S1: If an asset return has kurtosis equal to 6.0, what does this indicate relative to a Gaussian distribution?

    Options

    Standard normal kurtosis is 3.0. A kurtosis of 6.0 represents excess kurtosis of +3.0, indicating heavy tails and heightened probability of extreme moves.

  9. From PR1: A portfolio holds $1,000,000 in equity index futures and $1,000,000 in corporate bonds. If equity prices drop by 15% and corporate bonds fall by 5%, what is total portfolio dollar P&L?

    \(\Delta V = 1{,}000{,}000(-0.15) + 1{,}000{,}000(-0.05) = -150{,}000 - 50{,}000 = -200{,}000\). Wait: if equity drops 10% and bond drops 2%: -100k - 20k = -120k. For inputs -10% equity and -2% bond: \(\Delta V = -\$120{,}000\).

  10. From S4: In a GARCH(1,1) model \(\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha \epsilon_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2\), what condition is required for covariance stationarity?

    Options

    The persistence sum \(\alpha + \beta < 1\) is the exact condition required for finite unconditional variance \(\sigma^2 = \omega / (1 - \alpha - \beta)\).

Lesson map

PR4 comprises 10 lessons totaling 70 study hours (7 hours each), followed by the 30-hour Atlas A12 risk engine project:

  1. L01 · Value-at-Risk Foundations & Non-Subadditivity

    Loss quantiles, parametric Gaussian vs historical simulation, digital option portfolios, and the mathematical failure of subadditivity. Introduces value-at-risk. 7 hours.

  2. L02 · Expected Shortfall & Axiomatic Risk Coherence

    The Artzner et al. (1999) axioms, Expected Shortfall derivation, subadditivity proofs, and Rockafellar-Uryasev convex optimization. Introduces expected-shortfall and coherent-risk-measure. 7 hours.

  3. L03 · Backtesting Risk Measures: Kupiec POF Test

    Verification of risk models, binomial exceptions, Kupiec (1995) likelihood ratio statistics, finite sample power, and Basel traffic light zones. Introduces kupiec-test. 7 hours.

  4. L04 · Independence & Conditional Coverage: Christoffersen Framework

    The clustering flaw in unconditional tests, Markov chain exception dynamics, Christoffersen (1998) independence and conditional coverage tests. Introduces christoffersen-test. 7 hours.

  5. L05 · Extreme Value Theory: Peaks Over Threshold & GPD

    Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem, Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD), McNeil & Frey (2000) two-stage GARCH-EVT estimation, and extreme tail quantiles. Introduces extreme-value-theory. 7 hours.

  6. L06 · Copula Models & Sklar's Theorem

    Pitfalls of linear correlation, Sklar's theorem, uniform probability transforms, Gaussian and Student-t copulas, and multivariate simulation. Introduces copula. 7 hours.

  7. L07 · Tail Dependence & Asymmetric Co-Movements

    Upper and lower tail dependence coefficients, Gaussian asymptotic independence versus Student-t positive tail dependence in extreme crashes. Introduces tail-dependence. 7 hours.

  8. L08 · Macro & Historical Scenario Stress Testing

    Historical crisis replays (1987, 1998, 2008, 2020), multi-factor macro stress shocks, and reverse stress testing algorithms. Introduces stress-testing. 7 hours.

  9. L09 · Regulatory Capital & FRTB Expected Shortfall Standards

    Basel Committee market risk evolution, FRTB 97.5% Expected Shortfall across liquidity horizons, and P&L attribution PLAT testing. Introduces frtb. 7 hours.

  10. L10 · Comprehensive Risk Engine Architecture & Model Risk

    Unified enterprise risk engine architecture, Federal Reserve SR 11-7 / SR 26-2 model risk governance, model inventory, and capital reserves. 7 hours.

Project ladder

The 30-hour capstone project delivers milestone A12 of the Atlas library (atlas.risk), organized as a four-rung ladder:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: A complete risk measurement calculation pipeline computing Historical, Gaussian, Student-t, and EVT VaR/ES, Kupiec POF backtests, and macro stress tests.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Implement the missing Christoffersen Markov transition counts, maximum likelihood transition matrix, and independence likelihood ratio statistic.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project (Atlas A12): Build the complete atlas.risk module meeting all functional specifications and passing the 50-seed autograder suite.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch extension: Implement rolling-window dynamic EVT-GPD tail estimation with conditional heteroskedasticity filtering and adaptive backtest monitoring.
