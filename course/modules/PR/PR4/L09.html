The 2008 financial crisis exposed catastrophic flaws in the Basel II market risk framework: banks held minimal capital against credit products, assumed 10-day liquidation for illiquid derivatives, and relied on Value-at-Risk, which failed to measure tail crash severity. In response, the Basel Committee enacted the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB), overhauling global banking capital requirements with coherent 97.5% Expected Shortfall, tiered liquidity horizons, and rigorous desk-level P&L attribution tests.

1The FRTB Architectural Overhaul

The Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) establishes a comprehensive prudential standard governing bank trading activities (BCBS, 2014, 2019).

Definition 1.1 (FRTB Core Regulatory Pillars)

The FRTB introduces three structural shifts in regulatory capital calculation:

  1. Metric Transition: Replacing 99% 10-day Value-at-Risk with 97.5% Expected Shortfall calibrated to a stressed historical period (\(\mathrm{ES}_S\)).
  2. Varying Liquidity Horizons: Replacing the uniform 10-day holding period with risk factor-dependent liquidity horizons ranging from 10 to 120 days.
  3. Granular Desk-Level Approval: Requiring trading desks to independently qualify for the Internal Models Approach (IMA) through daily P&L Attribution Testing (PLAT) and backtesting.
Remark 1.1 (Calibration Equivalence under Normality)

Under a Gaussian distribution, the 97.5% Expected Shortfall equals \(\mu + \sigma \frac{\phi(\Phi^{-1}(0.975))}{0.025} \approx \mu + 2.3378\sigma\), virtually identical to the 99% Value-at-Risk quantile \(\mu + 2.3263\sigma\). However, under heavy-tailed fat tails (\(\xi > 0\)), 97.5% Expected Shortfall is substantially higher than 99% VaR, removing the regulatory incentive to load up on tail risk as demonstrated by McNeil et al. (2015).

2Scaled Liquidity Horizons and Aggregation

Under FRTB, risk factors are assigned to one of five liquidity horizons based on market depth and time required to execute an orderly liquidation without moving market prices:

  • \(LH_1 = 10\) days: Highly liquid large-cap equities, major currency pairs, sovereign benchmark interest rates.
  • \(LH_2 = 20\) days: Small-cap equities, investment-grade corporate bonds, cross-currency rates.
  • \(LH_3 = 40\) days: High-yield corporate credit, equity volatility smiles, energy commodities.
  • \(LH_4 = 60\) days: Exotic interest rate options, structured credit derivatives.
  • \(LH_5 = 120\) days: Illiquid credit tranches, bespoke exotic underlyings.
Theorem 2.1 (FRTB Liquidity Horizon Aggregation Formula)

Let \(\mathrm{ES}(P, Q_j)\) denote the 10-day Expected Shortfall evaluated by shocking only the subset of risk factors with liquidity horizon \(LH \ge LH_j\), with all more liquid factors frozen. The aggregated multi-horizon Expected Shortfall is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{ES} = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^5 \mathrm{ES}(P, Q_j)^2 \frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10}} \end{aligned} \]

where \(LH_0 = 0\) and \(LH_j \in \{10, 20, 40, 60, 120\}\).

Proof

Consider an orderly liquidation where position risk is held over horizon \(LH_j\). The variance contribution of shocks operating across the incremental duration \(\Delta LH_j = LH_j - LH_{j-1}\) scales linearly with time under random-walk diffusion: \(\sigma^2(\Delta LH_j) = \sigma^2(10) \frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10}\). Because Expected Shortfall scales linearly with standard deviation for elliptical distributions, the incremental squared ES contribution is \(\mathrm{ES}(P, Q_j)^2 \frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10}\). Summing orthogonal variance increments across all five horizon tiers and taking the square root yields the formula per BCBS MAR33 standards.

3Desk-Level P&L Attribution Testing (PLAT)

To prevent institutions from utilizing internal risk models that omit crucial pricing factors, FRTB mandates the daily P&L Attribution Test across 250 historical trading days.

Definition 3.1 (PLAT Metrics)

The test evaluates two parallel daily P&L series generated by the bank:

  • Hypothetical P&L (HPL): Produced by front-office pricing engines holding yesterday's portfolio constant against today's market moves.
  • Risk-Theoretical P&L (RTPL): Produced by the risk engine using the risk factors and pricing approximations embedded in the internal model.

PLAT evaluates two statistical metrics:

\[ \begin{aligned} r_S &= \text{Spearman Rank Correlation}(HPL, RTPL) \\ VR &= \frac{\Var(RTPL - HPL)}{\Var(HPL)} \quad \text{(Unexplained Variance Ratio)} \end{aligned} \]
Example 3.1 (PLAT Traffic Light Zones)

BCBS standards define three supervisory zones based on PLAT results:

  • Green Zone: \(r_S \ge 0.80\) and \(VR \le 0.20\). Full approval for the Internal Models Approach.
  • Amber Zone: \(r_S \in [0.70, 0.80)\) or \(VR \in (0.20, 0.40]\). IMA permitted but subject to a capital surcharge.
  • Red Zone: \(r_S < 0.70\) or \(VR > 0.40\). Automatic revocation of IMA status; the desk falls back to the Standardized Approach (SA).

Lab 9 · FRTB Liquidity Horizons and P&L Attribution Testing

Calculating the multi-horizon Expected Shortfall across five tiers (10 to 120 days) will demonstrate liquidity horizon scaling, and running the dual PLAT test across 250 simulated days will confirm Green Zone compliance (Spearman correlation >= 0.80, variance ratio <= 0.20).

"""Lab 9: FRTB Liquidity Horizons and Basel P&L Attribution Testing (PLAT).

Demonstrates the BCBS Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) standards:
scaled liquidity horizons and the dual PLAT test (Spearman rank correlation and variance ratio).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

import numpy as np
import scipy.stats


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(42)

    # 1. FRTB Liquidity Horizon Scaled Expected Shortfall Calculation
    # Liquidity horizons: LH1 = 10, LH2 = 20, LH3 = 40, LH4 = 60, LH5 = 120 days
    # Sub-portfolio ES estimates at base 10-day level for progressive factor subsets:
    es_subsets = [150_000.0, 190_000.0, 240_000.0, 280_000.0, 310_000.0]
    horizons = [10, 20, 40, 60, 120]

    # FRTB Aggregation Formula:
    # ES = sqrt( sum_{j=1}^5 (ES_j)^2 * (LH_j - LH_{j-1}) / 10 ) where LH_0 = 0
    es_sq_sum = 0.0
    lh_prev = 0
    for es_j, lh_j in zip(es_subsets, horizons, strict=True):
        weight = (lh_j - lh_prev) / 10.0
        es_sq_sum += (es_j**2) * weight
        lh_prev = lh_j

    es_frtb_total = math.sqrt(es_sq_sum)

    print("FRTB Liquidity Horizon Expected Shortfall Aggregation:")
    for j, (lh, es_val) in enumerate(zip(horizons, es_subsets, strict=True), start=1):
        print(f"  Level {j} (LH = {lh:3d} days): ES(T_{j}) = ${es_val:,.2f}")
    print(f"FRTB Total Scaled Market Risk Capital ES: ${es_frtb_total:,.2f}")
    print("=" * 70)

    # 2. Basel P&L Attribution Test (PLAT)
    # Compare 250 daily observations of Hypothetical P&L (HPL) vs Risk-Theoretical P&L (RTPL)
    t_days = 250
    hpl = rng.normal(loc=15_000.0, scale=120_000.0, size=t_days)
    # Model tracking noise (representing un-modellable basis risks and simplifications)
    tracking_noise = rng.normal(loc=0.0, scale=18_000.0, size=t_days)
    rtpl = hpl + tracking_noise

    # Metric 1: Spearman rank correlation (Green zone: >= 0.80)
    spearman_corr, _ = scipy.stats.spearmanr(hpl, rtpl)
    # Metric 2: Variance ratio Var(RTPL - HPL) / Var(HPL) (Green zone: <= 0.20)
    diff_var = float(np.var(rtpl - hpl, ddof=1))
    hpl_var = float(np.var(hpl, ddof=1))
    var_ratio = diff_var / hpl_var

    is_green = bool(spearman_corr >= 0.80 and var_ratio <= 0.20)

    print("Basel III P&L Attribution Test (PLAT) Results (T = 250 trading days):")
    print(f"  Spearman Rank Correlation r_S: {float(spearman_corr):.4f} (Threshold >= 0.80)")
    print(f"  Unexplained Variance Ratio VR:  {var_ratio:.4f} (Threshold <= 0.20)")
    print(f"  Trading Desk Assessment:        {'GREEN ZONE (IMA Approved)' if is_green else 'AMBER/RED ZONE'}")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

The operational complexity and regulatory burden of FRTB has created severe unintended market consequences. The most prominent breakdown is the Non-Modellable Risk Factor (NMRF) framework stipulated by BCBS. To qualify as a modellable risk factor, a price series must demonstrate continuous market liquidity: at least 24 real, observable verifiable price transactions per year, with at least 4 in each rolling 90-day window (the Risk Factor Eligibility Test). In illiquid markets (e.g. European municipal debt, exotic swaptions), factors fail RFET and are classified as NMRFs. Each NMRF must hold standalone stressed capital with zero cross-factor diversification benefits, often multiplying capital requirements by 300% to 500% and driving banks to shutter market-making desks entirely.

5Misconceptions

  1. “97.5% Expected Shortfall requires less capital than 99% Value-at-Risk because 97.5% is a lower confidence level.”

    Under a Gaussian distribution, \(ES_{0.975}\) and \(VaR_{0.99}\) are numerically identical (\(\approx 2.33\sigma\)). However, because financial returns have heavy power-law tails, Expected Shortfall integrates the entire severity beyond the quantile, producing substantially higher capital charges than VaR.

  2. “FRTB evaluates internal model approval at the bank-wide enterprise level.”

    FRTB mandates desk-level granularity. Each individual trading desk must separately pass backtesting and daily P&L Attribution Testing. If a single desk fails PLAT into the Red Zone, that desk immediately loses IMA approval and must use the punitive Standardized Approach, even if the enterprise as a whole is well-hedged.

  3. “Liquidity horizon scaling simply applies a square-root-of-time multiplier \(\sqrt{LH/10}\) to the total portfolio.”

    FRTB rejects naive portfolio-level time scaling. It requires evaluating sub-portfolios \(Q_j\) where liquid factors are held constant and only genuinely illiquid factors are shocked over longer horizons, preventing illiquidity surcharges on liquid hedges.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · FRTB Two-Tier Liquidity Horizon ES

A portfolio has two risk factor tiers: \(LH_1 = 10\) days with \(\mathrm{ES}_1 = \$200,000\), and \(LH_2 = 20\) days with \(\mathrm{ES}_2 = \$300,000\). Compute the aggregated FRTB Expected Shortfall.

Solution

Applying the FRTB aggregation formula: \(\mathrm{ES} = \sqrt{\mathrm{ES}_1^2 \frac{10 - 0}{10} + \mathrm{ES}_2^2 \frac{20 - 10}{10}} = \sqrt{(200,000)^2 (1.0) + (300,000)^2 (1.0)} = \sqrt{4.0 \times 10^{10} + 9.0 \times 10^{10}} = \sqrt{1.3 \times 10^{11}} \approx \$360,555.13\). The aggregated capital is $360,555.

M2 · PLAT Variance Ratio Calculation

Over \(T = 250\) trading days, a trading desk records \(\Var(HPL) = 1,000,000\) and \(\Var(RTPL - HPL) = 150,000\). Calculate the variance ratio \(VR\) and state whether it meets the Green Zone threshold (\(VR \le 0.20\)).

Solution

The variance ratio is \(VR = \frac{\Var(RTPL - HPL)}{\Var(HPL)} = \frac{150,000}{1,000,000} = 0.1500\). Since \(0.1500 \le 0.20\), the variance ratio passes the PLAT requirement and sits comfortably in the Green Zone.

M3 · Gaussian Equivalence of 97.5% ES and 99% VaR

For a standard normal loss \(L \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), verify that \(\mathrm{ES}_{0.975} \approx \mathrm{VaR}_{0.99}\) using standard normal constants \(\Phi^{-1}(0.975) = 1.95996\) and \(\Phi^{-1}(0.99) = 2.32635\).

Solution

For \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), \(\mathrm{VaR}_{0.99} = 2.32635\). For Expected Shortfall: \(\mathrm{ES}_{0.975} = \frac{\phi(1.95996)}{1 - 0.975}\). The PDF is \(\phi(1.95996) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-0.5(1.95996)^2} \approx 0.39894 \times 0.14649 \approx 0.05844\). Dividing gives \(\mathrm{ES}_{0.975} = \frac{0.05844}{0.025} \approx 2.3376\). The difference is only \(|2.3376 - 2.3263| = 0.0113\) (less than 0.5%).

Proof

P1 · Derivation of Liquidity Horizon Scaling Weights

Prove that under Brownian motion dynamics, the variance of an incremental shock over time interval \([LH_{j-1}, LH_j]\) equals \(\frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10} \sigma_{10}^2\).

Solution

Under a standard Brownian diffusion \(dW_t\), increments are independent with variance \(\Var(W_{t + \Delta t} - W_t) = \Delta t\). Over a 10-day base interval, \(\sigma_{10}^2 = 10 \sigma_1^2\). For an incremental horizon \(\Delta LH = LH_j - LH_{j-1}\), the variance is \(\Delta LH \sigma_1^2 = \Delta LH \frac{\sigma_{10}^2}{10} = \frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10} \sigma_{10}^2\). Taking the square root gives standard deviation scaling of \(\sqrt{\frac{LH_j - LH_{j-1}}{10}}\).

P2 · Non-Subadditivity Proof under Regulatory Truncation

Prove that capping a coherent risk measure by a statutory maximum threshold destroys its subadditivity property.

Solution

Let \(\rho(X)\) be coherent and define \(\rho_{\text{cap}}(X) = \min(\rho(X), M)\). Consider independent portfolios with \(\rho(A) = 0.8M\) and \(\rho(B) = 0.8M\). Then \(\rho_{\text{cap}}(A) = 0.8M\) and \(\rho_{\text{cap}}(B) = 0.8M\), giving sum \(1.6M\). For the joint portfolio, if \(\rho(A + B) = 1.5M\), then \(\rho_{\text{cap}}(A + B) = \min(1.5M, M) = M \le 1.6M\). But if we evaluate a floor: \(\rho_{\text{floor}}(X) = \max(\rho(X), m)\), then \(\rho(A) = 0.4m\) gives \(\rho_{\text{floor}}(A) = m\), \(\rho_{\text{floor}}(B) = m\), while \(\rho(A+B) = 0.8m \implies \rho_{\text{floor}}(A+B) = m \le 2m\). In contrast, scaling thresholds nonlinearly violates positive homogeneity: \(\rho(c X) \ne c \rho(X)\).

P3 · Elicitability of Expected Shortfall and the Fissler-Ziegel Theorem

Explain why Expected Shortfall is not elicitible on its own, but is jointly elicitible with Value-at-Risk under the Fissler and Ziegel (2016) scoring function.

Solution

A risk measure is elicitible if it is the unique minimizer of an expected scoring function \(\E[S(x, L)]\). Expected Shortfall alone lacks this property because it depends on the quantile threshold. Fissler and Ziegel (2016) proved that the pair \((v, e) = (\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha, \mathrm{ES}_\alpha)\) is jointly elicitible under the strictly consistent scoring function \(S(v, e, y) = (1_{\{y > v\}} - \alpha) g_1(v) - 1_{\{y > v\}} g_1(y) + a(e) (e - v) + \dots\), establishing the theoretical foundation for comparative backtesting.

Empirical

E1 · Transition from IMA to Standardized Approach

A global bank's equity exotics trading desk fails PLAT into the Red Zone (\(VR = 0.46\)). Contrast the capital impact of falling back to the Standardized Approach versus retaining IMA.

Solution

Under IMA, internal Expected Shortfall accounts for proprietary cross-asset hedging and portfolio diversification. Under the Standardized Approach (SA), the Sensitivities-Based Method prescribes rigid regulatory risk weights (e.g. 50% to 70% on equity volatility) and imposes conservative regulatory correlation parameters. Desk capital requirements typically surge by 2.5x to 4.0x, eroding return on equity and forcing position unwinds.

E2 · Risk Factor Eligibility Test (RFET) Operational Challenges

Why do commercial banks struggle to pass the RFET for corporate credit spread curves, and what market infrastructure has emerged to address this?

Solution

Corporate bonds trade over-the-counter with low secondary trading frequency, often failing the 24 verifiable price quotes per year requirement. To avoid punitive Non-Modellable Risk Factor capital surcharges, banks formed industry utility consortia (such as IHS Markit/S&P Global RFET pools) that aggregate anonymized trade and committed quote data across all member banks to establish regulatory quote sufficiency.

E3 · Calibration to Stressed Period (ES_S)

Describe how banks identify the historical 12-month stress period for FRTB calibration, and explain why this period differs across trading desks.

Solution

Banks must search historical data dating back to at least 2007 to identify the continuous 250-day window that maximizes the portfolio's Expected Shortfall. For interest rate and sovereign credit desks, the 2011–2012 European debt crisis often maximizes losses; for equity and credit derivatives, the 2008 Lehman crisis dominates; for energy desks, the 2020 crude oil collapse dominates.

Research

R1 · Procyclicality of Stressed Capital under FRTB

Analyze whether the FRTB requirement to scale current Expected Shortfall by a historical stress ratio \(ES_S / ES_C\) succeeds in dampening capital procyclicality.

Solution

The FRTB capital formula scales current ES by the stress multiplier \(ES_S / ES_C\). During tranquil market periods, current risk \(ES_C\) is low, causing the ratio to rise and forcing banks to hold high capital reserves. When a crisis strikes, \(ES_C\) rises toward \(ES_S\), stabilizing the capital requirement rather than causing an exponential surge. This dampens procyclicality compared to unadjusted Basel II VaR.

R2 · Machine Learning Approximations in RTPL Calculation

How can banks deploy neural network surrogate pricer models to accelerate Risk-Theoretical P&L computation without failing PLAT?

Solution

Full-revaluation Monte Carlo pricing of complex derivatives over 250 historical scenarios is computationally intractable within daily risk batch windows. Deep neural networks trained on front-office pricing engines act as ultra-fast surrogate pricers. To ensure PLAT compliance, the surrogate pricer must maintain an \(L_\infty\) pricing error below 0.5 bps of notional, ensuring that unexplained variance \(VR\) remains strictly within the 0.20 Green Zone threshold.

R3 · Capital Floor and Bank Business Model Impacts

Evaluate the impact of the Basel III 72.5% output floor on banks with highly sophisticated Internal Models Approaches.

Solution

The output floor mandates that total risk-weighted assets (RWA) calculated via internal models cannot fall below 72.5% of the RWA calculated under standardized approaches. For banks with advanced quantitative hedging and machine learning risk engines that previously achieved 50% capital reductions, the output floor binds, eliminating the economic return on investment for maintaining complex IMA infrastructure.

8Quiz

  1. What risk measure replaced 99% Value-at-Risk under the FRTB framework?

    Options

    FRTB officially replaced 99% 10-day VaR with 97.5% Expected Shortfall calibrated over a continuous 12-month period of significant financial stress.

  2. What is the range of liquidity horizons \(LH\) defined under FRTB?

    Options

    FRTB categorizes risk factors into five tiers of liquidity horizons: 10, 20, 40, 60, and 120 days based on market depth and execution time.

  3. What two statistical criteria must a trading desk satisfy to remain in the PLAT Green Zone?

    Options

    The PLAT Green Zone requires Spearman rank correlation between HPL and RTPL of at least 0.80 and unexplained variance ratio of at most 0.20.

  4. What consequence occurs if a trading desk falls into the PLAT Red Zone?

    Options

    A Red Zone PLAT result immediately revokes the trading desk's IMA approval, forcing it onto the punitive Standardized Approach.

  5. What minimum observable market quote threshold is required under the Risk Factor Eligibility Test (RFET)?

    Options

    RFET mandates at least 24 verifiable price quotes per year with no 90-day period having fewer than 4 quotes; otherwise, the factor is deemed non-modellable (NMRF).

  6. How are Non-Modellable Risk Factors (NMRFs) capitalized under FRTB?

    Options

    NMRFs must be capitalized using standalone stress scenarios with no portfolio diversification offsets, creating heavy capital penalties.

  7. What is the difference between Hypothetical P&L (HPL) and Risk-Theoretical P&L (RTPL)?

    Options

    HPL reflects front-office valuation keeping yesterday's portfolio constant, whereas RTPL reflects valuation from the risk engine's models and approximations.

  8. Under Gaussian innovations, how does 97.5% Expected Shortfall compare to 99% Value-at-Risk?

    Options

    For a standard normal distribution, \(\mathrm{ES}_{0.975} \approx 2.3378\) and \(\mathrm{VaR}_{0.99} \approx 2.3263\), exhibiting near-perfect calibration equivalence.
