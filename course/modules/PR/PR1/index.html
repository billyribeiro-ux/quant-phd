Mean-Variance in Practice

Bridging Markowitz theory and investment reality: the statistical mechanics of parameter estimation error, optimizer error maximization, the naive 1/N puzzle, linear and nonlinear covariance shrinkage, the Black-Litterman Bayesian synthesis, robust optimization under parameter uncertainty, and out-of-sample execution under realistic trading frictions.

  • Prerequisites: AP2 Mean-Variance, CAPM, ICAPM & APT, S2 Multiple Regression & Causal Inference
  • Unlocks: PR2 Risk Parity, Vol Targeting & Leverage, PR3 Factor Investing & Smart Beta
  • Project: Allocator shoot-out: automated Python allocator suite (allocators.py) and two-page executive evaluation memo (report.md)

50 h lessons · 20 h project (28.6%)

Objectives

By the end of PR1 you can:

  1. Derive the analytical sensitivity of unconstrained and long-only mean-variance portfolio weights to perturbations in expected returns and covariances.
  2. Demonstrate optimizer error maximization under resampling and explain why naive sample optimization behaves as an error amplifier in finite samples.
  3. Prove the necessary and sufficient mathematical conditions under which 1/N equal weighting coincides with mean-variance and minimum-variance efficiency.
  4. Formulate and prove eigenvalue regularization for linear covariance shrinkage toward structured targets, bounding the condition number and smallest eigenvalue.
  5. Derive the Black-Litterman posterior mean in both precision form and Woodbury generalized least squares form from a neutral CAPM equilibrium prior.
  6. Formulate robust portfolio optimization over rectangular and ellipsoidal uncertainty sets, proving long-only equivalence to conservative return penalties.
  7. Implement and evaluate a leak-free out-of-sample rolling backtest engine incorporating one-way turnover and proportional transaction cost accounting.

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic questions covering prerequisite foundations and two implementation skill checks from project rungs 1 and 2. The placement mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 of 10 correct, including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 2): When reallocating an investment portfolio from an equal-weight allocation \(w_0 = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)\) to target weights \(w_1 = (0.2985, 0.3819, 0.3197)\), what is the resulting one-way turnover \(\sum_{i=1}^3 |w_{1,i} - w_{0,i}|\)?

    \(\tau = |0.2985 - 0.3333| + |0.3819 - 0.3333| + |0.3197 - 0.3333| = 0.0348 + 0.0486 + 0.0136 = 0.09705\) (or exactly 398/4101).

  2. Skill check (rung 1): For the 3-asset window in the worked example with \(w_{\text{GMV}} = (408/1367, 522/1367, 437/1367)\), what is the exact weight \(w_1\) allocated to Asset 1 (expressed to 4 decimal places)?

    \(408 / 1367 \approx 0.29846 \approx 0.2985\).

  3. From AP2: In the absence of a risk-free asset, what geometric curve is traced out by the minimum-variance portfolio frontier in expected return–standard deviation \((\sigma_p, \mu_p)\) space?

    Options

    In standard deviation–mean space, the frontier is the right branch of a hyperbola \(\sigma_p^2 = 1/A + (A/D)(\mu_p - B/A)^2\).

  4. From M7: In quadratic programming with linear inequality constraints \(w \ge 0\) and budget \(\iota^\top w = 1\), what condition must Lagrange multipliers \(\lambda_i\) of binding lower bounds satisfy at optimality?

    Options

    By the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions for convex minimization with constraints \(w_i \ge 0\), the multipliers must be non-negative (\(\lambda_i \ge 0\)) and satisfy complementary slackness (\(\lambda_i w_i = 0\)).

  5. From M4: If \(S\) is an unbiased sample covariance matrix calculated from \(T\) independent observations of \(N\) assets, what divisor is used in the denominator?

    Options

    Bessel's correction divides by \(T - 1\) to provide an unbiased estimator of population covariance \(\E[S] = \Sigma\).

  6. From M3: When the sample size \(T\) is strictly less than the number of assets \(N\) (\(T < N\)), what is the rank of the sample covariance matrix \(S\)?

    Options

    \(S\) is formed from the outer products of \(T\) demeaned vectors, which span a subspace of dimension at most \(T - 1\). When \(T < N\), \(S\) is rank-deficient and singular.

  7. From S1: Under i.i.d. normally distributed returns, what is the asymptotic standard error of the sample Sharpe ratio \(\widehat{\SR}\) over \(K\) periods?

    Options

    By the delta method (Lo, 2002; S1), the asymptotic variance of the sample Sharpe ratio under i.i.d. normal returns is \((1 + \frac{1}{2}\SR^2)/K\).

  8. From AP2: In a two-asset universe with equal volatilities \(\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = 0.20\) and correlation \(\rho = 0\), what is the weight \(w_1\) of the global minimum-variance portfolio?

    By symmetry, minimizing \(\sigma_1^2 w_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 (1 - w_1)^2\) with \(\sigma_1 = \sigma_2\) yields \(w_1 = 0.5\).

  9. From S2: What occurs when an econometrician includes future realized return data \(r_t\) when estimating portfolio weights for period \(t\)?

    Options

    Look-ahead bias conditions decisions on information unavailable at execution time, invalidating the historical simulation.

  10. From CS1: In an investment universe of \(N = 4\) assets, what weight \(w_i\) does the naive 1/N allocation assign to each asset?

    \(w_i = 1 / N = 1 / 4 = 0.25\).

Lesson map

The 50 study hours of lesson material span eight sequenced units:

  1. L01 · Mean-Variance Inputs and the Estimation Error

    Analytical sensitivity of mean-variance weights to expected returns and covariances; derivation of input error magnification.

    6 study hours · Introduces mean-variance-estimation-error

  2. L02 · Optimizer Error Maximization

    The statistical mechanics of error maximization; Michaud resampling heuristic and resampling instability lab.

    6 study hours · Introduces error-maximization

  3. L03 · The 1/N Benchmark

    The equal-weight portfolio benchmark; analytic optimality theorem and DeMiguel et al. out-of-sample critique.

    7 study hours · Introduces equal-weight-portfolio

  4. L04 · Covariance Shrinkage

    Singularity when \(T < N\); linear shrinkage toward scaled identity; Ledoit-Wolf large-dimensional asymptotic regularization.

    6 study hours · Introduces covariance-shrinkage

  5. L05 · Black–Litterman Model

    Reverse optimization and CAPM equilibrium prior; Bayesian Gaussian conjugate updating in precision and Woodbury forms.

    7 study hours · Introduces black-litterman-model

  6. L06 · Robust Portfolio Optimization

    Worst-case optimization over rectangular box uncertainty sets; derivation of conservative return penalties in long-only QPs.

    6 study hours · Introduces robust-portfolio-optimization

  7. L07 · Turnover, Costs and Out-of-Sample Evaluation

    Strict rolling-window backtest protocol; one-way turnover accounting, proportional fee drag, and look-ahead diagnostics.

    6 study hours · Evaluation protocol

  8. L08 · Ranking the Allocators: Evidence and Limits

    Cross-sectional performance comparison across random market environments; standard errors of Sharpe differences and model boundaries.

    6 study hours · Literature synthesis

The project ladder

The capstone Allocator Shoot-Out (20 study hours) challenges you to test all five allocators in a rolling backtest with transaction costs and author an executive evaluation memo:

  1. Worked example

    Step through exact Fraction calculations of 1/N benchmark and GMV portfolio weights on a 3-asset window.

  2. Completion

    Calculate the rebalancing turnover and trading cost drag when transitioning from 1/N to GMV weights.

  3. Independent project

    Build allocators.py to pass the automated pytest suite across 50 seeds, and author the evaluation memo report.md.

  4. Stretch extensions

    Incorporate box-robust optimization, data-driven Ledoit-Wolf intensity formulas, or currency spot rate panels.
