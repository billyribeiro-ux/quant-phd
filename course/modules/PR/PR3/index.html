Growth-Optimal Sizing

The mathematics and practice of geometric compounding: the Kelly criterion in discrete bets, continuous diffusion log-optimal portfolios, Breiman's asymptotic dominance theorem, fractional Kelly trade-offs, sizing under parameter estimation error, Samuelson's utility critique, and institutional drawdown-constrained execution.

  • Prerequisites: PR1 Mean-Variance in Practice, SC6 Continuous-Time Stochastic Control
  • Concepts introduced: kelly-criterion, growth-optimal-portfolio, fractional-kelly, kelly-estimation-error, samuelson-kelly-critique
  • Project: Sizing policy under uncertainty: Python sizing library (sizing.py) and executive sizing policy memorandum (policy_memo.md)

35 h lessons · 15 h project (30.0%)

Objectives

By the end of PR3 you can:

  1. Derive the discrete Kelly criterion from expected log-wealth maximization, proving asymptotic ruin for any bet fraction exceeding double Kelly.
  2. Formulate the continuous-time multi-asset growth-optimal portfolio (GOP), proving Breiman's asymptotic dominance theorem and the numeraire martingale property.
  3. Quantify the growth-volatility-drawdown trade-off of fractional Kelly sizing, demonstrating that half-Kelly captures 75% of excess growth with 50% volatility.
  4. Derive the \(\frac{1}{2T}\) growth penalty of parameter estimation error and construct the optimal linear shrinkage factor \(\alpha^* = \frac{T \operatorname{SR}^2}{T \operatorname{SR}^2 + 1}\).
  5. Evaluate Samuelson's critique of growth-optimality under general CRRA utility functions, resolving the multi-period fallacy of large numbers.
  6. Implement an executable institutional sizing policy combining parameter estimation shrinkage, drawdown survival constraints, and governance protocols.

Placement diagnostic

Eight diagnostic questions covering prerequisite foundations and two implementation skill checks from project rungs 1 and 2. Mastery bar is 80% (at least 7 of 8 correct, including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 1): In a discrete coin-toss game with win probability \(p = 0.60\) and net payoff ratio \(b = 2.0\) (yielding \$2 net profit per \$1 wagered upon win), what is the exact optimal Kelly bet fraction \(f^* = \frac{bp - (1 - p)}{b}\)?

    \(f^* = \frac{2(0.60) - 0.40}{2} = \frac{1.20 - 0.40}{2} = \frac{0.80}{2} = 0.4000\).

  2. Skill check (rung 2): For an investor sizing at half-Kelly (\(c = 0.50\)) in continuous diffusion trading, what is the theoretical asymptotic probability \(P(\text{drawdown} \ge 50\%)\) of ever experiencing a 50% peak-to-trough drawdown (\(d = 0.50\)), calculated as \((1 - d)^{\frac{2}{c} - 1}\)?

    Exponent is \(\frac{2}{0.50} - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3\). The probability is \((1 - 0.50)^3 = 0.50^3 = 0.1250\) (12.5%).

  3. From SC6: In the classic continuous-time Merton portfolio problem with constant risk-free rate \(r\), risky asset drift \(\mu\), volatility \(\sigma\), and CRRA utility \(U(W) = \frac{W^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\), what is the optimal risky allocation fraction \(\pi^*\)?

    The Merton fraction is \(\pi^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\). For logarithmic utility (\(\gamma = 1\)), this coincides with continuous full Kelly leverage.

  4. From PR1: In Markowitz mean-variance optimization, why is naive sample optimization referred to as an "error maximizer" (Michaud 1989)?

    Optimizer error maximization occurs because the quadratic optimizer treats extreme positive estimation errors in drift as genuine investment opportunities.

  5. From SC1 / SC6: For geometric Brownian motion \(dS/S = 0.10 dt + 0.20 dW\) with risk-free rate \(r = 0.02\), what is the continuous Merton log-utility optimal fraction (\(\gamma = 1\))?

    \(f^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2} = \frac{0.10 - 0.02}{0.20^2} = \frac{0.08}{0.04} = 2.0000\).

  6. From M4 / AP2: If \(\bm{\Sigma}\) is a strictly positive definite covariance matrix, which statement is guaranteed to hold for any non-zero allocation vector \(\bm{w} \neq \mathbf{0}\)?

    By definition of positive definiteness, the quadratic form \(\bm{w}' \bm{\Sigma} \bm{w}\) is strictly positive for all non-zero vectors \(\bm{w}\).

  7. From SC6: Which relative risk aversion coefficient \(\gamma = -\frac{W U''(W)}{U'(W)}\) corresponds to logarithmic utility \(U(W) = \ln(W)\)?

    For \(U(W) = \ln(W)\), \(U'(W) = 1/W\) and \(U''(W) = -1/W^2\), so \(-W(-1/W^2) / (1/W) = 1\).

  8. From S1: If excess return \(\mu - r = 0.08\) and volatility \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the annualized Sharpe ratio?

    \(\operatorname{SR} = \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma} = \frac{0.08}{0.20} = 0.40\).

Lessons

The module is structured into five 7-hour lessons totaling 35 study hours:

  1. L01 · The Kelly Criterion 7 h

    Discrete repeated bets, expected log-wealth objective, derivation of \(f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}\), asymptotic optimality, comparison with expected wealth maximization, and proof of asymptotic ruin from overbetting.

  2. L02 · The Growth-Optimal Portfolio 7 h

    Continuous-time diffusion compounding, multi-asset log-optimal portfolio weights \(\bm{w}^* = \bm{\Sigma}^{-1}(\bm{\mu} - r\mathbf{1})\), Breiman's asymptotic dominance theorem, and the numeraire martingale property.

  3. L03 · Fractional Kelly and Volatility Drag 7 h

    Drawdown distributions under full Kelly, fractional Kelly scaling \(\bm{w} = c \bm{w}^*\), the half-Kelly sweet spot (75% excess growth, 50% volatility), and shadow risk aversion \(\gamma = 1/c\).

  4. L04 · Kelly Sizing Under Estimation Error 7 h

    The asymmetric penalty of overbetting, sampling distribution degradation \(\frac{1}{2T}\), Bayesian Kelly sizing, and the optimal linear shrinkage factor \(\alpha^* = \frac{T \operatorname{SR}^2}{T \operatorname{SR}^2 + 1}\).

  5. L05 · Samuelson's Critique and Sizing in Practice 7 h

    Paul Samuelson's 1979 critique, the fallacy of large numbers, CRRA expected utility vs log-wealth maximization, reconciliation via fractional Merton sizing, and institutional drawdown-constrained execution.

Project ladder

The capstone project Sizing Policy Under Uncertainty (15 study hours) applies these concepts to real-world quantitative portfolio sizing through a four-rung ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Step-by-step derivation of discrete and continuous Kelly allocations on a calibrated setup in exact Fraction arithmetic.

  2. Completion

    Interactive completion lab implementing closed-form estimation shrinkage \(\alpha^*\) and drawdown caps \(c_{\text{dd}}\).

  3. Independent

    Implement the complete sizing engine in sizing.py passing the autograder across seeds 0–49, and compose an executive sizing policy memo policy_memo.md.

  4. Stretch

    Extend sizing under parameter uncertainty to fat-tailed jump-diffusion processes and regime-switching drift models.
