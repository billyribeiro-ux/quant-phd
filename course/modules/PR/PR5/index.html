Drawdowns & Capital Allocation

Continuous-time path extremes, stochastic floor protection, stopping boundaries, and institutional risk budgeting: Magdon-Ismail et al. (2004) Brownian drawdown distributions, Grossman & Zhou (1993) optimal drawdown-constrained policies, Gambler's ruin dynamics, and multi-manager platform capital allocation under pod drawdown stops.

  • Prerequisites: PR3 Growth-Optimal Sizing
  • Concepts introduced: maximum-drawdown, drawdown-distribution, drawdown-constrained-investing, risk-of-ruin, multi-strategy-allocation, drawdown-stop-rule
  • Project: Pod allocation simulator: Multi-manager platform execution engine (simulator.py) and executive risk memo (memo.md)

28 h lessons · 12 h project (30.0%)

Objectives

By the end of PR5 you can:

  1. Derive the analytical expectation and cumulative distribution function of Brownian maximum drawdown across zero, positive, and negative drift regimes following Magdon-Ismail et al. (2004).
  2. Formulate the continuous-time Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman dynamic program for portfolio choice subject to a running maximum stochastic floor constraint following Grossman & Zhou (1993).
  3. Evaluate the optimal cushion-based investment policy and explain the endogenous risk reduction and market resistance that emerges at all-time highs.
  4. Analyze the Gambler's ruin hitting probabilities and stopping times for arithmetic Brownian motion under trailing drawdown boundaries.
  5. Critique the institutional drawdown stop-loss paradox, quantifying the skilled manager false positive liquidation rate versus toxic tail truncation.
  6. Optimize multi-strategy capital allocation across trading pods under pod-level drawdown stop rules and platform-level risk budgets.

Prerequisites & Mathematical Foundations

PR5 directly requires PR3 Growth-Optimal Sizing. The conceptual closure builds on continuous stochastic calculus and portfolio theory:

  • PR3: Growth-optimal portfolios, continuous Merton leverage \(\frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2}\), fractional Kelly sizing, and infinite-horizon drawdown probabilities \((1 - d)^{2/c - 1}\).
  • SC1 / SC2: Standard Brownian motion \(W_t\), quadratic variation, Itô's lemma, the reflection principle, and hitting times of diffusion processes.
  • SC6: Stochastic optimal control, Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, and verification theorems for continuous wealth processes.
  • PR1: Multi-asset mean-variance optimization, covariance matrices, and the 1/N equal weighting benchmark.

Placement diagnostic

Eight diagnostic questions covering prerequisite foundations and two implementation skill checks from project rungs 1 and 2. Mastery bar is 80% (at least 7 of 8 correct, including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 1): An investment portfolio achieves a historical peak wealth of \(M_t = 110.0\) and subsequently drops to \(W_t = 93.50\). What is the exact relative peak-to-trough drawdown \(D_t = \frac{M_t - W_t}{M_t}\)?

    \(D_t = \frac{110.0 - 93.50}{110.0} = \frac{16.50}{110.0} = 0.1500\) (15.0%).

  2. Skill check (rung 2): Under a Grossman-Zhou floor protection mandate with floor parameter \(\alpha = 0.85\), an investor's running peak wealth is \(M_t = 100.0\) and current wealth is \(W_t = 95.0\). What is the exact cushion \(C_t = W_t - \alpha M_t\)?

    The protected floor is \(F_t = \alpha M_t = 0.85 \times 100.0 = 85.0\). The cushion is \(C_t = 95.0 - 85.0 = 10.0000\).

  3. From PR3: For an investor sizing at half-Kelly (\(c = 0.50\)) in continuous diffusion trading, what is the theoretical asymptotic probability of ever experiencing a 50% peak-to-trough drawdown (\(d = 0.50\)), calculated as \((1 - d)^{\frac{2}{c} - 1}\)?

    Exponent is \(\frac{2}{0.50} - 1 = 3\). The probability is \((1 - 0.50)^3 = 0.50^3 = 0.1250\) (12.5%).

  4. From PR3 / SC6: For geometric Brownian motion with expected return \(\mu = 0.10\), risk-free rate \(r = 0.02\), and volatility \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the continuous Merton log-utility optimal leverage fraction \(f^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2}\)?

    \(f^* = \frac{0.10 - 0.02}{0.20^2} = \frac{0.08}{0.04} = 2.0000\).

  5. From SC1: By the reflection principle of standard Brownian motion \(W_t\), what is the distribution of the running maximum \(M(T) = \max_{0 \le t \le T} W_t\)?

    Options

    By the reflection principle, \(\mathbb{P}(M(T) \ge m) = 2 \mathbb{P}(W_T \ge m) = \mathbb{P}(|W_T| \ge m)\) for \(m \ge 0\).

  6. From PR1: In multi-asset portfolio optimization, what is a defining characteristic of the naive \(1/N\) equal-weight allocation benchmark?

    Options

    The \(1/N\) portfolio benchmark sets weights mechanically to \(1/N\), completely bypassing drift and covariance estimation error.

  7. From M4 / SC1: For a standard normal variable \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), what is the expected absolute value \(\mathbb{E}[|Z|] = \sqrt{2/\pi}\)?

    \(\mathbb{E}[|Z|] = \sqrt{2/\pi} \approx 0.79788\).

  8. From SC6: In dynamic programming with state variables wealth \(W\) and peak \(M\), what boundary condition must the value function \(V(W, M)\) satisfy at all-time highs (\(W = M\))?

    Options

    Because \(M_t\) increases only when \(W_t = M_t\), the principle of smooth pasting / reflection requires \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial M} = 0\) along the boundary \(W = M\).

Project · Pod Allocation Simulator

The PR5 Capstone Project (12 study hours) builds an institutional multi-manager hedge fund simulation engine. The project follows the four-rung ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Analytical evaluation of Magdon-Ismail zero-drift expected drawdown, Grossman-Zhou floor cushion allocation, and single-pod path drawdown monitoring.

  2. Completion

    Interactive completion lab implementing distance-to-stop cushion calculation and multi-pod capital allocation under inverse-volatility and cushion weighting.

  3. Independent project

    Full implementation of simulator.py simulating a 10-pod hedge fund platform with soft and hard drawdown stops, evaluated across 50 random market seeds against the browser pytest suite, accompanied by an executive allocation memo memo.md.

  4. Stretch

    Open-ended extension incorporating non-Gaussian jump-diffusion returns, endogenous liquidity spirals upon pod liquidation, and dynamic probation re-entry rules.
