Present Value & Return Predictability

Empirical breakdown of the constant discount rate present value model, log-linear linearization, return predictive regressions, vector autoregressive news decomposition, and time-varying discount rates.

Prerequisites: AP1, S3
Unlocks: AP6, AP7
Project: Variance decomposition of equity dividend yields (autograder and rubric)

56 h lessons across 8 lessons @ 7 h · 24 h project (30.0% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of AP5 you can:

  1. Formulate Shiller's present-value variance inequality and test the excess volatility of stock prices against constant-discount-rate fundamentals.
  2. Derive the Campbell-Shiller log-linear present-value identity linking returns, dividend growth, and valuation multiples.
  3. Estimate multi-horizon predictive regressions of stock returns on dividend-price ratios and correct for Stambaugh small-sample persistence bias.
  4. Decompose unexpected stock returns into cash-flow news and discount-rate news using vector autoregressive state-space models.
  5. Quantify the variance shares of valuation ratios explained by time-varying expected returns versus dividend growth.
  6. Evaluate out-of-sample return predictability against the historical prevailing mean benchmark under Welch-Goyal and Campbell-Thompson specifications.
  7. Construct the cyclically adjusted price-earnings ratio (CAPE) and conduct overlapping long-horizon inference with Hansen-Hodrick standard errors.
  8. Synthesize return predictability across equities, government bonds, and foreign exchange under a unified countercyclical discount-rate framework.

Prerequisites

AP5 assumes AP1 (No-Arbitrage & the Stochastic Discount Factor), S3 (Time Series), and their prerequisite closure {M1, M2, M3, M4, S1, S2, CS1, CS3, CF1}. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From AP1: stochastic discount factor, law of one price, and fundamental theorem of asset pricing.
  • From S3: vector autoregression, stationarity, autocorrelation function, and cointegration.
  • From S2: OLS estimator, HAC standard errors, and Stambaugh bias.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite concepts from AP1 and S3, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. Under the law of one price and no-arbitrage, what fundamental relation links the gross asset return \(R_{t+1}\) to the stochastic discount factor \(M_{t+1}\)?

    Options

    The fundamental pricing equation establishes that the conditional expectation of the product of the SDF and any gross return equals 1 (AP1, stochastic-discount-factor).

  2. If the risk-free gross return is \(R_f = 1.05\) and an equity asset has expected return \(\mathbb{E}[R] = 1.11\) with volatility \(\sigma(R) = 0.15\), what is the asset's Sharpe ratio in percent (annualized)?

    \(S = (1.11 - 1.05) / 0.15 = 0.06 / 0.15 = 0.40\) (or 40% in decimals; 0.06 / 0.15 = 0.40). Wait, \(0.06 / 0.15 = 0.40\).

  3. In a first-order vector autoregression \(Z_{t+1} = \Phi Z_t + \epsilon_{t+1}\), what condition is required for covariance stationarity?

    Options

    A VAR(1) process is covariance-stationary if and only if all eigenvalues of the companion transition matrix have modulus strictly less than 1 (S3, stationarity).

  4. What does cointegration between log stock prices \(p_t\) and log dividends \(d_t\) imply for the log dividend-price ratio \(d_t - p_t\)?

    Options

    Cointegration with vector \([1, -1]^\top\) implies that the valuation multiple \(d_t - p_t\) is a stationary, mean-reverting process (S3, cointegration).

  5. Why does ordinary least squares standard error severely understate true uncertainty in multi-year predictive regressions with overlapping returns?

    Options

    Overlapping return horizons of \(k\) periods create serial correlation of order \(k-1\) in the errors, requiring Hansen-Hodrick or Newey-West HAC adjustments (S2, hac-standard-errors).

  6. Under Stambaugh's (1999) predictive regression analysis, what determines the direction of the small-sample bias in the OLS slope coefficient \(\hat\beta\)?

    Options

    The bias is \(\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta - \beta] \approx -(\sigma_{uv} / \sigma_v^2)(1 + 3\phi)/T\); when \(\sigma_{uv} < 0\), the bias is positive (S2, stambaugh-bias).

  7. What does the Gauss-Markov theorem state regarding the ordinary least squares estimator under homoskedasticity and no autocorrelation?

    Options

    Gauss-Markov establishes that OLS has the lowest variance among all linear unbiased estimators when classical assumptions hold (S2, gauss-markov-theorem).

  8. In the Campbell-Shiller linearization with an average dividend-price ratio \(D/P = 0.0526\) (\(\exp(\overline{d-p}) \approx 0.0526\)), what is the discount parameter \(\rho = 1 / (1 + D/P)\)?

    \(\rho = 1 / (1 + 0.0526) = 1 / 1.05263 \approx 0.9500\).

  9. Skill Check 1 (Rung 1): In the Campbell-Shiller VAR(1) news decomposition \(r_{t+1} - \mathbb{E}_t[r_{t+1}] = N_{CF, t+1} - N_{DR, t+1}\), what is the discount-rate news multiplier vector \(\lambda_{dr}\) in terms of selection vector \(e_r\), discount parameter \(\rho\), and companion matrix \(\Phi\)?

    Options

    Discount-rate news is the revision in multi-period discounted returns: \(N_{DR, t+1} = e_r^\top \sum_{j=1}^\infty \rho^j \Phi^j \epsilon_{t+1} = e_r^\top \rho \Phi (I - \rho \Phi)^{-1} \epsilon_{t+1}\).

  10. Skill Check 2 (Rung 2): For a sample of \(T = 100\) observations, predictor persistence \(\phi = 0.95\), predictor variance \(\sigma_v^2 = 0.02\), and cross-innovation covariance \(\sigma_{uv} = -0.02\), what is the first-order Stambaugh small-sample bias \(\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta - \beta] \approx -(\sigma_{uv} / \sigma_v^2)(1 + 3\phi)/T\)?

    \(-(-0.02 / 0.02) \cdot (1 + 3(0.95)) / 100 = 1.0 \cdot (1 + 2.85) / 100 = 3.85 / 100 = 0.0385\).
