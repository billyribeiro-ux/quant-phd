Cash-Flow vs. Discount-Rate News

  • Uses: Campbell–Shiller decomposition, vector autoregressions, stationarity, covariance matrices
  • Introduces: cash-flow versus discount-rate news
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

When an asset's price moves unexpectedly, is the surprise caused by changes in expected future cash flows or by changes in required discount rates? Campbell and Shiller (1988) and Campbell (1991) proved that unexpected returns decompose identically into revisions in expected future dividend growth minus revisions in expected future discount rates. Using a vector autoregression (VAR) to model conditional expectations, the infinite sums of revised expectations collapse into simple closed-form matrix algebra. In empirical aggregate equity data, discount-rate news dominates return volatility, driving over 70% of market fluctuations.

1Unexpected returns and revision in long-run expectations

Recall the Campbell-Shiller log-linearization from Lesson 2: \(r_{t+1} \approx k - \rho\,\delta_{t+1} + \delta_t + \Delta d_{t+1}\). Taking the difference between expectations formed at date \(t+1\) and expectations formed at date \(t\) reveals how current news affects valuation.

Definition 1.1 (cash-flow news and discount-rate news)

The innovation in unexpected stock return \(\eta_{t+1} = r_{t+1} - \E_t[r_{t+1}]\) decomposes identically into cash-flow versus discount-rate news:

\[ \eta_{t+1} = N_{\text{CF}, t+1} - N_{\text{DR}, t+1}, \]

where cash-flow news \(N_{\text{CF}, t+1}\) and discount-rate news \(N_{\text{DR}, t+1}\) are defined as

\[ N_{\text{CF}, t+1} = (\E_{t+1} - \E_t)\sum_{j=0}^\infty \rho^j \Delta d_{t+1+j}, \qquad N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = (\E_{t+1} - \E_t)\sum_{j=1}^\infty \rho^j r_{t+1+j}. \]

Notice the crucial minus sign: a positive discount-rate shock (investors suddenly demand higher future returns) depresses current prices, creating an immediate negative unexpected return \(\eta_{t+1} < 0\).

2The vector autoregressive implementation

To compute the infinite sums of revisions empirically, Campbell (1991) assumes the state of the economy is described by a first-order vector autoregression (VAR(1)):

\[ z_{t+1} = \Phi\,z_t + \epsilon_{t+1}, \qquad \E[\epsilon_{t+1}] = 0, \quad \E[\epsilon_{t+1}\epsilon_{t+1}^\top] = \Sigma. \]

The state vector \(z_t \in \R^K\) contains demeaned log return as its first element, \(z_{1,t} = r_t - \E[r]\), followed by predictive state variables such as the log dividend-price ratio \(\delta_t - \E[\delta]\). Any higher-order VAR(\(p\)) can be written in companion VAR(1) form.

By the Markov property of the VAR, \(j\)-step ahead forecasts satisfy \(\E_t[z_{t+j}] = \Phi^j z_t\). The revision in expectations from date \(t\) to \(t+1\) is

\[ (\E_{t+1} - \E_t)[z_{t+1+j}] = \Phi^j z_{t+1} - \Phi^{j+1} z_t = \Phi^j (z_{t+1} - \Phi z_t) = \Phi^j \epsilon_{t+1}. \]
Theorem 2.1 (VAR news multipliers)

Let \(e_r = (1, 0, \dots, 0)^\top \in \R^K\) be the selection vector for returns. If the companion matrix \(\Phi\) is stable (spectral radius strictly inside the unit circle), then discount-rate news and cash-flow news are linear functions of the VAR innovation vector \(\epsilon_{t+1}\):

\[ N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = \lambda_{\text{DR}}^\top \epsilon_{t+1}, \qquad N_{\text{CF}, t+1} = \lambda_{\text{CF}}^\top \epsilon_{t+1}, \]

where the multiplier vectors are

\[ \lambda_{\text{DR}} = \rho\,\Phi^\top (I - \rho\,\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r, \qquad \lambda_{\text{CF}} = (I - \rho\,\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r. \]
Proof

By definition, discount-rate news is the discounted sum of revisions in future returns:

\[ N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \rho^j (\E_{t+1} - \E_t)[r_{t+1+j}] = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \rho^j e_r^\top \Phi^j \epsilon_{t+1} = e_r^\top \left( \sum_{j=1}^\infty (\rho\,\Phi)^j \right) \epsilon_{t+1}. \]

Summing the geometric series of matrices \(\sum_{j=1}^\infty (\rho\,\Phi)^j = \rho\,\Phi (I - \rho\,\Phi)^{-1}\), we obtain

\[ N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = e_r^\top \rho\,\Phi (I - \rho\,\Phi)^{-1} \epsilon_{t+1} = \lambda_{\text{DR}}^\top \epsilon_{t+1}, \quad \text{with } \lambda_{\text{DR}} = \rho\,\Phi^\top (I - \rho\,\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r. \]

From the unexpected return identity, \(\eta_{t+1} = e_r^\top \epsilon_{t+1} = N_{\text{CF}, t+1} - N_{\text{DR}, t+1}\). Rearranging for cash-flow news,

\[ N_{\text{CF}, t+1} = e_r^\top \epsilon_{t+1} + N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = \big[e_r^\top + e_r^\top \rho\,\Phi (I - \rho\,\Phi)^{-1}\big]\epsilon_{t+1} = e_r^\top \big[I + \rho\,\Phi (I - \rho\,\Phi)^{-1}\big]\epsilon_{t+1}. \]

Using the matrix identity \(I + A(I - A)^{-1} = (I - A + A)(I - A)^{-1} = (I - A)^{-1}\) with \(A = \rho\,\Phi\), we conclude

\[ N_{\text{CF}, t+1} = e_r^\top (I - \rho\,\Phi)^{-1} \epsilon_{t+1} = \lambda_{\text{CF}}^\top \epsilon_{t+1}. \]

3Variance decomposition of unexpected returns

Taking the variance of \(\eta_{t+1} = N_{\text{CF}, t+1} - N_{\text{DR}, t+1}\) yields the exact variance decomposition:

\[ \Var(\eta_{t+1}) = \Var(N_{\text{CF}, t+1}) + \Var(N_{\text{DR}, t+1}) - 2\Cov(N_{\text{CF}, t+1}, N_{\text{DR}, t+1}). \]

Dividing through by \(\Var(\eta_{t+1})\) gives the variance shares summing to 100%:

\[ 1 = \frac{\Var(N_{\text{CF}})}{\Var(\eta)} + \frac{\Var(N_{\text{DR}})}{\Var(\eta)} - \frac{2\Cov(N_{\text{CF}}, N_{\text{DR}})}{\Var(\eta)}. \]
Proposition 3.1 (empirical dominance of discount-rate news)

In post-war US equity data (S&P 500), discount-rate news accounts for 70% to 85% of unexpected return variance, while cash-flow news accounts for only 15% to 30%, with a small positive covariance between the two news components.

Lab 1 · Vector Autoregression and News Decomposition

Estimating a bivariate VAR(1) on simulated equity returns and the dividend-price ratio allows exact decomposition of return innovations into cash-flow and discount-rate news. The unexpected return identity \(\eta_{t+1} = N_{\text{CF}, t+1} - N_{\text{DR}, t+1}\) holds to machine precision, and discount-rate news will account for the majority of return variance.

"""Lab AP5-L04-1: Vector Autoregression and News Decomposition.

SIMULATED: estimates a first-order VAR on returns and the log dividend-price ratio
to decompose unexpected stock returns into cash-flow news and discount-rate news
(Campbell & Shiller 1988, Campbell 1991).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(888)
    n_obs = 250  # 250 annual observations
    rho = 0.96  # annual discount factor

    # True VAR(1) parameters: z_t = [r_t - E[r], dp_t - E[dp]]^T
    # dp_{t+1} = 0.92 * dp_t - 0.05 * r_t + v_{t+1}
    # r_{t+1}  = 0.12 * dp_t + u_{t+1}
    phi_true = np.array([[0.02, 0.12], [-0.03, 0.92]])
    sigma_u = 0.18
    sigma_v = 0.15
    cov_uv = -0.75 * sigma_u * sigma_v
    cov_true = np.array([[sigma_u**2, cov_uv], [cov_uv, sigma_v**2]])

    # Simulate VAR series
    eps = rng.multivariate_normal(mean=[0.0, 0.0], cov=cov_true, size=n_obs + 1)
    z = np.zeros((n_obs + 1, 2))
    for t in range(n_obs):
        z[t + 1] = phi_true @ z[t] + eps[t + 1]

    # Demean variables
    z_demean = z - np.mean(z, axis=0)
    z_lag = z_demean[:-1]
    z_lead = z_demean[1:]

    # OLS estimation of VAR(1): z_lead = z_lag @ Phi.T + residuals
    phi_hat = (np.linalg.inv(z_lag.T @ z_lag) @ (z_lag.T @ z_lead)).T
    resids = z_lead - (z_lag @ phi_hat.T)

    # News decomposition matrices
    # e_r = [1, 0]^T
    e_r = np.array([1.0, 0.0])
    eye_2 = np.eye(2)
    inv_matrix = np.linalg.inv(eye_2 - rho * phi_hat)

    # lambda_dr = e_r^T rho Phi (I - rho Phi)^(-1)
    lambda_dr = e_r @ (rho * phi_hat) @ inv_matrix
    # lambda_cf = e_r^T + lambda_dr = e_r^T (I - rho Phi)^(-1)
    lambda_cf = e_r @ inv_matrix

    # News series for each period t:
    n_dr = resids @ lambda_dr
    n_cf = resids @ lambda_cf
    eta_r = resids[:, 0]  # unexpected return = e_r^T resids

    # Verify return identity: eta_r = N_cf - N_dr
    identity_diff = np.max(np.abs(eta_r - (n_cf - n_dr)))

    # Compute variance-covariance decomposition
    var_eta = float(np.var(eta_r, ddof=1))
    var_cf = float(np.var(n_cf, ddof=1))
    var_dr = float(np.var(n_dr, ddof=1))
    cov_cf_dr = float(np.cov(n_cf, n_dr, ddof=1)[0, 1])

    # Algebraic variance identity: var(eta) = var(cf) + var(dr) - 2*cov(cf, dr)
    var_sum = var_cf + var_dr - 2.0 * cov_cf_dr
    var_diff = abs(var_eta - var_sum)

    # Share of return variance
    share_dr = var_dr / var_eta
    share_cf = var_cf / var_eta
    share_cov = -2.0 * cov_cf_dr / var_eta

    print("Campbell-Shiller (1988) VAR News Decomposition")
    print(f"Sample length T:             {n_obs} years")
    print(f"Discount factor rho:         {rho:.4f}")
    print(
        f"VAR eigenvalues:             {float(np.abs(np.linalg.eigvals(phi_hat))[0]):.4f}, "
        f"{float(np.abs(np.linalg.eigvals(phi_hat))[1]):.4f}"
    )
    print(f"Max identity discrepancy:    {identity_diff:.8e}")
    print(f"Return innovation var:       {var_eta:.6f}")
    print(f"Discount-rate news var:      {var_dr:.6f}")
    print(f"Cash-flow news var:          {var_cf:.6f}")
    print(f"CF-DR news covariance:       {cov_cf_dr:.6f}")
    print(f"Variance identity gap:       {var_diff:.8e}")
    print(f"DR news share of variance:   {share_dr * 100:.2f}%")
    print(f"CF news share of variance:   {share_cf * 100:.2f}%")
    print(f"Covariance share:            {share_cov * 100:.2f}%")

4Where the canon breaks

The VAR news decomposition relies on key identifying assumptions that face significant econometric challenges:

  • Omitted state variables: Cash-flow news is measured as a residual: \(N_{\text{CF}} = \eta + N_{\text{DR}}\). If the econometrician omits state variables that investors observe, \(N_{\text{DR}}\) will be mismeasured, and any forecast errors are erroneously attributed to cash-flow news.
  • In-sample VAR overfitting: In small samples, estimating \(K^2\) autoregressive parameters can lead to in-sample overfitting. Biased estimates of \(\Phi\) distort the news multipliers \(\lambda_{\text{DR}}\) and \(\lambda_{\text{CF}}\).
  • Bad beta vs. good beta ICAPM: Campbell and Vuolteenaho (2004) showed that an asset's market beta splits into a cash-flow beta and a discount-rate beta. While modern theory predicts that cash-flow beta carries a much higher risk premium ("bad beta"), empirical estimation of these betas is sensitive to the VAR specification.

5Misconceptions & pitfalls

  1. “A market crash indicates that investors dramatically lowered their expectations of future company earnings.”

    VAR news decompositions show that over 70% of stock market volatility is driven by discount-rate news. Most market crashes reflect sudden surges in required risk premia (investor risk aversion or uncertainty) rather than revisions in dividend forecasts.

  2. “Cash-flow news and discount-rate news must be negatively correlated.”

    In empirical data, cash-flow news and discount-rate news are often positively correlated (\(\Cov(N_{\text{CF}}, N_{\text{DR}}) > 0\)). Good news about future earnings frequently coincides with economic expansions that raise discount rates, dampening return volatility.

  3. “If a predictor has a small \(R^2\) in a return regression, it cannot affect discount-rate news.”

    Because the multiplier \(\lambda_{\text{DR}}\) involves the matrix inverse \((I - \rho\Phi)^{-1}\), even a small predictive coefficient is scaled by \(1 / (1 - \rho\phi) \approx 1 / (1 - 0.96 \times 0.95) \approx 11.4\). Small one-period predictability generates massive discount-rate news.

Mechanics

M1 · Calculate univariate news multiplier

Suppose return is predicted by its own lag: \(r_{t+1} = \phi r_t + \epsilon_{t+1}\) with \(\phi = 0.40\) and discount factor \(\rho = 0.96\). Compute the scalar discount-rate news multiplier \(\lambda_{\text{DR}}\) and cash-flow news multiplier \(\lambda_{\text{CF}}\).

Solution

In the scalar case, \(\lambda_{\text{DR}} = \frac{\rho\phi}{1 - \rho\phi} = \frac{0.96 \times 0.40}{1 - 0.96 \times 0.40} = \frac{0.384}{1 - 0.384} = \frac{0.384}{0.616} \approx 0.623377\). The cash-flow news multiplier is \(\lambda_{\text{CF}} = 1 + \lambda_{\text{DR}} = 1 + 0.623377 = 1.623377\).

M2 · Return variance decomposition arithmetic

In an estimated VAR, \(\Var(N_{\text{CF}}) = 0.010\), \(\Var(N_{\text{DR}}) = 0.035\), and \(\Cov(N_{\text{CF}}, N_{\text{DR}}) = 0.005\). Compute the variance of unexpected return \(\Var(\eta)\) and the variance shares.

Solution

The variance identity gives \(\Var(\eta) = \Var(N_{\text{CF}}) + \Var(N_{\text{DR}}) - 2\Cov(N_{\text{CF}}, N_{\text{DR}}) = 0.010 + 0.035 - 2(0.005) = 0.045 - 0.010 = 0.035\). The discount-rate news share is \(\Var(N_{\text{DR}}) / \Var(\eta) = 0.035 / 0.035 = 1.00\) (100%). The cash-flow share is \(0.010 / 0.035 \approx 0.2857\) (28.57%), and the covariance share is \(-2(0.005) / 0.035 \approx -0.2857\) (-28.57%). Sum: \(100\% + 28.57\% - 28.57\% = 100\%\).

M3 · News realization calculation

At date \(t+1\), the VAR innovation vector is \(\epsilon_{t+1} = \begin{pmatrix} -0.05 \\ 0.08 \end{pmatrix}\). The multipliers are \(\lambda_{\text{DR}} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.20 \\ 0.90 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\lambda_{\text{CF}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1.20 \\ 0.90 \end{pmatrix}\). Compute \(N_{\text{DR}, t+1}, N_{\text{CF}, t+1}\), and verify the identity.

Solution

\(N_{\text{DR}} = \lambda_{\text{DR}}^\top \epsilon = 0.20(-0.05) + 0.90(0.08) = -0.010 + 0.072 = +0.062\). \(N_{\text{CF}} = \lambda_{\text{CF}}^\top \epsilon = 1.20(-0.05) + 0.90(0.08) = -0.060 + 0.072 = +0.012\). Unexpected return is \(\eta = N_{\text{CF}} - N_{\text{DR}} = 0.012 - 0.062 = -0.050\), which exactly equals \(\epsilon_1 = -0.050\).

P1 · Matrix geometric series convergence

Prove that if all eigenvalues \(\mu_i\) of \(\Phi\) satisfy \(|\mu_i| < 1/\rho\), then the matrix geometric series \(\sum_{j=0}^\infty (\rho\Phi)^j\) converges absolutely to \((I - \rho\Phi)^{-1}\).

Solution

Consider the Jordan canonical form \(\Phi = P J P^{-1}\). Then \((\rho\Phi)^j = P (\rho J)^j P^{-1}\). The diagonal entries of \(\rho J\) are \(\rho \mu_i\). Since \(|\rho \mu_i| < 1\), each block \((\rho J_k)^j \to 0\) exponentially as \(j \to \infty\). Compute the partial sum \(S_n = \sum_{j=0}^n (\rho\Phi)^j\). Multiplying by \((I - \rho\Phi)\), \((I - \rho\Phi) S_n = I - (\rho\Phi)^{n+1}\). As \(n \to \infty\), \((\rho\Phi)^{n+1} \to 0\). Since \(\det(I - \rho\Phi) = \prod (1 - \rho\mu_i) \ne 0\), \((I - \rho\Phi)\) is invertible, yielding \(\lim_{n \to \infty} S_n = (I - \rho\Phi)^{-1}\).

P2 · Identity of cash-flow and discount-rate news

Prove algebraically from the definition of \(\lambda_{\text{CF}}\) and \(\lambda_{\text{DR}}\) that \(\lambda_{\text{CF}} - \lambda_{\text{DR}} = e_r\).

Solution

By definition, \(\lambda_{\text{CF}} = (I - \rho\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r\) and \(\lambda_{\text{DR}} = \rho\Phi^\top (I - \rho\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r\). Subtracting the two expressions, \(\lambda_{\text{CF}} - \lambda_{\text{DR}} = \big[(I - \rho\Phi^\top)^{-1} - \rho\Phi^\top (I - \rho\Phi^\top)^{-1}\big] e_r = (I - \rho\Phi^\top)(I - \rho\Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r = I e_r = e_r\). Therefore, for any shock vector \(\epsilon_{t+1}\), \(N_{\text{CF}, t+1} - N_{\text{DR}, t+1} = (\lambda_{\text{CF}} - \lambda_{\text{DR}})^\top \epsilon_{t+1} = e_r^\top \epsilon_{t+1} = \eta_{t+1}\).

P3 · Invariance to state vector rotations

Prove that the scalar news series \(N_{\text{DR}, t+1}\) and \(N_{\text{CF}, t+1}\) are invariant to non-singular linear transformations of the state vector \(\tilde{z}_t = M z_t\), provided the first element \(r_t\) is preserved.

Solution

Let \(\tilde{z}_t = M z_t\) with \(M\) invertible and \(e_r^\top M^{-1} = e_r^\top\). The transformed VAR has transition matrix \(\tilde{\Phi} = M \Phi M^{-1}\) and innovations \(\tilde{\epsilon} = M \epsilon\). The new multiplier is \(\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{DR}} = \rho \tilde{\Phi}^\top (I - \rho \tilde{\Phi}^\top)^{-1} e_r = \rho (M^{-1})^\top \Phi^\top M^\top \big(M^{-\top} (I - \rho \Phi^\top)^{-1} M^\top\big) e_r = M^{-\top} \rho \Phi^\top (I - \rho \Phi^\top)^{-1} e_r = M^{-\top} \lambda_{\text{DR}}\). Then the news realization is \(\tilde{N}_{\text{DR}} = \tilde{\lambda}_{\text{DR}}^\top \tilde{\epsilon} = (\lambda_{\text{DR}}^\top M^{-1})(M \epsilon) = \lambda_{\text{DR}}^\top \epsilon = N_{\text{DR}}\). The same holds for \(N_{\text{CF}}\).

Empirical

E1 · Sensitivity to the discount parameter rho

Campbell (1991) evaluated news shares across \(\rho \in [0.94, 0.98]\). Explain how changing \(\rho\) affects the relative variance of discount-rate news versus cash-flow news.

Solution

Higher \(\rho\) places greater weight on distant future expectations because \(\lambda_{\text{DR}} \propto (I - \rho\Phi)^{-1}\). As \(\rho\) increases from 0.94 to 0.98, the effective duration of equity increases, amplifying the sensitivity of current prices to persistent discount-rate changes. Consequently, higher \(\rho\) increases \(\Var(N_{\text{DR}})\) relative to \(\Var(N_{\text{CF}})\), reinforcing the dominance of discount-rate variation.

E2 · News decomposition during the 2008 Financial Crisis

During the fall of 2008, the S&P 500 plunged by over 30%. Did VAR news decompositions attribute this crash primarily to cash-flow news or discount-rate news?

Solution

VAR decompositions attributed the majority of the 2008 plunge to discount-rate news: required equity risk premia spiked to historic highs amid severe liquidity freeze and panic. While corporate earnings subsequently declined in 2009, real dividends were only trimmed by approximately 20%, whereas market valuation multiples collapsed by over 50%, confirming discount-rate dominance.

E3 · Individual stocks vs. market index

Vuolteenaho (2002) applied the Campbell news decomposition to individual firm-level stock returns. How do firm-level results differ from aggregate market results?

Solution

Vuolteenaho (2002) discovered that while aggregate market returns are driven predominantly by discount-rate news (75%+), individual stock returns are driven predominantly by firm-specific cash-flow news (over 80%). Because individual firm idiosyncratic cash-flow shocks diversify away in portfolios, aggregate market volatility is left reflecting macroeconomic discount-rate fluctuations.

Research

R1 · Campbell and Vuolteenaho (2004) Two-Beta ICAPM

Explain how Campbell and Vuolteenaho (2004) used the news decomposition to resolve the CAPM value and size anomalies.

Solution

In Merton's ICAPM, conservative investors fear shocks that reduce future investment opportunities (discount-rate increases that lower wealth) less than permanent cash-flow losses. Campbell and Vuolteenaho decomposed market beta into cash-flow beta ("bad beta", which demands a high risk premium) and discount-rate beta ("good beta", which carries a low premium). Value and small stocks have high bad cash-flow betas, explaining their high average returns, whereas growth stocks have low cash-flow betas and high good betas.

R2 · Residual measurement of cash-flow news

Chen and Zhao (2009) criticized the Campbell news decomposition for measuring cash-flow news as a residual rather than directly modeling dividend expectations. Summarize their critique and Campbell, Polk, and Vuolteenaho's (2010) response.

Solution

Chen and Zhao (2009) argued that defining \(N_{\text{CF}} = \eta + N_{\text{DR}}\) makes cash-flow news the "garbage collector" for any specification errors in the discount-rate VAR: if the VAR understates discount-rate predictability, cash-flow news variance is artificially inflated. Campbell, Polk, and Vuolteenaho (2010) responded by directly forecasting cash flows with vector autoregressions on earnings and dividends, proving that direct cash-flow forecasts closely match the residual news series and confirming discount-rate dominance.

R3 · State variable selection in VARs

What state variables are standard in the empirical asset pricing literature for capturing macroeconomic discount-rate variation in VARs?

Solution

Standard empirical VAR specifications include: (1) the log dividend-price ratio or smoothed earnings yield (CAPE); (2) the Treasury term spread (10-year minus 3-month yield); (3) the credit default spread (Baa minus Aaa corporate yield); (4) the short-term nominal risk-free interest rate (or relative bill rate); and (5) the consumption-wealth ratio (cay, Lettau & Ludvigson 2001). These variables collectively capture countercyclical variation in risk premia.
