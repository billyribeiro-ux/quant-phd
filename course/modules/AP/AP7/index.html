Behavioral Finance

Asset pricing under bounded rationality and psychological frictions: noise-trader risk and performance-based arbitrage limits, cognitive heuristics and under/over-reaction dynamics, composite investor sentiment indices, the clash between extrapolative survey expectations and rational discount rates, diagnostic expectations, and the empirical confrontation between risk and mispricing interpretations of market anomalies.

Prerequisites: AP5, E3
Unlocks: AP8, CAP3
Project: Sentiment-index replication (autograder and rubric)

60 h lessons across 5 lessons @ 12 h · 20 h project (25.0% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of AP7 you can:

  1. Formulate the De Long, Shleifer, Summers & Waldmann (1990) noise-trader risk model and prove how finite horizons and resale-price risk overturn Friedman's market selection hypothesis.
  2. Analyze cognitive heuristics (conservatism, representativeness, overconfidence, biased self-attribution) in the structural models of Barberis-Shleifer-Vishny (1998), Daniel-Hirshleifer-Subrahmanyam (1998), and Hong-Stein (1999).
  3. Replicate the Baker & Wurgler (2006) orthogonalized investor sentiment index from empirical proxies and quantify conditional return predictability across difficulty-to-arbitrage portfolios.
  4. Evaluate the Greenwood & Shleifer (2014) survey expectations puzzle and contrast extrapolative subjective beliefs with rational present-value discount rates.
  5. Apply Bordalo, Gennaioli, La Porta & Shleifer (2019) diagnostic expectations to model overreaction to earnings news innovations and predictable subsequent forecast reversals.
  6. Design rigorous empirical tests (Daniel-Titman characteristic vs covariance sorts, earnings announcement surprises, sentiment interactions, and publication decay) to arbitrate between rational risk and behavioral mispricing.

Prerequisites

AP7 assumes AP5 (Present Value & Return Predictability), E3 (Choice under Uncertainty), and their prerequisite closure {AP1, AP2, S1, S2, S3, FC1, M4, M7}. It relies on these concepts without redefining them:

  • From AP5: discount-rate variation, excess volatility, Campbell-Shiller decomposition, and dividend-price regression.
  • From E3: expected utility, Arrow-Pratt risk aversion, prospect theory, loss aversion, and probability weighting.
  • From AP1 & AP2: stochastic discount factor, law of one price, CAPM, beta, and linear factor model.
  • From S2 & S3: OLS estimator, stationarity, and vector autoregression.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite foundations from AP5, E3, and AP1, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. In modern empirical asset pricing (AP5), what economic mechanism accounts for virtually all time-series variance in dividend-price ratios?

    Options

    Campbell-Shiller decompositions establish that dividend yields reflect time-varying discount rates rather than expected dividend growth (AP5, discount-rate-variation).

  2. Under prospect theory (E3, Kahneman & Tversky 1979), how does an investor evaluate outcomes?

    Options

    Prospect theory posits that utility is defined on gains and losses relative to a reference point, exhibiting risk aversion for gains, risk seeking for losses, and loss aversion (E3, prospect-theory).

  3. Under the stochastic discount factor framework (AP1), an asset's expected excess return \(\mathbb{E}[R_i^e]\) is determined by…

    Options

    No-arbitrage requires \(\mathbb{E}[M R_i^e] = 0 \implies \mathbb{E}[R_i^e] = -R_f \operatorname{Cov}(M, R_i^e)\) (AP1, stochastic-discount-factor).

  4. What does loss aversion (E3) state regarding the psychological impact of losses versus gains?

    Options

    Empirical estimates of the loss aversion coefficient \(\lambda\) typically center around 2.25, meaning losses generate more than twice the subjective disutility of equivalent gains (E3, loss-aversion).

  5. If the market excess return variance is \(\operatorname{Var}(R_m^e) = 0.04\) and an asset's covariance with the market is \(\operatorname{Cov}(R_i^e, R_m^e) = 0.10\), what is the asset's CAPM beta \(\beta_i\)?

    \(\beta_i = \operatorname{Cov}(R_i^e, R_m^e) / \operatorname{Var}(R_m^e) = 0.10 / 0.04 = 2.50\) (AP2, beta).

  6. What did Shiller's (1981) excess volatility test establish regarding aggregate stock price movements (AP5)?

    Options

    Shiller (1981) demonstrated that price volatility violates the theoretical variance bounds implied by constant-discount-rate present-value models (AP5, excess-volatility).

  7. In a predictive return regression \(R_{t+1} = \alpha + \beta x_t + u_{t+1}\) with persistent predictor \(x_t = \phi x_{t-1} + v_t\) (AP5, Stambaugh 1999), when is the OLS slope biased upward in small samples?

    Options

    The small-sample bias is \(\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta - \beta] \approx -(\sigma_{uv} / \sigma_v^2)(1 + 3\phi) / T\); when \(\sigma_{uv} < 0\), the bias is positive (AP5, dividend-price-regression).

  8. If a perpetuity pays constant dividend \(d = \$1.00\) per year and the risk-free rate is \(r = 5.0\%\), what is its Gordon fundamental present value \(V = d / r\)?

    \(V = d / r = 1.00 / 0.05 = \$20.00\).

  9. Skill Check 1 (Rung 1): When extracting an investor sentiment index via principal components on a matrix of standardized proxies \(X\), why must a sign anchor proxy (such as the number of IPOs, NIPO) be specified?

    Options

    Eigenvectors are sign-indeterminate (\(\mathbf{C} v = \lambda v \implies \mathbf{C}(-v) = \lambda (-v)\)). Anchoring a proxy known to represent bullish sentiment (such as NIPO > 0) fixes the economic orientation so high index values correspond to high investor sentiment.

  10. Skill Check 2 (Rung 2): Under the Newey-West (1987) HAC estimator with lag truncation parameter \(L = 3\), what is the Bartlett triangular kernel weight \(w_l = 1 - l / (L + 1)\) applied to the sample autocovariance at lag \(l = 2\)?

    \(w_2 = 1 - 2 / (3 + 1) = 1 - 2/4 = 0.50\).

Project Ladder

The module concludes with a 20-hour computational and empirical research project replicating the Baker & Wurgler (2006) investor sentiment index and testing its predictive power. See project.html for the full specification, autograder panel, and rubric.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Step-by-step econometric estimation of proxy standardization, macroeconomic orthogonalization via OLS, first principal component extraction with sign anchor normalization, and Newey-West HAC inference.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: In-browser completion lab implementing the orthogonalization projection matrix, standardized first principal component extraction, and Newey-West HAC covariance matrix calculation.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Complete Sentiment-Index Replication Engine rebuilding the Baker-Wurgler orthogonalized sentiment index from raw proxies and testing conditional-return predictions and cross-sectional regime asymmetry.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Out-of-sample recursive expanding-window sentiment index construction, real-time regime filtering, and Hansen-Hodrick overlapping return adjustments.
