Intermediary & Institutional Asset Pricing

Frictional asset pricing where specialized intermediaries and institutional asset managers act as marginal investors: performance-based limits to arbitrage, dealer balance sheet constraints, slow-moving capital search dynamics, characteristics-based portfolio demand systems, and the inelastic-markets hypothesis.

Prerequisites: AP5, E4
Unlocks: SR2, CAP3
Project: Flow-impact estimation & inelastic markets engine (autograder and rubric)

60 h lessons across 5 lessons @ 12 h · 20 h project (25.0% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of AP6 you can:

  1. Formulate the Shleifer-Vishny performance-based arbitrage model and quantify how investor fund redemptions exacerbate mispricings during liquidity crises.
  2. Derive the intermediary asset pricing stochastic discount factor under dealer equity capital constraints and estimate cross-sectional factor risk premia across multiple asset classes.
  3. Solve the dynamic price paths and inventory trajectories of slow-moving capital under search-and-matching frictions following large market supply shocks.
  4. Estimate institutional portfolio demand systems using 2SLS instrumental variables to overcome latent quality endogeneity in Form 13F equity holdings.
  5. Compute the aggregate market demand elasticity and macro flow multiplier under the inelastic-markets hypothesis across institutional sectors.
  6. Measure the empirical price impact of index addition and deletion rebalancing flows using event-study methodology and relate them to structural inelasticity.

Prerequisites

AP6 assumes AP5 (Present Value & Return Predictability), E4 (General Equilibrium & Information), and their prerequisite closure {AP1, AP2, S2, S3, FC1, M3, M7}. It relies on these concepts without redefining them:

  • From AP5: discount-rate variation, excess volatility, and Campbell-Shiller decomposition.
  • From E4: Arrow-Debreu equilibrium, rational expectations equilibrium, and Grossman-Stiglitz paradox.
  • From AP1 & AP2: stochastic discount factor, law of one price, CAPM, and linear factor model.
  • From S2 & S3: OLS estimator, stationarity, and vector autoregression.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite foundations from AP5, E4, and AP1, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. In modern asset pricing theory (AP5), what economic mechanism accounts for virtually all time-series variance in dividend-price ratios?

    Options

    Campbell-Shiller decompositions establish that dividend yields reflect time-varying discount rates rather than expected dividend growth (AP5, discount-rate-variation).

  2. What does the Grossman-Stiglitz (1980) paradox (E4) establish regarding informational efficiency in competitive markets?

    Options

    The paradox proves that prices must contain noise (such as noise trader risk or supply shocks) for informed traders to earn a return on information acquisition (E4, grossman-stiglitz-paradox).

  3. Under the stochastic discount factor framework (AP1), an asset's expected excess return \(\mathbb{E}[R_i^e]\) is proportional to…

    Options

    No-arbitrage requires \(\mathbb{E}[M R_i^e] = 0 \implies \mathbb{E}[R_i^e] = -R_f \operatorname{Cov}(M, R_i^e)\) (AP1, stochastic-discount-factor).

  4. What distinguishes a noisy rational expectations equilibrium (REE) from full information revelation (E4)?

    Options

    Supply shocks obscure the information content of prices, creating partially revealing equilibrium prices (E4, noisy-rational-expectations-equilibrium).

  5. If the market excess return variance is \(\operatorname{Var}(R_m^e) = 0.04\) and an asset's covariance with the market is \(\operatorname{Cov}(R_i^e, R_m^e) = 0.10\), what is the asset's CAPM beta \(\beta_i\)?

    \(\beta_i = \operatorname{Cov}(R_i^e, R_m^e) / \operatorname{Var}(R_m^e) = 0.10 / 0.04 = 2.50\).

  6. What does Stambaugh (1999) bias predict for predictive return regressions when return shocks negatively correlate with valuation innovations?

    Options

    When \(\operatorname{Cov}(u, v) < 0\), the finite-sample bias \(\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta - \beta] \approx -(\sigma_{uv}/\sigma_v^2)(1+3\phi)/T > 0\) inflates the slope estimate (AP5, S2).

  7. Under the Arrow-Debreu general equilibrium model (E4), why are complete financial markets Pareto efficient?

    Options

    Full spanning across states equates state-price deflators across investors, satisfying the First Fundamental Welfare Theorem (E4, arrow-debreu-equilibrium).

  8. An AR(1) dealer equity capital ratio has autocorrelation \(\phi = 0.88\). What is its persistence parameter \(\phi\)?

    The persistence parameter in \(\eta_t = c + \phi \eta_{t-1} + u_t\) is 0.88.

  9. Skill Check 1 (Rung 1): In an event study of index inclusion with tracking flow fraction \(f = \Delta Q / Q\) and cumulative abnormal return \(\Delta \ln P\), how is the stock-level price elasticity of demand \(\zeta_{\text{stock}}\) recovered from the OLS regression slope \(\mu\) in \(\Delta \ln P = c + \mu f + \epsilon\)?

    Options

    Price impact \(\mu = \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln P}{\mathrm{d} Q / Q} = \frac{1}{\zeta}\). Therefore, the implied structural demand elasticity is the reciprocal of the impact slope: \(\zeta = 1 / \mu\).

  10. Skill Check 2 (Rung 2): An institutional sector has equity AUM shares \(s_1 = 0.60\) and \(s_2 = 0.40\) with estimated price elasticities \(\zeta_1 = 0.20\) and \(\zeta_2 = 0.325\). Under Gabaix & Koijen (2021), what is the aggregate market flow multiplier \(M = 1 / \zeta_m\)?

    \(\zeta_m = (0.60 \times 0.20) + (0.40 \times 0.325) = 0.120 + 0.130 = 0.250\). The macro multiplier is \(M = 1 / 0.250 = 4.00\).

Project Ladder

The module concludes with a 20-hour computational and empirical research project on institutional flow impact and the inelastic-markets hypothesis. See project.html for the full specification, autograder panel, and rubric.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Step-by-step econometric estimation of index addition flow impact, stock-level elasticity inversion, and macro multiplier derivation on calibrated market data.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: In-browser completion lab calculating Koijen-Yogo 2SLS structural elasticities, aggregate market elasticity, and total equity wealth impacts.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Complete Flow-Impact Estimation & Inelastic Markets Engine implementing the HKM intermediary SDF, Koijen-Yogo demand system, fixed-point market clearing, and event-study flow impact.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Multi-sector general equilibrium extension modeling institutional mandate shifts between passive indexers, active hedge funds, and constrained pension funds under dealer balance sheet stress.
