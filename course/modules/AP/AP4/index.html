Resolving the Puzzles

Resolving the failure of standard consumption asset pricing: Campbell-Cochrane external habit persistence, Bansal-Yaron recursive preferences and long-run risks, Rietz-Barro rare economic disasters, Constantinides-Duffie uninsurable idiosyncratic risk, and the Beeler-Campbell empirical horse race.

  • Prerequisites: AP3 Consumption-Based Pricing & Its Puzzles
  • Unlocks: CAP3 Qualifying Examination
  • Project: Canonical model horse race: simulate Campbell-Cochrane, Bansal-Yaron, and Barro-Rietz, score on 12 data moments, and defend findings against rubric review

70 h lessons · 30 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of AP4 you can:

  1. Formulate the Campbell-Cochrane (1999) external habit model, derive the non-linear sensitivity function \(\lambda(s_t)\) that guarantees an analytically constant risk-free rate, and solve for equilibrium price-dividend ratios as a function of the surplus consumption ratio.
  2. Construct the Bansal-Yaron (2004) long-run risks economy under Epstein-Zin recursive preferences, solve analytical Campbell-Shiller valuation coefficients \(A_1, A_2\), and prove why an elasticity of intertemporal substitution \(\psi > 1\) is mandatory for early resolution of uncertainty.
  3. Calibrate the Barro-Rietz (1988, 2006) rare disaster economy under compound Poisson jump processes, and demonstrate how macroeconomic tail risk resolves the equity premium and risk-free rate puzzles with modest risk aversion (\(\gamma \approx 4\)).
  4. Derive the Constantinides-Duffie (1996) incomplete-markets aggregation theorem, proving that persistent, countercyclical idiosyncratic labor income risk generates a large equity premium without aggregate consumption volatility.
  5. Execute the Beeler-Campbell (2012) moment-matching econometric assessment across 12 canonical macro-finance data moments, evaluating predictive regressions, persistence, and excess volatility.
  6. Arbitrate the foundational doctoral debate: “Is the equity premium a risk premium?”, distinguishing rational macroeconomic compensation from behavioral overreaction and institutional market segmentation.

Prerequisites

AP4 builds upon AP3. Concepts used without definition include:

  • From AP3: consumption CAPM, equity premium puzzle, risk-free rate puzzle, Hansen-Jagannathan bound, and Lucas tree model.
  • From E3 & AP1: CRRA utility, Epstein-Zin preferences, elasticity of intertemporal substitution, and stochastic discount factor.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions covering the prerequisite closure and two skill checks testing the implementation skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 of 10 correct including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 1). In the Campbell-Cochrane habit model, the steady-state surplus consumption ratio is \(\bar{S} = \sigma \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{1 - \phi}}\). Given consumption volatility \(\sigma = 0.0150\), curvature \(\gamma = 2.0\), and persistence \(\phi = 0.87\), compute \(\bar{S}\) to four decimal places.

    \(\bar{S} = 0.0150 \times \sqrt{2.0 / (1 - 0.87)} = 0.0150 \times \sqrt{2.0 / 0.13} = 0.0150 \times \sqrt{15.3846} = 0.0150 \times 3.9223 \approx 0.0588\).

  2. Skill check (rung 2). In the 12-moment evaluation scorecard, compute the annualized Sharpe ratio for an equity excess return series with sample mean \(\hat{\E}[R^e] = 0.0620\) and sample standard deviation \(\hat{\sigma}(R^e) = 0.1940\) to two decimal places.

    \(\text{SR} = \hat{\E}[R^e] / \hat{\sigma}(R^e) = 0.0620 / 0.1940 \approx 0.3196 \approx 0.32\).

  3. From AP3: What empirical finding constitutes the Mehra-Prescott (1985) equity premium puzzle?

    Options

    Under CRRA preferences, \(\E[R^e] \approx \gamma \Cov(\Delta c, R^e) \le \gamma \sigma(\Delta c) \sigma(R)\). Because aggregate consumption growth is smooth (\(\sigma(\Delta c) \approx 1.5\%\) to \(3.5\%\)), explaining a 6% premium requires \(\gamma > 30\) (AP3).

  4. From AP3: What is the Weil (1989) risk-free rate puzzle?

    Options

    In standard CRRA utility, \(\text{EIS} = 1/\gamma\). High \(\gamma\) implies near-zero EIS, so consumers resist postponing consumption during positive growth, driving the equilibrium risk-free rate to extreme levels (AP3).

  5. From AP3: The Hansen-Jagannathan (1991) volatility bound demonstrates that the Sharpe ratio of any asset provides a lower bound on which quantity?

    Options

    From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the Euler equation \(\E[M R^e] = 0\), \(|\E[R^e]| / \sigma(R^e) \le \sigma(M) / \E[M]\) (AP3).

  6. From E3: How do Epstein-Zin recursive preferences decouple risk preferences from intertemporal substitution?

    Options

    Epstein-Zin preferences separate relative risk aversion \(\gamma\) (governing aversion to static cross-state gambles) from the elasticity of intertemporal substitution \(\psi\) (governing willingness to shift consumption across time) (E3).

  7. From E3: Under Epstein-Zin recursive preferences with \(\theta = \frac{1-\gamma}{1-1/\psi}\), when does an agent exhibit a preference for early resolution of uncertainty?

    Options

    An agent prefers early resolution of uncertainty if and only if \(\gamma > 1/\psi\), meaning risk aversion exceeds the inverse of the EIS (E3).

  8. From AP1: In a complete market, why is the stochastic discount factor \(M\) unique?

    Options

    By the Riesz representation theorem, when payoffs span \(L^2\), the orthogonal subspace \(\underline{X}^\perp\) contains only the zero vector, making the SDF unique (AP1).

  9. From AP3: In the Lucas (1978) tree economy with i.i.d. consumption growth and log utility (\(\gamma = 1\)), how does the price-dividend ratio behave?

    Options

    With log utility, income and substitution effects exactly cancel, locking the price-dividend ratio to \(\beta/(1-\beta)\) (AP3).

  10. From S5: In GMM estimation of asset pricing Euler equations, what is the asymptotic distribution of Hansen's J-statistic under the null hypothesis?

    Options

    Under efficient weighting \(W = S^{-1}\), the minimized GMM quadratic form converges to \(\chi^2(K - P)\) (S5).

Lessons

  1. 1 The Benchmark Failure & Demands on a Resolution 10 h

    Revisiting the equity premium, risk-free rate, and excess volatility puzzles, Hansen-Jagannathan frontier diagnostics, and the taxonomy of candidate resolutions.

  2. 2 External Habit Formation: The Campbell–Cochrane Model 10 h

    Slow-moving surplus consumption ratio \(S_t\), the non-linear sensitivity function \(\lambda(s_t)\), constant risk-free rates, and countercyclical risk aversion.

  3. 3 Recursive Preferences & Long-Run Risks: The Bansal–Yaron Model 10 h

    Epstein-Zin preferences, small persistent growth component \(x_t\), fluctuating uncertainty \(\sigma_t^2\), Campbell-Shiller coefficient derivations, and early resolution of uncertainty.

  4. 4 Rare Disasters & Tail Risk: Rietz, Barro, and Wachter 10 h

    Rietz peso problem, Barro cross-country disaster calibration, compound Poisson Lucas trees, solving puzzles with \(\gamma = 4\), and Wachter time-varying disaster probability.

  5. 5 Heterogeneous Agents & Incomplete Markets: Constantinides–Duffie 10 h

    Uninsurable idiosyncratic labor risk, exact aggregation theorem, countercyclical cross-sectional variance, and why borrowing constraints fail without persistent shocks.

  6. 6 Econometric Evaluation: Moment Matching & the Beeler–Campbell Critique 10 h

    Simulated moment matching methodology, the 12 canonical macro-finance data moments, and the Beeler & Campbell (2012) empirical critique of long-run risk.

  7. 7 Is the Equity Premium a Risk Premium? Canonical Synthesis & Debate 10 h

    Comparing the four paradigms, market segmentation, behavioral expectations, and the formal doctoral debate brief: “Is the equity premium a risk premium?”.
