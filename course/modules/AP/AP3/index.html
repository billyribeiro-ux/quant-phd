Consumption-Based Pricing & Its Puzzles

The macroeconomic microfoundations of asset pricing: Lucas exchange economies, Breeden continuous-time consumption CAPM, Hansen-Singleton nonlinear GMM econometrics, the Mehra-Prescott equity-premium puzzle, the Weil risk-free-rate puzzle, and the model-free Hansen-Jagannathan volatility frontier.

  • Prerequisites: AP1 No-Arbitrage & the SDF, S5 GMM & Cross-Sectional Econometrics
  • Unlocks: AP4 Resolving the Puzzles, AP5 Present Value & Return Predictability, CAP3 Qualifying Examination
  • Project: Euler-equation GMM & Hansen-Jagannathan engine: automated test suite in ccapm_engine.py and technical evaluation memo in report.md

72 h lessons · 28 h project (28%)

Objectives

By the end of AP3 you can:

  1. Formulate the pure exchange equilibrium in a Lucas tree model, prove the existence and uniqueness of the pricing function using the contraction mapping theorem, and solve for the equilibrium price-dividend ratio on discrete Markov chains.
  2. Derive Breeden's continuous-time consumption CAPM from the intertemporal envelope condition, and prove that the single consumption beta collapses Merton's multi-beta state-variable hedging factors.
  3. Estimate nonlinear consumption Euler equations via two-step Generalized Method of Moments (Hansen & Singleton 1982) with instrumental variables, and execute the Hansen J-test of overidentifying restrictions.
  4. Calibrate the Mehra-Prescott (1985) two-state Markov endowment economy, and prove mathematically why historical equity returns cannot be reconciled with smooth consumption data under admissible risk aversion (\(\gamma \le 10\)).
  5. Diagnose the Weil (1989) risk-free-rate puzzle, demonstrating why separating relative risk aversion from the elasticity of intertemporal substitution is essential to prevent counterfactually high equilibrium interest rates.
  6. Construct the nonparametric Hansen-Jagannathan (1991) dual volatility bound from asset returns, and evaluate whether candidate macro-finance discount factors lie within the admissible frontier.

Prerequisites

AP3 builds upon AP1 and S5. The concepts used without definition include:

  • From AP1: stochastic discount factor, law of one price, minimum-variance SDF, and SDF mean-variance frontier.
  • From S5: generalized method of moments, efficient GMM weighting matrix, and Hansen's J-test.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions covering the prerequisite closure and two skill checks testing the implementation skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 of 10 correct including both skill checks).

  1. Skill check (rung 1). For an agent with discount factor \(\beta = 0.98\) and risk aversion \(\gamma = 2.0\), if consumption growth is \(c_{t+1}/c_t = 1.05\) and a gross return is \(R_{t+1} = 1.155\), compute the pricing residual \(u_{t+1} = \beta (c_{t+1}/c_t)^{-\gamma} R_{t+1} - 1.0\) to two decimal places.

    \(u_{t+1} = 0.98 \times (1.05)^{-2} \times 1.155 - 1.0 = 0.98 \times 0.907029 \times 1.155 - 1.0 = 1.0267 - 1.0 \approx 0.0267 \approx 0.03\).

  2. Skill check (rung 2). Under the Hansen-Jagannathan bound for a single risky asset with excess return mean \(\E[R^e] = 0.08\) and standard deviation \(\sigma(R^e) = 0.20\), what is the minimum Sharpe ratio bound \(\sigma(M)/\E[M]\)?

    \(\sigma(M)/\E[M] \ge |\E[R^e]|/\sigma(R^e) = 0.08 / 0.20 = 0.40\).

  3. From AP1: Under the Law of One Price without complete markets, what is the geometric relationship between the payoff space and the set of valid SDFs?

    Options

    By the Riesz representation theorem, every valid SDF can be decomposed as \(m = m^\ast + \epsilon\) where \(m^\ast \in \underline{X}\) and \(\epsilon \perp \underline{X}\) (AP1).

  4. From S5: In Generalized Method of Moments estimation with \(K\) moment conditions and \(P\) parameters where \(K > P\), what is the optimal weighting matrix \(W^\ast\)?

    Options

    Hansen (1982) proves that setting \(W = S^{-1}\) minimizes the asymptotic covariance matrix of the GMM parameter estimates (S5).

  5. From S5: What is the asymptotic distribution of Hansen's J-statistic under the null hypothesis of valid moment conditions?

    Options

    The test statistic \(J = T g_T(\hat{\theta})^\top S_T^{-1} g_T(\hat{\theta})\) converges in distribution to \(\chi^2(K - P)\) where \(K - P\) is the number of overidentifying restrictions (S5).

  6. From AP1: If an asset has gross return \(R_{i, t+1}\) and there exists a strictly positive SDF \(M_{t+1}\), what is the fundamental pricing equation?

    Options

    By definition of the stochastic discount factor, the price of a payoff equals its expected discounted value: \(p_t = \E_t[M_{t+1} x_{t+1}]\), which simplifies to \(\E_t[M_{t+1} R_{i, t+1}] = 1\) for returns (AP1).

  7. From AP1: What is the risk-neutral probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) in relation to the physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) and the SDF \(M\)?

    Options

    The normalized SDF \(M / \E[M] = M R_f\) acts as the change of measure density \(d\mathbb{Q}/d\mathbb{P}\) (AP1).

  8. From S5: When instruments \(z_t\) are predetermined at time \(t\), why does the conditional moment restriction \(\E_t[u_{t+1}(\theta)] = 0\) imply unconditional moment restrictions?

    Options

    Conditioning on \(\mathcal{F}_t\) and applying iterated expectations allows any \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable function \(z_t\) to form valid unconditional moments (S5).

  9. From AP1: The Sharpe ratio of any asset or portfolio \(p\) is bounded by the volatility of the stochastic discount factor according to which fundamental inequality?

    Options

    From \(\E[M R^e] = 0 \implies |\E[R^e]| \le \sigma(M) \sigma(R^e) / \E[M]\), which yields \(|SR| \le \sigma(M)/\E[M]\) (AP1).

  10. From S5: In empirical asset pricing, what is the primary diagnostic hazard of using too many weak instrumental variables in GMM?

    Options

    Many weak instruments overfit the sample moments, artificially deflating the J-test statistic and biasing parameter estimates (S5).

Lessons

  1. 1 The Lucas Tree & Pure Exchange Equilibrium 12 h

    Markov endowment dynamics, the Lucas asset pricing operator, contraction mappings, and CRRA equilibrium price-dividend ratios.

  2. 2 The Consumption CAPM & Envelope Conditions 12 h

    Breeden continuous-time intertemporal asset pricing, the envelope theorem, consumption beta, and the collapse of Merton's ICAPM.

  3. 3 Euler-Equation GMM & the Hansen J-Test 12 h

    Nonlinear rational expectations econometrics, instrumental variable moment conditions, two-step efficient weighting, and the overidentifying J-test.

  4. 4 The Equity-Premium Puzzle 12 h

    Mehra-Prescott two-state Markov calibration, aggregate consumption smoothness, Sharpe ratio bounds, and the requirement of \(\gamma > 30\).

  5. 5 The Risk-Free-Rate Puzzle & Preferences 12 h

    Weil's critique of CRRA power utility, the structural conflation of risk aversion and intertemporal substitution, and recursive Epstein-Zin utility.

  6. 6 The Hansen-Jagannathan Volatility Bound 12 h

    Dual projection theorem, model-free SDF volatility cups, Sharpe ratio envelope duality, and testing macro discount factors against the frontier.
