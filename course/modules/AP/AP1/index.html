No-Arbitrage & the Stochastic Discount Factor

Every asset pricing model is a claim about one random variable, the stochastic discount factor. This module proves where it comes from: the law of one price makes prices linear, no arbitrage makes them positive, and the geometry of expectations turns them into the equation \(p = \E[mx]\). It proves which SDF the prices identify, how volatile any SDF must be, how the mean-variance frontier looks from its side, and what risk-neutral pricing is. Then it recovers an SDF from fourteen years of currency data and measures how badly it prices the next fourteen.

  • Prerequisites: M6 Measure-Theoretic Probability, AP2 Mean-Variance, CAPM, ICAPM & APT
  • Unlocks: AP3 Consumption-Based Pricing & Its Puzzles, AP5 Return Predictability & Present Values
  • Project: SDF recovery: code autograded on your own instance, and a report with a proof appendix graded by rubric

84 h lessons · 36 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of AP1 you can:

  1. Prove that the law of one price is equivalent to a linear pricing functional on the payoff space and to the existence of state prices, and compute the dimension of the set of state-price vectors.
  2. Prove the fundamental theorem of asset pricing for finitely many states by separating a subspace from a simplex, and extend it to a finite multiperiod tree.
  3. Formulate arbitrage detection as a linear program, verify its certificates in both directions, and compute the arbitrage bounds on a new payoff by super-replication duality.
  4. Prove the existence and uniqueness of the SDF in the payoff space and that \(x^\ast\) is the projection of every SDF, and construct a market without arbitrage in which \(x^\ast\) is negative.
  5. Derive the minimum-variance SDF with a given mean, the variance bound and the frontier \(R^\ast + wR^{e\ast}\), and relate each to the Sharpe ratio and to beta pricing.
  6. Translate between strictly positive SDFs and equivalent martingale measures, and state the general fundamental theorems with their exact hypotheses.
  7. Estimate the SDF from data, prove that its in-sample fit is uninformative and its second moment biased upward, and measure its out-of-sample pricing errors.

Prerequisites

AP1 assumes M6 (Measure-Theoretic Probability) and AP2 (Mean-Variance, CAPM, ICAPM & APT), and everything they rest on. These are the concepts it uses without redefining them:

  • From M6: probability spaces, the Radon–Nikodym theorem, conditional expectation as an \(L^2\) projection, filtrations, martingales, stopping times, optional stopping and doubling strategies.
  • From AP2: the mean-variance frontier, the minimum-variance portfolio, the tangency portfolio, two-fund separation and beta.
  • From M3 and M7, through AP2: vector spaces, linear maps, orthogonal projection, least squares as projection, convex sets, convex functions and Lagrangian duality.
  • From S1 and M4: the Sharpe ratio, expectation, covariance matrices, the law of large numbers and consistent estimators.

Placement diagnostic

Eleven questions on the prerequisites (M6, AP2 and what they rest on) and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1–2 of the project teach. Skip rungs 1–2 only if you reach the 80% bar and answer both skill checks; then use the lessons' problem sets to fill gaps. Below the bar, revisit M6 and AP2 first.

  1. On a finite probability space with \(\Prob(\{\omega\}) > 0\) for every \(\omega\), a probability \(\mathbb Q\) has a Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to \(\Prob\) equal to…

    Options

    \(\mathbb Q(A) = \sum_{\omega \in A}\frac{\mathbb Q(\{\omega\})}{\Prob(\{\omega\})}\Prob(\{\omega\})\) (M6).

  2. \(\E[X \mid \mathcal G]\) for \(X \in L^2\) is…

    Options

    M6 constructs conditional expectation this way; AP1 projects onto the payoff space instead.

  3. A process \((M_t)\) adapted to \((\mathcal F_t)\) with \(\E|M_t| < \infty\) is a martingale if…

    Options

    The definition from M6.

  4. A doubling bettor on a fair coin stops at the first win or after 2 flips. With stakes 1 and 2, what is the probability of ending with a gain of +1? (two decimals)

    \(1 - 2^{-2} = 0.75\); otherwise the loss is 3, and the expected gain is \(0.75 - 0.25 \times 3 = 0\) (M6).

  5. Optional stopping, \(\E[M_\tau] = M_0\), can fail for…

    Options

    The bounded case always holds (M6); the doubling strategy is the standard failure.

  6. With a riskless asset, every mean-variance efficient portfolio is a combination of…

    Options

    Two-fund separation (AP2).

  7. A portfolio has an expected excess return of 0.5% and a volatility of 4% per period. What is its Sharpe ratio per period? (three decimals)

    \(0.005/0.04 = 0.125\) (S1, AP2).

  8. The beta of asset \(i\) on a portfolio \(R_p\) is…

    Options

    The regression slope (AP2).

  9. For a matrix \(A\) with linearly independent columns, the orthogonal projection of \(y\) onto the column space of \(A\) is…

    Options

    Least squares as projection (M3).

  10. Which set is convex?

    Options

    The simplex is an intersection of half-spaces and a hyperplane (M7). The sphere is not convex; the third set is two convex branches, not one convex set.

  11. Two assets have zero correlation and variance 0.04 each. What is the variance of the global minimum-variance portfolio? (two decimals)

    By symmetry the weights are equal, so the variance is \(0.5^2 imes 0.04 + 0.5^2 imes 0.04 = 0.02\) (AP2).

  12. Skill check (rung 1). Three equally likely states and one traded payoff \((2, 1, 0)\) with price 0.9. What is the value of \(x^\ast\), the SDF in the payoff space, in state 1? (two decimals)

    \(x^\ast = cz\) with \(c\,\E[z^2] = 0.9\) and \(\E[z^2] = 5/3\), so \(c = 0.54\) and \(x^\ast_1 = 1.08\) (L04, Problem M2). Using \(\E[z]\) instead of \(\E[z^2]\) gives 1.8.

  13. Skill check (rung 2). One asset with price 1 pays 1.2 and 0.9 in two observed months. What is the sample \(x^\ast\) weight \(b\), with sample moments dividing by \(T\)? (six decimals)

    \(\hat S = (1.44 + 0.81)/2 = 1.125\), \(b = 1/1.125 = 0.888889\) (L07, Problem M1). Dividing by \(T - 1\) gives 0.444444.

Lessons

Seven lessons of twelve study hours each. Every lesson has graded claims, complete proofs, runnable labs, a problem set of at least twelve problems with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 12 hPayoffs, Prices and the Law of One PriceThe one-period market, the law of one price as a kernel condition, its equivalence with linear pricing and state prices, and nearly redundant currency payoffs. L02 · 12 hNo Arbitrage and the Fundamental TheoremArbitrage, a separation lemma proved from compactness, and the fundamental theorem for finitely many states with a complete proof that a lab runs. L03 · 12 hArbitrage as a Linear ProgramThe detection program, certificates in both directions, the state-price margin, and super-replication duality with the arbitrage interval of a new payoff. L04 · 12 hThe Stochastic Discount Factor\(p = \E[mx]\), existence and uniqueness in the payoff space by Riesz representation, \(x^\ast\) as the projection of every SDF, and a negative \(x^\ast\). L05 · 12 hThe Minimum-Variance SDF and the Frontier\(R^\ast\), the minimum-variance SDF with a given mean, the variance bound, the frontier \(R^\ast + wR^{e\ast}\) and beta pricing. L06 · 12 hMartingale Measures and the General TheoremRisk-neutral probabilities as rescaled SDFs, the fundamental theorem on a finite tree, doubling, and the general theorems with their hypotheses. L07 · 12 hRecovering the SDF from DataThe sample \(x^\ast\): exact in-sample pricing, consistency, upward bias by convexity, ridge shrinkage, and out-of-sample errors on currency data.

The project ladder

The module ends in SDF recovery. At the master's tier the scaffolded rungs are deliberately short.

  1. Worked example

    \(x^\ast\) for Lesson 4's three-state market and for a sample drawn from it, annotated with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The two lines that compute the sample \(x^\ast\) weights, removed. It is graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement sdf_recovery.py, autograded on your own seeded instance; then a report on nine currency payoffs with a proof appendix, graded by rubric against annotated exemplars.

  4. Stretch

    Separate estimation error from change in the economy, roll the recovery, impose positivity, and rebuild the payoffs with deposit interest.

How this module is verified

Every claim carries an evidence grade and a key from the verified citation registry. Every theorem is proved on the page or cited with a locator. Every lab's output is produced by running its code, and every lab runs both in your browser and in the program's test suite. The autograder passes the reference solution on 50 seeds. An independent verifier, never the author, checks each claim, number, proof and test, writes deliberately wrong solutions that the autograder must fail, and records the result in the module's verification ledger.
