Market Efficiency: The Full Debate

Informational efficiency revisited with the full theoretical and econometric toolkit: the insurmountable joint-hypothesis problem, the impossibility of informational perfection and modern efficiently-inefficient equilibrium models, cross-sectional bootstrap methodologies distinguishing luck from skill in institutional money management, the rational economics of active management, and an adversarial doctoral debate brief arbitrating neoclassical and behavioral asset pricing paradigms.

Prerequisites: AP7 (Behavioral Finance), FC3 (Market Efficiency Primer)
Unlocks: CAP2 (The Great Debates), CAP3 (Qualifying Examination)
Project: Debate brief: Are markets efficient? (rubric-graded)

36 h lessons across 4 lessons @ 9 h · 14 h project (28.0% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of AP8 you can:

  1. Formulate the joint-hypothesis problem analytically and evaluate how equilibrium expected return models condition every empirical test of market efficiency.
  2. Derive the competitive rational expectations equilibrium with costly information acquisition and explain why equilibrium markets are efficiently inefficient.
  3. Implement the Fama & French (2010) cross-sectional bootstrap algorithm to distinguish true manager skill from sampling variation while preserving residual cross-correlation.
  4. Analyze the Berk & Green (2004) and Berk & van Binsbergen (2015) active management equilibrium, proving why competitive capital flows drive net alpha to zero while gross value added measures skill.
  5. Deconstruct published anomaly evidence, distinguishing between rational macroeconomic distress covariance, behavioral mispricing resolution, and data-snooping artifacts.
  6. Synthesize a doctoral-standard adversarial debate brief on market efficiency that defends both affirmative and negative resolutions and cross-examines opposing evidence.

Prerequisites

AP8 assumes mastery of AP7 (Behavioral Finance), FC3 (Market Efficiency Evidence Primer), and their prerequisite closure {AP1, AP2, AP5, E1, E2, E3, E4, FC1, FC2, S1, S2, S3}. It relies on these concepts without redefining them:

  • From FC3 & AP7: efficient-markets hypothesis, EMH forms, joint-hypothesis problem, arithmetic of active management, noise-trader risk, survey expectations, and risk vs. mispricing.
  • From E4: Grossman-Stiglitz paradox, rational expectations equilibrium, and price informativeness.
  • From AP1 & AP2: stochastic discount factor, CAPM, linear factor model, and Jensen's alpha.
  • From S1 & S2: bootstrap, hypothesis test, p-value, OLS estimator, and t-statistic.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite foundations from AP7, FC3, E4, and AP2, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. Why does testing market efficiency inherently require testing a joint hypothesis?

    Options

    Fama (1970, 1991) proved that because fundamental value is not directly observable, any test of pricing efficiency is simultaneously a test of the chosen asset pricing model.

  2. What constitutes the core contradiction of the Grossman-Stiglitz (1980) information paradox?

    Options

    Costly information acquisition requires an equilibrium return spread to reward informed searchers; perfectly revealing prices destroy this compensation.

  3. In Sharpe's (1991) arithmetic of active management, what must be true of actively managed portfolios in aggregate?

    Options

    By market-clearing accounting, passive investors hold market weights; active investors must sum to the remaining market, yielding identical gross weighted returns.

  4. How does noise-trader risk (De Long et al. 1990) limit the aggressiveness of rational arbitrageurs?

    Options

    Resale-price risk generated by persistent stochastic sentiment shocks creates fundamental volatility that risk-averse, short-horizon arbitrageurs cannot ignore.

  5. What empirical empirical phenomenon is documented by Greenwood & Shleifer (2014) regarding investor return surveys?

    Options

    Investors report the highest subjective return expectations at cyclical peaks when dividend yields are lowest and future realized returns are poorest.

  6. In Carhart's (1997) mutual fund evaluation, what factor explains the apparent persistence in top-decile fund returns?

    Options

    Carhart (1997) proved that 1-year winning funds held recent stock market winners, eliminating alpha persistence once controlling for momentum.

  7. Why does independent resampling of individual funds fail in evaluating mutual fund alpha distributions?

    Options

    Fama & French (2010) show that destroying contemporaneous cross-correlation inflates t-statistics and severely underestimates the spread of lucky performers.

  8. How does the Daniel & Titman (1997) test evaluate whether value is a risk factor or a characteristic?

    Options

    Holding characteristics fixed while varying factor loadings reveals whether expected returns co-move with covariance risk or with firm characteristics.

  9. Skill Check 1 (Debate Brief Architecture): When constructing an institutional debate brief on market efficiency, what requirement must every substantive empirical claim satisfy to meet the doctoral standard?

    Options

    Doctoral debate briefs require rigorous provenance: claims must be grounded in verified citations with observable data-locators and precise methodology specifications.

  10. Skill Check 2 (Adversarial Cross-Examination): In an adversarial cross-examination of an empirical asset pricing claim, what constitutes a valid methodological deconstruction of an anomaly finding?

    Options

    Valid adversarial cross-examinations isolate methodological vulnerabilities (joint-hypothesis confluences, implementation costs, survivorship, data-mining) and ground them in published evidence.

Lesson map

Four lessons totaling 36 study hours develop the complete theoretical and empirical architecture of the market efficiency debate:

  1. L01 · The Joint-Hypothesis Problem & Efficiently Inefficient Markets

    Fama's joint-hypothesis problem, information aggregation in competitive markets, the Grossman-Stiglitz information impossibility paradox, and Pedersen's modern efficiently-inefficient equilibrium framework. Introduces efficiently-inefficient-markets.

    9 study hours · 1 inline lab · 12 practice problems · 10-item quiz

  2. L02 · Cross-Sectional Bootstrap & Fund Skill

    The distribution of mutual fund performance, sampling variation and extreme lucky winners, the Kosowski et al. and Fama & French (2010) cross-sectional bootstrap methodology, preserving residual cross-correlation, and empirical evidence on active fund skill. Introduces bootstrap-skill-test.

    9 study hours · 1 inline lab · 12 practice problems · 10-item quiz

  3. L03 · The Equilibrium Economics of Active Management

    Sharpe's arithmetic of active management reconciled with rational equilibrium, the Berk & Green (2004) and Berk & van Binsbergen (2015) structural models, decreasing returns to scale, fee rent extraction, and why net alpha fails to measure skill while dollar value added measures economic surplus. Introduces active-management-economics.

    9 study hours · 1 inline lab · 12 practice problems · 10-item quiz

  4. L04 · The Market Efficiency Debate: Synthesis & Cross-Examination

    The Grand Synthesis: Neoclassical multi-factor risk versus behavioral finance and limits to arbitrage. Post-publication anomaly decay, survey expectations puzzles, earnings announcement returns, the full unit debate brief, and empirical settling conditions.

    9 study hours · 1 inline lab · 12 practice problems · 10-item quiz

The four-rung project ladder

The project (project.html) requires 14 study hours and culminates in an institutional-grade debate brief defending both sides of market efficiency, supported by an adversarial cross-examination of each side's strongest empirical evidence.

  1. Worked example

    Lab 1 on the project page provides a fully solved institutional debate brief instance evaluating affirmative and negative resolutions, audited by a Python validation suite for citation integrity, evidence grades, and cross-examination symmetry.

  2. Completion

    Lab 2 provides an interactive completion problem where you implement the cross-examination scoring metric, Bayesian likelihood ratio updating across empirical findings, and operational settling condition certification.

  3. Independent

    Author a complete doctoral debate brief on the resolution: 'Markets are efficient'. Write both sides to the program's debate standard, cross-examine the opposing side's three strongest empirical pillars against our 16-question attack bank, and self-assess against rubric criteria C1–C5.

  4. Stretch

    Construct a structural equilibrium calibration matching empirical anomaly decay rates with prime broker borrowing costs and leverage constraints, or stage a live cross-examination defense against a hostile committee.
