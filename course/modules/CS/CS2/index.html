Algorithms & Data Structures

A trading system stores orders, schedules events, and summarizes streams of prices, and each of these tasks has a cost that grows with the number of items. This module gives you the tools to state that cost exactly, to prove the bound, and to check the bound with counted operations. It covers operation counts and big-O notation, hash maps, binary heaps and the event queue, comparison sorting and binary search, balanced search trees, graph traversal, dynamic programming, the order-book data structure, Welford's online mean and variance, the monotonic deque, and a streaming quantile sketch. You leave able to prove an operation bound, to implement the structures in Python, and to test them against brute force, ending in the streaming statistics library.

  • Prerequisites: CS1 Programming in Python from Zero, and M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning. P0.1 supplies the evidence grades and the project ladder.
  • Project: Streaming statistics library, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. Every stream, key set, event log and graph is generated from a seed and labelled SIMULATED on the page that uses it.

100 h lessons · 30 h project (23%)

Objectives

By the end of CS2 you can:

  1. State big-O notation with a witness and a threshold, and prove a bound on an operation count, including the bound that a quadratic count does not satisfy (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the expected search cost of a chained hash map under a stated hashing assumption, and show a key set on which an unsuccessful search makes one probe per stored key (Lesson 2).
  3. Implement a binary min-heap and an event queue ordered by time and sequence number, and prove their operation bounds (Lesson 3).
  4. Prove the comparison lower bound for sorting, and bound the probes of binary search (Lesson 4).
  5. Prove the height bound of a balanced search tree, and check its invariant after each insertion (Lesson 5).
  6. Compute breadth-first and depth-first search, and solve a dynamic program by memoized recursion and by table fill, checking both against exhaustive search (Lessons 6 and 7).
  7. Prove the update formulas of Welford's method by induction, and prove the amortized bound of a monotonic deque and the error bound of a quantile sketch (Lessons 9, 10 and 11).

Prerequisites

CS2 assumes CS1 and M1, and it uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From CS1: built-in data structures, classes and objects, type hints, exceptions, the simple return and unit tests.
  • From M1: the exponential and the logarithm, direct proof, proof by contradiction, proof by induction and the geometric series.
  • Secondary-school algebra and the vocabulary of arrays, trees and graphs, used as prose. Each one is explained where a lesson first uses it.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on the prerequisites, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. A result below the bar shows which prerequisite to review before the first lesson.

  1. What does a type hint do when a Python function is called?

    Options

    Type hints are checked by a static checker such as mypy. CPython does not enforce them when the function runs (CS1, type hints).

  2. What is \(\log_2 1024\)?

    \(2^{10} = 1024\), so \(\log_2 1024 = 10\) (M1, exponential and logarithm).

  3. Which two parts does a proof by induction have?

    Options

    The base case checks the first value, and the inductive step passes the statement from one value to the next (M1, induction).

  4. What is \(1 + 2 + \dots + 10\)?

    \(10 \cdot 11 / 2 = 55\), the sum formula for the first integers.

  5. What does a unit test check?

    Options

    A unit test is a small, repeatable check of one function or class (CS1, unit test).

  6. A price rises from 100 to 110. What is the simple return, as a decimal?

    The simple return is \((110 - 100)/100 = 0.1\) (CS1, simple return).

  7. Which keyword raises an exception in Python?

    Options

    Python uses raise (CS1, exceptions).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): the list is \([5, 2, 8, 1]\), and the window has length 3. What is the minimum of the first window, \([5, 2, 8]\)?

    The minimum of \([5, 2, 8]\) is 2. Rung 1 computes a rolling minimum of this kind, window by window.

  9. Skill check (rung 2): with \(n\) values seen and running mean \(m\), a new value \(x\) arrives. Which expression is the updated mean?

    Options

    The mean of \(n + 1\) values is \((nm + x)/(n + 1) = m + (x - m)/(n + 1)\). Rung 2 completes this update in the project.

Lessons

Eleven lessons, of 7 to 10 study hours each, sum to the 100 lesson hours on this page. The hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Lessons 1 to 3 cover operation counts, hash maps, and the heap and the event queue. Lessons 4 to 8 cover sorting, search trees, graphs, dynamic programming and the order book. Lessons 9 to 11 cover the streaming statistics that the project asks for. The lesson pages share one structure: graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 8 hComplexity and ArraysOperation counts, big-O notation by witnesses and thresholds, and the bounds of a maximum scan and a nested loop. L02 · 9 hHash MapsChained buckets, probe counts, the expected search cost under a stated hashing assumption, and a colliding key set. L03 · 10 hBinary Heaps and the Event QueueThe binary min-heap in an array, its height and operation bounds, and the event queue with a time and sequence key, compared with a sorted list. L04 · 10 hComparison Sorting and Binary SearchThe decision-tree lower bound for comparison sorting, and the probe count of binary search. L05 · 9 hBalanced Search TreesAVL trees, their balance invariant under insertion with rotations, and the height bound. L06 · 9 hGraph TraversalBreadth-first and depth-first search, shortest hop counts, and the parenthesis property of discovery and finish times. L07 · 10 hDynamic ProgrammingMemoized recursion and table fill for two problems, checked against exhaustive search on small instances. L08 · 10 hOrder Books: Price Levels with FIFO QueuesInteger-cent price levels with first-in first-out queues, price-time priority, and best bid and ask. L09 · 10 hWelford's Online Mean and VarianceThe one-pass update of the mean and the sum of squared deviations, proved by induction and tested for numerical error. L10 · 8 hMonotonic Deque for Rolling Minimum and MaximumThe monotonic deque, its amortized bound on operations, and rolling extremes checked against brute force. L11 · 7 hStreaming Quantile SketchA deterministic quantile sketch, its rank-error guarantee, and a check of the guarantee on a seeded stream.

The project ladder

The module ends in the streaming statistics library. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated rolling minimum on a seeded series, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The Welford update with the mean and sum-of-squares lines removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement streamstats.py with the Welford accumulator, the rolling minimum and maximum, and the quantile sketch, then write the proof and the design note. Graded by the autograder and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a time-decayed Welford accumulator or a merge of two quantile sketches, suitable for your portfolio.
