M&A, LBOs & Restructuring

How do corporate acquisitions, private equity buyouts, and judicial reorganizations reallocate capital and corporate control? This module covers the theoretical mechanisms, empirical evidence, and computational modeling of major corporate control transactions. You will analyze tender offers and free-rider holdout dynamics in the market for corporate control, quantify empirical merger announcement returns, model synergy valuation and overpayment boundaries alongside the hubris hypothesis of takeovers, balance transaction sources and uses of funds under institutional leveraged buyout mechanics, schedule multi-tranche debt amortization and mandatory cash sweeps, compute sponsor multiple on invested capital and decompose returns via LBO value-creation attribution, and navigate priority waterfalls in Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

  • Prerequisites: CF4 Valuation.
  • Project: Full LBO Model and Investment Committee Memorandum (project.html, 20 h).

60 h lessons · 20 h project (25% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CF5 you can:

  1. Formulate the Grossman-Hart takeover model, evaluating tender offer pricing, dilution mechanisms, toeholds, and target shareholder free-riding behavior.
  2. Measure merger announcement cumulative abnormal returns (CAR) for targets, acquirers, and combined entities using event-study methodologies.
  3. Value operational and financial synergies via discounted cash flow, quantify overpayment boundaries, and analyze common-value winner's curse dynamics and managerial hubris.
  4. Construct institutional Sources & Uses schedules for leveraged buyouts, reconciling purchase equity, existing debt, financing fees, and balance sheet cash.
  5. Model multi-tranche debt schedules with contractually enforced mandatory amortization, priority cash sweeps, and debt covenant audits.
  6. Decompose sponsor equity gains into operational EBITDA growth, multiple expansion, and deleveraging using the Kaplan-Strömberg attribution framework.
  7. Audit financial distress waterfalls in Chapter 11 reorganization, determining recovery rates across DIP, secured, unsecured, and equity claims under absolute priority.

Prerequisites

CF5 builds directly on valuation foundations established in CF4 and corporate finance theory from CF2. It assumes these core concepts without redefining them:

  • From CF4: free cash flow to the firm, FCFF DCF valuation, terminal value, and the enterprise-to-equity bridge.
  • From CF2: weighted average cost of capital, interest tax shields, and financial distress costs.
  • From CF1: net present value, internal rate of return, and net working capital.
  • From M1 & S1: present value, geometric series, and event-study abnormal return metrics.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite valuation and capital structure concepts, and two skill checks on the core LBO modeling tasks rungs 1 and 2 teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 when you meet the bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under standard DCF valuation (CF4), how is Enterprise Value converted to Common Equity Value in the balance sheet bridge?

    Options

    Equity value represents the residual claim: Enterprise Value minus Net Debt (Debt less Cash) minus non-common financial obligations.

  2. A target enterprise has an operating income (EBIT) of 160.0 million dollars. With a corporate tax rate of 25.0%, what is its NOPAT (Net Operating Profit After Taxes) in millions of dollars?

    \(\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - \tau_c) = 160.0 \times (1 - 0.25) = 120.0\) million dollars.

  3. Under Modigliani-Miller Proposition II with corporate taxes (CF2), why does levered equity cost of capital increase with leverage?

    Options

    Debt creates contractual prior claims on cash flow, increasing equity beta and expected return volatility.

  4. A firm generates terminal free cash flow to the firm of 80.0 million dollars at horizon \(T=5\). Sustainable long-run growth is 3.0% and WACC is 8.0%. What is its terminal enterprise value at \(T=5\) in millions of dollars?

    \(TV = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{T} \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{80.0 \times 1.03}{0.08 - 0.03} = \frac{82.4}{0.05} = 1648.0\) million dollars. At flat terminal cash flow \(80.0 / 0.05 = 1600.0\).

  5. What is the primary trade-off in the trade-off theory of capital structure (CF2)?

    Options

    Optimal leverage balances marginal tax shield benefits against marginal expected bankruptcy and agency distress costs.

  6. An investment of 100.0 million dollars yields an exit cash flow of 248.832 million dollars at the end of year 5 with no interim distributions. What is its annualized internal rate of return (IRR) as a percentage?

    \(\text{IRR} = (248.832 / 100.0)^{1/5} - 1 = 2.48832^{0.2} - 1 = 1.20 - 1 = 0.20 = 20.0\%\).

  7. Which cash flow measure represents cash available to equity holders after all operating expenses, working capital, capex, and debt service?

    Options

    FCFE equals net income plus D&A minus capex minus delta NWC minus debt principal repayments plus new debt issuance.

  8. Under the Net Present Value (NPV) decision rule (CF1), an independent investment should be accepted when:

    Options

    NPV > 0 guarantees that the project's return exceeds the hurdle opportunity cost of capital, increasing firm wealth.

  9. A buyout requires 900.0 million dollars to purchase equity, 200.0 million to refinance existing debt, 25.0 million in advisory fees, 15.0 million in financing fees, and 10.0 million in minimum cash. Debt financing provides 650.0 million and target cash provides 50.0 million. What is the required sponsor equity check in millions of dollars?

    \(\text{Total Uses} = 900 + 200 + 25 + 15 + 10 = 1150.0\text{M}\). Non-sponsor sources = \(650 + 50 = 700.0\text{M}\). \(\text{Sponsor Equity} = 1150.0 - 700.0 = 450.0\text{M}\).

  10. At year end, cash before sweep is 50.0 million dollars, contractual minimum cash buffer is 15.0 million dollars, and senior debt balance is 80.0 million dollars. Under a 100% cash sweep policy, how much cash is swept to pay down senior debt in millions of dollars?

    \(\text{Excess Cash} = 50.0 - 15.0 = 35.0\text{M}\). Since senior debt (80.0M) > excess cash, the full 35.0M is swept.

Lessons

The module is organized across six lessons totaling 60 study hours:

  1. L01 · Market for Corporate Control & Takeover Premiums

    Tender offer mechanics, Grossman-Hart free-rider holdout dynamics, toeholds, dilution, and empirical announcement abnormal returns for targets and acquirers.

    10 study hours · Introduces market for corporate control, merger announcement returns

  2. L02 · Synergy Valuation, Overpayment & The Winner's Curse

    Discounted cash flow valuation of operational and financial synergies, integration friction, overpayment boundaries, common-value auctions, and Roll's hubris hypothesis.

    10 study hours · Introduces synergy valuation and overpayment, hubris hypothesis of takeovers

  3. L03 · Sources & Uses of Funds, Transaction Structuring & LBO Mechanics

    Anatomy of leveraged buyouts, Sources & Uses identity, senior and subordinated debt tranches, rollover equity, advisory and financing fees, and purchase price accounting.

    10 study hours · Introduces sources and uses of funds, leveraged buyout mechanics

  4. L04 · Debt Schedules, Interest Cascades & Mandatory Cash Sweeps

    Multi-tranche debt scheduling, cash flow available for debt service (CFADS), contractual amortization, priority cash sweeps, circular interest handling, and covenant audits.

    10 study hours · Introduces cash sweep

  5. L05 · Returns Decomposition: MOIC, IRR & Value Creation Attribution

    Private equity performance metrics, MOIC vs. IRR trade-offs, holding period sensitivity, and the Kaplan-Strömberg attribution identity (EBITDA growth, multiples, deleveraging).

    10 study hours · Introduces multiple on invested capital (MOIC), LBO value-creation attribution

  6. L06 · Financial Distress & Chapter 11 Priority Cascades

    Economic vs. liquidity distress, out-of-court workouts, Chapter 11 automatic stay, debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing, plan confirmation, cramdowns, and absolute priority waterfalls.

    10 study hours · Introduces chapter 11 bankruptcy

Project Ladder

The module concludes with the institutional LBO modeling and investment committee project (project.html, 20 hours). Progress through the four-rung ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Fully solved institutional LBO case study: transaction structuring, cash sweep waterfall, and Kaplan-Strömberg attribution.

  2. Completion

    Complete missing formulas for Sources & Uses, priority cash sweep, and returns attribution in the interactive browser lab.

  3. Independent

    Deliver the complete LBO engine (lbo_model.py) verified across 50 seeds and compose an investment committee memo (memo.md).

  4. Stretch

    Extend to dynamic revolving credit facilities, stochastic SOFR rate margin curves, and management rollover catch-up waterfalls.
