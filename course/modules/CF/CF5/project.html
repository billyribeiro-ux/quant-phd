Rungs 1 and 2

Rung 1 · Worked example

A financial sponsor evaluates an LBO of Target Co with LTM EBITDA of $100.0M. The agreed acquisition multiple is \(10.0\times\), establishing an enterprise value of $1,000.0M. Target Co has $200.0M of existing debt to refinance and $50.0M of balance sheet cash. Net debt is $150.0M, implying an equity purchase price of \(1,000.0 - 150.0 = 850.0\) million dollars.

Financing is structured across two tranches: Term Loan A (senior secured, \(3.0\times\) EBITDA = $300.0M) and Term Loan B (subordinated, \(2.0\times\) EBITDA = $200.0M), totaling $500.0M in funded debt (\(5.0\times\) leverage). Transaction advisory fees are 2.0% of enterprise value ($20.0M), and financing fees are 3.0% of funded debt ($15.0M). Minimum operating cash required on the opening balance sheet is $25.0M.

Total uses of funds equal:

\[ \text{Total Uses} = 850.0\text{ (equity)} + 200.0\text{ (debt refi)} + 20.0\text{ (advisory)} + 15.0\text{ (financing)} + 25.0\text{ (cash)} = 1,110.0\text{ million dollars.} \]

Sources comprise funded debt ($500.0M), target cash swept into closing ($50.0M), and sponsor equity plug ($560.0M), satisfying the fundamental Sources & Uses identity: \(\text{Total Sources} = 500.0 + 50.0 + 560.0 = 1,110.0\) million dollars.

Over a 5-year holding period, EBITDA expands from $100.0M to $145.0M through operational improvement. Mandatory amortization and a 100% free cash flow sweep reduce total net debt from $475.0M at closing ($500.0M debt less $25.0M cash) to $120.0M at exit. Exiting at \(11.5\times\) EBITDA yields an exit enterprise value of \(145.0 \times 11.5 = 1,667.5\) million dollars. Deducting ending net debt of $120.0M gives exit equity of $1,547.5M. The sponsor's MOIC is \(1,547.5 / 560.0 = 2.763\times\), yielding an annualized IRR of \(2.763^{1/5} - 1 = 22.54\%\).

def attribute_value_creation(

    total_gain = (exit_ev - exit_net_debt) - (entry_ev - entry_net_debt)

    delta_deleveraging = entry_net_debt - exit_net_debt

Investment Committee Question Bank (15 Attack Angles)

Prepare to defend your investment memo against these 15 hostile review angles:

  1. How does the model handle circular interest calculations if interest expense is computed on average rather than beginning debt balances?
  2. What happens to debt service coverage if SOFR widens by 300 basis points in Year 2?
  3. Why is Term Loan B not permitted to be swept before Term Loan A is retired?
  4. If the seller demands a cash-free, debt-free closing, how does the Sources & Uses schedule adjust?
  5. What is the impact on sponsor IRR if the exit multiple contracts to the entry multiple?
  6. How sensitive is equity value creation to capital expenditure intensity during years 2 and 3?
  7. What covenant breach remedies exist if Year 2 EBITDA falls 15% below budget?
  8. Why are debt financing fees treated as a cash use rather than netted against loan proceeds?
  9. Under what conditions does management rollover equity dilute sponsor returns at exit?
  10. How does working capital seasonality impact the quarterly adequacy of the minimum cash buffer?
  11. If a dividend recapitalization is executed at Year 3, how does it alter the Kaplan-Strömberg attribution?
  12. What structural seniority protects Term Loan A lenders ahead of Term Loan B in Chapter 11?
  13. How does the model account for restricted cash vs. freely accessible operating cash?
  14. Why does the attribution framework evaluate EBITDA growth at the entry multiple rather than exit multiple?
  15. If exit is delayed from Year 5 to Year 7, what happens to MOIC versus IRR?
