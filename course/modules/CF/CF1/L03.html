Inflation and Real Cash Flows

  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

A project's cash flows are stated in the money of the year in which they occur. Inflation changes the value of that money, so a forecast is either nominal or real, and each kind needs its own discount rate. This lesson defines both kinds of flow, derives the exact relation between the nominal and the real rate, and proves that discounting in one set of terms and reading the answer in the other gives the same NPV. It also proves the size of the error when the two are mixed. Lab 1 shows both cases on a SIMULATED project.

1Price levels, real flows and the Fisher relation

Definition 1.1 (price level, nominal and real flows)

Let \(\pi > -1\) be the inflation rate per year, held constant here. The price level in year \(t\), with base year \(0\), is \(P_t = (1+\pi)^t\), so \(P_0 = 1\). A nominal cash flow \(C^n_t\) is the amount paid or received in year \(t\) in that year's money. Its real counterpart is \(C^r_t = C^n_t / P_t\), stated in base-year money. Equivalently, \(C^n_t = C^r_t\,(1+\pi)^t\).

Definition 1.2 (Fisher relation)

The Fisher relation connects a nominal rate \(n\), a real rate \(r\) and an inflation rate \(\pi\), all per year and all greater than \(-1\), by \[ 1 + n = (1+r)(1+\pi). \] Given \(n\) and \(\pi\), it defines the real rate as \(r = (1+n)/(1+\pi) - 1\).

This lesson uses the relation as an exact definition of the real rate for a given inflation rate. Whether real rates are stable over time, or whether nominal rates move one for one with expected inflation, is an empirical question. This lesson does not settle it, and no result below depends on it.

Theorem 1.3 (exact expansion)

Under Definition 1.2, \(n = r + \pi + r\pi\). Hence \(n - (r + \pi) = r\pi\): the additive form \(n \approx r + \pi\) understates the nominal rate by exactly \(r\pi\).

Proof

Expand the product in Definition 1.2: \((1+r)(1+\pi) = 1 + r + \pi + r\pi\). Subtract \(1\) from both sides to get \(n = r + \pi + r\pi\). Subtracting \(r + \pi\) leaves \(r\pi\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.4 (a 6 per cent real rate and 3 per cent inflation)

With \(r = 0.06\) and \(\pi = 0.03\), Definition 1.2 gives \(n = 1.06 \times 1.03 - 1 = 0.0918\), or 9.18 per cent. The additive form gives \(0.09\). The gap is \(0.06 \times 0.03 = 0.0018\), which is 0.18 percentage points. Lab 1 prints this gap as 0.001800.

2Consistent discounting of real and nominal flows

Definition 2.1 (consistent discounting)

Consistent discounting means discounting nominal flows at the nominal rate \(n\), or real flows at the real rate \(r\), where \(n\) and \(r\) are linked by Definition 1.2 for the same inflation rate \(\pi\). Each side is an NPV: the sum of each flow divided by \((1+\text{rate})^t\), where \(t\) is the year and the rate is per year.

Theorem 2.2 (consistency)

Let \(r > -1\) and \(\pi > -1\) be constant, let \(n\) satisfy Definition 1.2, and let \(C^r_t\) be real flows with \(C^n_t = C^r_t (1+\pi)^t\) (Definition 1.1). Then \[ \sum_{t \ge 0} \frac{C^n_t}{(1+n)^t} = \sum_{t \ge 0} \frac{C^r_t}{(1+r)^t}. \] The two sums are the NPV of the nominal flows at the nominal rate and the NPV of the real flows at the real rate.

Proof

By Definition 1.2, \((1+n)^t = (1+r)^t(1+\pi)^t\) for every integer \(t \ge 0\). Therefore \[ \frac{C^n_t}{(1+n)^t} = \frac{C^r_t\,(1+\pi)^t}{(1+r)^t\,(1+\pi)^t} = \frac{C^r_t}{(1+r)^t}. \] Summing over \(t\) gives the claim. \(\blacksquare\) The proof uses only the identity in Definition 1.2 and the nominal flow definition, so it holds for any real flows, of any sign and any length.

Theorem 2.3 (mixed discounting overstates the NPV)

Let \(\pi > 0\), \(r > -1\), and let the real flows satisfy \(C^r_t \ge 0\) for every \(t \ge 1\), with \(C^r_t > 0\) for at least one \(t \ge 1\). Let \(M = \sum_{t \ge 0} C^n_t/(1+r)^t\) be the mixed NPV, which discounts nominal flows at the real rate. Then \[ M - \sum_{t \ge 0} \frac{C^r_t}{(1+r)^t} \;=\; \sum_{t \ge 1} C^r_t \,\frac{(1+\pi)^t - 1}{(1+r)^t} \;> 0. \] When \(\pi = 0\) the difference is zero.

Proof

Substitute \(C^n_t = C^r_t(1+\pi)^t\) into \(M\) to get \(M = \sum_{t \ge 0} C^r_t(1+\pi)^t/(1+r)^t\). Subtract the real NPV term by term. The \(t = 0\) term has \((1+\pi)^0 = 1\), so it cancels. Each remaining term is \(C^r_t((1+\pi)^t - 1)/(1+r)^t\). When \(\pi > 0\) and \(t \ge 1\), \((1+\pi)^t - 1 > 0\), and each term is non-negative and positive where \(C^r_t > 0\). The sum is therefore positive. When \(\pi = 0\), every term is zero. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.4 (the other mix)

Discounting real flows at the nominal rate gives \(\sum_t C^r_t/(1+n)^t\). Under the assumptions of Theorem 2.3, \(1 + n \ge 1 + r\) by Theorem 1.3, so each term is no larger than its consistent value. This mixed NPV understates the real NPV, and the understatement is positive wherever some real flow after \(t = 0\) is positive.

Example 2.5 (a fixed nominal contract)

A contract pays a fixed nominal amount of 100 in each of years 1 to 3, and the nominal rate is \(n = 0.08\) with \(\pi = 0.03\). Its nominal NPV is \(100/1.08 + 100/1.08^2 + 100/1.08^3 = 92.5926 + 85.7339 + 79.3832 = 257.7097\), which is 257.71 to two decimals. Its real flows fall to \(100/1.03^t\), which are 97.0874, 94.2596 and 91.5142 for \(t = 1, 2, 3\). The real rate is \(1.08/1.03 - 1 = 0.048544\), and discounting the real flows at it gives the same 257.71 (Theorem 2.2). The nominal rate is the one to use for the fixed nominal amounts, and the real rate is the one to use for the real amounts. Both give the same value, so the choice follows from the money in which the flows are stated, not from the project.

Lab 1 · One SIMULATED project in real and nominal terms

The lab discounts one SIMULATED project, with an outlay of 1,000,000 USD and five real inflows of 300,000 USD each, at a real rate of 0.06 and inflation of 0.03. Before running, the expected results are: the nominal rate is 0.0918 (Definition 1.2); the NPV of the nominal flows at the nominal rate equals the real NPV to floating-point precision (Theorem 2.2); discounting the nominal flows at the real rate overstates the NPV, and discounting the real flows at the nominal rate understates it (Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4). The lab also checks the consistency identity over a grid of 12 real-rate and inflation pairs. No random numbers are drawn.

"""CF1 L03 lab 1: one SIMULATED project discounted consistently in real and in nominal terms.

SIMULATED: the project is a fixed input written here. It is not any firm's project.
Units: money in cents. Real cash flows are stated at base-year prices, so each real flow is in base-year cents.
Rates are decimals per year. Inflation is constant at pi per year, so the price level at year t is (1 + pi)**t,
and the nominal rate n follows the exact Fisher form (1 + n) = (1 + r)(1 + pi), where r is the real rate.
Discounted and inflated amounts are floats, not rounded to whole cents. The lab draws no random numbers.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from collections.abc import Sequence

# t = 0 is the outlay; t = 1 to 5 are operating inflows, all in base-year cents.
REAL_FLOWS: tuple[int, ...] = (-100_000_000, 30_000_000, 30_000_000, 30_000_000, 30_000_000, 30_000_000)
REAL_RATE = 0.06
INFLATION = 0.03
INFLATION_GRID = (0.0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.05)
REAL_RATE_GRID = (0.02, 0.06, 0.10)


def nominal_rate(real: float, inflation: float) -> float:
    """Nominal rate per year from the exact Fisher form (1 + n) = (1 + r)(1 + pi); all inputs are decimals per year."""
    return (1.0 + real) * (1.0 + inflation) - 1.0


def npv(flows: Sequence[float], rate: float) -> float:
    """Net present value in cents: the sum of flows[t] / (1 + rate)**t over every year t, rate per year."""
    return sum(cf / (1.0 + rate) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(flows))


def nominal_flows(real: Sequence[float], inflation: float) -> list[float]:
    """Nominal cash flows in cents: real[t] * (1 + inflation)**t, the price-level conversion at constant inflation."""
    return [cf * (1.0 + inflation) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(real)]


def usd(cents: float) -> str:
    """Cents shown as dollars with two decimals and no thousands separator."""
    return f"{cents / 100:.2f}"


def mixed_identity(real: Sequence[float], real_rate: float, inflation: float) -> float:
    """The mixed-discounting gap, cents: sum over t >= 1 of real[t] * ((1 + pi)**t - 1) / (1 + r)**t."""
    return sum(cf * ((1.0 + inflation) ** t - 1.0) / (1.0 + real_rate) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(real) if t >= 1)


def main() -> None:
    n = nominal_rate(REAL_RATE, INFLATION)
    nominal = nominal_flows(REAL_FLOWS, INFLATION)
    print(f"real rate {REAL_RATE:.6f}, inflation {INFLATION:.6f}, nominal rate {n:.6f}")
    print("t  real_cents  nominal_cents")
    for t, (real_cf, nominal_cf) in enumerate(zip(REAL_FLOWS, nominal, strict=True)):
        print(f"{t}  {real_cf}  {nominal_cf:.2f}")

    real_npv = npv(REAL_FLOWS, REAL_RATE)
    consistent = npv(nominal, n)
    mixed_over = npv(nominal, REAL_RATE)
    mixed_under = npv(REAL_FLOWS, n)
    print(f"NPV real flows at real rate: {usd(real_npv)} USD")
    print(f"NPV nominal flows at nominal rate: {usd(consistent)} USD")
    print(f"NPV nominal flows at real rate (mixed): {usd(mixed_over)} USD")
    print(f"NPV real flows at nominal rate (mixed): {usd(mixed_under)} USD")
    print(f"largest absolute gap, consistent minus real, cents: {abs(consistent - real_npv):.6f}")
    gap = mixed_over - real_npv
    print(f"mixed minus real, cents: {gap:.2f}")
    print(
        "identity gap, mixed minus real less the sum over t >= 1, cents: "
        f"{abs(gap - mixed_identity(REAL_FLOWS, REAL_RATE, INFLATION)):.6f}"
    )

    additive = REAL_RATE + INFLATION
    print(
        f"exact nominal rate minus (real + inflation): {n - additive:.6f}; "
        f"real x inflation: {REAL_RATE * INFLATION:.6f}"
    )
    print(f"NPV real flows at additive rate r + pi = {additive:.6f}: {usd(npv(REAL_FLOWS, additive))} USD")

    print("grid of 12 cases (inflation 0 to 0.05, real rate 0.02 to 0.10): consistency and mixed gaps")
    worst_consistency = 0.0
    for inflation in INFLATION_GRID:
        for real_rate in REAL_RATE_GRID:
            nom = nominal_flows(REAL_FLOWS, inflation)
            rate_n = nominal_rate(real_rate, inflation)
            gap_case = npv(nom, rate_n) - npv(REAL_FLOWS, real_rate)
            worst_consistency = max(worst_consistency, abs(gap_case))
    print(f"largest absolute consistency gap over the grid, cents: {worst_consistency:.6f}")

    print("inflation  mixed minus real, USD (nominal flows at the real rate 0.06)")
    for inflation in INFLATION_GRID:
        mixed = npv(nominal_flows(REAL_FLOWS, inflation), REAL_RATE) - real_npv
        print(f"{inflation:.2f}  {mixed / 100:.2f}")

3Where the canon breaks

The additive form \(n \approx r + \pi\) is a shorthand. Theorem 1.3 says its error is exactly \(r\pi\), which is small at low rates and grows with them. At \(r = 0.10\) and \(\pi = 0.10\), the exact rate is \(1.10 \times 1.10 - 1 = 0.21\), and the additive form gives \(0.20\), a gap of one percentage point. The error is not a small display detail at high inflation.

Theorem 2.2 assumes that one inflation rate applies to every flow in every year. With different inflation rates for different lines, a single nominal rate for the total stream is a choice that matches the real NPV at one real rate, not a consequence of the lines. Take two lines, each with a real flow of 100 in year 1. One line has no inflation and the other has 5 per cent. The nominal total in year 1 is \(100 + 105 = 205\). The real NPV at \(r = 0.05\) is \(200/1.05 = 190.476\). The nominal rate that matches it is \(n = 205 \times 1.05 / 200 - 1 = 0.07625\). Its implied inflation is \(1.07625/1.05 - 1 = 0.025\), which is neither line's inflation. The correct procedure discounts each line at its own nominal rate.

Theorem 2.2 holds when the real rate is constant over the life of the project. A rate that changes by year needs the path-based identity in Problem R1, and the constant-rate result does not cover it.

4Misconceptions

  1. “The Fisher relation is \(n = r + \pi\).”

    The exact relation is \(1 + n = (1+r)(1+\pi)\), so \(n = r + \pi + r\pi\) (Theorem 1.3). The additive form drops \(r\pi\), which is 0.0018 at \(r = 0.06\) and \(\pi = 0.03\) (Example 1.4). Lab 1 prints the same gap.

  2. “Nominal cash flows should be discounted at the real rate, because the project is a real project.”

    The flows are nominal, so their value at the real rate is inflated. Theorem 2.3 gives the overstatement as a positive sum, which is 113,637.49 USD at \(\pi = 0.03\) in Lab 1, about 43 per cent of the real NPV. The label of the project does not change the arithmetic.

  3. “Real cash flows should be discounted at the nominal rate, because the nominal rate is the market rate.”

    Discounting real flows at \(n\) understates the NPV (Remark 2.4). Lab 1 prints 161,465.80 USD against a real NPV of 263,709.14 USD. A rate can be the market rate and still be the wrong rate for a flow stated in other money.

  4. “Inflation changes the discount rate and leaves the cash flows as they are.”

    Under Definition 1.1, a real flow of 30,000,000 cents in base-year money becomes 30,900,000 cents in year 1 at 3 per cent inflation, and 33,765,264.30 cents in year 4 (the nominal column of Lab 1). Inflation changes the flows and the rate, and the consistency identity needs both.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The exact nominal rate

A real rate of 4 per cent and inflation of 2.5 per cent are given. Find the exact nominal rate and the additive approximation, and state the gap.

Solution

Definition 1.2 gives \(n = 1.04 \times 1.025 - 1 = 1.066 - 1 = 0.066\), or 6.6 per cent. The additive form gives \(0.065\). The gap is \(0.04 \times 0.025 = 0.001\), which is 0.1 percentage points. This matches Theorem 1.3, \(n - (r + \pi) = r\pi\).

M2 · A real flow in a later year

A real flow of 1,000 USD in base-year money is received in year 3, with inflation of 2 per cent a year. What is the nominal flow in year 3?

Solution

By Definition 1.1, \(C^n_3 = C^r_3(1+\pi)^3 = 1000 \times 1.02^3 = 1000 \times 1.061208 = 1061.208\). The nominal flow is 1,061.21 USD to two decimals. The conversion multiplies by the price level, so it is not an additive 2 per cent a year. The additive shortcut would give 1,060, which is 1.208 short of the exact figure.

M3 · The real rate from a nominal rate

A nominal rate of 10 per cent is quoted, and inflation is 4 per cent. Find the real rate, and check it by discounting a real flow of 100 in year 1 at the real rate and at the nominal rate applied to the nominal flow.

Solution

Definition 1.2 gives \(r = 1.10/1.04 - 1 = 0.057692\), or 5.77 per cent. The nominal flow in year 1 is \(100 \times 1.04 = 104\). Its present value at the nominal rate is \(104/1.10 = 94.545\). The real flow discounted at the real rate is \(100/1.057692 = 94.545\), the same value by Theorem 2.2.

Proof

P1 · Consistency for a two-year project

Prove Theorem 2.2 directly for a project with real flows \(C^r_0\), \(C^r_1\) and \(C^r_2\), without using the general sum.

Solution

The nominal flows are \(C^n_0 = C^r_0\), \(C^n_1 = C^r_1(1+\pi)\) and \(C^n_2 = C^r_2(1+\pi)^2\). From Definition 1.2, \((1+n) = (1+r)(1+\pi)\), so \((1+n)^2 = (1+r)^2(1+\pi)^2\). The nominal NPV is \(C^r_0 + C^r_1(1+\pi)/((1+r)(1+\pi)) + C^r_2(1+\pi)^2/((1+r)^2(1+\pi)^2)\). The factors \((1+\pi)\) cancel in the second and third terms, which leaves \(C^r_0 + C^r_1/(1+r) + C^r_2/(1+r)^2\), the real NPV.

P2 · When the mixed error vanishes

Under the assumptions of Theorem 2.3, show that the mixed NPV equals the real NPV for every real flow if and only if \(\pi = 0\), and explain why the \(\pi = 0\) case needs no assumption on the flows.

Solution

If \(\pi = 0\), then \((1+\pi)^t - 1 = 0\) for every \(t\), so the difference in Theorem 2.3 is zero for every flow. Conversely, suppose \(\pi > 0\). Take the real flow with a single positive inflow at \(t = 1\), so \(C^r_1 = 1\) and every other flow is zero. The difference is \(((1+\pi) - 1)/(1+r) = \pi/(1+r) > 0\), so the NPVs differ. The \(\pi = 0\) case needs no sign assumption because every term vanishes identically, not because of the sign of the flows.

P3 · The rate at which the additive error reaches half a point

Find the common value \(x\) of \(r\) and \(\pi\), with \(x > 0\), at which the additive form misstates the nominal rate by 0.5 percentage points.

Solution

Theorem 1.3 gives the error as \(r\pi\). With \(r = \pi = x\), the error is \(x^2\). Setting \(x^2 = 0.005\) gives \(x = \sqrt{0.005} = 0.070711\), or about 7.07 per cent for each of the real rate and inflation. The error is the product of the two rates, so doubling both rates multiplies the error by four.

Empirical

E1 · Reading the mixed-error table

Lab 1 prints the mixed minus real NPV in USD at inflation 0.05 as 194,368.27, with real NPV 263,709.14 USD. What share of the real NPV is the mixed overstatement at that inflation rate? Give the answer to one decimal place.

Solution

The share is \(194368.27 / 263709.14 = 0.73706\), or 73.7 per cent. The basis is the two lab lines, both printed by Lab 1, and the arithmetic is the division shown. A student who wants the share at other inflation rates reads the same table at 0.01 (36,913.22 USD) and 0.03 (113,637.49 USD), which give 14.0 and 43.1 per cent.

E2 · Zero inflation in the lab

In a copy of Lab 1, set INFLATION to 0.00 and read the mixed lines. What value does the mixed-minus-real line print, and why?

Solution

The printed value is 0.00 USD. With \(\pi = 0\), the price level is 1 in every year, so the nominal flows equal the real flows and every term in Theorem 2.3 is \(C^r_t \cdot (1 - 1)/(1+r)^t = 0\). The nominal rate is then equal to the real rate, so the lab's other mixed line also equals the real NPV. The table row for inflation 0.00 in Lab 1 shows the same result.

E3 · Changing the real rate in a copy

In a copy of Lab 1, change REAL_RATE to 0.10 and keep INFLATION at 0.03. State the nominal rate that the lab will use, and say what the consistency gap will print.

Solution

The nominal rate is \(1.10 \times 1.03 - 1 = 0.133\), or 13.3 per cent. The consistency gap prints 0.000000 cents, because Theorem 2.2 holds for every real rate greater than \(-1\). The real NPV will change, since the discount rate has changed, but the consistency identity does not depend on the rate chosen. The student should check the printed value of the real NPV and not assume it.

Research

R1 · Time-varying inflation

Let inflation vary by year, with \(\pi_s\) the rate in year \(s\) and the price level \(P_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+\pi_s)\). Define \(1+n_s = (1+r_s)(1+\pi_s)\) for each year. Show that the nominal NPV, with the discount factor \(\prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+n_s)^{-1}\), equals the real NPV when real flows are discounted with \(\prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+r_s)^{-1}\).

Solution

The price level converts the real flow to the nominal one, so \(C^n_t = C^r_t P_t\). The nominal discount product is \(\prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+n_s) = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+r_s)(1+\pi_s) = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+r_s)\cdot P_t\). Dividing \(C^n_t\) by it gives \(C^r_t P_t / (P_t \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+r_s)) = C^r_t / \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1+r_s)\), the real discounted flow. The result holds for any path of inflation, because the price level cancels exactly. This lesson proves only the constant case in Theorem 2.2.

R2 · Mixed discounting of a perpetuity

A real perpetuity pays \(c\) per year from year 1 onward, with \(r > \pi > 0\). Compute its real value and its mixed value (nominal flows at the real rate), and give the ratio of the mixed value to the real value.

Solution

The real value is \(\sum_{t \ge 1} c/(1+r)^t = c/r\). The nominal flows are \(c(1+\pi)^t\). Let \(q = (1+\pi)/(1+r)\), which is less than 1 because \(r > \pi\). The mixed value is \(\sum_{t \ge 1} c\,q^t = cq/(1-q) = c(1+\pi)/(r - \pi)\). The ratio is \(r(1+\pi)/(r - \pi)\). At \(r = 0.06\) and \(\pi = 0.03\) it is \(0.06 \times 1.03/0.03 = 2.06\), so the mixed value is more than twice the real value. The ratio grows without bound as \(r\) approaches \(\pi\).

R3 · Which real flows give a zero mixed error at one inflation rate

Fix \(\pi > 0\). Give a real flow pattern, with some nonzero flow after \(t = 0\), for which the mixed error of Theorem 2.3 is exactly zero.

Solution

The error is \(\sum_{t \ge 1} C^r_t((1+\pi)^t - 1)/(1+r)^t\). Choose a flow with a positive term and a negative term that cancel: at \(t = 1\) and \(t = 2\), let \(C^r_1 = a\) and \(C^r_2 = -b\). The error is \(a\pi/(1+r) - b\,((1+\pi)^2 - 1)/(1+r)^2 = a\pi/(1+r) - b(2\pi + \pi^2)/(1+r)^2\). Setting it to zero gives \(b = a(1+r)/(2+\pi)\), so \(a = 1\), \(r = 0.06\), \(\pi = 0.03\) gives \(b = 1.06/2.03\), about 0.5222. This flow has a negative term, so the sign statement of Theorem 2.3 does not apply to it.

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Which relation between the nominal rate \(n\), the real rate \(r\) and inflation \(\pi\) is exact under Definition 1.2?

    Options

    Definition 1.2 is multiplicative. Expanding it gives \(n = r + \pi + r\pi\) (Theorem 1.3), so the additive form is an approximation with error \(r\pi\).

  2. A real rate of 0.06 and inflation of 0.03 give what nominal rate? Give the decimal.

    \(1.06 \times 1.03 - 1 = 0.0918\) (Example 1.4).

  3. With the same rates, what is the error of the additive form \(n \approx r + \pi\), as a decimal?

    The error is \(r\pi = 0.06 \times 0.03 = 0.0018\), which is 0.18 percentage points (Theorem 1.3).

  4. A project's real flows are discounted at the real rate, and its nominal flows are discounted at the nominal rate. What does Theorem 2.2 say about the two NPVs?

    Options

    The consistency identity uses \((1+n)^t = (1+r)^t(1+\pi)^t\) for each \(t\), so the price-level factors cancel term by term (Theorem 2.2).

  5. Nominal flows are discounted at the real rate, with positive inflation and nonnegative later real flows. What is the sign of the error?

    Options

    Theorem 2.3 gives the error as a sum of non-negative terms, positive when some later real flow is positive.

  6. A real flow of 1,000 USD in base-year money arrives in year 3, with inflation of 2 per cent a year. What is the nominal flow, to three decimals?

    \(1000 \times 1.02^3 = 1061.208\) (Problem M2).

  7. A nominal rate of 0.10 and inflation of 0.04 give what real rate? Give the decimal to four places.

    \(r = 1.10/1.04 - 1 = 0.057692\), which is 0.0577 to four places (Problem M3).

  8. In Lab 1, what does the mixed-minus-real line print when inflation is zero?

    Options

    With \(\pi = 0\), every term in Theorem 2.3 is zero, and the table row for inflation 0.00 prints 0.00 USD (Problem E2).

  9. Two lines of a project have different inflation rates. Which statement is correct?

    Options

    Section 3 gives the two-line example: the single nominal rate that matches the real NPV implies 2.5 per cent inflation, which is neither line's rate.

  10. In Lab 1, what is the largest absolute gap between the NPV of nominal flows at the nominal rate and the real NPV, over the 12-case grid, in cents? Give the printed value.

    The gap is zero to floating-point precision, as Theorem 2.2 requires. The lab prints it to six decimals.
