Project Cash Flows: Incremental, Tax and Working Capital

  • Uses: net present value (Lesson 1), depreciation and amortization, the balance sheet, accrual accounting
  • Introduces: incremental project cash flows, the depreciation tax shield, net working capital investment
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

The NPV rule in Lesson 1 takes a list of cash flows as given, and the list is the input that the rule cannot check for itself. A project’s cash flows are the change in the firm’s cash that the project causes, after tax, including the working capital it ties up. This lesson defines the incremental flow, derives the after-tax operating cash flow and its depreciation shield, and proves that working capital invested and recovered nets to minus the change in its balance, which is zero when the balance returns to its starting level. The lab checks each year's operating cash flow against Proposition 2.2 and checks that the working-capital flows sum to zero.

1Incremental cash flows

Definition 1.1 (incremental cash flow)

The incremental cash flow of a project in period \(t\) is the firm's cash flow in period \(t\) with the project less its cash flow in period \(t\) without it:

\[ \Delta C_t = C_t^{\text{with}} - C_t^{\text{without}}. \]

Proposition 1.2 (the project's NPV is the NPV of its increments)

For any rate \(r > -1\), \(\mathrm{NPV}(r; \Delta C) = \mathrm{NPV}(r; C^{\text{with}}) - \mathrm{NPV}(r; C^{\text{without}})\).

Proof

Write \(C^{\text{with}} = \Delta C + C^{\text{without}}\), which is the definition of \(\Delta C\) rearranged. The proof of Proposition 2.1 of Lesson 1 applies to the sum of two streams at one rate, so NPV splits over the two terms. Rearranging gives the claim. \(\blacksquare\)

Three rules decide which flows belong in \(\Delta C\). The first is proved in Proposition 1.3. The second and third are conventions of the method: they are stated here, not derived.

Proposition 1.3 (a cost paid in both scenarios cancels)

Let \(S\) be a cost that is paid in period \(s\) whether or not the project is undertaken. Then \(S\) does not appear in \(\Delta C\). If it is charged to every alternative, each NPV falls by the same present value \(S(1+r)^{-s}\), so the charge cannot change which of the alternatives is preferred.

Proof

Because \(S\) is paid in both scenarios, it appears in \(C^{\text{with}}_s\) and in \(C^{\text{without}}_s\) with the same sign and amount, so the difference \(\Delta C_s\) does not contain it. The second claim follows from the same identity: any charge \(S\) that is added to every alternative subtracts the same \(S(1+r)^{-s}\) from each NPV, so the ordering of the NPVs is unchanged. \(\blacksquare\)

The first rule is the one Proposition 1.3 supports: a cost that is already paid is sunk, and it is not a flow of the project. The second rule is a convention: an asset the firm already owns and would use elsewhere enters at its opportunity cost, which is what it would earn in its next best use. The third rule is also a convention: financing stays out of the flows. Interest and dividends are payments to the providers of capital, and the rate in Lesson 1 already charges for that capital, so counting interest as a project flow counts the cost of capital twice.

2Depreciation, tax and the operating cash flow

Let \(R_t\) be revenue, \(K_t\) cash operating costs, \(D_t\) depreciation and \(\tau\) the tax rate, all for period \(t\). Let \(\mathrm{EBITDA}_t = R_t - K_t\) and \(\mathrm{EBIT}_t = \mathrm{EBITDA}_t - D_t\). Depreciation is a non-cash charge: it reduces taxable income, and no cash leaves the firm for it. The accounting for it is set out under depreciation and amortization. Tax is \(\tau \cdot \mathrm{EBIT}_t\). A negative tax is a credit. The convention used here is that the firm uses the credit in the same period, which Remark 2.4 returns to.

Definition 2.1 (after-tax operating cash flow)

The after-tax operating cash flow is \(\mathrm{OCF}_t = \mathrm{EBIT}_t - \tau\,\mathrm{EBIT}_t + D_t\): the operating income after tax, plus the non-cash depreciation that was deducted to reach it.

Proposition 2.2 (the after-tax identity)

For any depreciation schedule \(D_t\) and any tax rate \(\tau\), \(\mathrm{OCF}_t = \mathrm{EBITDA}_t\,(1 - \tau) + \tau D_t\).

Proof

Substitute \(\mathrm{EBIT}_t = \mathrm{EBITDA}_t - D_t\) into Definition 2.1: \(\mathrm{OCF}_t = (\mathrm{EBITDA}_t - D_t)(1-\tau) + D_t = \mathrm{EBITDA}_t(1-\tau) - D_t + \tau D_t + D_t = \mathrm{EBITDA}_t(1-\tau) + \tau D_t\). The proof uses no property of \(D_t\), so the identity holds for every schedule. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 2.3 (the depreciation tax shield)

The part of \(\mathrm{OCF}_t\) that depreciation adds, over and above the after-tax EBITDA \(\mathrm{EBITDA}_t(1-\tau)\), is the depreciation tax shield, \(\tau D_t\). Depreciation itself adds no cash.

Proof

Set \(\mathrm{OCF}_t\) equal to \(\mathrm{EBITDA}_t(1-\tau)\) plus the shield. Proposition 2.2 gives the shield as \(\tau D_t\). Without depreciation (\(D_t = 0\)) the shield is zero, so depreciation's only cash effect is through tax. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.4 (the shield needs taxable income)

The shield is a saving of tax, and it is worth \(\tau D_t\) only if the firm has taxable income to offset. Proposition 2.2 is an identity and needs no such condition, but the convention that a credit is used in the same period does. If the credit must be carried forward, the shield arrives later, and Problem R1 gives its present value loss. The lab states this convention and does not test any firm's tax position.

3Net working capital

Definition 3.1 (net working capital)

Net working capital at the end of period \(t\), \(\mathrm{NWC}_t\), is the operating current assets tied up in the project (receivables, inventory and the like) less the operating current liabilities it creates (payables and the like), taken from the balance sheet lines that belong to operations. Cash and financing items are excluded. The net working capital investment in period \(t\) is \(I_t = \mathrm{NWC}_t - \mathrm{NWC}_{t-1}\), and its cash-flow effect is \(-I_t\).

Proposition 3.2 (investment and recovery net to the change in the balance)

For periods \(1, \dots, n\), the sum of the working-capital cash flows is \(\sum_{t=1}^{n}(-I_t) = \mathrm{NWC}_0 - \mathrm{NWC}_n\). In particular, if the project returns working capital to its starting level, \(\mathrm{NWC}_n = \mathrm{NWC}_0\), the investments and recoveries sum to zero.

Proof

\(\sum_{t=1}^{n} I_t = \sum_{t=1}^{n}(\mathrm{NWC}_t - \mathrm{NWC}_{t-1})\). The sum telescopes: every intermediate \(\mathrm{NWC}_t\) appears once with a plus and once with a minus, leaving \(\mathrm{NWC}_n - \mathrm{NWC}_0\). Taking the negative gives the claim, and when \(\mathrm{NWC}_n = \mathrm{NWC}_0\) the sum is zero. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3.3 (zero in sum is not zero in value)

Let \(X > 0\) be invested in period \(t_1\) and recovered in period \(t_2 > t_1\), with nothing else. For \(r > 0\) its present value is \(X\bigl[(1+r)^{-t_2} - (1+r)^{-t_1}\bigr]\), which is negative.

Proof

The present value is \(-X(1+r)^{-t_1} + X(1+r)^{-t_2}\). Since \(r > 0\) and \(t_2 > t_1\), we have \((1+r)^{-t_2} < (1+r)^{-t_1}\), so the bracket is negative. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.4 (the working-capital convention)

Proposition 3.2 holds for any path of \(\mathrm{NWC}_t\). The lab sets working capital at 10 percent of the same year's revenue, with a zero balance at the start and at the end of the project. That is a convention about the project, not a finding about any firm. The NPV effect of the stream is the one Proposition 3.3 describes.

4A worked machine project

Lab 2 builds one SIMULATED project from the three sections above. The machine costs 12,000 dollars at period 0 and lasts four periods, with straight-line depreciation of 3,000 dollars a year and no salvage. The tax rate is 25 percent. Revenue and cash costs are generated from a seeded generator. A sunk study cost of 2,000 dollars is excluded from the flows.

Take year 1 as the worked line. The lab prints revenue of 6,900.00 dollars and cash cost of 2,800.00 dollars, so \(\mathrm{EBITDA}_1 = 4{,}100.00\) dollars. Then \(\mathrm{EBIT}_1 = 4{,}100.00 - 3{,}000.00 = 1{,}100.00\) dollars and the tax is \(0.25 \times 1{,}100.00 = 275.00\) dollars. Definition 2.1 gives \(\mathrm{OCF}_1 = 1{,}100.00 - 275.00 + 3{,}000.00 = 3{,}825.00\) dollars. Proposition 2.2 gives the same value: \(4{,}100.00 \times 0.75 + 0.25 \times 3{,}000.00 = 3{,}075.00 + 750.00 = 3{,}825.00\) dollars. The working capital at the end of year 1 is 10 percent of 6,900.00 dollars, which is 690.00 dollars, so the year-1 investment of 690.00 dollars is a cash outflow, and the year-1 total is \(3{,}825.00 - 690.00 = 3{,}135.00\) dollars.

Year 4 closes the working capital. The balance at the end of year 3 is 680.00 dollars and the balance at the end of year 4 is zero, so year 4 recovers 680.00 dollars. Lab 2 prints the full table, and its working-capital flows sum to zero. The NPV of the incremental flows at 8 percent is 534.24 dollars. Charging the sunk study cost to the project would give \(-1{,}465.76\) dollars, a difference of 2,000.00 dollars, which is the sunk cost itself. The difference is not a future cash flow.

Lab 2 · After-tax incremental cash flows of a SIMULATED machine

The depreciation shield is 750.00 dollars a year, which is 25 percent of 3,000.00 dollars. The working-capital flows sum to zero because working capital returns to zero. SIMULATED: revenues and cash costs are generated from a seeded random number generator. The machine, the tax rate
and every figure are invented for this lab and are not a real company's. Money is entered in integer cents;

Conventions applied: straight-line depreciation over the life, with no salvage; tax at one rate on taxable income,

"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random

SEED = 20261028
TAX_RATE = 0.25
RATE = 0.08
LIFE_YEARS = 4
CAPEX_CENTS = 1_200_000
SUNK_STUDY_CENTS = 200_000
TOLERANCE = 1e-6


def npv_dollars(flows_dollars: list[float], rate: float) -> float:
    """Net present value in dollars of flows_dollars[t], received at the end of period t, at a rate per period."""
    return sum(flow / (1.0 + rate) ** t for t, flow in enumerate(flows_dollars))


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    depreciation_cents = CAPEX_CENTS // LIFE_YEARS
    revenue_cents = [0]
    cost_cents = [0]
    for _ in range(LIFE_YEARS):
        revenue_cents.append(rng.randrange(50, 71) * 10_000)
        cost_cents.append(rng.randrange(20, 31) * 10_000)
    # Working capital is 10 percent of revenue at the end of years 1 to 3, zero at the start and at the end.
    nwc_cents = [0] + [revenue_cents[t] // 10 for t in range(1, LIFE_YEARS)] + [0]

    print("SIMULATED machine: capex 12,000.00 dollars, four-year life, straight-line depreciation, tax rate 25 percent")
    print(f"depreciation per year, in dollars: {depreciation_cents / 100:.2f}")
    print(f"sunk study cost, excluded from the incremental flows, in dollars: {SUNK_STUDY_CENTS / 100:.2f}")
    print()
    print("year  revenue  cash_cost  ebitda      tax     after_tax_ocf  nwc_flow     total")

    flows: list[float] = [-CAPEX_CENTS / 100]
    nwc_flows: list[float] = []
    identity_ok = True
    shield_ok = True
    for t in range(1, LIFE_YEARS + 1):
        ebitda = revenue_cents[t] - cost_cents[t]
        ebit = ebitda - depreciation_cents
        tax = TAX_RATE * ebit
        ocf = ebit - tax + depreciation_cents
        shield = TAX_RATE * depreciation_cents
        # The identity: after-tax operating cash flow = EBITDA x (1 - tax rate) + depreciation x tax rate.
        identity_ok = identity_ok and abs(ocf - (ebitda * (1 - TAX_RATE) + shield)) < TOLERANCE
        # The shield, measured as the difference between the operating cash flow with depreciation and the one
        # without it (EBIT equal to EBITDA, no add-back), must equal depreciation x tax rate.
        ocf_without = ebitda - TAX_RATE * ebitda
        shield_ok = shield_ok and abs((ocf - ocf_without) - depreciation_cents * TAX_RATE) < TOLERANCE
        nwc_flow = -(nwc_cents[t] - nwc_cents[t - 1]) / 100
        nwc_flows.append(nwc_flow)
        total = ocf / 100 + nwc_flow
        flows.append(total)
        print(
            f"{t:>4}  {revenue_cents[t] / 100:>7.2f}  {cost_cents[t] / 100:>9.2f}  {ebitda / 100:>7.2f}"
            f"  {tax / 100:>7.2f}  {ocf / 100:>13.2f}  {nwc_flow:>8.2f}  {total:>8.2f}"
        )

    print()
    print(
        "after-tax operating cash flow equals EBITDA times (1 - tax rate) plus the shield, in every year:", identity_ok
    )
    print("depreciation shield equals depreciation times the tax rate, in every year:", shield_ok)
    print(f"depreciation shield per year, in dollars: {TAX_RATE * depreciation_cents / 100:.2f}")
    print(f"working-capital investment and recovery sum to zero: {sum(nwc_flows) == 0.0}")
    print(f"sum of working-capital flows, in dollars: {sum(nwc_flows):.2f}")
    print()
    npv = npv_dollars(flows, RATE)
    print(f"NPV at {RATE:.2f} of the incremental flows, in dollars: {npv:.2f}")
    with_sunk = npv_dollars([flows[0] - SUNK_STUDY_CENTS / 100, *flows[1:]], RATE)
    print(f"NPV if the sunk study cost is charged to the project, in dollars: {with_sunk:.2f}")
    print(f"difference, in dollars: {npv - with_sunk:.2f}")

5Where the canon breaks

The identities in this lesson are exact. The inputs they take are conventions, and four of them need checking before the result is read.

  • Accounting earnings are not cash flows. Net income contains accruals, which are the non-cash items of accrual accounting. The NPV rule discounts cash, so a project built from reported earnings must be rebuilt from its cash effects. The lab builds the flows directly and never uses an earnings line.
  • The shield depends on taxable income. Proposition 2.2 holds whatever the firm's tax position, but the value of \(\tau D_t\) to the firm requires income to absorb it in the same period (Remark 2.4). A firm without taxable income in some year cannot use the shield in that year, and Remark 2.4 gives the timing convention.
  • Working capital may not return. Proposition 3.2 gives a zero sum only when \(\mathrm{NWC}_n = \mathrm{NWC}_0\). A project whose inventory is never sold, or whose receivables are never collected, leaves a balance that is not recovered, and the flows then sum to \(\mathrm{NWC}_0 - \mathrm{NWC}_n\), which is not zero.
  • Overhead allocations are not incremental. An existing cost that the firm pays whether or not it takes the project enters the flows only when the firm's cash changes. An allocation that moves cost between projects changes the accounting, not the firm's cash.

6Misconceptions

  1. “Depreciation is a cash flow, so it adds to the project's value.”

    Depreciation is a non-cash charge. Its only cash effect is the tax it saves, which is \(\tau D_t\) by Corollary 2.3. With the tax rate at zero, the operating cash flow is EBITDA, and depreciation adds nothing. Lab 2 prints a shield of 750.00 dollars a year, which is 25 percent of the 3,000.00 dollars of depreciation, not the full 3,000.00 dollars.

  2. “A sunk study cost should be charged to the project, because it reduces the NPV.”

    A cost paid in every scenario cancels from the incremental flows (Proposition 1.3). Charging it lowers the NPV by a constant that carries no information about the decision. Lab 2 shows the NPV falling from 534.24 dollars to \(-1{,}465.76\) dollars when the 2,000.00 dollar study cost is charged. That figure is the study cost, not a future cash effect.

  3. “Working capital that is recovered at the end has no effect on value.”

    The investment and the recovery net to zero in sum (Proposition 3.2), but they fall in different periods. Proposition 3.3 gives a negative present value when the investment comes first and \(r > 0\). For the lab's working-capital flows at periods 1 to 4, \(-690.00, 10.00, 0.00, 680.00\), the present value at 8 percent is \(-130.50\) dollars. The sum is zero and the value is not.

7Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Amounts are in dollars.

Mechanics

M1 · Shield and operating cash flow

A project costs 1,000 dollars, depreciates straight-line over five years to zero, earns EBITDA of 500 dollars a year, and faces a tax rate of 30 percent. Find the shield and the operating cash flow in each year.

Solution

Depreciation is \(1{,}000/5 = 200\) dollars a year. The shield is \(\tau D = 0.30 \times 200 = 60.00\) dollars. By Proposition 2.2, \(\mathrm{OCF} = 500 \times 0.70 + 60 = 410.00\) dollars. Check by the long route: \(\mathrm{EBIT} = 500 - 200 = 300\), tax \(= 90\), and \(\mathrm{OCF} = 300 - 90 + 200 = 410\) dollars. The two routes agree, and the flows are the same in each of the five years because EBITDA and depreciation do not change.

M2 · Working capital that returns to zero

Revenue is 800, 1,000, 1,000 and 600 dollars in years 1 to 4. Working capital is 20 percent of the same year's revenue at the end of years 1 to 3, and zero at the start and at the end of year 4. Find the working-capital cash flow in each year and their sum.

Solution

The balances are \(\mathrm{NWC}_0 = 0\), \(\mathrm{NWC}_1 = 160\), \(\mathrm{NWC}_2 = 200\), \(\mathrm{NWC}_3 = 200\) and \(\mathrm{NWC}_4 = 0\). The cash flows \(-I_t\) are \(-160\), \(-40\), \(0\) and \(+200\). Their sum is zero, as Proposition 3.2 requires, because \(\mathrm{NWC}_4 = \mathrm{NWC}_0\).

M3 · A sunk cost and the NPV

A study costing 500 dollars was paid last year and cannot be recovered. The project's own flows are \(-2{,}000\) at period 0 and 1,200 at periods 1 and 2. Find the NPV at \(r = 0.10\) of the incremental flows, and the NPV if the study is charged to the project.

Solution

The incremental NPV excludes the study, by Proposition 1.3: \(-2000 + 1200/1.1 + 1200/1.21 = 82.6446\ldots\), which is 82.64 dollars. Charging the study subtracts 500 dollars and gives \(-417.3553\ldots\), which is \(-417.36\) dollars. The sign of the decision differs between the two, and the correct one is the first, because the study is paid whatever the decision.

Proof

P1 · The identity for any schedule

Show that Proposition 2.2 holds when \(D_t\) is any real number, including one larger than \(\mathrm{EBITDA}_t\), so that \(\mathrm{EBIT}_t\) is negative.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 2.2 uses only the algebra \(\mathrm{EBIT} = \mathrm{EBITDA} - D\) and the definition of OCF. Neither assumes a sign for \(\mathrm{EBIT}\) or for \(D\). With \(\mathrm{EBIT}\) negative, the tax \(\tau\,\mathrm{EBIT}\) is a credit, and Definition 2.1 still gives \(\mathrm{OCF} = \mathrm{EBITDA}(1-\tau) + \tau D\) by the same expansion. The identity is therefore valid for every real \(D\), and the sign of \(\mathrm{EBIT}\) matters only for whether the credit is usable (Remark 2.4).

P2 · Telescoping over three periods

Write out \(\sum_{t=1}^{3} I_t\) for \(I_t = \mathrm{NWC}_t - \mathrm{NWC}_{t-1}\) and check that it equals \(\mathrm{NWC}_3 - \mathrm{NWC}_0\).

Solution

\(I_1 = \mathrm{NWC}_1 - \mathrm{NWC}_0\), \(I_2 = \mathrm{NWC}_2 - \mathrm{NWC}_1\), \(I_3 = \mathrm{NWC}_3 - \mathrm{NWC}_2\). Adding the three lines, \(\mathrm{NWC}_1\) and \(\mathrm{NWC}_2\) appear once with each sign and cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{NWC}_3 - \mathrm{NWC}_0\). This is the telescoping step of Proposition 3.2 written out for \(n = 3\).

P3 · The residual when working capital does not return

Show that if \(\mathrm{NWC}_n \ne \mathrm{NWC}_0\), the working-capital cash flows sum to \(\mathrm{NWC}_0 - \mathrm{NWC}_n\), and say what that residual is economically.

Solution

By Proposition 3.2 the sum is \(\mathrm{NWC}_0 - \mathrm{NWC}_n\) for any path. The residual is the balance that the project leaves tied up at the end: a positive \(\mathrm{NWC}_n\) above \(\mathrm{NWC}_0\) is a cash outflow that is never recovered in the flows, and it must be recognized as a cost of the project or as a terminal asset.

Empirical

E1 · The largest working-capital flow in Lab 2

Using only the printed table in Lab 2, which year has the largest working-capital cash flow in absolute value, and what is it?

Solution

The working-capital column prints \(-690.00\) in year 1, \(10.00\) in year 2, \(0.00\) in year 3 and \(680.00\) in year 4. The largest in absolute value is the year-1 outflow of 690.00 dollars, which is the first build of working capital. The year-4 recovery of 680.00 dollars is smaller because the balance at the end of year 3 is 680.00 dollars, not 690.00 dollars.

E2 · Why 3,000.00 becomes 750.00

Lab 2 deducts depreciation of 3,000.00 dollars a year, and the shield it prints is 750.00 dollars. Explain the relation between the two numbers, and say why the 3,000.00 dollars is not a cash inflow.

Solution

The shield is \(\tau D = 0.25 \times 3{,}000.00 = 750.00\) dollars, by Corollary 2.3. The depreciation itself is a deduction from taxable income, and no cash moves for it. The firm keeps the 750.00 dollars it would otherwise have paid in tax. The 3,000.00 dollars is not a cash inflow, because the firm did not receive it.

E3 · The two NPVs in Lab 2

Lab 2 prints an NPV of 534.24 dollars for the incremental flows and \(-1{,}465.76\) dollars when the sunk study is charged. Which number supports the decision, and what does the difference of 2,000.00 dollars measure?

Solution

The incremental NPV of 534.24 dollars supports the decision, because the study is paid in every scenario and is not a flow of the project (Proposition 1.3). The difference of 2,000.00 dollars is the amount of the sunk study, which the second computation charges to the project for no future cash effect.

Research

R1 · A shield carried forward

Suppose the credit \(\tau L\) from a loss \(L\) in period 1 is used in period 2 instead of period 1, at rate \(r > 0\). Find the loss in present value, and show that it is a positive fraction of the credit.

Solution

In the same-period convention the credit is worth \(\tau L (1+r)^{-1}\) at period 0, since it arrives at period 1. Carried forward, it arrives at period 2 and is worth \(\tau L (1+r)^{-2}\). The loss is \(\tau L\bigl[(1+r)^{-1} - (1+r)^{-2}\bigr] = \tau L\,r\,(1+r)^{-2}\). As a fraction of the same-period value \(\tau L (1+r)^{-1}\), the loss is \(r/(1+r)\), which is positive for \(r > 0\). For \(r > 0\), a carried-forward credit has a smaller present value.

R2 · The size of the working-capital effect

An investment of 100 dollars is made in period 1 and recovered in period 3, with nothing else, at \(r = 0.08\). Find its present value and check it against Proposition 3.3.

Solution

The present value is \(100\,[1.08^{-3} - 1.08^{-1}] = 100\,[0.7938322 - 0.9259259] = -13.2094\ldots\), which is \(-13.21\) dollars to the cent. The bracket is negative because \(1.08^{-3} < 1.08^{-1}\), as Proposition 3.3 requires for \(r > 0\). The sum of the two flows is zero, but their present value is 13.2 percent of the investment, since \(13.2094 / 100 \approx 0.132\).

R3 · The present value of a straight-line shield

A project depreciates an amount \(K\) straight-line over \(n\) periods to zero and faces tax rate \(\tau\), with the same-period convention. Show that the present value of its shields at rate \(r \ne 0\) is \(\tau (K/n)\,A(r,n)\), where \(A\) is the annuity factor of Proposition 5.1 of Lesson 1.

Solution

Each period's shield is \(\tau D_t = \tau K/n\), a level amount received at the end of periods \(1, \dots, n\). Its present value is therefore \(\tau (K/n)\) times the annuity factor \(A(r,n) = (1 - (1+r)^{-n})/r\), by Proposition 5.1 of Lesson 1. The result does not depend on the operating flows, which is why the shield can be valued separately from the EBITDA.

9Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Which item belongs in a project's incremental cash flows?

    Options

    Definition 1.1 gives the increment. A sunk cost is the same in both scenarios, and financing is charged through the rate of Lesson 1.

  2. EBITDA is 500, depreciation is 200 and the tax rate is 30 percent. What is the after-tax operating cash flow, in dollars?

    \(500 \times 0.70 + 0.30 \times 200 = 350 + 60 = 410\), as Proposition 2.2 gives.

  3. What is the only cash effect of depreciation in the operating cash flow?

    Options

    Corollary 2.3: depreciation adds \(\tau D\), the shield, and no cash beyond that.

  4. Depreciation is 200 a year and the tax rate is 30 percent. What is the depreciation tax shield, in dollars?

    \(\tau D = 0.30 \times 200 = 60\).

  5. If working capital returns to its starting balance, what do its investment and recovery sum to?

    Options

    Proposition 3.2: the sum is \(\mathrm{NWC}_0 - \mathrm{NWC}_n\), which is zero when the two balances are equal.

  6. A 100-dollar investment is made in period 1 and recovered in period 3, at \(r = 0.08\). What is its present value, in dollars to the cent?

    \(100(1.08^{-3} - 1.08^{-1}) = -13.2094\ldots\), which is \(-13.21\) dollars (Problem R2).

  7. Which statement about a sunk cost that both scenarios pay is correct?

    Options

    Proposition 1.3: a cost paid in both scenarios is absent from the difference.

  8. In Lab 2, what is the sum of the working-capital cash flows, in dollars?

    The printed flows are \(-690.00\), \(10.00\), \(0.00\) and \(680.00\), which sum to zero, as Proposition 3.2 requires.

  9. An existing overhead is paid whether or not the project is taken and is only reallocated to the project. Is it an incremental flow?

    Options

    Definition 1.1 counts only the change in the firm's cash that the project causes. An allocation moves cost between projects and leaves the firm's cash unchanged.

  10. A machine costs 1,200 dollars, depreciates straight-line over four years to zero, and the tax rate is 25 percent. What is its annual shield, in dollars?

    \(D = 1{,}200/4 = 300\), and \(\tau D = 0.25 \times 300 = 75\).
