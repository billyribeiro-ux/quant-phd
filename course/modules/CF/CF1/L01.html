The Net Present Value Rule and the Capital Budget

  • Uses: present value, discount factor, annuity, geometric series
  • Introduces: net present value (NPV) and the NPV decision rule
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A capital budget decides which projects to fund. Each project spends money now and returns money later, so its cash flows fall on different dates and cannot be added as they stand. The net present value rule turns the dated flows into one number in today’s money, at a rate the analyst states. This lesson defines the rule, proves the three properties that make it usable (additivity over projects, a fall in NPV as the rate rises for a conventional stream, and an exact replication by borrowing and lending at one rate), derives the annuity closed form, and marks the inputs the rule does not supply.

1Dated flows and the NPV rule

Time runs in periods \(0, 1, \dots, n\). A period is one year in the worked numbers and the labs on this page, and \(r\) is the rate for one such period. The cash flow \(C_t\) is the net amount the project pays out (a negative number) or receives (a positive number) at the end of period \(t\). The rate \(r\) is the return per period that the owners could earn on an alternative of the same risk. It must exceed \(-1\), so that every discount factor is finite and positive. Two conventions are fixed here: flows fall at period ends, and one rate applies to every period. The label SIMULATED marks a project made up for this lesson; no project on this page is a real company's.

Definition 1.1 (net present value)

The net present value of the flows \(C_0, \dots, C_n\) at rate \(r\) is

\[ \mathrm{NPV}(r) = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1+r)^t}. \]

Each term \(C_t (1+r)^{-t}\) is the present value of \(C_t\), and \((1+r)^{-t}\) is the discount factor for period \(t\).

The decision rule. Accept a project whose \(\mathrm{NPV}(r)\) is positive, reject one whose NPV is negative, and treat zero as indifference. The rule is a convention about how to read the number. Section 4 gives the reason it is the right reading under one set of assumptions.

Example 1.2 (the worked project)

A SIMULATED project costs 10,000 dollars at period 0 and pays 3,000, 3,200, 3,400, 3,600 and 3,800 dollars at periods 1 to 5. At \(r = 0.08\) the discount factors are \(1.08^{-t}\). Rounded to four places they are 0.9259, 0.8573, 0.7938, 0.7350 and 0.6806 for \(t = 1, \dots, 5\). Each discounted inflow is computed from the unrounded factor and then rounded to cents: 2,777.78, 2,743.48, 2,699.03, 2,646.11 and 2,586.22 dollars. The rounded terms sum to 13,452.62 dollars. The unrounded sum is 13,452.615 dollars, which rounds to the same cent. So \(\mathrm{NPV}(0.08) = 13{,}452.62 - 10{,}000 = 3{,}452.62\) dollars. Lab 1, Part A, prints the same value.

Remark 1.3 (round late)

The reader who multiplies each flow by the four-place factor gets 13,452.26 dollars, which is 36 cents less than the sum in Example 1.2. The difference comes from rounding the factors before multiplying, not from the rule. The factors are computed from unrounded powers, and per-term cents are rounded before they are summed. Remark 5.2 shows a case where rounding changes the last digit.

2Additivity over projects

Proposition 2.1 (additivity)

Let \(C_t\) and \(D_t\) be two flow streams over the same periods, and let \(E_t = C_t + D_t\) be their flow-by-flow sum. For any rate \(r > -1\), \(\mathrm{NPV}(r; E) = \mathrm{NPV}(r; C) + \mathrm{NPV}(r; D)\).

Proof

For each \(t\), \((C_t + D_t)(1+r)^{-t} = C_t(1+r)^{-t} + D_t(1+r)^{-t}\). The sum over \(t = 0, \dots, n\) is a finite sum, so it may be split into two finite sums: one of the \(C\) terms and one of the \(D\) terms. Each is the NPV of its stream at the same rate \(r\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.2 (one rate for every stream)

The proposition is proved for one rate applied to both streams. Two projects valued at different rates cannot be added this way, because the sum of their NPVs would mix two different prices of time. The consequence used in the lab is that a bundle of projects that share one rate can be assessed one project at a time.

3The NPV falls as the rate rises

A flow stream is conventional here when \(C_t \ge 0\) for every \(t \ge 1\) and \(C_t > 0\) for at least one \(t \ge 1\). A conventional stream has nonnegative flows after period 0, at least one of them positive. The sign of \(C_0\) is not used in the result below.

Proposition 3.1 (NPV falls with the rate)

Let the stream be conventional. Then \(r \mapsto \mathrm{NPV}(r)\) is strictly decreasing on \((-1, \infty)\), and \(\mathrm{NPV}(r) \to C_0\) as \(r \to \infty\).

Proof

Differentiate term by term, which is allowed because the sum is finite. The term \(C_0\) does not depend on \(r\). For \(t \ge 1\), \(\frac{d}{dr}(1+r)^{-t} = -t(1+r)^{-t-1}\), so

\[ \frac{d}{dr}\mathrm{NPV}(r) = -\sum_{t=1}^{n} t\, C_t\, (1+r)^{-t-1}. \]

Every term is at most zero, because \(C_t \ge 0\) and \((1+r)^{-t-1} > 0\). At least one term is strictly negative, because some \(C_t > 0\) with \(t \ge 1\). The derivative is therefore strictly negative on \((-1, \infty)\), and the function is strictly decreasing there. For the limit, each \((1+r)^{-t}\) with \(t \ge 1\) tends to zero as \(r \to \infty\), so only \(C_0\) remains. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.2 (what the monotonicity does and does not say)

Proposition 3.1 says that for a conventional stream the NPV falls as the rate rises, so such a project can have a positive NPV at a low rate and a negative NPV at a high one. It does not say which rate is the right one. That is an input to the rule, and Section 6 returns to it.

4Replication by borrowing and lending at one rate

The next result gives the reason for the rule. It rests on one assumption, stated here in full.

Assumption 4.0 (one-rate market)

At every period the owner can lend or borrow any amount at the rate \(r\), with no fees, no limit and no risk of default. The project's flows \(C_0, \dots, C_n\) are known in advance.

Proposition 4.1 (the project with its loan is equivalent to its NPV today)

Under Assumption 4.0, let \(N = \mathrm{NPV}(r)\). Define a loan with balance \(B_0 = N - C_0\) at period 0 and \(B_t = (1+r)B_{t-1} - C_t\) for \(t = 1, \dots, n\). Then \(B_n = 0\). The owner who takes the project and the loan receives \(N\) at period 0 and nothing at any later period.

Proof

Claim: for \(t = 0, \dots, n\),

\[ B_t = (1+r)^t \Bigl[\, N - C_0 - \sum_{s=1}^{t} C_s (1+r)^{-s} \Bigr]. \]

For \(t = 0\) the sum is empty and the claim is \(B_0 = N - C_0\). Suppose the claim holds for \(t - 1\). Then \(B_t = (1+r)B_{t-1} - C_t = (1+r)^t[\cdots] - C_t\), and \(C_t = (1+r)^t \cdot C_t (1+r)^{-t}\). Substituting gives the claim for \(t\). At \(t = n\) the bracket is \(N - \sum_{s=0}^{n} C_s (1+r)^{-s}\), which is zero by Definition 1.1. So \(B_n = 0\).

For the owner, the project's cash at period 0 is \(C_0\), and the loan proceeds at period 0 are \(N - C_0\). Their total is \(N\). At each \(t \ge 1\) the project pays \(C_t\) and the loan takes \(C_t\) out as its repayment, so the total is zero. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 4.2 (the larger NPV leaves more cash today)

Under Assumption 4.0, of two mutually exclusive projects (only one of them can be taken), each taken together with its replicating loan, the one with the larger \(\mathrm{NPV}(r)\) leaves the owner with more cash at period 0 and zero cash at every later period.

Proof

By Proposition 4.1 each project, taken with its loan, leaves exactly its NPV at period 0 and zero afterwards. Comparing two numbers that are the only nonzero entries gives the claim. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 4.3 (where the assumption is used)

The proof uses one rate for borrowing and lending at every period. Problem R3 shows what the rule does when the two rates differ. The proof does not depend on the sign of \(N\), so it covers a negative NPV as well: the owner then needs \(|N|\) at period 0 to take the project.

5The annuity closed form

A project may return a level amount for several periods. For that case the sum in Definition 1.1 has a closed form, which is the annuity factor.

Proposition 5.1 (the annuity factor)

Let \(r \ne 0\), and let a level amount \(C\) be received at the end of each period \(1, \dots, n\). Its present value is \(C \cdot A(r, n)\), where \(A(r, n) = \dfrac{1 - (1+r)^{-n}}{r}\). For an outlay \(I\) at period 0, \(\mathrm{NPV}(r) = -I + C \cdot A(r, n)\).

Proof

Let \(x = (1+r)^{-1}\). The present value is \(C \sum_{t=1}^{n} x^t\). By the geometric sum, which is the geometric series of M1, \(\sum_{t=1}^{n} x^t = x\,(1 - x^n)/(1 - x)\). Here \(1 - x = 1 - \frac{1}{1+r} = \frac{r}{1+r}\), and \(x/(1-x) = \frac{1/(1+r)}{r/(1+r)} = \frac{1}{r}\). Hence the sum is \((1 - x^n)/r = (1 - (1+r)^{-n})/r\). The NPV follows from Definition 1.1, since the period-0 term is \(-I\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 5.2 (round the factor last)

For \(r = 0.10\) and \(n = 3\), \(1.1^{-3} = 0.7513148\ldots\), so \(A(0.10, 3) = (1 - 0.7513148)/0.10 = 2.486852\), which is 2.4869 to four places. Subtracting the rounded value 0.7513 instead gives \((1 - 0.7513)/0.10 = 2.4870\). The two results differ in the last digit, so the factor must be computed from the unrounded power and rounded only at the end.

Example 5.3 (the outlay that makes the NPV zero)

A SIMULATED project pays 120 dollars at the end of each of three periods at \(r = 0.10\). The outlay that makes its NPV zero is \(120 \times 2.486852 = 298.42\) dollars, to the cent. This is the largest outlay the project can bear at that rate and still have a non-negative NPV.

Lab 1 · NPV of SIMULATED projects

Predicted from Propositions 2.1, 3.1, 4.1 and 5.1 before running. On the rate grid 0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20, the NPV of the fixed SIMULATED project falls at each step. The discounted sum and the annuity closed form agree for each of the 200 seeded draws. The NPV of the sum of two projects equals the sum of their NPVs. The replication loan ends at zero. A project whose undiscounted total is positive can have a negative NPV. The projects are SIMULATED: the fixed project and the counterexample are typed in, and the seeded draws in Parts B and C are generated. The lab needs no network.

"""CF1 lab L01: net present value of SIMULATED projects.

SIMULATED: no project in this lab is a real company's. The fixed project in Part A and Part D and the
counterexample in Part E are typed in; the projects in Parts B and C are generated from a seeded random number
generator. Money is entered in integer cents; discounted values are computed in floating point and printed
in dollars to two decimals. Standard library only; no network; every result printed is computed by the code below.
The section titles restate typed-in inputs.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import random
from collections.abc import Sequence

SEED = 20261027
RATE = 0.08
TOLERANCE = 1e-6


def npv_dollars(flows_cents: Sequence[int], rate: float) -> float:
    """Net present value in dollars of flows_cents[t], received at the end of period t, at a rate per period.

    The rate must exceed -1, so that every discount factor (1 + rate) ** -t is finite and positive.
    """
    if rate <= -1.0:
        raise ValueError("the rate must exceed -1")
    total = 0.0
    for t, flow in enumerate(flows_cents):
        total += flow / 100.0 / (1.0 + rate) ** t
    return total


def annuity_npv_dollars(outlay_cents: int, level_cents: int, years: int, rate: float) -> float:
    """Closed form in dollars: minus the outlay plus level times the M1 annuity factor (1 - (1 + r)^-n) / r."""
    factor = (1.0 - (1.0 + rate) ** -years) / rate
    return -outlay_cents / 100.0 + level_cents / 100.0 * factor


def replication_balance_path(flows_cents: Sequence[int], rate: float) -> list[float]:
    """Balance of the loan that replicates the project, in dollars, at each date.

    The owner borrows NPV minus the date-0 flow at date 0 and then pays the project's later flows out of the loan.
    The balance B_t satisfies B_0 = NPV - C_0 and B_t = (1 + rate) * B_(t-1) - C_t, and must end at zero.
    """
    balance = npv_dollars(flows_cents, rate) - flows_cents[0] / 100.0
    path = [balance]
    for t in range(1, len(flows_cents)):
        balance = (1.0 + rate) * balance - flows_cents[t] / 100.0
        path.append(balance)
    return path


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(SEED)

    print("A. One SIMULATED five-year project: outlay of 10,000 dollars, then yearly inflows, in dollars")
    project = [-1_000_000, 300_000, 320_000, 340_000, 360_000, 380_000]
    print("flows:", " ".join(f"{flow / 100:.2f}" for flow in project))
    rates = [0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20]
    values = [npv_dollars(project, r) for r in rates]
    for r, value in zip(rates, values, strict=True):
        print(f"NPV at {r:.2f}: {value:.2f}")
    decreasing = all(a > b for a, b in itertools.pairwise(values))
    print("NPV strictly decreasing in the rate on this grid:", decreasing)
    print(f"NPV at the lab rate {RATE:.2f}: {npv_dollars(project, RATE):.2f}")

    print()
    print("B. Two hundred seeded level-annuity projects: sum of discounted flows against the closed form")
    worst = 0.0
    accepted = 0
    for _ in range(200):
        outlay = rng.randint(50_000, 2_000_000)
        level = rng.randint(10_000, 400_000)
        years = rng.randint(1, 15)
        rate = rng.uniform(0.02, 0.15)
        flows = [-outlay] + [level] * years
        summed = npv_dollars(flows, rate)
        closed = annuity_npv_dollars(outlay, level, years, rate)
        worst = max(worst, abs(summed - closed))
        if summed > 0:
            accepted += 1
    print("largest gap between the two computations, in dollars, below 1e-6:", worst < TOLERANCE)
    print("projects with NPV above zero, out of 200:", accepted)

    print()
    print("C. Additivity: NPV of the sum of two projects equals the sum of their NPVs (fifty seeded pairs)")
    additive = True
    for _ in range(50):
        first = [rng.randint(-500_000, 100_000) for _ in range(4)]
        second = [rng.randint(-500_000, 100_000) for _ in range(4)]
        combined = [a + b for a, b in zip(first, second, strict=True)]
        gap = npv_dollars(combined, RATE) - (npv_dollars(first, RATE) + npv_dollars(second, RATE))
        additive = additive and abs(gap) < TOLERANCE
    print("additivity holds to within 1e-6 dollars for all fifty pairs:", additive)

    print()
    print("D. Replication: a loan of NPV minus the date-0 flow pays the later flows and ends at zero")
    path = replication_balance_path(project, RATE)
    print(f"opening loan balance at date 0, in dollars: {path[0]:.2f}")
    print(f"balance at date 5, in dollars: {path[-1]:.6f}")
    print("the balance ends at zero to within 1e-6 dollars:", abs(path[-1]) < TOLERANCE)

    print()
    print("E. A positive undiscounted sum can hide a negative NPV")
    counterexample = [-100_000, 60_000, 60_000]
    print(f"undiscounted sum, in dollars: {sum(counterexample) / 100:.2f}")
    print(f"NPV at 20 percent, in dollars: {npv_dollars(counterexample, 0.20):.2f}")

6Where the canon breaks

The rule is exact inside Assumption 4.0 and a convention outside it. Four places where the analyst must act:

  • The rate is an input. The rule takes \(r\) as given. Proposition 3.1 shows that the decision can change with \(r\). In Lab 1, Part A, the NPV is 1,211.49 dollars at 0.15 and \(-46.94\) dollars at 0.20. Choosing \(r\) is a judgment about the project's risk, which this lesson does not supply.
  • The one-rate market is an assumption. Proposition 4.1 needs the owner to lend and borrow the same amounts at one rate, with no limit. When borrowing and lending rates differ, the proof of Proposition 4.1 does not apply, and Problem R3 shows the decision can change with the rate that applies.
  • The timing convention is a choice. Flows here fall at period ends. Problem R1 shows that moving every flow half a period earlier multiplies the NPV by \((1+r)^{1/2}\). The sign is unchanged, so the decision is unchanged, but the number is not.
  • The flows are taken as given. The rule values the flows listed. It does not value a choice made later that changes them, such as delaying the project or stopping it, and it does not say how to choose among combinations of projects when a budget limits the total spent.

7Misconceptions

  1. “A project whose cash flows add up to a positive total has a positive NPV.”

    The total ignores when each flow arrives and what the money could earn meanwhile. Take an outlay of 1,000 dollars at period 0 and inflows of 600 dollars at periods 1 and 2. The undiscounted total is \(-1{,}000 + 600 + 600 = 200\) dollars. At \(r = 0.20\), the NPV is \(-1{,}000 + 600/1.2 + 600/1.44 = -83.33\) dollars. Lab 1, Part E, prints the same two numbers. Definition 1.1 is the test that counts timing.

  2. “If the NPV is positive at the rate I chose, the project is worth doing at any rate.”

    Proposition 3.1 says that for a conventional stream, such as this one, the NPV falls as the rate rises, so a positive NPV at one rate can turn negative at a higher one. Lab 1, Part A, prints 7,000.00 dollars at 0.00 and \(-46.94\) dollars at 0.20 for the same project. The decision depends on the rate, so the rate must be justified before the result is read.

  3. “A project's rate is the firm's loan rate by default.”

    The rate in Definition 1.1 is the return on an alternative of the same risk. A loan rate is set by the lender's terms for the borrower, so it need not match the risk of this project's flows. Assumption 4.0 is also stated for one rate at which the owner can lend and borrow the same amounts, so a different rate for the two does not fit the proof. Problem R3 gives the numbers.

Mechanics

M1 · NPV of three flows

An outlay of 100 dollars at period 0 is followed by 60 dollars at the end of period 1 and 60 dollars at the end of period 2. Find the NPV at \(r = 0.10\), to the cent.

Solution

By Definition 1.1, \(\mathrm{NPV} = -100 + 60/1.1 + 60/1.21\). The unrounded terms are \(54.5454\ldots\) and \(49.5868\ldots\), whose sum is \(104.1322\ldots\). So \(\mathrm{NPV} = 4.1322\ldots\), which is 4.13 dollars to the cent. The rounded terms 54.55 and 49.59 sum to 104.14 and give 4.14, which is the rounding error of the kind Remark 1.3 describes, so the unrounded sum is the one to report.

M2 · Annuity closed form

An outlay of 500 dollars at period 0 is followed by 150 dollars at the end of each of periods 1 to 4. Find the NPV at \(r = 0.06\), using Proposition 5.1, to the cent.

Solution

The factor is \(A(0.06, 4) = (1 - 1.06^{-4})/0.06\). Here \(1.06^4 = 1.26247696\), so \(1.06^{-4} = 0.79209366\ldots\). Then \(A = (1 - 0.79209366)/0.06 = 3.46510561\ldots\), computed from the unrounded power. The NPV is \(-500 + 150 \times 3.46510561 = -500 + 519.76584 = 19.76584\), which is 19.77 dollars to the cent.

M3 · Sign change with the rate

A project has flows \(-200\) at period 0 and 120 at periods 1 and 2. Find its NPV at \(r = 0\) and at \(r = 0.20\), and say whether the NPV is larger at the lower rate.

Solution

At \(r = 0\) the NPV is the undiscounted sum, \(-200 + 120 + 120 = 40.00\) dollars. At \(r = 0.20\) it is \(-200 + 120/1.2 + 120/1.44 = -200 + 100 + 83.3333\ldots = -16.6666\ldots\), which is \(-16.67\) dollars to the cent. The NPV is larger at the lower rate, as Proposition 3.1 requires for a conventional stream.

Proof

P1 · Scaling a project

Show that for every \(k > 0\), \(\mathrm{NPV}(r; kC) = k\,\mathrm{NPV}(r; C)\), and that the two have the same sign.

Solution

Each term of \(\mathrm{NPV}(r; kC)\) is \(kC_t(1+r)^{-t}\). Factoring \(k\) out of the finite sum gives \(k\,\mathrm{NPV}(r; C)\). Because \(k > 0\), the number \(k\,x\) has the sign of \(x\) for every real \(x\), so the two NPVs share a sign. The rule's decision is therefore unchanged when every flow is measured in other units, as long as the unit change is a positive factor.

P2 · Proposition 4.1 for two periods

Write out the loan of Proposition 4.1 for flows \(C_0, C_1, C_2\) and show that \(B_2 = 0\) when \(N = \mathrm{NPV}(r)\).

Solution

\(B_0 = N - C_0\). \(B_1 = (1+r)B_0 - C_1 = (1+r)(N - C_0) - C_1\). \(B_2 = (1+r)B_1 - C_2 = (1+r)^2(N - C_0) - (1+r)C_1 - C_2\). Factor \((1+r)^2\): \(B_2 = (1+r)^2\bigl[N - C_0 - C_1(1+r)^{-1} - C_2(1+r)^{-2}\bigr]\). The bracket is \(N - \mathrm{NPV}(r)\), which is zero by the choice of \(N\). So \(B_2 = 0\).

P3 · The NPV ranking reverses with the rate

Project A has flows \((-100, 0, 130)\) and project B has flows \((-100, 120, 0)\). Show that the difference in their NPVs is \((1+r)^{-2}(10 - 120r)\), find the rate at which the two NPVs are equal, and say which project has the larger NPV on each side of that rate.

Solution

\(\mathrm{NPV}_A - \mathrm{NPV}_B = 130(1+r)^{-2} - 120(1+r)^{-1} = (1+r)^{-2}\bigl[130 - 120(1+r)\bigr] = (1+r)^{-2}(10 - 120r)\). The difference is zero at \(r^\ast = 1/12\), which is 8.33 percent. Below that rate the factor \(10 - 120r\) is positive, so A has the larger NPV: at \(r = 0\) the NPVs are 30 and 20 dollars. Above it B has the larger NPV: at \(r = 0.10\) they are 7.44 and 9.09 dollars. At \(r^\ast\) both equal \(-100 + 1440/13 = 10.77\) dollars.

E2 · The undiscounted total and the rule

Lab 1, Part E, prints an undiscounted sum of 200.00 dollars and an NPV of \(-83.33\) dollars at 20 percent. Which number does the rule use, and what is the decision?

Solution

The rule uses the NPV at the stated rate, 20 percent, which is \(-83.33\) dollars. The decision is to reject. The undiscounted total of 200.00 dollars ignores the timing of the flows and is not part of the rule. The lab shows the two numbers side by side so that the reader sees how far they differ.

Research

R1 · Timing within the period

Suppose every flow of a project occurs half a period earlier than the end-of-period convention, so the flow \(C_t\) falls at \(t - \tfrac{1}{2}\). Show that the NPV under this convention is \((1+r)^{1/2}\) times the NPV of Definition 1.1, and state what this implies for the sign of the NPV.

Solution

Under the shifted convention each term is \(C_t(1+r)^{-(t - 1/2)} = (1+r)^{1/2} \cdot C_t(1+r)^{-t}\). Summing over \(t\) gives \(\mathrm{NPV}_{\text{mid}}(r) = (1+r)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{NPV}(r)\). The factor is positive for every \(r > -1\), so the sign of the NPV is unchanged and the decision is unchanged. The size of the number changes by the factor, which matters when the NPV is compared with another figure stated under the other convention.

R2 · NPV and NPV per dollar of outlay

Show that two projects with the same outlay rank the same by NPV and by NPV per dollar of outlay. Then give a pair with different outlays for which the two rankings disagree.

Solution

If the outlays \(|C_0|\) are equal, NPV per dollar is \(\mathrm{NPV}/|C_0|\), a positive multiple of the NPV, so the orderings agree. For a conflict, take project X with outlay 100 and NPV 50 dollars, and project Y with outlay 1,000 and NPV 120 dollars. By NPV, Y ranks first (120 against 50). Per dollar of outlay, X ranks first: 0.50 against 0.12. The ranking depends on which criterion is used, and the rule as stated in Section 1 chooses by NPV.

R3 · Two rates for borrowing and lending

A project has flows \(-1\) at period 0 and \(1.05\) at period 1. Find its value at period 0 when the owner can lend at 3 percent or borrow at 6 percent, and say what the rule decides at each rate.

Solution

A value at period 0 is obtained by discounting the period-1 flow at the rate that applies to the owner's position: the borrowing rate if the owner must borrow to fund the outlay, and the lending rate if the owner can lend the amount. At the borrowing rate of 6 percent the value is \(-1 + 1.05/1.06 = -0.0094\), which is \(-0.009434\ldots\) to six places. At the lending rate of 3 percent it is \(-1 + 1.05/1.03 = +0.0194\), which is \(+0.019417\ldots\). The rule rejects the project at the borrowing rate and accepts it at the lending rate. The decision therefore depends on which rate applies, which is why Assumption 4.0 needs one rate.

10Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Under the NPV decision rule, which project is accepted?

    Options

    The rule reads the NPV at the stated rate, Section 1. The undiscounted total and the size of the outlay are not part of it.

  2. A project costs 100 at period 0 and returns 110 at period 1. What is its NPV at \(r = 0.10\), in dollars?

    \(-100 + 110/1.1 = 0\). The project earns exactly the rate, so its NPV is zero.

  3. For a conventional stream, what does Proposition 3.1 say about the NPV as the rate rises?

    Options

    Each discount factor with \(t \ge 1\) falls as \(r\) rises, so the sum of the nonnegative later flows falls. Only \(C_0\) is left in the limit.

  4. What is the discount factor for period 3 at \(r = 0.10\), to four places?

    \(1.1^{-3} = 0.7513148\ldots\), which is 0.7513 to four places.

  5. Two projects are valued at the same rate. What does Proposition 2.1 say about the NPV of their combined flows?

    Options

    NPV is a finite sum of discounted flows at one rate, so it splits across any sum of streams that share that rate.

  6. What is the annuity factor \(A(0.10, 3)\), to four places?

    \((1 - 1.1^{-3})/0.10 = 2.486852\ldots\), which is 2.4869. Remark 5.2 shows that the rounded power gives 2.4870 instead.

  7. In the proof of Proposition 4.1, what is the opening balance of the replicating loan?

    Options

    \(B_0 = N - C_0\). The owner's period-0 cash is then \(C_0 + (N - C_0) = N\).

  8. Lab 1, Part E, has a positive undiscounted total. What does the lab show about its NPV at 20 percent?

    Options

    The lab prints 200.00 dollars for the total and \(-83.33\) dollars for the NPV at 20 percent. Timing matters.

  9. At what rate, in percent to two places, do projects A and B in Problem P3 have equal NPVs?

    The difference is \((1+r)^{-2}(10 - 120r)\), which is zero at \(r = 1/12 = 8.33\) percent.

  10. Under Assumption 4.0, of two mutually exclusive projects, which leaves more cash at period 0?

    Options

    Corollary 4.2: each project, taken with its replicating loan, leaves exactly its NPV at period 0 and nothing afterwards.
