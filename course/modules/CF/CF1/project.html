The capital-budgeting engine and a case memo

A capital committee has one project on its desk. You write capbudget.py, the engine that turns a project's inputs into after-tax cash flows, net present value, internal rate of return roots, payback, the Fisher relation (the exact link between real and nominal rates) and a seeded Monte Carlo NPV. Then you write a case memo that recommends a decision. The autograder runs your engine on a SIMULATED project generated from your workspace seed. The rubric grades the memo.

Rungs 1 and 2

Lab 1 · Worked example: NPV with tax, depreciation and working capital

The project costs 9,000.00 dollars at t = 0 and runs three years on straight-line depreciation of 3,000.00 dollars a year. EBITDA is revenue less cash cost. The depreciation tax shield is the tax that depreciation saves, depreciation times the tax rate. Before running the code, predict the sign of the working-capital flow in each year: working capital is invested while revenue grows and recovered at the end. Each numbered step prints its own lines. The NPV at 10 percent comes out at 926.00 dollars (step 7), and the shield's present value at 1,865.14 dollars (step 8).

"""CF1 project lab 1 (rung 1, worked example): NPV with tax, depreciation and working capital.

SIMULATED: a three-year machine project written here for teaching. It is not any company's project.
Money is entered in integer cents; every flow is a float in cents, printed in dollars to two decimals. Standard
library only, no randomness, no network. The conventions are those of the specification on the project page:
straight-line depreciation with no salvage; tax at one rate on taxable income, with a negative tax (a credit) when
taxable income is negative; working capital is 10 percent of the year's revenue, held at the end of years 1 and 2
and recovered at the end of year 3.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

CAPEX = 900_000  # cents, paid at t = 0
LIFE = 3  # years
REVENUE = [600_000, 650_000, 700_000]  # cents, years 1 to 3
CASH_COSTS = [200_000, 210_000, 220_000]  # cents, years 1 to 3, entered as positives
TAX_RATE = 0.25
NWC_SHARE = 0.10
RATE = 0.10  # nominal, per year


def usd(cents: float) -> str:
    """Cents shown as dollars with two decimals."""
    return f"{cents / 100:.2f}"


def main() -> None:
    depreciation = CAPEX / LIFE
    print(f"step 1, depreciation: {usd(CAPEX)} / {LIFE} = {usd(depreciation)} dollars a year")
    print(f"step 2, working capital is {NWC_SHARE} of revenue at the end of years 1 and 2, zero at the start and end")
    nwc = [0.0] + [NWC_SHARE * REVENUE[t - 1] for t in range(1, LIFE)] + [0.0]
    print(f"        nwc_1 = {usd(nwc[1])}, nwc_2 = {usd(nwc[2])}")
    flows = [-float(CAPEX)]
    working_capital_flows: list[float] = []
    identity_holds = True
    print("step 3, year by year, in dollars: revenue, cash cost, EBITDA, tax, shield, working capital, flow")
    for t in range(1, LIFE + 1):
        ebitda = REVENUE[t - 1] - CASH_COSTS[t - 1]
        tax = TAX_RATE * (ebitda - depreciation)
        shield = TAX_RATE * depreciation
        working_capital = -(nwc[t] - nwc[t - 1])
        flow = ebitda - tax + working_capital
        flows.append(flow)
        working_capital_flows.append(working_capital)
        identity_holds = identity_holds and abs((ebitda - tax) - (ebitda * (1 - TAX_RATE) + shield)) < 1e-6
        print(
            f"  year {t}: {usd(REVENUE[t - 1])}  {usd(CASH_COSTS[t - 1])}  {usd(ebitda)}  {usd(tax)}  "
            f"{usd(shield)}  {usd(working_capital)}  {usd(flow)}"
        )
    print(
        f"step 4, EBITDA x (1 - tax rate) + shield equals after-tax operating cash flow in every year: {identity_holds}"
    )
    print(f"step 5, the working-capital flows sum to zero: {sum(working_capital_flows) == 0.0}")
    print("step 6, flows at t = 0 to 3, in dollars:", ", ".join(usd(cf) for cf in flows))
    npv = sum(cf / (1.0 + RATE) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(flows))
    print(f"step 7, NPV at {RATE:.2f}: {usd(npv)} dollars")
    shield_pv = sum(TAX_RATE * depreciation / (1.0 + RATE) ** t for t in range(1, LIFE + 1))
    print(f"step 8, present value of the depreciation shield at {RATE:.2f}: {usd(shield_pv)} dollars")
    print(f"step 9, NPV with no shield, npv minus the shield's present value: {usd(npv - shield_pv)} dollars")

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
The committee wants one engine that any analyst can call. Given a project's capital outlay, its revenues and cash costs, the tax rate, the working-capital share and the inflation rate, the engine returns the after-tax cash flows, the net present value at a rate, the internal rate of return roots, the payback, the Fisher conversion between real and nominal rates, and a seeded distribution of NPVs under revenue uncertainty. The memo then recommends a decision on one project, using the engine's numbers and stating its assumptions.
Inputs
A project is a Python dict with seven fields. Money is whole cents on input, and every rate is a decimal per year. capex: a positive integer, the outlay at t = 0. life: an integer of at least 1, in years. revenue: a list of life non-negative numbers, the nominal revenue of years 1 to life. cash_costs: a list of life non-negative numbers, the nominal cash costs of those years, entered as positive numbers. tax_rate: at least 0 and below 1. nwc_share: at least 0 and below 1. inflation: above −1. The autograder's projects come from grader/instance.py: make_instance(seed) returns a dict whose payload field is such a project as a JSON string, with nominal_rate, volatility and mc_draws. Lab 1's project is the rung-1 example.
Required outputs
A file capbudget.py at the root of your repository, defining these six functions with these names and signatures: after_tax_cash_flows(project, real=False) -> list[float]; npv(rate, flows) -> float; irr_roots(flows) -> list[float]; payback_period(flows, rate=0.0) -> float; fisher_nominal(real, inflation) -> float; npv_monte_carlo(project, rate, n_draws, seed, volatility) -> list[float]. A file memo.md, the case memo, of about 600 to 900 words in five sections: recommendation, cash flows, IRR and payback, uncertainty, and evidence.
Constraints
The engine uses the Python standard library and does not import instance.py. Each function is pure: it validates its inputs, never prints or warns, never changes its arguments, and returns fresh objects. Every named error raises ValueError. The autograder checks the exception type; the wording of a message is not graded. The conventions below define the numbers the engine returns, except the IRR roots that lie closer together than one step of the search grid: there the grid decides, and the autograder does not test that case.
Grading mode
Grading: both. The autograder runs the engine on your seed's project. grader/test_project.py collects 74 checks (counted with pytest --collect-only on the reference solution; the count is identical for every seed), and it runs with warnings as errors. The rubric grades the case memo against the five criteria below, with the two annotated sample memos (exemplars) in the memo section.

Conventions, defined once

  • EBITDA in year t is revenue less cash cost in year t. Depreciation is straight-line: capex / life in each year, with no salvage value.
  • Tax in year t is tax_rate × (EBITDA − depreciation). A negative tax is a credit: the firm is assumed to have other taxable income in that year that absorbs the loss.
  • After-tax operating cash flow is EBITDA less tax. Algebra gives EBITDA × (1 − tax_rate) + depreciation × tax_rate. The last term is the depreciation tax shield.
  • Working capital is nwc_t = nwc_share × revenue_t at the end of years 1 to life − 1, zero at the start, and zero at the end of year life, when it is recovered. Its flow in year t is −(nwc_t − nwc_(t−1)), so the flows sum to zero.
  • Cash flows are indexed from t = 0. The flow at 0 is −capex; the flow at t ≥ 1 is the operating flow plus the working-capital flow. The flows are floats in cents and are not rounded.
  • Real flows. With real=True, each flow is divided by (1 + inflation) ** t; the flow at 0 is unchanged. Real flows are discounted at a real rate; nominal flows at a nominal rate.
  • Net present value at rate r is the sum over t of the flow at t divided by (1 + r) ** t. The rate is per period, and must be finite and above −1.
  • The Fisher relation is the exact identity (1 + nominal) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation). It is an identity, not a rule of thumb that adds the two rates.
  • IRR roots are the rates above −1 at which the NPV is zero. The engine searches the discount factor x = 1 / (1 + r) over the interval from 0.0001 to 10000, a rate range from −0.9999 to 9999, sampled at 4,001 points spaced evenly in log x, so that neighbouring points are a factor of 1.0046 apart in 1 + r. Each root the search finds is returned to within 1e-7 in the rate, in ascending order. Two roots that fall between the same pair of neighbouring points can both be missed, because the NPV has the same sign at both points. For example, a three-flow project whose NPV has the roots 0.1 and 0.101 returns neither root from this grid, while one whose NPV has the roots 0.1 and 0.11 returns both; the autograder does not test close pairs. A tangent root, where the NPV touches zero without changing sign, is not reported, and neither is a zero exactly at an end of the window: only a sign change between two samples inside the window is reported. A root outside the window is not reported either. For the flows −1 and +100,000 the root has discount factor 0.00001 (rate 99,999), below the low end of the window; for the flows −20,000 and +1 it has discount factor 20,000 (rate −0.99995), above the high end.
  • Payback is the first year in which the cumulative flow reaches zero or more. With t that year, payback is (t − 1) + (−cumulative at t − 1) / flow at t: a fraction of a year. With rate above zero, each flow is first divided by (1 + rate) ** t. If the outlay is never recovered, payback is math.inf.
  • Monte Carlo NPV. Draw k takes one standard normal number z from random.Random(seed).gauss(0, 1), in draw order, and multiplies every revenue by m = exp(volatility × z − volatility² / 2). Then E[m] = 1. Cash costs are unchanged. The function returns one NPV in cents for each draw, in draw order, at the given rate. The NPV is an affine function of m (a constant plus a multiple of m), so the expected NPV over the draws equals the deterministic NPV at any volatility.
  • Tolerances. NPVs and flows are compared to a relative 1e-9, with an absolute floor of 1e-9 cents. Six checks that sum several products of the flows use an absolute floor of 1e-6 cents instead: the depreciation shield, the working-capital flows (the year-1 flow and every year's flow), the one-year project, the real-rate NPV and the Monte Carlo draws. The NPV at each IRR root is at most 1e-9 times the sum of the absolute flows. The Fisher identity holds to a relative 1e-12, with an absolute floor of 1e-12. The Monte Carlo mean lies within three standard errors of the deterministic NPV. The standard error is the sample standard deviation of the draws, with n − 1 degrees of freedom, divided by the square root of the number n of draws.

Named errors

Each of these raises ValueError: a capex that is not a positive integer number of cents, so a float such as 1000.0 is refused; a life that is not a whole number of at least 1; revenue or cash-cost lists of the wrong length or with a negative or non-finite entry; a tax rate outside 0 to 1, or a working-capital share outside 0 to 1; inflation or a rate at or below −1; an NPV over an empty list of flows; an IRR request with fewer than two flows, or with every flow zero; a payback whose first flow is not negative; a Monte Carlo request with fewer than two draws or a negative volatility. The autograder checks the exception type for each of them; the wording of a message is not graded.

Lab 3 · Reference numbers to check your engine against

Lab 3 · Sanity run on the rung-1 project and a two-root flow

The code below computes, with its own standard-library functions, the numbers your engine should give on two inputs: the rung-1 project of Lab 1 and the flow −100, +230, −132, which has two sign changes and the roots 0.1 and 0.2. It also runs 2,000 seeded revenue draws with volatility 0.2 and seed 42, the draws that npv_monte_carlo makes. Run the engine on these inputs and compare line by line. The engine works in cents, so divide its money values by 100 before comparing them with the dollar lines; rates and roots need no conversion. A line that differs is a defect in one of the two.

"""CF1 project lab 3 (rung 3, sanity run): reference numbers the engine must reproduce.

SIMULATED: the rung-1 project and the two-root flow below are written here. Neither is any company's project.
Money is in integer cents on input; the printed values are dollars or decimal rates. Standard library only. The random
draws use random.Random(42), in order, one gauss(0, 1) per draw, as the specification of npv_monte_carlo.

Run it once and compare each printed line with the engine on the same inputs. A line that differs is a defect in
one of the two. The printed numbers are a check, not a target to copy.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import math
import random

CAPEX = 900_000  # cents
LIFE = 3
REVENUE = [600_000, 650_000, 700_000]  # cents, years 1 to 3
CASH_COSTS = [200_000, 210_000, 220_000]  # cents, years 1 to 3
TAX_RATE = 0.25
NWC_SHARE = 0.10
RATE = 0.10
TWO_ROOT_FLOWS = [-100.0, 230.0, -132.0]  # dollars; the NPV has two sign changes and roots 0.1 and 0.2


def project_flows(revenue_scale: float) -> list[float]:
    """After-tax flows in dollars, t = 0 to 3, with every revenue multiplied by revenue_scale (the rung-1 rules)."""
    depreciation = CAPEX / LIFE
    revenue = [revenue_scale * x for x in REVENUE]
    nwc = [0.0] + [NWC_SHARE * revenue[t - 1] for t in range(1, LIFE)] + [0.0]
    flows = [-CAPEX / 100.0]
    for t in range(1, LIFE + 1):
        ebitda = revenue[t - 1] - CASH_COSTS[t - 1]
        tax = TAX_RATE * (ebitda - depreciation)
        flows.append((ebitda - tax - (nwc[t] - nwc[t - 1])) / 100.0)
    return flows


def npv(rate: float, flows: list[float]) -> float:
    """Net present value: sum of flows[t] / (1 + rate) ** t, t = 0 first."""
    return sum(cf / (1.0 + rate) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(flows))


def roots(flows: list[float]) -> list[float]:
    """Sign changes of the NPV on the grid -0.5 to 1.0, each bracket bisected for 60 halvings."""
    grid = [-0.5 + 0.0005 * k for k in range(3001)]
    found: list[float] = []
    for lo, hi in itertools.pairwise(grid):
        if npv(lo, flows) * npv(hi, flows) < 0.0:
            a, b = lo, hi
            for _ in range(60):
                mid = 0.5 * (a + b)
                if npv(a, flows) * npv(mid, flows) <= 0.0:
                    b = mid
                else:
                    a = mid
            found.append(0.5 * (a + b))
    return found


def payback(flows: list[float], rate: float = 0.0) -> float:
    """Payback in fractional years on flows discounted at rate: (t - 1) + (-cumulative at t - 1) / flow at t."""
    discounted = [cf / (1.0 + rate) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(flows)]
    cumulative = discounted[0]
    for t in range(1, len(discounted)):
        if cumulative + discounted[t] >= 0.0:
            return (t - 1) + (-cumulative) / discounted[t]
        cumulative += discounted[t]
    return math.inf


def monte_carlo_draws(volatility: float, draws: int, seed: int) -> list[float]:
    """NPV in dollars of each draw, each scaling all revenue by m = exp(volatility * z - volatility ** 2 / 2)."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    out: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(draws):
        m = math.exp(volatility * rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0) - volatility * volatility / 2.0)
        out.append(npv(RATE, project_flows(m)))
    return out


def main() -> None:
    base = project_flows(1.0)
    print("rung-1 flows at t = 0 to 3, dollars:", ", ".join(f"{cf:.2f}" for cf in base))
    print(f"NPV of the rung-1 project at {RATE:.2f}: {npv(RATE, base):.2f} dollars")
    print("IRR roots of the two-root flow, to 6 decimals:", [round(r, 6) for r in roots(TWO_ROOT_FLOWS)])
    print("IRR roots of the rung-1 project, to 6 decimals:", [round(r, 6) for r in roots(base)])
    print(f"simple payback of the rung-1 project: {payback(base):.6f} years")
    print(f"discounted payback of the rung-1 project at {RATE:.2f}: {payback(base, RATE):.6f} years")
    print(f"simple payback of [-100, 60, 60]: {payback([-100.0, 60.0, 60.0]):.6f} years")
    nominal = (1.0 + 0.05) * (1.0 + 0.03) - 1.0
    print(f"Fisher nominal rate for real 0.05 and inflation 0.03: {nominal:.10f}")
    zero = monte_carlo_draws(0.0, 10, 42)
    print(f"Monte Carlo mean with zero volatility, 10 draws: {sum(zero) / len(zero):.2f} dollars")
    draws = monte_carlo_draws(0.2, 2000, 42)
    n = len(draws)
    mean = sum(draws) / n
    sd = math.sqrt(sum((d - mean) ** 2 for d in draws) / (n - 1))
    print(f"Monte Carlo with volatility 0.2, 2000 draws, seed 42: mean {mean:.2f} dollars")
    print(f"standard deviation {sd:.2f} dollars, standard error of the mean {sd / math.sqrt(n):.2f} dollars")
    negative = sum(1 for d in draws if d < 0.0)
    print(f"draws with a negative NPV: {negative} of {n}")
    print(f"lowest draw {min(draws):.2f} dollars, highest draw {max(draws):.2f} dollars")


main()

Rung 3 · The case memo

The memo recommends accept, reject or defer for the rung-1 project or for a project of your own, under the engine's conventions. Write it in five sections, in this order: 1 Recommendation, 2 Cash flows, 3 IRR and payback, 4 Uncertainty, 5 Evidence. Take every number from your engine's output or from a lab, state its unit, and state the discount rate and its basis. Give a rule for the decision before you show the numbers that apply it.

For the evidence section, cite registry keys. The curriculum names graham2001theory as a source for this unit; for any statement taken from a source, give the locator. Mark each claim with its grade (T, A, B, C, D or X, as defined in the course's evidence standard). A claim about what firms do, with no confirmed locator, is graded C at most and quotes no figure.

The rubric

Two exemplars

Two memos written to this brief, on the rung-1 project, at two levels. Each number in them is printed by Lab 1 or Lab 3, or is computed from those printed values, with the calculation shown. Each passage carries an annotation that names the rubric criteria it meets or misses. The official scoring is shown beside each exemplar.

Exemplar A

Official scoring: C1 2 · C2 2 · C3 2 · C4 2 · C5 2.

1 Recommendation

Accept. The IRR is 15.32 percent, which is above the 10 percent rate, so the project is good.

C1, level 2: the recommendation rests on the IRR alone, and the NPV is not stated with its rate. The rate is named only as "the 10 percent rate", with no basis.

2 Cash flows

The depreciation shield is worth 750 dollars a year. Working capital is treated as a cost in year 3 and is not recovered.

C2, level 2: the working-capital recovery is misread. Lab 1, step 3 shows a recovery of 650 dollars in year 3, and the working-capital flows sum to zero (step 5).

3 IRR and payback

The IRR is 15.32 percent. Payback is 2.37 years.

C3, level 2: both figures are correct, but no sign-change count is given, and the memo does not say how many IRR roots exist. The number is correct for this SIMULATED flow (Lab 3). The discounted payback is missing.

4 Uncertainty

A simulated run gives a mean of 885.85 dollars. The seed is not recorded.

C4, level 2: the run cannot be reproduced without its seed, draw count and volatility. The mean matches Lab 3 for seed 42, 2,000 draws and volatility 0.2, and the memo should have stated those three settings.

5 Evidence

Practitioners use payback.

C5, level 2: a practice claim with no key, no grade and no locator. A claim of this kind needs a registry key and a grade of C at most, and it quotes no figure.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 4.

1 Recommendation

Decision rule, stated before the numbers: accept an investment when its NPV at the stated rate is positive. The rate is 10 percent nominal per year, a choice made for this exercise and not a sourced market rate. The NPV at 10 percent is 926.00 dollars (Lab 1, step 7). The recommendation is accept. The NPV reaches zero at an IRR of 15.3177 percent (Lab 3), so the recommendation reverses at that rate. Each flow after t = 0 is positive, so for every rate above −1 each discount term falls as the rate rises and the NPV falls with it; the NPV therefore crosses zero once, at the IRR that Lab 3 finds.

C1, level 4: the rule comes first; the rate and its basis are stated; the recommendation follows from the NPV; the reversal rate is named and computed.

2 Cash flows

The rung-1 project has no inflation term, so its flows are nominal and are discounted at the nominal rate of 10 percent. Flows in dollars at t = 0 to 3: −9,000.00, 3,150.00, 4,000.00 and 5,000.00 (Lab 1, step 6). The depreciation shield is 750.00 dollars a year, with a present value of 1,865.14 dollars (Lab 1, step 8). Working capital is invested as −600.00 and −50.00 and recovered as +650.00 (Lab 1, step 3); the three working-capital flows sum to zero (Lab 1, step 5).

C2, level 4: every flow includes the shield and the recovery; the real or nominal basis is stated, and the rate matches it.

3 IRR and payback

The flow has one sign change, which bounds the IRRs above −1 at one; that algebraic bound is not proved on this page. The root is 15.3177 percent (Lab 3). Simple payback is 2.37 years, found by interpolation in year 3 (Lab 3). Discounted at 10 percent, payback is 2.7535 years. If this project is ranked against another that differs in scale, the NPV counts the extra outlay and the IRR does not (Lesson 4, Remark 4.3); Lesson 4 takes up the ranking conflicts between NPV and IRR.

C3, level 4: the sign-change count and the root count it implies are stated; both paybacks are reported; the NPV-versus-IRR conflict is named, with the lesson that takes it up.

4 Uncertainty

Each draw multiplies revenue by m = exp(0.2 z − 0.02), where z is one standard normal number from random.Random(42).gauss(0, 1); the mean of m is one. Volatility 0.2, seed 42, 2,000 draws. The mean NPV of the draws is 885.85 dollars, with a sample standard deviation of 2,445.52 dollars and a standard error of 54.68 dollars (Lab 3). The deterministic NPV is 926.00 dollars. The gap is 40.15 dollars (926.00 − 885.85, from the printed values; from the unrounded values it lies between 40.14 and 40.15), which is 0.73 standard errors (40.15 ÷ 54.68 = 0.73). The share of negative draws is 787 of 2,000, or 39.35 percent. The lowest draw is −4,808.83 dollars and the highest 13,800.44 dollars.

C4, level 4: the seed, draw count and volatility are stated; the mean-one assumption is stated; the mean is compared with the deterministic NPV in standard errors, with the calculation shown.

5 Evidence

The curriculum names Graham and Harvey (2001), Journal of Financial Economics 60(2–3), 187–243 as a source for this unit; its registry title is "The theory and practice of corporate finance: evidence from the field". This memo states no finding from it, because the locator for any finding has not been confirmed; the memo quotes no figure from it. The Monte Carlo method is cited to Glasserman (2003), Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering; no result from that book is quoted, and the simulation numbers above are SIMULATED outputs of the engine.

C5, level 4: each source carries its key and grade; no unlocated figure is quoted; the SIMULATED numbers are marked as engine or lab outputs.

Self-assessment and calibration

Score your own memo on each criterion first. Then score Exemplars A and B with their annotations hidden, and reveal the official scoring. The table then shows, criterion by criterion, where your scoring of each exemplar diverged from it. A criterion on which you were more generous to an exemplar than the rubric is one on which to re-read your own memo. Your scores stay in this browser.

Rung 4 · Stretch

  • A term structure of discount rates. Replace the single nominal rate with rates for each year, r_1 to r_life, using the discount factor that compounds them. State a hypothesis about how the rates rise or fall, compute the NPV on the rung-1 project under that structure, and say what result would falsify the hypothesis.
  • A sensitivity table. Compute the NPV of the rung-1 project over a grid of revenue multipliers from 0.8 to 1.2, and report the multiplier at which the NPV is zero, with its method. This is a deterministic sensitivity, not a real option; real options belong to CF4.
