Time Value & Capital Budgeting

A project spends money now and returns money later, and the capital budget decides which projects are worth that exchange. This module builds the tools for the decision. You will discount dated cash flows, construct the after-tax flows a project actually causes, separate real from nominal money, examine the internal rate of return where it misleads, test payback against the same project, and estimate the spread of an NPV by simulation. The module ends in a capital-budgeting engine, graded by an autograder and by a case memo that you write.

  • Prerequisites: M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning (its present-value, annuity and perpetuity concepts), AC1 Financial Accounting. M1 assumes P0.1, How to Study This Program.
  • Project: Capital-budgeting engine (autograded, with a rubric-graded case memo)

48 h lessons · 12 h project (20.0%)

Objectives

By the end of CF1 you can:

  1. Compute the net present value of dated flows at a stated rate, and show how the sign of the NPV changes with that rate.
  2. Construct the incremental, after-tax cash flows of a project, including the depreciation tax shield and the working capital it ties up, and check each flow against its identity.
  3. Convert between real and nominal cash flows and rates, and show that a project discounted consistently in either terms gives the same NPV.
  4. Locate the multiple-IRR problem and the NPV and IRR ranking conflicts in a project, and state which criterion the decision should follow.
  5. Compare simple and discounted payback with the NPV of the same project, and state which cash flows payback ignores.
  6. Estimate the distribution of an NPV by seeded simulation, and check the estimate for convergence against a case with a closed form.

Prerequisites

CF1 assumes M1 (Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning) and AC1 (Financial Accounting), and everything they rest on, including P0.1 (How to Study This Program), which M1 assumes. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From M1: present value, discount factor, annuity, perpetuity, growing perpetuity, geometric series and compounding frequency.
  • From AC1: the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash-flow statement, depreciation and amortization and accrual accounting.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: five on M1, two on AC1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct, since seven is 78 percent. Skip rungs 1 and 2 only if you reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, revisit M1 and AC1 first.

  1. What is the present value at 10 percent of 110 received at the end of one period?

    Options

    \(110/1.1 = 100\) (M1, present value).

  2. What is the present value at 10 percent of 100 received at the end of each of three periods, to the cent?

    The annuity factor is \((1 - 1.1^{-3})/0.1 = 2.486852\ldots\), and \(100 \times 2.486852 = 248.69\) (M1, annuity).

  3. A perpetuity pays 50 at the end of every period, forever, at 5 percent. What is its present value?

    The present value of a level perpetuity is \(C/r = 50/0.05 = 1{,}000\) (M1, perpetuity).

  4. A growing perpetuity pays 100 at the end of period 1, grows at 2 percent a period, and is discounted at 6 percent a period. Which expression gives its present value?

    Options

    The growing perpetuity with first payment \(C\), growth \(g\) and rate \(r > g\) is worth \(C/(r-g)\) (M1, growing perpetuity).

  5. A deposit of 1,000 dollars earns 5 percent a year, compounded annually. What is its balance after two years, in dollars?

    \(1{,}000 \times 1.05^2 = 1{,}102.50\) (M1, compounding frequency).

  6. Which identity holds for every balance sheet?

    Options

    The balance sheet identity: total assets equal total liabilities plus equity (AC1, balance sheet).

  7. Why is depreciation added back to net income in operating cash flow?

    Options

    Depreciation is a non-cash charge in the income statement (AC1, depreciation and amortization).

  8. Skill check (rung 1). A machine costs 600 dollars and is depreciated straight-line over two years to zero. Its EBITDA is 400 dollars in each year, and the tax rate is 25 percent. What is the after-tax operating cash flow in year 1, in dollars?

    Depreciation is 300 dollars. Then \(\mathrm{OCF} = 400 \times 0.75 + 0.25 \times 300 = 300 + 75 = 375\) dollars (CF1 Lesson 2, Proposition 2.2).

  9. Skill check (rung 2). Depreciation is 300 dollars a year and the tax rate is 25 percent. What is the depreciation tax shield, in dollars a year?

    The shield is \(\tau D = 0.25 \times 300 = 75\) dollars (CF1 Lesson 2, Corollary 2.3).

Lessons

Six lessons of eight study hours each. Every lesson has graded claims, a runnable lab, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck. The lessons follow the capital budget in order: the rule, the cash flows, inflation, the rate of return, payback, and simulation.

L01 · 8 hThe Net Present Value Rule and the Capital BudgetDated flows, the NPV rule, additivity over projects, the fall of NPV with the rate, and replication by borrowing and lending at one rate. L02 · 8 hProject Cash Flows: Incremental, Tax and Working CapitalIncremental flows and sunk costs, the after-tax operating cash flow, the depreciation tax shield, and working capital that is invested and recovered. L03 · 8 hInflation: Real and Nominal Cash FlowsThe Fisher relation as an exact identity, and the same project discounted consistently in real and in nominal terms. L04 · 8 hInternal Rate of Return and Its PitfallsThe IRR, its relation to the NPV, multiple IRRs with two sign changes, and the NPV and IRR ranking conflict. L05 · 8 hPayback, and What Firms Actually UseSimple and discounted payback on one project, what payback ignores after the cut-off, and the two-sided evidence on how firms use it. L06 · 8 hSimulation-Based NPVA seeded Monte Carlo distribution of NPV, its standard error, and a convergence check against a case with a closed form.

The project ladder

The module ends in the capital-budgeting engine. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. You skip them only by reaching the placement bar and answering both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated after-tax NPV on a small SIMULATED project, with the reason for every line.

  2. Completion problem

    The after-tax cash-flow line of the same project with its key steps removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the engine to the project specification: after-tax cash flows, NPV and IRR roots, payback, the Fisher relation and a seeded Monte Carlo NPV. Your workspace seed generates your projects. The autograder checks the engine, and the rubric grades the case memo. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An NPV under a term structure of discount rates on the same project, with a stated hypothesis and a test on your own generated projects.
