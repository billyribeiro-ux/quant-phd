Governance Scorecard

An institutional shareholder advisory committee requires a quantitative evaluation of corporate governance structures across a portfolio of corporate issuers. You write governance_scorecard.py, the analytical engine that ingests proxy-statement anti-takeover provisions, computes the Bebchuk, Cohen & Ferrell (2008) entrenchment index (E-index) and Gompers, Ishii & Metrick (2003) governance index (G-index), calculates Tobin's Q approximations from integer-cent balance sheet data, estimates bivariate cross-sectional OLS regressions with exact degrees-of-freedom normalization, and evaluates analytical statistical power. Then you draft an executive case memorandum (memo.md) presenting your empirical findings, interpreting valuation discounts, and articulating honest small-sample statistical power caveats. The project uses dual grading: the autograder tests your engine across 50 pseudo-random instance seeds, while the rubric evaluates your case memo.

Follow the four rungs in sequence. If you demonstrated mastery on the module hub placement quiz and answered both skill checks, you may proceed directly to rung 3.

    print(f"  t-statistic:          {t_stat:.4f}")
    print(f"  R-squared:            {r2:.4%}")

    # Analytical statistical power at alpha_sig = 0.05
    dist = NormalDist()
    z_crit = dist.inv_cdf(1.0 - 0.05 / 2.0)
    delta = abs(beta) / se_beta
    power = (1.0 - dist.cdf(z_crit - delta)) + dist.cdf(-z_crit - delta)

    print("\nStatistical Power Analysis (Small-Sample Caveat):")
    print(f"  Significance level:   alpha = 0.05 (two-sided critical z = {z_crit:.4f})")
    print(f"  Non-centrality delta: |beta| / se = {delta:.4f}")
    print(f"  Statistical power:    {power:.2%}")
    print("  Interpretation:       With N = 10 firms, power is low (~30-40%).")
    print("                        Failing to reject H0: beta = 0 does NOT prove governance does not matter;")
    print("                        it reflects inadequate sample size to detect modest economic effects.")

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
An institutional investment committee requires an algorithmic governance scoring engine (governance_scorecard.py) and an executive case memorandum (memo.md) evaluating governance quality across corporate issuers. The engine ingests binary anti-takeover provisions from annual proxy filings (Form DEF 14A) and integer-cent accounting data, constructs governance indices, computes Tobin's Q, estimates bivariate valuation regressions, and evaluates statistical power.
Inputs
The estimation engine ingests a panel of 10 corporate issuers generated by make_instance(seed: int):
  1. Provisions dictionary: Binary indicators (0 or 1) for 10 anti-takeover provisions, containing the 6 Bebchuk et al. (2008) entrenchment provisions (staggered_board, poison_pill, golden_parachute, limit_bylaw_amendment, limit_charter_amendment, supermajority_merger) and 4 additional provisions.
  2. Financial fundamentals: Total assets (assets_cents), total debt (debt_cents), book equity (book_equity_cents), and market capitalization (market_equity_cents) in integer cents.
  3. Significance parameters: Test significance level \(\alpha_{\mathrm{sig}} = 0.05\).
Required outputs
Two core deliverables:
  • governance_scorecard.py: Standard-library Python module implementing the seven required functions: compute_e_index, compute_g_index, compute_tobins_q, cross_sectional_ols, statistical_power_t_test, score_firm_panel, and governance_regression.
  • memo.md: Executive case memorandum (1–2 pages) presenting the scored firm table, OLS regression statistics, statistical power analysis, and governance policy recommendations.
Constraints
  • Financial fundamentals must be computed and ingested in integer cents; index scores and provision counts are integers; regression statistics and rates are floats.
  • Standard library only: no external dependencies (numpy, pandas, scipy). Use math and statistics.NormalDist.
  • OLS degrees of freedom for residual variance must strictly use Bessel normalization \(n - 2\).
  • All invalid inputs (e.g. non-binary provisions, missing core provisions, non-positive assets, negative debt or equity, sample size \(n < 3\), zero variance in explanatory variable, non-positive standard error) must raise ValueError.
Grading
Grading: both autograder and rubric. The autograder runs the automated test suite across 50 pseudo-random instance seeds to verify exact mathematical correctness, degrees-of-freedom normalization, and input validation. The rubric grades the executive case memo across five analytical criteria.

Exemplars and scoring calibration

Two memos on the reference portfolio (Seed 42), illustrating Level 4 and Level 2 performance:

Exemplar A (Level 4 · Exemplary)

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 4.

1 Provision Extraction & Classification

Portfolio consists of 10 corporate issuers evaluated from annual proxy statements (Form DEF 14A) under a SIMULATED data regime (Seed 42). We extract 10 binary anti-takeover provisions per firm, isolating the 6 core entrenchment provisions identified by Bebchuk, Cohen & Ferrell (2008): staggered board, poison pill, golden parachute, limits to amend bylaws, limits to amend charter, and supermajority merger requirements. Total assets range from $82.49M (Beacon Capital) to $444.21M (Delta Semiconductor), with debt leverage \(D/A\) spanning 26.1% to 47.6%.

C1, Level 4: Clear identification of 10 issuers, exact provision taxonomy based on verified literature, integer-cent balance sheet scale, and prominent SIMULATED disclosure.

2 Governance Index Construction

We construct the Bebchuk et al. (2008) entrenchment index \(E_i \in [0, 6]\) by unweighted summation of the 6 core provisions, and the Gompers et al. (2003) index \(G_i \in [0, 10]\) over all provisions. In our sample, \(E_i\) ranges from 1 (Delta Semiconductor) to 5 (Echo Logistics, Ironclad Materials), with mean \(\bar{E} = 3.20\). Unweighted summation reflects literature consensus (Gompers et al. 2003; Bebchuk et al. 2008), avoiding arbitrary subjective weighting schemes while capturing cumulative anti-takeover resistance.

C2, Level 4: Exact mathematical index formulations, sample distribution summary, and literature justification for unweighted provision indices.

3 Valuation & OLS Estimation

Using the Chung-Pruitt approximation \(Q = (Assets - Book\ Equity + Market\ Equity) / Assets\), sample Tobin's Q averages 2.200 (range: 1.731 to 2.640). Bivariate OLS of Tobin's Q on E-index yields:

\[ Q_i = 2.9682 - 0.2401 \cdot E_i + e_i. \]

Using exact Bessel normalization with \(n - 2 = 8\) degrees of freedom, the residual standard deviation is \(s = 0.1402\), yielding \(\mathrm{se}(\hat{\beta}) = 0.0355\), \(t = -6.762\), and \(R^2 = 85.11\%\). Economically, each additional entrenchment provision is associated with a 0.240 drop in Tobin's Q (approximately 10.9% of sample mean valuation).

C3, Level 4: Complete OLS reporting with n - 2 degrees of freedom, standard error, t-stat, R^2, and economic magnitude interpretation.

4 Statistical Power & Sample Size Caveats

With \(N = 10\) observations, classical hypothesis testing faces severe small-sample power limitations. Under a true economic slope of \(\beta = -0.10\) and noise variance \(\sigma^2 = 0.05\), the analytical non-centrality parameter is \(\delta = |\beta| / \mathrm{se}(\beta) = 0.10 / 0.075 = 1.33\), yielding statistical power of only \(1 - \Phi(1.96 - 1.33) + \Phi(-1.96 - 1.33) \approx 26.5\%\) at \(\alpha_{\mathrm{sig}} = 0.05\). Consequently, an institutional investor must recognize that failing to reject \(H_0: \beta = 0\) in a 10-firm portfolio provides virtually no evidence of governance irrelevance; it primarily reflects the small sample size.

C4, Level 4: Rigorous power derivation, explicit non-centrality calculation, and sophisticated interpretation of small-sample failure to reject null.

5 Literature Evidence & Policy Limits

Our findings align with Bebchuk, Cohen & Ferrell (2008, citing bebchuk2008matters) and Gompers, Ishii & Metrick (2003, citing gompers2003corporate). However, cross-sectional regressions cannot establish causality: entrenchment provisions may be endogenously adopted by firms facing operational stagnation or takeover threats (reverse causality). Committees should combine index scoring with qualitative assessments of board independence (shleifer1997survey) and managerial equity incentives (jensen1976theory).

C5, Level 4: Connects results to verified registry citations with evidence grades, analyzes endogeneity and reverse causality, and provides nuanced policy recommendations.

Rung 4 · Stretch

In advanced corporate governance research, cross-sectional OLS regressions suffer from severe endogeneity: unobserved firm quality may simultaneously determine both governance structures and valuation multiples (omitted variable bias), or poorly performing firms may adopt defensive provisions to fend off hostile acquirers (reverse causality). To address this, implement a two-stage least squares (2SLS) instrumental variables regression or firm fixed-effects panel estimator using state-level takeover legislation (e.g., Delaware business combination statutes) as an exogenous instrument for firm-level anti-takeover defenses, following the empirical strategy of Bebchuk, Cohen & Ferrell (2008).
