Payout, Governance & Agency

How should corporations return cash to investors, and how do agency frictions and internal and external governance institutions align managerial decisions with shareholder value? This module investigates corporate payout policy and the economics of corporate governance. You will model the mechanics of cash dividends versus share repurchases, analyze Modigliani–Miller payout irrelevance and its empirical breakdowns, estimate the Lintner partial-adjustment model of dividend smoothing, formulate agency costs of equity and debt, explore the free-cash-flow agency problem and the disciplinary role of debt commitments, examine the monitoring and advisory trade-offs of corporate boards, dissect executive compensation incentives, and evaluate shareholder rights indices.

  • Prerequisites: CF2 Cost of Capital & Capital Structure, E2 Game Theory & Information Economics. Assumes foundational calculus and probability (M1, M4, S1).
  • Project: Governance scorecard (autograded engine plus rubric-graded memo, 20 h)

40 h lessons · 20 h project (33.3% of the 60 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CF3 you can:

  1. Compare cash dividends and share repurchases under frictionless markets and differential personal taxation, proving shareholder wealth conservation.
  2. Prove the Modigliani–Miller (1961) payout irrelevance theorem and delineate the specific market frictions causing empirical non-neutrality.
  3. Estimate the Lintner (1956) partial-adjustment model of corporate dividends, recovering speed-of-adjustment parameters and target payout ratios.
  4. Evaluate dividend signaling mechanisms under information asymmetry, explaining asymmetric market reactions to dividend increases versus cuts.
  5. Formulate the Jensen and Meckling (1976) agency costs of outside equity and debt, deriving the deadweight loss from managerial perquisite consumption.
  6. Analyze the free-cash-flow agency problem (Michael Jensen 1986) and quantify how hard debt commitments curb negative-NPV overinvestment.
  7. Model internal board governance, solving for the optimal fraction of independent directors balancing external monitoring against advisory information costs.
  8. Construct shareholder rights indices (Gompers et al. 2003; Bebchuk et al. 2008) and assess cross-sectional valuation relations under small-sample power constraints.

Placement diagnostic

Test your preparation before starting CF3. Achieving 80% mastery demonstrates readiness to advance directly to the project ladder.

  1. Under perfect capital markets (no taxes, no transaction costs, no asymmetric information), how does replacing cash dividends with share repurchases affect shareholder wealth?

    Options

    Under Modigliani–Miller (1961), dividend policy and payout form are completely irrelevant to shareholder wealth in frictionless markets.

  2. A firm has a share price of $50.00 immediately prior to going ex-dividend. If it distributes a cash dividend of $5.00 per share, what is the theoretical ex-dividend share price in dollars under perfect markets?

    In perfect capital markets, the ex-dividend share price drops by the exact cash dividend amount: \(50.00 - 5.00 = 45.00\) dollars.

  3. What empirical regularity regarding corporate dividend policy was first documented by John Lintner (1956)?

    Options

    Lintner (1956) established that managers smooth dividends toward a target payout ratio with partial annual adjustment.

  4. If a firm's speed of dividend adjustment is \(c = 0.32\) per year, what is the half-life of a dividend deviation from target in years? (Formula: \(\ln(0.5)/\ln(1 - c)\)).

    \(t_{1/2} = \ln(0.5) / \ln(1 - 0.32) = -0.69315 / \ln(0.68) = -0.69315 / (-0.38566) \approx 1.80\) years.

  5. In Jensen and Meckling (1976), what is the primary source of the agency cost of outside equity?

    Options

    Diluted managerial ownership (\(\alpha < 1\)) creates an incentive to over-consume private perks because outside shareholders bear \(1 - \alpha\) of the cash cost.

  6. According to Michael Jensen's (1986) free-cash-flow hypothesis, how does issuing debt improve corporate governance?

    Options

    Hard debt service acts as a disciplinary bonding mechanism, draining excess cash flow and preventing wasteful overinvestment.

  7. Skill check (rung 1). A firm's proxy statement discloses the following binary provisions: staggered board (1), poison pill (1), golden parachute (1), limits to amend bylaws (0), limits to amend charter (0), supermajority for mergers (0). Compute the Bebchuk, Cohen & Ferrell (2008) E-index score.

    The E-index is the unweighted sum of the 6 core entrenchment provisions: \(1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 3\).

  8. Skill check (rung 2). A firm has total book assets of 100 million dollars, book equity of 60 million dollars, debt of 40 million dollars, and market equity of 110 million dollars. Compute its Tobin's Q approximation: \((Assets - Book\ Equity + Market\ Equity) / Assets\).

    \(Q = (100 - 60 + 110) / 100 = 150 / 100 = 1.50\).

Lessons

Six lessons totaling 40 study hours. Every lesson features rigorous mathematical definitions, analytical proofs, runnable inline Python labs, common misconceptions with evidence, four tiers of problem sets with worked solutions, a key-results summary table, and a spaced-retrieval diagnostic quiz.

L01 · 7 h Dividends vs. Buybacks & Payout Irrelevance Cash dividend vs share repurchase mechanics, the Modigliani–Miller (1961) payout irrelevance theorem, homemade dividends, and empirical tax wedges. L02 · 7 h Dividend Dynamics: Lintner Model & Signaling John Lintner (1956) partial-adjustment model, dividend smoothing, adjustment speed, half-life estimation, and costly dividend signaling under asymmetric information. L03 · 7 h Agency Costs of Equity and Debt Jensen and Meckling (1976) separation of ownership and control, perquisite consumption, monitoring and bonding costs, residual loss, and debt agency conflicts. L04 · 7 h Free Cash Flow and Corporate Control Michael Jensen (1986) free cash flow hypothesis, managerial empire building, debt service as a bonding device, and corporate control mechanisms. L05 · 6 h Boards and Corporate Governance Legal and economic functions of boards of directors, independent versus inside directors, optimal monitoring versus advisory information costs, and board size effects. L06 · 6 h Executive Compensation & Governance Indices Executive incentive compensation, Gompers et al. (2003) G-Index, Bebchuk et al. (2008) E-Index, and cross-sectional valuation relations under attenuation noise.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Walk through an end-to-end governance scoring and cross-sectional OLS regression on 10 corporate issuers with balance sheet fundamentals.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion

    Complete the core calculations of E-index, Tobin's Q, OLS Bessel normalization, and statistical power in the interactive completion lab.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Implement governance_scorecard.py to pass the 50-seed autograder suite and draft an executive case memorandum evaluated by the rubric.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Explore endogeneity, reverse causality, and instrumental variables (2SLS) in corporate governance empirical finance.
