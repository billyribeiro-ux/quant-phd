The 13-Week Direct Cash-Flow Forecast

  • Uses: DSO, DIO and DPO, trade credit and credit policy, the cash-flow statement
  • Introduces: the 13-week direct cash-flow forecast
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A treasurer needs one number for each week: will the company hold enough cash to make its payments? The 13-week direct cash-flow forecast answers that question by listing the cash that arrives and leaves each week, and by stating what the credit line does when cash falls short. This lesson defines the forecast, proves that its weekly cash identity holds by construction, and proves when the minimum-cash rule holds. Lab 1 runs one SIMULATED 13-week case.

1Receipts, disbursements and the cash identity

Definition 1.1 (13-week direct cash-flow forecast)

Let \(H = 13\) be the horizon in weeks, and index the weeks by \(w = 1, \ldots, H\). Week \(w\) ends on day \(7w\), and every flow of week \(w\) is dated at that week end. A 13-week direct cash-flow forecast is a schedule, for each week, of cash receipts \(\mathrm{Rec}_w\) and cash disbursements \(\mathrm{Dis}_w\), in integer cents, together with the opening cash \(C_0\), in integer cents. The forecast is direct because it lists cash that moves in the week. An accrual forecast of sales or expenses is not a direct forecast, because it records obligations before any cash moves.

Definition 1.2 (cash before credit-line movements)

Let \(C_w\) be the ending cash of week \(w\). The weekly net flow is \(\nu_w = \mathrm{Rec}_w - \mathrm{Dis}_w\). The cash before credit-line movements in week \(w\) is \(P_w = C_{w-1} + \nu_w\). It is the cash the company would hold at week end if it neither drew on nor repaid its credit line in that week.

Theorem 1.3 (weekly cash identity)

Assumptions. (1) Receipts, disbursements, draws and repayments are integers in cents. (2) In week \(w\), the draw is \(\mathrm{Dr}_w\), the repayment is \(\mathrm{Rp}_w\), and the ending cash is \(C_w = P_w + \mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w\).

Statement. For every week \(w\) from 1 to \(H\), \[ C_w = C_{w-1} + \mathrm{Rec}_w - \mathrm{Dis}_w + \mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w, \] and therefore \[ C_H = C_0 + \sum_{w=1}^{H} \nu_w + \sum_{w=1}^{H} (\mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w). \]

Proof

Assumption (2) and Definition 1.2 give \(C_w = C_{w-1} + \nu_w + \mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w\), which is the first display because \(\nu_w = \mathrm{Rec}_w - \mathrm{Dis}_w\). Summing the first display over \(w = 1, \ldots, H\) makes \(C_{w-1}\) cancel against the \(C_{w-1}\) term of the next week, leaving \(C_H - C_0\) equal to the two sums. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.4 (an identity is not a forecast)

Theorem 1.3 holds for any receipts and any disbursements. A forecast whose receipts are wrong still satisfies the identity, so a passing identity check says nothing about the receipts. The check confirms that the arithmetic is consistent. Whether the receipts are right is a separate question, and this lesson does not answer it. Lab 1 checks the identity in every week, in integer cents, and that is the whole of what it checks about the forecast's accuracy.

2The credit line and the minimum-cash rule

The receipts line is where trade-credit terms enter the forecast, because the terms set when cash arrives. The disbursements line is where payment timing enters. This lesson takes both lines as inputs. What it adds is the rule for the credit line, which is the committed facility that the company can draw when cash runs short. L04 defines that facility as a revolving credit facility; this lesson uses only the term credit line, and the rule does not depend on any later definition.

Definition 2.1 (credit line and minimum-cash rule)

Let \(K\) be the commitment of the credit line, in integer cents. Let \(B_{w}\) be the drawn balance at the end of week \(w\), with \(B_0\) given and \(0 \le B_0 \le K\). Let \(m\) be the minimum cash, in integer cents. In week \(w\):

  1. If \(P_w < m\), the draw is \(\mathrm{Dr}_w = \min(m - P_w,\; K - B_{w-1})\), and \(\mathrm{Rp}_w = 0\).
  2. If \(P_w > m\) and \(B_{w-1} > 0\), the repayment is \(\mathrm{Rp}_w = \min(P_w - m,\; B_{w-1})\), and \(\mathrm{Dr}_w = 0\).
  3. Otherwise \(\mathrm{Dr}_w = \mathrm{Rp}_w = 0\).

The drawn balance is \(B_w = B_{w-1} + \mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w\). The undrawn availability is \(A_w = K - B_w\). The shortfall is \(\mathrm{Sh}_w = \max(0,\, m - C_w)\). Week \(w\) is a capped week when rule 1 applies and \(m - P_w > K - B_{w-1}\), so that the draw is limited by the commitment.

Theorem 2.2 (the rule holds the minimum, within the commitment)

Assumptions. (1) \(0 \le B_0 \le K\). (2) The rules of Definition 2.1 apply in every week, with \(P_w\) and \(C_w\) as in Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3.

Statement. For every week \(w\):

  • (a) \(0 \le B_w \le K\), \(\mathrm{Dr}_w \ge 0\) and \(\mathrm{Rp}_w \ge 0\).
  • (b) If week \(w\) is not capped, then \(C_w \ge m\). Where rule 1 applies and the week is not capped, \(C_w = m\) exactly.
  • (c) If week \(w\) is capped, then \(\mathrm{Sh}_w = m - P_w - (K - B_{w-1}) > 0\), and \(A_w = 0\).
Proof

(a) Induct on \(w\). For \(w = 0\), Assumption (1) gives the bounds. Suppose \(0 \le B_{w-1} \le K\). Under rule 1, \(m - P_w > 0\) and \(K - B_{w-1} \ge 0\), so \(0 \le \mathrm{Dr}_w \le K - B_{w-1}\), which gives \(B_w = B_{w-1} + \mathrm{Dr}_w \le K\). Under rule 2, \(P_w - m > 0\) and \(B_{w-1} > 0\), so \(0 < \mathrm{Rp}_w \le B_{w-1}\), which gives \(B_w \ge 0\) and \(B_w < B_{w-1} \le K\). Under rule 3, \(B_w = B_{w-1}\). The rules are exclusive, because rule 1 needs \(P_w < m\) and rule 2 needs \(P_w > m\). This completes the induction.

(b) If \(P_w \ge m\), rule 2 gives \(\mathrm{Rp}_w \le P_w - m\), so \(C_w = P_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w \ge m\). If \(P_w < m\) and the week is not capped, then \(m - P_w \le K - B_{w-1}\), so \(\mathrm{Dr}_w = m - P_w\) and \(C_w = P_w + m - P_w = m\).

(c) In a capped week \(\mathrm{Dr}_w = K - B_{w-1}\), so \(B_w = K\) and \(A_w = 0\). Also \(C_w = P_w + K - B_{w-1}\), which is less than \(m\) because \(m - P_w > K - B_{w-1}\). Hence \(\mathrm{Sh}_w = m - C_w = m - P_w - (K - B_{w-1}) > 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.3 (week 6 of Lab 1)

Lab 1 ends week 5 with \(C_5 = 1{,}068{,}160\) cents and \(B_5 = 0\). In week 6 the receipts are 530,408 and the disbursements are 1,138,473 cents, so \(P_6 = 1{,}068{,}160 + 530{,}408 - 1{,}138{,}473 = 460{,}095\). Since \(P_6 < m = 1{,}000{,}000\) and \(m - P_6 = 539{,}905\) is less than \(K - B_5 = 4{,}000{,}000\), the week is not capped. The draw is \(\mathrm{Dr}_6 = 539{,}905\), the ending cash is \(C_6 = 1{,}000{,}000\), the drawn balance is \(B_6 = 539{,}905\), and the availability is \(A_6 = 4{,}000{,}000 - 539{,}905 = 3{,}460{,}095\) cents. The table in Lab 1 prints the same draw, ending cash and drawn balance for week 6.

Remark 2.4 (a draw moves cash and borrowing together)

In Example 2.3 the draw raises the ending cash and the drawn balance by the same 539,905 cents, and nothing else changes. The draw is not part of the weekly net flow \(\nu_6\), which is \(530{,}408 - 1{,}138{,}473\). Theorem 2.2(a) keeps the drawn balance between zero and the commitment, so the rule can never borrow more than the facility allows.

Lab 1 · One SIMULATED 13-week case

The lab draws one SIMULATED case from seed 7: 13 weeks of receipts and disbursements, an opening cash balance of 1,200,000 cents, a minimum of 1,000,000 cents, a commitment of 4,000,000 cents and no opening drawn balance. Before running, the expected results are: the cash identity of Theorem 1.3 holds in every week; the minimum-cash result of Theorem 2.2 holds in every week, because the commitment is large enough that no week is capped; the line draws in weeks where cash before movements falls below the minimum and repays in weeks where it rises above; and the closing cash from the summed identity equals the last week's ending cash.

"""CF8 L03 lab 1: a SIMULATED 13-week direct cash-flow forecast with a revolving credit facility.

SIMULATED: the weekly receipts and disbursements are drawn from random.Random(SEED) in this file. They are not any
firm's data, and the case is one draw, not a forecast of a real company.
Units: integer cents throughout. A week is 7 days, the horizon is 13 weeks, and week w ends on day 7w.
Every input is an integer number of cents and the forecast uses integer arithmetic only, so no rounding occurs.
Revolver rule: when cash before any draw or repayment is below minimum cash, the revolver draws the
shortfall, capped at undrawn availability. When cash before any repayment is above minimum cash and the
drawn balance is positive, the revolver repays the excess, capped at the drawn balance. Revolver interest
is not modelled in this lab.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

HORIZON_WEEKS = 13
SEED = 7
OPENING_CASH = 1_200_000
OPENING_DRAWN = 0
MINIMUM_CASH = 1_000_000
COMMITMENT = 4_000_000


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Week:
    """One row of the forecast. Every money field is an int in cents."""

    week: int
    receipts: int
    disbursements: int
    draw: int
    repay: int
    ending_cash: int
    drawn: int
    shortfall: int


def simulate_inputs(seed: int) -> tuple[list[int], list[int]]:
    """Weekly receipts and disbursements in cents for the horizon, from random.Random(seed). SIMULATED."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    receipts: list[int] = []
    disbursements: list[int] = []
    for week in range(1, HORIZON_WEEKS + 1):
        slump = -350_000 if 5 <= week <= 8 else 0
        receipts.append(1_000_000 + rng.randint(-150_000, 150_000) + slump)
        disbursements.append(1_020_000 + rng.randint(-120_000, 120_000))
    return receipts, disbursements


def forecast(
    receipts: list[int],
    disbursements: list[int],
    opening_cash: int,
    opening_drawn: int,
    minimum: int,
    commitment: int,
) -> list[Week]:
    """Weekly rows of the direct forecast in cents, applying the revolver rule in the module docstring."""
    cash = opening_cash
    drawn = opening_drawn
    rows: list[Week] = []
    for week, (receipt, payment) in enumerate(zip(receipts, disbursements, strict=True), start=1):
        before = cash + receipt - payment
        draw = 0
        repay = 0
        if before < minimum:
            draw = min(minimum - before, commitment - drawn)
        elif before > minimum and drawn > 0:
            repay = min(before - minimum, drawn)
        ending = before + draw - repay
        drawn += draw - repay
        rows.append(Week(week, receipt, payment, draw, repay, ending, drawn, max(0, minimum - ending)))
        cash = ending
    return rows


def identity_holds(rows: list[Week], opening_cash: int, receipts: list[int], disbursements: list[int]) -> bool:
    """True when each week's ending cash equals the prior ending cash, plus receipts, less disbursements,
    plus the draw, less the repayment."""
    previous = opening_cash
    for row, receipt, payment in zip(rows, receipts, disbursements, strict=True):
        if row.ending_cash != previous + receipt - payment + row.draw - row.repay:
            return False
        previous = row.ending_cash
    return True


def main() -> None:
    receipts, disbursements = simulate_inputs(SEED)
    rows = forecast(receipts, disbursements, OPENING_CASH, OPENING_DRAWN, MINIMUM_CASH, COMMITMENT)
    print(f"SIMULATED case, seed {SEED}, {HORIZON_WEEKS} weeks, integer cents")
    print(
        f"opening cash {OPENING_CASH}, opening drawn {OPENING_DRAWN}, "
        f"minimum cash {MINIMUM_CASH}, commitment {COMMITMENT}"
    )
    print("week  receipts  disbursements  draw  repay  ending_cash  drawn  shortfall")
    for row in rows:
        print(
            f"{row.week:>4}  {row.receipts:>8}  {row.disbursements:>13}  {row.draw:>4}  {row.repay:>5}  "
            f"{row.ending_cash:>11}  {row.drawn:>5}  {row.shortfall:>9}"
        )
    print(f"cash identity holds in every week: {identity_holds(rows, OPENING_CASH, receipts, disbursements)}")
    print(f"minimum cash held in every week: {all(row.shortfall == 0 for row in rows)}")
    print(
        f"weeks with a draw: {sum(1 for row in rows if row.draw > 0)}; weeks with a repayment: "
        f"{sum(1 for row in rows if row.repay > 0)}"
    )
    print(f"total drawn {sum(row.draw for row in rows)}; total repaid {sum(row.repay for row in rows)}")
    print(
        f"peak drawn balance {max(row.drawn for row in rows)}; least availability "
        f"{COMMITMENT - max(row.drawn for row in rows)}"
    )
    closing = (
        OPENING_CASH
        + sum(receipts)
        - sum(disbursements)
        + sum(row.draw for row in rows)
        - sum(row.repay for row in rows)
    )
    print(f"closing cash from the summed identity {closing}; from the last week {rows[-1].ending_cash}")

3Where the forecast's claims stop

The identity of Theorem 1.3 checks arithmetic, not the forecast. It holds for any receipts, so it cannot show that the receipts are right. The forecast is only as good as the receipts and disbursements entered in it, and this lesson gives no evidence about their size.

The forecast omits items on purpose. It has no revolver interest, no commitment fee, no tax and no capital spending. Interest computed on a balance that the same week's draw determines would make a disbursement depend on that draw, which is a circular case that a later unit treats. Theorem 2.2 is stated without any interest term, so it does not cover a forecast with interest.

All flows are dated at week end. A shortfall that opens and closes inside a week is therefore invisible to the forecast. This is a convention of the lesson, and a treasurer who needs intra-week cash must use a finer schedule.

The rules of Definition 2.1 are policies. Theorem 2.2 holds for these rules, and it says nothing about whether they are the rules a treasurer should choose. Repaying as soon as cash exceeds the minimum is one policy among several, and this lesson does not prove that it is the best one.

4Misconceptions

  1. “Forecast cash is forecast profit.”

    A direct forecast lists the cash that moves in each week (Definition 1.1). A sale that has not been collected and an expense that has not been paid are excluded, so the forecast can show a cash shortfall in a profitable period. Lab 1 has no profit line at all.

  2. “A draw is income, so a draw raises the week's operating flow.”

    The draw is not part of the weekly net flow \(\nu_w\) (Definition 1.2). It raises the ending cash and the drawn balance by the same amount (Remark 2.4). Example 2.3 shows the week 6 draw of 539,905 cents, which Lab 1 prints as the draw column and not as receipts.

  3. “Minimum cash can be breached whenever the credit line has availability left.”

    By Theorem 2.2(b), a week that is not capped holds minimum cash exactly or more. By Theorem 2.2(c), a shortfall needs a capped week, and a capped week leaves \(A_w = 0\). A breach therefore needs a fully drawn line. Lab 1 shows a zero shortfall in every week, which matches this, because its commitment is not exhausted.

  4. “A passing identity check proves the forecast is right.”

    Theorem 1.3 holds for any receipts (Remark 1.4). The check confirms that the stated flows add up, and it is silent on whether the flows are correct. Lab 1 prints True for the identity in every week while its receipts are a seeded draw, not evidence about a company.

5Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. All amounts are integer cents.

Mechanics

M1 · A draw to restore minimum cash

Opening cash is 500,000 cents, with no drawn balance. Receipts in week 1 are 800,000 cents and disbursements are 1,000,000 cents. The minimum cash is 600,000 cents and the commitment is 1,000,000 cents. Find the draw, the ending cash, the drawn balance and the availability after week 1.

Solution

The cash before movements is \(P_1 = 500{,}000 + 800{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000 = 300{,}000\). Since \(P_1 < 600{,}000\), rule 1 applies with \(K - B_0 = 1{,}000{,}000\), which exceeds \(600{,}000 - 300{,}000 = 300{,}000\). The draw is \(\mathrm{Dr}_1 = 300{,}000\). The ending cash is \(C_1 = 600{,}000\), the drawn balance is \(B_1 = 300{,}000\), and the availability is \(A_1 = 700{,}000\) cents. The week is not capped, so Theorem 2.2(b) gives \(C_1 = m\).

M2 · A repayment capped by the balance

Opening cash is 900,000 cents and the drawn balance is 250,000 cents. In week 1, receipts are 400,000 cents and disbursements are 300,000 cents. The minimum cash is 600,000 cents and the commitment is 1,000,000 cents. Find the repayment, the ending cash and the drawn balance.

Solution

The cash before movements is \(P_1 = 900{,}000 + 400{,}000 - 300{,}000 = 1{,}000{,}000\). The excess over the minimum is \(1{,}000{,}000 - 600{,}000 = 400{,}000\). Rule 2 gives \(\mathrm{Rp}_1 = \min(400{,}000,\, 250{,}000) = 250{,}000\), because the balance caps the repayment. The ending cash is \(C_1 = 1{,}000{,}000 - 250{,}000 = 750{,}000\), the drawn balance is zero, and the surplus of 150,000 cents stays in cash above the minimum.

M3 · A capped draw and its shortfall

Opening cash is 200,000 cents and the drawn balance is 100,000 cents. In week 1, receipts are 100,000 cents and disbursements are 400,000 cents. The minimum cash is 500,000 cents and the commitment is 300,000 cents. Find the draw, the ending cash, the availability and the shortfall.

Solution

The cash before movements is \(P_1 = 200{,}000 + 100{,}000 - 400{,}000 = -100{,}000\). The need is \(500{,}000 - (-100{,}000) = 600{,}000\). The availability is \(300{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 200{,}000\), which is less than the need, so the week is capped and \(\mathrm{Dr}_1 = 200{,}000\). The ending cash is \(C_1 = -100{,}000 + 200{,}000 = 100{,}000\), the availability is zero, and the shortfall is \(500{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 400{,}000\). This matches Theorem 2.2(c): \(m - P_1 - (K - B_0) = 500{,}000 + 100{,}000 - 200{,}000 = 400{,}000\).

Proof

P1 · The identity over two weeks

Prove Theorem 1.3 directly for \(H = 2\), without citing the general sum.

Solution

Week 1 gives \(C_1 = C_0 + \nu_1 + \mathrm{Dr}_1 - \mathrm{Rp}_1\) by Definition 1.2 and Assumption (2). Week 2 gives \(C_2 = C_1 + \nu_2 + \mathrm{Dr}_2 - \mathrm{Rp}_2\). Substituting the first line into the second gives \(C_2 = C_0 + \nu_1 + \nu_2 + (\mathrm{Dr}_1 + \mathrm{Dr}_2) - (\mathrm{Rp}_1 + \mathrm{Rp}_2)\), which is the two-week sum of Theorem 1.3. The step uses only the definition of \(C_1\), so no assumption about the rules is needed.

P2 · Minimum cash after a repayment

Show that if \(P_w > m\) and rule 2 applies, then \(C_w \ge m\), and find the week's shortfall.

Solution

Rule 2 gives \(\mathrm{Rp}_w = \min(P_w - m,\, B_{w-1})\), which is at most \(P_w - m\). Therefore \(C_w = P_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w \ge P_w - (P_w - m) = m\). Because \(C_w \ge m\), the shortfall \(\max(0, m - C_w)\) is zero. This is the case of Theorem 2.2(b) for a repayment week.

P3 · The commitment is a ceiling only because of the cap

Let \(K = 1{,}000{,}000\), \(B_0 = 900{,}000\) and \(m = 500{,}000\). Let \(P_1 = 300{,}000\). Show that without the cap in rule 1 the drawn balance would exceed \(K\), and show that the cap keeps it at \(K\).

Solution

Without the cap, the draw would be \(m - P_1 = 200{,}000\), and the drawn balance would become \(900{,}000 + 200{,}000 = 1{,}100{,}000\), which exceeds \(K = 1{,}000{,}000\). With the cap in rule 1, the draw is \(\min(200{,}000,\, 1{,}000{,}000 - 900{,}000) = 100{,}000\), so \(B_1 = 1{,}000{,}000 = K\). The week is capped, and the shortfall is \(500{,}000 - (300{,}000 + 100{,}000) = 100{,}000\), which Theorem 2.2(c) gives as \(m - P_1 - (K - B_0) = 200{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 100{,}000\).

Empirical

E1 · Recovering the operating flows from Lab 1

Lab 1 prints a closing cash of 1,000,000 cents, a total drawn of 1,356,374 cents and a total repaid of 438,816 cents, with opening cash of 1,200,000 cents. Find the sum of weekly net flows over the 13 weeks.

Solution

By Theorem 1.3, \(C_{13} = C_0 + \sum_w \nu_w + (\text{total drawn} - \text{total repaid})\). Rearranging gives \(\sum_w \nu_w = C_{13} - C_0 - (1{,}356{,}374 - 438{,}816) = 1{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}200{,}000 - 917{,}558 = -1{,}117{,}558\) cents. Summing the weekly receipts and disbursements that the twin's code defines gives the same total. The result is a sum of the lab's seeded flows, not a claim about any company.

E2 · The peak and the availability it leaves

From Lab 1, find the week with the largest drawn balance and the undrawn availability in that week.

Solution

The drawn balance is largest in week 11, at 1,288,640 cents, which Lab 1 prints as the peak drawn balance. The commitment is 4,000,000 cents, so the availability in week 11 is \(4{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}288{,}640 = 2{,}711{,}360\) cents. Lab 1 prints the same figure as its least availability, and that is the availability in the peak week because the drawn balance is largest there.

E3 · Pre-repayment cash in week 9

Lab 1 repays 67,734 cents in week 9 and ends that week at 1,000,000 cents. Find the cash before the repayment, and check it against the weekly net flow of week 9 in the lab's table.

Solution

The cash before the repayment is \(1{,}000{,}000 + 67{,}734 = 1{,}067{,}734\) cents, which is \(P_9\) by Definition 1.2 when the repayment is applied after it. The week 9 receipts are 1,077,355 and disbursements are 1,009,621, so \(\nu_9 = 67{,}734\), and \(C_8 + \nu_9 = 1{,}000{,}000 + 67{,}734\) gives the same figure. The repayment equals the excess over minimum cash, which is the cap in rule 2 not binding because the drawn balance of 1,133,016 cents exceeds the repayment.

Research

R1 · A minimum that changes by week

Let the minimum cash be \(m_w\), which may differ by week. Show that Theorem 2.2 holds with \(m_w\) in place of \(m\).

Solution

The identity of Theorem 1.3 does not involve \(m\), so part (a) of the theorem is unchanged. Each part of the proof of Theorem 2.2 uses only the minimum of its own week: the bounds in (a) use the sign of \(m_w - P_w\) and \(P_w - m_w\) for that week, and parts (b) and (c) use \(m_w\) in place of \(m\) in the same algebra. Induction over weeks needs no assumption that the minimum is constant. The result therefore holds for any sequence of minima.

R2 · Net borrowing equals the change in the balance

Show that \(\sum_{w=1}^{H}(\mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w) = B_H - B_0\) for any rule that obeys Definition 2.1, and check the identity on Lab 1.

Solution

Definition 2.1 gives \(B_w = B_{w-1} + \mathrm{Dr}_w - \mathrm{Rp}_w\). Summing over \(w\) telescopes the balance, so the sum of net draws equals \(B_H - B_0\). On Lab 1, \(B_0 = 0\) and \(B_{13} = 917{,}558\), and the printed totals give \(1{,}356{,}374 - 438{,}816 = 917{,}558\). The identity holds for the printed case.

R3 · No draw in week 1 when cash starts above the minimum

Suppose \(C_0 \ge m\) and \(\mathrm{Rec}_1 \ge \mathrm{Dis}_1\). Show that the line is not drawn in week 1.

Solution

The cash before movements is \(P_1 = C_0 + \nu_1 \ge C_0 \ge m\), because \(\nu_1 \ge 0\). Rule 1 needs \(P_1 < m\), so it does not apply, and \(\mathrm{Dr}_1 = 0\). Rule 2 may apply, but it repays and does not draw. The week 1 ending cash is at least \(m\), so the shortfall in week 1 is zero by Theorem 2.2(b).

7Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Under Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3, which expression is the ending cash of week \(w\)?

    Options

    Theorem 1.3 adds the weekly net flow and the net draw to the prior ending cash. The first option omits the operating flows and the second omits the draw, so each is incomplete.

  2. Opening cash is 500,000 cents, receipts are 800,000 and disbursements are 1,000,000 in week 1, and the minimum is 600,000 with a commitment of 1,000,000. What is the week 1 ending cash, in cents?

    \(P_1 = 300{,}000\), the draw is 300,000 (Problem M1), and the ending cash is 600,000 cents.

  3. In a week that is not capped, where the cash before movements is below the minimum, what is the ending cash?

    Options

    The draw is \(m - P_w\), so \(C_w = P_w + (m - P_w) = m\) (Theorem 2.2(b)).

  4. Opening cash is 900,000, the drawn balance is 250,000, receipts are 400,000 and disbursements are 300,000 in week 1. The minimum is 600,000. How many cents are repaid in week 1?

    The excess over minimum is 400,000, but the balance is 250,000, so the repayment is capped at 250,000 (Problem M2).

  5. A capped draw leaves a shortfall. What is the availability after that draw?

    Options

    A capped week draws the whole availability, \(K - B_{w-1}\), so \(A_w = 0\) (Theorem 2.2(c)).

  6. Opening cash is 200,000, the drawn balance is 100,000, receipts are 100,000 and disbursements are 400,000 in week 1. The minimum is 500,000 and the commitment is 300,000. What is the week 1 shortfall, in cents?

    The draw is capped at 200,000, the ending cash is 100,000, and the shortfall is 400,000 (Problem M3).

  7. Which of these is not part of the weekly net flow \(\nu_w\)?

    Options

    \(\nu_w = \mathrm{Rec}_w - \mathrm{Dis}_w\) by Definition 1.2. Draws and repayments enter the ending cash separately (Remark 2.4).

  8. In Lab 1, what is the peak drawn balance, in cents?

    Lab 1 prints a peak drawn balance of 1,288,640 cents, which the table shows in week 11 (Example E2).

  9. Lab 1's week 9 repayment is 67,734 cents. Which statement about week 9 is correct?

    Options

    \(1{,}000{,}000 + 67{,}734 = 1{,}067{,}734\) (Problem E3). The repayment is set by the excess over the minimum, not by the commitment, and no shortfall occurs.

  10. In Lab 1, what is the total repaid over the 13 weeks, in cents?

    The repayments are 67,734 in week 9, 157,075 in week 12 and 214,007 in week 13. Their sum is 438,816 cents, which Lab 1 prints as the total repaid.
