The 13-week cash-flow forecast and liquidity plan

A treasurer has thirteen weeks of slower collections ahead. You write treasury_forecast.py, the engine that turns a case into a week-by-week direct cash forecast, sizes the revolver, finds the week in which the covenant headroom is smallest, and gives the cash impact of two working-capital actions. Then you write a memo that recommends a plan. The autograder runs your engine on a SIMULATED case generated from your workspace seed. The rubric grades the memo.

Lab 1 · Worked example: a 13-week forecast with a revolver

The case is SIMULATED: sales are 10,000.00 dollars a week in weeks 1 to 3 and 8,000.00 dollars a week from week 4; purchases are 7,000.00 dollars a week; opex is 2,500.00 dollars a week; the collection lag is 45 days and the payment lag 30 days; opening cash is 1,500.00 dollars, minimum cash 4,000.00 dollars, the commitment 10,000.00 dollars, and the covenant threshold 12,000.00 dollars. Liquidity, shortfall, required commitment and the pinch week are defined in the conventions below, and the labs use them in that sense. Before running the code, predict three things from the rules in the conventions below: the weeks in which the revolver draws, the week in which headroom (liquidity less the covenant threshold, defined in the conventions below) is smallest, and whether the commitment binds. The run prints each step, and its output is the expected output shown after the code.

"""CF8 project lab 1 (rung 1, worked example): a 13-week forecast with a revolver on a SIMULATED stressed case.

SIMULATED: a manufacturer case written here for teaching. It is not any company's data. Money is in whole cents; the
printed values, daily rates included, are dollars to two decimals. Standard library only, no randomness, no
network. The rules are those of the specification on the project page:

* a week has seven days, and a day's sales are collected collection_lag days after the sale;
* a day's purchases are paid payment_lag days after the purchase, and opex is paid in its week;
* before day 1 the daily rate of sales and purchases is week 1's rate (the run-rate before the horizon);
* if cash before a draw is below minimum cash, the revolver draws the shortfall, capped at availability;
* if cash before a draw is at or above minimum cash, the revolver repays from the cash above minimum, capped at the
  drawn balance;
* liquidity is cash plus undrawn availability; headroom is liquidity minus the covenant threshold.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

COLLECTION_LAG = 45  # days: the collection lag, the days of sales a steady business holds as receivables
PAYMENT_LAG = 30  # days
SALES = [1_000_000] * 3 + [800_000] * 10  # cents of sales in weeks 1 to 13: a slowdown from week 4
PURCHASES = [700_000] * 13  # cents of purchases in weeks 1 to 13
OPEX = [250_000] * 13  # cents of operating cost paid in weeks 1 to 13
OPENING_CASH = 150_000
MINIMUM_CASH = 400_000
COMMITMENT = 1_000_000
OPENING_DRAWN = 0
THRESHOLD = 1_200_000


def usd(cents: int) -> str:
    """Cents shown as dollars with two decimals."""
    return f"{cents / 100:.2f}"


def daily(weekly: list[int], day: int) -> Fraction:
    """Cents of one day's flow on day `day`: a week's amount spread over its seven days, week 1's rate before day 1."""
    week = 1 if day < 1 else (day - 1) // 7 + 1
    return Fraction(weekly[week - 1], 7)


def week_total(weekly: list[int], lag: int, week: int) -> Fraction:
    """Exact cents paid or collected in `week` when each day's flow falls `lag` days after it is due."""
    first = 7 * (week - 1) + 1
    return sum((daily(weekly, day - lag) for day in range(first, first + 7)), Fraction(0))


def half_up(value: Fraction) -> int:
    """The exact value rounded once to the nearest cent, a half going up."""
    return math.floor(value + Fraction(1, 2))


def main() -> None:
    print(f"step 1, the run-rate before week 1: sales {usd(SALES[0])} a week, {SALES[0] / 7 / 100:.2f} a day")
    print(f"step 2, the collection lag is {COLLECTION_LAG} days and the payment lag is {PAYMENT_LAG} days")
    receipts = [half_up(week_total(SALES, COLLECTION_LAG, w)) for w in range(1, 14)]
    disbursements = [half_up(week_total(PURCHASES, PAYMENT_LAG, w) + OPEX[w - 1]) for w in range(1, 14)]
    print("step 3, receipts in dollars, weeks 1 to 13:", ", ".join(usd(x) for x in receipts))
    print("step 4, disbursements in dollars, weeks 1 to 13:", ", ".join(usd(x) for x in disbursements))
    cash, drawn = OPENING_CASH, OPENING_DRAWN
    print("step 5, week by week: receipts, disbursements, draw, repay, ending cash, drawn, headroom (dollars)")
    rows: list[tuple[int, int, int, int, int, int, int]] = []
    for w in range(13):
        before = cash + receipts[w] - disbursements[w]
        draw = min(max(MINIMUM_CASH - before, 0), COMMITMENT - drawn)
        repay = 0 if draw > 0 else min(max(before - MINIMUM_CASH, 0), drawn)
        new_cash = before + draw - repay
        new_drawn = drawn + draw - repay
        liquidity = new_cash + COMMITMENT - new_drawn
        headroom = liquidity - THRESHOLD
        rows.append((receipts[w], disbursements[w], draw, repay, new_cash, new_drawn, headroom))
        print(
            f"  week {w + 1:2d}: {usd(receipts[w])}  {usd(disbursements[w])}  {usd(draw)}  {usd(repay)}  "
            f"{usd(new_cash)}  {usd(new_drawn)}  {usd(headroom)}"
        )
        cash, drawn = new_cash, new_drawn
    draws, repays = sum(row[2] for row in rows), sum(row[3] for row in rows)
    closing_check = OPENING_CASH + sum(receipts) - sum(disbursements) + draws - repays
    print(f"step 6, opening cash plus the horizon's flows equals week 13 ending cash: {closing_check == cash}")
    peak = max([OPENING_DRAWN] + [row[5] for row in rows])
    print(f"step 7, peak drawn balance: {usd(peak)} dollars")
    pinch = min(range(13), key=lambda i: (rows[i][6], i))
    print(f"step 8, covenant pinch: week {pinch + 1}, headroom {usd(rows[pinch][6])} dollars")
    lowest = min(row[4] for row in rows)
    print(f"step 9, lowest week-end cash: {usd(lowest)} dollars, against minimum cash of {usd(MINIMUM_CASH)}")

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A manufacturer draws on a revolving credit facility and faces slower customer payments for thirteen weeks. Its credit agreement sets a minimum-liquidity covenant: cash plus undrawn availability must stay at or above a threshold at each week end. The treasurer must size the revolver, find the week in which covenant headroom is smallest, and choose working-capital actions with their cash impact.
Inputs
A case is a Python dict with these keys. horizon_weeks: the whole number 13. opening_cash_cents: a whole number of cents, at least 0. minimum_cash_cents: a positive whole number of cents. revolver_commitment_cents: a whole number of cents, zero or more. opening_drawn_cents: a whole number of cents from 0 up to the commitment. covenant_threshold_cents: a whole number of cents, at least 0. collection_lag_days and payment_lag_days: whole numbers of days, zero or more. weekly_sales_cents, weekly_purchases_cents and weekly_opex_cents: lists of 13 whole numbers of cents, zero or more, one for each week. The autograder's cases come from grader/instance.py: make_instance(seed) returns a dict whose payload field is such a case as a JSON string, with collection lags from 35 to 80 days, payment lags from 25 to 50 days, and the minimum cash and commitment as positive whole cents. Every such case is SIMULATED. Real company filings are not embedded: their reuse terms are not recorded in the program's data sources, so every case on this page and in the autograder is generated.
Required outputs
A file treasury_forecast.py at the root of your repository, defining these four functions with these names and signatures. forecast(case) -> list[dict[str, int]]: thirteen weekly rows, in week order, each with the keys receipts_cents, disbursements_cents, draw_cents, repay_cents, ending_cash_cents, drawn_balance_cents, liquidity_cents and headroom_cents. revolver_plan(case) -> dict[str, int]: the keys peak_drawn_cents, required_commitment_cents and shortfall_cents. covenant_pinch(case) -> dict[str, int]: the keys week (1 to 13) and headroom_cents. action_cash_impact(case, action, days) -> int. A file memo.md, the treasurer memo, of about 600 to 900 words in five sections: 1 Forecast, 2 Revolver sizing, 3 Covenant pinch, 4 Working-capital actions, 5 Recommendation and limits.
Constraints
The engine uses the Python standard library and does not import instance.py. Every money output is a Python int in cents, computed by the rules in the conventions below. Each function is pure: it never prints or warns, never changes its argument, and returns fresh objects. Three named conditions raise ValueError, and the autograder checks the exception type: a horizon other than 13 weeks, which forecast, revolver_plan, covenant_pinch and action_cash_impact all reject; a negative revolver commitment, which those four functions also reject; and an action that is not "dso" or "dpo", which action_cash_impact rejects. The wording of a message is not graded. No other invalid input is tested, so for those any behaviour is acceptable. The engine runs unchanged in the browser (Pyodide) and under pytest.
Grading mode
Grading: both. The autograder runs your engine on your seed's case. grader/test_project.py collects 99 checks (counted with pytest --collect-only on the reference solution; the count does not depend on the seed, because no parametrize list depends on it), and it runs with warnings as errors. The rubric grades the memo against the five criteria below, with the two annotated exemplar memos in the memo section.

Conventions, defined once

  • Weeks and days. The horizon is 13 weeks of seven days each. Week w is days 7w − 6 to 7w, with day 1 the first day of week 1. Days before day 1 exist only as the pre-horizon run-rate below.
  • Pre-horizon run-rate. A day's sales, and a day's purchases, before day 1 are taken at the daily rate of week 1. Every flow the case gives for week w is spread evenly over the seven days of that week, so one day carries weekly_sales_cents[w − 1] / 7 cents of sales.
  • Day counts. DSO is receivables times 365 over revenue, and DIO and DPO use the same 365-day form on cost of sales and on purchases. A steady business with a collection lag of L days holds receivables of L days of sales, so its DSO is L; the case states that lag directly as collection_lag_days. A week is seven days and the year is 365 days; no other day count appears.
  • Receipts and disbursements. A day's sales are collected collection_lag_days after the sale. Receipts in week w are the sum over its seven days of the sales of the day that is that many days earlier. A day's purchases are paid payment_lag_days after the purchase. Disbursements in week w are those paid purchases plus weekly_opex_cents[w − 1], which is paid in its own week.
  • Revolver. Let C be cash before the draw, m minimum cash, and A the availability, which is the commitment less the drawn balance at the start of the week. If C is below m, the draw is the smaller of m − C and A, and there is no repayment. Otherwise the draw is zero and the repayment is the smaller of C − m and the drawn balance at the start of the week. Ending cash is C plus the draw less the repayment, and the drawn balance moves by the same draw and repayment.
  • Liquidity and headroom. Liquidity at week end is ending cash plus the commitment less the drawn balance. Headroom is liquidity less covenant_threshold_cents. A draw moves cash and availability by equal and opposite amounts, so it leaves liquidity unchanged; liquidity changes only with receipts and disbursements.
  • Shortfall and required commitment. The shortfall in a week is the amount by which ending cash is below minimum cash, or zero. shortfall_cents is the largest weekly shortfall. The free path is the same calculation with the draw never capped by availability. required_commitment_cents is the largest drawn balance on the free path, opening balance included. A commitment at or above it leaves no shortfall; a commitment below it leaves one, because the two paths agree until the first week in which the free path's drawn balance exceeds the commitment, and in that week the capped draw is less than the need.
  • Peak drawn balance. The largest drawn balance over the opening balance and the 13 week ends.
  • Covenant pinch. The week in 1 to 13 with the smallest headroom; on a tie, the earliest week. headroom_cents is that week's headroom.
  • Action cash impact. The change in cumulative cash by the end of week 13 from a change in terms, before any revolver effect. Shortening DSO by d days ("dso", with d from 0 up to the current lag) brings forward the collection of the sales of the d days whose collection falls after day 91 before the change and on or before day 91 after it; the impact is the sum of those days' daily sales. Lengthening DPO by d days ("dpo", with d zero or more) keeps the purchases of the d days whose payment, before the change, fell on or before day 91 and now falls after it; the impact is the sum of those days' daily purchases. Flows that move before week 1 count, because they change cash at week 13.
  • Rounding. Every money output is the exact value rounded to the nearest cent, with a half going up. Each daily amount is a whole number of cents divided by seven, so every exact value the engine forms is a whole number of sevenths of a cent, and no exact value sits at exactly one half of a cent. The truncation of an exact value is therefore wrong for four sevenths and for five and six sevenths of a cent.

Named errors

Each of these raises ValueError: a case whose horizon_weeks is not 13, in each of the four functions; a case whose revolver_commitment_cents is negative, in each of the four functions; and an action that is not "dso" or "dpo", in action_cash_impact. Zero is a valid commitment, and a commitment equal to the opening drawn balance is valid. Just inside these bounds the input is valid, and each function returns a value.

    """Week rows: receipts, disbursements, draw, repay, ending cash, drawn, liquidity, headroom, in cents."""
    rows = []
    cash = CASE["opening_cash_cents"]
    drawn = CASE["opening_drawn_cents"]
    for w in range(1, 14):
        got = half_up(week_total(CASE["weekly_sales_cents"], CASE["collection_lag_days"], w))
        out = half_up(week_total(CASE["weekly_purchases_cents"], CASE["payment_lag_days"], w))
        out += CASE["weekly_opex_cents"][w - 1]
        before = cash + got - out
        need = max(CASE["minimum_cash_cents"] - before, 0)
        room = CASE["revolver_commitment_cents"] - drawn
        draw = min(need, room) if capped else need
        repay = 0 if need > 0 else min(before - CASE["minimum_cash_cents"], drawn)
        cash = before + draw - repay
        drawn = drawn + draw - repay
        liquidity = cash + CASE["revolver_commitment_cents"] - drawn
        rows.append((got, out, draw, repay, cash, drawn, liquidity, liquidity - CASE["covenant_threshold_cents"]))
    return rows


def usd(cents: int) -> str:
    return f"{cents / 100:.2f}"


def cumulative(weekly: list[int], lag: int) -> Fraction:
    """Cents that flow by day 91 when each day's amount falls lag days later; sales from day -300 onwards."""
    return sum((daily(weekly, k) for k in range(-300, 92 - lag)), Fraction(0))

Rung 3 · The treasurer memo

The memo recommends a plan for the case, either the Lab 1 case or a case of your own from the autograder's seed. It has five sections, in this order: 1 Forecast, 2 Revolver sizing, 3 Covenant pinch, 4 Working-capital actions and 5 Recommendation and limits. Take every number from your engine's output or from a lab, state its unit, and state the day count and the rounding you used. Give the decision rule before the numbers that apply it.

The memo needs no literature. Any source it cites must be a registry key from this page's reading list, with the locator of the statement it supports, and each claim carries its grade (T, A, B, C, D or X, as the course's evidence standard defines them). A practice claim without a confirmed locator is graded C at most and quotes no figure.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 4.

1 Forecast

The inputs are the 45-day collection lag and the 30-day payment lag, the weekly sales (10,000.00 dollars in weeks 1 to 3 and 8,000.00 dollars from week 4), purchases of 7,000.00 dollars a week, opex of 2,500.00 dollars a week, opening cash of 1,500.00 dollars and an opening drawn balance of zero. Receipts in a week are the sales of the days that are 45 days earlier. Each week satisfies ending cash equals opening cash plus receipts less disbursements plus draws less repayments. Week 1 is 1,500.00 + 10,000.00 − 9,500.00 + 2,000.00 − 0.00 = 4,000.00 dollars, which matches Lab 1, step 5. Week 10 receipts are the sales of days 19 to 25, three days at 1,000,000 cents a week and four at 800,000: (3 × 1,000,000 + 4 × 800,000) / 7 = 885,714.29 cents, which is 8,857.14 dollars to the cent (Lab 1, step 3).

C1, level 4: names each input the rows use, states the identity, and checks it on week 1 against the printed output.

2 Revolver sizing

The rule: if cash before the draw is below minimum cash of 4,000.00 dollars, draw the smaller of the shortfall and the undrawn availability; otherwise repay the smaller of the cash above minimum and the drawn balance. No week's draw is capped: the largest need is 2,000.00 dollars (week 1), and availability at the start of every week is at least 8,000.00 dollars, since the drawn balance at the start of a week is at most 2,000.00 dollars (the week 1 draw). The peak drawn balance is 3,142.86 dollars (314,286 cents), in week 13 (Lab 1, step 7). The required commitment is the largest drawn balance on the free path, which here equals the capped path, so it is also 314,286 cents, 3,142.86 dollars. The commitment does not bind.

C2, level 4: states the rule and the cap, gives the peak and the required commitment in cents, and says the commitment does not bind.

3 Covenant pinch

The covenant is minimum liquidity: cash plus undrawn availability at week end, less the threshold of 12,000.00 dollars, must be zero or more. The pinch is week 13, where liquidity is 10,857.14 dollars and headroom is −1,142.86 dollars (−114,286 cents). The tie rule is the earliest week; no tie occurs in this case. A draw leaves liquidity unchanged, so the pinch reflects receipts and disbursements only, and the revolver does not move it.

C3, level 4: names the week and its headroom in cents, states the definition and the earliest-week tie rule.

4 Working-capital actions

Action 1, shortening DSO by five days, brings the sales of days 47 to 51 into the horizon. Those days fall in weeks 7 and 8, when sales are 800,000 cents a week, so the impact is 5 × 800,000 / 7 = 571,428.57 cents, which is 571,429 cents, or 5,714.29 dollars, to the cent. Action 2, lengthening DPO by five days, keeps the purchases of days 57 to 61, which fall in week 9 at 700,000 cents a week, so the impact is 5 × 700,000 / 7 = 500,000 cents, or 5,000.00 dollars. Both impacts are positive. The seven-day week sets the daily rate; the 365-day DSO and DPO form defines the lags and does not enter the impact. The 5,714.29-dollar figure is the change in cash by the end of week 13 before any revolver effect.

C4, level 4: both actions, each with its sign, its number in cents and dollars, and its day-count convention, with the calculation shown.

5 Recommendation and limits

Recommend both actions and no change to the commitment of 10,000.00 dollars, because the peak drawn balance of 3,142.86 dollars leaves 6,857.14 dollars of availability. The covenant pinch is the limit that matters: a threshold of 10,857.14 dollars or less would leave no week in breach, because that is the lowest week-end liquidity in the case; a threshold above it puts week 13 in breach. The inputs are SIMULATED, and the covenant is defined as minimum liquidity.

C5, level 4: recommends, names one change that would reverse the recommendation with its computed threshold, and states the SIMULATED inputs and the covenant definition as limits.
