Working Capital, Treasury & Short-Term Finance

A profitable firm can still run out of cash. The gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers is financed by someone, and the treasurer decides how. This module measures that gap in days, forecasts cash week by week, sizes the credit line that covers the gap, tests the covenants that the line carries, and asks how much cash a firm should hold against a shortfall. The module ends in a treasury forecast engine, graded by an autograder, and a treasurer memo, graded by a rubric that you write.

  • Prerequisites: CF1 Time Value & Capital Budgeting (its net present value and net working capital), AC1 Financial Accounting (the balance sheet, income statement and cash-flow statement). CF1 assumes M1 and AC1, and M1 assumes P0.1.
  • Project: 13-week cash-flow forecast (autograded engine, with a rubric-graded treasurer memo)

40 h lessons · 10 h project (20.0% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CF8 you can:

  1. Compute days sales outstanding, days inventory outstanding, days payable outstanding and the cash conversion cycle from a firm's statements, and show that the cycle is the sum of the first two less the third.
  2. Evaluate a change in credit terms by its break-even margin, and state the annual cost that an early-payment discount implies.
  3. Construct a 13-week direct cash forecast, and check the cash identity in every week.
  4. Size a revolving credit facility against a minimum-cash rule, and state the shortfall that the commitment leaves uncovered.
  5. Locate the covenant pinch week and its headroom, and state the covenant definition that the headroom uses.
  6. Compare a cash buffer with a simulated distribution of shortfall, and state what the simulation can and cannot show.

Prerequisites

CF8 assumes CF1 (Time Value & Capital Budgeting) and AC1 (Financial Accounting), and everything they rest on, including M1 (Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning) and P0.1 (How to Study This Program). It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From CF1: net present value and net working capital.
  • From M1: present value and discount factor.
  • From AC1: the balance sheet, the income statement and the cash-flow statement.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: six on CF1, M1 and AC1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder below teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct, since seven of nine is 78 percent. Skip rungs 1 and 2 when you reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, revisit CF1, M1 and AC1 first.

  1. Which identity holds for every balance sheet?

    Options

    Total assets equal total liabilities plus equity (AC1, balance sheet).

  2. What is the present value at 10 percent of 110 received at the end of one year?

    \(110/1.1 = 100\) (M1, present value).

  3. What is the discount factor at 5 percent for two years, to three decimals?

    \(1/1.05^2 = 1/1.1025 = 0.9070\) (M1, discount factor).

  4. A project costs 1000 now and returns 1200 at the end of one year. What is its net present value at 10 percent, in currency units to two decimals?

    \(-1000 + 1200/1.1 = -1000 + 1090.909\ldots = 90.91\) (CF1, net present value rule).

  5. What does the cash-flow statement's three sections add up to?

    Options

    The three sections of the cash-flow statement sum to the net change in cash (AC1, cash-flow statement).

  6. Why is depreciation added back to net income when operating cash flow is computed?

    Options

    Depreciation is a non-cash charge in the income statement (AC1, depreciation and amortization).

  7. Skill check (rung 1). In one week a firm opens with 30000 cents of cash, receives 40000 cents, and disburses 120000 cents. Minimum cash is 20000 cents, and the revolver has no balance and ample availability. What draw, in cents, brings ending cash back to minimum?

    Ending cash before a draw is \(30000 + 40000 - 120000 = -50000\). The shortfall against the 20000-cent minimum is \(20000 - (-50000) = 70000\) cents, and availability covers it, so the draw is 70000 cents (L03 and L04, and the project's revolver rule).

  8. Skill check (rung 2). The same week, with the same 70000-cent shortfall, but the revolver commitment is 50000 cents and 20000 cents are already drawn. What draw, in cents, does the revolver make?

    Availability is \(50000 - 20000 = 30000\) cents. The draw is the shortfall capped at availability, so it is 30000 cents. The remaining \(70000 - 30000 = 40000\) cents is reported as a shortfall, not hidden (L04 and the project's revolver rule).

Lessons

Five lessons of eight study hours each. Every lesson has graded claims, a runnable lab, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck. The lessons follow the working-capital and treasury cycle: the cash conversion cycle, credit and supply-chain terms, the 13-week forecast, the revolver with its covenants, and the cash buffer with commercial paper.

L01 · 8 hThe Cash Conversion Cycle and Working-Capital DaysDays sales outstanding, days inventory outstanding and days payable outstanding, their identity in the cash conversion cycle, and the working-capital investment they imply. L02 · 8 hTrade Credit, Credit Policy and Supply-Chain FinanceThe break-even of a credit-term extension, the implied annual cost of an early-payment discount, and the financing that a supplier or a bank can provide against payables. L03 · 8 hThe 13-Week Direct Cash-Flow ForecastA week-by-week forecast of receipts and disbursements, the weekly cash identity, and the minimum-cash rule that fixes the revolver draw. L04 · 8 hRevolving Credit Facilities and Financial CovenantsRevolver availability, minimum-liquidity headroom, the pinch week, and the effect of a slip in collections on both. L05 · 8 hCommercial Paper and Precautionary Cash HoldingsCommercial paper as a short-term unsecured note, the precautionary motive, and a seeded SIMULATED comparison of shortfall frequency across cash buffers.

The project ladder

The module ends in the 13-week cash-flow forecast. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. You may skip them when you reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved 13-week forecast of a SIMULATED stressed case, with the revolver draw, the covenant headroom and one working-capital action, each step explained.

  2. Completion problem

    The same forecast with the revolver draw line replaced by blanks. Its output must match the expected output when the blanks are right.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the treasury forecast engine to the project specification, and write the treasurer memo. The autograder checks the engine, and the rubric grades the memo. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    The covenant pinch week under a range of collection slips on the same case, with the hypothesis stated before the run.

Data status

No dataset is used. No CF8 page embeds a dataset or reads a market data file. Every case in the labs and the project is generated from a seed or typed in for the module, and it is labelled SIMULATED. Real company filings in XBRL are not embedded. Their reuse terms are not recorded in tools/data_sources.yaml, so the unit does not use them. No CF8 result depends on it.

Core reading

This unit lists no core reading yet. Its lessons cite no registry key for a finding, and the only quantitative results are proofs on the page and SIMULATED lab output. The credit, covenant and commercial-paper topics are taught as definitions and proofs, with no cited finding about firm practice.
