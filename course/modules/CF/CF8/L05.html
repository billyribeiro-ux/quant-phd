Commercial Paper and Precautionary Cash Holdings

  • Uses: the cash-flow statement, the revolving credit facility, the direct cash forecast

  • Ttheorem

  • Bpublished, not settled

A firm that runs short of cash can borrow, sell an asset or delay a payment. Holding cash is one way to cover such a shortfall; this lesson models it and no other. This lesson defines commercial paper, a short-term note that firms sell to investors, and the precautionary motive for holding cash. It proves two properties of a cash buffer against a net outflow. Its one simulation, labelled SIMULATED, measures shortfall frequency across buffers. The lesson states no finding on how firms hold cash in practice.

1Two definitions and a shortfall

Definition 1.1 (net flow, buffer and shortfall)

Let \(X_1, \ldots, X_N\) be a firm's net cash flows in months \(1\) to \(N\), in cents, where a positive \(X_t\) is a surplus of receipts over disbursements in month \(t\). Let \(S_0 = 0\) and \(S_t = X_1 + \cdots + X_t\) be the cumulative net flow. The net flow is the difference between receipts and disbursements, the quantity that the direct cash forecast of L03 lists line by line. A cash buffer of \(B \ge 0\) cents is the cash the firm holds at the start of the year. A shortfall occurs in the year when \(B + S_t \lt 0\) for some month \(t\) with \(1 \le t \le N\). That is, the year runs short when the cash balance, with no outside funding, goes negative in some month. Write \(M = \min_{1 \le t \le N} S_t\) for the lowest cumulative net flow of the year. A shortfall at buffer \(B\) is the same event as \(M \lt -B\).

Definition 1.2 (commercial paper)

Commercial paper is a short-term debt security. A firm issues it to investors for a stated maturity, and repays it at maturity. It is unsecured, which means that no specific asset is pledged to the holder. It is a source of short-term funds that does not come from a bank agreement, so it differs from the revolving credit facility of L04, which is a bank line that the firm draws on under its agreement. This lesson states no market size, no maximum maturity and no claim about which firms issue commercial paper. It models no commercial paper and treats it only as a named source of funds.

Definition 1.3 (precautionary motive)

Precautionary cash holdings are cash that a firm keeps so that it can cover a net outflow whose size or timing it does not know in advance. The motive is a reason for holding cash, not a quantity. The quantity this lesson measures is the shortfall frequency at a buffer \(B\), defined in Definition 1.1. The lesson models no cost of a shortfall, so it cannot say which buffer is best for a firm.

2What a buffer guarantees

Proposition 2.1 (a larger buffer cannot add shortfall years)

Assumptions. (1) A finite set of years is fixed, and each year \(y\) has net flows \(X_1(y), \ldots, X_N(y)\) with cumulative flows \(S_t(y)\). (2) The same years are used for every buffer. (3) Buffers satisfy \(0 \le B \le B'\), in cents.

Statement. Let \(R(B)\) be the set of years that run short at buffer \(B\). Then \(R(B') \subseteq R(B)\). Hence the number of shortfall years at \(B'\) is at most the number at \(B\), and the shortfall frequency is non-increasing in the buffer.

Proof

Let year \(y\) be in \(R(B')\). Then \(B' + S_t(y) \lt 0\) for some \(t\) with \(1 \le t \le N\). Since \(B \le B'\), we have \(B + S_t(y) \le B' + S_t(y) \lt 0\) for that same \(t\), so \(y\) is in \(R(B)\). This proves the inclusion. Both counts are taken over the same fixed set of years, so the inclusion of sets gives the inequality of counts, and dividing both sides by the number of years gives the inequality of frequencies.

Proposition 2.2 (a buffer that covers the worst outflow path)

Assumptions. (1) Each monthly net flow satisfies \(X_t \ge -L\) for a fixed \(L \ge 0\), in cents. (2) The horizon is \(N\) months. (3) The buffer satisfies \(B \ge NL\). Statement. Under these assumptions no year runs short, for any flows that satisfy assumption (1).

Proof

For each \(t\) with \(1 \le t \le N\), assumption (1) gives \(S_t = \sum_{s=1}^{t} X_s \ge -tL \ge -NL\). Then \(B + S_t \ge B - NL \ge 0\) by assumption (3). No month therefore has \(B + S_t \lt 0\), so no year runs short.

Remark 2.3 (what the two propositions do not say)

Proposition 2.1 says that a buffer can only remove shortfall years on fixed years. It does not say by how much. Proposition 2.2 is a sufficient condition under assumption (1), a lower bound on each month's flow. A firm whose flows can fall below \(-L\) is outside its scope. Neither proposition states a probability, because neither assumes a distribution for the flows.

Remark 2.4 (the buffer for 95 percent of years)

Let \(N_Y\) years be fixed, and let \(k = \lceil 0.05\,N_Y \rceil\). The nearest-rank 5 percent quantile \(q\) of the \(N_Y\) yearly minima is the \(k\)-th smallest value. At most \(k - 1\) of the minima lie strictly below \(q\), because the values below \(q\) can occupy at most the first \(k - 1\) places in the sorted list. The buffer \(B = -q\), when \(q \le 0\), therefore runs short in at most \(k - 1\) of the \(N_Y\) years, which is a share of at most \((k-1)/N_Y\). With \(N_Y = 2000\), \(k = 100\), and the share is at most \(99/2000 = 4.95\) percent. This is a derivation, with the value of \(q\) read from Lab 1.

Lab 1 · Shortfall frequency across cash buffers on SIMULATED years

The lab draws 2000 SIMULATED years of twelve monthly net flows. Each flow is an integer number of cents drawn uniformly from minus 100000 to plus 100000, with mean zero, and the generator is seeded with 2026. The lab counts the years that run short at six buffers, from 0 to 1200000 cents, using the same years for every buffer. Proposition 2.1 predicts that the count does not rise with the buffer. Proposition 2.2 predicts no shortfall year at 1200000 cents, which is twelve times the outflow bound of 100000 cents, because every flow here is at least minus 100000 cents. The lab also finds the buffer that covers 95 percent of the years, from the nearest-rank 5 percent quantile of the yearly minima (Remark 2.4).

"""CF8 L05 lab 1: a seeded simulation of one year of net cash flow, and how often a cash buffer runs short. SIMULATED.

SIMULATED: every monthly net cash flow in this lab is drawn from the seeded generator written in this file. No company,
filing or market series is read, and the flows are not a forecast for any firm.

The lab draws N_YEARS simulated years of twelve monthly net cash flows. Each month's flow is the expected flow
MEAN_CENTS plus an integer shock drawn uniformly from minus SPREAD_CENTS to SPREAD_CENTS. The cumulative net flow after
month t is S_t, and the lowest value of S_t over months 1 to 12 is the year's minimum M. A cash buffer of B cents runs
short in a year when B + S_t is negative for some month t, which is the same as M < -B. The shortfall frequency at
buffer B is the share of years with M < -B. The lab uses the same draws for every buffer, so the buffers are compared on
identical years. It checks that the frequency does not rise with the buffer, and it finds the buffer that covers 95
percent of the years.

Units: money is an integer number of cents. A frequency is a share of years. A quantile uses the nearest-rank rule: the
q-quantile of N sorted values is the value at position ceil(qN), counted from 1. The standard error of a frequency p
over N years is sqrt(p(1 - p)/N), which assumes that the years are independent draws.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import math
import random

SEED = 2026  # seed for the generator; printed in the output
N_YEARS = 2000  # number of simulated years
MONTHS = 12  # months in one simulated year
MEAN_CENTS = 0  # expected monthly net cash flow, a SIMULATED break-even firm
SPREAD_CENTS = 100_000  # each monthly shock is an integer uniform draw from minus this to plus this
BUFFERS_CENTS = (0, 250_000, 500_000, 750_000, 1_000_000, 1_200_000)  # cash buffers compared, in cents


def draw_year(rng: random.Random) -> list[int]:
    """Twelve monthly net cash flows, in integer cents, for one simulated year."""
    return [MEAN_CENTS + rng.randint(-SPREAD_CENTS, SPREAD_CENTS) for _ in range(MONTHS)]


def min_cumulative(flows: list[int]) -> int:
    """The lowest cumulative net flow over months 1 to MONTHS, in cents: the minimum of the partial sums of flows."""
    return min(itertools.accumulate(flows))


def shortfall_years(minima: list[int], buffer: int) -> int:
    """The number of years in which a buffer of the given cents runs short: years with minimum below minus buffer."""
    return sum(1 for m in minima if m < -buffer)


def nearest_rank(sorted_values: list[int], numerator: int, denominator: int) -> int:
    """The nearest-rank quantile numerator/denominator of sorted values: the value at position ceil(qN), from 1."""
    n = len(sorted_values)
    rank = -((-numerator * n) // denominator)
    return sorted_values[rank - 1]


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    years = [draw_year(rng) for _ in range(N_YEARS)]
    minima = [min_cumulative(year) for year in years]
    print(f"seed {SEED} years {N_YEARS} months {MONTHS} mean_cents {MEAN_CENTS} shock_cents {SPREAD_CENTS} SIMULATED")
    q05 = nearest_rank(sorted(minima), 5, 100)
    print(f"min_cumulative_mean_cents {sum(minima) / N_YEARS:.2f} min_cumulative_q05_cents {q05}")
    previous = None
    nonincreasing = True
    for buffer in BUFFERS_CENTS:
        count = shortfall_years(minima, buffer)
        p = count / N_YEARS
        se = math.sqrt(p * (1 - p) / N_YEARS)
        print(f"buffer_cents {buffer} shortfall_years {count} frequency {p:.4f} standard_error {se:.4f}")
        if previous is not None and count > previous:
            nonincreasing = False
        previous = count
    print(f"frequency_nonincreasing_in_buffer {nonincreasing}")
    cover = -q05
    count = shortfall_years(minima, cover)
    print(f"buffer_covering_95pct_cents {cover} shortfall_years {count} frequency {count / N_YEARS:.4f}")
    print(f"frequency_at_covering_buffer_at_most_5pct {count / N_YEARS <= 0.05}")
    bound = MONTHS * SPREAD_CENTS
    print(f"buffer_twelve_shocks_cents {bound} shortfall_years {shortfall_years(minima, bound)}")


main()

The output agrees with Proposition 2.1: the shortfall count is 1676 at buffer 0 and falls to 356, 15 and then 0, and the printed flag for non-increasing frequency is True. At 750000 cents and above, the lab finds no shortfall year, and this is a fact about these 2000 years only. Proposition 2.2 gives the guaranteed buffer for this generator, 1200000 cents, which the lab prints as having 0 shortfall years, as the proposition requires. The 95 percent buffer is 365525 cents, from a quantile of minus 365525 cents, and it runs short in 99 of the 2000 years, a frequency of 0.0495, which is at most 5 percent (Remark 2.4). The lab does not show how often any firm runs short.

3What the model leaves out

The model in Lab 1 has four features, and each one limits what its numbers mean. It has no cost of a shortfall, so the frequency is the only output, and no buffer is called optimal. It has no backstop: the revolving credit facility of L04 and commercial paper of Definition 1.2 do not appear in the years, so the buffer is cash only. Its months are independent draws with a fixed spread, so serial correlation across months is excluded by assumption. Its years come from one seed, so the standard error column is a sampling-error formula for these years, and it says nothing about another seed or another generator.

The lesson therefore makes no claim about how firms set their cash holdings. Evidence on that question is an empirical matter, and no source for a finding on it is cited in this lesson.

The canon of precautionary cash has four limits, and each one is a limit of the model, not a finding about firms.

  • The buffer has no cost of its own. Holding cash avoids a shortfall and forgoes some other use of the cash. The lesson has no term for that forgone use, so Proposition 2.1 says that a larger buffer does not raise shortfall, and it says nothing about whether the larger buffer is worth holding.
  • The months are independent by assumption. Lab 1 draws each month separately. A generator in which one month's flow carries into the next would give a different frequency. The lesson does not compute that case, so it states no direction for it.
  • The backstop is absent. A firm facing a shortfall can draw a revolving credit facility or, where it has access, issue commercial paper. Lab 1 counts only cash, so its shortfall frequency is the frequency of a cash-only firm.
  • The sample is one seed. The 95 percent buffer is one value from one seed. A different seed moves the printed quantile and the printed counts. The standard error gives the scale of that movement only under the independence assumption of the standard-error formula of Lab 1 (the years are independent draws).

4Misconceptions

  1. “A bigger buffer always lowers the shortfall frequency.”

    Proposition 2.1 says the frequency is non-increasing, which allows it to stay flat. Lab 1 shows the flat case: the shortfall count is 0 at 750000, 1000000 and 1200000 cents. On the same years, a larger buffer can only remove shortfall years, and once no year runs short, a further increase removes none.

  2. “Commercial paper is a bank loan the firm can draw at will.”

    Commercial paper is a note that the firm sells to investors, with a stated maturity, and it is unsecured (Definition 1.2). A bank line such as the revolving credit facility of L04 is a different instrument with different terms. The two can both serve as a backstop, and neither one is the other.

  3. “A 2000-year simulation gives the firm its exact probability of a shortfall.”

    Lab 1 gives a frequency from one seed and one generator. Its standard error at a frequency of 0.1780 is 0.0086 under the independence formula (Lab 1 output, buffer 250000). The frequency describes the generator, and it changes with the spread and the mean that the generator sets.

5Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · The smallest buffer that avoids shortfall

A year has three monthly net flows of \(-300\), \(+100\) and \(-200\) cents. Compute the cumulative flows \(S_1\), \(S_2\) and \(S_3\). Find the smallest buffer \(B \ge 0\) that avoids a shortfall in this year.

Solution

The cumulative flows are \(S_1 = -300\), \(S_2 = -200\) and \(S_3 = -400\). A year avoids shortfall when \(B + S_t \ge 0\) for every \(t\), which is \(B \ge -S_t\) for every \(t\). The largest of the values \(-S_t\) is \(400\), at \(t = 3\), so the smallest buffer is \(B = 400\) cents. At \(B = 250\), the months \(t = 1\) and \(t = 3\) both run short, since \(250 - 300 = -50\) and \(250 - 400 = -150\).

M2 · A standard error by hand

A frequency of 356 shortfall years out of 2000 is \(p = 0.178\). Compute the standard error \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}\) for \(N = 2000\), to four decimals.

Solution

\(p(1-p) = 0.178 \times 0.822 = 0.146316\). Dividing by 2000 gives \(0.000073158\). Its square root is \(0.0085532\) to seven decimals (more precisely \(0.00855324\ldots\)), which rounds to \(0.0086\) at four decimals, the value that Lab 1 prints for buffer 250000 (rounding is to the fourth decimal of the unrounded root). The formula assumes independent years.

M3 · The nearest-rank position

Thirty yearly minima are sorted. Find the position of the nearest-rank 5 percent quantile, counted from 1, and the position for 2000 yearly minima.

Solution

For \(N = 30\), \(0.05 \times 30 = 1.5\), and the ceiling is \(2\). The 5 percent quantile is the second smallest value. For \(N = 2000\), \(0.05 \times 2000 = 100\) exactly, so the position is \(100\), and the quantile is the 100th smallest value. Lab 1 uses this position, through the integer form \(\lceil 5N/100 \rceil\), and it reads the value at index 99 of the sorted list.

Proof

P1 · The buffer that covers one year

Fix one year with cumulative flows \(S_1, \ldots, S_N\). Show that \(B^\ast = \max(0, \max_t(-S_t))\) is the smallest buffer \(B \ge 0\) that avoids a shortfall in that year.

Solution

A buffer \(B \ge 0\) avoids shortfall in the year exactly when \(B + S_t \ge 0\) for each \(t\) from 1 to \(N\), which is when \(B \ge -S_t\) for each \(t\). Since the set of months is finite, the maximum \(\max_t(-S_t)\) exists, and \(B\) satisfies all the inequalities exactly when \(B\) is at least that maximum. Together with \(B \ge 0\), the smallest such buffer is \(\max(0, \max_t(-S_t))\). In M1 the maximum is 400, so \(B^\ast = 400\).

P2 · The 95 percent buffer in general

Let \(N_Y\) yearly minima be fixed, with \(k = \lceil 0.05 N_Y \rceil\), and let \(q\) be the \(k\)-th smallest minimum, with \(q \le 0\). Show that the buffer \(B = -q\) runs short in at most \(k - 1\) years.

Solution

A year runs short at buffer \(B = -q\) exactly when its minimum \(M\) satisfies \(M \lt -B = q\). The minima strictly below \(q\) are values that come before the \(k\)-th smallest in the sorted order, so there are at most \(k - 1\) of them. Hence at most \(k - 1\) years run short. This is the statement of Remark 2.4 without the numbers, and it uses only the nearest-rank definition.

P3 · The guarantee in Proposition 2.2 is sharp

Under assumption (1) of Proposition 2.2 with \(L \ge 0\) and horizon \(N\), show that a buffer of \(NL - 1\) cents does not guarantee that no year runs short.

Solution

Take the admissible year with \(X_t = -L\) for every month \(t\). It satisfies assumption (1), because \(-L \ge -L\). Its cumulative flow at the horizon is \(S_N = -NL\). With buffer \(B = NL - 1\), the value is \(B + S_N = -1 \lt 0\), so this year runs short. The bound in Proposition 2.2 cannot be lowered by one cent for the whole class of flows that satisfy assumption (1), because this one year is in the class. This is the worked form of the sharpness claim.

Empirical

E1 · Years that a buffer of zero runs short, but 250000 cents does not

Lab 1 prints 1676 shortfall years at buffer 0 and 356 at buffer 250000 cents, over the same 2000 years. How many years run short at buffer 0 and do not run short at 250000 cents? Use the inclusion of Proposition 2.1.

Solution

By Proposition 2.1 the set of years that run short at 250000 cents is contained in the set that runs short at 0. The years in the second set but not the first number \(1676 - 356 = 1320\). The inclusion is what makes the subtraction a count of years. The answer, 1320 of 2000 years, is SIMULATED, from Lab 1.

E2 · The 95 percent buffer in currency units

Lab 1 prints a buffer of 365525 cents for the 95 percent covering buffer. Convert it to currency units, and state the shortfall frequency that the lab prints for it.

Solution

In currency units the buffer is \(365525 / 100 = 3655.25\). The lab prints 99 shortfall years of 2000, a frequency of \(99/2000 = 0.0495\). That is at most 0.05, as Remark 2.4 requires. Both figures are SIMULATED output of Lab 1, from seed 2026 and the generator in the code.

E3 · Is 750000 cents a guarantee?

Lab 1 prints zero shortfall years at 750000 cents. Does that show that a buffer of 750000 cents avoids shortfall for this generator in general? Give the reason.

Solution

No. The lab shows zero shortfall years in its 2000 draws, which is a fact about those draws only. The generator admits the year with every flow equal to \(-100000\) cents, which is in the class of Proposition 2.2 with \(L = 100000\). In that year \(S_t = -100000\,t\), and \(750000 - 800000 = -50000\) at \(t = 8\), so the year runs short at 750000 cents. The guarantee in Proposition 2.2 needs 1200000 cents, and the printed zero is consistent with it.

Research

R1 · Comparing buffers under two generators

Design a SIMULATED study that compares the shortfall frequency at 250000 cents under two generators: one with mean zero, as in Lab 1, and one with a mean of minus 50000 cents a month. State the seed, the number of years, the statistic, the common draws, and what the design cannot show.

Solution

Use random.Random(seed) with one fixed seed, and draw \(N = 2000\) years of twelve monthly shocks for each generator, reusing the same shock stream for both so that the two comparisons share years. The statistic is the shortfall frequency at 250000 cents for each generator, with its standard error from the independence formula. The design shows how the frequency responds to the mean under this shock distribution. It cannot show the frequency for a firm, because the shock distribution, the independence of months and the absence of a backstop are all set by the design.

R2 · The standard error of a difference between two simulations

Two independent simulations with \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) years give shortfall frequencies \(p_1\) and \(p_2\). Under independence of years within and between the simulations, derive the standard error of \(p_1 - p_2\).

Solution

Each frequency is a mean of independent indicators with variance \(p(1-p)\), so \(\operatorname{Var}(p_1) = p_1(1-p_1)/N_1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(p_2) = p_2(1-p_2)/N_2\). Independence of the two simulations makes the variances add: \(\operatorname{Var}(p_1 - p_2) = p_1(1-p_1)/N_1 + p_2(1-p_2)/N_2\). The standard error is the square root of that sum. This is a derivation under the stated independence, so it is graded T for the algebra and gives no claim about firms.

R3 · How many years for a given precision

At a true frequency near 0.178, how many independent years give a standard error of 0.002 under the independence formula? Use the answer to say whether Lab 1's 2000 years are enough for a standard error of 0.002.

Solution

Set \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/N} = 0.002\), so \(N = p(1-p)/0.002^2 = 0.146316/0.000004 = 36579\), rounded up to 36579 years. Lab 1's 2000 years give a standard error of 0.0086 at this frequency (M2), which is about 4.3 times the target (0.0086 against 0.0020), so 2000 years are not enough for that precision. The answer is a computation under the independence assumption and applies to this generator.
