Valuation

How do market participants estimate the intrinsic economic value of an operating enterprise and its common equity claims? This module covers the theoretical foundations, accounting articulation, and computational modeling of corporate valuation. You will formulate free cash flow to the firm (FCFF), implement discounted cash flow (FCFF DCF) under explicit WACC discounting, model terminal value subject to long-run macroeconomic convergence, navigate the complete enterprise-to-equity bridge, decompose multi-segment conglomerates via sum-of-the-parts (SOTP), value managerial flexibility as a real option, and analyze the dominant sources of valuation error alongside the Ohlson clean-surplus residual income model.

  • Prerequisites: CF2 Cost of Capital & Capital Structure, AC2 Financial Statement Analysis & Earnings Quality.
  • Project: Atlas A16: Valuation Engine (autograded library in atlas/valuation.py, 28 h).

72 h lessons · 28 h project (28% of the 100 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CF4 you can:

  1. Formulate and compute Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) and articulate its distinctions from CFO and FCFE across working capital and capital expenditure cycles.
  2. Implement discounted cash flow (DCF) enterprise valuation using WACC under both end-of-year and mid-year discounting conventions.
  3. Model terminal value using Gordon growth and exit multiple methodologies, enforcing macroeconomic growth bounds and return on new invested capital (RONIC) convergence.
  4. Construct the complete enterprise-to-equity bridge, incorporating debt, cash, minority interests, preferred claims, and option dilution via the Treasury stock method.
  5. Value multi-segment conglomerates using sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) and evaluate managerial flexibility using Dixit–Pindyck real option timing and abandonment frameworks.
  6. Decompose empirical valuation errors and prove the equivalence of DCF and Ohlson residual income under clean surplus accounting.

Prerequisites

CF4 builds directly on corporate financial theory from CF2 and accounting analysis from AC2. It assumes these core concepts without redefining them:

  • From CF2: weighted average cost of capital, levered and unlevered beta, and Modigliani–Miller propositions.
  • From AC2: return on invested capital, accruals, and standardized accounting statements.
  • From CF1: net present value and net working capital.
  • From M1 & S1: present value, geometric series, and growing perpetuity.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: six on prerequisite concepts from CF2, AC2, and CF1, and four skill checks on the core valuation engine tasks rungs 1 and 2 teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 when you meet the bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the WACC identity (CF2), how are capital weights calculated?

    Options

    Cost of capital measures the opportunity cost of capital in current capital market equilibrium, which requires market values of debt and equity.

  2. A firm has operating income (EBIT) of 100.0 million dollars and a corporate marginal tax rate of 25.0%. What is its Net Operating Profit After Taxes (NOPAT) in millions of dollars?

    \(\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - \tau_c) = 100.0 \times (1 - 0.25) = 75.0\) million dollars.

  3. Which cash flow metric represents cash generated by core operations available to both debt and equity holders?

    Options

    FCFF is unlevered cash flow generated prior to interest expense, belonging to all financial claimants.

  4. A perpetual cash flow of 60.0 million dollars grows at 3.0% per year. What is its capitalized present value when discounted at a WACC of 8.0% in millions of dollars?

    Capitalized value \(= \text{CF}_1 / (\text{WACC} - g) = 60.0 / (0.08 - 0.03) = 60.0 / 0.05 = 1{,}200.0\) million dollars.

  5. When transitioning from operating Enterprise Value to common Equity Value, what is the correct treatment of cash and financial debt?

    Options

    Enterprise value reflects operating assets; common equity equals EV minus non-equity claims (debt) plus non-operating liquid assets (cash).

  6. Enterprise value is 1000.0 million dollars, total debt is 200.0 million, and cash is 100.0 million. If there are 50.0 million shares outstanding and no other claims, what is equity value per share in dollars?

    \(\text{Net debt} = 200 - 100 = 100\). \(\text{Equity value} = 1000 - 100 = 900\). Per share \(= 900 / 50 = 18.00\) dollars.

  7. What economic constraint governs the perpetual terminal growth rate \(g\) in a DCF model?

    Options

    If a firm grew faster than the aggregate economy indefinitely, its revenues would eventually exceed global GDP.

  8. A company has invested capital of 500.0 million dollars and generates NOPAT of 75.0 million dollars. What is its Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) in percent?

    \(\text{ROIC} = \text{NOPAT} / \text{Invested Capital} = 75.0 / 500.0 = 15.0\%\).

  9. Under clean surplus accounting, what is the definition of residual income (abnormal earnings)?

    Options

    Residual income measures accounting profit generated in excess of shareholders' required opportunity cost of capital.

  10. Why does a standard static DCF undervalue projects that possess significant managerial flexibility under uncertainty?

    Options

    Real options account for managerial adaptiveness: exercising expansion when market outcomes are favorable and truncating downside when unfavorable (Dixit & Pindyck 1994).

Lessons

Six lessons covering 72 study hours. Each lesson introduces rigorous definitions, mathematical theorems with proofs, inline numerical labs, and misconceptions.

L01 · Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) Architecture

12 study hours · free cash flow to the firm

Formal derivation of FCFF from NOPAT, depreciation, capital expenditures, and working capital. Reinvestment rate dynamics and articulation with balance sheet capital.

L02 · Discounted Cash Flow Valuation & Discounting Mechanics

12 study hours · FCFF DCF valuation

Discounting explicit multi-year operating projections using WACC. Year-end versus mid-year cash flow arrival conventions, discount factor curves, and cash flow consistency.

L03 · Terminal Value Modeling & Long-Run Convergence

12 study hours · terminal value

The Gordon growth model and exit multiple heuristics. Fundamental value driver formulation, macroeconomic growth caps, and competitive advantage fade dynamics.

L04 · Enterprise-to-Equity Bridge, Capital Claims & Multiples

12 study hours · enterprise-to-equity bridge

Reconciling operating enterprise value to equity value. Net debt, minority interest, non-operating assets, Treasury stock method option dilution, and trading multiple consistency.

L05 · Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) & Real Options in Capital Allocation

12 study hours · sum-of-the-parts · real option

Segment-by-segment valuation of conglomerates and corporate overhead capitalization. Dixit–Pindyck irreversible investment timing, expansion, and abandonment options.

L06 · Valuation Error Decomposition & Residual Income Synthesis

12 study hours · valuation error sources

Taxonomy and empirical decomposition of forecast error, discount rate error, and terminal bias. Clean-surplus accounting and mathematical equivalence with the Ohlson residual income model.
