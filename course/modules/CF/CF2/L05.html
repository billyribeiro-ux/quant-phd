The Interest Tax Shield and MM With Corporate Taxes

  • Uses: modigliani-miller-propositions, depreciation tax shield
  • Introduces: interest tax shield
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

Under corporate tax regimes, interest paid on corporate debt is deductible from taxable earnings, whereas dividend distributions to equity are paid from after-tax income. This asymmetric tax treatment creates an interest tax shield that subsidizes debt financing. Introducing corporate taxes dismantles the capital structure irrelevance of Modigliani–Miller: issuing debt increases total cash flows available to investors by reducing payments to the tax authority. This lesson establishes the value of the tax shield, proves that for permanent debt its present value is tau_c times debt, derives levered firm value, and traces the downward path of WACC.

1The tax subsidy of debt

Consider a firm generating operating earnings before interest and taxes (\(\mathrm{EBIT}\)). Let \(\tau_c \in (0, 1)\) be the marginal corporate income tax rate. If the firm is 100% equity financed (unlevered), corporate taxes are \(\tau_c \mathrm{EBIT}\), leaving after-tax cash flow to investors of

\[ CF_U = \mathrm{EBIT}(1 - \tau_c). \]

Now suppose the firm issues debt with market value \(D\) paying interest \(r_D D\). Taxable income is \(\mathrm{EBIT} - r_D D\), and corporate taxes paid are \(\tau_c(\mathrm{EBIT} - r_D D)\). Net income to equity is \((\mathrm{EBIT} - r_D D)(1 - \tau_c)\). Total cash flow distributed to both debt and equity investors is

\[ \begin{aligned} CF_L &= (\mathrm{EBIT} - r_D D)(1 - \tau_c) + r_D D \\ &= \mathrm{EBIT}(1 - \tau_c) - r_D D(1 - \tau_c) + r_D D \\ &= \mathrm{EBIT}(1 - \tau_c) + \tau_c r_D D \\ &= CF_U + \tau_c r_D D. \end{aligned} \]

Definition 5.1 (interest tax shield)

Assuming interest is fully deductible at the corporate tax rate \(\tau_c\) (Assumption A9), the annual interest tax shield is the reduction in corporate taxes resulting from the debt interest deduction:

\[ \mathrm{Tax\ Shield}_t = \tau_c r_D D. \]

Remark 5.1 (contrast with depreciation tax shield)

In CF1, the depreciation tax shield was defined as \(\tau_c \times \mathrm{Depreciation}\), arising from the non-cash write-off of capital assets. The interest tax shield operates identically in tax accounting by shielding operating income from taxation, but arises from the contractual financing choice rather than asset depreciation schedules.

2Valuation of the interest tax shield

Theorem 5.1 (present value of permanent tax shield)

Under Assumption A6 (permanent debt at constant level \(D\)), Assumption A9 (full interest deductibility at rate \(\tau_c\)), and discount rate for the tax shield equal to the cost of debt \(r_D\), the present value of the interest tax shield is

\[ \mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{Tax\ Shield}) = \tau_c D. \]

Proof

Because the debt is permanent and maintains a constant balance \(D\), the annual tax savings \(\tau_c r_D D\) form a level perpetual cash flow stream. Because the tax shield has the same default risk as the debt interest that creates it, its appropriate discount rate is \(r_D\). Applying the standard perpetuity formula from M1:

\[ \mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{Tax\ Shield}) = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{\tau_c r_D D}{(1 + r_D)^t} = \frac{\tau_c r_D D}{r_D} = \tau_c D. \]

The interest rate \(r_D\) cancels exactly, proving the result. \(\blacksquare\)

Theorem 5.2 (levered firm value with corporate taxes)

Under Assumptions A6 and A9, the total market value of the levered firm \(V_L\) equals the value of an otherwise identical unlevered firm \(V_U\) plus the present value of the interest tax shield:

\[ V_L = V_U + \tau_c D. \]

Proof

Total cash flows to the levered firm equal unlevered operating cash flows plus the interest tax shield: \(CF_L = CF_U + \tau_c r_D D\). By value additivity, the present value of the sum of two cash flow streams equals the sum of their present values: \(V_L = \mathrm{PV}(CF_U) + \mathrm{PV}(\tau_c r_D D) = V_U + \tau_c D\). \(\blacksquare\)

3Cost of equity and WACC under corporate taxes

Theorem 5.3 (MM Proposition II with corporate taxes)

Under Assumptions A6 and A9, the cost of equity \(r_E\) for a levered firm satisfies

\[ r_E = r_U + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D) \frac{D}{E}, \]

where \(r_U\) is the unlevered cost of equity capital. Furthermore, WACC declines monotonically with leverage according to

\[ r_{\mathrm{WACC}} = r_U \left(1 - \tau_c \frac{D}{V_L}\right). \]

Proof

Net income to equity is \((\mathrm{EBIT} - r_D D)(1 - \tau_c) = \mathrm{EBIT}(1 - \tau_c) - r_D D(1 - \tau_c) = r_U V_U - r_D D(1 - \tau_c)\). Since \(V_U = V_L - \tau_c D = E + D - \tau_c D = E + (1 - \tau_c) D\), substitute for \(V_U\):

\[ \begin{aligned} r_E E &= r_U [E + (1 - \tau_c) D] - r_D D(1 - \tau_c) \\ &= r_U E + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D) D. \end{aligned} \]

Dividing both sides by \(E\) gives \(r_E = r_U + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D) D/E\). Now compute WACC: \(r_{\mathrm{WACC}} = \frac{E}{V_L} r_E + \frac{D}{V_L} r_D(1 - \tau_c)\). Substituting \(r_E E\) yields

\[ \begin{aligned} r_{\mathrm{WACC}} &= \frac{r_U E + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D) D + (1 - \tau_c) r_D D}{V_L} \\ &= \frac{r_U E + (1 - \tau_c) r_U D}{V_L} \\ &= \frac{r_U [E + D - \tau_c D]}{V_L} \\ &= \frac{r_U [V_L - \tau_c D]}{V_L} \\ &= r_U \left(1 - \tau_c \frac{D}{V_L}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Because \(\tau_c > 0\), the factor \(1 - \tau_c(D/V_L)\) decreases strictly as \(D/V_L\) increases, proving WACC falls with leverage. \(\blacksquare\)

Lab 1 · The interest tax shield and MM with corporate taxes

Verifies that the present value of a permanent tax shield equals tau_c * D, tabulates enterprise value V_L, equity value E, and checks that component WACC matches r_U * (1 - tau_c * D / V_L) on an exact rational grid.

"""CF2 lab L05-1: The interest tax shield and MM with corporate taxes.

Model: Exact closed-form capital structure calculations with corporate income taxes.
Uses fractions.Fraction for exact rational calculations.
Demonstrates:
  1. Annual tax shield equals tau_c * r_D * D.
  2. PV of permanent tax shield discounted at r_D equals exactly tau_c * D.
  3. Levered value V_L = V_U + tau_c * D.
  4. Cost of equity r_E = r_U + (1 - tau_c) * (r_U - r_D) * (D / E).
  5. WACC declines monotonically with leverage as r_WACC = r_U * (1 - tau_c * D / V_L).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    print("CF2 Lab L05-1: Interest Tax Shield and MM with Corporate Taxes")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------")

    ebit = Fraction(10_000_000, 1)  # $10M perpetual EBIT
    tau_c = Fraction(25, 100)  # 25% corporate tax rate
    r_u = Fraction(10, 100)  # 10% unlevered cost of capital
    r_d = Fraction(5, 100)  # 5% pre-tax cost of debt

    # Unlevered firm value: after-tax operating flow / r_U
    after_tax_ebit = ebit * (Fraction(1, 1) - tau_c)
    v_u = after_tax_ebit / r_u

    print(f"Perpetual EBIT:                     ${ebit:,.2f}")
    print(f"Corporate tax rate (tau_c):          {tau_c} = {float(tau_c):.1%}")
    print(f"After-tax unlevered flow:           ${after_tax_ebit:,.2f}")
    print(f"Unlevered cost of equity (r_U):     {r_u} = {float(r_u):.2%}")
    print(f"Unlevered firm value (V_U):         ${v_u:,.2f}")

    print("\nLeverage and Tax Shield Progression:")
    print(
        f"{'Debt ($M)':>10} | {'PV(Shield)':>11} | {'V_L ($M)':>10} | {'Equity ($M)':>11} | "
        f"{'D / V':>7} | {'r_E (%)':>8} | {'WACC (%)':>8}"
    )
    print("-" * 78)

    for debt_mil in [0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60]:
        d = Fraction(debt_mil * 1_000_000, 1)

        # 1. Tax shield per period and PV
        annual_shield = tau_c * r_d * d
        pv_shield = annual_shield / r_d  # Exact discount rate r_D => pv_shield = tau_c * D
        assert pv_shield == tau_c * d, "PV(Tax Shield) must equal tau_c * D"

        # 2. Levered value
        v_l = v_u + pv_shield
        e_val = v_l - d

        # 3. Cost of equity (MM Proposition II with tax)
        # r_E = r_U + (1 - tau_c) * (r_U - r_D) * (D / E)
        de_ratio = d / e_val if e_val > 0 else Fraction(0, 1)
        r_e = r_u + (Fraction(1, 1) - tau_c) * (r_u - r_d) * de_ratio

        # 4. WACC calculated via component weights
        w_e = e_val / v_l
        w_d = d / v_l
        r_wacc_components = w_e * r_e + w_d * r_d * (Fraction(1, 1) - tau_c)

        # 5. WACC direct formula: r_U * (1 - tau_c * D / V_L)
        r_wacc_direct = r_u * (Fraction(1, 1) - tau_c * (d / v_l))
        assert r_wacc_components == r_wacc_direct, "Component WACC must match direct MM formula"

        print(
            f"{debt_mil:10.1f} | {float(pv_shield) / 1e6:10.2f}M | {float(v_l) / 1e6:10.2f} | "
            f"{float(e_val) / 1e6:11.2f} | {float(w_d):7.4f} | {float(r_e) * 100:8.2f} | "
            f"{float(r_wacc_components) * 100:8.2f}"
        )

    print("-" * 78)
    print("Numerical confirmation: PV of permanent tax shield equals tau_c * D; WACC falls as D/V rises.")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

If Theorem 5.2 and Theorem 5.3 described reality without qualification, every corporation would maximize shareholder wealth by adopting 100% debt financing, driving WACC to its lowest possible level. In real markets, firms rarely exceed 50% to 60% leverage. The formula breaks because it ignores personal taxes (Miller 1977), non-debt tax shields (depreciation and tax credit exhaustion), and, most critically, the costs of financial distress and bankruptcy, which we introduce in Lesson 6.

5Misconceptions

  • The interest tax shield is the full dollar amount of interest paid by the company: \(r_D D\).

    False. The interest payment is a cash outflow paid to lenders. The tax shield is only the tax savings generated by deducting that interest from taxable profits: \(\tau_c \times r_D D\).

  • A firm with zero or negative taxable earnings receives the full benefit of the interest tax shield immediately.

    False. If a firm operates at a loss, interest deductions cannot shield current income and must be carried forward as net operating losses (NOLs), reducing their present value and creating non-debt tax shield exhaustion.

  • The present value of the tax shield is always \(\tau_c D\), regardless of debt repayment horizon or debt policy.

    False. The formula \(\mathrm{PV} = \tau_c D\) holds only for permanent debt maintaining a constant dollar balance. If debt is rebalanced to target a constant leverage ratio, future tax shields fluctuate with firm value and must be discounted at \(r_U\) (Miles and Ezzell 1980).

6Problems

Mechanics

M1 · Hand calculation of permanent tax shield

An unlevered firm has enterprise value \(V_U = 80\) million dollars. It issues 30 million dollars of permanent debt at 6.0% interest. The corporate tax rate is 25.0%. Find the annual interest tax shield, the present value of the shield, and the new levered firm value \(V_L\).

Solution

Annual interest is \(30 \times 0.06 = 1.80\) million dollars. Annual tax shield is \(\tau_c r_D D = 0.25 \times 1.80 = 0.45\) million dollars. Present value of permanent shield is \(\tau_c D = 0.25 \times 30 = 7.50\) million dollars. New levered value is \(V_L = V_U + \tau_c D = 80.00 + 7.50 = 87.50\) million dollars.

M2 · WACC reduction with corporate taxes

For the firm in M1, if the unlevered cost of equity is \(r_U = 10.0\%\), compute WACC at the new leverage level using Theorem 5.3.

Solution

\(V_L = 87.50\) million, \(D = 30.00\) million. Debt ratio is \(D / V_L = 30 / 87.50 = 12/35 \approx 0.342857\). WACC formula: \(r_{\mathrm{WACC}} = r_U [1 - \tau_c (D / V_L)] = 10.0\% \times [1 - 0.25 \times (12/35)] = 10.0\% \times [1 - 3/35] = 10.0\% \times (32/35) = 320/35\% \approx 9.1429\%\) (9.14%).

M3 · Equity cost under corporate taxes

For the firm in M1 and M2, compute the cost of equity \(r_E\) under MM Proposition II with corporate taxes.

Solution

Equity value is \(E = V_L - D = 87.50 - 30.00 = 57.50\) million dollars. \(D/E = 30 / 57.50 = 12/23 \approx 0.521739\). Formula: \(r_E = r_U + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D)(D/E) = 10.0\% + (1 - 0.25)(10.0\% - 6.0\%) \times (12/23) = 10.0\% + 0.75 \times 4.0\% \times (12/23) = 10.0\% + 3.0\% \times (12/23) = 10.0\% + 1.5652\% = 11.5652\%\) (11.57%).

Proof

P1 · Component WACC equivalence

Prove directly that substituting \(r_E\) and \(r_D(1 - \tau_c)\) into \(w_E r_E + w_D r_D(1 - \tau_c)\) matches \(r_U [1 - \tau_c (D/V_L)]\).

Solution

\(w_E r_E + w_D r_D (1 - \tau_c) = \frac{E}{V_L} [r_U + (1 - \tau_c)(r_U - r_D) \frac{D}{E}] + \frac{D}{V_L} r_D (1 - \tau_c) = \frac{r_U E + (1 - \tau_c) r_U D - (1 - \tau_c) r_D D + (1 - \tau_c) r_D D}{V_L} = \frac{r_U [E + (1 - \tau_c) D]}{V_L}\). Since \(E + D = V_L\), \(E + (1 - \tau_c) D = V_L - \tau_c D\). Dividing by \(V_L\) yields \(r_U [1 - \tau_c (D/V_L)]\). \(\blacksquare\)

P2 · Finite-maturity tax shield

Show that if a firm issues a bond of face value \(D\) maturing in \(T\) periods paying coupon \(r_D D\), the present value of the tax shield is \(\tau_c D [1 - (1 + r_D)^{-T}]\).

Solution

The annual tax shield is \(\tau_c r_D D\) for \(T\) periods. Using the finite annuity formula from M1 with discount rate \(r_D\): \(\mathrm{PV} = \tau_c r_D D \cdot \frac{1 - (1 + r_D)^{-T}}{r_D} = \tau_c D [1 - (1 + r_D)^{-T}]\). As \(T \to \infty\), \((1 + r_D)^{-T} \to 0\), recovering \(\tau_c D\).

P3 · Shareholder value creation from levering up

Prove that when an unlevered firm issues permanent debt \(D\) and uses the proceeds to repurchase shares, existing shareholders capture the entire gain \(\tau_c D\).

Solution

Initial value is \(V_U\) with \(N_0\) shares at price \(P_0 = V_U / N_0\). Announcing the debt issue raises enterprise value to \(V_L = V_U + \tau_c D\). Since debt holders lend \(D\) at fair market yield, the pre-repurchase share price rises to \(P_1 = (V_L - D + D) / N_0 = V_L / N_0 = (V_U + \tau_c D) / N_0\). The firm repurchases \(D / P_1\) shares. Remaining shares retain price \(P_1\). Total wealth of initial shareholders is \(N_0 P_1 = V_U + \tau_c D\), an increase of exactly \(\tau_c D\).

E2 · Miller (1977) personal tax equilibrium

How does introducing personal income taxes on interest (\(\tau_D\)) and equity gains (\(\tau_E\)) modify the effective tax shield per dollar of debt?

Solution

Merton Miller (1977) showed that the net tax gain per dollar of debt is \(1 - \frac{(1 - \tau_c)(1 - \tau_E)}{1 - \tau_D}\). If personal tax rates on interest income \(\tau_D\) exceed tax rates on equity \(\tau_E\) by enough to satisfy \((1 - \tau_D) = (1 - \tau_c)(1 - \tau_E)\), the net tax advantage of debt is completely eliminated in macro equilibrium.

E3 · Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) of 2017

Explain how the TCJA of 2017 reduced the corporate interest tax shield in the United States.

Solution

TCJA reduced the federal statutory corporate tax rate \(\tau_c\) from 35% to 21%, directly cutting the value of \(\tau_c D\). Additionally, Section 163(j) capped net interest deductions at 30% of adjusted taxable income (EBITDA, later EBIT), restricting interest deductibility for highly levered corporations.

Research

R1 · Graham (2000) tax shield valuation

How did Graham (2000) estimate the capitalized value of corporate tax shields across US corporations using simulation?

Solution

Graham simulated multi-year earnings paths accounting for non-debt tax shields, operating loss carryforwards, and the progressive tax schedule, finding that expected marginal tax benefits decline as debt increases. He concluded typical large firms leave money on the table by under-levering relative to static tax shield benefits.

R2 · Discount rate controversy for tax shields

Discuss the theoretical debate between discounting tax shields at the debt yield \(r_D\) versus the unlevered asset return \(r_U\).

Solution

Discounting at \(r_D\) (Modigliani-Miller) assumes permanent dollar debt \(D\), so interest deductions are as risky as debt cash flows. Discounting at \(r_U\) (Miles-Ezzell, Harris-Pringle) assumes debt is continuously adjusted to maintain target leverage \(D/V\), meaning future debt and tax shields fluctuate directly with enterprise asset value.

R3 · International tax arbitrage and debt placement

How do multinational corporations exploit cross-border differences in corporate tax rates through internal debt placement?

Solution

Multinationals issue intercompany debt from subsidiaries in low-tax jurisdictions to subsidiaries in high-tax jurisdictions. The high-tax entity deducts interest at a high rate \(\tau_{\text{high}}\), while the low-tax entity receives interest income taxed at \(\tau_{\text{low}}\), capturing a cross-border tax shield arbitrage of \((\tau_{\text{high}} - \tau_{\text{low}}) r_D D\).
