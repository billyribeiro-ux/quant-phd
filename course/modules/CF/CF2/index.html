Cost of Capital & Capital Structure

How should a firm finance its productive assets, and what discount rate properly reflects the blended opportunity cost of its capital? This module examines the foundational theories and empirical realities of corporate capital structure. You will compute the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) with market-value weights, propagate estimation uncertainties in beta, equity risk premium, and debt spreads via the delta method, derive the benchmark Modigliani–Miller propositions with and without corporate taxes, model the trade-off between tax shields and bankruptcy distress costs, contrast trade-off predictions with the pecking-order hierarchy and market timing, and track dynamic leverage target adjustment.

  • Prerequisites: CF1 Time Value & Capital Budgeting, AP2 Asset Pricing II (CAPM). Assumes closure concepts from M1, M4, S1.
  • Project: Cost-of-capital estimation under uncertainty (autograded engine plus rubric-graded case memo, 17 h)

63 h lessons · 17 h project (21.25% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CF2 you can:

  1. Compute the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) using market-value capital weights, CAPM equity cost, and after-tax cost of debt.
  2. Unlever and relever equity betas under corporate taxes and debt-beta zero assumptions, demonstrating the exact mathematical round trip.
  3. Propagate parameter estimation uncertainty in beta, equity risk premium, and debt spreads into WACC standard error using the delta method, and quantify the omitted second-order interaction term.
  4. Derive Modigliani–Miller Propositions I and II under perfect capital markets, and prove the invariance of enterprise value and WACC to leverage without taxes.
  5. Evaluate the present value of the interest tax shield under permanent debt, and derive the downward-sloping WACC curve under corporate taxes.
  6. Locate the optimal capital structure under the static trade-off theory balancing tax shields against expected financial distress costs across a discrete grid.
  7. Analyze corporate financing decisions under pecking-order hierarchy (internal funds to debt to equity) driven by adverse selection.
  8. Model leverage dynamics, measuring partial target adjustment speed and the half-life of leverage deviations.

CF2 builds directly on CF1 (Time Value & Capital Budgeting) and AP2 (Capital Asset Pricing Model), as well as foundation mathematical and statistical tools from the prerequisite closure. It relies upon these concepts without redefining them:

  • From S1: estimator, delta method, confidence interval, and bootstrap.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: six on prerequisite concepts from CF1, AP2, and S1, and four skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 when you meet the bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Capital Asset Pricing Model (AP2), what is the expected return of an equity with beta 1.20 when the risk-free rate is 4.0% and the equity risk premium is 5.0%?

    Options

    \(r_E = r_f + \beta \cdot \mathrm{ERP} = 4.0\% + 1.20 \times 5.0\% = 10.0\%\) (AP2, CAPM).

  2. A perpetual cash flow of 50 dollars per period grows at 2.0% annually and is discounted at 6.0%. What is its present value in dollars?

    Growing perpetuity formula: \(V = C_1 / (r - g) = 50 / (0.06 - 0.02) = 50 / 0.04 = 1{,}250\) dollars (M1, growing perpetuity).

  3. When computing weights for the weighted average cost of capital, which values must be used for debt and equity?

    Options

    Cost of capital measures current market opportunity cost, requiring market values of debt and equity (Section 1 of L01).

  4. In linear regression (OLS), what is the formula for the sample beta of asset returns \(r_i\) against market returns \(r_m\)?

    Options

    The regression slope beta is sample covariance divided by market sample variance.

  5. A firm issues 1,000 dollars of debt paying 8.0% interest. If the corporate tax rate is 30%, what is the annual interest tax shield in dollars?

    Annual interest payment is \(1{,}000 \times 0.08 = 80\). The tax shield is \(\tau_c \times \text{Interest} = 0.30 \times 80 = 24\) dollars. (Wait, check: \(\tau_c D = 0.30 \times 1{,}000 = 300\) dollars PV for permanent debt, while annual flow is 24 dollars. The prompt asks for PV of permanent debt shield: \(0.30 \times 1000 = 300\)).

  6. Under the delta method (S1), if \(Y = f(X)\) with \(f'(X_0) = a\), what is the first-order approximation for the variance of \(Y\)?

    Options

    The delta method linearisation gives \(\mathrm{Var}(f(X)) \approx [f'(E[X])]^2 \mathrm{Var}(X)\).

  7. Skill check (rung 1). A firm has equity beta \(\beta_L = 1.40\), debt of 50 million dollars, equity of 50 million dollars (\(D/E = 1.0\)), and corporate tax rate \(\tau_c = 25\%\). Assuming debt beta is zero, compute the unlevered asset beta \(\beta_U\) via the Hamada equation.

    \(\beta_U = \beta_L / [1 + (1 - \tau_c)(D/E)] = 1.40 / [1 + (1 - 0.25) \times 1.0] = 1.40 / 1.75 = 0.80\).

  8. Skill check (rung 2). A firm has 60 million dollars in equity with cost 10.0%, 40 million dollars in debt with pre-tax cost 6.0%, and corporate tax rate 25.0%. What is its WACC in percent?

    \(w_E = 0.60, w_D = 0.40\). After-tax debt cost \(= 6.0\% \times (1 - 0.25) = 4.5\%\). \(\mathrm{WACC} = 0.60 \times 10.0\% + 0.40 \times 4.5\% = 6.0\% + 1.8\% = 7.80\%\).

  9. According to Modigliani–Miller Proposition I without corporate taxes, how does firm enterprise value change with debt leverage?

    Options

    Under perfect capital markets without taxes, total firm value is determined by asset earnings power, not financing mix (L04).

  10. Under the pecking-order theory of Myers and Majluf (1984), which financing source is preferred first when funding new capital investments?

    Options

    Internal cash carries zero information asymmetry or adverse selection discount, making it the top of the financing waterfall (L07).

Lessons

Nine lessons of seven study hours each (63 study hours). Every lesson features numbered formal definitions and theorems with complete proofs, runnable inline Python labs, common misconceptions with evidence, four tiers of problem sets with worked solutions, a key-results summary table, and a spaced-retrieval diagnostic quiz.

L01 · 7 h The Cost of Capital: The WACC Identity Definition of WACC, market-value capital weights, CAPM equity cost, after-tax debt cost, and the exact weighted-average identity. L02 · 7 h Beta: Estimation, Unlevering, and Relevering OLS market regression, Bessel correction, Hamada unlevering formula under zero debt beta, and the exact round-trip transformation. L03 · 7 h Error in the Cost of Capital Delta-method propagation of beta, ERP, and spread standard errors into WACC uncertainty; exact second-order interaction terms. L04 · 7 h Modigliani–Miller Without Taxes Perfect capital market assumptions, arbitrage proof of Proposition I (value invariance), and Proposition II (linear equity cost). L05 · 7 h The Interest Tax Shield and MM With Corporate Taxes Perpetual debt interest tax deductibility, proof that PV(shield) = tau_c * D, levered valuation, and the declining WACC schedule. L06 · 7 h Financial Distress and the Trade-Off Theory Direct and indirect bankruptcy costs, the optimal-leverage curve (curriculum lab), and empirical tests of trade-off predictions. L07 · 7 h The Pecking-Order Theory Asymmetric information, financing hierarchy (cash to debt to equity), issue costs, and empirical tests of pecking order behavior. L08 · 7 h Market Timing in Capital Structure Equity issuance at high market-to-book ratios, debt issuance at low valuations, and the long-term persistence of timing decisions. L09 · 7 h Leverage Dynamics and Target Adjustment Partial adjustment models toward target leverage, adjustment speed lambda, geometric convergence, and half-life derivations.

The project ladder

The module concludes with an independent corporate finance project: Cost-of-Capital Estimation Under Uncertainty (17 study hours). Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the core pipeline skills tested in the placement diagnostic.

  1. Worked example

    Lab 1 on the project page: a complete, annotated estimation pipeline on a SIMULATED corporate issuer, computing beta unlevering, WACC, delta standard errors, and valuation bands in integer cents.

  2. Completion problem

    Lab 2 on the project page: a completion lab with blanks for the unlevering formula, WACC weighted average, delta-method covariance terms, and half-up rounded valuation endpoints.

  3. Independent project

    Build the full Python cost-of-capital estimation engine (wacc_engine.py) evaluated against the 24-property automated test suite across 50 seeds, and draft a professional investment committee memo evaluating valuation sensitivity under estimation risk. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    Implement non-parametric bootstrap standard errors for beta and ERP, compare with delta-method asymptotic standard errors, and analyze multi-firm industry beta unlevering portfolios.

SIMULATED / Bring-Your-Own-Data: Stock return series, equity risk premiums, credit spreads, and firm financial statements are labelled SIMULATED or provided in bring-your-own-data mode (validated in browser memory, never transmitted over the network). No private corporate data is embedded.
