Beta: Estimation, Unlevering, and Relevering

  • Uses: wacc, beta, variance, covariance matrix, estimator, consistent estimator
  • Introduces: levered and unlevered beta
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

An equity beta estimated from stock returns bundles two distinct risks: the fundamental business risk of the underlying operating assets and the financial risk created by debt obligations. Comparing operating risks across peer firms or evaluating a project at a new target leverage requires isolating asset risk from financial structure. Unlevering strips out debt leverage, producing pure asset beta, while relevering projects asset risk back onto any desired capital structure. This lesson derives the Hamada equations under corporate taxes, proves their exact inversion, and demonstrates the estimation pipeline.

1Market beta as a regression slope

Under the market model, excess returns of asset \(i\) are linked to market excess returns by linear regression:

\[ r_{i,t} - r_{f,t} = \alpha_i + \beta_i (r_{m,t} - r_{f,t}) + \varepsilon_{i,t}. \]

The population market beta is the ratio of covariance to market variance:

\[ \beta_i = \frac{\Cov(r_i, r_m)}{\Var(r_m)}. \]

Definition 2.1 (sample beta estimator)

Given \(N \ge 2\) return observations, the sample OLS estimator \(\hat{\beta}\) is

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat{\beta} &= \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N (r_{i,t} - \bar{r}_i)(r_{m,t} - \bar{r}_m)}{\sum_{t=1}^N (r_{m,t} - \bar{r}_m)^2} \\ &= \frac{S_{im}}{S_{mm}}, \end{aligned} \]

where \(S_{im} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{t=1}^N (r_{i,t} - \bar{r}_i)(r_{m,t} - \bar{r}_m)\) is sample covariance and \(S_{mm} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{t=1}^N (r_{m,t} - \bar{r}_m)^2\) is sample variance.

Remark 2.1 (Bessel correction cancellation)

Both sample covariance and sample variance use the Bessel degrees-of-freedom divisor \(N-1\). In the ratio \(S_{im}/S_{mm}\), the divisor \(N-1\) cancels algebraically, ensuring that doubling or replicating the sample leaves the estimated beta completely unchanged.

2Unlevering and relevering beta

Let \(\beta_L\) denote the observed levered equity beta. When a firm issues debt, fixed interest commitments amplify equity volatility and equity beta. To assess pure asset operating risk, we define levered and unlevered beta.

Theorem 2.1 (Hamada unlevering formula)

Assume riskless debt with debt beta \(\beta_D = 0\) (Assumption A7), permanent debt at constant market value \(D\) (Assumption A6), and corporate interest tax deductibility at rate \(\tau_c \in [0, 1)\) (Assumption A9). Then the unlevered asset beta \(\beta_U\) satisfies

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - \tau_c) \frac{D}{E}}, \]

and the relevered equity beta is its exact algebraic inverse:

\[ \beta_L = \beta_U \left[1 + (1 - \tau_c) \frac{D}{E}\right]. \]

Proof

Under corporate taxes with permanent debt, the total enterprise value is \(V_L = E + D = V_U + \tau_c D\), which implies \(E = V_U + \tau_c D - D = V_U - (1 - \tau_c) D\). Expressing this in terms of asset risk, the dollar cash flow to equity equals unlevered asset cash flow minus after-tax debt interest: \(CF_E = CF_U - (1 - \tau_c) CF_D\). Taking covariances with the market return on both sides yields

\[ \Cov(r_E, r_m) E = \Cov(r_U, r_m) V_U - (1 - \tau_c) \Cov(r_D, r_m) D. \]

Dividing by \(\Var(r_m)\) gives the beta relationship:

\[ \beta_L E = \beta_U V_U - (1 - \tau_c) \beta_D D. \]

Under Assumption A7, debt beta is zero (\(\beta_D = 0\)). Since \(V_U = E + (1 - \tau_c) D\), substitution gives

\[ \beta_L E = \beta_U \left[E + (1 - \tau_c) D\right]. \]

Dividing both sides by \(E\) gives \(\beta_L = \beta_U [1 + (1 - \tau_c) D/E]\). Dividing both sides by the bracketed term yields the unlevering relation \(\beta_U = \beta_L / [1 + (1 - \tau_c) D/E]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.2 (round-trip numerical verification)

A firm has levered equity beta \(\beta_L = 1.4156\), debt \(D = 40{,}000{,}000\) dollars, equity \(E = 60{,}000{,}000\) dollars (\(D/E = 2/3\)), and corporate tax rate \(\tau_c = 25\%\). The denominator factor is

\[ 1 + (1 - 0.25) \times \frac{40}{60} = 1 + 0.75 \times \frac{2}{3} = 1 + 0.50 = 1.50. \]

Unlevering yields

\[ \beta_U = \frac{1.4156}{1.50} = 0.943733\ldots \]

Relevering back onto the same capital structure yields

\[ \beta_L' = 0.943733\ldots \times 1.50 = 1.4156, \]

recovering the initial levered beta with exact zero discrepancy.

Lab 1 · Beta estimation, unlevering, and relevering

Estimates sample OLS beta on 60 periods of SIMULATED market and firm returns, computes unlevered asset beta under Hamada's formula, verifies round-trip relevering to 1e-14 tolerance, and tabulates relevered betas across a target D/E grid.

"""CF2 lab L02-1: Beta estimation, unlevering, and relevering.

SIMULATED: Firm and market returns are generated from a seeded normal distribution.
No real financial company data is used.
Demonstrates OLS beta estimation via sample covariance over sample variance,
unlevering equity beta to asset beta under the Hamada relationship, and the
exact mathematical inversion of the relevering round-trip.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from collections.abc import Sequence

SEED = 20261110


def simulate_returns(
    n_periods: int,
    true_beta: float,
    seed: int,
) -> tuple[list[float], list[float]]:
    """Generate SIMULATED monthly market and firm returns."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    market = [rng.gauss(0.008, 0.045) for _ in range(n_periods)]
    firm = [0.002 + true_beta * m + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.030) for m in market]
    return firm, market


def estimate_beta(firm: Sequence[float], market: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """Compute OLS beta as sample covariance over sample variance (with n - 1)."""
    n = len(firm)
    if n != len(market) or n < 2:
        raise ValueError("Return series must have identical length >= 2")
    mean_m = sum(market) / n
    mean_f = sum(firm) / n
    cov_fm = sum((f - mean_f) * (m - mean_m) for f, m in zip(firm, market, strict=True)) / (n - 1)
    var_m = sum((m - mean_m) ** 2 for m in market) / (n - 1)
    return cov_fm / var_m


def unlever(beta_l: float, de_ratio: float, tax_rate: float) -> float:
    """Hamada formula: beta_U = beta_L / [1 + (1 - tau_c) * (D/E)]."""
    return beta_l / (1.0 + (1.0 - tax_rate) * de_ratio)


def relever(beta_u: float, de_ratio: float, tax_rate: float) -> float:
    """Inverse Hamada: beta_L = beta_U * [1 + (1 - tau_c) * (D/E)]."""
    return beta_u * (1.0 + (1.0 - tax_rate) * de_ratio)


def main() -> None:
    print("CF2 Lab L02-1: Beta Estimation and Hamada Unlevering/Relevering")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------")

    true_b = 1.35
    n_obs = 60
    firm_ret, mkt_ret = simulate_returns(n_obs, true_b, SEED)

    b_est = estimate_beta(firm_ret, mkt_ret)
    print(f"SIMULATED 60-month sample: true beta = {true_b:.2f}")
    print(f"Estimated levered beta (beta_L): {b_est:.4f}")

    # Unlevering under stated capital structure
    debt = 40_000_000
    equity = 60_000_000
    de_ratio = debt / equity
    tax_rate = 0.25

    beta_u = unlever(b_est, de_ratio, tax_rate)
    beta_relevered = relever(beta_u, de_ratio, tax_rate)
    round_trip_diff = abs(beta_relevered - b_est)

    print(f"\nCapital structure: D = ${debt:,}, E = ${equity:,}, D/E = {de_ratio:.4f}")
    print(f"Corporate tax rate (tau_c): {tax_rate:.1%}")
    print(f"Unlevered asset beta (beta_U):  {beta_u:.4f}")
    print(f"Relevered equity beta:          {beta_relevered:.4f}")
    print(f"Round-trip difference:          {round_trip_diff:.2e}")
    print(f"Exact round-trip recovered:     {round_trip_diff < 1e-14}")

    print("\nRelevered equity beta across target D/E ratios:")
    for target_de in [0.0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0]:
        target_bl = relever(beta_u, target_de, tax_rate)
        print(f"  Target D/E = {target_de:4.2f}: Relevered beta_L = {target_bl:.4f}")


main()

3Where the canon breaks

The Hamada relationship assumes debt beta is zero (\(\beta_D = 0\)). For investment-grade corporate bonds with low default risk, empirical debt betas are small (typically 0.05 to 0.15). However, for high-yield issuers or distressed firms, debt claims absorb substantial systematic cash flow risk (\(\beta_D \in [0.20, 0.50]\)). Assuming \(\beta_D = 0\) for distressed debt overstates asset risk \(\beta_U\), incorrectly inflating project hurdle rates.

4Misconceptions

  • A firm with an equity beta of 1.50 is inherently riskier in its core business operations than a firm with an equity beta of 1.10.

    False. The first firm may operate low-risk assets with heavy debt leverage (e.g., \(\beta_U = 0.70\) levered to 1.50), while the second may operate high-risk assets with zero debt (\(\beta_U = 1.10\)). Operating risks can only be compared after unlevering.

  • Unlevering equity beta eliminates all risk from the investment analysis.

    False. Unlevering strips out financial risk caused by debt financing, leaving systematic asset operating risk \(\beta_U\) intact.

  • The corporate tax rate does not affect the translation between asset beta and equity beta.

    False. The interest tax deduction absorbs a portion \(\tau_c\) of debt service costs, muting the financial leverage magnification on common equity by the factor \((1 - \tau_c)\).

5Problems

Mechanics

M1 · Hand calculation of Hamada unlevering

A firm has equity beta \(\beta_L = 1.25\), debt of 30 million dollars, equity of 70 million dollars, and corporate tax rate 20.0%. Compute its unlevered asset beta to four decimal places.

Solution

\(D/E = 30/70 = 3/7\). The denominator is \(1 + (1 - 0.20) \times (3/7) = 1 + 0.80 \times 0.428571 = 1 + 0.342857 = 1.342857\). Then \(\beta_U = 1.25 / 1.342857 = 0.930851\ldots\), which rounds to 0.9309.

M2 · Relevering onto a new target structure

Using the unlevered asset beta from M1 (\(\beta_U = 0.9309\)), relever onto a target capital structure with \(D/E = 1.00\) at the same 20.0% tax rate.

Solution

Relevering formula: \(\beta_L = \beta_U [1 + (1 - \tau_c)(D/E)] = 0.930851 \times [1 + 0.80 \times 1.00] = 0.930851 \times 1.80 = 1.6755\).

M3 · Zero-debt boundary check

Show that when \(D = 0\), the unlevered beta and relevered beta both equal the observed equity beta.

Solution

When \(D = 0\), \(D/E = 0\). The term \(1 + (1 - \tau_c)(0) = 1\). Thus \(\beta_U = \beta_L / 1 = \beta_L\), and relevering gives \(\beta_L = \beta_U \times 1 = \beta_U = \beta_L\).

Proof

P1 · General unlevering with non-zero debt beta

Derive the generalized unlevering formula when debt beta \(\beta_D > 0\).

Solution

From Theorem 2.1 proof, \(\beta_L E = \beta_U V_U - (1 - \tau_c) \beta_D D\). Substitute \(V_U = E + (1 - \tau_c) D\): \(\beta_L E = \beta_U [E + (1 - \tau_c) D] - (1 - \tau_c) \beta_D D\). Dividing by \(E\) gives \(\beta_L = \beta_U [1 + (1 - \tau_c) D/E] - \beta_D (1 - \tau_c) D/E\). Rearranging gives \(\beta_U = \frac{\beta_L + (1 - \tau_c) \frac{D}{E} \beta_D}{1 + (1 - \tau_c) \frac{D}{E}}\). Setting \(\beta_D = 0\) recovers Hamada’s formula.

P2 · Monotonicity of relevered beta in leverage

Prove that for any \(\beta_U > 0\) and \(\tau_c < 1\), the relevered beta \(\beta_L\) is strictly increasing in the debt-to-equity ratio \(L = D/E\).

Solution

\(\beta_L(L) = \beta_U [1 + (1 - \tau_c) L]\). Differentiating with respect to \(L\) yields \(d\beta_L / dL = \beta_U (1 - \tau_c)\). Since \(\beta_U > 0\) and \(\tau_c < 1\), the product \(\beta_U (1 - \tau_c) > 0\). Thus \(\beta_L(L)\) is strictly increasing in \(L\).

P3 · Exact inversion identity

Prove that unlevering followed immediately by relevering with the same parameters forms an identity operator on the real numbers.

Solution

Let \(f(x) = x / K\) and \(g(y) = y \cdot K\) where \(K = 1 + (1 - \tau_c)(D/E)\). For any \(E > 0\) and \(\tau_c \in [0, 1)\), \(K > 0\). Then \(g(f(x)) = (x / K) \cdot K = x\) for all \(x \in \R\). Thus unlever and relever are exact mathematical inverses.

Empirical

E1 · Pure-play peer group analysis

To evaluate a chemical division project, an analyst identifies three pure-play public chemical firms with betas 1.10, 1.40, and 1.60, and D/E ratios 0.20, 0.50, and 0.80, with \(\tau_c = 25\%\). Find the unlevered beta for each and the mean asset beta.

Solution

Firm 1: \(\beta_{U,1} = 1.10 / [1 + 0.75 \times 0.20] = 1.10 / 1.15 = 0.9565\). Firm 2: \(\beta_{U,2} = 1.40 / [1 + 0.75 \times 0.50] = 1.40 / 1.375 = 1.0182\). Firm 3: \(\beta_{U,3} = 1.60 / [1 + 0.75 \times 0.80] = 1.60 / 1.60 = 1.0000\). The mean unlevered asset beta is \((0.9565 + 1.0182 + 1.0000) / 3 = 0.9916\).

E2 · Reading Lab 1 unlevering output

From Lab 1 output, what was the estimated equity beta, the unlevered asset beta, and the relevered beta at D/E = 1.50?

Solution

Lab 1 prints: Estimated equity beta \(\beta_L = 1.4156\), unlevered asset beta \(\beta_U = 0.9438\), and relevered beta at D/E = 1.50 of 2.0055.

E3 · Blume and Vasicek beta adjustments

Why do empirical practitioners often shrink historical OLS betas toward 1.0 prior to unlevering?

Solution

Historical regression betas suffer from sampling noise and mean reversion toward the cross-sectional mean of 1.0. Shrinkage estimators (such as Blume’s \(0.33 + 0.67\beta_{\text{OLS}}\) or Vasicek’s Bayesian weighting) mitigate out-of-sample forecast error.

Research

R1 · Miles-Ezzell vs Hamada unlevering

Contrast Hamada’s unlevering formula with the Miles-Ezzell formula. Under what corporate rebalancing policy is each derived?

Solution

Hamada assumes permanent debt at a constant dollar amount \(D\), with tax shields discounted at the debt rate \(r_D\). Miles and Ezzell assume a constant target debt-to-value ratio rebalanced periodically, meaning future tax shields fluctuate with enterprise value and are discounted at the unlevered cost of equity \(r_U\).

R2 · Endogenous debt beta

How does debt beta change as a firm approaches financial distress, and what bias does assuming \(\beta_D = 0\) introduce into asset beta estimation?

Solution

As default risk rises, debt behaves increasingly like equity, causing \(\beta_D\) to rise from near-zero to substantial positive values. Neglecting \(\beta_D\) attributes all market covariance to equity, causing the Hamada equation to overestimate unlevered asset beta \(\beta_U\).

R3 · Cash holdings as negative debt

Explain how substantial corporate cash balances affect the unlevering calculation when debt is measured as net debt (\(D - \mathrm{Cash}\)).

Solution

Cash is a risk-free asset with zero beta. Holding cash reduces net debt. If cash exceeds debt, net debt is negative, meaning equity beta is lower than asset beta. Failure to net out cash misidentifies a cash-rich company as having artificially low operating risk.
