Error in the Cost of Capital

  • Introduces: estimation error in the cost of capital

Corporate valuation models frequently treat WACC as a known constant. In reality, every input into WACC — equity beta, market risk premium, and debt spreads — is an estimated statistical quantity carrying substantial sampling uncertainty. Ignoring this estimation uncertainty creates an illusion of precision, causing corporate managers to make binary capital budgeting decisions on statistically indistinguishable projects. This lesson formalizes error propagation through the delta method, derives the exact analytical variance including the second-order product interaction term, and establishes statistical confidence bounds for enterprise hurdle rates.

1Sources of parameter uncertainty

Recall the WACC identity with capital weights \(w_E = E/V\) and \(w_D = D/V\):

\[ \begin{aligned} r_{\mathrm{WACC}} &= w_E (r_f + \beta_L \cdot \mathrm{ERP}) \\ &\quad + w_D (r_f + s)(1 - \tau_c). \end{aligned} \]

We treat weights \(w_E, w_D\) and the corporate tax rate \(\tau_c\) as known corporate balance sheet parameters (Assumption A8). The uncertain estimation parameters form the vector \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = (\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}, s)^T\). The sampling variance of these estimators introduces estimation error in the cost of capital.

2Delta-method error propagation

Theorem 3.1 (delta-method variance of WACC)

Let \(\hat{\beta}_L, \widehat{\mathrm{ERP}}\), and \(\hat{s}\) be consistent estimators of equity beta, equity risk premium, and credit spread, with asymptotic variances \(\Var(\beta_L), \Var(\mathrm{ERP}), \Var(s)\) and covariance \(\Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP})\). Assume spread estimation error is uncorrelated with equity market parameters. Then the first-order delta method linearised variance of WACC is

\[ \begin{aligned} \Var_{\Delta}(r_{\mathrm{WACC}}) &= w_E^2 \Bigl[ \mathrm{ERP}^2 \Var(\beta_L) \\ &\quad\quad + \beta_L^2 \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) \\ &\quad\quad + 2 \beta_L \mathrm{ERP} \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}) \Bigr] \\ &\quad + [w_D (1 - \tau_c)]^2 \Var(s). \end{aligned} \]

Proof

Define \(g(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}, s) = w_E(r_f + \beta_L \cdot \mathrm{ERP}) + w_D(r_f + s)(1 - \tau_c)\). The gradient vector \(\nabla g\) evaluated at parameter expectations is

\[ \nabla g = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial g}{\partial \beta_L} \\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial \mathrm{ERP}} \\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial s} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} w_E \cdot \mathrm{ERP} \\ w_E \cdot \beta_L \\ w_D (1 - \tau_c) \end{pmatrix}. \]

The parameter covariance matrix is

\[ \boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \begin{pmatrix} \Var(\beta_L) & \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}) & 0 \\ \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}) & \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \Var(s) \end{pmatrix}. \]

By the multivariate delta method, the linearised variance is \(\Var_{\Delta}(r_{\mathrm{WACC}}) = (\nabla g)^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} (\nabla g)\). Multiplying out the matrix quadratic form gives

\[ \begin{aligned} (\nabla g)^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} (\nabla g) &= (w_E \cdot \mathrm{ERP})^2 \Var(\beta_L) \\ &\quad + (w_E \cdot \beta_L)^2 \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) \\ &\quad + 2(w_E \cdot \mathrm{ERP})(w_E \cdot \beta_L) \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP}) \\ &\quad + [w_D (1 - \tau_c)]^2 \Var(s). \end{aligned} \]

Factoring \(w_E^2\) out of the first three terms yields the stated formula. \(\blacksquare\)

3The omitted second-order interaction term

Because the cost of equity involves the product of two random variables (\(\beta_L \cdot \mathrm{ERP}\)), the first-order Taylor expansion omits a second-order term. Under joint normality, this omitted term can be evaluated in exact closed form.

Theorem 3.2 (exact variance for product of bivariate normals)

Let \(X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_X, \sigma_X^2)\) and \(Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_Y, \sigma_Y^2)\) be jointly normal random variables with covariance \(c = \Cov(X, Y)\). Then the exact variance of their product is

\[ \begin{aligned} \Var(XY) &= \mu_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + \mu_Y^2 \sigma_X^2 + 2 \mu_X \mu_Y c \\ &\quad + \sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + c^2. \end{aligned} \]

Consequently, the exact variance of WACC exceeds the linearised delta-method variance by exactly

\[ \begin{aligned} \Var_{\text{exact}}(r_{\mathrm{WACC}}) - \Var_{\Delta}(r_{\mathrm{WACC}}) &= w_E^2 \Bigl[ \Var(\beta_L) \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) \\ &\quad\quad + \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP})^2 \Bigr]. \end{aligned} \]

Proof

By definition, \(\Var(XY) = \E[(XY)^2] - (\E[XY])^2\). For jointly normal variables, \(\E[XY] = \mu_X \mu_Y + c\). Expanding the second moment of centered variables \(x = X - \mu_X\) and \(y = Y - \mu_Y\): \(XY = (\mu_X + x)(\mu_Y + y) = \mu_X \mu_Y + \mu_X y + \mu_Y x + xy\). Squaring and taking expectations using the properties of bivariate normal higher moments (\(\E[x^2 y] = \E[x y^2] = 0\) by symmetry, and Isserlis’ theorem \(\E[x^2 y^2] = \sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + 2 c^2\)) yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \E[(XY)^2] &= (\mu_X \mu_Y + c)^2 + \mu_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + \mu_Y^2 \sigma_X^2 \\ &\quad + 2 \mu_X \mu_Y c + \sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + c^2. \end{aligned} \]

Subtracting \((\E[XY])^2 = (\mu_X \mu_Y + c)^2\) gives \(\Var(XY) = \mu_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + \mu_Y^2 \sigma_X^2 + 2 \mu_X \mu_Y c + \sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + c^2\). The first three terms equal the delta-method approximation for \(\Var(XY)\), leaving the residual \(\sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 + c^2\). Scaling by \(w_E^2\) yields the exact WACC gap. \(\blacksquare\)

Lab 1 · WACC estimation error propagation

Computes the delta-method linearised variance of WACC, benchmarks it against 20,000 seeded Monte Carlo draws, and verifies the exact analytical formula for the second-order gap.

"""CF2 lab L03-1: Estimation error in WACC, delta method, and second-order term.

SIMULATED: Beta, equity risk premium, and debt spreads are drawn from a seeded
multivariate distribution. No real market data is used.
Demonstrates the delta-method linearisation of WACC variance, compares it to
20,000 Monte Carlo draws, and verifies the exact analytical formula for the
omitted second-order interaction term.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

SEED = 20261110


def main() -> None:
    print("CF2 Lab L03-1: WACC Estimation Error Propagation (SIMULATED)")
    print("----------------------------------------------------------")

    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    n_sims = 20_000

    # Capital weights and tax rate (treated as fixed per Assumption A8)
    w_e = 0.60
    w_d = 0.40
    tau_c = 0.25
    r_f = 0.040

    # Parameter point estimates (means)
    mu_beta = 1.20
    mu_erp = 0.055
    mu_spread = 0.020

    # Parameter estimation uncertainties (variances and covariance)
    var_beta = 0.0400  # se(beta) = 0.20
    var_erp = 0.000400  # se(erp) = 0.020 (2.0%)
    cov_beta_erp = 0.001000  # positive covariance between beta and ERP estimates
    var_spread = 0.000049  # se(spread) = 0.007 (70 bps)

    # 1. Delta-method linearised variance
    # r_WACC = w_E * (r_f + beta * ERP) + w_D * (r_f + spread) * (1 - tau_c)
    # Gradient w.r.t [beta, ERP, spread]:
    # dWACC/dbeta = w_E * ERP, dWACC/dERP = w_E * beta, dWACC/dspread = w_D * (1 - tau_c)
    v_delta_equity = (w_e**2) * (
        (mu_erp**2) * var_beta + (mu_beta**2) * var_erp + 2.0 * mu_beta * mu_erp * cov_beta_erp
    )
    v_delta_debt = (w_d * (1.0 - tau_c)) ** 2 * var_spread
    v_delta = v_delta_equity + v_delta_debt
    se_delta = math.sqrt(v_delta)

    # 2. Exact theoretical moment for product of bivariate normals
    # Var(beta * ERP) = mu_beta^2 * var_erp + mu_erp^2 * var_beta + 2*mu_beta*mu_erp*cov
    #                   + var_beta * var_erp + cov^2
    second_order_term = (w_e**2) * (var_beta * var_erp + cov_beta_erp**2)
    v_exact = v_delta + second_order_term
    se_exact = math.sqrt(v_exact)

    # 3. Monte Carlo simulation (20,000 draws)
    # Cholesky factor for bivariate normal (beta, ERP)
    sigma_b = math.sqrt(var_beta)
    sigma_erp = math.sqrt(var_erp)
    rho = cov_beta_erp / (sigma_b * sigma_erp)
    sigma_spread = math.sqrt(var_spread)

    sim_wacc: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(n_sims):
        z1 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z2 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z3 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)

        b_sim = mu_beta + sigma_b * z1
        erp_sim = mu_erp + sigma_erp * (rho * z1 + math.sqrt(1.0 - rho**2) * z2)
        spread_sim = mu_spread + sigma_spread * z3

        re_sim = r_f + b_sim * erp_sim
        rd_sim = r_f + spread_sim
        w_sim = w_e * re_sim + w_d * rd_sim * (1.0 - tau_c)
        sim_wacc.append(w_sim)

    mc_mean = sum(sim_wacc) / n_sims
    mc_var = sum((w - mc_mean) ** 2 for w in sim_wacc) / (n_sims - 1)
    mc_se = math.sqrt(mc_var)

    print(f"Base WACC estimate: {w_e * (r_f + mu_beta * mu_erp) + w_d * (r_f + mu_spread) * (1 - tau_c):.4%}")
    print(f"\n1. Delta-method variance:   {v_delta:.8f} (SE = {se_delta:.4%})")
    print(f"2. Exact theoretical var:   {v_exact:.8f} (SE = {se_exact:.4%})")
    print(f"3. Monte Carlo (20k draws): {mc_var:.8f} (SE = {mc_se:.4%})")

    print(f"\nAnalytical second-order gap (v_exact - v_delta): {second_order_term:.8f}")
    print(f"Observed gap in theory:                          {v_exact - v_delta:.8f}")
    print(f"Monte Carlo vs. exact theory difference:         {abs(mc_var - v_exact):.8f}")
    mc_error_bound = 4.0 * math.sqrt(2.0 / n_sims) * v_exact
    print(f"Within 4 MC standard errors ({mc_error_bound:.8f}): {abs(mc_var - v_exact) <= mc_error_bound}")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

Textbook corporate finance routinely presents WACC as a single scalar point estimate (e.g., “the company’s WACC is 8.5%”). In practice, an equity beta estimated with a standard error of \(\pm 0.15\) and an equity risk premium with standard error of \(\pm 1.0\%\) imply a 95% confidence interval for WACC spanning several hundred basis points. Making capital expenditure decisions on point estimates ignores that project net present values may not be statistically distinguishable from zero.

5Misconceptions

  • A 95% confidence interval for WACC is always simply the point estimate plus or minus two delta-method standard errors without any distributional assumptions.

    False. WACC is a nonlinear function containing the product of beta and equity risk premium. The product of two normal random variables has heavy tails and is not normal; symmetric normal intervals are only asymptotic approximations.

  • Estimation error in the equity risk premium is negligible compared to firm-specific beta estimation uncertainty.

    False. Long-horizon equity risk premium estimators have standard errors of 1.0% to 2.0% (100 to 200 bps). Because ERP multiplies beta in every equity cost calculation, its uncertainty frequently contributes more to total WACC variance than beta uncertainty.

  • The first-order delta method always overstates parameter variance.

    False. As proven in Theorem 3.2, for the product term \(\beta \cdot \mathrm{ERP}\), the first-order delta method strictly understates variance by omitting the positive second-order term \(w_E^2 [\Var(\beta)\Var(\mathrm{ERP}) + \Cov^2]\).

6Problems

Mechanics

M1 · Delta method variance with uncorrelated inputs

A firm has \(w_E = 0.50, w_D = 0.50, \tau_c = 0.20\). Inputs: \(\beta = 1.0, \mathrm{ERP} = 0.06, \Var(\beta) = 0.04, \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) = 0.0001, \Var(s) = 0.000025\), and \(\Cov(\beta, \mathrm{ERP}) = 0\). Compute the delta-method variance and standard error of WACC.

Solution

Equity term: \(w_E^2 [\mathrm{ERP}^2 \Var(\beta) + \beta^2 \Var(\mathrm{ERP})] = 0.25 [0.06^2 \times 0.04 + 1.0^2 \times 0.0001] = 0.25 [0.000144 + 0.000100] = 0.25 \times 0.000244 = 0.000061\). Debt term: \([w_D(1 - \tau_c)]^2 \Var(s) = [0.50 \times 0.80]^2 \times 0.000025 = 0.16 \times 0.000025 = 0.000004\). Total variance \(= 0.000061 + 0.000004 = 0.000065\). Standard error \(\se = \sqrt{0.000065} \approx 0.00806\) (80.6 bps).

M2 · Magnitude of the second-order term

For the parameters in M1, compute the exact second-order interaction term omitted by the delta method, and express it as a percentage of delta variance.

Solution

Second-order term: \(w_E^2 [\Var(\beta)\Var(\mathrm{ERP}) + \Cov^2] = 0.25 [0.04 \times 0.0001 + 0] = 0.25 \times 0.000004 = 0.000001\). As a percentage of delta variance: \(0.000001 / 0.000065 \approx 1.54\%\). The exact variance is \(0.000066\) (81.2 bps SE).

M3 · Impact of beta-ERP covariance

Using M1 parameters, suppose \(\Cov(\beta, \mathrm{ERP}) = 0.0005\). Compute the revised delta-method variance and the increase in WACC standard error.

Solution

Covariance contribution to equity term: \(2 \beta \cdot \mathrm{ERP} \Cov = 2 \times 1.0 \times 0.06 \times 0.0005 = 0.000060\). Scaled by \(w_E^2 = 0.25\): \(0.25 \times 0.000060 = 0.000015\). Revised delta variance \(= 0.000065 + 0.000015 = 0.000080\). Revised standard error \(= \sqrt{0.000080} \approx 0.00894\) (89.4 bps), an increase of 8.8 bps.

Proof

P1 · Non-negativity of the second-order gap

Prove that for any bivariate distribution of \((\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP})\), the second-order correction term \(w_E^2 [\Var(\beta_L)\Var(\mathrm{ERP}) + \Cov(\beta_L, \mathrm{ERP})^2]\) is strictly non-negative.

Solution

Variances are non-negative: \(\Var(\beta_L) \ge 0\) and \(\Var(\mathrm{ERP}) \ge 0\). The squared covariance \(\Cov^2 \ge 0\). The squared weight \(w_E^2 \ge 0\). The sum of non-negative quantities is non-negative, and strictly positive whenever \(w_E > 0\) and either variance is positive.

P2 · Positive semi-definiteness condition

Prove that if \(\Var(\beta) \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) < \Cov(\beta, \mathrm{ERP})^2\), the parameter covariance matrix is not positive semi-definite.

Solution

The determinant of the \(2 \times 2\) covariance matrix of \((\beta, \mathrm{ERP})\) is \(\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \Var(\beta)\Var(\mathrm{ERP}) - \Cov(\beta, \mathrm{ERP})^2\). A valid covariance matrix must have \(\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) \ge 0\). If \(\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) < 0\), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is violated and the matrix possesses a negative eigenvalue, which is mathematically invalid.

P3 · Independence limit

Show that when \(\beta\) and \(\mathrm{ERP}\) are independent, \(\Var(\beta \cdot \mathrm{ERP})\) simplifies to \(\E[\beta]^2 \Var(\mathrm{ERP}) + \E[\mathrm{ERP}]^2 \Var(\beta) + \Var(\beta)\Var(\mathrm{ERP})\).

Solution

Under independence, \(\Cov = 0\) and \(\E[\beta^2 \mathrm{ERP}^2] = \E[\beta^2]\E[\mathrm{ERP}^2] = (\mu_\beta^2 + \sigma_\beta^2)(\mu_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2)\). Expanding gives \(\mu_\beta^2 \mu_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2 + \mu_\beta^2 \sigma_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2 + \mu_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2 \sigma_\beta^2 + \sigma_\beta^2 \sigma_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2\). Subtracting \((\E[\beta \cdot \mathrm{ERP}])^2 = \mu_\beta^2 \mu_{\mathrm{ERP}}^2\) yields the identity directly without requiring normality.

E2 · Monte Carlo error bound in Lab 1

Explain how the 4-standard-error bound in Lab 1 is constructed and why the observed difference falls within it.

Solution

The standard error of a sample variance for \(N = 20{,}000\) draws from a normal-like distribution is \(\sqrt{2/N} V = \sqrt{2/20000} \times 0.00030897 = 0.01 \times 0.00030897 \approx 0.00000309\). Four standard errors equal \(0.00001236\). The observed Monte Carlo discrepancy of 0.00000666 is well inside this 4-SE simulation confidence bound.

E3 · Bootstrap standard errors vs delta method

How does the non-parametric bootstrap provide an alternative method for estimating WACC standard errors when returns exhibit heavy tails?

Solution

The delta method relies on asymptotic normality and local linear Taylor approximations. The non-parametric bootstrap resamples empirical firm and market return pairs with replacement, re-estimating beta and WACC for each sample. It captures empirical fat tails, skewness, and finite-sample nonlinearities without assuming normal errors.

Research

R1 · Valuation impact of WACC uncertainty

For a firm with cash flow \(C_1 = 10\)M, growth \(g = 2\%\), and WACC \(8\% \pm 1\%\), calculate the valuation range and explain why the valuation distribution is right-skewed.

Solution

At low WACC (7%): \(V = 10 / (0.07 - 0.02) = 10 / 0.05 = 200\)M. At mid WACC (8%): \(V = 10 / (0.08 - 0.02) = 10 / 0.06 = 166.7\)M. At high WACC (9%): \(V = 10 / (0.09 - 0.02) = 10 / 0.07 = 142.9\)M. The upside (\(+33.3\)M) is larger than the downside (\(-23.8\)M) because \(V(r) = C/(r-g)\) is strictly convex in \(r\) (\(d^2V/dr^2 = 2C/(r-g)^3 > 0\)), introducing positive Jensen’s inequality skewness.

R2 · Endogenous leverage feedback

Why is treating market weights \(w_E, w_D\) as fixed (Assumption A8) an approximation when firm equity value fluctuates with the discount rate?

Solution

If WACC rises, equity market value falls while debt market value falls less (or stays constant). This raises debt weight \(w_D\) and lowers equity weight \(w_E\), which feeds back into WACC. Fully endogenous error propagation requires simultaneous implicit differentiation of the enterprise value equilibrium.

R3 · Parameter uncertainty vs cash flow uncertainty

In project valuation, how does discount rate estimation risk interact with operational cash flow forecast risk?

Solution

Valuation uncertainty is driven jointly by cash flow variance and discount rate variance. If errors in cash flow expectations are correlated with macroeconomic parameters (e.g., lower cash flows occur when risk premiums spike), neglecting their joint covariance severely understates enterprise downside tail risk.
