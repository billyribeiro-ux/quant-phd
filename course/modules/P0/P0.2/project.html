Personal Study Plan

Doctoral completion in quantitative finance requires sustained execution across several thousand hours of rigorous coursework. Without a dated roadmap incorporating diagnostic test-out exemptions, prerequisite directed acyclic graph dependencies, and structured spaced retrieval review schedules, even brilliant students fall victim to cognitive overload or prerequisite decay. You will construct a complete, validated personal study plan: exporting a machine-readable plan.json artifact and writing a defensible half-page rationale evaluated by rubric against five observable criteria.

The ladder

The four-rung ladder transitions you from guided worked examples to independent schedule construction. If you passed the hub placement diagnostic (scoring at least 80 percent and answering both skill checks correctly), you may skip directly to rung 3.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved study plan calculation for a simulated incoming researcher, detailing track selection, diagnostic test-out deductions, weekly study hour constraints, JSON schema formatting, and annotated rationale.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    A structured schedule completion challenge testing net study hour calculations, milestone week projections, and FSRS review date intervals. Graded instantly with diagnostic feedback.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    An independent personal study plan deliverable: exporting your personalized plan.json data artifact and drafting a half-page rationale evaluated by a five-criterion rubric with model exemplars.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    An open-ended dynamic schedule audit incorporating stochastic diagnostic exam outcomes, simulated weekly study hour shocks, and automated FSRS review deck rebalancing under shifting time constraints.

Specification

Context
Doctoral quantitative finance candidates balance intense research, empirical coding, and continuous-time mathematics across multi-year trajectories. Inadequate planning produces high attrition when prerequisite dependencies fail or when un-reviewed foundational knowledge decays. Researchers must establish an auditable, dated study schedule that optimizes time allocation, enforces prerequisite closures, and embeds spaced retrieval directly into the daily workflow.
Inputs
The primary inputs are: (1) Your individual diagnostic placement test results across foundational modules; (2) Target track module hours and milestone targets from the curriculum registry (e.g. Quantitative Researcher track: 4,086 module hours across 40 modules; Risk & Financial Stability: 2,946 hours across 31 modules); (3) Your declared available weekly study hours (e.g. 15 to 25 hours per week); and (4) The FSRS-4.5 spaced retrieval parameters (Ye, Su & Cao 2022) for scheduling active review sessions.
Required outputs
Two coordinated deliverables: (1) An exported machine-readable JSON file (plan.json) conforming strictly to the program study plan schema (containing student_id, track, total_track_hours, placement_exemptions, net_study_hours, weekly_hours, projected_weeks, milestones, and review_cadence); and (2) A concise, structured half-page rationale document (rationale.md, maximum 400 words) defending track feasibility, test-out integration, and cognitive review cadence.
Constraints
The written rationale must not exceed 400 words of prose. Track hour totals must be quoted exactly from curriculum records. All test-out exemptions must satisfy prerequisite DAG topological sequencing (no module may be exempted unless all its antecedents are satisfied or also exempted). Cognitive science justifications must cite verified registry keys (roediger2006test, cepeda2006distributed, rohrer2007shuffling, kalyuga2003expertise, ye2022stochastic) with valid evidence grades.
Grading
This project is evaluated via a formal rubric comprising five criteria across four observable performance levels. Passing requires achieving Level 3 (Proficient) or Level 4 (Exemplary) on every criterion, calibrated against the two provided exemplars.

2. Annotated Half-Page Rationale (rationale.md)

This plan charts a rigorous, mathematically sequenced trajectory through the Quantitative Researcher track (4,086 curriculum hours). Diagnostic placement testing grants 104 hours of test-out exemptions across P0.1 (16 h), M1 (48 h), and CS1 (40 h), having cleared the 80% mastery threshold without violating DAG prerequisite closures. By bypassing introductory material where mental schemas already exist, this plan avoids the expertise-reversal effect, eliminating redundant extraneous cognitive load (Tetzlaff et al. 2025).

The resulting net requirement of 3,982 study hours is scheduled across 20 hours per week over 50 calendar weeks annually, yielding a feasible completion horizon of 199.1 study weeks (3.98 calendar years). Milestones track sequential capstone targets from initial statistical testing (S0, S7) to algorithmic backtesting (QR2) and replication defense (CAP1).

To prevent the rapid forgetting that follows cramming (Cepeda et al. 2006), 20 minutes of daily study time are reserved for active retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013). Review intervals are managed dynamically by the FSRS-4.5 scheduler (Ye, Su & Cao 2022) targeting 90% retrievability (\(R = 0.90\)), with review cards shuffled across topics to train discriminative problem diagnosis through interleaved practice (Rohrer & Taylor 2007).

Rung 2 · Completion problem

Complete the schedule calculation and review cadence check below. Verify each answer to confirm your quantitative planning mastery before constructing your independent personal study plan.

  1. A student enrolls in the Derivatives & Volatility track (4,376 curriculum hours). Through diagnostic placement testing, the student tests out of foundational modules totaling 376 hours. If the student commits 25 hours per week across 50 weeks per year, what is the projected completion timeline?

    Options

    Net study hours equal total hours minus exemptions: \(4{,}376 - 376 = 4{,}000\text{ hours}\). At 25 hours per week, completion requires \(4{,}000 / 25 = 160\text{ weeks}\). At 50 weeks per year, this equals \(160 / 50 = 3.20\text{ calendar years}\).

  2. Under the FSRS-4.5 optimal interval formula \(I(S, r) = \frac{81 S}{19}(r^{-2} - 1)\), what is the scheduled review interval for a card with stability \(S = 19.0\text{ days}\) when the student targets \(r = 0.90\) retention?

    Options

    By Theorem 4.2, \(I(S, 0.90) = S\) exactly. For \(S = 19.0\text{ days}\), the interval to maintain 90% retention is exactly 19.0 days.

  3. Why must daily spaced retrieval cards be presented in shuffled (interleaved) order rather than grouped by course module?

    Options

    Rohrer & Taylor (2007) showed that interleaving trains discriminative facility—the capacity to recognize problem types and select the correct formula—substantially outperforming blocked practice on delayed tests.

Rung 3 · Rubric

The personal study plan and rationale are evaluated against five criteria with four observable performance levels each. Level 3 (Proficient) or Level 4 (Exemplary) on all criteria is required to pass.

Personal study plan rubric: five criteria, four observable levels each.
Criterion 4 · Exemplary 3 · Proficient 2 · Developing 1 · Beginning
C1 · Dated Schedule & Milestone Feasibility Every week's study hours sum exactly to the declared weekly commitment. Projected completion weeks cover all net required hours. Milestone targets include calendar dates or week numbers checkable against DAG sequence. Schedule covers required track hours and states weekly commitment, but minor arithmetic discrepancy exists (under 5%) or milestone dates are unspecified. Timeline fails to account for total track hours or assumes an unrealistic weekly study load (e.g. > 40 h/week alongside full-time work) without justification. No dated schedule or weekly hour allocation provided; timeline is arbitrary or uncomputable.
C2 · Prerequisite DAG & Placement Integration Explicitly lists all diagnostic placement exemptions with test scores clearing the 80% threshold. Verifies that all exempted modules satisfy prerequisite DAG closure without circularities or orphaned dependencies. Lists placement exemptions with scores, but does not explicitly verify transitive prerequisite closure against the curriculum DAG. Exempts modules without diagnostic test scores, or exempts advanced modules while failing to clear foundational prerequisites. Ignores placement results entirely, or claims exemptions that violate the fundamental curriculum prerequisite structure.
C3 · Review Cadence & Spacing Model Specifies an explicit daily or weekly review time allocation. Implements FSRS-4.5 retrievability dynamics with target retention \(R \ge 0.85\). Explicitly mandates interleaved card presentation across active modules. Allocates time for review and mentions spaced repetition, but omits mathematical retention target or fails to mandate interleaved card presentation. Relies on massed review (e.g. cramming before capstone milestones) or specifies fixed linear intervals without adaptive stability adjustments. Omits review schedule entirely; treats learning as one-time completion without retention maintenance.
C4 · JSON Schema Conformance & Articulation Exported plan.json strictly validates against all schema properties (types, required fields, arithmetic consistency between gross, exempt, and net hours). All data articulates perfectly with written rationale. Valid JSON file provided with all required fields, but minor typographical discrepancy between JSON values and rationale text. JSON file has missing required fields, invalid types, or substantial arithmetic discrepancies with the written plan. No valid JSON artifact submitted, or JSON fails basic syntax parsing.
C5 · Evidence Citations & Empirical Grounding Every cognitive claim in the rationale carries an evidence grade and verified citation key from the registry with exact locators. Cites counter-evidence or scope boundaries where appropriate. Cites verified registry keys for major claims with grades, but omits specific page or table locators. Cites sources without evidence grades, cites unverified keys not in the registry, or makes unsubstantiated claims. No citations provided; assertions based entirely on unsubstantiated opinion.

Rationale Document (rationale.md)

This study plan establishes a realistic, mathematically consistent completion path for the Risk & Financial Stability track (2,946 gross hours across 31 modules). Based on diagnostic placement results, exemptions are established for P0.1 (16 h, score 95%) and CS1 (40 h, score 90%), clearing the 80% mastery threshold (Kulik et al. 1990) without prerequisite violations. Bypassing introductory Python programming eliminates redundant extraneous cognitive load (Kalyuga et al. 2003; Tetzlaff et al. 2025).

The resulting net workload of 2,890 hours is budgeted at 15 hours per week over 50 calendar weeks annually, producing an achievable completion schedule of 192.7 study weeks (3.85 calendar years). Milestone gates track prerequisite progression from core exploratory statistics (S0) to portfolio risk measurement (PR4) and macroprudential regulation (SR3, BK3).

To ensure long-term retention of continuous-time risk estimators, 15 minutes of each study session are reserved for active retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013). Reviews are scheduled via the FSRS-4.5 algorithm (Ye, Su & Cao 2022) targeting 90% retrievability (\(R = 0.90\)), with review prompts shuffled across active modules to maintain discriminative facility through interleaved practice (Rohrer & Taylor 2007).

Evaluation Annotation: Meets Level 4 on all criteria. C1: Exact arithmetic (\(2{,}890 / 15 = 192.7\text{ weeks}\)), realistic weekly allocation (15 h/wk), clear milestones. C2: Explicit test scores (95%, 90%) clearing the 80% threshold, strictly valid DAG exemptions. C3: FSRS-4.5 model with \(R = 0.90\), 15 min/day dedicated budget, explicit interleaving rule. C4: Perfectly valid JSON schema articulating 1:1 with prose. C5: Every claim cites verified registry keys with exact locators and grades.
