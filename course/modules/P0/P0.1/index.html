How to Study This Program

Every quantitative claim in this program carries an evidence grade, a verified citation, or a formal mathematical proof. This orientation establishes the methodological foundation of your doctoral major: how to evaluate evidence on the six-tier scale, navigate the prerequisite directed acyclic graph, deconstruct research papers into their structural anatomy, separate causal identification from confounded correlation, and complete projects across the four-rung ladder.

  • Prerequisites: None (foundational entry point)
  • Unlocks: P0.2 Diagnostics, Mastery Gating & Spaced Retrieval, and the doctoral curriculum
  • Project: Paper teardown (rubric-graded against six criteria)

12 h lessons · 4 h project (25.0%)

Objectives

By the end of P0.1 you can:

  1. Classify quantitative claims into the program's six evidence grades (T, A, B, C, D, X) and verify citations against the cryptographic registry.
  2. Traverse the curriculum prerequisite directed acyclic graph to compute topological study sequences and transitive antecedent closures.
  3. Steelman competing financial and economic hypotheses before evaluating model specifications and capital allocation decisions.
  4. Deconstruct published empirical research into the canonical five-part paper anatomy: question, identification, data, inference, and robustness.
  5. Distinguish credible causal identification from omitted variable bias and spurious statistical correlation in financial econometric models.
  6. Execute the four-rung project ladder (worked example, completion problem, independent teardown, stretch audit) conforming to Atlas engineering contracts.

Prerequisites

P0.1 has no formal program prerequisites. As the inaugural unit of the doctoral-track major, it assumes undergraduate mathematical literacy (elementary calculus, basic probability distributions, matrix algebra notation) and basic programming familiarity in Python. All specialized financial concepts, econometric identification frameworks, and program engineering tools are introduced directly within the module.

Placement diagnostic

Five diagnostic items: three assessing fundamental methodological readiness and two skill checks assessing the exact competencies taught in project rungs 1 and 2. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (four out of five items correct). Students meeting the mastery threshold and answering both skill checks correctly may skip directly to project rung 3.

  1. In a directed acyclic graph (DAG) representing course prerequisites, what mathematical condition guarantees that a valid topological study sequence exists?

    Options

    A directed graph has a topological sort if and only if it is acyclic. A finite DAG always contains at least one source vertex (in-degree zero) from which traversal can begin.

  2. Under the program's evidence scale, what is the key distinction between Grade A and Grade B empirical findings?

    Options

    Per the program standard, Grade A requires independent out-of-sample replication across multiple samples or periods. Grade B denotes peer-reviewed top-tier publication that has not yet achieved conclusive replication or faces active published counter-evidence.

  3. What does the principle of steelmanning require when analyzing an opposing economic argument?

    Options

    Steelmanning requires constructing the strongest possible formulation of a competing thesis, identifying the core mechanism and best supporting data, before assessing whether empirical evidence refutes it.

  4. Skill check (rung 1). An empirical working paper published on SSRN documents a novel cross-sectional return predictor using a single sample of US equities from 2010 to 2020. The finding has not undergone peer review, nor has it been independently replicated. On the program's evidence scale, what grade must this claim receive?

    Options

    Unpublished working papers, practitioner white papers, and single-sample unreplicated empirical studies receive Grade C. Grade B requires peer review, and Grade A requires independent out-of-sample replication.

  5. Skill check (rung 2). In Ball and Brown (1968), what specific source of variation provides causal identification for evaluating the information content of accounting earnings?

    Options

    Ball and Brown (1968) achieved identification by aligning stock returns in event time relative to the specific month of the annual earnings announcement, separating pre-announcement anticipation from announcement-month impact.

Lessons

Two lessons of six study hours each. Each lesson provides rigorous theoretical exposition, runnable Python labs, canon-break analyses, misconception teardowns, tiered problem sets with full worked solutions, and an active retrieval quiz.

L01 · 6 h Evidence Grades, the Registry, and Program Conventions The six-tier evidence standard (T/A/B/C/D/X), verified citation mechanics, registered notation conventions, topological DAG traversal, and the discipline of steelmanning. L02 · 6 h Paper Anatomy, Identification, and the Project Ladder The canonical five-part paper structure, econometric identification vs omitted variable bias, sensitivity testing, independent replication, and the four-rung project ladder.

The ladder

All program modules structure hands-on work through a progressive four-rung ladder designed to transition students from guided comprehension to independent research execution.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully deconstructed and annotated teardown of Ball & Brown (1968), detailing question, mechanism, data locators, identification strategy, weakest assumption, and evidence grade.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    A structured teardown template with identification strategy and weakest assumption fields blanked. Graded instantly against precision rubrics.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    An independent one-page structured teardown of Banz (1981), evaluated against a six-criterion, four-level rubric with annotated exemplars. See project specification.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    An open-ended evidence audit of a contested empirical paper from asset pricing, corporate finance, or market microstructure, defending a formal evidence grade.
