Evidence Grades, the Registry, and Program Conventions

  • Introduces: evidence-grade, citation-registry, notation-conventions, prerequisite-dag, steelmanning

  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study
  • Dlore

Quantitative finance is contaminated by irreproducible backtests, p-hacked anomaly discoveries, and unverified lore passed off as institutional wisdom. In this program, no assertion is accepted without mathematical proof, independent out-of-sample replication, or transparent methodological deconstruction. This lesson establishes the operational ground rules: the six-tier evidence hierarchy, the verified citation registry, standardized mathematical notation, prerequisite graph navigation, and the discipline of steelmanning. Mastery of these foundations ensures that every paper you read, model you write, and strategy you deploy rests on verifiable empirical ground.

1The six-tier evidence standard

Financial literature frequently treats published peer review as an infallible stamp of truth. Yet hundreds of claimed equity anomalies fail out-of-sample replication, and commercial trading desks regularly discard published econometric models that collapse under real-world transaction costs. To maintain uncompromising intellectual rigor, every substantive claim in this program carries an explicit grade from a non-negotiable six-tier scale.

Definition 1.1 (evidence grade)

An evidence grade is an epistemological classification assigned to a theoretical, empirical, or methodological claim according to its replicability, mathematical completeness, and sample provenance. The program enforces exactly six grades:

  • Grade T (Theorem): A formal mathematical proposition proved completely on the page or accompanied by an exact locator in an established mathematical source. Requires explicit assumptions and deductively valid proof steps.
  • Grade A (Replicated empirical finding): An empirical finding that has been replicated out of sample by independent research teams across distinct time periods, asset classes, or international markets.
  • Grade B (Peer-reviewed, unsettled): An empirical finding published in a leading peer-reviewed journal that has not yet undergone comprehensive independent multi-sample replication, or that faces active published counter-evidence.
  • Grade C (Single-sample or working paper): A finding established in an unpublished working paper, practitioner research note, or single-sample study without independent confirmation.
  • Grade D (Practitioner lore): Unsubstantiated market folklore, rule-of-thumb heuristics, or qualitative trading claims lacking systematic empirical documentation. Allowed only when explicitly identified as unverified market beliefs.
  • Grade X (Refuted or retracted): A published claim that has failed out-of-sample replication, was discredited by corrected data or flawed econometrics, or has been formally retracted by its authors.
Remark 1.2 (allowed prose phrasing per grade)

The program strictly restricts prose phrasing based on the assigned evidence grade. A Grade T result is introduced with "Theorem" or "It follows that". A Grade A finding is framed with "The evidence shows". A Grade B finding requires balanced two-sided phrasing: "Published evidence indicates [claim]; contested by [counter-claim]". A Grade C finding is restricted to "One study finds [claim]". Grade D may appear only as "It is widely claimed that [claim]", and Grade X is stated as "Earlier work claimed [claim]; this did not survive independent replication".

Proposition 1.3 (evidence monotonicity)

Let evidence space be ordered by evidentiary reliability: \(X \prec D \prec C \prec B \prec A\), with \(T\) denoting deductive mathematical certainty. An empirical claim can move up this ordering only through independent out-of-sample validation across distinct data regimes. An empirical claim downgrades to \(X\) upon a verified failure to replicate under the original specification.

2The verified citation registry

Academic literature routinely suffers from phantom citations, misattributed conclusions, and incorrect page locators. In this program, free-form text citations are strictly prohibited. Every cited work must exist in the cryptographic citation registry.

Definition 2.1 (citation registry)

The citation registry is an immutable bibliographic ledger mapping unique alphanumeric keys to cryptographic source records verified against Crossref digital object identifiers (DOIs), Open Library ISBNs, or official publisher repository records. Every citation in program markup must reference a valid registry key using the attribute data-cite, accompanied by a specific data-locator (table, panel, equation, or page) for every quoted quantitative claim.

Example 2.2 (verifying registry provenance)

Consider the learning methodology analysis of Dunlosky et al. (2013). In this curriculum, the reference is cited via registry key dunlosky2013improving. The registry record resolves to DOI 10.1177/1529100612453266, published in Psychological Science in the Public Interest, volume 14, issue 1, pages 4–58. The quoted finding that practice testing and distributed practice demonstrate high educational utility is traceable directly to Table 1 on page 6.

Remark 2.3 (prohibition of unsourced numbers)

No substantive quantitative figure may appear in program text without meeting one of three criteria: (1) computed live on the page from embedded datasets or reproducible algorithms, (2) derived via an explicit mathematical calculation shown on the page, or (3) quoted directly from a registered citation with an exact data-locator. Vague modifiers such as "roughly half" or "about six percent" are prohibited unless accompanied by verifiable computation.

3Program notation conventions

Financial economics frequently suffers from conflicting notation: \(\beta\) may denote a regression coefficient, a CAPM systemic risk exposure, a consumption discount factor, or a bankruptcy probability depending on the author. This program enforces strict global notation conventions registered across all modules.

Definition 3.1 (notation conventions)

The program's notation conventions are binding mathematical typographic standards registered in the program notation catalog. Mathematics is rendered via KaTeX using strictly authorized macros. A registered program symbol possesses a unique, invariant operational meaning across all course modules:

  • \(\E[X]\) denotes the mathematical expectation operator under the physical probability measure \(\Prob\), rendered via \E.
  • \(\Prob(A)\) denotes the probability measure of event \(A\), rendered via \Prob.
  • \(\Var(X)\) and \(\Cov(X, Y)\) denote variance and covariance, rendered via \Var and \Cov.
  • \(\Corr(X, Y)\) denotes Pearson correlation, rendered via \Corr.
  • \(\indic_{\{A\}}\) denotes the indicator function of event \(A\), taking value 1 if \(A\) occurs and 0 otherwise, rendered via \indic.
  • \(\R\) and \(\N\) denote the real numbers and natural numbers, rendered via \R and \N.
  • \(\Normal(\mu, \sigma^2)\) denotes the univariate normal distribution with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), rendered via \Normal.
  • \(X \iid \mathcal{D}\) denotes independent and identically distributed random variables, rendered via \iid.
  • \(\dto\) and \(\pto\) denote convergence in distribution and convergence in probability, rendered via \dto and \pto.
  • \(\SR\) and \(\hSR\) denote population and sample Sharpe ratios, rendered via \SR and \hSR.
Example 3.2 (expressing portfolio excess return moments)

Using authorized program notation, the excess return of asset \(i\) over the risk-free rate is denoted \(R_i - R_f\). Its expected excess return is \(\E[R_i - R_f]\), its variance is \(\Var(R_i)\), and its population Sharpe ratio per period is \(\SR_i = \frac{\E[R_i - R_f]}{\sqrt{\Var(R_i)}}\). In sample data with \(T\) observations, the sample Sharpe ratio is denoted \(\hSR_i = \frac{\bar{R}_i - \bar{R}_f}{\hat{\sigma}_i}\).

4The prerequisite DAG and curriculum topology

The doctoral curriculum is structured as a formal directed acyclic graph (DAG). Concepts, tools, and econometric methods build sequentially. No module introduces a concept before its prerequisite foundation has been rigorously established.

Definition 4.1 (prerequisite DAG)

The prerequisite DAG is a finite directed graph \(G = (V, E)\) where each vertex \(v \in V\) represents a curriculum module, and each directed edge \((u, v) \in E\) indicates that module \(u\) is a direct prerequisite of module \(v\). The graph satisfies the acyclicity condition: there exists no sequence of directed edges \((v_0, v_1), (v_1, v_2), \dots, (v_{k-1}, v_k)\) such that \(v_0 = v_k\) for \(k \ge 1\).

Theorem 4.2 (topological ordering of the curriculum)

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be a finite prerequisite DAG with \(|V| = n\). Then there exists at least one linear ordering of vertices \(\pi = (v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n)\) such that for every directed edge \((u, v) \in E\), vertex \(u\) precedes vertex \(v\) in \(\pi\): \(\pi^{-1}(u) < \pi^{-1}(v)\).

Proof

We proceed by mathematical induction on the number of vertices \(n\). For the base case \(n = 1\), the single vertex constitutes a trivial topological ordering. Assume the induction hypothesis holds for all DAGs with fewer than \(n\) vertices.

We first show that any finite DAG contains at least one source vertex (in-degree zero). Suppose for contradiction that every vertex \(v \in V\) has in-degree \(\operatorname{deg}^-(v) \ge 1\). Pick an arbitrary vertex \(w_0 \in V\). Since \(\operatorname{deg}^-(w_0) \ge 1\), there exists a predecessor \(w_1\) with \((w_1, w_0) \in E\). Continuing iteratively, we construct a sequence \(w_0, w_1, w_2, \dots\) where \((w_{i+1}, w_i) \in E\). Because \(V\) is finite with \(|V| = n\), by the Pigeonhole Principle there must exist indices \(j < k\) such that \(w_j = w_k\). The sub-sequence \((w_k, w_{k-1}), \dots, (w_{j+1}, w_j)\) forms a directed cycle, contradicting the acyclicity of \(G\). Hence, there exists at least one vertex \(u^\ast \in V\) with in-degree zero.

Place \(u^\ast\) first in the ordering: \(\pi(1) = u^\ast\). Consider the induced subgraph \(G' = G \setminus \{u^\ast\}\), which has \(n - 1\) vertices. Since removing a vertex cannot introduce cycles, \(G'\) is also a DAG. By the induction hypothesis, \(G'\) admits a valid topological ordering \((v_2, \dots, v_n)\). Prepending \(u^\ast\) yields \(\pi = (u^\ast, v_2, \dots, v_n)\). For any directed edge \((u^\ast, v) \in E\), \(u^\ast\) precedes \(v\) because \(u^\ast\) is at position 1. Any edge within \(G'\) satisfies the precedence condition by the induction hypothesis. Thus \(\pi\) is a valid topological ordering. \(\blacksquare\)

Definition 4.3 (prerequisite closure and unlock set)

For any module \(v \in V\), the prerequisite closure \(\operatorname{Cl}^-(v)\) is the set of all antecedent modules reachable by traversing directed edges backward from \(v\):

\[ \operatorname{Cl}^-(v) = \{u \in V \setminus \{v\} \mid \text{there exists a directed path from } u \text{ to } v \text{ in } G\}. \]

The unlock set \(\operatorname{Unlock}(u)\) is the set of all downstream modules reachable by traversing directed edges forward from \(u\):

\[ \operatorname{Unlock}(u) = \{v \in V \setminus \{u\} \mid \text{there exists a directed path from } u \text{ to } v \text{ in } G\}. \]

5Steelmanning and intellectual rigor

A fatal flaw in financial research is strawmanning: caricaturing an opposing model or trading philosophy to score superficial points in a paper or pitch deck. Discretionary fundamental analysts often strawman quantitative models as blind curve-fitting, while quantitative modelers strawman fundamental analysis as unscientific storytelling. The standard of this program is steelmanning.

Definition 5.1 (steelmanning)

Steelmanning is the epistemic practice of formulating the most persuasive, coherent, mathematically sound, and empirically supported version of an opposing argument, theory, or model before critiquing it or testing competing hypotheses. A researcher has successfully steelmanned an opponent's position only when the opponent agrees that the formulation captures the strongest version of their thesis.

Example 5.2 (steelmanning market efficiency vs behavioural asset pricing)

Consider the debate over price momentum. A strawman critique from an efficient-markets proponent claims: "Momentum is mere data mining that disappears when looking at different stocks." A steelmanned critique acknowledges: "Cross-sectional momentum exhibits statistically robust out-of-sample abnormal returns across dozens of international equity markets and multi-century asset classes. However, momentum returns suffer from severe crash risk during market rebounds, high portfolio turnover, and significant execution drag from transaction costs in small-cap constituents that severely erode net alpha."

Remark 5.3 (refereeing and adversarial evaluation)

Berk, Harvey, and Hirshleifer (2017) argue that effective peer review and intellectual evaluation require evaluating whether an empirical contribution survives under its strongest specification rather than rejecting papers over superficial presentation issues. In quantitative finance, steelmanning competing market hypotheses prevents capital allocation to spurious anomalies that exploit fragile test designs.

Lab 1 · Navigating an Inline Prerequisite Graph

Given an inline curriculum subgraph \(G = (V, E)\) with 10 modules representing orientation, mathematics, computation, and accounting, Kahn's algorithm performs cycle detection and returns a valid topological study sequence. The algorithm verifies acyclicity (0 cycles detected), confirms that the prerequisite closure for P0.2 is {P0.1}, determines that AC2 requires 7 antecedent modules, and demonstrates that P0.1 unlocks all remaining 9 modules in the graph.

"""Lab 1 (L01): Navigating an inline prerequisite DAG and computing topological ordering.

Standard library only. This lab defines an inline curriculum subgraph (a declared subset of program
modules and their prerequisite edges), performs cycle detection via Kahn's algorithm, computes a
deterministic topological sort order, calculates the transitive prerequisite closure for downstream
modules, and identifies the unlock set (all reachable downstream modules) of an entry node.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

# Declarative inline prerequisite DAG: child module -> list of direct prerequisite parents.
GRAPH: dict[str, list[str]] = {
    "P0.1": [],
    "P0.2": ["P0.1"],
    "M1": ["P0.1"],
    "M2": ["M1"],
    "M3": ["M2"],
    "CS1": ["P0.1"],
    "M4": ["M2"],
    "S0": ["CS1", "M4"],
    "AC1": ["P0.1"],
    "AC2": ["AC1", "S0"],
}


def topological_sort(graph: dict[str, list[str]]) -> list[str]:
    """Kahn's algorithm with deterministic alphabetical tie-breaking."""
    in_degree: dict[str, int] = {u: len(graph[u]) for u in graph}
    adj: dict[str, list[str]] = {u: [] for u in graph}
    for u, prereqs in graph.items():
        for p in prereqs:
            adj[p].append(u)

    ready: list[str] = sorted([u for u in graph if in_degree[u] == 0])
    order: list[str] = []
    while ready:
        u = ready.pop(0)
        order.append(u)
        for v in sorted(adj[u]):
            in_degree[v] -= 1
            if in_degree[v] == 0:
                ready.append(v)
                ready.sort()

    if len(order) != len(graph):
        raise ValueError("Cycle detected in prerequisite graph")
    return order


def prerequisite_closure(graph: dict[str, list[str]], target: str) -> set[str]:
    """Transitive closure of all antecedents required before target can be studied."""
    visited: set[str] = set()
    stack: list[str] = list(graph.get(target, []))
    while stack:
        node = stack.pop()
        if node not in visited:
            visited.add(node)
            stack.extend(graph.get(node, []))
    return visited


def unlock_set(graph: dict[str, list[str]], source: str) -> set[str]:
    """Transitive set of all downstream nodes reachable from source."""
    adj: dict[str, list[str]] = {u: [] for u in graph}
    for u, prereqs in graph.items():
        for p in prereqs:
            adj[p].append(u)

    visited: set[str] = set()
    stack: list[str] = list(adj.get(source, []))
    while stack:
        node = stack.pop()
        if node not in visited:
            visited.add(node)
            stack.extend(adj.get(node, []))
    return visited


def verify_topological_invariants(graph: dict[str, list[str]], order: list[str]) -> bool:
    """Verify that every prerequisite appears before its dependent module in order."""
    pos: dict[str, int] = {node: i for i, node in enumerate(order)}
    for node, prereqs in graph.items():
        for p in prereqs:
            if pos[p] >= pos[node]:
                return False
    return True


def main() -> None:
    print("Prerequisite DAG Analysis (inline curriculum subset)")
    print(f"Total modules: {len(GRAPH)}")

    order = topological_sort(GRAPH)
    valid = verify_topological_invariants(GRAPH, order)
    print("Cycles detected: 0")
    print(f"Topological order ({len(order)} nodes): {' -> '.join(order)}")
    print(f"Topological ordering invariant satisfied: {valid}")

    p02_closure = prerequisite_closure(GRAPH, "P0.2")
    print(f"Prerequisite closure for P0.2 ({len(p02_closure)} modules): {sorted(p02_closure)}")

    ac2_closure = prerequisite_closure(GRAPH, "AC2")
    print(f"Prerequisite closure for AC2 ({len(ac2_closure)} modules): {sorted(ac2_closure)}")

    p01_unlocks = unlock_set(GRAPH, "P0.1")
    print(f"Downstream modules unlocked by P0.1 ({len(p01_unlocks)} modules): {sorted(p01_unlocks)}")


main()

Cycles detected: 0

Topological ordering invariant satisfied: True

6Where the canon breaks

Conventional academic pedagogy often operates under implicit assumptions that fail in institutional quantitative research. Four canonical assumptions break down in professional quantitative practice:

  • Peer-reviewed publication is not proof of replication. In traditional academia, publication in a top journal is treated as settled evidence. In empirical asset pricing, over half of published return anomalies fail independent out-of-sample replication once accounting for multiple testing, data revisions, and transaction costs. The program's separation of Grade B from Grade A codifies this divergence.
  • Textbook mathematical notation is non-standardized. Econometrics textbooks use conflicting symbols across chapters, obscuring the mathematical equivalence of seemingly disparate models. The program's rigid KaTeX notation catalog treats mathematical vocabulary as an unambiguous software API.
  • Curriculum sequencing is rarely treated as a topological sort. Traditional university courses allow disjointed elective prerequisites that create hidden circularities or unfulfilled dependencies. The program treats learning pathways as formal DAG traversals with machine-enforced prerequisite closures.
  • Academic review frequently rewards strawmanning. Published debates often attack weakened versions of competing paradigms. In capital management, strawmanning a competitor's alpha thesis leads directly to unhedged portfolio risk and capital destruction.

7Misconceptions

  1. “A published paper with a t-statistic greater than 2.0 warrants Grade A evidence.”

    A t-statistic exceeding 2.0 in a single published sample merely satisfies standard two-tailed 5 percent significance under a single test. In financial economics, hundreds of factors have been tested across identical CRSP/Compustat samples, inflating false discovery rates. Grade A requires independent out-of-sample replication across different time windows or markets by an independent research team.

  2. “A prerequisite DAG allows students to skip fundamental modules if they can code.”

    A topological ordering requires that every antecedent in a module's transitive prerequisite closure \(\operatorname{Cl}^-(v)\) is fully satisfied before module \(v\) is attempted. Coding ability in Python does not substitute for mathematical probability, linear algebra, or econometric identification. The prerequisite closure cannot be bypassed without failing downstream project gates.

  3. “Steelmanning means agreeing with an opposing economic perspective.”

    Steelmanning is an adversarial testing protocol, not intellectual surrender. By formulating the strongest possible version of an opposing hypothesis, a quantitative researcher ensures that when empirical evidence refutes the steelmanned model, the conclusion is robust, definitive, and immune to simple counter-arguments.

Mechanics

M1 · Tracing Kahn's algorithm step by step

Consider a small directed graph with vertices \(V = \{A, B, C, D\}\) and directed edges \(E = \{(A, B), (A, C), (B, D), (C, D)\}\). Compute the in-degree of each vertex, specify the initial queue of source vertices, and trace the step-by-step state of the queue and the output array under Kahn's algorithm with alphabetical tie-breaking.

Solution

Step 1: Compute initial in-degrees. \(\operatorname{deg}^-(A) = 0\), \(\operatorname{deg}^-(B) = 1\), \(\operatorname{deg}^-(C) = 1\), \(\operatorname{deg}^-(D) = 2\).

Step 2: Initialize queue with vertices having in-degree 0: queue = [A], output = [].

Step 3: Dequeue \(A\). Append \(A\) to output: output = [A]. Decrement in-degrees of neighbors: \(\operatorname{deg}^-(B) = 1 - 1 = 0\), \(\operatorname{deg}^-(C) = 1 - 1 = 0\). Add \(B\) and \(C\) to queue in alphabetical order: queue = [B, C].

Step 4: Dequeue \(B\). Append \(B\) to output: output = [A, B]. Decrement neighbor \(D\): \(\operatorname{deg}^-(D) = 2 - 1 = 1\). \(D\) is not zero, so queue remains queue = [C].

Step 5: Dequeue \(C\). Append \(C\) to output: output = [A, B, C]. Decrement neighbor \(D\): \(\operatorname{deg}^-(D) = 1 - 1 = 0\). Add \(D\) to queue: queue = [D].

Step 6: Dequeue \(D\). Append \(D\) to output: output = [A, B, C, D]. No outgoing edges. Queue is empty.

Final topological sort: \([A, B, C, D]\). Length is 4, matching \(|V|\), confirming zero cycles.

M3 · Classifying claims on the evidence scale

Classify each of the following research claims into its appropriate evidence grade (T, A, B, C, D, X):

  1. "For any random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) with finite second moments, \(|\Cov(X, Y)| \le \sqrt{\Var(X)\Var(Y)}\)."
  2. "Trading on golden-cross 50-day and 200-day moving average crossovers reliably doubles market Sharpe ratios."
  3. "A 2024 NBER working paper finds that natural language sentiment in 8-K filings predicts 3-day abnormal returns with a t-statistic of 2.4 in a 2015–2022 sample."
Solution

Item 1: Grade T. This is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for covariances, a deductively proven mathematical theorem requiring only existence of second moments.

Item 2: Grade D (or X if tested against historical data). As a standalone heuristic claim without rigorous risk-adjustment, execution costs, or published proof, it is pure practitioner lore.

Item 3: Grade C. An unpublished NBER working paper examining a single sample (2015–2022) without independent multi-sample replication is classified as Grade C.

Proof

P1 · Every non-empty finite DAG has a sink vertex

Prove that every non-empty finite directed acyclic graph \(G = (V, E)\) contains at least one sink vertex, that is, a vertex \(w \in V\) with out-degree \(\operatorname{deg}^+(w) = 0\).

Solution

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be a finite DAG with \(|V| = n \ge 1\). Suppose for contradiction that \(\operatorname{deg}^+(v) \ge 1\) for every vertex \(v \in V\).

Select an initial vertex \(v_0 \in V\). Since \(\operatorname{deg}^+(v_0) \ge 1\), there exists a directed edge \((v_0, v_1) \in E\). Inductively, given vertex \(v_k\), since \(\operatorname{deg}^+(v_k) \ge 1\), we can choose \(v_{k+1}\) such that \((v_k, v_{k+1}) \in E\).

Consider the sequence of \(n + 1\) vertices \((v_0, v_1, \dots, v_n)\). Because there are only \(n\) distinct vertices in \(V\), by the Pigeonhole Principle there must exist at least two indices \(i\) and \(j\) with \(0 \le i < j \le n\) such that \(v_i = v_j\).

The sub-sequence \((v_i, v_{i+1}), (v_{i+1}, v_{i+2}), \dots, (v_{j-1}, v_j)\) forms a directed path from \(v_i\) back to \(v_i = v_j\). This constitutes a directed cycle of length \(j - i \ge 1\), directly contradicting the assumption that \(G\) is acyclic. Therefore, the hypothesis that every vertex has out-degree at least 1 is false. There must exist at least one vertex \(w \in V\) with \(\operatorname{deg}^+(w) = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

P2 · Characterization of acyclicity via topological sort

Prove that a finite directed graph \(G = (V, E)\) admits a topological ordering if and only if it is acyclic.

Solution

Direction 1 (\(\implies\)): Suppose \(G\) admits a topological ordering \(\pi: V \to \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\) such that \((u, v) \in E \implies \pi(u) < \pi(v)\). Suppose for contradiction that \(G\) contains a directed cycle \(C = (v_0, v_1, \dots, v_k)\) with \(v_k = v_0\) and \(k \ge 1\). For each edge in the cycle, \(\pi(v_i) < \pi(v_{i+1})\). By transitivity of the strict inequality on \(\N\), we have \(\pi(v_0) < \pi(v_1) < \dots < \pi(v_k) = \pi(v_0)\), implying \(\pi(v_0) < \pi(v_0)\), a contradiction. Hence \(G\) must be acyclic.

Direction 2 (\(\impliedby\)): Proved in Theorem 4.2: by induction on \(n = |V|\), every finite DAG contains a source vertex with in-degree zero. Placing the source first and applying induction on the induced subgraph yields a complete valid topological ordering. \(\blacksquare\)

P3 · Acyclicity of transitive closure

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be a DAG. The transitive closure \(G^+ = (V, E^+)\) contains an edge \((u, v) \in E^+\) if and only if there is a non-empty directed path from \(u\) to \(v\) in \(G\). Prove that \(G^+\) is also a DAG and that \(E^+\) defines a strict partial order on \(V\).

Solution

Let relation \(\prec\) on \(V\) be defined by \(u \prec v \iff (u, v) \in E^+\).

1. Irreflexivity: Suppose \(u \prec u\). Then there exists a non-empty directed path from \(u\) to \(u\) in \(G\), which constitutes a directed cycle in \(G\). Since \(G\) is a DAG, no such path exists. Hence \(u \not\prec u\) for all \(u \in V\).

2. Transitivity: Suppose \(u \prec v\) and \(v \prec w\). Then there exists a path \(p_1\) from \(u\) to \(v\) in \(G\) and a path \(p_2\) from \(v\) to \(w\) in \(G\). Concatenating \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) yields a directed path from \(u\) to \(w\) in \(G\). Thus \((u, w) \in E^+\), meaning \(u \prec w\).

3. Asymmetry: Suppose \(u \prec v\) and \(v \prec u\). By transitivity, \(u \prec u\), contradicting irreflexivity. Hence if \((u, v) \in E^+\), then \((v, u) \notin E^+\).

Because \(\prec\) is irreflexive and transitive, it is a strict partial order. Furthermore, any cycle in \(G^+\) would imply \(v_i \prec v_i\) by transitivity, violating irreflexivity. Therefore \(G^+\) is an acyclic directed graph. \(\blacksquare\)

R1 · Cryptographic registries vs academic citation rot

Evaluate how a machine-readable citation registry with SHA-256 DOI resolution prevents three distinct forms of academic error: (a) phantom citations, (b) unverified secondary attribution, and (c) retroactive revision drift. Compare this with standard BibTeX workflows.

Solution

(a) Phantom citations: Standard BibTeX allows authors to enter fictitious keys and unverified strings that compile cleanly into PDFs. A machine-enforced registry queries official Crossref/OpenLibrary APIs during build gates; any key lacking an authenticated publisher record causes build failure.

(b) Unverified secondary attribution: Authors frequently cite paper A for a claim actually made in paper B because they only read a summary in paper A. Mandatory data-locator attributes force the researcher to pin every quoted number or empirical finding to a specific table, panel, or page.

(c) Retroactive revision drift: Working papers often alter specifications and results between drafts. The registry records the exact year, container, and version date, preventing an author from attributing an updated result to an older unamended manuscript.

R2 · Steelmanning the rational asset pricing paradigm

A behavioural economist argues that cross-sectional value and size premia are pure manifestations of investor irrationality and overreaction. Formulate the strongest rational risk-based steelman of the value and size anomalies, citing multi-factor equilibrium theory.

Solution

A rigorous rational steelman posits that value firms are characterized by high financial leverage, irreversible physical capital, and elevated distress risk in macroeconomic downturns. In bad economic states (recessions, liquidity crunches), the marginal utility of consumption of investors is high. Value firms suffer disproportionate earnings impairment because their fixed assets cannot be quickly liquidated, making their cash flows covary positively with aggregate economic distress. Consequently, risk-averse investors demand an equilibrium risk premium to hold value equities. Similarly, small firms face severe credit market access frictions and higher vulnerability to bank lending contractions during crises. Their higher average returns compensate for systematic exposure to state-dependent distress risk rather than mispricing.

R3 · Designing an evidence-gated investment committee protocol

Design a 4-step formal evaluation protocol for an institutional quantitative investment committee evaluating a proposed systematic alpha strategy. Specify the required evidence grades at each gate.

Solution

Step 1 (Theoretical Formulation): The economic or market microstructure mechanism generating the alpha must be explicitly stated as a testable proposition (Grade T for mathematical foundations, or Grade B/C for published empirical precedent). Strawman benchmarks are rejected.

Step 2 (Data Provenance & Cleanliness): All input data must originate from verified market snapshots with documented survivorship-bias corrections and point-in-time point-of-sale dates (no restatement lookahead bias).

Step 3 (Replication & Out-of-Sample Testing): The strategy must demonstrate out-of-sample profitability across at least two independent market regimes or international asset universes not used in factor discovery (achieving Grade A empirical standing for the core phenomenon).

Step 4 (Falsification & Transaction Cost Stress): The strategy must pass sensitivity checks: parameter perturbation, execution slippage modeling, and delisting return adjustments. If performance collapses under any plausible market friction, the strategy is classified as Grade X (refuted) and rejected.
