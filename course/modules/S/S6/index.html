S6 · Causal Inference & Event Studies

Identification Frameworks, Policy Shocks, and Abnormal Return Econometrics (100 Hours · Tier M)

Course Overview

Empirical asset pricing and quantitative finance require distinguishing true causal mechanisms from spurious statistical correlations. Financial markets constantly react to macroeconomic policy shifts, central bank interest rate announcements, regulatory interventions, and corporate disclosures. Identifying whether an asset price movement is caused by a policy shock requires precise counterfactual reasoning.

This module provides rigorous doctoral-level training in modern microeconometric causal inference and high-frequency financial event studies. We begin with the Neyman-Rubin potential outcomes framework and the decomposition of naive observational differences into average treatment effects and selection bias. We develop the instrumental variables estimator, diagnose weak-instrument bias in finite samples, and formalize difference-in-differences under parallel trends. We analyze why canonical two-way fixed effects regressions fail under staggered rollout timing via the Goodman-Bacon decomposition, derive sharp regression discontinuity limits, formalize the exact variance of abnormal returns in market models, implement clustering-robust event study test statistics, execute multi-date placebo tests, and construct optimal synthetic controls.

Learning Objectives

  1. Formulate causal questions within the potential outcomes framework and decompose observational differences into treatment effects and selection bias.
  2. Derive the Wald instrumental variables estimator and diagnose finite-sample weak-instrument bias toward ordinary least squares.
  3. Prove the algebraic equivalence between canonical 2x2 difference-in-differences and two-way fixed effects interaction regressions under parallel trends.
  4. Decompose staggered-adoption panel estimators using the Goodman-Bacon decomposition to detect negative control group weighting.
  5. Estimate treatment jumps at sharp cutoffs using local linear regression discontinuity with explicit bandwidth trade-offs.
  6. Derive the exact finite-sample variance of market-model abnormal returns incorporating regressor estimation uncertainty.
  7. Construct cluster-robust event-study test statistics and evaluate false rejection rates using randomized placebo calendars.
  8. Optimize synthetic control weights on the unit simplex to construct data-driven counterfactual trajectories for treated units.

Placement Diagnostic

Complete this diagnostic before starting the lessons. Scoring 80% or higher and demonstrating competence on all marked core skills allows fast-tracking of foundational material.

  1. In an event study with estimation window length \(L = 50\), estimated residual variance \(s^2 = 0.0004\), estimation market variance \(\sum_{\tau} (R_{m\tau} - \bar{R}_m)^2 = 0.0050\), and event-day market return deviation \((R_{m0} - \bar{R}_m)^2 = 0.0001\), what is the correct analytical variance of the event-day abnormal return \(\Var(AR_0)\)?

    The exact variance formula accounts for parameter estimation error: \(\Var(AR_0) = s^2 [1 + 1/L + (R_{m0} - \bar{R}_m)^2 / SS_m] = 0.0004 \times [1 + 0.02 + 0.02] = 0.000416\).

  2. Given daily abnormal returns over a 3-day event window \([-1, +1]\) of \(AR_{-1} = +0.012\), \(AR_{0} = +0.025\), and \(AR_{+1} = -0.005\), what is the Cumulative Abnormal Return \(CAR(-1, +1)\)?

    Cumulative abnormal return is the arithmetic sum of abnormal returns across the event window: \(0.012 + 0.025 - 0.005 = 0.0320\).

  3. Under the Neyman-Rubin potential outcomes framework, what does the selection bias term represent in the decomposition of the naive difference in means between treated (\(D=1\)) and untreated (\(D=0\)) units?

    Selection bias is the baseline counterfactual difference between groups in the absence of treatment: \(\E[Y(0) \mid D=1] - \E[Y(0) \mid D=0]\).

  4. What essential condition must hold for an instrument \(Z\) to be considered valid for endogenous regressor \(D\) in estimating outcome \(Y\)?

    A valid instrument requires both relevance (\(\Cov(Z, D) \ne 0\)) and exogeneity / exclusion restriction (\(\Cov(Z, \varepsilon) = 0\)).

Lesson Map

The 70 hours of structured lessons cover the identification spectrum from potential outcomes to event study engines:

  1. Lesson 1 · Potential Outcomes & The Selection Problem (7 h)

    The Neyman-Rubin framework, observed vs counterfactual outcomes, average treatment effects, and selection bias decomposition.

  2. Lesson 2 · Instrumental Variables & Two-Stage Least Squares (9 h)

    Endogeneity, omitted variable bias, relevance and exclusion restrictions, the Wald estimator, and 2SLS projection.

  3. Lesson 3 · Weak Instruments & First-Stage Diagnostics (8 h)

    Finite-sample bias of 2SLS toward OLS, non-normal distributions, first-stage F-statistics, and identification breakdown.

  4. Lesson 4 · Difference-in-Differences & Parallel Trends (8 h)

    The canonical 2x2 DiD design, counterfactual parallel trends, no-anticipation assumptions, and exact TWFE equivalence.

  5. Lesson 5 · Staggered Adoption & Two-Way Fixed Effects Bias (8 h)

    Differential rollout timing, Goodman-Bacon decomposition, already-treated units as bad controls, and negative weighting bias.

  6. Lesson 6 · Regression Discontinuity Designs (8 h)

    Sharp RDD at threshold cutoffs, conditional expectation continuity, local linear estimation, and bandwidth trade-offs.

  7. Lesson 7 · Event Studies & Abnormal Returns (8 h)

    Market models, abnormal returns, exact AR variance derivation, CAR aggregation, and cross-sectional clustered inference.

  8. Lesson 8 · Placebo Tests & Permutation Inference (7 h)

    In-time and in-space falsification tests, event exclusion zones, and multi-date false discovery probabilities under independence.

  9. Lesson 9 · Synthetic Control Methods (7 h)

    Comparative case studies, constrained simplex weight optimization, pre-intervention MSPE minimization, and placebo inference.
