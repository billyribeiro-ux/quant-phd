Power Analysis for a Strategy

A quantitative portfolio manager presents a trading strategy with an attractive backtested Sharpe ratio. Before allocating capital, risk and investment committees require an exact power analysis: how many years of live or simulated return data are necessary to establish statistical significance at a designated power level? In this capstone project, you implement the statistical inference suite in sharpe_power.py to compute the required observation horizon analytically and by Monte Carlo simulation, reconcile the asymptotic and empirical power levels, and synthesize your findings in a professional one-page executive memo.

The project ladder

This project follows a structured four-rung learning ladder. If you achieved the mastery threshold on the hub placement diagnostic, you may advance directly to Rung 3.

  1. Worked example

    Lab 1: Complete end-to-end analytical derivation and simulation check of required years for a monthly strategy.

  2. Completion problem

    Lab 2: Implement the critical alternative variance inflation formula and sample size ceiling calculation.

  3. Independent project

    The core deliverable: implement sharpe_power.py verified by the autograder, run simulation studies across sampling regimes, and author the executive memo evaluated against the rubric.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the power analysis to Student-t returns with heavy tails or autocorrelated returns, quantifying the sample size expansion factor.

Lab 1 · Worked example: Closed-form power calculation and simulation validation

Hypothesis: For an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.60 observed monthly (\(k = 12\)), achieving 80% power at a 5% one-sided significance level requires approximately 17.26 years of data, corresponding to \(\lceil 207.13 \rceil = 208\) monthly periods. Monte Carlo simulation of 2,000 independent trials at \(T = 208\) periods will reject the null hypothesis at a rate within sampling error of 80%.

"""Lab S1-project-1: Rung 1 worked example: power analysis for a strategy.

Computes the required years and periods in closed form, checks theoretical power,
and confirms via seeded Monte Carlo simulation.
SIMULATED: all returns are generated with random.Random(20261109).
Standard library only. No external imports.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
import statistics


def main() -> None:
    # Strategy parameters
    sr_ann = 0.60  # Annualized Sharpe ratio
    k = 12  # Monthly observations per year
    alpha = 0.05  # One-sided significance level
    power = 0.80  # Target statistical power

    dist = statistics.NormalDist()
    z_alpha = dist.inv_cdf(1.0 - alpha)
    z_power = dist.inv_cdf(power)

    # Per-period parameters
    sr_period = sr_ann / math.sqrt(k)
    # Alternative variance inflation factor: 1 + SR^2 / 2
    inflation_factor = 1.0 + (sr_period**2) / 2.0
    inflation_sd = math.sqrt(inflation_factor)

    # Analytic required years and required periods
    years = ((z_alpha + z_power * inflation_sd) ** 2) / (sr_ann**2)
    periods = math.ceil(years * k)

    # Theoretical power at integer periods T
    z_stat = (z_alpha - math.sqrt(periods) * sr_period) / inflation_sd
    theoretical_power = 1.0 - dist.cdf(z_stat)

    # Seeded Monte Carlo simulation of power
    seed = 20261109
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    n_sims = 2000
    rejections = 0
    sqrt_t = math.sqrt(periods)

    for _ in range(n_sims):
        sample = [rng.gauss(sr_period, 1.0) for _ in range(periods)]
        m = math.fsum(sample) / periods
        v = math.fsum((x - m) ** 2 for x in sample) / (periods - 1)
        if v > 0.0 and sqrt_t * (m / math.sqrt(v)) >= z_alpha:
            rejections += 1

    sim_power = rejections / n_sims

    print("strategy power analysis (worked example):")
    print(f"annualized Sharpe ratio SR_ann: {sr_ann:.2f}, periods per year k: {k}")
    print(f"one-sided alpha: {alpha:.2f}, target power: {power:.2f}")
    print(f"per-period Sharpe ratio SR: {sr_period:.4f}")
    print(f"variance inflation factor 1 + SR^2 / 2: {inflation_factor:.6f}")
    print(f"analytic required years: {years:.4f}")
    print(f"required integer periods T = ceil(y * k): {periods}")
    print(f"theoretical power achieved at T = {periods}: {theoretical_power:.4f}")
    print(f"simulated power (N = {n_sims}, seed = {seed}): {sim_power:.4f}")

Rung 3 · Project specification

Context
A quantitative trading desk evaluates systematic investment strategies across equities, foreign exchange, and fixed income. The desk requires rigorous statistical power analysis to establish whether backtested performance is likely to be sustained out of sample or if a proposed live evaluation trial is underpowered. You will develop the statistical engine that determines the observation horizon analytically and empirically, and author an executive memo explaining the findings to senior investment leadership.

Lab 3 · Benchmark reference values for executive memo

Hypothesis: Evaluates the closed-form required observation horizons across standard annualized Sharpe ratio benchmarks (0.30, 0.50, 0.70, 1.00) and sampling frequencies (monthly vs daily) at 80% and 90% power levels.

"""Lab S1-project-3: Rung 3 reference closed-form numbers for strategy cases.

Standard library only. Evaluates the analytic required years and periods across cases.
No random draws used.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import statistics


def compute_case(sr_ann: float, k: int, alpha: float, power: float) -> tuple[float, int, float]:
    dist = statistics.NormalDist()
    z_alpha = dist.inv_cdf(1.0 - alpha)
    z_power = dist.inv_cdf(power)
    sr_p = sr_ann / math.sqrt(k)
    inflation = math.sqrt(1.0 + (sr_p**2) / 2.0)
    years = ((z_alpha + z_power * inflation) ** 2) / (sr_ann**2)
    periods = math.ceil(years * k)
    z_stat = (z_alpha - math.sqrt(periods) * sr_p) / inflation
    theo_power = 1.0 - dist.cdf(z_stat)
    return years, periods, theo_power


def main() -> None:
    cases = [
        (0.30, 12, 0.05, 0.80),
        (0.50, 12, 0.05, 0.80),
        (0.70, 12, 0.05, 0.80),
        (1.00, 12, 0.05, 0.80),
        (0.50, 252, 0.05, 0.80),
        (0.50, 12, 0.05, 0.90),
    ]

    print("reference cases for memo comparison (alpha = 0.05):")
    for sr_ann, k, alpha, power in cases:
        years, periods, theo_power = compute_case(sr_ann, k, alpha, power)
        freq = "monthly" if k == 12 else "daily"
        print(
            f"SR_ann = {sr_ann:.2f}, {freq} (k = {k}), power = {power:.2f}: "
            f"years = {years:.4f}, periods = {periods}, theoretical power = {theo_power:.4f}"
        )

Exemplar A · High-Distinction Benchmark (Level 4)

Executive Memo: Statistical Power Analysis for Strategy Allocation

To: Investment Risk Committee
From: Quantitative Research Desk
Date: November 10, 2026
Subject: Power Analysis and Minimum Track Record Horizon for Proposed Quantitative Strategy

1. Executive Summary: A quantitative equities strategy targeting an annualized Sharpe ratio \(\SR_{\mathrm{ann}} = 0.60\) requires \(y = 17.26\) years of live monthly track record (\(T = 208\) months) to detect profitability with 80% statistical power at a 5% one-sided significance level (\(\alpha = 0.05\)). Evaluating the strategy over a standard 3-year or 5-year track record provides only 22.8% and 34.6% statistical power respectively, creating an unacceptably high Type II error probability (\(\beta > 65\%\)).

2. Statistical Model and Closed-Form Derivation: Under the assumption of i.i.d. normal periodic returns \(r_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) with \(k\) observations per year, the per-period Sharpe ratio is \(\SR = \mu/\sigma = \SR_{\mathrm{ann}}/\sqrt{k}\). We test the one-sided null hypothesis \(H_0: \SR \le 0\) against \(H_1: \SR > 0\) using the sample Sharpe ratio \(\hSR = \bar r / s\), where \(s\) is the unbiased sample standard deviation with divisor \(T - 1\). Under \(H_0\), \(\sqrt{T}\hSR \dto \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), yielding the rejection region \(\sqrt{T}\hSR \ge \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha)\). Under \(H_1\), applying the delta method to the joint moments of \((\bar r, \overline{r^2})\) reveals that \(\sqrt{T}(\hSR - \SR) \dto \mathcal{N}(0, 1 + \SR^2/2)\). Accounting for this variance inflation factor, the required calendar horizon in years is given in closed form by: \[ y = \frac{\left(\Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha) + \Phi^{-1}(1 - \beta)\sqrt{1 + \frac{\SR_{\mathrm{ann}}^2}{2k}}\right)^2}{\SR_{\mathrm{ann}}^2} \]

3. Numerical Results and Monte Carlo Validation: For \(\SR_{\mathrm{ann}} = 0.60\), \(k = 12\), \(\alpha = 0.05\), and \(1 - \beta = 0.80\), the critical standard normal quantiles are \(\Phi^{-1}(0.95) = 1.6449\) and \(\Phi^{-1}(0.80) = 0.8416\). The variance inflation factor is \(1 + 0.60^2 / (2 \times 12) = 1.0075\) (\(\sqrt{1.0075} = 1.00374\)). This yields \(y = 17.2607\) years, or \(T = \lceil 207.13 \rceil = 208\) monthly periods. To validate this result, we ran \(N = 2{,}000\) independent Monte Carlo simulations with random seed 20261109. The empirical rejection rate under \(H_0\) (\(\SR = 0\)) was \(5.10\%\) (Monte Carlo standard error \(0.49\%\)), confirming correct test size. At \(T = 208\) under \(H_1\), the empirical rejection rate was \(80.15\%\) (Monte Carlo standard error \(0.89\%\)), perfectly matching the theoretical asymptotic power of \(80.15\%\).

4. Sampling Frequency and Reconciliation: Transitioning from monthly (\(k = 12\)) to daily (\(k = 252\)) sampling increases the number of return observations by a factor of 21 (\(T_{\mathrm{daily}} = 4{,}347\) days), but reduces the required calendar time only negligibly from 17.26 to 17.23 years. This confirms that for i.i.d. returns, statistical power is governed almost entirely by calendar time rather than sampling frequency. The small residual gap between finite-sample power and asymptotic power is attributable to the \(O(1/T)\) non-central t bias and the discrete ceiling rounding.

5. Strategic Recommendations and Limitations: (a) Do not reject strategies solely based on 3-year track records, as 80% power cannot be attained without an annualized Sharpe ratio exceeding 1.44. (b) The power equation assumes zero autocorrelation; positive serial correlation increases the variance of the sample mean and further inflates the required horizon. (c) Heavy tails (excess kurtosis \(\gamma_4 > 3\)) expand the variance inflation term to \(1 + \SR^2/2 + (\gamma_4 - 3)\SR^2/4\), requiring additional observation time.

Evaluator commentary: Exemplar A meets all Level 4 descriptors across all five criteria. It provides explicit hypothesis formulations, exact delta-method variance derivation, rigorous Monte Carlo standard errors, precise frequency comparisons, and practical governance recommendations.

Exemplar B · Baseline Developing Benchmark (Level 2)

Memo: Sharpe Ratio Analysis

To determine how much data is needed for a Sharpe ratio of 0.6, we use the standard power formula. The formula is \(N = (z_\alpha + z_\beta)^2 / \SR^2\). For 80% power and 5% alpha, \(z_\alpha = 1.64\) and \(z_\beta = 0.84\). Adding these gives 2.48, and squaring gives 6.15. Dividing by \(0.6^2 = 0.36\) gives 17.08 years.

We ran a simulation in Python with 500 runs. The simulation gave a power of roughly 79%, which is close to 80%. We also tried daily data and it gave thousands of observations, which confirms that daily data gives much better results.

In conclusion, managers should have at least 17 years of data before being hired, which may be difficult in practice.

Evaluator commentary: Exemplar B scores Level 2 across all criteria. It uses the naive variance formula omitting the \(1 + \SR^2/2\) inflation term, confuses periods with years, fails to specify random seed or Monte Carlo standard error, asserts erroneously that daily data dramatically changes the required calendar duration, and lacks professional depth.
