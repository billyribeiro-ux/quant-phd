Statistical Inference

Quantitative finance demands distinguishing true economic signal from random sampling variability. A positive backtested return or Sharpe ratio is meaningless without an exact inference framework: is the performance statistically distinguishable from zero, how wide is the uncertainty band, and what sample size is required to make that determination reliably? This module develops classical and modern statistical inference from foundational principles: point estimation and sampling distributions, maximum likelihood and Fisher information, asymptotic normality and Cramér–Rao lower bounds, hypothesis tests and statistical power, confidence intervals and coverage probability, Bayesian posterior updating, the non-parametric bootstrap, and the exact asymptotic standard error of the Sharpe ratio under i.i.d. returns. It culminates in an end-to-end power analysis for trading strategies.

  • Prerequisites: M4 Probability (random variables, expectation, variance, central limit theorem) and S0 Data & Exploration (empirical distribution, sample quantiles).
  • Project: Power analysis for a strategy (dual grading: autograder for the computational suite and rubric for the executive memo).
  • Data status: All data, samples, and instances in this unit are SIMULATED. No proprietary market data is required.

100 h lessons · 30 h project (23.1% of 130 h total)

Objectives

By completing module S1, you will master the ability to:

  1. Derive the finite-sample bias, variance, and mean squared error of parametric estimators, and prove estimator consistency under Chebyshev's inequality (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the likelihood and score equations for parametric models, and calculate Fisher information analytically (Lesson 2).
  3. Evaluate the Cramér–Rao lower bound, prove the asymptotic normality of maximum likelihood estimators under regularity conditions, and apply the multivariate delta method to non-linear transformations (Lesson 3).
  4. Construct exact and asymptotic hypothesis tests, compute exact p-values, and characterize uniformly most powerful decision boundaries (Lesson 4).
  5. Quantify Type I error, Type II error, and statistical power curves across sample sizes and effect sizes (Lesson 5).
  6. Implement likelihood-ratio and Student t-tests, and derive confidence intervals with verifiable coverage probabilities (Lessons 6 and 7).
  7. Execute non-parametric bootstrap resampling, perform Bayesian posterior inference across conjugate and discrete priors, and derive the asymptotic standard error of the Sharpe ratio under i.i.d. returns (Lessons 8, 9, and 10).

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic items testing prerequisite readiness and capstone project skills. An 80% score (8/10 correct) including both marked skill checks permits skipping Project Rungs 1 and 2.

  1. Let \(X_1, \dots, X_n\) be i.i.d. draws with finite mean \(\mu\) and finite variance \(\sigma^2\). What is the variance of the sample mean \(\bar X = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\)?

    Options

    By independence, \(\Var(\sum X_i) = n\sigma^2\). Scaling by \(1/n\) squares the scalar: \(\Var(\bar X) = (1/n^2)(n\sigma^2) = \sigma^2/n\) (M4 Variance).

  2. If \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\), what is the value of the cumulative distribution function \(\Phi(0)\)?

    By symmetry of the standard normal density about zero, \(\Phi(0) = 0.5\) exactly (M4 Normal distribution).

  3. What does Chebyshev's inequality bound for any random variable \(X\) with finite mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\)?

    Options

    Chebyshev's inequality states \(\Prob(|X - \mu| \ge \epsilon) \le \sigma^2/\epsilon^2\), which for \(\epsilon = k\sigma\) yields \(1/k^2\) (M4 Probability).

  4. What is the 95th percentile of the standard normal distribution, \(\Phi^{-1}(0.95)\), to four decimal places?

    \(\Phi^{-1}(0.95) \approx 1.64485\), rounded to 1.6449 (M4 Normal quantiles).

  5. Under the classical Central Limit Theorem for i.i.d. draws with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), what is the limiting distribution of \(\sqrt{n}(\bar X_n - \mu)\)?

    Options

    The standard CLT establishes that \(\sqrt{n}(\bar X_n - \mu) \dto \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) (M4 Central limit theorem).

  6. A sample consists of three values: \(\{1.0, 2.0, 3.0\}\). What is the sample variance with Bessel's correction (divisor \(n - 1\))? Enter as decimal.

    The sample mean is 2.0. Sum of squared deviations is \((1-2)^2 + (2-2)^2 + (3-2)^2 = 1 + 0 + 1 = 2\). Divisor is \(3 - 1 = 2\). Sample variance is \(2 / 2 = 1.0\). Wait: with values \(\{1, 2, 3\}\), variance is \(1.0\). If values are \(\{1, 2, 4\}\), mean is \(7/3\). For \(\{0, 1, 3\}\), mean is \(4/3\), sum of sq dev is \((16/9 + 1/9 + 25/9) = 42/9 = 14/3\), divided by 2 is \(7/3 \approx 2.333\).

  7. What does the empirical distribution function \(F_n(x)\) represent for a sample \(X_1, \dots, X_n\)?

    Options

    By definition, the empirical distribution function places mass \(1/n\) at each sample observation (S0 Empirical distribution).

  8. If the log-likelihood function \(\ell(\theta)\) is twice continuously differentiable and strictly concave, where does the maximum likelihood estimator \(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{MLE}}\) lie?

    Options

    For an unconstrained strictly concave differentiable function, the unique global maximizer satisfies the first-order condition \(\ell'(\theta) = 0\) (M2 Optimization).

  9. Skill check 1 (Project Rung 1): Under i.i.d. normality, the asymptotic variance of \(\sqrt{T}\hSR\) under the alternative is \(1 + \SR^2/2\). For a strategy with periodic Sharpe ratio \(\SR = 0.20\), what is the numerical value of the variance inflation factor \(1 + \SR^2/2\)?

    \(1 + (0.20)^2 / 2 = 1 + 0.04 / 2 = 1.02\) exactly (Lesson 10 and Project Rung 1).

  10. Skill check 2 (Project Rung 2): An analytical power calculation determines that exactly \(y = 8.682\) years of data are required at monthly frequency (\(k = 12\)). What is the minimum integer number of monthly observations \(T = \lceil y \cdot k \rceil\) needed to guarantee the power target?

    \(8.682 \times 12 = 104.184\). Taking the ceiling \(\lceil 104.184 \rceil\) gives 105 monthly periods (Project Rung 2).

Lessons

Ten lessons of 10 study hours each, totaling 100 study hours:

L01 · 10 h Estimators and Their Sampling Distributions Definition of an estimator as a sample statistic, sampling distributions, bias, variance, mean squared error, and consistency via Chebyshev's inequality. L02 · 10 h Maximum Likelihood and Fisher Information The likelihood principle, score functions, Fisher information as expected negative Hessian, and the information inequality. L03 · 10 h MLE Asymptotics, Cramér–Rao Bound and the Delta Method Proof of the Cramér–Rao lower bound, asymptotic normality and efficiency of MLEs, and the univariate and multivariate delta methods. L04 · 10 h Hypothesis Tests and p-Values Null and alternative hypotheses, test statistics, critical regions, exact and asymptotic p-values, and the Neyman–Pearson lemma. L05 · 10 h Type I and Type II Errors, and Power Significance levels, Type I and II error probabilities, statistical power functions, sample size determination, and the power trade-off. L06 · 10 h The t-Statistic and the Likelihood-Ratio Test Student's t-statistic under normal and non-normal sampling, the likelihood-ratio test statistic, and Wilks' theorem on chi-square limits. L07 · 10 h Confidence Intervals and Coverage Inversion of hypothesis tests, pivotal quantities, Wald versus Wilson intervals, and exact coverage probability across parameter spaces. L08 · 10 h The Bootstrap The plug-in principle, empirical distribution resampling, percentile and bias-corrected bootstrap intervals, and breakdown under serial dependence. L09 · 10 h Bayesian Inference Prior, likelihood, and posterior distributions; Bayes' rule; conjugate families; credible intervals; and prior sensitivity at finite sample sizes. L10 · 10 h The Sharpe Ratio and Its Standard Error Asymptotic distribution of the sample Sharpe ratio under i.i.d. returns via the delta method, skewness and kurtosis corrections, and bootstrap intervals.

    Fully annotated derivation of closed-form required years and Monte Carlo simulation verification for a monthly strategy.

    Fill in the critical variance inflation and integer ceiling functions, graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the full sharpe_power.py module, validate across seeds with the autograder, and author the executive memo evaluated against the rubric. Project specifications.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the statistical engine to heavy-tailed Student-t returns and autocorrelated series, computing track record expansion multiples.
