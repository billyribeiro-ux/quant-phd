Linear Regression

Linear regression is the foundational workhorse of empirical quantitative finance, connecting asset returns to risk factors, testing pricing models, and estimating return predictability. However, financial data routinely violate classical assumptions through conditional heteroskedasticity, persistent time-series autocorrelation, multi-level firm and time cluster dependencies, regressor measurement error, and endogenous lagged predictors. This module develops the theory, geometry, and asymptotic distribution of ordinary least squares estimation from first principles: orthogonal projections and the normal equations, partialling out via Frisch–Waugh–Lovell, the Gauss–Markov theorem under homoskedasticity, \(R^2\) decomposition, finite-sample omitted-variable and measurement error attenuation biases, sandwich asymptotics, White heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors, Newey–West HAC covariance estimation, Petersen one-way and two-way clustering, and the exact finite-sample Stambaugh bias in predictive regressions. It culminates in building a production-grade inference engine.

  • Prerequisites: M3 Linear Algebra and S1 Statistical Inference.
  • Project: Inference engine (autograded implementation of OLS with classical, White, Newey–West, and two-way clustered standard errors).
  • Data status: All data, samples, and instances in this unit are SIMULATED. No external market feeds are required.

84 h lessons · 36 h project (30.0% of 120 h total)

Objectives

By completing module S2, you will master the ability to:

  1. Derive the ordinary least squares estimator as an orthogonal projection onto the column space of the design matrix, and prove residual orthogonality from the normal equations (Lesson 1).
  2. Apply the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem to partial out nuisance controls and prove algebraic equivalence between multivariate regression coefficients and univariate residual regressions (Lesson 2).
  3. Deconstruct the coefficient of determination \(R^2\) into explained and residual sums of squares, and prove that adding regressors weakly increases fit (Lesson 3).
  4. Prove the Gauss–Markov theorem establishing OLS as the best linear unbiased estimator under spherical errors, and characterize efficiency loss under heteroskedasticity (Lesson 4).
  5. Quantify omitted-variable bias and errors-in-variables attenuation in regression slopes via exact projection identities (Lessons 5 and 6).
  6. Formulate asymptotic distribution theory for OLS, construct White heteroskedasticity-robust and Newey–West HAC sandwich estimators, and derive one-way and two-way cluster-robust standard errors for panel data (Lessons 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11).
  7. Derive the finite-sample Stambaugh bias in predictive return regressions with persistent predictors, and evaluate empirical coverage rates of nominal confidence intervals across financial panel structures (Lesson 12 and Project).

Prerequisites

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic items testing prerequisite readiness and capstone project skills. An 80% score (8/10 correct) including both marked skill checks permits skipping Project Rungs 1 and 2.

  1. Let \(X\) be an \(n \times k\) matrix with full column rank \(k < n\). What is the geometric interpretation of the hat matrix \(H = X(X'X)^{-1}X'\)?

    Options

    \(H\) is symmetric (\(H' = H\)) and idempotent (\(H^2 = H\)), projecting any vector \(y \in \R^n\) onto the \(k\)-dimensional subspace \(\mathrm{col}(X)\) (M3 Orthogonal projection).

  2. In an OLS regression containing an explicit intercept term, what is the exact sum of the residuals \(\sum_{i=1}^n e_i\)? Enter as a number.

    The first normal equation requires \(X'e = 0\). When the first column of \(X\) is a vector of ones \(\mathbf{1}\), \(\mathbf{1}'e = \sum e_i = 0\) exactly (Lesson 1).

  3. Under the classical Gauss–Markov assumptions (linearity, strict exogeneity \(\E[\varepsilon|X] = 0\), spherical errors \(\Var(\varepsilon|X) = \sigma^2 I_n\), and full rank), which property does the OLS estimator \(\hat\beta\) possess?

    Options

    Gauss–Markov establishes that \(\Var(\tilde\beta|X) - \Var(\hat\beta|X)\) is positive semidefinite for any linear unbiased estimator \(\tilde\beta\) (Lesson 4).

  4. Suppose the true model contains two regressors \(x_1\) and \(x_2\), but \(x_2\) is omitted from the regression of \(y\) on \(x_1\). Under what condition is the short-regression slope \(\hat\beta_1^{\mathrm{short}}\) unbiased for \(\beta_1\)?

    Options

    The omitted-variable bias formula states \(\E[\hat\beta_1^{\mathrm{short}}] = \beta_1 + \beta_2 \frac{\Cov(x_1, x_2)}{\Var(x_1)}\). Bias vanishes if \(\beta_2 = 0\) or \(\Cov(x_1, x_2) = 0\) (Lesson 5).

  5. A regressor \(x^*\) has variance \(\sigma_{x^*}^2 = 4.0\). It is measured with classical independent noise \(u\) having variance \(\sigma_u^2 = 1.0\), yielding observed \(x = x^* + u\). What is the reliability ratio \(\lambda = \sigma_{x^*}^2 / (\sigma_{x^*}^2 + \sigma_u^2)\)?

    \(\lambda = 4.0 / (4.0 + 1.0) = 4.0 / 5.0 = 0.80\). The OLS slope is attenuated toward zero by a factor of 0.80 (Lesson 6).

  6. What is the central distinction between White (HC0) heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors and Newey–West (HAC) standard errors?

    Options

    White HC0 sets off-diagonal autocovariances to zero. Newey–West incorporates Bartlett-weighted autocovariances up to lag \(L\) (Lessons 8 and 9).

  7. In a panel dataset with cluster size \(m = 5\) observations per firm and an intraclass error correlation \(\rho_{\mathrm{ic}} = 0.50\), what is the design effect variance inflation factor \(1 + (m - 1)\rho_{\mathrm{ic}}\)?

    \(1 + (5 - 1) \times 0.50 = 1 + 4 \times 0.50 = 3.0\). OLS variance is understated by a factor of 3 under classical formulas (Lesson 10).

  8. In predictive regressions of future returns on persistent predictors, what causes Stambaugh bias in the OLS slope estimate \(\hat\beta\)?

    Options

    Stambaugh bias arises because the finite-sample Hurwicz bias in estimating regressor persistence is transmitted into \(\hat\beta\) through innovation covariance (Lesson 12).

  9. Skill check 1 (Project Rung 1): In the Newey–West estimator with lag bound \(L = 1\), what is the Bartlett kernel weight \(w_1 = 1 - 1/(L + 1)\)?

    For \(L = 1\) and \(j = 1\), \(w_1 = 1 - 1/2 = 0.50\) exactly (Project Rung 1 and Lesson 9).

  10. Skill check 2 (Project Rung 2): An econometrician computes two-way clustered variance with firm variance \(0.045\), time variance \(0.038\), and intersection variance \(0.021\). What is the two-way variance \(V = 0.045 + 0.038 - 0.021\)?

    \(V = 0.045 + 0.038 - 0.021 = 0.062\) exactly (Project Rung 2 and Lesson 11).

Lessons

Twelve lessons of 7 study hours each, totaling 84 study hours:

L01 · 7 h Ordinary least squares as orthogonal projection Matrix formulation of the linear model, normal equations, projection geometry of the hat matrix, and algebraic residual orthogonality. L02 · 7 h Partialling out: the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem The anatomy of multivariate coefficients, residual-on-residual regressions, orthogonal decomposition, and partialling out fixed controls. L03 · 7 h Fit and the coefficient of determination Sum of squares decomposition TSS = ESS + RSS, R-squared as squared correlation of actual and fitted values, and monotonicity under regressor addition. L04 · 7 h Gauss–Markov: the best linear unbiased estimator Proof of BLUE optimality under spherical errors, positive semidefiniteness of alternative covariance matrices, and breakdown under heteroskedasticity. L05 · 7 h Omitted-variable bias The omitted-variable bias formula beta_1 + beta_2 * delta, signed bias conditions, and the perils of bad controls. L06 · 7 h Errors-in-variables bias Classical measurement error, attenuation toward zero via the reliability ratio, dependent variable measurement noise, and Shanken correction context. L07 · 7 h The asymptotic distribution of OLS Consistency via the Law of Large Numbers, asymptotic normality via Central Limit Theorem, sandwich covariance, and validity under non-normal errors. L08 · 7 h Heteroskedasticity-robust (White) standard errors The White HC0 sandwich estimator, finite-sample coverage distortions under conditional heteroskedasticity, and fourth-moment consistency requirements. L09 · 7 h HAC (Newey–West) standard errors Autocorrelated errors in time-series finance, Bartlett triangular kernel weighting, positive semidefiniteness, and the lag-zero White equivalence. L10 · 7 h Clustered standard errors Cluster-robust CR0 covariance, group score aggregation, design effect variance inflation, and Petersen panel benchmarking in corporate finance. L11 · 7 h Two-way clustered standard errors Simultaneous firm and time correlation in finance panels, the inclusion-exclusion variance formula V_A + V_B - V_AB, and identical clustering reduction. L12 · 7 h Predictive regressions and Stambaugh bias First-order predictive regression bias, persistent autoregressive regressors, correlated innovations, and false discovery in equity premium prediction.

The project ladder

The module concludes with a 36-hour capstone project: Inference engine. The four-rung ladder structure scaffolds mastery:

  1. Worked example

    Complete annotated calculation of OLS beta and four standard error methods on a seeded bivariate design.

  2. Completion problem

    Fill in the critical meat summation, Bartlett weighting, and two-way variance combination, checked instantly.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the complete inference_engine.py module, validated by the autograder suite across 50 seeds and simulation coverage studies. Project specifications.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the inference engine to small-sample HC2 and HC3 corrections, and evaluate block bootstrap confidence intervals.
