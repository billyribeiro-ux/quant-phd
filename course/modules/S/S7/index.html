Research Integrity & Multiple Testing

A statistic reported after a search across strategies, specifications, factors, managers or holdout peeks is the maximum of that search, not a single draw. This module measures what that search does to the statistics you report, and builds the tools that undo it. You leave able to say, with a proof or a bootstrap behind you, whether a backtest is evidence.

  • Prerequisites: S1 Statistical Inference, S2 Linear Regression, S3 Time Series
  • Unlocks: F5 The Factor Zoo & Replication, QR2 Backtesting Architecture, CAP1, STUDIO-S Predictive-Regression Stress Test, STUDIO-F Factor Research Report
  • Project: Atlas A8, autograded

56 h lessons · 24 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of S7 you can:

  1. Derive the expected maximum of \(N\) null test statistics, bound it under any dependence, and compute the luck hurdle a stated research process must clear.
  2. Prove the error-rate guarantees of Bonferroni, Holm, Benjamini–Hochberg and Benjamini–Yekutieli, and choose between family-wise and false-discovery control for a given decision.
  3. Implement the stationary bootstrap, White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA and the Romano–Wolf StepM, and state the conditions under which each controls its error rate.
  4. Compute t-statistic hurdles, minimum Bayes factors and Bayesianized p-values, and argue both sides of the replication evidence in empirical finance.
  5. Derive the variance of the Sharpe ratio under skewness and kurtosis, and compute probabilistic and deflated Sharpe ratios with an explicit effective number of trials.
  6. Estimate the probability of backtest overfitting by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation and interpret it without overreach.
  7. Ship Atlas A8, a tested research-integrity module attached to every backtest the program's platform produces.

Prerequisites

S7 assumes S1 (Statistical Inference), S2 (Linear Regression) and S3 (Time Series), and everything they rest on. These are the concepts it uses without redefining them:

  • From S1: the Sharpe ratio and its iid standard error, hypothesis tests, p-values, type I error, power, t-statistics, the bootstrap.
  • From S2: OLS and HAC standard errors.
  • From S3: stationarity, the autocorrelation function and ARMA models; the bootstrap tests of Lessons 4–5 resample dependent data.
  • From M4: the normal distribution, the central limit theorem, skewness and kurtosis.

Placement diagnostic

Eleven questions on the prerequisites (S1–S3) and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1–2 of the project teach. Skip rungs 1–2 only if you reach the 80% bar and answer both skill checks; then use the lessons' problem sets to fill gaps. Below the bar, revisit S1–S3 first.

  1. For iid normal excess returns with true Sharpe ratio 0, what is the asymptotic variance of \(\sqrt{T}\,\hSR\)?

    Options

    The iid-normal variance is \(1 + \SR^2/2\), which equals 1 at \(\SR = 0\).

  2. A p-value is…

    Options

    It conditions on the null. It is not a posterior probability.

  3. \(1 - \Phi(1.96)\), to three decimals?

    \(\Phi(1.96) = 0.975\), so the upper tail is 0.025.

  4. The size of a test is…

    Options

    Size is the type I error probability. Power is the probability of rejecting a false null.

  5. \(X_1, \dots, X_{100}\) are iid with variance 4. What is \(\Var(\bar X)\)?

    \(\Var(\bar X) = \sigma^2/T = 4/100 = 0.04\).

  6. The kurtosis (not excess) of a normal distribution?

    \(\E[(X-\mu)^4]/\sigma^4 = 3\) for any normal.

  7. Why would you use HAC (Newey–West type) standard errors instead of classical ones?

    Options

    HAC estimators stay consistent under heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation of unknown form.

  8. The nonparametric bootstrap approximates the sampling distribution of a statistic by…

    Options

    The empirical distribution stands in for the unknown population.

  9. The central limit theorem says that, for iid observations with finite variance, the standardized sample mean…

    Options

    Convergence in distribution as the sample grows. With fat tails it can be slow.

  10. A monthly Sharpe ratio of 0.458 corresponds to what annualized Sharpe ratio? (three decimals)

    Multiply by \(\sqrt{12} = 3.4641\): \(0.458 \times 3.4641 = 1.587\).

  11. A stationary AR(1) process has coefficient \(\phi = 0.5\). What is its autocorrelation at lag 2?

    For an AR(1), \(\rho(h) = \phi^{|h|}\), so \(\rho(2) = 0.5^2 = 0.25\) (S3).

  12. Skill check (rung 1). Run Benjamini–Hochberg at \(q = 0.05\) on the six p-values 0.001, 0.012, 0.020, 0.024, 0.041, 0.300. How many hypotheses does it reject?

    The lines are \(kq/m = 0.0083, 0.0167, 0.025, 0.0333, 0.0417, 0.05\). The largest \(k\) with \(p_{(k)} \le kq/m\) is \(k = 5\) (\(0.041 \le 0.0417\)), so BH rejects the five smallest (L03).

  13. Skill check (rung 2). A strategy shows a per-period Sharpe ratio of 0.1 over \(T = 1000\) periods, with skewness \(-1\) and kurtosis 6 (not excess). What is the standard error of its Sharpe ratio when returns are not normal? (four decimals)

    \(\sqrt{(1 - \gamma_3\,\SR + \tfrac{\gamma_4 - 1}{4}\SR^2)/(T-1)} = \sqrt{(1 + 0.1 + 1.25 \times 0.01)/999} = \sqrt{1.1125/999} = 0.0334\) (L07).

Lessons

Eight lessons of seven study hours each. Every lesson has graded claims, runnable labs, a twelve-problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 7 hThe Selection Problem: Why the Best Backtest LiesData-snooping bias, a dependence-free bound on the expected maximum, the extreme-value approximation, holdout reuse. L02 · 7 hFamily-Wise Error: Bonferroni and HolmThe multiple-testing table, FWER, the union bound, Holm's step-down procedure and its proof. L03 · 7 hFalse Discovery Rate: Benjamini–Hochberg and Benjamini–YekutieliFDR, the BH step-up procedure and its proof, dependence, and the realized false discovery proportion. L04 · 7 hBootstrapping the Search: White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPAPerformance differentials, the stationary bootstrap, the Reality Check and the test for superior predictive ability. L05 · 7 hStepwise Multiple Testing: The Romano–Wolf StepMStep-down bootstrap testing, studentization, and why strategies must be resampled jointly. L06 · 7 hThe Factor Zoo, p-Hacking and the Bayesian ViewThe t-statistic hurdle, researcher degrees of freedom, minimum Bayes factors, and the replication evidence on both sides. L07 · 7 hDeflating the Sharpe RatioThe Sharpe ratio's variance under skewness and kurtosis, the probabilistic and deflated Sharpe ratios, and the effective number of trials. L08 · 7 hThe Probability of Backtest OverfittingBacktest overfitting as a property of selection, PBO, and combinatorially symmetric cross-validation.

The project ladder

The module ends in Atlas A8, the research-integrity module. At the master's tier the scaffolded rungs are deliberately short.

  1. Worked example

    A Benjamini–Hochberg implementation, solved and annotated with the reason for every line.

  2. Completion problem

    The probabilistic Sharpe ratio with its two key lines removed. It is graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement atlas.integrity to the A8 contract: DSR, PBO, Romano–Wolf and BH, attached to every backtest result. Autograded on your own seeded instance.

  4. Stretch

    Extend A8 with Holm, Benjamini–Yekutieli and Hansen's SPA, an effective-trials estimator, and HAC-studentized StepM, then audit a published strategy grid.

How this module is verified

Every claim carries an evidence grade and a key from the verified citation registry. Every lab's output is produced by running its code, and every lab runs both in your browser and in the program's test suite. The autograder passes the reference solution on 50 seeds, and every deliberately wrong solution the verifier wrote (ledgers/S7/mutants/) fails it. An independent verifier, never the author, checks each claim, number, proof and test and records the result in the module's verification ledger.
