Structural VAR Identification

Reduced-Form Innovations vs. Structural Shocks, The Order Condition, and Cholesky Orthogonalization

Why It Matters

Reduced-form vector autoregressions estimate correlated residuals across equations, making it impossible to answer causal questions such as: "How does an unexpected monetary policy rate hike impact equity valuations?" A reduced-form shock to interest rates is contaminated by contemporaneous equity price moves. Structural VAR identification bridges statistical correlation and economic causation by mapping correlated reduced-form innovations to mutually orthogonal, economically meaningful structural primitives.

1. Reduced-Form vs. Structural Systems

Recall from Lesson 7 that a reduced-form vector autoregression of order \(p\) is given by: \[ Y_t = c + \sum_{i=1}^p A_i Y_{t-i} + u_t, \] where \(u_t \sim \operatorname{WN}(0, \Sigma_u)\) with symmetric positive definite covariance matrix \(\Sigma_u\). Because off-diagonal elements of \(\Sigma_u\) are generally non-zero, reduced-form innovations \(u_t\) cannot be perturbed independently.

Definition 8.1 · Structural VAR Identification

Structural VAR identification is the specification of a mapping between observable reduced-form innovations \(u_t\) and unobservable structural shocks \(\varepsilon_t\): \[ u_t = B \varepsilon_t, \quad \text{or equivalently} \quad A_0 u_t = \varepsilon_t \quad (A_0 = B^{-1}), \] such that the structural shocks are mutually uncorrelated and normalized to have unit variance: \[ \E[\varepsilon_t] = 0, \quad \E[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_t^\top] = I_k, \] which implies the fundamental covariance identity: \[ \Sigma_u = \E[u_t u_t^\top] = B \E[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_t^\top] B^\top = B B^\top. \]

Theorem 8.1 · Order Condition for SVAR Exact Identification

Exact identification of the \(k \times k\) structural impact matrix \(B\) requires imposing exactly \(k(k - 1) / 2\) independent restrictions.

Proof: The sample covariance matrix \(\Sigma_u\) is symmetric and contains: \[ \frac{k(k + 1)}{2} \] unique, observable variance and covariance parameters. The structural impact matrix \(B\) contains \(k^2\) unknown parameters. Setting the observable parameters equal to the structural parameters via \(\Sigma_u = B B^\top\) provides \(k(k + 1) / 2\) non-linear equations. The deficit in equations is: \[ k^2 - \frac{k(k + 1)}{2} = \frac{2k^2 - k^2 - k}{2} = \frac{k(k - 1)}{2}. \] Therefore, at least \(k(k - 1) / 2\) additional theoretical restrictions must be imposed to achieve exact identification.

2. Cholesky Identification (Recursive Causal Ordering)

Definition 8.2 · Cholesky Identification Scheme

Sims (1980) proposed identifying structural shocks by imposing a lower-triangular structure on \(B = P\), where \(P\) is the unique lower-triangular Cholesky factor of \(\Sigma_u\) with strictly positive diagonal entries: \[ \Sigma_u = P P^\top, \quad P = \begin{bmatrix} p_{11} & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ p_{21} & p_{22} & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ p_{k1} & p_{k2} & \dots & p_{kk} \end{bmatrix}. \] Setting all elements above the main diagonal to zero (\(p_{ij} = 0\) for \(j > i\)) imposes exactly \(k(k - 1) / 2\) zero restrictions, satisfying the order condition for exact identification.

Remark 8.1 · Economic Meaning of Recursive Ordering

The lower-triangular restriction imposes a strict contemporaneous recursive ordering among variables:

  • Variable \(y_{1t}\) responds contemporaneously only to its own structural shock \(\varepsilon_{1t}\).
  • Variable \(y_{2t}\) responds contemporaneously to \(\varepsilon_{1t}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2t}\), but cannot affect \(y_{1t}\) in period \(t\).
  • Variable \(y_{kt}\) is contemporaneously affected by all \(k\) structural shocks.

This causal chain is entirely an identifying assumption rather than a testable empirical finding. Permuting the ordering of the variables in \(Y_t\) alters the Cholesky factor \(P\) and yields fundamentally different structural shocks.

3. Dynamic Propagation and Impulse Responses

Once identified, structural shocks allow researchers to trace the dynamic transmission of shocks through the system over time.

Definition 8.3 · Impulse Response Function

The impulse response function traces the expected marginal response of variable \(y_{i, t+h}\) to a one-standard-deviation structural shock \(\varepsilon_{j, t} = 1\) occurring at time \(t\), holding all other shocks constant: \[ \operatorname{IRF}_{ij}(h) = \frac{\partial Y_{t+h}}{\partial \varepsilon_{jt}} = \Psi_h B_{\cdot j}, \] where \(B_{\cdot j}\) is the \(j\)-th column of the structural impact matrix \(B\), and \(\Psi_h\) is the \(h\)-th moving average coefficient matrix obtained from the Wold vector representation \(Y_t = \sum_{h=0}^\infty \Psi_h u_{t-h}\), satisfying the recursion: \[ \Psi_h = \sum_{m=1}^{\min(h, p)} \Psi_{h-m} A_m, \quad \Psi_0 = I_k. \] At lag \(h = 0\), the contemporaneous response matrix is identically \(\Psi_0 B = B\).

4. Numerical Verification

The following lab twin verifies the Cholesky decomposition of a covariance matrix \(\Sigma_u = P P^\top\) to numerical precision, and demonstrates how changing the causal ordering alters the estimated contemporaneous structural responses.

Lab 1 · Cholesky SVAR Identification and Ordering Sensitivity

Verifies that the lower-triangular Cholesky factor satisfies P P^T = Sigma_u to 1e-12, and demonstrates that changing the recursive variable ordering produces completely different contemporaneous structural shock impacts.

"""Lab S3-L08-1: Structural VAR identification via Cholesky decomposition.

Exact check: verifies Cholesky factorization of reduced-form covariance matrix
and shows the sensitivity of contemporaneous structural impacts to variable ordering.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def main() -> None:
    # Reduced-form residual covariance matrix: Sigma_u = [[4.0, 2.0], [2.0, 5.0]]
    s11, s12, s22 = 4.0, 2.0, 5.0

    print("Reduced-form Covariance Matrix Sigma_u:")
    print(f"  [[{s11:.4f}, {s12:.4f}], [{s12:.4f}, {s22:.4f}]]")

    # Ordering 1: y1 precedes y2 (P1 lower triangular)
    p11 = math.sqrt(s11)
    p21 = s12 / p11
    p22 = math.sqrt(s22 - p21**2)

    # Verification: P1 * P1^T == Sigma_u
    rec1_11 = p11 * p11
    rec1_12 = p11 * p21
    rec1_22 = p21 * p21 + p22 * p22
    err_p1 = max(abs(rec1_11 - s11), abs(rec1_12 - s12), abs(rec1_22 - s22))

    print("\nOrdering 1 (y1 -> y2):")
    print(f"  Cholesky factor P1: [[{p11:.4f}, {0.0:.4f}], [{p21:.4f}, {p22:.4f}]]")
    print(f"  Factorization error max|P1 P1^T - Sigma_u|: {err_p1:.2e}")
    print("  Contemporaneous shock response of (y1, y2):")
    print(f"    Shock 1 -> y1: {p11:.4f}, y2: {p21:.4f}")
    print(f"    Shock 2 -> y1: {0.0:.4f}, y2: {p22:.4f}")

    # Ordering 2: y2 precedes y1 (P2 lower triangular on permuted variables)
    q11 = math.sqrt(s22)
    q21 = s12 / q11
    q22 = math.sqrt(s11 - q21**2)

    rec2_11 = q11 * q11
    rec2_12 = q11 * q21
    rec2_22 = q21 * q21 + q22 * q22
    err_p2 = max(abs(rec2_11 - s22), abs(rec2_12 - s12), abs(rec2_22 - s11))

    print("\nOrdering 2 (y2 -> y1):")
    print(f"  Cholesky factor P2: [[{q11:.4f}, {0.0:.4f}], [{q21:.4f}, {q22:.4f}]]")
    print(f"  Factorization error max|P2 P2^T - Sigma_permuted|: {err_p2:.2e}")
    print("  Contemporaneous shock response of (y1, y2):")
    print(f"    Shock 1 (y2-ordered) -> y1: {q21:.4f}, y2: {q11:.4f}")
    print(f"    Shock 2 (y1-residual) -> y1: {q22:.4f}, y2: {0.0:.4f}")

    print("\nEconomic Interpretation:")
    print(f"  Under Ordering 1, immediate impact of Shock 1 on y2 is {p21:.4f}.")
    print(f"  Under Ordering 2, immediate impact of Shock 1 on y1 is {q21:.4f}.")
    print("  Structural identification depends strictly on the non-testable ordering assumption.")

Where the Canon Breaks

Cholesky orthogonalization suffers from severe methodological and practical shortcomings:

  • Untestable Contemporaneous Exogeneity: The recursive assumption that one variable has zero instantaneous response to another (e.g., asset prices do not react instantaneously to policy rate changes) is fundamentally untestable from observational data. Both orderings produce identical likelihood functions and identical reduced-form fits.
  • Order Permutation Ambiguity: For a system of \(k\) variables, there exist \(k!\) possible recursive causal orderings. For \(k = 5\), there are \(120\) different Cholesky models, each producing different impulse responses and variance decompositions.
  • Omission of Non-Recursive Structures: True economic systems frequently exhibit simultaneous feedback (e.g., supply and demand, foreign exchange and interest rates). Cholesky forces a triangular recursive DAG that precludes instantaneous bidirectional causality. Advanced identification schemes (such as sign restrictions or Blanchard-Quah long-run neutrality restrictions) must be utilized instead.

Misconceptions

  • Wrong belief: Cholesky factorization identifies the true economic causality in the data.

    Why it is wrong: Cholesky factorization is a purely linear algebraic algorithm that triangularizes any symmetric positive definite matrix. It imposes a recursive causal chain by assumption, not by statistical estimation.

  • Wrong belief: The ordering of variables in a VAR does not matter for impulse response analysis.

    Why it is wrong: While the reduced-form VAR coefficients and residuals are invariant to variable ordering, the Cholesky factor \(P\) depends directly on variable ordering. Switching the order shifts the zero restrictions, altering the estimated structural shocks and dynamic responses.

  • Wrong belief: Any orthogonal set of shocks represents genuine structural economic shocks.

    Why it is wrong: For any orthogonal matrix \(Q\) satisfying \(Q Q^\top = I\), the matrix \(\tilde{P} = P Q\) also satisfies \(\tilde{P} \tilde{P}^\top = \Sigma_u\). There are infinitely many orthogonal shock representations; only restrictions grounded in economic theory can claim structural validity.

Practice Problems

Mechanics Tier

Given a bivariate reduced-form covariance matrix \(\Sigma_u = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix}\), compute the lower-triangular Cholesky factor \(P\) such that \(P P^\top = \Sigma_u\).

Worked Solution

Let \(P = \begin{bmatrix} p_{11} & 0 \\ p_{21} & p_{22} \end{bmatrix}\). Equating terms: 1. \(p_{11}^2 = 9 \implies p_{11} = 3\). 2. \(p_{11} p_{21} = 3 \implies 3 p_{21} = 3 \implies p_{21} = 1\). 3. \(p_{21}^2 + p_{22}^2 = 5 \implies 1^2 + p_{22}^2 = 5 \implies p_{22}^2 = 4 \implies p_{22} = 2\). Thus, \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\).

In a 4-variable VAR system, how many zero restrictions must be imposed on the structural impact matrix \(B\) to achieve exact identification?

Worked Solution

Applying the order condition formula from Theorem 8.1: \[ \frac{k(k - 1)}{2} = \frac{4(4 - 1)}{2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6. \] Exactly 6 independent zero restrictions are required to achieve exact identification.

Using the Cholesky factor \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\) from Problem 1, compute the contemporaneous response of \(y_{2t}\) to a unit structural shock \(\varepsilon_{1t} = 1\).

Worked Solution

Contemporaneously (\(h = 0\)), \(u_t = P \varepsilon_t\). Setting \(\varepsilon_t = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\): \[ u_t = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \] The contemporaneous response of \(y_{2t}\) is \(p_{21} = 1\).

Proof Tier

Prove that for any orthogonal matrix \(Q \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\) satisfying \(Q Q^\top = I_k\), the rotated matrix \(B^* = P Q\) satisfies the covariance factorization \(B^* (B^*)^\top = \Sigma_u\).

Worked Solution

Starting with \(B^* = P Q\), take the product with its transpose: \[ B^* (B^*)^\top = (P Q) (P Q)^\top = P Q Q^\top P^\top = P I_k P^\top = P P^\top. \] Since \(P P^\top = \Sigma_u\), it follows directly that \(B^* (B^*)^\top = \Sigma_u\). This proves that the reduced-form covariance matrix can only identify the structural impact matrix up to an arbitrary orthogonal rotation \(Q\).

Show why the order condition \(k(k - 1) / 2\) is a necessary but not sufficient condition for identification (rank condition failure).

Worked Solution

The order condition counts equations and unknowns, ensuring that the number of free parameters equals the number of independent constraints. However, if the imposed restrictions are linearly dependent or fail to fix the orientation of specific equations, the Jacobian matrix of the parameter transformation has reduced rank. In such cases, multiple isolated solutions or continuous manifolds of solutions exist, causing global identification failure.

Prove that if \(\Sigma_u\) is positive definite, its Cholesky factor \(P\) with positive diagonal entries is unique.

Worked Solution

Suppose there exist two lower-triangular matrices \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) with strictly positive diagonal entries such that \(P_1 P_1^\top = P_2 P_2^\top = \Sigma_u\). Since \(\Sigma_u\) is positive definite, both matrices are invertible. Rearranging gives \(P_2^{-1} P_1 = P_2^\top (P_1^\top)^{-1} = (P_1^{-1} P_2)^\top\). The left side is lower-triangular, while the right side is upper-triangular. Thus, \(P_2^{-1} P_1\) must be a diagonal matrix \(D\). Then \(D = D^{-1} \implies D^2 = I\). Because diagonal elements of \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) are strictly positive, all diagonal entries of \(D\) must be \(+1\). Hence \(D = I\), proving \(P_1 = P_2\).

Empirical Tier

An analyst estimates a macro-finance VAR with policy rates \(R_t\) and stock prices \(S_t\). Under ordering \((R_t, S_t)\), rate shocks cause a drop of 1.5% in stocks. Under ordering \((S_t, R_t)\), rate shocks cause a drop of only 0.2%. Explain the empirical reason for this discrepancy.

Worked Solution

Under ordering \((R_t, S_t)\), rate shocks are allowed to immediately affect stock prices, capturing all contemporaneous covariance in the policy shock. Under ordering \((S_t, R_t)\), stock shocks are ordered first and absorb all shared contemporaneous correlation; rate shocks are restricted to have zero contemporaneous impact on equities. The 1.3% discrepancy illustrates how the ordering assumption allocates common covariance to the first-ordered variable.

In a VAR(1) with \(A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 \\ 0.2 & 0.6 \end{bmatrix}\) and Cholesky factor \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\), compute the impulse response of \(y_{2, t+1}\) to a unit shock \(\varepsilon_{1t} = 1\).

Worked Solution

The lag-1 response matrix is \(\Psi_1 B = A_1 P\): \[ A_1 P = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 \\ 0.2 & 0.6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (0.5)(2) + 0 & 0 \\ (0.2)(2) + (0.6)(1) & (0.6)(3) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 0 \\ 1.0 & 1.8 \end{bmatrix}. \] The response of \(y_{2, t+1}\) to shock \(\varepsilon_{1t} = 1\) is the \((2, 1)\) element, which is \(1.0\).

Why is Forecast Error Variance Decomposition (FEVD) sensitive to Cholesky ordering, and how does this affect risk factor attribution?

Worked Solution

FEVD attributes the \(h\)-step forecast error variance of each variable to structural shocks: \(\operatorname{MSE}(h) = \sum_{j=0}^{h-1} \Psi_j P P^\top \Psi_j^\top\). Because each column of \(P\) represents an orthogonalized shock that depends on ordering, placing an asset first guarantees that its shock accounts for 100% of its own 1-step forecast variance. Reordering shifts variance attribution across assets, making risk attribution subjective without economic justification.

Research Tier

How does sign-restriction identification (Uhlig, 2005) avoid the arbitrary recursive ordering of Cholesky factorization in structural monetary VARs?

Worked Solution

Sign restrictions sample random orthogonal rotation matrices \(Q\) (via QR decomposition of standard normal matrices) and evaluate Candidate structural matrices \(B = P Q\). Rather than imposing dogmatic zero restrictions, the algorithm retains only those rotations where impulse responses satisfy qualitative economic priors (e.g., a contractionary monetary shock must raise rates and lower prices/inflation for \(h\) quarters). This produces set-identified credible intervals rather than point-identified estimates.

Describe Blanchard and Quah's (1989) long-run restriction approach to separating permanent productivity shocks from transitory demand shocks.

Worked Solution

Blanchard and Quah impose restrictions on the long-run cumulative impulse response matrix \(C(1) = (I_k - \sum A_i)^{-1} B\). By assuming that demand shocks have zero long-run effect on output levels, the corresponding entry in \(C(1)\) is set to zero, making \(C(1)\) lower-triangular. Since \(C(1) C(1)^\top = (I - A)^{-1} \Sigma_u ((I - A)^{-1})^\top\), \(C(1)\) can be estimated by Cholesky factorization of the long-run covariance matrix, exactly identifying \(B = (I - A) C(1)\).

Why can non-fundamentalness of structural shocks occur in VAR models (e.g., news shocks and fiscal foresights), rendering SVAR identification invalid?

Worked Solution

When market participants possess information about future policies before they appear in realized data (fiscal foresight or news), the econometrician's information set is strictly smaller than the agents' information set. The structural shocks cannot be recovered from the history of observable variables because the roots of the moving average polynomial lie inside the unit circle (non-invertibility). The true shocks are non-fundamental relative to the VAR, causing reduced-form residuals to misrepresent economic innovations.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. How many free parameters must be restricted to achieve exact identification of a structural impact matrix \(B\) in a \(k\)-variable VAR?

    The covariance matrix \(\Sigma_u\) provides \(k(k+1)/2\) equations for \(k^2\) unknown parameters in \(B\). The difference \(k^2 - k(k+1)/2 = k(k-1)/2\) represents the exact identification deficit.

  2. What structural assumption is imposed by using the Cholesky factor \(P\) where \(\Sigma_u = P P^\top\)?

    The lower-triangular form of \(P\) sets upper-diagonal entries to zero, enforcing a recursive ordering where earlier variables cannot respond contemporaneously to later variables.

  3. Why can the validity of a Cholesky causal ordering not be tested from observational data?

    All \(k!\) recursive orderings are observationally equivalent in reduced form; they achieve identical maximized likelihood values and cannot be distinguished statistically.

  4. If \(\Sigma_u = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 9 \end{bmatrix}\), what is the Cholesky factor \(P\)?

    For a diagonal covariance matrix with zero cross-equation correlation, the Cholesky factor is simply the diagonal matrix of individual standard deviations \(\sqrt{4} = 2\) and \(\sqrt{9} = 3\).

  5. What is the contemporaneous impulse response matrix \(\operatorname{IRF}(0)\) equal to?

    At lag zero, \(\Psi_0 = I_k\), so the immediate impact of structural shocks \(\varepsilon_t\) on variables \(Y_t\) is \(\Psi_0 B = B\).

  6. In a 3-variable system (GDP, Inflation, Fed Funds), if Fed Funds is ordered last in a Cholesky SVAR, what does this assume?

    Ordering Fed Funds last means its equation contains non-zero entries for GDP and Inflation shocks, while GDP and Inflation equations have zeros for monetary policy shocks contemporaneously.

  7. If \(Q\) is any orthonormal matrix (\(Q Q^\top = I\)), what happens to the structural covariance \(\E[\varepsilon_t^* (\varepsilon_t^*)^\top]\) when \(\varepsilon_t^* = Q^\top \varepsilon_t\)?

    Because \(\E[\varepsilon_t^* (\varepsilon_t^*)^\top] = Q^\top \E[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_t^\top] Q = Q^\top I Q = Q^\top Q = I\), orthogonal rotations preserve unit variance and mutual orthogonality.

  8. Which identification approach uses sign restrictions on impulse responses instead of zero contemporaneous restrictions?

    Harald Uhlig (2005) introduced sign-restriction identification, evaluating rotations that satisfy qualitative direction constraints rather than imposing dogmatic zero restrictions.
