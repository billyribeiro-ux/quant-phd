Cointegration and Error Correction

Equilibrium Relationships, Common Stochastic Trends, and Error-Correction Dynamics

Why It Matters

While Lesson 4 proved that generic non-stationary regressions are spurious, economic forces often tie asset prices together. Term structures of interest rates, spot and futures prices, and foreign exchange rates frequently share common underlying stochastic drivers. Cointegration resolves the spurious regression paradox by defining stationary linear combinations of integrated series, establishing the econometric foundation for pairs trading, yield curve modeling, and dynamic error correction.

1. The Cointegration Concept

Definition 5.1 · Cointegration

Let \(Y_t = (y_{1,t}, \dots, y_{n,t})^\top\) be an \(n\)-dimensional vector of stochastic processes, where each individual component is integrated of order one, \(y_{i,t} \sim I(1)\). The components of \(Y_t\) are said to be cointegrated of order \((1, 1)\), denoted \(Y_t \sim CI(1, 1)\), if there exists a non-zero vector \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}^n\) such that the linear combination:

\[ s_t = \beta^\top Y_t = \beta_1 y_{1,t} + \dots + \beta_n y_{n,t} \]

is integrated of order zero, \(s_t \sim I(0)\). The vector \(\beta\) is termed a cointegrating vector, and the stationary process \(s_t\) is called the equilibrium spread or error.

Theorem 5.1 · Common Stochastic Trends Representation

Assumptions:

  1. Let \(Y_t\) be an \(n\)-dimensional \(I(1)\) process with \(r\) linearly independent cointegrating vectors \(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_r\) (\(0 < r < n\)).
  2. Let \(B = [\beta_1, \dots, \beta_r]^\top\) be the \(r \times n\) cointegrating matrix of full row rank \(r\).

Conclusion: The system \(Y_t\) can be represented as the sum of \(k = n - r\) common independent random walk trends \(W_t\) and stationary transitory dynamics:

\[ Y_t = Y_0 + A W_t + S_t, \]

where \(A\) is an \(n \times (n - r)\) factor loading matrix satisfying \(B A = 0\), \(W_t = W_{t-1} + \eta_t\) is a \((n - r)\)-dimensional random walk, and \(S_t \sim I(0)\) is a stationary multivariate component.

Proof

Premultiplying \(Y_t = Y_0 + A W_t + S_t\) by the cointegrating matrix \(B\) yields:

\[ B Y_t = B Y_0 + B A W_t + B S_t. \]

Since \(B A = 0\) by construction, the non-stationary stochastic trend \(W_t\) is annihilated: \(B Y_t = B Y_0 + B S_t\). Because \(S_t \sim I(0)\), \(B Y_t \sim I(0)\), producing \(r\) linearly independent stationary spreads and verifying that \(n\) integrated series are driven by exactly \(n - r\) common stochastic trends.

2. The Error-Correction Mechanism and Super-Consistency

Theorem 5.2 · Engle–Granger Representation Theorem

Assumptions: Let \(Y_t\) be a bivariate cointegrated system with \(y_t, x_t \sim I(1)\) and stationary spread \(s_t = y_t - \alpha - \beta x_t \sim I(0)\).

Conclusion: Engle and Granger (1987) proved that cointegration is mathematically equivalent to the existence of an error-correction model (ECM). The differenced variables satisfy:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta y_t &= c_y + \alpha_y (y_{t-1} - \alpha - \beta x_{t-1}) + \sum_{j=1}^p \delta_{yy,j} \Delta y_{t-j} + \sum_{j=1}^p \delta_{yx,j} \Delta x_{t-j} + \varepsilon_{yt}, \\ \Delta x_t &= c_x + \alpha_x (y_{t-1} - \alpha - \beta x_{t-1}) + \sum_{j=1}^p \delta_{xy,j} \Delta y_{t-j} + \sum_{j=1}^p \delta_{xx,j} \Delta x_{t-j} + \varepsilon_{xt}, \end{aligned} \]

where the speed-of-adjustment coefficients \((\alpha_y, \alpha_x)\) cannot both be zero. Dynamic stability requires \(\alpha_y < 0\) (or \(\alpha_x > 0\)) to pull deviations back toward the long-run equilibrium.

Theorem 5.3 · Super-Consistency of the Cointegrating OLS Estimator

Assumptions: Let \(y_t = \beta x_t + u_t\) where \(x_t \sim I(1)\), \(u_t \sim I(0)\), and \(x_t\) may be endogenous (\(\mathbb{E}[x_t u_t] \ne 0\)).

Conclusion: The OLS estimator \(\hat{\beta} = \sum x_t y_t / \sum x_t^2\) converges to the true parameter \(\beta\) at rate \(T\) (super-consistency):

\[ T(\hat{\beta} - \beta) = O_p(1). \]

Asymptotically, regressor endogeneity has zero effect on consistency because the \(I(1)\) variance of \(x_t\) dominates the stationary cross-covariance by a factor of \(T\).

Proof

The OLS error satisfies \(\hat{\beta} - \beta = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T x_t u_t}{\sum_{t=1}^T x_t^2} = \frac{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T x_t u_t}{T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^T x_t^2} \frac{1}{T}\). Under unit root asymptotics, the denominator satisfies \(T^{-2}\sum x_t^2 \Rightarrow \sigma_x^2 \int_0^1 [W(r)]^2 dr = O_p(1)\). The numerator cross-product between an \(I(1)\) process and an \(I(0)\) process satisfies \(T^{-1}\sum x_t u_t = O_p(1)\). Therefore, \(\hat{\beta} - \beta = O_p(1/T)\), converging at rate \(T\).

3. Numerical Verification: Variance Invariance and Error Correction

We generate a synthetic cointegrated pair with common random walk \(w_t\), verify sub-block variance invariance of the spread, and estimate the error-correction speed of adjustment.

Lab 1 · Cointegration Variance Invariance and ECM Speed of Adjustment

A cointegrated pair with true beta=1.50 and spread phi=0.70 exhibits non-stationary variance growth in levels across sub-blocks, stationary constant variance in the spread, and recovers an ECM adjustment coefficient alpha_e ~ -(1 - phi) = -0.30.

"""Lab S3-L05-1: Cointegration and error correction.

SIMULATED: generates a cointegrated pair from a common stochastic trend,
verifies variance stability of the spread across sub-blocks, and estimates
the error-correction speed of adjustment.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(42)
    n = 1000
    beta_true = 1.50
    phi_spread = 0.70

    # Common stochastic trend
    w = [0.0]
    for _ in range(n - 1):
        w.append(w[-1] + rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0))

    x = [w[t] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.5) for t in range(n)]
    s = [0.0]
    for _ in range(1, n):
        s.append(phi_spread * s[-1] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.8))

    y = [2.0 + beta_true * x[t] + s[t] for t in range(n)]
    spread = [y[t] - 2.0 - beta_true * x[t] for t in range(n)]

    # Sub-block variances (4 blocks of 250 observations)
    block_size = 250
    print("Sub-block Variance Comparison (4 blocks of 250):")
    print("Block | Var(x_t) [I(1)] | Var(spread_t) [I(0)]")
    print("-" * 46)
    for b in range(4):
        bx = x[b * block_size : (b + 1) * block_size]
        bs = spread[b * block_size : (b + 1) * block_size]
        mx = sum(bx) / block_size
        ms = sum(bs) / block_size
        vx = sum((v - mx) ** 2 for v in bx) / block_size
        vs = sum((v - ms) ** 2 for v in bs) / block_size
        print(f"  {b + 1}   |      {vx:6.2f}    |         {vs:.4f}")

    # Error-correction regression: Delta y_t = c + alpha_e * s_{t-1} + e_t
    dy = [y[t] - y[t - 1] for t in range(1, n)]
    s_lag = [spread[t - 1] for t in range(1, n)]
    m = n - 1
    mean_s = sum(s_lag) / m
    mean_dy = sum(dy) / m
    cov = sum((s_lag[i] - mean_s) * (dy[i] - mean_dy) for i in range(m))
    var_s = sum((s_lag[i] - mean_s) ** 2 for i in range(m))
    alpha_e = cov / var_s
    expected_alpha_e = -(1.0 - phi_spread)

    print("\nError-Correction Dynamics:")
    print(f"  Theoretical adjustment speed: {expected_alpha_e:+.4f} (-(1 - phi))")
    print(f"  Estimated adjustment speed:   {alpha_e:+.4f}")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

Cointegration provides a powerful equilibrium framework, but financial practitioners must account for structural real-world breakdowns:

  • Structural breaks in the cointegrating vector: The true hedge ratio \(\beta\) is rarely fixed over decades. Shifts in technology, regulations, or central bank regimes create structural breaks in \(\beta\). Fitting a single constant \(\beta\) across a regime shift results in non-stationary residual drift and catastrophic pairs-trading losses.
  • Second-order finite-sample bias: Although super-consistent, the OLS cointegrating estimator exhibits severe finite-sample bias if innovations \(e_t\) and \(\eta_t\) are strongly cross-correlated. Dynamic OLS (DOLS) or fully modified OLS (FM-OLS) is required to correct second-order bias in small samples.
  • Non-linear and threshold cointegration: Arbitrage forces only operate when the spread exceeds transaction costs (bid-ask spread and borrowing fees). Inside the no-arbitrage band, the spread behaves as an unconstrained random walk, requiring threshold autoregressive (TAR) cointegration models.

Misconceptions

  • Wrong belief: Cointegration is just a fancy name for high correlation between two financial assets.

    Why it is wrong: Correlation measures linear co-movement in returns over a specific window and can be high between independent random walks. Cointegration is a long-run equilibrium property: it requires a linear combination of price levels to be strictly stationary and mean-reverting.

  • Wrong belief: Two random walks can never be cointegrated.

    Why it is wrong: If two random walks share a common stochastic trend (\(y_t = w_t + \varepsilon_{1,t}\) and \(x_t = w_t + \varepsilon_{2,t}\)), their difference \(y_t - x_t = \varepsilon_{1,t} - \varepsilon_{2,t}\) is strictly stationary white noise. They are perfectly cointegrated with vector \((1, -1)\).

  • Wrong belief: Finding a cointegrated pair guarantees a profitable pairs-trading strategy.

    Why it is wrong: Cointegration guarantees eventual mean-reversion over infinite horizons. In real markets, the half-life of mean reversion may be months or years, during which capital constraints, financing costs, and adverse spread excursions can force liquidation before convergence occurs.

Practice Problems

Mechanics Tier

Two commodity price series \(y_t\) and \(x_t\) are cointegrated with normalized cointegrating vector \(\beta = (1, -1.8)^\top\). If \(x_t = 50.0\) and \(y_t = 94.0\), calculate the current equilibrium spread value \(s_t\), assuming intercept \(\alpha = 2.0\).

Worked Solution

The equilibrium spread is \(s_t = y_t - \alpha - \beta x_t = 94.0 - 2.0 - 1.8(50.0) = 92.0 - 90.0 = +2.0\). The spread is positive, indicating that \(y_t\) is currently overpriced relative to its long-run equilibrium with \(x_t\).

In an estimated bivariate ECM, the equation for \(\Delta y_t\) is \(\Delta y_t = -0.15 (y_{t-1} - 1.2 x_{t-1}) + 0.1 \Delta y_{t-1} + e_{yt}\). If the current spread \(y_{t-1} - 1.2 x_{t-1} = +4.0\), calculate the expected change in \(y_t\) due to the error-correction term.

Worked Solution

The adjustment term contributes \(\alpha_y s_{t-1} = -0.15 \times (+4.0) = -0.60\). The asset \(y_t\) is expected to decrease by 0.60 units in the next period, closing 15% of the equilibrium disequilibrium per period.

A 3-variable system \(Y_t = (y_{1,t}, y_{2,t}, y_{3,t})^\top\) has cointegrating rank \(r = 1\). How many common stochastic trends drive the system?

Worked Solution

By the Stock-Watson common trends theorem, an \(n\)-dimensional system with cointegrating rank \(r\) is driven by \(k = n - r\) common stochastic trends. For \(n = 3\) and \(r = 1\), there are \(k = 3 - 1 = 2\) independent common stochastic trends driving the system.

Proof Tier

Prove that if \(Y_t\) has cointegrating matrix \(B\), any non-singular linear transformation \(M B\) is also a valid cointegrating matrix.

Worked Solution

By definition, \(S_t = B Y_t \sim I(0)\). Let \(M\) be any \(r \times r\) non-singular invertible real matrix. Consider the transformed spread \(\tilde{S}_t = (M B) Y_t = M (B Y_t) = M S_t\). Because \(S_t\) is covariance-stationary, any linear combination of its components \(M S_t\) has finite constant variance \(M \Gamma_S(0) M^\top\) and lag-dependent autocovariances \(M \Gamma_S(h) M^\top\). Thus \(\tilde{S}_t \sim I(0)\), proving that cointegrating vectors are identifiable only up to a choice of basis.

Prove that two non-stationary series with deterministic linear trends of different slopes cannot be cointegrated without including a deterministic time trend in the cointegrating regression.

Worked Solution

Let \(y_t = \mu_y t + w_{y,t}\) and \(x_t = \mu_x t + w_{x,t}\) with random walks \(w_{y,t}, w_{x,t}\). Any linear combination \(s_t = y_t - \beta x_t = (\mu_y - \beta \mu_x)t + (w_{y,t} - \beta w_{x,t})\). Even if \(\beta\) eliminates the stochastic trend (\(w_{y,t} - \beta w_{x,t} \sim I(0)\)), the deterministic drift term \((\mu_y - \beta \mu_x)t\) persists unless \(\beta = \mu_y / \mu_x\). If the cointegrating vector differs from the trend slope ratio, \(s_t\) contains a deterministic trend, violating constant mean stationarity.

Show why the OLS estimator in a cointegrated regression has a non-Gaussian limiting distribution and exhibits second-order bias in finite samples.

Worked Solution

Writing \(T(\hat{\beta} - \beta) = \frac{T^{-1}\sum x_t u_t}{T^{-2}\sum x_t^2}\), the numerator converges to \(\int_0^1 W_x(r) dW_u(r) + \Delta_{xu}\), where \(\Delta_{xu} = \sum_{h=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}[\Delta x_{t-h} u_t]\) is the one-sided long-run cross-covariance. When contemporaneous or lagged feedback exists between the regressor innovation and the spread error, \(\Delta_{xu} \ne 0\). This nuisance parameter shifts the numerator mean away from zero, generating second-order finite-sample bias and non-standard limiting distributions.

Empirical Tier

In analyzing the crack spread between crude oil futures (\(x_t\)) and gasoline futures (\(y_t\)), an econometrician finds that \(\alpha_y = -0.25\) and \(\alpha_x = +0.02\). Interpret the economic leadership in this price discovery mechanism.

Worked Solution

The speed of adjustment \(\alpha_y = -0.25\) is large, negative, and statistically significant, meaning gasoline prices bear 93% of the burden of adjusting back to equilibrium. In contrast, \(\alpha_x = +0.02\) is small and near zero, indicating that crude oil is weakly exogenous. Crude oil acts as the primary price discovery leader, while refined gasoline prices adjust dynamically to crude shocks.

Describe how to implement Dynamic OLS (DOLS) to eliminate second-order asymptotic bias in estimating the cointegrating vector between two equity index ETFs.

Worked Solution

Stock and Watson (1993) Dynamic OLS augments the levels cointegrating regression with leads and lags of the differenced regressor: \(y_t = \alpha + \beta x_t + \sum_{k=-p}^p \theta_k \Delta x_{t-k} + v_t\). Including leads and lags orthogonalizes the error term \(v_t\) with respect to the entire history of \(x_t\) innovations, eliminating the \(\Delta_{xu}\) endogeneity bias and restoring standard Gaussian asymptotic inference for \(\hat{\beta}\).

Explain why sovereign CDS spreads and cash bond yield spreads between the same sovereign issuer frequently exhibit threshold cointegration with a non-zero band.

Worked Solution

Arbitrage between sovereign cash bonds and credit default swaps requires executing a basis trade: buying the cash bond and buying CDS protection (negative basis), or shorting the bond and selling CDS (positive basis). Bond repo borrowing costs, CDS counterparty margin requirements, and short-selling fees create a non-zero arbitrage transaction band (e.g. \(\pm 15\) basis points). Inside the band, trades are unprofitable and the basis wanders as a random walk; outside the band, arbitrage capital forces mean reversion.

Research Tier

Evaluate the Gonzalo-Granger (1995) permanent-transitory decomposition: how does it identify the dominant price discovery share in cointegrated satellite and parent exchanges?

Worked Solution

Gonzalo and Granger decompose cointegrated series into permanent components \(W_t\) and transitory components \(S_t\) by defining \(Y_t = A_1 f_t + A_2 s_t\), where the common factor \(f_t = \alpha_\perp^\top Y_t\) is linear in the orthogonal complement of the adjustment vector \(\alpha\). The permanent price discovery share of market \(i\) is proportional to \(\alpha_{\perp,i}\). If market 1 has \(\alpha_1 \approx 0\), its share is 100%, establishing that the satellite market merely reflects prices discovered on the primary venue.

Analyze how fractional cointegration relaxes the classical \(CI(1, 1)\) integer assumption in high-frequency statistical arbitrage.

Worked Solution

Classical cointegration requires both asset prices to be \(I(1)\) and the spread to be \(I(0)\) with exponential memory decay. Fractional cointegration allows \(Y_t \sim I(d)\) and spread \(s_t \sim I(d - b)\) with real \(d, b\). In high-frequency order flow, asset prices may have \(d = 1.0\) while the spread has \(d - b = 0.35\). The spread is mean-reverting (variance is bounded) but possesses hyperbolic long memory, which standard ARMA and Dickey-Fuller tests misdiagnose as non-stationary.

How does cross-currency covered interest parity (CIP) failure post-2008 alter the econometric cointegration between spot FX and forward FX contracts?

Worked Solution

Pre-2008, spot and forward exchange rates were strictly cointegrated with vector \((1, -1)\) after adjusting for policy interest differentials. Post-2008, regulatory leverage constraints (Basel III SLR) created balance sheet costs for bank dealers, generating persistent, non-zero cross-currency basis spreads (e.g. in EUR/USD and USD/JPY). The cointegrating relationship between spot and forward rates now requires including the non-stationary shadow price of balance sheet capacity as an additional cointegrating regressor.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What is the formal definition of cointegration between two time series \(y_t\) and \(x_t\)?

    Cointegration requires non-stationary \(I(1)\) variables to share a linear combination that eliminates stochastic trends, yielding an \(I(0)\) stationary spread.

  2. What does the Engle-Granger representation theorem prove regarding cointegrated systems?

    The Engle-Granger representation theorem proves the one-to-one equivalence between cointegration and dynamic error-correction models.

  3. If a 4-variable system of \(I(1)\) economic series has cointegrating rank \(r = 2\), how many common stochastic trends drive the system?

    The number of common stochastic trends is \(k = n - r\). For \(n = 4\) and \(r = 2\), \(k = 4 - 2 = 2\) common trends drive the system.

  4. What rate of convergence does the OLS estimator achieve when estimating the cointegrating vector between \(I(1)\) series (super-consistency)?

    Because the regressor variance expands at rate \(O(T^2)\) while the cross-covariance is \(O(T)\), the estimator converges at rate \(T\), known as super-consistency.

  5. In an error-correction model \(\Delta y_t = c_y + \alpha_y (y_{t-1} - \beta x_{t-1}) + \dots\), what sign must the speed-of-adjustment coefficient \(\alpha_y\) take for dynamic stability?

    A positive spread deviation (\(y_{t-1} > \beta x_{t-1}\)) requires \(\Delta y_t < 0\) to pull \(y\) downward back toward equilibrium, necessitating a negative adjustment coefficient \(\alpha_y < 0\).

  6. Why is the OLS cointegrating estimator immune to asymptotic regressor endogeneity bias?

    Endogeneity affects terms of order \(O(T)\), but the regressor variance scales at \(O(T^2)\). The ratio converges to zero as \(T \to \infty\), ensuring consistency.

  7. If the speed of adjustment in an ECM equation for \(\Delta x_t\) is \(\alpha_x = 0\), what is the econometric interpretation of variable \(x_t\)?

    When \(\alpha_x = 0\), variable \(x_t\) does not adjust to disequilibria in the cointegrating relation, making it weakly exogenous and the primary driver of common trend innovations.

  8. How does a threshold cointegration model improve upon linear cointegration for pairs trading?

    Threshold cointegration models capture realistic market microstructures where transaction costs create an inactive inner band, with mean reversion triggered only when the spread widens beyond trading friction costs.
