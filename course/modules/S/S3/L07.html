Vector Autoregressions and the Johansen Test

Multivariate Linear Dynamics, Companion Stability, Reduced-Rank Regression, and Cointegration Systems

Why It Matters

Univariate time series models treat each asset in isolation, ignoring cross-asset feedback, lead-lag relationships, and shared systemic shocks. Vector autoregressions (VAR) extend univariate ARMA models to multidimensional systems. Furthermore, when multiple integrated time series are examined together, the Engle-Granger two-step method fails if more than one cointegrating relationship exists. The Johansen procedure resolves this limitation through reduced-rank regression, determining the exact number of cointegrating vectors and providing system-wide maximum likelihood estimators.

1. Vector Autoregressions and Companion Stability

Definition 7.1 · Vector Autoregression (VAR)

Let \(Y_t = (y_{1t}, y_{2t}, \dots, y_{kt})^\top\) be a \(k \times 1\) vector of time series. A vector autoregression of order \(p\), denoted \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\), satisfies: \[ Y_t = c + \sum_{i=1}^p A_i Y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t, \] where \(c \in \mathbb{R}^k\) is a vector of intercepts, \(A_i \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\) are coefficient matrices, and \(\varepsilon_t \sim \operatorname{WN}(0, \Sigma_\varepsilon)\) is a vector white noise process with mean zero and positive definite covariance matrix \(\Sigma_\varepsilon\).

To analyze the stability and dynamic properties of a \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\), we rewrite the system into a first-order companion form \(\operatorname{VAR}(1)\) of dimension \(kp\):

Definition 7.2 · Companion Form Matrix

Defining the stacked state vector \(Z_t = [Y_t^\top, Y_{t-1}^\top, \dots, Y_{t-p+1}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{kp}\), the \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\) can be expressed as: \[ \begin{aligned} Z_t &= C + \mathbf{F} Z_{t-1} + U_t, \\ \mathbf{F} &= \begin{bmatrix} A_1 & A_2 & \dots & A_{p-1} & A_p \\ I_k & 0 & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & I_k & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & I_k & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 7.1 · Asymptotic Stability of VAR

A \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\) process \(Y_t\) is covariance stationary if and only if all eigenvalues of the companion matrix \(\mathbf{F}\) lie strictly inside the complex unit circle: \[ |\lambda_j(\mathbf{F})| < 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, \dots, kp\}. \] Equivalently, the reverse characteristic polynomial has no roots on or inside the unit circle: \[ \det\left(I_k - \sum_{i=1}^p A_i z^i\right) \neq 0 \quad \forall |z| \le 1. \]

2. Vector Error Correction Model (VECM)

When the components of \(Y_t\) are integrated of order 1, \(I(1)\), estimating a VAR in levels can lead to non-standard asymptotic distributions, while first-differencing ignores valuable long-run equilibrium relationships. The Vector Error Correction Model resolves this dichotomy.

Theorem 7.2 · VECM Algebraic Form

Any \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\) in levels can be identically rewritten in Vector Error Correction form: \[ \Delta Y_t = c + \Pi Y_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{p-1} \Gamma_i \Delta Y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t, \] where the impact matrix \(\Pi\) and short-run dynamic matrices \(\Gamma_i\) are defined by: \[ \begin{aligned} \Pi &= \sum_{j=1}^p A_j - I_k, \\ \Gamma_i &= -\sum_{j=i+1}^p A_j \quad (i = 1, \dots, p-1). \end{aligned} \]

The rank of the matrix \(\Pi \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\) governs the cointegration behavior of the system:

  • Case 1 (\(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = 0\)): \(\Pi = 0\). There are no cointegrating relationships. The variables are not cointegrated, and the system is a stationary VAR in first differences \(\Delta Y_t\).
  • Case 2 (\(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = k\)): \(\Pi\) has full rank. The original variables \(Y_t\) are covariance stationary in levels, \(I(0)\).
  • Case 3 (\(0 < \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = r < k\)): There exist \(r\) linearly independent cointegrating vectors. The matrix \(\Pi\) admits a reduced-rank factorization: \[ \Pi = \alpha \beta^\top, \] where \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times r}\) are matrices of full column rank \(r\). The columns of \(\beta\) are the cointegrating vectors (so that \(\beta^\top Y_{t-1}\) is stationary \(I(0)\)), and \(\alpha\) is the adjustment (loading) matrix governing how quickly each equation responds to disequilibrium errors.

3. The Johansen Reduced-Rank Cointegration Test

Definition 7.3 · Johansen Cointegration Test

The Johansen test is a multivariate maximum likelihood procedure for testing the cointegration rank \(r = \operatorname{rank}(\Pi)\) in a VECM system using canonical correlation analysis and reduced-rank regression.

Theorem 7.3 · Johansen Reduced-Rank Solution

Johansen (1991) proved that maximum likelihood estimates of \(\beta\) and tests of the rank \(r\) are obtained by solving the generalized eigenvalue problem: \[ |\lambda S_{11} - S_{10} S_{00}^{-1} S_{01}| = 0, \] where \(S_{00}, S_{01}, S_{10}, S_{11}\) are residual moment matrices obtained from partialing out the lagged differences \(\Delta Y_{t-1}, \dots, \Delta Y_{t-p+1}\) from \(\Delta Y_t\) (residual \(R_{0t}\)) and from \(Y_{t-1}\) (residual \(R_{1t}\)).

Sorting the resulting eigenvalues in descending order: \[ 1 > \hat{\lambda}_1 > \hat{\lambda}_2 > \dots > \hat{\lambda}_k \ge 0, \] the Johansen Trace Statistic for testing the null hypothesis \(H_0: \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) \le r\) against the alternative \(H_1: \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) > r\) is: \[ \lambda_{\text{trace}}(r) = -T \sum_{i=r+1}^k \ln(1 - \hat{\lambda}_i). \] The Johansen Maximum Eigenvalue Statistic for testing \(H_0: \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = r\) against \(H_1: \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = r+1\) is: \[ \lambda_{\max}(r) = -T \ln(1 - \hat{\lambda}_{r+1}). \]

Remark 7.1 · Non-Standard Asymptotic Critical Values

Under the null hypothesis of \(r\) cointegrating vectors, the trace and maximum eigenvalue test statistics do not follow a standard chi-squared distribution. Instead, their asymptotic distributions are non-standard functionals of standard Brownian motions, depending on the dimension \(k - r\) and whether deterministic constants and linear trends are restricted to the cointegrating space or allowed in the data. Critical values are generated numerically via simulation tables tabulating these distributions.

4. Numerical Verification

The following lab twin estimates a bivariate \(\operatorname{VAR}(1)\) system by equation-by-equation OLS, constructs the companion matrix, and evaluates companion eigenvalues against the stability boundary.

Lab 1 · VAR(1) Estimation and Companion Matrix Stability

Verifies that equation-by-equation OLS on a seeded bivariate VAR(1) system recovers true matrix parameters within sampling tolerance, and confirms that companion eigenvalues lie strictly inside the unit circle.

"""Lab S3-L07-1: Vector Autoregression estimation and companion eigenvalues.

SIMULATED: generates a bivariate VAR(1) process, estimates coefficient matrices
by equation-by-equation OLS, and computes companion eigenvalues for stability check.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def main() -> None:
    # True VAR(1) parameters: Y_t = A * Y_{t-1} + U_t
    a11, a12 = 0.50, 0.20
    a21, a22 = 0.10, 0.60

    # Companion eigenvalues analytically:
    # lambda^2 - (a11+a22)*lambda + (a11*a22 - a12*a21) = 0
    # lambda^2 - 1.1*lambda + 0.28 = 0 -> (lambda - 0.7)*(lambda - 0.4) = 0
    true_eig1 = 0.70
    true_eig2 = 0.40

    rng = random.Random(42)
    n = 1500
    y1 = [0.0]
    y2 = [0.0]

    for _ in range(n - 1):
        u1 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        u2 = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        next_y1 = a11 * y1[-1] + a12 * y2[-1] + u1
        next_y2 = a21 * y1[-1] + a22 * y2[-1] + u2
        y1.append(next_y1)
        y2.append(next_y2)

    # OLS estimation equation-by-equation
    y1_dep = y1[1:]
    y2_dep = y2[1:]
    x1 = y1[:-1]
    x2 = y2[:-1]
    m = len(y1_dep)

    s11 = sum(x1[i] * x1[i] for i in range(m))
    s12 = sum(x1[i] * x2[i] for i in range(m))
    s22 = sum(x2[i] * x2[i] for i in range(m))
    det = s11 * s22 - s12 * s12

    inv11 = s22 / det
    inv12 = -s12 / det
    inv22 = s11 / det

    # Equation 1: y1_t on y1_{t-1}, y2_{t-1}
    sy1_1 = sum(x1[i] * y1_dep[i] for i in range(m))
    sy2_1 = sum(x2[i] * y1_dep[i] for i in range(m))
    hat_a11 = inv11 * sy1_1 + inv12 * sy2_1
    hat_a12 = inv12 * sy1_1 + inv22 * sy2_1

    # Equation 2: y2_t on y1_{t-1}, y2_{t-1}
    sy1_2 = sum(x1[i] * y2_dep[i] for i in range(m))
    sy2_2 = sum(x2[i] * y2_dep[i] for i in range(m))
    hat_a21 = inv11 * sy1_2 + inv12 * sy2_2
    hat_a22 = inv12 * sy1_2 + inv22 * sy2_2

    # Estimated companion eigenvalues
    tr = hat_a11 + hat_a22
    det_hat = hat_a11 * hat_a22 - hat_a12 * hat_a21
    disc = tr * tr - 4.0 * det_hat
    hat_eig1 = (tr + math.sqrt(disc)) / 2.0
    hat_eig2 = (tr - math.sqrt(disc)) / 2.0

    print("Bivariate VAR(1) Estimation (T=1500, seed=42):")
    print(f"True Matrix A:       [[{a11:.2f}, {a12:.2f}], [{a21:.2f}, {a22:.2f}]]")
    print(f"Estimated Matrix A:  [[{hat_a11:.4f}, {hat_a12:.4f}], [{hat_a21:.4f}, {hat_a22:.4f}]]")
    print("\nCompanion Eigenvalue Analysis:")
    print(f"  True Eigenvalues:      [{true_eig1:.4f}, {true_eig2:.4f}]")
    print(f"  Estimated Eigenvalues: [{hat_eig1:.4f}, {hat_eig2:.4f}]")
    is_stable = abs(hat_eig1) < 1.0 and abs(hat_eig2) < 1.0
    print(f"  VAR Stability Check (max |lambda| < 1): {is_stable}")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

Standard VAR and VECM analyses rely on restrictive structural and numerical premises:

  • Fixed Lag Order: In empirical finance, the lag order \(p\) chosen by information criteria (AIC, BIC) is assumed constant. During liquidity cascades or market regime shifts, propagation dynamics change abruptly, causing under-lagged models to exhibit residual serial correlation.
  • Linear Dynamics and Constant Cointegrating Vectors: The cointegrating matrix \(\beta\) is assumed constant throughout the sample. Institutional structural breaks (such as changes in monetary policy, regulatory leverage caps, or algorithmic market making protocols) often cause equilibrium relationships to drift or break permanently.
  • Sensitivity to Deterministic Components: The asymptotic critical values of the Johansen test vary dramatically depending on whether a linear drift is included in the cointegrating vector or in the exterior trend. Mis-specifying the deterministic term can lead to severe over-rejection or under-rejection of the cointegration rank.

Misconceptions

  • Wrong belief: A VAR is a structural causal model.

    Why it is wrong: A standard VAR is strictly a reduced-form statistical representation. The error terms \(\varepsilon_t\) are contemporaneous linear combinations of underlying economic shocks with a non-diagonal covariance matrix \(\Sigma_\varepsilon\). Without identifying structural restrictions (such as Cholesky ordering or sign restrictions, covered in Lesson 8), impulse responses cannot be given causal interpretations.

  • Wrong belief: An impact matrix \(\Pi\) having full rank indicates cointegration.

    Why it is wrong: If \(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = k\), the system has no unit roots at all; all series \(Y_t\) are stationary \(I(0)\) in levels. Cointegration specifically requires that the series be non-stationary \(I(1)\) individually but possess stationary linear combinations, which occurs strictly when \(0 < \operatorname{rank}(\Pi) < k\).

  • Wrong belief: The Johansen test strictly requires Gaussian errors.

    Why it is wrong: Although Johansen derived the test via Gaussian maximum likelihood, the asymptotic limiting distributions of the trace and max-eigenvalue statistics hold under much broader martingale difference sequence conditions. However, in finite samples, heavy financial tails can reduce test power and distort nominal rejection rates.

Practice Problems

Mechanics Tier

Consider a bivariate \(\operatorname{VAR}(1)\) with matrix \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.0 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\). Determine the eigenvalues of \(A\) and state whether the system is asymptotically stable.

Worked Solution

Since \(A\) is upper triangular, its eigenvalues are simply its diagonal entries: \(\lambda_1 = 0.6\) and \(\lambda_2 = 0.4\). Since \(|\lambda_1| = 0.6 < 1\) and \(|\lambda_2| = 0.4 < 1\), all companion eigenvalues lie strictly inside the complex unit circle. Therefore, the bivariate VAR(1) system is asymptotically covariance stationary.

In a 3-variable system, a Johansen trace test produces eigenvalues \(\hat{\lambda}_1 = 0.15\), \(\hat{\lambda}_2 = 0.08\), and \(\hat{\lambda}_3 = 0.02\) for sample size \(T = 500\). Compute the trace test statistic \(\lambda_{\text{trace}}(1)\) for the null hypothesis \(H_0: r \le 1\).

Worked Solution

The trace statistic tests whether the remaining \(k - r\) eigenvalues are zero: \[ \lambda_{\text{trace}}(1) = -T [\ln(1 - \hat{\lambda}_2) + \ln(1 - \hat{\lambda}_3)]. \] Evaluating the logarithms: \(\ln(1 - 0.08) = \ln(0.92) \approx -0.08338\), and \(\ln(1 - 0.02) = \ln(0.98) \approx -0.02020\). Summing gives \(-0.10358\). Multiplying by \(-T = -500\) yields \(\lambda_{\text{trace}}(1) \approx -500 \times (-0.10358) = 51.79\).

For a \(\operatorname{VAR}(2)\) model with \(A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(A_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix}\), write down the explicit \(4 \times 4\) companion matrix \(\mathbf{F}\).

Worked Solution

The companion matrix stacks the coefficient matrices along the top row blocks and identity matrices below: \[ \mathbf{F} = \begin{bmatrix} A_1 & A_2 \\ I_2 & 0_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 & 0.2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 0 & 0.2 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \] This companion representation transforms the second-order system into an equivalent first-order Markovian state representation.

Proof Tier

Prove that a \(\operatorname{VAR}(1)\) model \(Y_t = A Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t\) can be rewritten in VECM form \(\Delta Y_t = \Pi Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t\) with \(\Pi = A - I_k\).

Worked Solution

Subtracting \(Y_{t-1}\) from both sides of the VAR(1) equation gives: \[ \Delta Y_t = Y_t - Y_{t-1} = A Y_{t-1} - Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t = (A - I_k) Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t. \] Defining \(\Pi \equiv A - I_k\), we obtain exactly the VECM specification \(\Delta Y_t = \Pi Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t\). If \(A\) has an eigenvalue equal to 1, then \(\det(A - I_k) = \det(\Pi) = 0\), showing that \(\Pi\) is singular with reduced rank.

Show that if \(\Pi = \alpha \beta^\top\) with \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times r}\), the cointegrating vectors \(\beta\) are only identified up to an arbitrary non-singular linear transformation \(G \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}\).

Worked Solution

Let \(G \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}\) be any invertible matrix. Define transformed parameters \(\alpha^* = \alpha G\) and \(\beta^* = \beta (G^{-1})^\top\). Then: \[ \alpha^* (\beta^*)^\top = (\alpha G) (G^{-1} \ \beta^\top) = \alpha (G G^{-1}) \beta^\top = \alpha \beta^\top = \Pi. \] Thus, \(\alpha^* (\beta^*)^\top\) generates the identical matrix \(\Pi\) and identical observational likelihood. Cointegrating vectors define the cointegrating subspace rather than unique individual basis vectors without normalization.

Prove that if all eigenvalues of the companion matrix \(\mathbf{F}\) satisfy \(|\lambda| < 1\), the infinite moving average representation \(Z_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \mathbf{F}^j U_{t-j}\) converges almost surely and in mean square.

Worked Solution

By Gelfand's formula or Jordan decomposition, if the spectral radius \(\rho(\mathbf{F}) < 1\), there exist constants \(M > 0\) and \(\delta \in (\rho(\mathbf{F}), 1)\) such that \(\|\mathbf{F}^j\| \le M \delta^j\) for all \(j \ge 0\). The variance of the sum is bounded by \(\sum_{j=0}^\infty \|\mathbf{F}^j\|^2 \|\Sigma_U\| \le M^2 \|\Sigma_U\| \sum_{j=0}^\infty \delta^{2j} = \frac{M^2 \|\Sigma_U\|}{1 - \delta^2} < \infty\). By Kolmogorov's convergence theorem, the series converges absolutely almost surely and in \(L^2\).

Empirical Tier

In a 4-currency system (AUD, CAD, EUR, GBP), you run the Johansen trace test. The trace statistics for \(r=0, 1, 2, 3\) are \(62.4, 31.8, 14.1, 3.2\), with corresponding 5% critical values \(47.8, 29.8, 15.5, 3.8\). Determine the sequential test conclusion for the cointegration rank.

Worked Solution

We test sequentially starting from \(r = 0\): 1. \(H_0: r = 0\): Test statistic \(62.4 > 47.8\). Reject null; there is at least one cointegrating vector. 2. \(H_0: r \le 1\): Test statistic \(31.8 > 29.8\). Reject null; there are at least two cointegrating vectors. 3. \(H_0: r \le 2\): Test statistic \(14.1 < 15.5\). Fail to reject null. The sequential Johansen trace test concludes that the cointegrating rank is \(r = 2\).

Why does the Engle-Granger two-step method yield biased or incomplete results when applied to a system of 4 currencies where the Johansen rank is \(r = 2\)?

Worked Solution

The Engle-Granger regression assumes a single cointegrating relationship by projecting one variable onto the other three. When \(r = 2\), the true cointegrating space is a 2-dimensional plane. OLS produces an arbitrary linear combination of the two true cointegrating vectors, depending entirely on which asset is chosen as the dependent variable. Furthermore, Engle-Granger cannot identify the second independent stationary spread, leaving half of the cointegrating relationships undetected.

Explain how equation-by-equation OLS yields efficient estimates for an unrestricted VAR, and when seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) becomes strictly necessary.

Worked Solution

When every equation in a VAR contains the exact same set of regressors (identical lagged values and constants), generalized least squares (GLS) on the stacked system simplifies algebraically to equation-by-equation OLS. By Kruskal's theorem, OLS is numerically identical to SUR. SUR becomes strictly necessary when different equations impose asymmetric parameter restrictions, such as zero constraints on specific cross-asset lags.

Research Tier

How does the "curse of dimensionality" impact high-dimensional VAR models (e.g., modeling 50 equity return series), and what regularized or Bayesian approaches address this failure?

Worked Solution

In a VAR with \(k\) variables and \(p\) lags, the number of estimated parameters is \(k + p k^2\). For \(k = 50\) and \(p = 2\), the system requires estimating \(50 + 2(2500) = 5050\) parameters. With daily data, sample size \(T \approx 1000\) results in severe over-fitting and singular covariance matrices. Quantitative finance addresses this via Minnesota Bayesian priors, Ridge/Lasso shrinkage on transition matrices, factor-augmented VARs (FAVAR), or low-rank state-space decompositions.

Why is testing cointegration in cross-asset systems (equities, bonds, commodities) sensitive to asynchronous market closing hours, and how does this affect Johansen rank estimation?

Worked Solution

Asynchronous trading hours across global markets (such as Asian equities, European debt, and US commodity futures) introduce artificial lead-lag relationships and non-synchronous observation errors. This induces spurious short-run lag dynamics in \(\Gamma_i\) and attenuates contemporaneous correlations. In the Johansen test, observation noise inflates the residual variance \(S_{00}\), reducing estimated canonical correlations \(\hat{\lambda}_i\) and frequently biasing the test toward under-estimating the true cointegration rank.

In pairs and statistical arbitrage portfolios, explain why the Johansen eigenvectors \(\beta\) may not represent minimum-variance tradable portfolios without transaction cost penalization.

Worked Solution

The Johansen procedure finds cointegrating vectors that maximize canonical correlations, which optimizes statistical mean reversion rather than trading profitability. The resulting eigenvector \(\beta\) may assign non-zero weights to illiquid assets or demand frequent portfolio rebalancing. Without incorporating a turnover penalty or transaction cost matrix into the objective function, statistically cointegrated portfolios often incur slippage and financing costs that fully erode theoretical statistical arbitrage profits.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What condition on the companion matrix \(\mathbf{F}\) guarantees that a \(\operatorname{VAR}(p)\) process is covariance stationary?

    Covariance stationarity requires that all roots of the characteristic polynomial lie outside the unit circle, which is equivalent to all eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{F}\) having absolute value strictly less than 1.

  2. In a VECM model \(\Delta Y_t = c + \Pi Y_{t-1} + \sum \Gamma_i \Delta Y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t\), what does \(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = 0\) imply?

    When \(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = 0\), \(\Pi\) is the zero matrix, eliminating the error-correction term and leaving a pure VAR in first differences \(\Delta Y_t\).

  3. If the impact matrix is decomposed as \(\Pi = \alpha \beta^\top\), what do the columns of \(\beta\) represent?

    The \(k \times r\) matrix \(\beta\) contains the cointegrating vectors, ensuring that the \(r \times 1\) vector \(\beta^\top Y_{t-1}\) is stationary \(I(0)\).

  4. Why is ordinary equation-by-equation OLS efficient for estimating an unrestricted VAR?

    By Zellner's SUR theorem and Kruskal's theorem, when all equations share identical explanatory variables, GLS is numerically identical to equation-by-equation OLS.

  5. What null hypothesis is tested by the Johansen Trace Statistic \(\lambda_{\text{trace}}(r)\)?

    The Johansen trace test tests the null hypothesis that there are at most \(r\) cointegrating vectors against the unrestricted alternative of \(k\) vectors.

  6. What does finding \(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = k\) (full rank) indicate in a system of \(k\) variables?

    Full rank \(\operatorname{rank}(\Pi) = k\) means \(\Pi\) is non-singular, which implies that the levels \(Y_t\) themselves are covariance stationary \(I(0)\).

  7. How do Johansen asymptotic critical values compare to standard statistical distributions?

    Because the test evaluates integrated \(I(1)\) processes, the asymptotic distribution of the test statistic is non-standard and derived from integrals of Brownian motions.

  8. In a bivariate \(\operatorname{VAR}(1)\) with \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix}\), the eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(\lambda_1 = 1.0\) and \(\lambda_2 = 0.5\). What is the time-series character of this system?

    The eigenvalue \(\lambda = 1.0\) corresponds to a single unit root in the system, while \(|\lambda_2| = 0.5 < 1\) provides one stationary cointegrating relationship.
