S3 · Time Series

Stationarity, ARMA, Unit Roots, Cointegration, VARs, State-Space Filtering, and Regime Switching (130 Hours · Tier M)

Course Overview

Time series econometrics forms the bedrock of quantitative asset pricing, macro-financial forecasting, and algorithmic statistical arbitrage. Financial data violate the classic cross-sectional assumption of independent and identically distributed observations: asset returns, yields, volatilities, and macroeconomic variables exhibit rich temporal dependence, non-stationary trends, and sudden structural shifts.

This module provides rigorous doctoral-level training in discrete-time stochastic processes, separating genuine dynamic relationships from spurious non-stationary artifacts. The syllabus advances systematically from weak stationarity and ARMA representation theory to cointegrated systems, structural vector autoregressions, state-space Kalman filtering, and non-linear Markov regime switching.

Learning Objectives

  1. Formulate necessary and sufficient conditions for covariance stationarity, causality, and invertibility in linear autoregressive moving-average processes.
  2. Derive the asymptotic properties of estimators under unit roots and prove why standard regression produces spurious correlation between independent random walks.
  3. Test for cointegration using Engle-Granger two-step and Johansen reduced-rank vector error-correction specifications with exact finite-sample critical values.
  4. Identify structural macroeconomic and financial shocks in vector autoregressions using recursive Cholesky and sign-restriction schemes.
  5. Implement state-space representations and the recursive Kalman filter for optimal linear state estimation and continuous parameter tracking.
  6. Estimate Hamilton Markov-switching autoregressive models to identify and date unobserved latent macroeconomic and volatility regimes.

Placement Diagnostic

Complete this diagnostic before starting the lessons. Scoring 80% or higher and demonstrating competence on all marked core skills allows fast-tracking of introductory material.

  1. Consider a discrete-time spread process modeled as an AR(1): \(s_t = 0.80 s_{t-1} + e_t\). What is its continuous mean-reversion half-life \(h^*\) in periods?

    The continuous half-life is the horizon \(h^*\) where the conditional expectation decays by half: \(\phi^{h^*} = 0.5 \implies h^* = \ln(0.5) / \ln \phi = \ln 2 / (-\ln \phi)\). For \(\phi = 0.80\), \(\ln 2 / (-\ln 0.80) \approx 0.69315 / 0.22314 \approx 3.1063\).

  2. In an Engle-Granger pairs audit, why is it fatal to test residuals using standard Dickey-Fuller critical values instead of Engle-Granger critical values?

    Because first-stage OLS optimizes the cointegrating vector to minimize residual sum of squares, the residuals \(\hat{u}_t\) are biased toward stationarity. The residual unit root distribution is shifted substantially to the left (e.g. -3.34 vs -2.86 at 5%), so using Dickey-Fuller critical values causes rampant false discoveries.

  3. From S2 (Econometrics): Which of the following conditions guarantees that the OLS estimator in \(y_t = x_t \beta + u_t\) is unbiased?

    Unbiasedness requires strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[u_t \mid X] = 0\). In time series, lagged dependent variables violate strict exogeneity, resulting in finite-sample bias even when consistency holds under predetermined regressors.

  4. From M4 (Probability): If \(X\) and \(Y\) are jointly Gaussian random vectors, what is the mathematical form of the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = y]\)?

    For jointly Gaussian random vectors, the conditional distribution is Gaussian with an affine conditional mean \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = y] = \mu_X + \Sigma_{XY} \Sigma_{YY}^{-1} (y - \mu_Y)\), which forms the foundation of the Kalman filter measurement update.

  5. From M3 (Linear Algebra): A linear system \(y_t = A y_{t-1}\) is asymptotically stable if and only if:

    Asymptotic stability of discrete-time dynamic systems requires all companion or transition matrix eigenvalues to lie strictly inside the complex unit circle (\(|\lambda| < 1\)).

Capstone Project: Pairs-Trading Audit

The module concludes with a 30-hour applied research project: Pairs-Trading Audit. The project progresses across four rigorous rungs:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked Example: Step-by-step annotation and execution of the complete econometric audit on a synthetic pair.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion Problem: Interactive implementation and verification of out-of-sample persistence calculation and decision rules.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent Project: Pure-Python econometric test suite evaluated by autograder across 50 seeds, paired with a diagnostic audit memo on Federal Reserve FX rates evaluated against a 5-criterion rubric.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Multivariate cointegration extension via the Johansen trace test on three-currency triples.
