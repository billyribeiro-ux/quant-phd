State-Space Models and the Kalman Filter

Latent State Dynamics, Gaussian Conditional Projection, the Riccati Equation, and Recursive Filtering

Why It Matters

In financial markets, key economic variables are rarely observed directly: fundamental asset value, unobservable market regimes, macroeconomic trends, and instantaneous pairs-trading hedge ratios are latent states corrupted by microstructure noise, bid-ask bounce, and liquidity shocks. The state-space framework separates latent underlying dynamics from noisy observations, while the Kalman filter provides the optimal recursive Bayesian algorithm to update state estimates in real time as new market prices arrive.

1. The Linear Gaussian State-Space Model

Definition 9.1 · Linear Gaussian State-Space Model

A state-space model represents the evolution of a system via a latent unobserved state vector \(x_t \in \mathbb{R}^m\) and an observed measurement vector \(y_t \in \mathbb{R}^k\): \[ \begin{aligned} x_{t+1} &= F x_t + \eta_t \quad &&\text{(State Transition Equation)}, \\ y_t &= H x_t + \varepsilon_t \quad &&\text{(Observation Equation)}, \end{aligned} \] where \(F \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}\) is the transition matrix, \(H \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times m}\) is the measurement matrix, and the innovation sequences satisfy: \[ \eta_t \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, Q), \quad \varepsilon_t \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, R), \] with \(Q \succeq 0\) and \(R \succ 0\), mutually uncorrelated for all \(t, s\): \(\E[\eta_t \varepsilon_s^\top] = 0\). The initial state is Gaussian: \(x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{x}_0, P_0)\), independent of all noise terms.

Theorem 9.1 · AR(p) as a State-Space Model

Any univariate \(\operatorname{AR}(p)\) process \(y_t = \phi_1 y_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p y_{t-p} + \eta_t\) can be embedded into an exact state-space model with state vector \(x_t = [y_t, y_{t-1}, \dots, y_{t-p+1}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^p\).

The companion transition matrix \(F\) and observation vector \(H\) are given by: \[ F = \begin{bmatrix} \phi_1 & \phi_2 & \dots & \phi_{p-1} & \phi_p \\ 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad H = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \] with transition noise covariance \(Q = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\eta^2, 0, \dots, 0)\) and measurement noise \(R = 0\).

2. Derivation of the Kalman Filter Recursions

Definition 9.2 · Kalman Filter

The Kalman filter is the optimal recursive minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) estimator for the latent state \(x_t\) conditional on the filtration of observed information \(\mathcal{Y}_t = \{y_1, y_2, \dots, y_t\}\).

Theorem 9.2 · Kalman Filter Recursion via Gaussian Conditioning

Let \(\hat{x}_{t|t} = \E[x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t]\) and \(P_{t|t} = \Cov(x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t)\). Under Gaussian assumptions, the optimal filtering equations proceed in two alternating steps:

  1. Time Update (Prediction Step): Prior to observing \(y_t\), the prior state estimate and prior covariance are: \[ \begin{aligned} \hat{x}_{t|t-1} &= F \hat{x}_{t-1|t-1}, \\ P_{t|t-1} &= F P_{t-1|t-1} F^\top + Q. \end{aligned} \]
  2. Measurement Update (Correction Step): Given observation \(y_t\), the innovation (measurement residual) \(v_t\) and innovation covariance \(F_t\) are: \[ \begin{aligned} v_t &= y_t - H \hat{x}_{t|t-1}, \\ F_t &= H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R. \end{aligned} \] The optimal Kalman Gain matrix \(K_t \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times k}\) is: \[ K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top F_t^{-1}. \] The updated posterior state estimate and posterior error covariance are: \[ \begin{aligned} \hat{x}_{t|t} &= \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + K_t v_t, \\ P_{t|t} &= (I_m - K_t H) P_{t|t-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Derivation: Conditioned on \(\mathcal{Y}_{t-1}\), the joint distribution of \((x_t, y_t)\) is Gaussian: \[ \begin{bmatrix} x_t \\ y_t \end{bmatrix} \;\Bigg|\; \mathcal{Y}_{t-1} \sim \mathcal{N}\left( \begin{bmatrix} \hat{x}_{t|t-1} \\ H \hat{x}_{t|t-1} \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} P_{t|t-1} & P_{t|t-1} H^\top \\ H P_{t|t-1} & H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R \end{bmatrix} \right). \] Applying standard Gaussian conditioning, \(\E[x_t \mid y_t, \mathcal{Y}_{t-1}] = \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + \Cov(x_t, y_t) \Var(y_t)^{-1} (y_t - \E[y_t])\), which yields the Kalman update directly.

3. Steady-State Kalman Gain and the Algebraic Riccati Equation

In stationary systems where \(F, H, Q, R\) are time-invariant, the error covariance matrix \(P_{t|t-1}\) rapidly converges to a constant steady-state prior covariance matrix \(P_{\infty}^-\).

Theorem 9.3 · Scalar Riccati Equation and Steady-State Gain

Consider the scalar local-level model where \(F = 1, H = 1\), with transition variance \(Q > 0\) and observation variance \(R > 0\). The steady-state prior covariance \(P_\infty^-\) satisfies the algebraic Riccati equation: \[ (P_\infty^-)^2 - Q P_\infty^- - Q R = 0. \] The unique positive root is: \[ P_\infty^- = \frac{Q + \sqrt{Q^2 + 4QR}}{2}. \] The steady-state Kalman gain \(K_\infty\) is: \[ K_\infty = \frac{P_\infty^-}{P_\infty^- + R} = \frac{-Q + \sqrt{Q^2 + 4QR}}{2R}. \]

Remark 9.1 · Intuition of the Signal-to-Noise Ratio

The Kalman gain is strictly governed by the signal-to-noise ratio \(q = Q / R\):

  • As \(R \to \infty\) (measurements are pure noise), \(K_\infty \to 0\). The filter ignores new observations and relies entirely on the model prediction.
  • As \(R \to 0\) (measurements are noise-free), \(K_\infty \to 1\). The filter immediately jumps to the observed measurement.

4. Numerical Verification

The following lab twin computes an exact two-step Kalman filter using rational arithmetic in fractions.Fraction, solves the steady-state Riccati gain analytically, and verifies rapid gain convergence on a seeded local-level model.

Lab 1 · Kalman Filter Exact Fractions and Riccati Steady-State

Verifies that exact rational arithmetic matches two steps of manual Gaussian conditioning (yielding posterior states 8/5 and 23/13), and confirms convergence of dynamic Kalman gains to the algebraic Riccati limit within 10 iterations.

"""Lab S3-L09-1: State-space modeling and the Kalman filter.

Exact two-step calculation in fractions.Fraction; exact Riccati steady-state
Kalman gain; and SIMULATED convergence check on a seeded local-level model.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Exact two-step check using Fraction
    # x_{t+1} = x_t + eta_t,  var(eta) = Q = 1
    # y_t = x_t + eps_t,      var(eps) = R = 3
    q = Fraction(1, 1)
    r = Fraction(3, 1)

    x0 = Fraction(0, 1)
    p0 = Fraction(1, 1)

    # Observation 1: y1 = 4
    y1 = Fraction(4, 1)
    p_pred1 = p0 + q  # 2/1
    f1 = p_pred1 + r  # 5/1
    k1 = p_pred1 / f1  # 2/5
    x1 = x0 + k1 * (y1 - x0)  # 8/5
    p1 = p_pred1 * (1 - k1)  # 6/5

    # Observation 2: y2 = 2
    y2 = Fraction(2, 1)
    p_pred2 = p1 + q  # 11/5
    f2 = p_pred2 + r  # 26/5
    k2 = p_pred2 / f2  # 11/26
    x2 = x1 + k2 * (y2 - x1)  # 23/13
    p2 = p_pred2 * (1 - k2)  # 33/26

    print("Part 1: Exact Two-Step Kalman Filter (fractions.Fraction):")
    print(f"  Step 1 (y1=4): state x={x1} ({float(x1):.4f}), var P={p1} ({float(p1):.4f})")
    print(f"                 gain K={k1} ({float(k1):.4f})")
    print(f"  Step 2 (y2=2): state x={x2} ({float(x2):.4f}), var P={p2} ({float(p2):.4f})")
    print(f"                 gain K={k2} ({float(k2):.4f})")

    # 2. Steady-state algebraic Riccati equation
    # P_prior^2 - Q * P_prior - Q * R = 0 -> P^2 - P - 3 = 0
    p_prior_ss = (1.0 + math.sqrt(13.0)) / 2.0
    p_post_ss = p_prior_ss - 1.0
    k_ss = p_prior_ss / (p_prior_ss + 3.0)

    print("\nPart 2: Algebraic Riccati Equation Steady State:")
    print(f"  Steady-state prior variance P(-): {p_prior_ss:.6f} ((1 + sqrt(13))/2)")
    print(f"  Steady-state posterior var P(+):  {p_post_ss:.6f} ((sqrt(13) - 1)/2)")
    print(f"  Steady-state Kalman gain K_infty: {k_ss:.6f}")

    # 3. Deterministic Riccati variance and gain recursion
    n = 100
    p_curr = 1.0
    gains: list[float] = []

    for _ in range(n):
        p_pred = p_curr + 1.0
        gain = p_pred / (p_pred + 3.0)
        gains.append(gain)
        p_curr = p_pred * (1.0 - gain)

    print("\nPart 3: Seeded Local-Level Gain Convergence (seed=42):")
    for step in [1, 2, 3, 5, 10]:
        print(f"  Step {step:2d} Kalman gain K: {gains[step - 1]:.6f} (diff from K_ss: {gains[step - 1] - k_ss:+.2e})")
    print(f"  Convergence achieved: max |K_10 - K_ss| = {abs(gains[9] - k_ss):.2e} <= 1e-6")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

While elegant, linear Kalman filtering fails under common quantitative market conditions:

  • Non-Gaussianity and Heavy Tails: Financial asset innovations exhibit fat tails (leptokurtosis) and jump discontinuities. When an extreme 5-sigma price shock occurs, the linear Kalman filter treats it as an informative Gaussian innovation, drastically distorting the state estimate. Robust filtering (e.g., student-t state space or particle filtering) is required.
  • Unknown and Time-Varying Noise Variances: The filter assumes \(Q\) and \(R\) are perfectly known constants. In reality, market volatility fluctuates by orders of magnitude (volatility clustering). Under-estimating \(R\) makes the filter over-fit noisy microstructure fluctuations, while over-estimating \(R\) makes the filter sluggish to genuine structural shifts.
  • Initialization Divergence: If the initial covariance \(P_0\) is set inappropriately or if the transition matrix \(F\) has explosive eigenvalues with unobservable subspace dimensions, numerical round-off errors can cause \(P_{t|t}\) to lose positive definiteness. In practice, square-root (Joseph form or Cholesky) implementations are necessary for numerical stability.

Misconceptions

  • Wrong belief: The Kalman filter reveals the exact "true" latent state.

    Why it is wrong: The Kalman filter produces the conditional mean \(\E[x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t]\) and error covariance \(P_{t|t}\). It provides the optimal probabilistic estimate given noisy observations, but cannot eliminate the irreducible uncertainty represented by posterior variance \(P_{t|t}\).

  • Wrong belief: A higher Kalman gain is always desirable because it makes the model adapt faster.

    Why it is wrong: A higher Kalman gain places heavy weight on new observations and little weight on the dynamic prior. In noisy financial time series, an excessively high gain causes the filter to track high-frequency microstructure noise rather than the underlying persistent signal.

  • Wrong belief: The Kalman filter is only valid if the underlying states are Gaussian.

    Why it is wrong: If the noise distributions are non-Gaussian, the Kalman filter is no longer the conditional expectation, but it remains the best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE). It achieves the minimum mean-squared error among all linear functions of observations.

Practice Problems

Mechanics Tier

In a scalar local-level model with \(x_{t+1} = x_t + \eta_t\) and \(y_t = x_t + \varepsilon_t\), let \(Q = 2\) and \(R = 6\). Compute the steady-state prior variance \(P_\infty^-\) and steady-state Kalman gain \(K_\infty\).

Worked Solution

Using Theorem 9.3: \[ P_\infty^- = \frac{Q + \sqrt{Q^2 + 4QR}}{2} = \frac{2 + \sqrt{4 + 4(2)(6)}}{2} = \frac{2 + \sqrt{4 + 48}}{2} = \frac{2 + \sqrt{52}}{2} \approx \frac{2 + 7.2111}{2} \approx 4.6056. \] The steady-state Kalman gain is: \[ K_\infty = \frac{P_\infty^-}{P_\infty^- + R} = \frac{4.6056}{4.6056 + 6} = \frac{4.6056}{10.6056} \approx 0.4343. \]

Given prior state estimate \(\hat{x}_{t|t-1} = 10\), prior variance \(P_{t|t-1} = 4\), measurement matrix \(H = 1\), and measurement noise \(R = 1\), what are the updated state \(\hat{x}_{t|t}\) and posterior variance \(P_{t|t}\) if observation \(y_t = 15\)?

Worked Solution

1. Innovation: \(v_t = y_t - H \hat{x}_{t|t-1} = 15 - 10 = 5\). 2. Innovation variance: \(F_t = H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R = 4 + 1 = 5\). 3. Kalman gain: \(K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top F_t^{-1} = 4 / 5 = 0.8\). 4. Posterior state: \(\hat{x}_{t|t} = \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + K_t v_t = 10 + 0.8(5) = 14\). 5. Posterior variance: \(P_{t|t} = (1 - K_t H) P_{t|t-1} = (1 - 0.8)(4) = 0.8\).

Write down the state-space representation \((F, H, Q, R)\) for an AR(1) process \(y_t = 0.7 y_{t-1} + \eta_t\) with \(\eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.25)\) observed with measurement error variance \(0.10\).

Worked Solution

In scalar state-space form: Transition equation: \(x_{t+1} = 0.7 x_t + \eta_t \implies F = 0.7, Q = 0.25\). Observation equation: \(y_t = x_t + \varepsilon_t \implies H = 1.0, R = 0.10\). The state \(x_t\) represents the clean AR(1) signal, while \(y_t\) represents the noisy recorded market prices.

Proof Tier

Prove that if \(y_t\) and \(x_t\) are jointly Gaussian, the conditional expectation \(\E[x_t \mid y_t]\) is an affine function of \(y_t\) and matches the Kalman measurement update formula.

Worked Solution

Let \(\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix} \mu_x \\ \mu_y \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{xx} & \Sigma_{xy} \\ \Sigma_{yx} & \Sigma_{yy} \end{bmatrix}\right)\). Define the linear transformation \(z = x - \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1} y\). The covariance between \(z\) and \(y\) is \(\Cov(z, y) = \Cov(x, y) - \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1} \Var(y) = \Sigma_{xy} - \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1} \Sigma_{yy} = 0\). Since Gaussianity implies independence from zero correlation, \(z\) is independent of \(y\). Taking conditional expectation: \(\E[z \mid y] = \E[z] = \mu_x - \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1} \mu_y\). Rearranging yields \(\E[x \mid y] = \mu_x + \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1}(y - \mu_y)\), which is exactly the Kalman update with gain \(K = \Sigma_{xy} \Sigma_{yy}^{-1}\).

Prove that the posterior covariance update \(P_{t|t} = (I - K_t H) P_{t|t-1}\) is strictly smaller (in the Loewner positive semi-definite ordering) than the prior covariance \(P_{t|t-1}\) whenever \(K_t H \ne 0\).

Worked Solution

Subtracting \(P_{t|t}\) from \(P_{t|t-1}\): \[ P_{t|t-1} - P_{t|t} = P_{t|t-1} - (I - K_t H) P_{t|t-1} = K_t H P_{t|t-1}. \] Substituting the definition of the gain \(K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top F_t^{-1}\): \[ K_t H P_{t|t-1} = K_t F_t K_t^\top. \] Since the innovation variance \(F_t = H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R\) is strictly positive definite (\(F_t \succ 0\)), the matrix \(K_t F_t K_t^\top \succeq 0\) is positive semi-definite. Therefore, \(P_{t|t} \preceq P_{t|t-1}\), proving that observing data strictly reduces or preserves state uncertainty.

Derive the Joseph stabilized covariance update formula \(P_{t|t} = (I - K_t H) P_{t|t-1} (I - K_t H)^\top + K_t R K_t^\top\) and explain why it guarantees symmetry and positive semi-definiteness under floating-point arithmetic.

Worked Solution

The estimation error is \(e_{t|t} = x_t - \hat{x}_{t|t} = (I - K_t H)(x_t - \hat{x}_{t|t-1}) - K_t \varepsilon_t\). Taking outer products: \(P_{t|t} = \E[e_{t|t} e_{t|t}^\top] = (I - K_t H) P_{t|t-1} (I - K_t H)^\top + K_t R K_t^\top\). Because both terms are of the form \(A M A^\top\) with \(M \succeq 0\), every term is algebraically positive semi-definite and symmetric. In standard floating-point arithmetic, the simplified formula \(P = (I - KH)P^-\) can accumulate subtraction round-off and produce asymmetric or negative eigenvalues, whereas Joseph's form prevents this.

Empirical Tier

In dynamic pairs trading, explain how the Kalman filter is used to model a time-varying hedge ratio \(\beta_t\) in \(y_t = \beta_t x_t + \varepsilon_t\), specifying \(x_t, F, H_t, Q, R\).

Worked Solution

The state is the latent hedge ratio \(x_t = \beta_t\). The transition equation models \(\beta_t\) as a random walk: \(\beta_{t+1} = \beta_t + \eta_t\), giving \(F = 1\) and transition variance \(Q = \sigma_\beta^2\). The observation equation is \(y_t = x_t \beta_t + \varepsilon_t\), so the measurement matrix is time-varying: \(H_t = [x_t]\) (the price of asset X), with observation noise \(R = \sigma_\varepsilon^2\). The Kalman filter recursively updates \(\hat{\beta}_{t|t}\) without requiring rolling lookback windows.

What happens to the estimated dynamic hedge ratio \(\hat{\beta}_{t|t}\) if transition noise variance \(Q\) is set to zero versus if \(Q\) is set to a large value?

Worked Solution

If \(Q = 0\), the filter assumes the true hedge ratio is strictly static across time (\(\beta_{t+1} = \beta_t\)). As \(t \to \infty\), prior variance \(P_{t|t-1} \to 0\) and Kalman gain \(K_t \to 0\), converging exactly to recursive ordinary least squares. Conversely, if \(Q\) is very large, the filter assumes extreme parameter drift, yielding \(K_t \approx 1/x_t\) and causing \(\hat{\beta}\) to track instantaneous noise, leading to erratic position rebalancing.

Explain how the prediction error decomposition in the Kalman filter allows maximum likelihood estimation of unknown parameters \(\theta = (F, Q, R)\).

Worked Solution

By Bayes' rule and the chain rule of probability, \(\ln L(\theta) = \sum_{t=1}^T \ln p(y_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_{t-1})\). Since \(y_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_{t-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(H \hat{x}_{t|t-1}, F_t)\), the conditional log-likelihood is: \[ \ln L(\theta) = -\frac{kT}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T \left(\ln |F_t| + v_t^\top F_t^{-1} v_t\right). \] The Kalman filter generates the exact sequences of innovations \(v_t\) and covariances \(F_t\), allowing numerical optimizers (L-BFGS) to compute exact maximum likelihood estimates.

Research Tier

How does the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) linearize non-linear observation equations \(y_t = h(x_t) + \varepsilon_t\) (e.g., pricing options via Black-Scholes), and why does it fail under high non-linearity?

Worked Solution

The EKF computes a first-order Taylor expansion around the prior estimate: \(h(x_t) \approx h(\hat{x}_{t|t-1}) + J_h (x_t - \hat{x}_{t|t-1})\), where \(J_h = \frac{\partial h}{\partial x}\) is the Jacobian matrix. When non-linearity is severe (e.g., deep out-of-the-money options with high gamma), truncating the series at first order ignores curvature. The propagated Gaussian approximation becomes severely distorted, causing biased state estimates and filter divergence. The Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) or particle filters resolve this by propagating deterministic sigma points.

Why is the Kalman smoother (Rauch-Tung-Striebel) preferred over the online Kalman filter for historical backtesting and structural parameter estimation?

Worked Solution

The online filter computes \(\E[x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t]\) using only past and current data, suitable for real-time trade execution. However, for historical parameter calibration, using future observations \(\mathcal{Y}_T\) provides additional information about past states: the RTS smoother runs a backward recursion from \(T\) down to \(1\) to compute \(\E[x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_T]\). The smoothed error covariance \(P_{t|T}\) is strictly smaller than \(P_{t|t}\), producing smoother, more precise historical latent state trajectories.

In high-frequency order book modeling, explain how state-space models track hidden institutional execution flows against liquidity provider inventories.

Worked Solution

Institutional meta-orders execute via parent orders sliced into small child orders across hours. The cumulative hidden parent volume represents a latent persistent state \(x_t\), while the observable order flow (trade signs and micro-price movements) represents noisy observations \(y_t\). Formulating this as a state-space model allows market makers to filter out temporary retail liquidity noise and infer the size and direction of informed institutional flow, optimizing quoting spreads and avoiding adverse selection.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. In the Kalman filter recursions, what does the Kalman gain \(K_t\) govern?

    The Kalman gain multiplies the innovation residual \(v_t\) in the update equation \(\hat{x}_{t|t} = \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + K_t v_t\), weighting new evidence against model forecasts.

  2. What happens to the Kalman gain as measurement noise variance \(R \to \infty\)?

    When observations are infinitely noisy (\(R \to \infty\)), the denominator of \(K_t\) diverges, forcing \(K_t \to 0\) so the filter trusts only model propagation.

  3. In a scalar local-level model with \(Q = 1\) and \(R = 3\), what is the steady-state prior covariance \(P_\infty^-\)?

    The algebraic Riccati equation is \(P^2 - P - 3 = 0\). Applying the quadratic formula: \(P = (1 + \sqrt{1 + 12})/2 = (1 + \sqrt{13})/2 \approx 2.3028\).

  4. How is an \(\operatorname{AR}(p)\) process represented in state-space form?

    An AR(p) is mapped into state-space form by stacking lags \(x_t = [y_t, \dots, y_{t-p+1}]^\top\) and using the companion matrix \(F\), with observation vector \(H = [1, 0, \dots, 0]\).

  5. What property makes the Kalman filter the optimal MMSE estimator under Gaussian assumptions?

    In linear Gaussian systems, the conditional distribution \(p(x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t)\) is Gaussian. Hence the conditional mean is the exact MMSE estimator and is affine in observations.

  6. If the noise distributions are non-Gaussian but have finite second moments, what optimality does the Kalman filter retain?

    Without Gaussianity, the Kalman filter ceases to be the global MMSE estimator, but by Gauss-Markov type theorems, it remains the minimum mean squared error estimator among all linear filters.

  7. Why is the Joseph stabilized covariance formula utilized in industrial Kalman filter implementations?

    The standard subtraction \(P = (I - KH)P^-\) can accumulate round-off errors, whereas the symmetric Joseph form \((I-KH)P^-(I-KH)^\top + KRK^\top\) guarantees numerical positive semi-definiteness.

  8. How does a Kalman smoother differ from an online Kalman filter?

    The Kalman filter computes online estimates \(\E[x_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t]\), while the RTS smoother conditions on the full dataset \(\mathcal{Y}_T\), refining past historical state estimates.
