Panel Fixed Effects

Within Demeaning, LSDV Equivalence via Frisch–Waugh–Lovell, and Clustered Covariance

Why It Matters

In cross-sectional and corporate finance, unobserved firm heterogeneity—such as management skill or corporate culture—often correlates with explanatory variables, creating severe omitted variable bias in pooled OLS regressions. The panel fixed effects model resolves this endogeneity by permitting each cross-sectional unit to possess an unobserved, time-invariant intercept arbitrarily correlated with regressors. By projecting data onto within-entity deviations from time averages, the within estimator absorbs all unobserved heterogeneity without requiring explicit estimation of thousands of dummy parameters. Combined with entity- and time-clustered standard errors, panel fixed effects provide the standard identification workhorse for empirical asset pricing, market microstructure, and corporate governance research.

1. The Panel Model and Unobserved Heterogeneity

Consider panel observations on \(i = 1, \dots, N\) entities across \(t = 1, \dots, T\) time periods. The one-way individual fixed effects model specifies:

\[ \begin{aligned} y_{it} = \alpha_i + x_{it}^\top \beta + \varepsilon_{it}, \end{aligned} \]

where \(y_{it} \in \R\) is the scalar outcome, \(x_{it} \in \R^K\) is a vector of time-varying explanatory variables, \(\beta \in \R^K\) is the parameter vector of primary interest, \(\alpha_i \in \R\) represents unobserved entity heterogeneity, and \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is the idiosyncratic disturbance term.

Definition 7.1 · Panel Fixed Effects

A panel fixed effects model treats the individual-specific intercepts \(\alpha_i\) as arbitrary parameters that can be correlated with the observed explanatory variables \(x_{it}\) without restriction:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Cov(\alpha_i, x_{it}) \neq \mathbf{0}. \end{aligned} \]

Under this setting, pooled ordinary least squares regression of \(y_{it}\) on \(x_{it}\) and a single common intercept yields an inconsistent estimator of \(\beta\) due to omitted variable bias from ignoring \(\alpha_i\).

Identification of \(\beta\) in the presence of arbitrary \(\alpha_i\) requires the following exogeneity condition:

Definition 7.2 · Strict Exogeneity

The idiosyncratic disturbances satisfy strict exogeneity conditional on the individual effects and the entire history and future of regressors for entity \(i\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \E[\varepsilon_{it} \mid x_{i1}, \dots, x_{iT}, \alpha_i] = 0, \end{aligned} \]

for all \(t = 1, \dots, T\). Strict exogeneity rules out feedback from past idiosyncratic shocks \(\varepsilon_{is}\) (\(s < t\)) to future regressor values \(x_{it}\), as well as contemporaneous endogeneity.

2. The Within Transformation and LSDV Equivalence

Averaging the structural equation over time for each cross-sectional entity \(i\) yields the cross-sectional time-mean equation:

\[ \begin{aligned} \bar{y}_i = \alpha_i + \bar{x}_i^\top \beta + \bar{\varepsilon}_i, \end{aligned} \]

where \(\bar{y}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T y_{it}\), \(\bar{x}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T x_{it}\), and \(\bar{\varepsilon}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \varepsilon_{it}\). Because \(\alpha_i\) is time-invariant, subtracting the time-mean equation from the original equation completely sweeps out \(\alpha_i\).

Definition 7.3 · Within Transformation

The within transformation (or entity-demeaning transformation) defines the deviations of variables from their entity-specific time averages:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{y}_{it} = y_{it} - \bar{y}_i, \quad \tilde{x}_{it} = x_{it} - \bar{x}_i. \end{aligned} \]

The transformed equation contains only time-demeaned variables and the demeaned idiosyncratic shock:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{y}_{it} = \tilde{x}_{it}^\top \beta + \tilde{\varepsilon}_{it}. \end{aligned} \]

The within estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) is the OLS estimator applied to the demeaned variables:

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \tilde{x}_{it}\tilde{x}_{it}^\top\right)^{-1} \left(\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \tilde{x}_{it}\tilde{y}_{it}\right). \end{aligned} \]
Theorem 7.1 · Frisch–Waugh–Lovell Equivalence with LSDV

Let the Least Squares Dummy Variable (LSDV) model specify regression of the stacked \(NT \times 1\) outcome vector \(y\) on the \(NT \times K\) regressor matrix \(X\) and \(N\) entity indicator variables \(D = I_N \otimes \mathbf{1}_T\):

\[ \begin{aligned} y = X \beta + D \alpha + \varepsilon. \end{aligned} \]

Under the full column rank condition \(\operatorname{rank}([X \quad D]) = K + N\), the OLS estimator of \(\beta\) from the LSDV regression is numerically identical to the within estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\).

Proof Via Projection Operators

By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, the OLS slope vector \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{LSDV}}\) in the partitioned regression \(y = X\beta + D\alpha + \varepsilon\) equals the slope from regressing \(M_D y\) on \(M_D X\), where \(M_D\) is the orthogonal projection matrix:

\[ \begin{aligned} M_D = I_{NT} - D (D^\top D)^{-1} D^\top. \end{aligned} \]

Observe that \(D^\top D = T \cdot I_N\), so \((D^\top D)^{-1} = \frac{1}{T} I_N\). Therefore:

\[ \begin{aligned} D (D^\top D)^{-1} D^\top &= (I_N \otimes \mathbf{1}_T) \left(\frac{1}{T} I_N\right) (I_N \otimes \mathbf{1}_T^\top) \\ &= I_N \otimes \left(\frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top\right). \end{aligned} \]

Subtracting from \(I_{NT} = I_N \otimes I_T\) gives the block-diagonal projection matrix:

\[ \begin{aligned} M_D = I_N \otimes \left(I_T - \frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top\right) = I_N \otimes M_T. \end{aligned} \]

For any entity \(i\), multiplying an entity's \(T\)-vector by \(M_T\) computes \((M_T z_i)_t = z_{it} - \bar{z}_i\). Hence \(M_D y = \tilde{y}\) and \(M_D X = \tilde{X}\). The FWL slope is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat{\beta}_{\text{LSDV}} = (X^\top M_D X)^{-1} X^\top M_D y = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1} \tilde{X}^\top \tilde{y} = \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}. \end{aligned} \]
Remark 7.1 · Residual Degrees of Freedom

Although the within transformation avoids inverting an \(NT \times (N + K)\) matrix, the statistical degrees of freedom must explicitly account for the \(N\) estimated individual effects \(\alpha_i\). The unbiased residual variance estimator is:

\[ \begin{aligned} s_{\text{FE}}^2 = \frac{1}{NT - N - K} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \hat{\varepsilon}_{it}^2, \end{aligned} \]

where \(\hat{\varepsilon}_{it} = \tilde{y}_{it} - \tilde{x}_{it}^\top \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\). Using the naive OLS formula \(\frac{1}{NT - K}\) without subtracting \(N\) leads to an artificially deflated variance estimate that understates standard errors by a factor of \(\sqrt{\frac{NT - N - K}{NT - K}}\).

3. Clustered Standard Errors in Panel Settings

In empirical finance, panel disturbances are rarely independent across time for a given firm. Unmodeled persistence in firm performance causes serial correlation in \(\varepsilon_{it}\). Furthermore, financial ratios and regressors (such as leverage, book-to-market, or firm size) are highly persistent across consecutive quarters.

Theorem 7.2 · Entity-Clustered Covariance Estimator

Petersen demonstrated that when regressors and errors are serially correlated within entity clusters, naive OLS and White standard errors are severely biased downward, overstating t-statistics by factors of 2 to 4.

The entity-clustered standard errors covariance matrix is constructed using the sandwich formula:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Var(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}) = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N u_i u_i^\top \right] (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1}, \end{aligned} \]

where \(u_i = \sum_{t=1}^T \tilde{x}_{it} \hat{\varepsilon}_{it} \in \R^K\) is the cluster score vector for firm \(i\). A small-sample degrees-of-freedom adjustment scales this matrix by:

\[ \begin{aligned} c_{\text{adj}} = \left(\frac{N}{N - 1}\right) \left(\frac{NT - 1}{NT - N - K}\right). \end{aligned} \]

When shocks are correlated across both firms within the same time period (macro shocks) and across time within the same firm, researchers apply two-way clustering across entity and time dimensions.

Lab 1 · Panel Fixed Effects: Within Transformation vs LSDV

Under unobserved entity fixed effects correlated with persistent regressors, the within estimator and the LSDV estimator produce numerically identical point estimates within machine precision, while entity-clustered standard errors correctly account for within-entity error persistence and strictly exceed naive standard errors.

"""Lab S5-L07-1: Panel fixed effects within transformation vs LSDV regression.

SIMULATED: generates synthetic panel data with unobserved entity fixed effects correlated
with persistent regressors and serially correlated errors. Verifies exact numerical equivalence
between the within estimator and LSDV, and compares naive versus entity-clustered standard errors.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    seed = 606
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n_entities = 40
    t_periods = 10
    total_obs = n_entities * t_periods
    beta_true = 2.40

    # Unobserved entity fixed effects alpha_i
    alpha_entities = rng.normal(0.0, 1.5, size=n_entities)

    # Regressors x_it correlated with alpha_i and persistent across time
    x = np.empty((n_entities, t_periods))
    y = np.empty((n_entities, t_periods))

    rho_x = 0.70
    rho_e = 0.60
    for i in range(n_entities):
        x_curr = 0.8 * alpha_entities[i] + rng.normal(0.0, 0.8)
        e_curr = rng.normal(0.0, 0.8)
        for t in range(t_periods):
            x_curr = rho_x * x_curr + rng.normal(0.0, 0.5)
            e_curr = rho_e * e_curr + rng.normal(0.0, 0.5)
            x[i, t] = x_curr
            y[i, t] = alpha_entities[i] + beta_true * x_curr + e_curr

    # Flatten for regression
    y_flat = y.ravel()
    x_flat = x.ravel()

    # 1. Within (demeaning) estimator
    y_mean = np.mean(y, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    x_mean = np.mean(x, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    y_tilde = (y - y_mean).ravel()
    x_tilde = (x - x_mean).ravel()

    s_xx = float(np.sum(x_tilde**2))
    s_xy = float(np.sum(x_tilde * y_tilde))
    beta_within = s_xy / s_xx

    # 2. Least Squares Dummy Variable (LSDV) regression
    # Design matrix: [x_flat, D_1, ..., D_N] (total_obs, 1 + N)
    d_dummies = np.zeros((total_obs, n_entities))
    for i in range(n_entities):
        d_dummies[i * t_periods : (i + 1) * t_periods, i] = 1.0

    z_lsdv = np.column_stack([x_flat, d_dummies])
    coef_lsdv = np.linalg.lstsq(z_lsdv, y_flat, rcond=None)[0]
    beta_lsdv = float(coef_lsdv[0])

    abs_diff = abs(beta_within - beta_lsdv)

    # 3. Standard errors for within estimator
    # Residuals
    e_within = y_tilde - beta_within * x_tilde
    df_resid = total_obs - n_entities - 1  # NT - N - K
    sigma2_naive = float(np.sum(e_within**2)) / df_resid
    se_naive = np.sqrt(sigma2_naive / s_xx)

    # Entity-clustered standard errors (sandwich estimator)
    e_within_mat = e_within.reshape((n_entities, t_periods))
    x_tilde_mat = x_tilde.reshape((n_entities, t_periods))
    # Score for entity i: u_i = sum_t (x_tilde_it * e_it)
    scores = np.sum(x_tilde_mat * e_within_mat, axis=1)  # (N,)
    meat = float(np.sum(scores**2))

    # Small-sample cluster adjustment: (N / (N-1)) * ((NT - 1) / (NT - N - K))
    adj = (n_entities / (n_entities - 1)) * ((total_obs - 1) / df_resid)
    var_cluster = (1.0 / (s_xx**2)) * meat * adj
    se_cluster = np.sqrt(var_cluster)

    print("Lab S5-L07-1: Panel Fixed Effects Within Transformation vs LSDV")
    print(f"  Entities N: {n_entities}, Periods T: {t_periods}, Total obs: {total_obs} (SIMULATED, seed {seed})")
    print(f"  True slope beta: {beta_true:.4f}")
    print(f"  Within (demeaned) estimator: {beta_within:.8f}")
    print(f"  LSDV dummy-variable slope:  {beta_lsdv:.8f}")
    print(f"  Absolute difference (|Within - LSDV|): {abs_diff:.2e}")
    print(f"  FWL exact equivalence (|diff| < 1e-10): {abs_diff < 1e-10}")
    print(f"  Naive OLS standard error:     {se_naive:.6f}")
    print(f"  Entity-clustered standard error: {se_cluster:.6f}")
    print(f"  Clustered SE strictly larger than naive SE: {se_cluster > se_naive}")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The panel fixed effects within transformation breaks down completely when regressors include lagged dependent variables, known as Nickell (1981) dynamic panel bias.

Consider the autoregressive dynamic panel model:

\[ \begin{aligned} y_{it} = \alpha_i + \rho y_{i, t-1} + \varepsilon_{it}, \quad |\rho| < 1. \end{aligned} \]

Applying the within transformation yields:

\[ \begin{aligned} (y_{it} - \bar{y}_i) = \rho (y_{i, t-1} - \bar{y}_{i, -1}) + (\varepsilon_{it} - \bar{\varepsilon}_i). \end{aligned} \]

Observe that the demeaned regressor \((y_{i, t-1} - \bar{y}_{i, -1})\) is correlated with the demeaned error \((\varepsilon_{it} - \bar{\varepsilon}_i)\) by construction! Specifically, \(\bar{\varepsilon}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=1}^T \varepsilon_{is}\) contains \(\varepsilon_{i, t-1}\), which is directly correlated with \(y_{i, t-1}\). This mechanical correlation violates strict exogeneity, introducing an asymptotic bias in the within estimator of order \(O(1/T)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \plim_{N \to \infty} (\hat{\rho}_{\text{FE}} - \rho) = -\frac{1 + \rho}{T - 1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{T}\right)^{-1} < 0. \end{aligned} \]

In corporate finance panels where \(T\) is small (e.g. \(T = 5\) or \(10\) years), \(\hat{\rho}_{\text{FE}}\) suffers severe downward bias. In such dynamic panel settings, researchers cannot rely on the static within estimator and must transition to instrumental variable or Generalized Method of Moments difference and system estimators (such as Arellano–Bond).

Misconceptions

  1. Running pooled OLS with White heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors yields unbiased slope estimates even if unobserved firm characteristics are correlated with regressors.

    This is fundamentally false. White standard errors adjust only the standard errors for general heteroskedasticity; they do not alter the point estimates. If unobserved firm heterogeneity \(\alpha_i\) correlates with \(x_{it}\), the pooled OLS point estimator is biased and inconsistent due to omitted variable endogeneity. Only fixed effects (within transformation or first-differencing) removes \(\alpha_i\) and restores consistency.

  2. For a panel with 5,000 firms over 20 years, running a fixed effects regression requires constructing and inverting a 5,000 × 5,000 indicator dummy variable matrix.

    By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, the within transformation computes \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) purely by subtracting firm-specific time averages from each variable and running OLS on the \(K\) demeaned variables. This requires inverting only a \(K \times K\) matrix \((\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1}\), regardless of whether \(N\) is 50 or 500,000.

  3. One can include time-invariant firm characteristics, such as incorporated state or industry sector, in a firm fixed effects model.

    For any variable \(z_i\) that does not vary across time (\(z_{it} = z_i\) for all \(t\)), its time mean is \(\bar{z}_i = z_i\). The within transformation yields \(\tilde{z}_{it} = z_{it} - \bar{z}_i = 0\) for every observation. The regressor becomes identically zero and drops out due to perfect collinearity. Fixed effects absorbs all time-invariant variation, making identification of time-invariant coefficients impossible without additional structural assumptions.

Practice Problems

A firm's leverage ratio over \(T = 4\) quarters is \(x_i = [0.20, 0.25, 0.35, 0.40]^\top\). Compute the entity time mean \(\bar{x}_i\) and the demeaned vector \(\tilde{x}_i\).

Worked Solution

The time mean is \(\bar{x}_i = \frac{0.20 + 0.25 + 0.35 + 0.40}{4} = \frac{1.20}{4} = 0.30\). Subtracting \(\bar{x}_i\) from each component yields \(\tilde{x}_i = [0.20 - 0.30, 0.25 - 0.30, 0.35 - 0.30, 0.40 - 0.30]^\top = [-0.10, -0.05, +0.05, +0.10]^\top\). Notice that the elements sum to zero by construction: \(-0.10 - 0.05 + 0.05 + 0.10 = 0.00\).

A panel dataset contains \(N = 500\) firms observed over \(T = 12\) quarters with \(K = 4\) explanatory variables. Compute the total number of observations \(NT\) and the residual degrees of freedom for the within estimator.

Worked Solution

Total observations equal \(NT = 500 \times 12 = 6,000\). The residual degrees of freedom must account for both the \(K\) slopes and the \(N\) firm fixed effects: \(\text{df} = NT - N - K = 6,000 - 500 - 4 = 5,496\). Using the unadjusted OLS formula \(NT - K = 5,996\) would artificially inflate degrees of freedom and underestimate error variance.

Using the dimensions from Problem 7.1.2 (\(N = 500, T = 12, K = 4\)), calculate the ratio of the naive OLS standard error to the correct within standard error assuming independent errors.

Worked Solution

The ratio of naive to correct standard error is \(\sqrt{\frac{NT - N - K}{NT - K}} = \sqrt{\frac{5,496}{5,996}} \approx \sqrt{0.91661} \approx 0.9574\). Ignoring the \(N\) degrees of freedom consumed by the fixed effects causes the naive OLS standard error to be approximately 4.26% too small.

Let \(M_T = I_T - \frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top\). Prove that \(M_T\) is symmetric and idempotent, and compute its trace.

Worked Solution

Symmetry: \(M_T^\top = I_T^\top - \frac{1}{T} (\mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top)^\top = I_T - \frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top = M_T\). Idempotency: \(M_T M_T = (I_T - \frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top)(I_T - \frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top) = I_T - \frac{2}{T}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top + \frac{1}{T^2} \mathbf{1}(\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{1})\mathbf{1}^\top\). Since \(\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{1} = T\), the last term simplifies to \(\frac{1}{T} \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\), giving \(M_T M_T = I_T - \frac{1}{T}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top = M_T\). Trace: \(\tr(M_T) = \tr(I_T) - \frac{1}{T}\tr(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top) = T - \frac{1}{T}\tr(\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{1}) = T - \frac{T}{T} = T - 1\).

Prove that for any constant vector \(c \in \R\), \(M_T (c \mathbf{1}_T) = \mathbf{0}_T\), confirming that the within transformation eliminates entity fixed effects \(\alpha_i\).

Worked Solution

Multiplying \(M_T\) by \(c \mathbf{1}_T\) yields \(M_T (c \mathbf{1}_T) = (I_T - \frac{1}{T}\mathbf{1}_T \mathbf{1}_T^\top) (c \mathbf{1}_T) = c \mathbf{1}_T - \frac{c}{T} \mathbf{1}_T (\mathbf{1}_T^\top \mathbf{1}_T)\). Because \(\mathbf{1}_T^\top \mathbf{1}_T = T\), this equals \(c \mathbf{1}_T - c \mathbf{1}_T = \mathbf{0}_T\). Thus, any time-invariant component is completely annihilated by the projection matrix \(M_T\).

Given the within estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\), derive the formula for the estimated entity fixed effect \(\hat{\alpha}_i\) for firm \(i\).

Worked Solution

From the LSDV normal equations, the first-order condition with respect to \(\alpha_i\) is \(\sum_{t=1}^T (y_{it} - \alpha_i - x_{it}^\top \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}) = 0\). Dividing by \(T\) gives \(\bar{y}_i - \hat{\alpha}_i - \bar{x}_i^\top \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}} = 0\), which yields the closed-form estimator \(\hat{\alpha}_i = \bar{y}_i - \bar{x}_i^\top \hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\). Notice that \(\hat{\alpha}_i\) is consistent only as \(T \to \infty\), whereas \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) is consistent as \(N \to \infty\) for fixed \(T\).

In a simulated panel with \(N = 40, T = 10\) (Lab S5-L07-1), the naive OLS standard error is 0.0556 while the entity-clustered standard error is 0.0693. Calculate the percentage understatement of the naive standard error and its impact on the t-statistic.

Worked Solution

The naive standard error understates the true clustered uncertainty by \(\frac{0.0693 - 0.0556}{0.0693} \approx 19.8\%\). Consequently, the naive t-statistic \(\hat{\beta} / \operatorname{SE}_{\text{naive}}\) is inflated by a factor of \(\frac{0.0693}{0.0556} \approx 1.246\) (a 24.6% inflation), which can lead to spurious rejections of the null hypothesis at conventional significance levels.

Explain why a corporate finance regression of firm investment on Tobin's Q and cash flow requires clustering along both firm and year dimensions.

Worked Solution

Firm-level clustering accounts for serial persistence within a firm across quarters (firm effect in errors). Year-level clustering accounts for common macroeconomic shocks that affect all firms in the same calendar quarter (cross-sectional correlation in errors). As shown by Petersen, failure to cluster on the firm dimension distorts inference when regressors are persistent, and failure to cluster on the time dimension distorts inference when macroeconomic shocks induce cross-sectional residual correlation.

Compare the first-differenced estimator \(\Delta y_{it} = \Delta x_{it}^\top \beta + \Delta \varepsilon_{it}\) with the within estimator. Under what specific error structure is first-differencing more efficient than the within estimator?

Worked Solution

Both transformations eliminate the unobserved entity effect \(\alpha_i\). When idiosyncratic disturbances \(\varepsilon_{it}\) follow a random walk (\(\varepsilon_{it} = \varepsilon_{i, t-1} + \eta_{it}\) where \(\eta_{it}\) is white noise), the first difference \(\Delta \varepsilon_{it} = \eta_{it}\) is serially uncorrelated, making OLS on first differences the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE). Conversely, when \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is independently and identically distributed white noise, the within estimator is BLUE.

For an autoregressive dynamic panel with \(\rho = 0.50\) and \(T = 5\), compute the approximate asymptotic bias of the within estimator \(\plim_{N \to \infty} (\hat{\rho}_{\text{FE}} - \rho)\) using Nickell's formula.

Worked Solution

Using the Nickell formula \(\text{Bias} \approx -\frac{1 + \rho}{T - 1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{T}\right)^{-1}\), substitute \(\rho = 0.50\) and \(T = 5\): \(\text{Bias} \approx -\frac{1.50}{4} \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{-1} = -0.375 \times 1.25 = -0.46875\). The estimated autoregressive coefficient would converge to \(\plim \hat{\rho} \approx 0.50 - 0.469 = 0.031\), severely understating true persistence.

Explain why the incidental parameters problem does not corrupt consistency of \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) in linear panel models as \(N \to \infty\) with fixed \(T\), but destroys consistency in non-linear models like panel Probit.

Worked Solution

In linear models, the within transformation cleanly separates the structural slope \(\beta\) from the \(N\) nuisance parameters \(\alpha_i\) via the linear projection matrix \(M_T\), allowing consistent estimation of \(\beta\) at rate \(\sqrt{NT}\). In non-linear models (such as Probit), no linear projection can separate \(\alpha_i\) from the likelihood function. Maximum likelihood must estimate \(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N\) simultaneously; with only \(T\) observations per \(\alpha_i\), the estimation error of \(\hat{\alpha}_i\) contaminates the score function of \(\beta\), rendering \(\hat{\beta}\) inconsistent for fixed \(T\).

State the null and alternative hypotheses of the Hausman test comparing the random effects estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}}\) and the fixed effects estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\), and describe its asymptotic distribution.

Worked Solution

The null hypothesis is \(H_0: \Cov(\alpha_i, x_{it}) = \mathbf{0}\) (entity effects are uncorrelated with regressors). Under \(H_0\), \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}}\) is consistent and efficient, while \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) is consistent but inefficient. Under \(H_1: \Cov(\alpha_i, x_{it}) \neq \mathbf{0}\), \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) remains consistent, while \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}}\) is inconsistent. The test statistic \(H = (\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}})^\top [\Var(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}) - \Var(\hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}})]^{-1} (\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}})\) converges asymptotically to \(\chi^2(K)\).

Retrieval Practice

  1. What is the defining distinction between a fixed effects and a random effects panel model?

    Fixed effects places no restrictions on the correlation between unobserved heterogeneity \(\alpha_i\) and explanatory variables \(x_{it}\).

  2. By which mathematical theorem is the within transformation estimator identical to the Least Squares Dummy Variable (LSDV) estimator?

    Frisch–Waugh–Lovell guarantees that partialling out dummy variables via the projection matrix \(M_D\) produces the identical slope vector as full multivariate OLS regression.

  3. What happens if a researcher attempts to include a time-invariant regressor (such as headquarters country) in a panel fixed effects regression?

    Because \(\bar{z}_i = z_i\), the demeaned variable \(\tilde{z}_{it} = z_{it} - \bar{z}_i = 0\) has zero variance and cannot be identified.

  4. Why are naive OLS standard errors typically too small in empirical financial panels?

    As shown by Petersen, positive serial correlation in both regressors and residuals causes naive OLS formulas to substantially underestimate true sampling variance.

  5. How many degrees of freedom are consumed by individual fixed effects in a balanced panel of \(N\) entities over \(T\) periods?

    Estimating or sweeping out the \(N\) firm intercepts consumes \(N\) degrees of freedom from the total \(NT\) observations.

  6. What is Nickell dynamic panel bias?

    In dynamic panels with lagged dependent variables, \(\bar{\varepsilon}_i\) contains past shocks that correlate with lagged outcomes, violating strict exogeneity.

  7. What small-sample cluster correction is standard in Petersen-style entity clustering?

    The finite-sample cluster adjustment scales the sandwich variance by \(\frac{N}{N-1} \cdot \frac{NT-1}{NT-N-K}\) to correct for degrees of freedom lost to clusters and fixed effects.

  8. Under what condition on the sample dimensions \(N\) and \(T\) does Nickell bias become negligible in dynamic panels?

    Because the bias shrinks at rate \(1/T\), panels with long time series (large \(T\)) have negligible Nickell bias, while short \(T\) panels suffer severe bias.
