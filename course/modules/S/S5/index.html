S5 · GMM & Cross-Sectional Econometrics

Moment Conditions, Asymptotic Efficiency, Two-Pass Pricing Regressions, and Panel Invariants (100 Hours · Tier M)

Course Overview

Cross-sectional asset pricing seeks to understand why different financial assets earn different average returns. Testing multi-factor linear pricing models requires moving beyond classical single-equation ordinary least squares toward system estimators that account for cross-asset residual correlation, measurement error in generated regressors, and overidentifying moment restrictions.

This module provides rigorous doctoral-level training in modern cross-sectional econometrics and Generalized Method of Moments (GMM). Beginning with Hansen's semiparametric foundation and efficient two-step weighting, the curriculum establishes the asymptotic distribution of moment estimators and Hansen's J-test. It then analyzes the classic Fama–MacBeth two-pass regression, derives Shanken's errors-in-variables correction for generated factor loadings, formulates the exact finite-sample Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (GRS) test of portfolio efficiency, and concludes with panel fixed effects and multi-way clustering.

Learning Objectives

  1. Formulate population moment conditions and derive the asymptotic distribution of Generalized Method of Moments (GMM) estimators under stationary ergodic observations.
  2. Derive the optimal GMM weighting matrix as the inverse of the long-run moment covariance and calculate the sandwich asymptotic covariance.
  3. Implement Hansen's J-test of overidentifying restrictions and interpret its asymptotic chi-squared null distribution and empirical size properties.
  4. Estimate factor risk premia and zero-beta rates using Fama–MacBeth two-pass cross-sectional regressions.
  5. Compute the Shanken errors-in-variables correction to adjust cross-sectional standard errors for first-pass factor loading estimation error.
  6. Execute the Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (GRS) test of mean-variance efficiency and evaluate exact finite-sample F-statistics.
  7. Apply the within transformation for panel fixed effects estimation and compute cluster-robust standard errors.

This module requires mastery of foundations from S2 (Linear Regression) and S3 (Time Series Econometrics). Key competencies assumed throughout include:

  • Linear Regression & Asymptotics: Ordinary least squares, the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, HAC standard errors, and errors-in-variables bias.

Placement Diagnostic

Complete this diagnostic before starting the lessons. Scoring 80% or higher and demonstrating competence on all marked core skills allows fast-tracking of introductory material.

  1. In a two-pass Fama–MacBeth procedure, monthly cross-sectional regressions yield slope estimates \(\hat{\lambda}_t\) for \(t = 1, \dots, T_2\). How is the risk premium estimator \(\bar{\lambda}\) computed, and what is its naive standard error?

    Fama and MacBeth (1973) define the factor risk premium as the time-series mean of the monthly cross-sectional slope coefficients, with the naive standard error equal to the standard error of the sample mean: \(s(\hat{\lambda}_t)/\sqrt{T_2}\).

  2. Why is the naive Fama–MacBeth standard error for factor risk premia typically downward-biased when true loadings are replaced by estimated first-pass loadings \(\hat{\beta}_i\)?

    Shanken (1992) proved that replacing true loadings with estimated regressors adds an asymptotic variance component proportional to \(\hat{\lambda}_f^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \hat{\lambda}_f\), scaling the naive variance by a factor strictly greater than 1.

  3. Under the null hypothesis that all \(q\) moment conditions hold (\(\E[g(w_t, \theta_0)] = 0\)) in an overidentified GMM model with \(k\) parameters (\(q > k\)), what is the asymptotic distribution of Hansen's J-statistic \(J = T \bar{g}_T(\hat{\theta})^\top \hat{S}^{-1} \bar{g}_T(\hat{\theta})\)?

    Hansen (1982) showed that under optimal weighting \(W = S^{-1}\), the minimized GMM criterion multiplied by sample size \(T\) converges in distribution to a chi-squared random variable with \(q - k\) degrees of freedom.

  4. In panel data analysis, under what key identification assumption does the within (demeaning) estimator eliminate omitted variable bias from unobserved entity fixed effects?

    The within transformation subtracts time averages \(\bar{x}_i\) and \(\bar{y}_i\), which sweeps out the time-invariant effect \(\alpha_i\) regardless of its correlation with \(x_{it}\), but consistency requires strict exogeneity across all time periods.

Lesson Map

The curriculum consists of seven 10-hour lessons (70 hours total) developing the theoretical derivations and empirical implementations of cross-sectional econometric methods:

  1. Lesson 1: Moment Conditions & Generalized Method of Moments — Population moment conditions, GMM objective function, just-identified vs overidentified systems, consistency and asymptotic normality.
  2. Lesson 2: Efficient Weighting & the Sandwich Covariance — Asymptotic covariance of GMM estimators, optimal weighting matrix \(W = S^{-1}\), two-step GMM, and iterated GMM.
  3. Lesson 3: Hansen's J-Test of Overidentifying Restrictions — Specification testing, quadratic form of sample moments, degrees of freedom \(q - k\), empirical size, and local power.
  4. Lesson 4: The Fama–MacBeth Two-Pass Regression — First-pass time-series factor loadings, second-pass cross-sectional premia regressions, monthly slope distributions, and GMM representation.
  5. Lesson 5: Shanken's Errors-in-Variables Correction — Loading measurement error, the Shanken multiplier \(c = 1 + \lambda_f^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda_f\), standard error inflation, and inference reversal.
  6. Lesson 6: The Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (GRS) Test — Joint tests of zero pricing errors, exact finite-sample F-distribution, factor Sharpe ratio bounds, and mean-variance efficiency.
  7. Lesson 7: Panel Fixed Effects & Clustered Standard Errors — Within transformation, Least Squares Dummy Variable (LSDV) equivalence by FWL, strict exogeneity, and one-way and two-way cluster-robust covariance.

After completing the lessons, proceed to the 30-hour capstone project: Two-Pass Estimator with Errors-in-Variables Correction.
