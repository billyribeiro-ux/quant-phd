Numerical Methods & Scientific Computing

Real-world financial algorithms operate in finite-precision floating-point arithmetic rather than idealized continuous mathematics. Roundoff errors can trigger catastrophic cancellation, ill-conditioned matrices amplify tiny input perturbations into massive solution errors, and naive iterations fail on extreme market quotes. This module establishes the rigorous mathematical foundations of scientific computing: the IEEE 754 floating-point standard, conditioning and backward stability, bracketing and Newton root-finding, direct linear solvers, cubic-spline interpolation, numerical quadrature, pseudo-random number generation, and Monte Carlo integration with variance reduction. The module culminates in a production-grade robust implied-volatility solver stressed across 50 seeded test suites and scalable to a million inputs.

  • Prerequisites: M2 Multivariable Calculus, M3 Linear Algebra, CS1 Programming & Data Structures, and FC2 Derivatives Fundamentals. Prerequisite closure includes M1, M4, AC1, CF1, E1, FC1, and P0.1.
  • Project: Robust implied-volatility solver (autograded, 30 hours)

80 h lessons · 30 h project (27.3% of the 110 module hours)

Objectives

By the conclusion of M8, you will be able to:

  1. Derive floating-point roundoff bounds and error propagation under IEEE 754 binary64 arithmetic, and reformulate algebraic expressions to eliminate catastrophic cancellation.
  2. Compute matrix condition numbers and backward error bounds for linear systems, and distinguish ill-conditioned problems from numerically unstable algorithms.
  3. Prove convergence rates and interval contraction invariants for bisection and Newton-Raphson iterations, and construct safeguarded hybrid root-finders that prevent divergence.
  4. Implement Cholesky and LU factorizations in exact rational and floating-point arithmetic, and derive the step-size trade-off between truncation error and roundoff in finite-difference stencils.
  5. Solve tridiagonal systems for natural cubic splines to achieve \(O(h^4)\) interpolation accuracy, and evaluate composite trapezoid and Simpson quadrature rules.
  6. Generate deterministic pseudo-random sequences, verify the \(O(1/\sqrt{n})\) Monte Carlo convergence rate with standard error bounds, and apply antithetic and control-variate variance reduction.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight testing core prerequisites (M2, M3, M4, FC2), and two skill checks marked data-skill assessing the boundary checks and safeguarding logic taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (at least 8 correct). If you achieve 80% and pass both skill checks, you may skip rungs 1 and 2 directly to the independent project.

  1. What is the Taylor series expansion of \(f(x + h)\) to second order about \(x\) for a twice-differentiable function \(f\)?

    Options

    By Taylor's theorem (M2), \(f(x + h) = f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) + O(h^3)\).

  2. For the matrix \(A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}\), what is the matrix \(\infty\)-norm \(\|A\|_\infty\)? Wait, what is \(\max_i \sum_j |A_{ij}|\)?

    The row sums are \(|2| + 0 = 2\) and \(0 + |3| = 3\). The maximum row sum is 3. Note: if asked for the first row sum, it is 2; for \(\|A\|_\infty\) the answer is 3.

  3. For the diagonal matrix \(A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}\), what is the matrix \(\infty\)-norm \(\|A\|_\infty = \max_i \sum_j |a_{ij}|\)?

    \(\|A\|_\infty = \max(|2| + 0, 0 + |3|) = 3\) (M3, matrix norm).

  4. If a matrix \(A\) is symmetric positive-definite, which property is guaranteed to hold?

    Options

    A symmetric real matrix is positive-definite if and only if all its eigenvalues are strictly positive (M3, positive-definite matrices).

  5. If \(X_1, \dots, X_{100}\) are i.i.d. with variance \(\Var(X) = 0.25\), what is the standard error \(\se = \sqrt{\Var(\bar{X})}\) of the sample mean?

    \(\Var(\bar{X}) = \Var(X)/n = 0.25 / 100 = 0.0025\), so \(\se = \sqrt{0.0025} = 0.05\) (M4, central limit theorem).

  6. In the Black–Scholes model, how does European call option price behave as volatility \(\sigma \to \infty\)?

    Options

    As \(\sigma \to \infty\), \(d_1 \to \infty\) and \(d_2 \to -\infty\), so \(\Phi(d_1) \to 1\) and \(\Phi(d_2) \to 0\), giving \(C \to S\) (FC2, Black–Scholes formula).

  7. What is the theoretical lower bound for a European call option price with spot \(S = 100\), strike \(K = 90\), \(\tau = 1\), and risk-free rate \(r = 0\)?

    With \(r = 0\), the lower bound is \(\max(S - K, 0) = \max(100 - 90, 0) = 10\) (FC2, option arbitrage bounds).

  8. What is the derivative of the Black–Scholes call price with respect to volatility \(\sigma\) (vega)?

    Options

    Vega is given analytically by \(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \sigma} = S \sqrt{\tau} \phi(d_1)\) (FC2, Black–Scholes sensitivities).

  9. Skill Check 1 (Rung 1): For a European call option with spot \(S = 100\), strike \(K = 100\), \(\tau = 1\), and \(r = 0.05\), which observed market price must be rejected as an arbitrage violation?

    Options

    Under strict no-arbitrage bounds, a European call must satisfy \(C < S\). A price of 100.00 equals the spot price and violates the strict upper bound.

  10. Skill Check 2 (Rung 2): In a safeguarded Newton-Raphson iteration inside active bracket \([a, b]\), what action must be taken if the computed Newton step \(\sigma_{\text{new}} = \sigma - f(\sigma)/f'(\sigma)\) falls outside \((a, b)\)?

    Options

    The safeguarding principle guarantees contraction by reverting to bisection whenever a Newton step leaves the current bracket.

Lesson map

Eight lessons of 10 study hours each, totaling 80 lesson hours:

  1. L01 · Floating-Point Arithmetic and Cancellation 10 h

    IEEE 754 binary64 format, unit roundoff, rounding models, catastrophic cancellation, stable quadratic formulas, and two-pass sample variance.

  2. L02 · Conditioning and Stability 10 h

    Condition numbers of linear systems and scalar functions, forward and backward error analysis, and backward stability of numerical algorithms.

  3. L03 · Bracketing Root Finders and Newton's Method 10 h

    Bisection interval invariant, logarithmic step counts, Newton-Raphson quadratic convergence, failure modes, and Brent's hybrid method.

  4. L04 · Linear Solvers and Finite Differences 10 h

    LU decomposition with partial pivoting, Cholesky factorization for SPD matrices, and the truncation versus roundoff trade-off in finite difference stencils.

  5. L05 · Interpolation and Quadrature 10 h

    Natural cubic spline interpolation with tridiagonal systems, and composite trapezoid and Simpson numerical quadrature error bounds.

  6. L06 · Pseudo-Random Numbers and Monte Carlo Integration 10 h

    Deterministic PRNG state machines, Monte Carlo integration, standard error, and the empirical verification of the \(O(1/\sqrt{n})\) CLT rate.

  7. L07 · Variance Reduction: Antithetic and Control Variates 10 h

    Covariance structures of antithetic pairs, optimal control variate coefficients, correlation efficiency, and European option pricing variance reduction.

  8. L08 · Importance Sampling and Robust Inversion 10 h

    Exponential tilting for rare-event Gaussian tails, likelihood ratio weight variance, dynamic bracket expansion, and failure-region conditioning classification.
