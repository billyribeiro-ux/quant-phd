Bracketing Root Finders and Newton's Method

Solving nonlinear equations of the form \(f(x) = 0\) is the core computational task in quantitative finance: extracting implied volatilities from option prices, backing out yields to maturity from bond prices, and calibrating local volatility surfaces. Bracketing methods, such as bisection, guarantee global convergence by maintaining an invariant sign change, but converge linearly. Newton's method achieves rapid quadratic convergence near simple roots, but diverges or cycles when initialized far from the solution or near inflection points. Combining guaranteed bracketing with Newton acceleration produces robust hybrid algorithms that never fail in production.

1Bracketing methods and the bisection invariant

A root-finding problem for a continuous function \(f: [a, b] \to \R\) seeks \(x^* \in [a, b]\) such that \(f(x^*) = 0\).

Definition 1.1 (bracketing root finder)

A bracketing root finder maintains an interval \([a_k, b_k]\) satisfying the bracket condition \(f(a_k) f(b_k) \le 0\). By the Intermediate Value Theorem, continuity guarantees that at least one root lies in \([a_k, b_k]\).

Proposition 1.2 (bisection convergence and step count)

Let \(f \in C[a_0, b_0]\) with \(f(a_0) f(b_0) \le 0\). The bisection method generates a nested sequence of intervals \([a_k, b_k]\) with width \(W_k = b_k - a_k = 2^{-k} (b_0 - a_0)\) containing a root \(x^*\). To guarantee an absolute error bound \(|x_k - x^*| \le \text{tol}\), the required number of evaluations is:

\[ k = \left\lceil \log_2 \left(\frac{b_0 - a_0}{\text{tol}}\right) \right\rceil \]

Proof

At each step \(k \ge 0\), the midpoint is \(m_k = a_k + \frac{b_k - a_k}{2}\). If \(f(m_k) = 0\), \(m_k\) is an exact root. If \(f(a_k) f(m_k) < 0\), the new bracket is \([a_{k+1}, b_{k+1}] = [a_k, m_k]\); otherwise \([a_{k+1}, b_{k+1}] = [m_k, b_k]\). In both cases, \(f(a_{k+1}) f(b_{k+1}) \le 0\) and \(W_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2} W_k\). By mathematical induction, \(W_k = 2^{-k} W_0\). Since \(x^* \in [a_k, b_k]\) and the approximation is the midpoint \(x_k = m_k\), the error satisfies \(|x_k - x^*| \le \frac{1}{2} W_k = 2^{-(k+1)} W_0 \le 2^{-k} W_0\). Enforcing \(2^{-k} W_0 \le \text{tol}\) gives \(2^k \ge W_0 / \text{tol}\), or \(k \ge \log_2(W_0 / \text{tol})\). Taking the ceiling gives the exact integer step count. \(\blacksquare\)

2Newton's method and quadratic convergence

Definition 2.1 (Newton-Raphson iteration)

Given a differentiable function \(f: \R \to \R\), Newton's method constructs successive approximations from a starting guess \(x_0\) via the tangent line approximation:

\[ x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)} \]

Theorem 2.2 (quadratic convergence of Newton's method)

Let \(f \in C^2[a, b]\) and let \(x^* \in (a, b)\) be a simple root, meaning \(f(x^*) = 0\) and \(f'(x^*) \ne 0\). If \(x_0\) is chosen sufficiently close to \(x^*\), the sequence \(x_k\) converges quadratically to \(x^*\):

\[ \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{|x_{k+1} - x^*|}{|x_k - x^*|^2} = \frac{|f''(x^*)|}{2 |f'(x^*)|} \]

Proof

Expand \(f(x^*)\) about \(x_k\) using Taylor's theorem with remainder:

\[ 0 = f(x^*) = f(x_k) + f'(x_k)(x^* - x_k) + \frac{1}{2} f''(\xi_k)(x^* - x_k)^2 \]

where \(\xi_k\) lies between \(x_k\) and \(x^*\). Dividing by \(f'(x_k) \ne 0\):

\[ 0 = \frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)} + (x^* - x_k) + \frac{f''(\xi_k)}{2 f'(x_k)} (x^* - x_k)^2 \]

Rearranging using \(x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}\):

\[ x_{k+1} - x^* = \frac{f''(\xi_k)}{2 f'(x_k)} (x_k - x^*)^2 \]

Writing \(e_k = |x_k - x^*|\):

\[ e_{k+1} = \frac{|f''(\xi_k)|}{2 |f'(x_k)|} e_k^2 \]

As \(x_k \to x^*\), \(\xi_k \to x^*\) and \(f'(x_k) \to f'(x^*)\), establishing the quadratic asymptotic error constant \(M = \frac{|f''(x^*)|}{2 |f'(x^*)|}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (meaning of quadratic convergence)

Quadratic convergence implies that the number of correct significant digits roughly doubles at each iteration. If \(e_k \approx 10^{-2}\) and \(M \approx 1\), then \(e_{k+1} \approx 10^{-4}\), \(e_{k+2} \approx 10^{-8}\), and \(e_{k+3} \approx 10^{-16}\), reaching full double precision in 3 to 5 iterations.

3Failure modes and safeguarded root finding

Newton's method fails when its underlying local Taylor approximation breaks down:

  • Stationary points (\(f'(x_k) \approx 0\)): Division by near-zero sends \(x_{k+1}\) to infinity. In option pricing, when vega \(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \sigma} \to 0\), the unconstrained Newton step explodes.
  • Limit cycles: For functions with inflection points, such as \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x + 2\), the iteration can oscillate endlessly between fixed points without converging.
  • Overshoot outside physical domain: For Black–Scholes inversion, a raw Newton step can produce a negative volatility \(\sigma < 0\).

A safeguarded root finder maintains a bracket \([a_k, b_k]\) containing the root. At each iteration, a Newton step is proposed. If the proposed step lies strictly inside the bracket and contracts the interval sufficiently, it is accepted; otherwise, the algorithm falls back to bisection. Brent's method combines this safeguard with inverse quadratic interpolation for rapid superlinear convergence.

Lab 1 · Bisection step count invariant and Newton's quadratic convergence

For \(f(x) = x^2 - 2\) on \([1, 2]\), bisection requires exactly \(\lceil \log_2(1/\text{tol}) \rceil\) steps for tolerances from \(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-12}\). Newton's method starting from \(x_0 = 1.0\) converges in 5 iterations to machine precision with error ratio \(e_{k+1}/e_k^2\) converging to \(M = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.353553\).

"""Lab M8-L03-1: Bisection step count invariant and Newton's quadratic convergence.

Standard library only. Demonstrates theoretical bisection step count bound
and Newton-Raphson quadratic error recursion on f(x) = x^2 - 2.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def bisection(a: float, b: float, tol: float) -> tuple[float, int]:
    """Bisection root finder tracking iteration count."""
    fa = a * a - 2.0
    fb = b * b - 2.0
    if fa * fb > 0.0:
        raise ValueError("Root not bracketed")

    steps = 0
    while (b - a) > tol:
        steps += 1
        m = a + 0.5 * (b - a)
        fm = m * m - 2.0
        if fm == 0.0:
            return m, steps
        if fa * fm < 0.0:
            b = m
            fb = fm
        else:
            a = m
            fa = fm
    return a + 0.5 * (b - a), steps


def newton(x0: float, max_iter: int = 7) -> list[tuple[int, float, float, float]]:
    """Newton-Raphson iteration on f(x) = x^2 - 2 with error tracking."""
    root = math.sqrt(2.0)
    x = x0
    history: list[tuple[int, float, float, float]] = []

    for k in range(max_iter):
        err = abs(x - root)
        prev_err = history[-1][2] if history else 0.0
        ratio = (err / (prev_err * prev_err)) if prev_err > 1e-15 else 0.0
        history.append((k, x, err, ratio))

        # f'(x) = 2x
        fx = x * x - 2.0
        dfx = 2.0 * x
        if abs(fx) < 1e-16 or dfx == 0.0:
            break
        x = x - fx / dfx

    return history


def main() -> None:
    print("=== Part A: Bisection Step Count Invariant ===")
    a, b = 1.0, 2.0
    width = b - a

    for tol in [1e-3, 1e-6, 1e-10, 1e-12]:
        theoretical_steps = math.ceil(math.log2(width / tol))
        approx_root, actual_steps = bisection(a, b, tol)
        print(
            f"tol={tol:.0e}: theoretical={theoretical_steps:2d}, "
            f"actual={actual_steps:2d}, root={approx_root:.12f}, "
            f"match={actual_steps == theoretical_steps}"
        )

    print("\n=== Part B: Newton's Quadratic Convergence on x^2 - 2 = 0 ===")
    # f''(root) / (2 f'(root)) = 2 / (2 * 2 * sqrt(2)) = 1 / (2 sqrt(2)) ~ 0.353553
    history = newton(x0=1.0)
    print(f"Theoretical asymptotic error ratio M = f''(x*)/(2 f'(x*)) = {1.0 / (2.0 * math.sqrt(2.0)):.6f}")
    print("Iter  Estimate x_k        Absolute Error e_k  Ratio e_k / (e_{k-1})^2")
    for k, x, err, ratio in history:
        ratio_str = f"{ratio:.6f}" if k > 0 and ratio > 0.0 else "      -"
        print(f" {k:2d}   {x:.16f}  {err:.16e}    {ratio_str}")


main()

5Where the canon breaks

  • Multiple roots degrade Newton to linear convergence. If \(x^*\) has multiplicity \(m > 1\) (i.e. \(f(x^*) = f'(x^*) = 0\)), Taylor expansion shows that \(e_{k+1} \approx \frac{m-1}{m} e_k\). For a double root (\(m=2\)), error contracts only by a factor of 0.5 per iteration, slower than bisection with an evaluation cost twice as high.
  • Discontinuous functions break bisection. Bisection requires \(f(a) f(b) \le 0\) and continuity. For discontinuous functions such as \(f(x) = 1/x\), bisection on \([-1, 1]\) converges to the vertical asymptote \(x = 0\) rather than a root.

6Misconceptions

  1. “Newton's method is always faster than bisection.”

    Newton's method is faster only in an asymptotic neighborhood of a simple root. Far from the root, Newton steps can overshoot wildly, enter infinite periodic orbits, or diverge. Bisection is unconditionally reliable and often reaches a rough bracket faster than an unconstrained Newton iteration.

  2. “A root finder should terminate when \(|f(x_k)| < \text{tol}\).”

    A small residual \(|f(x_k)|\) does not imply a small error \(|x_k - x^*|\). By the mean value theorem, \(|x_k - x^*| \approx \frac{|f(x_k)|}{|f'(x^*)|}\). When the derivative is tiny (e.g. In option pricing when vega \(< 0.01\)), \(|f(x_k)| \le 10^{-12}\) can easily coexist with an error \(|x_k - x^*| > 10^{-2}\).

  3. “Secant method requires the root to be bracketed.”

    Unlike bisection or false position (regula falsi), the standard secant method \(x_{k+1} = x_k - f(x_k) \frac{x_k - x_{k-1}}{f(x_k) - f(x_{k-1})}\) does not check signs and does not preserve a bracket. It can escape the initial interval and diverge.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Bisection step count calculation

How many bisection iterations are required to find a root on \([0, 10]\) with an absolute error bound less than \(10^{-8}\)?

Solution

The initial interval width is \(W_0 = 10 - 0 = 10\). By Proposition 1.2, \(k = \lceil \log_2(W_0 / \text{tol}) \rceil = \lceil \log_2(10 / 10^{-8}) \rceil = \lceil \log_2(10^9) \rceil = \lceil 29.897 \rceil = 30\) steps. Exactly 30 bisection steps guarantee the interval width is \(10 \times 2^{-30} \approx 9.31 \times 10^{-9} < 10^{-8}\).

M2 · Newton iteration for cube root

Derive the Newton iteration formula for computing \(\sqrt[3]{R}\) by finding the root of \(f(x) = x^3 - R\).

Solution

The derivative is \(f'(x) = 3x^2\). The Newton iteration is \(x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{x_k^3 - R}{3x_k^2} = x_k - \frac{1}{3}x_k + \frac{R}{3x_k^2} = \frac{2 x_k + R / x_k^2}{3}\). This is Halley's/Hero's cube root formula, which converges quadratically without division by the root itself.

M3 · Cycle in Newton's method

Show that for \(f(x) = x^3 - 5x\), starting Newton's method at \(x_0 = 1\) results in a 2-cycle.

Solution

Compute \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 5\). At \(x_0 = 1\), \(f(1) = 1 - 5 = -4\) and \(f'(1) = 3(1) - 5 = -2\). The next iterate is \(x_1 = 1 - \frac{-4}{-2} = 1 - 2 = -1\). At \(x_1 = -1\), \(f(-1) = -1 + 5 = 4\) and \(f'(-1) = 3(1) - 5 = -2\). The next iterate is \(x_2 = -1 - \frac{4}{-2} = -1 + 2 = 1 = x_0\). The sequence oscillates indefinitely between \(+1\) and \(-1\), never converging to any of the roots \(\{0, \pm\sqrt{5}\}\).

P1 · Order of convergence for multiple roots

Let \(f(x) = (x - x^*)^m g(x)\) with \(g(x^*) \ne 0\) and \(m \ge 2\). Prove that Newton's method converges only linearly with asymptotic error constant \(1 - \frac{1}{m}\).

Solution

Compute \(f'(x) = m (x - x^*)^{m-1} g(x) + (x - x^*)^m g'(x)\). The Newton step is \(x_{k+1} - x^* = x_k - x^* - \frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)} = (x_k - x^*) - \frac{(x_k - x^*)^m g(x_k)}{m(x_k - x^*)^{m-1} g(x_k) + (x_k - x^*)^m g'(x_k)}\). Factoring \((x_k - x^*)\): \(x_{k+1} - x^* = (x_k - x^*) \left[1 - \frac{g(x_k)}{m g(x_k) + (x_k - x^*) g'(x_k)}\right]\). Taking the limit as \(x_k \to x^*\): \(\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{x_{k+1} - x^*}{x_k - x^*} = 1 - \frac{g(x^*)}{m g(x^*)} = 1 - \frac{1}{m}\). Since \(m \ge 2\), the limit is strictly between 0 and 1, proving linear convergence.

P2 · Modified Newton for multiple roots

Prove that the modified Newton iteration \(x_{k+1} = x_k - m \frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}\) restores quadratic convergence when the multiplicity \(m\) is known.

Solution

From the proof of P1, \(x_{k+1} - x^* = (x_k - x^*) \left[1 - m \frac{g(x_k)}{m g(x_k) + (x_k - x^*) g'(x_k)}\right]\). Writing \(\frac{m g}{m g + (x - x^*) g'} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{(x - x^*) g'}{m g}} = 1 - \frac{(x - x^*) g'}{m g} + O((x - x^*)^2)\). Substituting back: \(x_{k+1} - x^* = (x_k - x^*) \left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{(x_k - x^*) g'}{m g} + O(e_k^2)\right)\right] = \frac{g'(x^*)}{m g(x^*)} (x_k - x^*)^2 + O(e_k^3)\). Thus \(\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{|x_{k+1} - x^*|}{|x_k - x^*|^2} = \frac{|g'(x^*)|}{m |g(x^*)|}\), restoring quadratic convergence.

P3 · Secant method golden ratio convergence

Show that the secant method error recursion \(e_{k+1} \approx C e_k e_{k-1}\) implies an order of convergence equal to the golden ratio \(\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618\).

Solution

Assume an asymptotic relation \(e_{k+1} = A e_k^p\). Then \(e_k = A e_{k-1}^p\), so \(e_{k-1} = (e_k / A)^{1/p}\). Substituting into \(e_{k+1} = C e_k e_{k-1}\) gives \(A e_k^p = C e_k A^{-1/p} e_k^{1/p} = C A^{-1/p} e_k^{1 + 1/p}\). Matching the exponents of \(e_k\) requires \(p = 1 + \frac{1}{p}\), or \(p^2 - p - 1 = 0\). The positive root of this characteristic equation is \(p = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} = \phi \approx 1.618\), proving superlinear convergence with order \(\phi\).

E3 · Safeguarded step acceptance

In a bracket \([a, b] = [0.1, 1.0]\) with current iterate \(\sigma = 0.5\), suppose \(f(\sigma) = 0.05\) and vega \(v = 0.04\). Determine whether the Newton step is accepted under the safeguard rule \(a < \sigma - f/v < b\).

Solution

The proposed Newton step is \(\sigma_{\text{new}} = \sigma - f/v = 0.5 - \frac{0.05}{0.04} = 0.5 - 1.25 = -0.75\). Since \(-0.75 < a = 0.1\), the step falls outside the active bracket. The safeguard rejects \(\sigma_{\text{new}}\) and reverts to the bisection midpoint \(\frac{0.1 + 1.0}{2} = 0.55\), preventing negative volatility.

Research

R1 · Brent's method and Dekker's algorithm

Explain how Brent's method improves upon Dekker's algorithm by enforcing safeguards against slow bisection degradation.

Solution

Dekker's algorithm alternates between the secant method and bisection. However, on contrived functions, the secant step can contract the bracket by an infinitesimal amount (e.g. \(1 - 10^{-6}\)) at each step, causing \(O(1/\varepsilon)\) steps. Brent introduced four strict safeguarding tests: if the previous step was bisection, the current interpolation step must reduce the bracket by at least half; if interpolation fails to contract the bracket sufficiently over two consecutive steps, bisection is forcibly invoked. This guarantees that Brent's method is asymptotically superlinear while having a worst-case performance no worse than bisection.

R2 · High-dimensional nonlinear systems and Broyden updates

Why is full Newton's method computationally prohibitive for large-scale financial calibration problems (\(F(x) = 0, x \in \R^N\)), and how does Broyden's quasi-Newton update address this?

Solution

Multidimensional Newton requires computing the \(N \times N\) Jacobian matrix \(J(x_k)\) and solving the linear system \(J(x_k) \Delta x_k = -F(x_k)\) at each iteration, requiring \(O(N^3)\) operations. Broyden's method replaces the Jacobian with an approximation \(B_k\) updated via a rank-one formula satisfying the secant equation \(B_{k+1}(x_{k+1} - x_k) = F(x_{k+1}) - F(x_k)\). Using the Sherman-Morrison formula, \(B_k^{-1}\) can be updated directly in \(O(N^2)\) operations, substantially accelerating calibration for large volatility surfaces.

R3 · Halley's method for implied volatility

Derive Halley's third-order iteration for root finding and analyze its application to implied volatility using option Greeks (vega and vomma).

Solution

Halley's method uses a second-order Taylor expansion: \(x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{2 f(x_k) f'(x_k)}{2 [f'(x_k)]^2 - f(x_k) f''(x_k)}\). In implied volatility inversion, \(f(\sigma) = C(\sigma) - P\), \(f'(\sigma) = \mathcal{V}\) (vega), and \(f''(\sigma) = \frac{\partial \mathcal{V}}{\partial \sigma} = \text{vomma}\). Halley's step becomes \(\Delta \sigma = -\frac{2 (C - P) \mathcal{V}}{2 \mathcal{V}^2 - (C - P) \text{vomma}}\). When vega is moderate, Halley's method exhibits cubic convergence (\(e_{k+1} = O(e_k^3)\)), reducing the required evaluations to 2 or 3 steps. However, near expiry where vomma can be large and vega small, the denominator can vanish, necessitating bracket safeguards.
