Financial models frequently require reconstructing continuous functional relationships from discrete market observations, such as interpolating discount factors along a zero-coupon yield curve or pricing variance swaps via integral payoffs. High-degree global polynomial interpolation suffers from Runge's phenomenon—catastrophic boundary oscillations. Natural cubic splines solve this by fitting piecewise cubic polynomials with continuous second derivatives, yielding smooth curves that minimize total curvature. When evaluating derivative expectation integrals that lack closed forms, numerical quadrature rules provide high-order deterministic integration with rigorous error bounds.

1Runge's phenomenon and piecewise interpolation

Given \(n + 1\) distinct data points \((x_0, y_0), (x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_n, y_n)\), there exists a unique polynomial \(P_n(x)\) of degree at most \(n\) satisfying \(P_n(x_i) = y_i\). However, on equispaced grids, the interpolation error for smooth functions such as the Runge function \(f(x) = \frac{1}{1 + 25 x^2}\) on \([-1, 1]\) diverges near the interval endpoints: \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \max_{x \in [-1, 1]} |f(x) - P_n(x)| = \infty\).

To avoid high-degree oscillations, scientific computing relies on piecewise polynomial interpolation, partitioning \([a, b]\) into \(n\) panels and fitting low-degree polynomials on each subinterval.

2Natural cubic-spline interpolation

Definition 2.1 (cubic spline)

A cubic-spline interpolation for nodes \(a = x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_n = b\) and values \(y_i\) is a function \(S(x)\) such that:

  1. On each interval \([x_i, x_{i+1}]\), \(S(x) = S_i(x)\) is a cubic polynomial: \[ S_i(x) = a_i + b_i(x - x_i) + c_i(x - x_i)^2 + d_i(x - x_i)^3 \]
  2. Interpolation condition: \(S(x_i) = y_i\) for \(i = 0, \dots, n\).
  3. Global \(C^2\) continuity: \(S(x)\), \(S'(x)\), and \(S''(x)\) are continuous across every interior node \(x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}\).

A natural cubic spline imposes the boundary condition \(S''(x_0) = S''(x_n) = 0\).

Theorem 2.2 (existence and uniqueness of natural cubic spline)

For any set of distinct nodes \(x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_n\) and values \(y_0, \dots, y_n\), there exists a unique natural cubic spline \(S(x)\). Its second derivatives \(M_i = S''(x_i)\) satisfy a strictly diagonally dominant tridiagonal system:

\[ h_{i-1} M_{i-1} + 2(h_{i-1} + h_i) M_i + h_i M_{i+1} = 6 \left(\frac{y_{i+1} - y_i}{h_i} - \frac{y_i - y_{i-1}}{h_{i-1}}\right) \]

for \(i = 1, \dots, n-1\), with boundary conditions \(M_0 = M_n = 0\), where \(h_i = x_{i+1} - x_i\).

Proof

On \([x_i, x_{i+1}]\), \(S''_i(x)\) is linear. Since \(S''_i(x_i) = M_i\) and \(S''_i(x_{i+1}) = M_{i+1}\):

\[ S''_i(x) = M_i \frac{x_{i+1} - x}{h_i} + M_{i+1} \frac{x - x_i}{h_i} \]

Integrating twice and applying \(S_i(x_i) = y_i\) and \(S_i(x_{i+1}) = y_{i+1}\) determines the integration constants. Differentiating once gives \(S'_i(x)\). Equating the left derivative \(S'_{i-1}(x_i)\) with the right derivative \(S'_i(x_i)\) at each interior node \(x_i\) yields the tridiagonal relation above. The coefficient matrix has diagonal entry \(2(h_{i-1} + h_i)\) and off-diagonal row sum \(h_{i-1} + h_i\). Since \(2(h_{i-1} + h_i) > h_{i-1} + h_i\), the matrix is strictly diagonally dominant, symmetric, and positive-definite, guaranteeing a unique solution via the Thomas algorithm in \(O(n)\) operations. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (minimum curvature property)

The natural cubic spline minimizes total bending energy among all interpolating \(C^2\) functions: \(\int_a^b [S''(x)]^2 dx \le \int_a^b [g''(x)]^2 dx\) for any \(g \in C^2[a, b]\) with \(g(x_i) = y_i\). For smooth functions \(f \in C^4\), the interpolation error scales as \(O(h^4)\) where \(h = \max_i h_i\).

3Numerical quadrature

Numerical quadrature approximates a definite integral \(\int_a^b f(x) dx\) by a weighted sum of function values at discrete quadrature nodes: \(I(f) \approx \sum_{i=0}^n w_i f(x_i)\).

Definition 3.1 (composite Newton-Cotes rules)

Dividing \([a, b]\) into \(n\) equal panels of width \(h = (b - a)/n\) with \(x_i = a + i h\):

  • Composite trapezoid rule: \[ T_n(f) = h \left[\frac{1}{2} f(x_0) + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} f(x_i) + \frac{1}{2} f(x_n)\right] \]
  • Composite Simpson's rule (for even \(n\)): \[ S_n(f) = \frac{h}{3} \left[f(x_0) + 4 \sum_{i=1, 3, \dots}^{n-1} f(x_i) + 2 \sum_{i=2, 4, \dots}^{n-2} f(x_i) + f(x_n)\right] \]
Proposition 3.2 (quadrature error bounds)

Assume \(f\) is sufficiently smooth on \([a, b]\). The global approximation errors satisfy:

  • Trapezoid error: \(\int_a^b f(x) dx - T_n(f) = -\frac{b - a}{12} h^2 f''(\xi)\), \(\xi \in (a, b)\) (second-order, \(O(h^2)\))
  • Simpson error: \(\int_a^b f(x) dx - S_n(f) = -\frac{b - a}{180} h^4 f^{(4)}(\eta)\), \(\eta \in (a, b)\) (fourth-order, \(O(h^4)\))
Proof

For a single panel \([x_0, x_1]\) of width \(h\), let \(P_1(x)\) be the linear interpolant of \(f\). By the polynomial interpolation error theorem, \(f(x) - P_1(x) = \frac{1}{2} f''(\xi_x) (x - x_0)(x - x_1)\). Integrating over \([x_0, x_1]\): \(\int_{x_0}^{x_1} (f(x) - P_1(x)) dx = \frac{1}{2} f''(\xi) \int_{x_0}^{x_1} (x - x_0)(x - x_1) dx = -\frac{h^3}{12} f''(\xi)\) by the mean value theorem for integrals. Summing over all \(n\) panels gives total error \(-\frac{h^3}{12} \sum_{i=1}^n f''(\xi_i) = -\frac{n h^3}{12} f''(\xi) = -\frac{b - a}{12} h^2 f''(\xi)\). For Simpson's rule, quadratic interpolation across pairs of panels yields local error \(-\frac{h^5}{90} f^{(4)}(\eta_i)\), summing to \(-\frac{b - a}{180} h^4 f^{(4)}(\eta)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.3 (Gauss-Legendre quadrature)

Newton-Cotes rules fix node locations a priori. Gauss-Legendre quadrature optimizes both the nodes \(x_i\) and weights \(w_i\). By choosing \(x_i\) as the roots of the degree-\(n\) Legendre polynomial \(P_n(x)\) on \([-1, 1]\), an \(n\)-point Gauss-Legendre rule integrates all polynomials up to degree \(2n - 1\) exactly, achieving exponential convergence for analytic integrands.

4Inline laboratory: convergence orders in practice

Lab 1 · Natural cubic spline interpolation and numerical quadrature convergence

Natural cubic spline interpolation of \(\sin(x)\) on \([0, \pi]\) achieves \(O(h^4)\) convergence as panel count doubles (\(n = 4, 8, 16, 32\)). Numerical quadrature of \(\int_0^1 e^x dx = e - 1\) demonstrates second-order convergence (\(O(h^2)\)) for composite trapezoid and fourth-order convergence (\(O(h^4)\)) for composite Simpson's rule.

"""Lab M8-L05-1: Natural cubic spline interpolation and numerical quadrature convergence.

Standard library only. Demonstrates O(h^4) convergence of cubic splines,
O(h^2) for composite trapezoid, and O(h^4) for composite Simpson's rule.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def natural_cubic_spline(
    x_nodes: list[float], y_nodes: list[float]
) -> tuple[list[float], list[float], list[float], list[float]]:
    """Fit natural cubic spline passing through given nodes.

    Returns coefficients (a, b, c, d) for each interval i:
    S_i(x) = a_i + b_i*(x - x_i) + c_i*(x - x_i)^2 + d_i*(x - x_i)^3.
    """
    n = len(x_nodes) - 1
    h = [x_nodes[i + 1] - x_nodes[i] for i in range(n)]

    # Tridiagonal system for c_i = M_i / 2:
    # h_{i-1} c_{i-1} + 2(h_{i-1} + h_i) c_i + h_i c_{i+1} = 3 ((y_{i+1} - y_i)/h_i - (y_i - y_{i-1})/h_{i-1})
    alpha = [0.0] * (n + 1)
    for i in range(1, n):
        alpha[i] = 3.0 * ((y_nodes[i + 1] - y_nodes[i]) / h[i] - (y_nodes[i] - y_nodes[i - 1]) / h[i - 1])

    # Thomas algorithm for tridiagonal system with c_0 = c_n = 0
    l_diag = [1.0] * (n + 1)
    mu = [0.0] * (n + 1)
    z = [0.0] * (n + 1)

    for i in range(1, n):
        l_diag[i] = 2.0 * (x_nodes[i + 1] - x_nodes[i - 1]) - h[i - 1] * mu[i - 1]
        mu[i] = h[i] / l_diag[i]
        z[i] = (alpha[i] - h[i - 1] * z[i - 1]) / l_diag[i]

    c = [0.0] * (n + 1)
    b = [0.0] * n
    d = [0.0] * n
    a = [y_nodes[i] for i in range(n)]

    for j in range(n - 1, -1, -1):
        c[j] = z[j] - mu[j] * c[j + 1]
        b[j] = (y_nodes[j + 1] - y_nodes[j]) / h[j] - h[j] * (c[j + 1] + 2.0 * c[j]) / 3.0
        d[j] = (c[j + 1] - c[j]) / (3.0 * h[j])

    return a, b, c[:n], d


def eval_spline(
    x: float,
    x_nodes: list[float],
    a: list[float],
    b: list[float],
    c: list[float],
    d: list[float],
) -> float:
    n = len(x_nodes) - 1
    # Locate interval
    if x <= x_nodes[0]:
        idx = 0
    elif x >= x_nodes[-1]:
        idx = n - 1
    else:
        idx = 0
        for i in range(n):
            if x_nodes[i] <= x <= x_nodes[i + 1]:
                idx = i
                break
    dx = x - x_nodes[idx]
    return a[idx] + b[idx] * dx + c[idx] * (dx**2) + d[idx] * (dx**3)


def composite_trapezoid(f_vals: list[float], h: float) -> float:
    """Composite trapezoid rule."""
    return h * (0.5 * f_vals[0] + sum(f_vals[1:-1]) + 0.5 * f_vals[-1])


def composite_simpson(f_vals: list[float], h: float) -> float:
    """Composite Simpson's rule (requires even number of intervals)."""
    n = len(f_vals) - 1
    assert n % 2 == 0
    s_odd = sum(f_vals[i] for i in range(1, n, 2))
    s_even = sum(f_vals[i] for i in range(2, n, 2))
    return (h / 3.0) * (f_vals[0] + 4.0 * s_odd + 2.0 * s_even + f_vals[-1])


def main() -> None:
    print("=== Part A: Natural Cubic Spline Interpolation of sin(x) on [0, pi] ===")
    fine_x = [math.pi * i / 1000.0 for i in range(1001)]
    true_fine = [math.sin(x) for x in fine_x]

    prev_err_spline = 0.0
    for n in [4, 8, 16, 32]:
        x_nodes = [math.pi * i / n for i in range(n + 1)]
        y_nodes = [math.sin(x) for x in x_nodes]
        a, b, c, d = natural_cubic_spline(x_nodes, y_nodes)

        max_err = max(
            abs(eval_spline(x, x_nodes, a, b, c, d) - y_true) for x, y_true in zip(fine_x, true_fine, strict=False)
        )
        order_str = f"{math.log2(prev_err_spline / max_err):.2f}" if prev_err_spline > 0 else "-"
        print(f"n={n:2d} panels: max error = {max_err:.8e}, order = {order_str}")
        prev_err_spline = max_err

    print("\n=== Part B: Numerical Quadrature of int_0^1 exp(x) dx ===")
    true_integral = math.e - 1.0
    print(f"Exact integral e - 1: {true_integral:.16f}")
    print(" n    Trap Error     Trap Order    Simp Error     Simp Order")

    prev_trap_err = 0.0
    prev_simp_err = 0.0

    for n in [2, 4, 8, 16, 32]:
        h = 1.0 / n
        f_vals = [math.exp(i * h) for i in range(n + 1)]
        trap_val = composite_trapezoid(f_vals, h)
        simp_val = composite_simpson(f_vals, h)

        trap_err = abs(trap_val - true_integral)
        simp_err = abs(simp_val - true_integral)

        trap_order = f"{math.log2(prev_trap_err / trap_err):.2f}" if prev_trap_err > 0 else "   -  "
        simp_order = f"{math.log2(prev_simp_err / simp_err):.2f}" if prev_simp_err > 0 else "   -  "

        print(f"{n:2d}   {trap_err:.8e}    {trap_order}    {simp_err:.8e}    {simp_order}")
        prev_trap_err = trap_err
        prev_simp_err = simp_err


main()

5Where the canon breaks

  • Curse of dimensionality in quadrature. In 1 dimension, Simpson's rule requires \(N\) evaluations for error \(O(N^{-4})\). In \(d\) dimensions, a tensor product grid requires \(N = n^d\) points, degrading the error rate to \(O(N^{-4/d})\). For multi-asset basket options or CDO tranches (\(d \ge 5\)), deterministic quadrature becomes computationally intractable, necessitating Monte Carlo or sparse grids.
  • Cubic spline oscillations under knot clustering. If nodes \(x_i\) and \(x_{i+1}\) are clustered very closely while neighboring intervals are large, the tridiagonal system condition number grows as \(O(h_{\max} / h_{\min})\), introducing spurious oscillations. Monotone cubic splines (such as the Fritsch-Carlson algorithm) must be used when interpolating discount curves to avoid negative forward rates.

6Misconceptions

  1. “Simpson's rule is exact only for quadratic polynomials because it fits parabolas.”

    Simpson's rule is exact for all cubic polynomials (\(P \in \mathcal{P}_3\)) as well. Due to symmetry of the three-point panel about its midpoint, the \(x^3\) error term integrates to zero, giving Simpson's rule an extra degree of polynomial precision for free.

  2. “Higher-degree polynomial splines (e.g. quintic or septic) are always preferable to cubic splines.”

    Cubic splines represent the optimal balance: they provide continuous curvature (\(C^2\)), which human visual perception and physical beam deflection require, without introducing the severe ill-conditioning and oscillation tendencies of higher-order splines.

  3. “Periodic boundary conditions are identical to natural boundary conditions for splines.”

    Natural splines set \(S''(a) = S''(b) = 0\), forcing zero curvature at the boundaries. Periodic splines enforce \(S'(a) = S'(b)\) and \(S''(a) = S''(b)\), suitable only for closed loops or cyclical data.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Single panel Simpson's rule

Evaluate \(\int_0^2 x^3 dx\) using a single panel of Simpson's rule with nodes \(x = 0, 1, 2\), and check against the exact integral.

Solution

The panel width is \(h = (2 - 0)/2 = 1\). Function values are \(f(0) = 0\), \(f(1) = 1^3 = 1\), and \(f(2) = 2^3 = 8\). Simpson's rule gives \(S = \frac{h}{3}[f(0) + 4f(1) + f(2)] = \frac{1}{3}[0 + 4(1) + 8] = \frac{12}{3} = 4\). The exact integral is \(\int_0^2 x^3 dx = [\frac{x^4}{4}]_0^2 = \frac{16}{4} = 4\). Simpson's rule is exact for cubics, as demonstrated.

M2 · Degree of precision of Gauss-Legendre

State the degree of polynomial precision of an \(n\)-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature rule, and compute the number of points required to integrate a degree-7 polynomial exactly.

Solution

An \(n\)-point Gauss-Legendre rule has \(n\) nodes and \(n\) weights (giving \(2n\) degrees of freedom), achieving exact integration for all polynomials of degree at most \(2n - 1\). To integrate a degree-7 polynomial exactly, we require \(2n - 1 \ge 7\), so \(2n \ge 8\), which gives \(n = 4\) points.

M3 · Panel count for specified trapezoid tolerance

How many panels \(n\) are needed to evaluate \(\int_0^1 e^x dx\) with the composite trapezoid rule so that the truncation error bound is less than \(10^{-4}\)?

Solution

For \(f(x) = e^x\), \(f''(x) = e^x\), so \(M_2 = \max_{x \in [0, 1]} e^x = e \approx 2.7183\). The trapezoid error bound is \(\frac{b - a}{12} h^2 M_2 = \frac{1}{12 n^2} e\). Setting \(\frac{e}{12 n^2} \le 10^{-4}\) yields \(n^2 \ge \frac{e \times 10^4}{12} \approx \frac{27183}{12} \approx 2265.25\). Taking the square root: \(n \ge \sqrt{2265.25} \approx 47.6\), so \(n = 48\) panels are required.

Proof

P1 · Simpson's rule exactness for cubics

Prove that Simpson's rule on \([-h, h]\) is exact for \(f(x) = x^3\).

Solution

The exact integral of the odd function \(x^3\) over symmetric interval \([-h, h]\) is \(\int_{-h}^h x^3 dx = 0\). Simpson's formula on \([-h, h]\) evaluates at \(x_0 = -h, x_1 = 0, x_2 = h\): \(S = \frac{h}{3}[f(-h) + 4 f(0) + f(h)] = \frac{h}{3}[(-h)^3 + 4(0)^3 + h^3] = \frac{h}{3}[-h^3 + h^3] = 0\). Thus \(S = \int_{-h}^h x^3 dx\) exactly. By linearity, since Simpson's rule is exact for \(\{1, x, x^2\}\) by interpolation construction, it is exact for all polynomials of degree \(\le 3\).

P2 · Minimum curvature property of natural splines

Prove that among all interpolating functions \(g \in C^2[a, b]\) with \(g(x_i) = y_i\), the natural cubic spline \(S\) minimizes \(\int_a^b [g''(x)]^2 dx\).

Solution

Write \(g(x) = S(x) + \delta(x)\) where \(\delta(x_i) = g(x_i) - S(x_i) = 0\) for all \(i\). Then \(\int_a^b [g'']^2 dx = \int_a^b [S'' + \delta'']^2 dx = \int_a^b [S'']^2 dx + 2 \int_a^b S'' \delta'' dx + \int_a^b [\delta'']^2 dx\). Integrating the cross term by parts over each panel: \(\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S'' \delta'' dx = [S'' \delta']_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} - \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S''' \delta' dx\). Since \(S\) is cubic, \(S'''\) is constant \(c_i\) on each panel: \(\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S''' \delta' dx = c_i [\delta]_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} = c_i(0 - 0) = 0\). Summing the boundary terms across all panels cancels interior terms by \(C^2\) continuity, leaving \([S''(b) \delta'(b) - S''(a) \delta'(a)]\). Under natural boundary conditions, \(S''(a) = S''(b) = 0\), so the cross term vanishes entirely. Thus \(\int_a^b [g'']^2 dx = \int_a^b [S'']^2 dx + \int_a^b [\delta'']^2 dx \ge \int_a^b [S'']^2 dx\).

P3 · Tridiagonal strict diagonal dominance

Prove that the tridiagonal matrix in Theorem 2.2 is strictly diagonally dominant, guaranteeing non-singularity.

Solution

For row \(i\) (\(1 \le i \le n-1\)), the diagonal entry is \(D_i = 2(h_{i-1} + h_i)\). The off-diagonal entries in that row are \(L_i = h_{i-1}\) and \(R_i = h_i\). Since node spacings are strictly positive (\(h_k > 0\)), the sum of off-diagonal entries is \(|L_i| + |R_i| = h_{i-1} + h_i\). Comparing: \(|D_i| - (|L_i| + |R_i|) = 2(h_{i-1} + h_i) - (h_{i-1} + h_i) = h_{i-1} + h_i > 0\). By the Levy-Desplanques / Gershgorin circle theorem, strict diagonal dominance guarantees that all eigenvalues have positive real parts, hence the matrix is strictly non-singular.

Research

R1 · Variance swap replication via log contract quadrature

Explain how the Demeterfi et al. (1999) replication of a variance swap payoff \(\sigma_{\text{realized}}^2\) uses numerical quadrature over a continuum of OTM put and call strikes.

Solution

Carr and Madan showed that \(\ln(S_T/S_0) = \frac{S_T - S_*}{S_*} - \int_0^{S_*} \frac{1}{K^2} (K - S_T)^+ dK - \int_{S_*}^\infty \frac{1}{K^2} (S_T - K)^+ dK\). The fair variance swap strike requires integrating \(\int_0^{S_*} \frac{1}{K^2} P(K) dK + \int_{S_*}^\infty \frac{1}{K^2} C(K) dK\). In market practice, quotes exist only on discrete strikes \(\{K_i\}\). Desks evaluate this integral using composite trapezoid or Simpson quadrature after fitting a cubic spline across the implied volatility smile, weighting each strike by \(1/K_i^2\).

R2 · Monotone cubic splines in yield curve fitting

Why do natural cubic splines sometimes produce negative forward interest rates when fitting discount curves \(P(t)\), and how does the Hagan-West (2006) monotonic spline resolve this?

Solution

Instantaneous forward rates are given by \(f(t) = -\frac{P'(t)}{P(t)}\). While \(P(t)\) is strictly decreasing, natural cubic splines minimize curvature across the whole domain, which can cause \(P'(t)\) to oscillate and briefly become positive near knots, implying an arbitrageable negative forward rate. Hagan and West enforce Fritsch-Carlson monotonicity constraints on the spline slopes: if the slope changes sign, \(S'_i(t)\) is clamped to zero, guaranteeing that \(P(t)\) is monotonically decreasing and forward rates remain strictly positive.

R3 · Clenshaw-Curtis versus Gauss quadrature

Compare the efficiency of Clenshaw-Curtis quadrature (based on Chebyshev extrema) to Gauss-Legendre quadrature for practical financial engineering applications.

Solution

Gauss-Legendre integrates polynomials of degree \(2n - 1\) exactly, whereas Clenshaw-Curtis integrates polynomials of degree \(n\). Trefethen (2008) demonstrated that for non-polynomial analytic functions, Clenshaw-Curtis converges at virtually the same exponential rate as Gauss quadrature. Crucially, Clenshaw-Curtis nodes \(x_k = \cos(k \pi / n)\) can be evaluated via the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) in \(O(n \log n)\), and nested grids \(n \to 2n\) reuse existing function evaluations, making Clenshaw-Curtis far more efficient for adaptive integration in financial option pricing.
