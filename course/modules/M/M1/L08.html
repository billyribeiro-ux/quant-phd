Loan Amortization Schedules

A fixed-rate loan is repaid in equal installments, and each installment splits into interest on the balance and a reduction of the balance. The schedule of those splits is the loan's most used table. Its level payment is an annuity value (Lesson 7), and its balance has a closed form that follows by induction. This lesson derives both, then sets out the same schedule in integer cents, where the last payment must be adjusted. The illustrative loan here is an example for the arithmetic, not a market quote.

1Setup and conventions

A loan of amount \(L\) (in CU) is repaid by \(n\) equal payments of \(A\) at the ends of months \(1, \ldots, n\). The loan carries a nominal annual rate \(j\), the annual rate quoted before compounding, which is compounded monthly. This lesson uses the convention that the monthly rate is \(i = j/12\), so that interest for one month on a balance \(B\) is \(iB\). The convention is an assumption about how the contract is quoted, not a result, and Section 4 shows what it changes.

Definition 1.1 (amortization schedule)

A loan amortization schedule is the sequence of rows \(k = 1, \ldots, n\) defined from the opening balance \(B_0 = L\) by \[ I_k = i B_{k-1}, \qquad p_k = A - I_k, \qquad B_k = B_{k-1} - p_k. \] Here \(I_k\) is the interest in month \(k\), \(p_k\) is the principal repaid in month \(k\), and \(B_k\) is the balance after month \(k\). By construction, \(I_k + p_k = A\) in every row.

The payment \(A\) is fixed by the requirement that the balance reaches zero after \(n\) months. Lesson 7, Theorem 1.2, says that the present value of \(n\) payments of \(A\) at the monthly rate \(i\) is \(A(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})/i\). A loan is the present value of its payments, so \(L = A(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})/i\), and solving gives the level payment below.

Proposition 1.2 (level payment)

For \(i > 0\), the level payment that repays \(L\) in \(n\) months is \[ A = \frac{L\, i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}. \]

Proof

By the annuity identity, \(L = A(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})/i\) for the payment \(A\) of Definition 1.1 to repay the loan. Multiply both sides by \(i\) and divide by \(1 - (1 + i)^{-n}\), which is positive for \(i > 0\) and \(n \ge 1\), to get the formula.

2The exact schedule

Work first with exact arithmetic, with no conversion to cents. This shows what the schedule is before any convention about money enters.

Proposition 2.1 (closed form of the balance)

With the exact payment \(A\) of Proposition 1.2, for every integer \(k\) with \(0 \le k \le n\), \[ B_k = L(1 + i)^{k} - A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k} - 1}{i}. \]

Proof

Let \(P(k)\) be the statement of the proposition, for \(k \ge 0\), and use Theorem 1.2 of Lesson 6 with \(n_0 = 0\). The base case is \(B_0 = L\), and the formula gives \(L \cdot 1 - A \cdot 0/i = L\).

Assume \(P(k)\). Then \(B_{k+1} = B_k - p_{k+1} = (1 + i)B_k - A\), because \(p_{k+1} = A - iB_k\). Substituting the hypothesis, \[ B_{k+1} = L(1 + i)^{k+1} - A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k+1} - (1 + i)}{i} - A = L(1 + i)^{k+1} - A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k+1} - 1}{i}, \] and the two fractions combine because \(-(1 + i) + i = -1\), so \(-A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k+1} - (1 + i)}{i} - A = -A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k+1} - (1 + i) + i}{i} = -A\,\frac{(1 + i)^{k+1} - 1}{i}\). This is \(P(k+1)\).

Corollary 2.2 (the balance reaches zero)

With \(A\) as in Proposition 1.2, \(B_n = 0\).

Proof

Substitute \(A = Li/(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})\) into Proposition 2.1 with \(k = n\). The second term is \(\frac{Li}{1 - (1+i)^{-n}} \cdot \frac{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}{i} = L\frac{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}\). Multiplying the numerator and denominator by \((1 + i)^{n}\) turns the denominator into \((1 + i)^{n} - 1\), so this term equals \(L(1 + i)^n\), and \(B_n = L(1 + i)^n - L(1 + i)^n = 0\).

Proposition 2.3 (principal grows at the monthly rate)

With exact arithmetic and the payment \(A\) of Proposition 1.2, \(p_{k+1} = (1 + i)\,p_k\) for every \(1 \le k \le n - 1\). Hence \(p_k = p_1 (1 + i)^{k-1}\).

Proof

From \(B_k = B_{k-1} - p_k\) and \(I_k = iB_{k-1}\), we have \(p_{k+1} = A - iB_k = A - i(B_{k-1} - p_k) = (A - iB_{k-1}) + ip_k\). Since \(A - iB_{k-1} = p_k\), this gives \(p_{k+1} = p_k + ip_k = (1 + i)p_k\). The closed form follows by induction on \(k\) (Theorem 1.2 of Lesson 6).

Proposition 2.4 (totals)

With exact arithmetic and the payment \(A\), the principal repaid over the \(n\) months is \(L\), and the total interest is \(nA - L\).

Proof

Since \(p_k = B_{k-1} - B_k\), the sum telescopes: \(\sum_{k=1}^{n} p_k = B_0 - B_n = L - 0 = L\) by Corollary 2.2. Each row has \(I_k = A - p_k\), so \(\sum_{k=1}^{n} I_k = nA - \sum_{k=1}^{n} p_k = nA - L\).

3The schedule in integer cents

A borrower's statement shows amounts in whole cents, and a convention is needed for each. This section takes three conventions, and Lab 1 applies them to an illustrative loan.

  1. Interest. Each month's interest \(I_k = iB_{k-1}\) is taken to the nearest cent, with exact halves going up.
  2. Regular payment. The payment \(A\) of Proposition 1.2 is taken to the nearest cent, and that amount is paid in months \(1\) to \(n - 1\).
  3. Final payment. The last payment is \(B_{n-1} + I_n\), so that the balance closes at exactly zero.
Proposition 3.1 (the cent schedule closes)

Under conventions 1 to 3, with integer-cent balances, the schedule has \(n\) rows, its final balance is exactly zero, the principal repaid sums to the loan amount, and the total paid is the total interest plus \(L\).

Proof

Each row sets \(p_k = \text{payment}_k - I_k\) and \(B_k = B_{k-1} - p_k\), so the balance is an integer number of cents at every step. Row \(n\) sets \(\text{payment}_n = B_{n-1} + I_n\), which gives \(p_n = B_{n-1}\) and \(B_n = 0\). The principal sum telescopes exactly as in Proposition 2.4, so it equals \(L\), and the total paid equals the sum of the \(p_k\) plus the sum of the \(I_k\). The statement that the balances never go negative is not proved here; Lab 1 checks it for the illustrative loan.

The statement of Proposition 3.1 is an accounting identity. What the cent schedule does to the exact schedule of Section 2 is a separate question, and the lab answers it for one loan.

Lab 1 · A full schedule for an illustrative 250,000 CU loan

ILLUSTRATIVE INPUT, NOT DATA: a loan of 250,000.00 CU, a nominal rate of 6 per cent compounded monthly (\(i = 0.005\)), and 360 monthly payments. Stated before running: the exact payment should be about 1,498.88 CU; the exact-arithmetic balances should match Proposition 2.1 and the principal should grow by a factor of \(1.005\) each month; the cent schedule should close at zero, and its balances should differ from the exact balances by well under one CU. The first month's interest should be 1,250.00 CU.

"""M1 L08 lab 1: a full amortization schedule for an illustrative loan, in integer cents.

ILLUSTRATIVE INPUT, NOT DATA: the principal, rate and term are chosen for the example and are not a market quote.
Convention (project Q5): the nominal annual rate j is compounded monthly, so the monthly rate is i = j / 12.
Part A is exact (fractions.Fraction, no conversion to cents): the balance formula, the closed-form payment, the zero
final balance, and the principal recursion P(k+1) = (1 + i) P(k). Part B is the schedule a borrower sees:
every amount is an integer number of cents, interest is taken to the nearest cent with halves going up, the
regular payment is the exact payment taken to the nearest cent, and the final payment is adjusted so the
balance closes at exactly zero. Part B reports how far its balances drift from the exact balances.
Standard library only; no randomness and no network access.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

PRINCIPAL_CENTS = 25_000_000  # 250,000.00 CU
ANNUAL_NOMINAL = Fraction(6, 100)
PERIODS = 360
MONTHLY = ANNUAL_NOMINAL / 12  # i = 1/200


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Row:
    period: int
    opening: int
    interest: int
    principal: int
    payment: int
    closing: int


def half_up(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """Nearest whole number, halves going up, for a non-negative exact amount."""
    return math.floor(x + Fraction(1, 2))


def cu(cents: int) -> str:
    """Format integer cents as currency units with two decimals, for example 1498.88."""
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    value = abs(cents)
    return f"{sign}{value // 100:,}.{value % 100:02d}"


def exact_payment(principal: int, i: Fraction, n: int) -> Fraction:
    """Level payment P * i / (1 - (1 + i)**-n), exact."""
    return principal * i / (1 - (1 + i) ** (-n))


def exact_balance(principal: int, payment: Fraction, i: Fraction, k: int) -> Fraction:
    """Balance after k payments: P (1 + i)**k - A ((1 + i)**k - 1) / i, exact."""
    growth = (1 + i) ** k
    return principal * growth - payment * (growth - 1) / i


def cent_schedule(principal: int, i: Fraction, n: int) -> list[Row]:
    """The schedule in integer cents, with the final payment adjusted so the balance closes at zero."""
    regular = half_up(exact_payment(principal, i, n))
    rows: list[Row] = []
    balance = principal
    for k in range(1, n + 1):
        interest = half_up(Fraction(balance) * i)
        payment = regular if k < n else balance + interest
        principal_part = payment - interest
        closing = balance - principal_part
        rows.append(Row(k, balance, interest, principal_part, payment, closing))
        balance = closing
    return rows


def part_a() -> None:
    p = Fraction(PRINCIPAL_CENTS)
    payment = exact_payment(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, MONTHLY, PERIODS)
    if exact_balance(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, payment, MONTHLY, PERIODS) != 0:
        raise AssertionError("the exact balance does not reach zero after n payments")
    balance = p
    principal_prev: Fraction | None = None
    for k in range(1, PERIODS):
        interest = balance * MONTHLY
        principal_k = payment - interest
        balance = balance - principal_k
        if exact_balance(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, payment, MONTHLY, k) != balance:
            raise AssertionError(f"balance formula fails at k = {k}")
        if principal_prev is not None and principal_k != (1 + MONTHLY) * principal_prev:
            raise AssertionError(f"principal recursion fails at k = {k}")
        principal_prev = principal_k
    print(f"part A: exact balances match the closed form and the principal grows by 1 + i, for k = 1 to {PERIODS - 1}")
    print(f"part A: exact payment = {float(payment / 100):.6f} CU; exact balance after {PERIODS} payments = 0")
    print(f"part A: effective annual rate (1 + i)**12 - 1 = {float((1 + MONTHLY) ** 12 - 1):.6%}")


def part_b() -> None:
    regular = half_up(exact_payment(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, MONTHLY, PERIODS))
    rows = cent_schedule(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, MONTHLY, PERIODS)
    if len(rows) != PERIODS:
        raise AssertionError("the schedule must have one row per payment")
    if rows[-1].closing != 0:
        raise AssertionError("the final balance is not zero")
    if any(row.closing < 0 for row in rows):
        raise AssertionError("a balance went negative")
    total_interest = sum(row.interest for row in rows)
    total_paid = sum(row.payment for row in rows)
    total_principal = sum(row.principal for row in rows)
    if total_principal != PRINCIPAL_CENTS or total_interest != total_paid - PRINCIPAL_CENTS:
        raise AssertionError("totals do not reconcile to the loan")
    drift = 0
    for row in rows[:-1]:
        exact = exact_balance(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, Fraction(regular), MONTHLY, row.period)
        drift = max(drift, abs(row.closing - math.floor(exact + Fraction(1, 2))))
    exact_cu = float(exact_payment(PRINCIPAL_CENTS, MONTHLY, PERIODS) / 100)
    print(f"part B: regular payment = {cu(regular)} CU (the exact payment is {exact_cu:.6f} CU)")
    print(f"part B: final payment = {cu(rows[-1].payment)} CU (adjustment {cu(rows[-1].payment - regular)} CU)")
    print(f"part B: total interest = {cu(total_interest)} CU; total paid = {cu(total_paid)} CU")
    print(f"part B: total principal = {cu(total_principal)} CU; final balance = {cu(rows[-1].closing)} CU")
    print(f"part B: largest drift of a cent balance from the exact balance (in cents) = {drift}")
    exact_interest = Fraction(PRINCIPAL_CENTS)
    for _ in range(PERIODS - 1):
        exact_interest = exact_interest * (1 + MONTHLY) - regular
    final_exact_interest = float((exact_interest * (1 + MONTHLY)) / 100)
    print(f"part B: final payment if interest is not taken to the cent = {final_exact_interest:.2f} CU")
    print("period  opening        interest     principal    payment       closing")
    for row in rows[:3] + rows[-2:]:
        print(
            f"{row.period:6d}  {cu(row.opening):>13}  {cu(row.interest):>10}  {cu(row.principal):>10}"
            f"  {cu(row.payment):>10}  {cu(row.closing):>13}"
        )


def main() -> None:
    part_a()
    part_b()

Part A is exact: the balances match Proposition 2.1 at every month, the principal grows by the factor \(1 + i\), and the exact payment leaves a zero balance. Part B is the cent schedule. Its regular payment is 1,498.88 CU, which is the exact payment of 1,498.876313 CU to the nearest cent. The final payment is 1,495.45 CU, 3.43 CU below the regular payment. Problem R3 explains the difference; the cent schedule is the one a borrower sees, and the exact schedule is the one the mathematics describes. The largest drift from the exact balances is 27 cents, all of it from taking interest to the nearest cent, since the regular payment is held fixed. The lab shows this one loan. It does not show how large the drift is for other loans, and it does not model prepayment or a change in the rate.

4Where the canon breaks

  • The nominal rate is not the effective rate. A nominal 6 per cent compounded monthly is an effective annual rate of \((1 + 0.06/12)^{12} - 1 = 6.1678\%\), a figure printed by Lab 1, part A. A borrower who compares a 6 per cent loan with a 6 per cent deposit that pays annually is comparing different quantities. The convention \(i = j/12\) is how a monthly loan is quoted, and it is not a law.
  • Money is not real arithmetic. Proposition 2.3 holds in exact arithmetic. In cents, the principal of month \(k+1\) is no longer exactly \((1 + i)\) times that of month \(k\), because each interest figure is taken to the nearest cent. Section 3 and Lab 1 keep the cent schedule and the exact schedule apart.
  • The schedule assumes a fixed rate and no prepayment. Proposition 2.1 needs one rate \(i\) for all \(n\) months. A variable rate needs a new payment at each change, and a prepayment changes the balance and therefore every later row. Neither is modelled here.

5Misconceptions

  1. “The principal repaid is the same in every month of a level-payment loan.”

    The principal grows each month by the factor \(1 + i\) (Proposition 2.3). Interest falls as the balance falls, so a larger share of each fixed payment repays principal. The first month of Lab 1 repays 248.88 CU of principal, and the last month repays 1,488.01 CU.

  2. “The monthly rate on a 6 per cent nominal loan is 6 per cent.”

    Under the convention of Section 1, the monthly rate is \(0.06/12 = 0.005\). A monthly rate of 0.06 would be 72 per cent a year in simple terms, which is a different loan.

  3. “The last payment of a cent schedule equals the regular payment, because the formula says the balance is zero.”

    The formula gives zero only in exact arithmetic. The cent schedule's last payment in Lab 1 is 1,495.45 CU, which is 3.43 CU below the regular payment, because the regular payment is the exact payment taken to the cent, which is 0.37 cents above it, and that excess compounds (Problem R3). The final payment is adjusted so the balance closes at zero.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · The payment on a small loan

A loan of 10,000 CU is repaid over 12 months at a nominal 12 per cent compounded monthly. Find the exact level payment.

Solution

Here \(i = 0.12/12 = 0.01\), \(n = 12\), and \(L = 10000\). Proposition 1.2 gives \(A = 10000 \times 0.01/(1 - 1.01^{-12}) = 100/0.1125508\). Since \(1.01^{12} = 1.1268250\), \(1.01^{-12} = 0.8874492\) and \(1 - 0.8874492 = 0.1125508\). So \(A = 888.4879\) CU, which to the nearest cent is 888.49 CU.

M2 · The first month's split

For the loan of M1, find the interest and the principal repaid in month 1, using the exact payment.

Solution

Interest is \(I_1 = iB_0 = 0.01 \times 10000 = 100.00\) CU. The principal is \(p_1 = A - I_1 = 888.4879 - 100 = 788.4879\) CU, which is 788.49 CU to the nearest cent. The balance after month 1 is \(B_1 = 10000 - 788.4879 = 9211.5121\) CU.

M3 · The second month's principal

For the loan of M1 in exact arithmetic, find the principal repaid in month 2, using Proposition 2.3.

Solution

By Proposition 2.3, \(p_2 = (1 + i)p_1 = 1.01 \times 788.4879 = 796.3728\) CU, which is 796.37 CU to the nearest cent. Check directly: \(I_2 = 0.01 \times 9211.5121 = 92.1151\) and \(p_2 = 888.4879 - 92.1151 = 796.3728\), the same figure.

Proof

P1 · The principal by induction

Prove by induction on \(k\) that \(p_k = p_1(1 + i)^{k-1}\) for \(1 \le k \le n\), using only the recursion of Definition 1.1 and the exact payment.

Solution

Base case. \(p_1 = p_1(1 + i)^0\). Inductive step. Assume \(p_k = p_1(1 + i)^{k-1}\) for some \(k < n\). The proof of Proposition 2.3 shows \(p_{k+1} = (1 + i)p_k\) from the recursion, using \(A - iB_{k-1} = p_k\). So \(p_{k+1} = (1 + i)p_1(1 + i)^{k-1} = p_1(1 + i)^{k}\). By Theorem 1.2 of Lesson 6, the formula holds for all \(1 \le k \le n\).

P2 · An overpayment at the end

Suppose each payment is the exact payment plus a fixed amount \(\delta > 0\), with the same schedule otherwise and no final adjustment. Show that the balance after month \(n\) is \(-\delta((1 + i)^n - 1)/i\), a negative number.

Solution

Replace \(A\) by \(A + \delta\) in Proposition 2.1 with \(k = n\). Then \(B_n = L(1 + i)^n - (A + \delta)((1 + i)^n - 1)/i\). Corollary 2.2 says \(L(1 + i)^n - A((1 + i)^n - 1)/i = 0\), so \(B_n = -\delta((1 + i)^n - 1)/i\). Since \(\delta > 0\), \(i > 0\) and \((1 + i)^n > 1\), the balance is negative: the borrower has overpaid, and the overpayment has compounded.

P3 · Total interest

Prove Proposition 2.4's total-interest identity \(\sum_{k=1}^{n} I_k = nA - L\) in exact arithmetic, and use it to find the total interest on the loan of M1.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 2.4 gives \(\sum_k I_k = nA - \sum_k p_k = nA - L\). For the loan of M1, \(n = 12\) and \(A = 888.4879\), so the total interest is \(12 \times 888.4878867 - 10000 = 10661.8546 - 10000 = 661.8546\) CU, which is 661.85 CU to the nearest cent.

Empirical

E1 · Lower the rate

Before running anything, predict whether the regular payment on the Lab 1 loan falls or rises if the nominal rate is 5 per cent instead of 6. Then compute it exactly.

Solution

A lower rate means less interest each month, so the payment falls. With \(i = 0.05/12\), Proposition 1.2 gives \(A = 250000 \times (0.05/12)/(1 - (1 + 0.05/12)^{-360}) = 1342.0541\) CU, which is 1,342.05 CU to the nearest cent, about 157 CU below the 6 per cent payment of 1,498.88 CU.

E2 · The interest share over the loan

In Lab 1, what share of the first payment is interest, and what share of the last payment is interest? Use the cent schedule's figures.

Solution

Month 1: \(1{,}250.00/1{,}498.88 = 83.4\%\) of the payment is interest. Month 360: \(7.44/1{,}495.45 = 0.5\%\). The share moves from most of the payment to almost none, because the interest falls with the balance while the payment stays level, as Misconception 1 explains.

E3 · The effective annual rate

Compute the effective annual rate of a nominal 6 per cent compounded monthly, and compare it with the value printed in Lab 1, part A.

Solution

The effective annual rate is \((1 + 0.06/12)^{12} - 1 = 1.005^{12} - 1\). Since \(1.005^{12} = 1.0616778\), the rate is \(0.0616778\), or 6.1678 per cent. Lab 1, part A prints 6.167781 per cent, which agrees to the printed precision.

Research

R1 · The balance as the value of the remaining payments

Using the exact payment \(A\), show that \(B_k = A\,\dfrac{1 - (1 + i)^{-(n-k)}}{i}\) for \(0 \le k \le n\).

Solution

From Proposition 1.2, \(L = A(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})/i\). Substitute into Proposition 2.1: \(B_k = \frac{A}{i}\bigl[(1 - (1 + i)^{-n})(1 + i)^{k} - ((1 + i)^{k} - 1)\bigr]\). Expanding the bracket gives \((1 + i)^{k} - (1 + i)^{k-n} - (1 + i)^{k} + 1 = 1 - (1 + i)^{-(n-k)}\). So \(B_k = A(1 - (1 + i)^{-(n-k)})/i\). This is the present value of the \(n - k\) remaining payments, which is the Lesson 7 annuity formula with \(n - k\) periods.

R2 · A half-cent overpayment each month

A borrower pays the exact payment plus 0.005 CU each month, for the 360-month loan of Lab 1. Use P2 to find the balance after month 360, and state what it means.

Solution

Here \(\delta = 0.005\) and \(i = 0.005\). The factor is \((1.005^{360} - 1)/0.005\). Since \(1.005^{360} = 6.0226\), the factor is \(5.0226/0.005 = 1004.5\). The balance after month 360 is \(-0.005 \times 1004.5 = -5.02\) CU. The borrower has paid 5.02 CU more than the loan requires, and the overpayment has grown at the monthly rate over the term. A lender would return that amount or credit it against the final payment.

R3 · The final payment in exact arithmetic

In exact arithmetic with the payment \(A\), show that the final payment \(B_{n-1} + I_n\) equals \(A\). Then explain why Lab 1's final payment is 3.43 CU below its regular payment, using the lab's printed values.

Solution

By R1 with \(k = n - 1\), \(B_{n-1} = A(1 - (1 + i)^{-1})/i = A/(1 + i)\). So \(B_{n-1} + I_n = B_{n-1} + iB_{n-1} = (1 + i)B_{n-1} = A\). In Lab 1 the regular payment is 1,498.88 CU, but the exact payment is 1,498.876313 CU. Each month, the regular payment overpays by 0.003687 CU, and by P2 that overpayment compounds to \(0.003687 \times 1004.5 = 3.70\) CU by month 360. With interest taken exactly, the lab's final payment would be 1,495.18 CU, which is 3.70 CU below the regular payment. Taking interest to the nearest cent moves the final payment to 1,495.45 CU, 0.27 CU higher, which is the drift the lab reports.
