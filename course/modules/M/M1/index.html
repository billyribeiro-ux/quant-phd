Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning

Some quantitative claims in finance have a quantifier, a domain and a formula inside them: a cash flow is discounted at a stated rate, some rate makes two schedules equal, a payment stream has a closed-form value. This module gives you the tools to read those claims exactly, to prove the formulas that value them, and to test the proofs against computed cases. It covers the two quantifiers and the proof techniques that use them, the exponential and logarithm from which the compounding formulas in this module are built, compounding and present value, geometric series and induction, annuities, perpetuities, growing perpetuities and loan amortization. You leave able to state a claim with its domain, to prove a closed form, and to implement and test an annuity and mortgage engine in Python.

  • Prerequisites: P0.1 How to Study This Program (the evidence grades, the notation conventions and the project ladder). Secondary-school algebra is assumed.
  • Project: Annuity and mortgage engine, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. The results are computed by the labs or the grader, or derived on the page where they appear. The placement bar and the hours are design choices, not results.

70 h lessons · 30 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of M1 you can:

  1. State a quantified claim with its domain, negate it by the rules of Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 of Lesson 1, and give a counterexample that refutes a universal claim.
  2. Prove a conditional claim by a direct proof, and a claim by contradiction, including the irrationality of the square root of 2 (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the rules of the exponential and the logarithm from their stated properties, and use them to solve for a time, a rate or a doubling time (Lesson 3).
  4. Compute compounding, discount factors and present values under a stated convention, and show how the convention changes the answer (Lessons 4 and 5).
  5. Prove a closed form for a finite geometric sum by induction, and derive the closed forms for annuities, perpetuities and growing perpetuities from it (Lessons 6 and 7).
  6. Construct a loan amortization schedule and check that its balance reaches zero and that each payment splits into interest and principal (Lesson 8).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): a payment of 1 is made at time 0, a payment of one half at time 1, and a payment of one quarter at time 2. What is the total of these three payments, as a decimal?

    Options

    The sum to \(n+1\) adds the term with exponent \(n\) to the sum to \(n\). This is the missing step of the completion problem in rung 2.

Lessons

Eight lessons of six to twelve study hours each, and the hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Lessons 1 to 3 cover the logic of quantifiers and proof and the exponential and logarithm. Lessons 4 and 5 cover compounding and present value. Lessons 6 and 7 cover geometric series, induction and the closed forms of the payment streams, and Lesson 8 covers loan amortization schedules. The lesson pages share one structure: graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 8 hSets, Quantifiers and Logical StatementsPredicates over a stated domain, the two quantifiers, the rules for negating a quantified statement, the order of quantifiers, and what a finite check can refute. L02 · 9 hDirect Proof and Proof by ContradictionImplications, direct proofs, the parity of integers and squares, and the proof that no rational number has square 2. L03 · 9 hExponential and Logarithm FunctionsThe exponential and natural logarithm from three stated properties, the rules derived from them, doubling time and the rate of a growth factor. L04 · 9 hCompounding Frequency and Continuous CompoundingThe effective annual rate under different compounding frequencies, and the convergence of \((1 + r/m)^m\) to \(e^r\) as \(m\) grows, observed numerically. L05 · 8 hPresent Value and the Discount FactorDiscount factors under annual and continuous conventions, and how the convention changes a present value. L06 · 9 hGeometric Series and Proof by InductionProof by induction, defined before it is used, and the closed form of a finite geometric sum. L07 · 9 hAnnuities, Perpetuities and Growing PerpetuitiesClosed forms for the three payment streams, checked against direct summation, and the condition \(r > g\) for a growing perpetuity. L08 · 9 hLoan Amortization SchedulesA full amortization table for an illustrative loan, the final-payment rule, and the identity that interest plus principal equals the payment.

The project ladder

The module ends in the annuity and mortgage engine. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated closed-form computation for a fixed annuity, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same task with the induction step and the amortization update removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement annuity_engine.py with the closed forms for annuity, perpetuity and growing perpetuity, the amortization schedule, and the proof write-up. Graded by the autograder against generated inputs and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a schedule with irregular payments or a proof that the amortization balance is a geometric sequence, suitable for your portfolio.

Data status
