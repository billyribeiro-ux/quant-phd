Rungs 1 and 2

Both labs use one illustrative loan: $250,000.00 at a nominal annual rate of 6.00 %, compounded monthly (the monthly rate is that annual rate divided by 12), repaid over 360 months. The loan is an illustrative input. It is not market data, and no file is read.

Lab 1 · Worked example: one loan, step by step

The monthly rate is \(i = 0.06/12 = 0.005\). The annuity factor is \(a = (1-(1+i)^{-360})/i\), and the level payment is the loan divided by \(a\), to the nearest cent. Expected: a payment of 149888 cents, interest of 125000 cents in month 1, and a closing balance of exactly 0 cents in month 360.

"""Rung 1 worked example: the level payment and the full schedule of one fixed-rate loan, in integer cents."""

import math


def nearest_cent(amount: float) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of a non-negative amount, with a tie going up."""
    return math.floor(amount + 0.5)


def main() -> None:
    principal_cents = 25_000_000  # $250,000.00, an illustrative loan, not market data
    annual_rate = 0.06  # nominal annual rate, compounded monthly
    months = 360
    # Step 1. The monthly rate is the nominal annual rate divided by 12, not a conversion to an effective rate.
    i = annual_rate / 12
    # Step 2. The annuity factor a = (1 - (1 + i)^-n) / i is the present value of one dollar per month.
    factor = (1 - (1 + i) ** -months) / i
    # Step 3. The level payment is the loan divided by the factor, to the nearest cent.
    payment = nearest_cent(principal_cents / factor)
    print("monthly rate:", f"{i:.6f}", "| annuity factor:", f"{factor:.6f}")
    print("level payment (cents):", payment)
    # Step 4. Each month: interest on the opening balance, principal is what remains of the payment.
    balance = principal_cents
    total_interest = 0
    for month in range(1, months + 1):
        interest = nearest_cent(balance * i)
        paid = payment if month < months else balance + interest  # the last payment clears the balance exactly
        principal = paid - interest
        balance = balance - principal
        total_interest += interest
        if month <= 2 or month == months:
            print(f"month {month}: payment {paid}, interest {interest}, principal {principal}, balance {balance}")
    print("final balance (cents):", balance)
    print("total interest (cents):", total_interest)

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A lender quotes a monthly payment on each fixed-rate loan it makes. It needs the value of an annuity, of a perpetuity and of a growing perpetuity, each in closed form, and the schedule of each loan with every amount in exact cents, so that the schedule can be checked by a test suite rather than by eye. You write the module to the interface below, prove the four identities it rests on, and show that it reproduces the reference numbers of one illustrative loan.
Inputs
No data file. The autograder's instance comes from grader/instance.py, seeded by your workspace ID. It holds a loan in integer cents, an annual rate in basis points and a term in months, together with an annuity, a perpetuity and a growth rate. All of it is SIMULATED and none of it is a real loan or market rate. The report uses the illustrative loan of Lab 3 ($300,000.00 at 4.50 % over 300 months), which is an illustrative input and not data.
Required outputs
In a repository of your own: annuity_engine.py at its root, and a report/ folder beside it. The module has these names: the frozen dataclass ScheduleRow, with the fields period, opening_cents, interest_cents, principal_cents, payment_cents and closing_cents in that order; the functions annuity_factor(rate, periods), annuity_due_factor(rate, periods), perpetuity_factor(rate), growing_perpetuity_factor(rate, growth), level_payment_cents(principal_cents, annual_rate, months) and amortization_schedule(principal_cents, annual_rate, months). Start from the starter file, whose signatures and ScheduleRow are complete. For the report, commit three more files: report/run_report.py, which is Lab 3 with its closed-form and schedule calls replaced by calls to your module and which prints the same lines; report/report.md, the report described in the brief with its proof appendix; and report/self_assessment.md, the summary that the form in the self-assessment section produces.
Constraints
Standard library only. Every function validates its inputs and raises ValueError for the conditions the conventions below name, including a non-finite number, a boolean where a number is meant, and a float where a whole number is meant. No function prints or warns. A run in your browser stops after 120 seconds. The conventions are listed in the next subsection, and they are binding.
Grading mode
Both modes apply. The code is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which runs 188 checks with warnings as errors: the closed forms against direct summation and against exact rational arithmetic, the finite identities behind them, the schedule row for row against the exact schedule of your seed, the invariants of the schedule, the zero-rate case, the invalid inputs, booleans included, and the purity of the module. The same file runs in your browser and on your machine. It is not secret: its integrity comes from your seeded instance and from a second instance that a fixed answer cannot pass. The report and its proof appendix are graded by rubric, against the five criteria of the report rubric, each with four observable levels. You score it yourself with the form in the self-assessment section and calibrate that scoring on two annotated exemplars. The report meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Conventions

  • A rate is a per-period decimal: \(0.005\) is half a percent. The monthly rate of a loan is its nominal annual rate divided by 12, compounded monthly. It is not converted to an effective annual rate, the rate that compounds to the same growth in one year.
  • Money in level_payment_cents and amortization_schedule is an integer number of cents. Each cent amount is the exact value of its formula, computed in exact rational arithmetic on the annual rate as it is written as a decimal, before it is rounded: it goes to the nearest cent, and an exact half cent goes up. The closed forms return floats.
  • annuity_factor(rate, periods) is \(a = \sum_{k=1}^{n} v^k\) with \(v = 1/(1+r)\). It requires \(r > -1\) and a whole number of periods of zero or more. Zero periods give 0, and a zero rate gives the number of periods.
  • annuity_due_factor counts the first payment at the start of the first period: the ordinary factor times \((1+r)\).
  • perpetuity_factor(rate) is \(1/r\), the value of a payment of 1 at the end of every period. It requires \(r > 0\). At a rate of zero or less it raises ValueError.
  • growing_perpetuity_factor(rate, growth) is \(1/(r-g)\), the value of a payment of 1 at the end of period 1 that grows by \((1+g)\) each period. It requires \(r > 0\) and \(-1 < g < r\), and raises ValueError otherwise.
  • level_payment_cents is \(L\,i/(1-(1+i)^{-n})\) with \(L\) the principal in cents, \(n\) the months and \(i = r/12\), to the nearest cent. At a zero rate it is \(L/n\), to the nearest cent.
  • amortization_schedule returns a tuple of ScheduleRow, one per month. Month 1 opens at the loan. Each month's interest is the opening balance times \(i\), to the nearest cent. Months 1 to \(n-1\) pay the level payment. Month \(n\) pays the opening balance plus its interest. The closing balance is the opening balance less the principal paid, and the closing balance of month \(n\) is exactly 0.
  • The loan inputs of level_payment_cents and amortization_schedule are: principal_cents, a whole number of cents of one or more; annual_rate, a rate of zero or more; months, a whole number of months of one or more. Each of these raises ValueError: a principal that is not a whole number of cents of one or more, including zero and a negative amount; a rate that is negative or not finite; a term that is not a whole number of months of one or more, including zero and a negative term.
  • Each invalid input named in this section raises ValueError. An integer amount given as a float, or a boolean, is invalid.

Lab 3 · The report's reference numbers

The loan is $300,000.00 (30000000 cents) at a nominal 4.50 %, compounded monthly, repaid over 300 months, so \(i = 0.00375\). Stated before running: the level payment is a whole number of cents, the final balance is exactly 0, the principal sums to the loan exactly, and the final payment is within the bound \(n(1+i)^n + 1\) cents of the level payment. The growing perpetuity's closed form is compared with a direct sum of 400 terms, and the lab finds the least number of payments N whose perpetuity partial sum has error \(v^N/r\) below 10^-6, where \(v = 1/(1+r)\), against the perpetuity value 1/r at r = 5 %. """Rung 3 reference numbers: the payments, the schedule totals and the closed forms the report quotes.

Standard library only. Copy this file into report/run_report.py, then replace the schedule and factor calls
with calls to your annuity_engine module. Your module must print the same numbers.
"""

import math


def nearest_cent(amount: float) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of a non-negative amount, with a tie going up."""
    return math.floor(amount + 0.5)


def annuity_factor(rate: float, periods: int) -> float:
    """PV of one unit paid at the end of each of `periods` periods at a per-period rate; rate 0 gives periods."""
    if rate == 0:
        return float(periods)
    return (1 - (1 + rate) ** -periods) / rate


def main() -> None:
    principal_cents = 30_000_000  # $300,000.00, an illustrative loan
    annual_rate = 0.045  # nominal, compounded monthly
    months = 300
    i = annual_rate / 12
    factor = annuity_factor(i, months)
    payment = nearest_cent(principal_cents / factor)
    balance = principal_cents
    interest_total = 0
    principal_total = 0
    payments_total = 0
    for month in range(1, months + 1):
        interest = nearest_cent(balance * i)
        paid = payment if month < months else balance + interest
        principal = paid - interest
        balance -= principal
        interest_total += interest
        principal_total += principal
        payments_total += paid
    print("level payment (cents):", payment)
    first_interest = nearest_cent(principal_cents * i)
    print("month 1 (cents): payment", payment, "| interest", first_interest, "| principal", payment - first_interest)
    print("final payment (cents):", paid, "| minus level payment:", paid - payment)
    print("final balance (cents):", balance)
    bound = months * (1 + i) ** months + 1
    print("bound on final minus level payment (cents):", f"{bound:.4f}")
    print("final minus level within bound:", abs(paid - payment) <= bound)
    print("sum of principal = loan:", principal_total == principal_cents)
    print("total interest (cents):", interest_total)
    print("total paid (cents):", payments_total)
    print("annuity factor:", f"{factor:.9f}")
    print("annuity due factor:", f"{factor * (1 + i):.9f}")
    print("perpetuity factor at 5%:", f"{1 / 0.05:.9f}")
    growth_factor = 1 / (0.05 - 0.02)
    print("growing perpetuity factor at 5%, growth 2%:", f"{growth_factor:.9f}")
    tail = sum(((1 + 0.02) ** (k - 1)) / (1.05**k) for k in range(1, 401))
    print("direct sum, 400 terms, growth 2%:", f"{tail:.9f}")
    print("exact tail after 400 terms, q^N / (r - g):", f"{((1 + 0.02) / 1.05) ** 400 / 0.03:.3e}")
    # The perpetuity's partial sum after N payments has error v^N / r exactly; find the least N with error below 1e-6.
    v = 1 / 1.05
    n_tolerance = 1
    while v**n_tolerance / 0.05 >= 1e-6:
        n_tolerance += 1
    print("least N with perpetuity error below 1e-6 (payments):", n_tolerance)
    print("perpetuity error at N - 1 payments:", f"{v ** (n_tolerance - 1) / 0.05:.4e}")

The level payment is \(P = L\,i/(1-(1+i)^{-n})\), to the nearest cent, computed by level_payment_cents. Each month's interest is the opening balance times \(i\), to the nearest cent, and the last payment is the opening balance plus its interest; the schedule is computed by amortization_schedule. The four factors are computed by annuity_factor, annuity_due_factor, perpetuity_factor and growing_perpetuity_factor. Every amount and factor the report quotes is printed by run_report.py, as Lab 3 prints it; the loan amount, the term and the annual rate 0.045 are inputs, and the monthly rate is written only as \(0.045/12\), which the run does not print. Lab 3 prints the level payment, the first month, the final payment and the totals, and the suite checks each row of the schedule, for a seeded loan, against the exact rule.

  • Meets C5 level 3: the section sits under its heading and names the module function behind each number, as brief item 2 requires.
Closed forms

Let \(v = 1/(1+r)\). The annuity factor is \(a = (1-v^n)/r\), and its due version is \(a(1+r)\). Lab 3 prints 179.9 for \(a\) at \(r = 0.045/12\), \(n = 300\), and 180.6 for the due factor. The perpetuity value \(1/r\) is 20.00 at \(r = 5\,\%\). Its partial sum after \(N\) payments is \((1-v^N)/r\), so the error after \(N\) payments is exactly \(v^N/r\). For the stated tolerance \(10^{-6}\), \(v^N/r < 10^{-6}\) is \(v^N < 10^{-6}\,r\), which holds when \(N > \ln(10^{-6}\,r)/\ln v\); the least such whole \(N\) is 345, and at 344 payments the error is \(1.028 \times 10^{-6}\), both printed by Lab 3. The growing perpetuity \(1/(r-g)\) is 33.33 at \(r = 5\,\%\) and \(g = 2\,\%\). The 400-term sum is 33.33 to four significant digits, and its tail is \(q^{400}/(r-g) = 3.071 \times 10^{-4}\), with \(q = (1+g)/(1+r)\). The proofs are in A1 and A2.

  • Meets C1 level 4: gives the exact error of the perpetuity's partial sum, the least number of payments \(N = 345\) that brings it below the stated tolerance \(10^{-6}\), printed by Lab 3, and the tail that the 400-term check leaves.
  • Meets C3 level 3: every factor is named as a factor and quoted to four significant digits, as the brief requires.
The schedule

The first month pays 166750 cents, with interest of 112500 cents and principal of 54250 cents. Months 1 to 299 pay the level payment, and month 300 pays 166630 cents. The principal column sums to the loan, 30000000 cents, by integer equality (the run prints True). The total paid, 50024880 cents, is the loan plus the total interest of 20024880 cents. The cent amounts are exact integers. The factors are floats, and the report quotes them to four significant digits. The difference matters for the checks: the principal sum equals the loan by integer equality, which holds exactly, while the non-zero factors are compared to tolerances set in the suite, relative where the value is at least one and absolute below one; the zero-rate and zero-period factors are compared exactly. The schedule's other months are not quoted here; the report quotes their totals. The final payment is 120 cents below the level payment, inside the bound of 923.1228 cents that A4 derives.

Limitations

The monthly rate is the nominal rate over 12, which is \(0.045/12\). Compounded monthly, it gives an effective annual rate of \((1+0.045/12)^{12}\), which exceeds the 4.50 % quoted: by the binomial theorem, \((1+i)^{12} = 1 + 12i + \dots > 1 + 12i\), where \(12i\) is the input rate 0.045, since every later term is positive. So the monthly rate that compounds to exactly 4.50 % in a year is smaller than \(0.045/12\). The annuity factor is a sum of \(v^k\), each of which falls as \(i\) rises, so the level payment \(L/a\) rises with \(i\): the nominal convention gives a larger level payment than the effective-rate convention would. The cent rule leaves the final payment 120 cents below the level payment, and A4 bounds that gap at 923.1228 cents. The model has no fees, taxes, insurance, prepayment or rate changes. At a zero rate the payment is the loan over the months, and a perpetuity has no value. A zero closing balance says that the payment rule clears the loan. It says nothing about whether the borrower can pay it.

  • Meets C4 level 3: says the zero balance is a fact of the payment rule, and names the convention, the cent rule and the missing fees and prepayment.
  • Misses C4 level 4: does not state the direction the omitted fees, taxes and prepayment would move the payment.
Appendix A · Proofs

A1. Assume \(r \ne 0\) and \(r > -1\), so \(v = 1/(1+r)\) is defined and \(v \ne 1\). Telescoping gives \((1-v)\sum_{k=1}^{n} v^k = v - v^{n+1}\), so \(\sum_{k=1}^{n} v^k = v(1-v^n)/(1-v)\). Since \(1-v = r/(1+r)\), \(v/(1-v) = 1/r\), and the sum is \((1-v^n)/r\). For \(r = 0\) the sum is \(n\), by counting.

A2. Assume \(r > 0\), so \(0 < v < 1\), and \(-1 < g < r\). The sum \(\sum_{k=1}^{N} v^k = (1-v^N)/r\) by A1, so the error after \(N\) payments against \(1/r\) is exactly \(v^N/r\). Let \(q = (1+g)v\), so \(0 < q < 1\). Then \(\sum_{k=1}^{N} (1+g)^{k-1}v^k = v(1-q^N)/(1-q)\). Since \(v(1+r) = 1\), \(v/(1-q) = v(1+r)/(r-g) = 1/(r-g)\), so the sum is \((1-q^N)/(r-g)\), with tail \(q^N/(r-g)\).

A3. Assume \(i \ne 0\), since the identity divides by \(i\); at \(i = 0\) the same recursion gives \(B_k = L - kP\). In exact arithmetic let \(B_k\) be the balance after \(k\) payments, with \(B_0 = L\) and \(B_{k+1} = (1+i)B_k - P\). Claim: \(B_k = L(1+i)^k - P((1+i)^k-1)/i\). For \(k = 0\) this is \(L\). If it holds for \(k\), then \(B_{k+1} = L(1+i)^{k+1} - P((1+i)^{k+1}-1)/i\), by expanding \((1+i)B_k - P\). With \(P = Li/(1-(1+i)^{-n})\) and \(1-(1+i)^{-n} = ((1+i)^n-1)/(1+i)^n\), the balance at \(k = n\) is \(L(1+i)^n - L(1+i)^n = 0\).

A4. Assume \(i \ge 0\), as the conventions require, so \((1+i)^k \ge 1\) for every \(k\). Let the cent schedule have payment \(P\) and interest \(B_k i + \varepsilon_k\) with \(|\varepsilon_k| \le 1/2\), and let \(P_0\) be the exact payment, so \(|P-P_0| \le 1/2\). The exact path \(B^e\) has \(B^e_{k+1} = (1+i)B^e_k - P_0\), and \(D_k = B_k - B^e_k\) obeys \(D_0 = 0\), \(D_{k+1} = (1+i)D_k + \delta_k\) with \(|\delta_k| \le 1\). By induction, \(|D_k| \le k(1+i)^{k-1}\): the case \(k = 1\) is \(|D_1| \le 1\), and \(|D_{k+1}| \le (1+i)\,k(1+i)^{k-1} + 1 = k(1+i)^k + 1 \le (k+1)(1+i)^k\), since \(1 \le (1+i)^k\). The final payment is \(F = (1+i)B_{n-1} + \varepsilon_{n-1}\), and \((1+i)B^e_{n-1} = P_0\), so \(|F - P_0| \le (n-1)(1+i)^{n-1} + 1/2\). Since \(F - P_0 = (1+i)D_{n-1} + \varepsilon_{n-1}\) and \(|P-P_0| \le 1/2\), the triangle inequality gives \(|F-P| \le (n-1)(1+i)^{n-1} + 1 \le n(1+i)^n + 1\) cents.

  • Meets C1 level 4: states the assumptions of A1 and A2, and the error \(v^N/r\) exactly.
  • Meets C2 level 4: derives the bound in A4 step by step, and A3 by induction.
