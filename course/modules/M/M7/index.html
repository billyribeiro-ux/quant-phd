Optimization

A portfolio is a choice of weights that must satisfy a budget and a set of limits, and the best choice is the one that optimizes an objective under those constraints. This module gives you the theory that makes such problems tractable: convex sets and convex functions, the Lagrange multiplier for equality constraints, the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions for inequality constraints, Lagrangian duality, quadratic programming, the mean-variance problem with linear portfolio constraints, and the principle of optimality with the Bellman equation for problems that unfold over time. You leave able to write the optimality conditions of a constrained problem, to check a claimed solution against them, and to implement and certify a constrained allocator in Python.

  • Prerequisites: M2 Calculus (gradients, Hessians, the implicit function theorem) and M3 Linear Algebra (positive semidefinite matrices, eigenvalues, the spectral theorem). Both reach M1 and P0.1 through their own prerequisites.
  • Project: Constrained allocator, graded by an autograder against generated instances
  • Data: none. The instances and the inputs of this module are generated from a seed or chosen by hand, and each is labelled SIMULATED on the page that uses it. Estimation of those inputs is outside this module.

77 h lessons · 33 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of M7 you can:

  1. State the definitions of a convex set and a convex function, and prove that a quadratic form is convex exactly when its matrix is positive semidefinite (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the Lagrange condition for an equality constraint under a stated regularity hypothesis, and solve the minimum-variance problem under a budget constraint in closed form (Lesson 2).
  3. Write the KKT conditions for a problem with equality and inequality constraints, and compute their four residuals at a candidate solution (Lesson 3).
  4. Derive the Lagrangian dual of a problem, and verify weak duality and the zero gap of a convex quadratic program under Slater's condition (Lesson 4).
  5. Solve an equality-constrained quadratic program from its KKT linear system, and check the answer against an independent enumeration (Lesson 5).
  6. Formulate a mean-variance allocation with box, turnover and sector constraints as a quadratic program, and trace the efficient frontier with and without a short-sale constraint (Lesson 6).
  7. Compute a finite-horizon value function by backward induction, and state the Bellman equation that it satisfies (Lessons 7 and 8).
  8. Certify a constrained allocator by checking the KKT residuals of its output on generated instances (project).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on the prerequisites M2 and M3 and on the logic of M1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. For \(f(x) = x_1^2 + 3 x_1 x_2\), what is \(\partial f / \partial x_1\) at the point \((1, 0)\)?

    \(\partial f / \partial x_1 = 2 x_1 + 3 x_2\), which equals 2 at \((1, 0)\). This is the M2 gradient.

  2. For the same \(f(x) = x_1^2 + 3 x_1 x_2\), what is the mixed second derivative \(\partial^2 f / \partial x_1 \partial x_2\)?

    Differentiating \(2 x_1 + 3 x_2\) with respect to \(x_2\) gives 3. The Hessian of \(f\) has entries \(2\), \(3\) and \(0\).

  3. Which matrix is positive semidefinite?

    Options

    The identity gives \(x^{\top} I x = \|x\|^2 \ge 0\). The other two matrices have a negative quadratic form at some vector, so they are not positive semidefinite (M3).

  4. What is the smaller eigenvalue of \(\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\)?

    The eigenvalues are 3 and 1, with eigenvectors \((1, 1)\) and \((1, -1)\) (M3).

  5. What is the negation of "every asset in the portfolio has a non-negative weight"?

    Options

    The negation of a universal statement is an existential statement with the negated predicate (M1 Lesson 1, Theorem 3.1).

  6. Which statement about a real symmetric matrix is true?

    Options

    The spectral theorem (M3) gives real eigenvalues and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for every real symmetric matrix. Positivity is not implied, as the matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1, -1)\) shows.

  7. Skill check (rung 1): for the problem of minimizing \(\tfrac12 w^{\top} \Sigma w\) subject to \(\mathbf{1}^{\top} w = 1\) with \(\Sigma\) positive definite, which equation is the stationarity condition at the minimizer \(w^*\)?

    Options

    The worked example of rung 1 derives this line: the gradient of \(\tfrac12 w^{\top} \Sigma w\) is \(\Sigma w\), and it must be a multiple of the constraint normal \(\mathbf{1}\) (Lesson 2, Proposition 3.2).

  8. Skill check (rung 2): for \(\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)\) and the budget \(w_1 + w_2 = 1\), the completion problem asks for the multiplier \(\lambda\) in \(\Sigma w^* = \lambda \mathbf{1}\). What is \(\lambda\), as a decimal to four places?

    Solving \(\Sigma u = \mathbf{1}\) gives \(u = (1, \tfrac12)\), so \(\lambda = 1 / (1 + \tfrac12) = \tfrac23 = 0.6667\) to four places. This is the stationarity step of the completion problem in rung 2.

  9. What is the derivative of \(x^2\) at \(x = 3\)?

    The derivative of \(x^2\) is \(2x\), which equals 6 at \(x = 3\) (M2).

Lessons

Eight lessons of nine or ten study hours each, totalling 77 hours. Lessons 1, 2, 7 declare 9 hours and the others 10 hours, and the hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Lessons 1 and 2 cover convexity and the Lagrange condition. Lessons 3 and 4 cover the KKT conditions and duality. Lessons 5 and 6 cover quadratic programming and the mean-variance problem. Lessons 7 and 8 cover the principle of optimality and the Bellman equation. Each lesson page has graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 9 hConvex Sets and Convex FunctionsThe chord definitions of convex sets and convex functions, the identity that gives the gap between a quadratic form and its chord, and the eigenvalue test for convexity of a quadratic form. L02 · 9 hLagrange Multipliers for Equality ConstraintsThe Lagrange condition with its regularity hypothesis, why stationarity is not sufficient, and the closed form of the minimum-variance weights under a budget constraint. L03 · 10 hThe KKT ConditionsStationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility and complementary slackness for problems with inequality constraints, and their residuals at a candidate solution. L04 · 10 hLagrangian DualityThe dual function and the dual problem, weak duality, and the zero duality gap of a convex quadratic program under Slater's condition, checked on a grid. L05 · 10 hQuadratic ProgrammingThe equality-constrained quadratic program solved from its KKT linear system in exact arithmetic, checked against an enumeration of the active sets. L06 · 10 hMean-Variance Optimization and Linear Portfolio ConstraintsThe mean-variance problem as a quadratic program, the box, turnover and sector constraints, and the efficient frontier with and without a short-sale constraint, on simulated inputs. L07 · 9 hThe Principle of Optimality and Finite-Horizon Dynamic ProgrammingBackward induction for a finite-horizon problem, checked against an enumeration of all plans on a finite-state toy problem. L08 · 10 hThe Bellman EquationThe finite-horizon Bellman recursion, value iteration on a finite-state problem with the error reported, and a statement of what this module does not treat: the infinite-horizon result.

The project ladder

The module ends in the constrained allocator. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated three-asset quadratic program with box bounds, with the reason for every KKT line.

  2. Completion

    The same problem with the stationarity line and two dual-sign lines removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent

    Implement constrained_allocator.py with the equality and inequality solver, the linear constraints and the KKT certificate. Graded by the autograder against generated instances. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a parametric frontier traced by changing the risk-aversion parameter, comparing the budget multiplier with the rate at which the optimal value changes with the budget, a case computed in Lesson 2, Problem R3.

Data status

No real data is used in M7. The labs and the grader compute from formulas and from inputs that are generated from a stated seed or chosen by hand, and each input is labelled SIMULATED on the page that uses it. The module does not estimate its inputs from data: the mean-variance inputs are given, and estimating a covariance matrix is outside M7. No lab reads a data file, and no figure in this module shows a real asset, company or market.

Core reading

No passage locator from either has been confirmed, so no statement on any lesson page is attributed to them. Lesson pages cite them only by their registry keys.
