The mean-variance problem of this lesson is a quadratic program: a quadratic risk term, a linear reward for return, and linear constraints. This lesson writes the problem in that form, solves its equality-constrained case in closed form, and proves how its solution moves with the parameter \(\gamma\) of Definition 1.1, which weights the risk term against return. It then adds linear constraints (Section 3). One of them limits total trading, a sum of absolute values; Section 3 writes it as linear inequalities (Proposition 3.4). Lab 1 computes a frontier on simulated inputs with and without a nonnegativity constraint. Its numbers describe that one sample only.

1The mean-variance objective

Let \(n\) assets have one-period returns. Take as given a vector \(\mu \in \R^{n}\) of returns for the period, and a symmetric positive definite \(n \times n\) matrix \(\Sigma\). Both are inputs, and this unit does not estimate them: estimating a covariance matrix is outside M7. A weight vector \(w\) lists the fraction of wealth in each asset, and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) means the weights sum to one.

Definition 1.1 (the mean-variance objective)

For a risk-aversion parameter \(\gamma > 0\), the objective is \(f_{\gamma}(w) = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\,w^{\top}\Sigma w - \mu^{\top}w\). The first term is \(\gamma/2\) times the quadratic form \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\), which this unit calls the risk term and does not identify with any risk measure. The second term is the return \(\mu^{\top}w\). The parameter \(\gamma\) sets their relative weight.

Definition 1.2 (mean-variance optimization)

The mean-variance optimization problem at \(\gamma\) is \(\min f_{\gamma}(w)\) over the feasible set \(\{w : \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\). It is the quadratic program of L05 with \(Q = \gamma\Sigma\), \(c = -\mu\), \(A = \mathbf{1}^{\top}\) and \(b = 1\). Lab 1 adds the constraint \(w \ge 0\) in its second case.

Theorem 1.3 (the minimiser in closed form)

Let \(\gamma > 0\) and let \(\Sigma\) be symmetric positive definite, as in Section 1. The mean-variance problem with \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) has a unique minimiser \(w(\gamma)\). With \(y = \Sigma^{-1}\mu\) and \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}\), it is

\[w(\gamma) = \frac{y - \lambda z}{\gamma}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}y - \gamma}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}z}, \]

and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\). The multiplier \(\lambda\) is the one in the KKT system of L05, with the sign used there.

Proof

The matrix \(\gamma\Sigma\) is positive definite, because \(\Sigma\) is and \(\gamma > 0\). The constraint matrix \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\) has full row rank, since it is a nonzero row. So Theorem 2.3 of L05 gives a unique minimiser, and it is the solution of \(\gamma\Sigma w + \lambda\mathbf{1} = \mu\), \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\). The first equation gives \(w = (\Sigma^{-1}\mu - \lambda\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1})/\gamma = (y - \lambda z)/\gamma\). Multiplying by \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\) and using \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) gives \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}y - \lambda\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = \gamma\), which is the formula for \(\lambda\). For positivity, \(z\) is not zero, since \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1} \ne 0\). Then \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = z^{\top}\Sigma z > 0\), using \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1}\) and the positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\).

Remark 1.4 (positive definiteness of the simulated Sigma)

Lab 1 constructs \(\Sigma = M^{\top}M/16 + I/10\) for an integer matrix \(M\). For any \(x\), \(x^{\top}\Sigma x = \|Mx\|^{2}/16 + \|x\|^{2}/10\), which is positive unless \(x = 0\). So the matrix is positive definite, and Theorem 1.3 applies to it.

2How the solution moves with risk aversion

Call the points \(\bigl(w(\gamma)^{\top}\Sigma w(\gamma),\; \mu^{\top}w(\gamma)\bigr)\), for \(\gamma > 0\), the trade-off curve of the problem. The lab's title calls this curve the efficient frontier, in an informal sense. This lesson does not prove that the curve traced by \(\gamma\) is the whole efficient set.

Proposition 2.1 (the risk term and the return do not rise as \(\gamma\) rises)

Let \(F'\) be any set of weight vectors, and for \(\gamma > 0\) let \(w(\gamma)\) minimise \(f_{\gamma}\) over \(F'\). Write \(V(\gamma) = w(\gamma)^{\top}\Sigma w(\gamma)\) and \(R(\gamma) = \mu^{\top}w(\gamma)\). If \(0 < \gamma_{2} < \gamma_{1}\), then \(V(\gamma_{1}) \le V(\gamma_{2})\) and \(R(\gamma_{1}) \le R(\gamma_{2})\).

Proof

Write \(w_{1} = w(\gamma_{1})\) and \(w_{2} = w(\gamma_{2})\). Both lie in \(F'\), so minimality of each against the other gives

\[\tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}V(\gamma_{1}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}V(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{2}), \qquad \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}V(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{2}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}V(\gamma_{1}) - R(\gamma_{1}).\]

Adding the two inequalities, the \(R\) terms cancel, leaving \(\tfrac{\gamma_{1} - \gamma_{2}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{1}) - V(\gamma_{2})\big) \le 0\). Since \(\gamma_{1} > \gamma_{2}\), this gives \(V(\gamma_{1}) \le V(\gamma_{2})\). For the return, the second inequality gives \(R(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \ge \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1})\big)\), and the first gives \(R(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1})\big)\). Since \(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1}) \ge 0\) and \(\gamma_{2} > 0\), the lower bound is nonnegative, so \(R(\gamma_{2}) \ge R(\gamma_{1})\).

Remark 2.2 (the same objective, so the same argument)

Proposition 2.1 needs no property of \(\Sigma\) beyond the definition of \(V\), and no property of \(F'\) beyond the minimisation. So it covers the equality-only problem and the problem with \(w \ge 0\) used in Proposition 2.3 and Part B of Lab 1.

Proposition 2.3 (a constraint cannot lower the optimal objective)

Let \(F_{0} = \{w : \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\) and \(F_{+} = \{w \in F_{0} : w \ge 0\}\). For each \(\gamma > 0\), the infimum of \(f_{\gamma}\) over \(F_{+}\) is at least the infimum over \(F_{0}\).

Proof

\(F_{+} \subseteq F_{0}\), so every point of \(F_{+}\) is a point of \(F_{0}\). The infimum over the larger set is no larger than the infimum over the smaller one.

3Linear portfolio constraints

Definition 3.1 (linear portfolio constraint)

A linear portfolio constraint is an inequality \(a^{\top}w \le \beta\) or an equality \(a^{\top}w = \beta\), with \(a\) and \(\beta\) given. Section 1 and Part A of Lab 1 use the budget constraint alone. Proposition 2.3 and Part B of Lab 1 add the nonnegativity constraint. The sector constraint is defined here and not used on this page. The turnover constraint of Definition 3.2 is written in linear form in Proposition 3.4 and checked in Part D of Lab 1. The budget constraint \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) is an equality. A box constraint \(l_{i} \le w_{i} \le u_{i}\) is two inequalities for each asset. A sector constraint \(\sum_{i \in S_{k}} w_{i} \le c_{k}\) caps the total weight of a set \(S_{k}\) of assets, with \(c_{k}\) given. The nonnegativity constraint \(w \ge 0\) is the box with \(l = 0\) and no upper bound.

Definition 3.2 (turnover)

Let \(w_{0}\) be a given starting portfolio. The turnover of \(w\) relative to \(w_{0}\) is \(\sum_{i} |w_{i} - w_{0,i}|\). The turnover constraint is that this quantity is at most a given \(\tau \ge 0\).

Remark 3.3 (an absolute value is not a linear inequality)

The constraint \(|w_{i} - w_{0,i}| \le s\) is not one linear inequality in \(w\) alone. It is a pair of linear inequalities, \(w_{i} - w_{0,i} \le s\) and \(w_{0,i} - w_{i} \le s\). Proposition 3.4 uses this to write the turnover constraint with auxiliary variables.

Proposition 3.4 (the turnover constraint with auxiliary variables)

Let \(w_{0}\) and \(\tau\) be given real vectors and a real number, with no sign assumption. Then \(\sum_{i}|w_{i} - w_{0,i}| \le \tau\) holds if and only if there is a vector \(t\) with \(t_{i} \ge w_{i} - w_{0,i}\), \(t_{i} \ge w_{0,i} - w_{i}\) for every \(i\), and \(\sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\).

Proof

Put \(d_{i} = w_{i} - w_{0,i}\). If the turnover is at most \(\tau\), take \(t_{i} = |d_{i}|\). Then \(t_{i} = |d_{i}| \ge d_{i}\) and \(t_{i} = |d_{i}| \ge -d_{i}\), and \(\sum_{i}t_{i} = \sum_{i}|d_{i}| \le \tau\). Conversely, let \(t\) satisfy the inequalities. Then \(t_{i} \ge d_{i}\) and \(t_{i} \ge -d_{i}\), so \(t_{i} \ge |d_{i}|\), and \(\sum_{i}|d_{i}| \le \sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\).

Remark 3.5 (the linear form is still a QP)

The variables \((w, t)\) enter linearly in the constraints of Proposition 3.4, and the objective \(f_{\gamma}\) depends on \(w\) only. The problem with the turnover constraint is therefore a quadratic program in \((w, t)\) with a positive semidefinite Hessian on the \(w\) block and zero on the \(t\) block. Lab 1 does not solve this problem. Its Part D checks only the linearization.

Lab 1 · The efficient frontier with and without the short-sale constraint, on simulated inputs

SIMULATED: the four assets A1 to A4, the return vector \(\mu\) and the matrix \(\Sigma\) are generated from a fixed seed by an integer recurrence, and no data are used. Stated before running. Part A solves the mean-variance problem with the budget alone, for \(\gamma\) in 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16, by the KKT system and by the closed form of Theorem 1.3. It predicts an exact match and an exact budget. Part B adds \(w \ge 0\), enumerates the 15 sets of assets fixed at zero that leave at least one asset free, and predicts one passing candidate for each \(\gamma\). Part C predicts that the risk term \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\) does not rise as \(\gamma\) rises in both cases, and that the constrained objective is at least the unconstrained one at each of the five values of \(\gamma\). Part D checks the linearization of Proposition 3.4 at the \(\gamma = 4\) solution of Part B. The return column is expected to fall as \(\gamma\) rises, as Proposition 2.1 requires.

"""M7 L06 lab 1: the efficient frontier as a quadratic program, with and without the short-sale constraint.

SIMULATED: every input is generated from the fixed seed SEED below by an integer recurrence. Nothing here is
market data, and the four assets are labelled A1 to A4 only.

For one period, the inputs are a return vector mu (exact fractions) and a given symmetric positive definite
matrix Sigma, not estimated (estimation is outside this unit). Sigma = M'M/16 + I/10 with M a 4 by 4 integer matrix, so
Sigma is symmetric positive definite. For a risk-aversion parameter gamma > 0 the portfolio solves

    minimise (gamma/2) w'Sigma w - mu'w   subject to  1'w = 1,

and the second case adds w >= 0. Every number is a fractions.Fraction, so each equality printed as True is exact.

Part A: no sign constraint. The KKT system [[gamma Sigma, 1], [1', 0]] [w; lambda] = [mu; 1] is solved exactly, and the
result is compared with the closed form w = (y - lambda z)/gamma, where y = Sigma^{-1} mu, z = Sigma^{-1} 1 and
lambda = (1'y - gamma)/(1'z).

Part B: with w >= 0. Each set S of assets fixed at zero gives a reduced equality problem on the other
assets. The 15 sets that leave at least one asset free are enumerated; the set that fixes all four at zero
cannot meet the budget and is skipped. A candidate is kept only if its free weights are nonnegative and the
multiplier nu_i = gamma (Sigma w)_i - mu_i + lambda is nonnegative for each i in S. The number of kept
candidates and the number of distinct weight vectors among them are printed.

Part C: the risk term w'Sigma w of the solution is checked to be nonincreasing in gamma for both cases, and the
constrained objective is checked to be at least the unconstrained objective at each gamma (the feasible set is
smaller).

Part D: turnover against the equal-weight portfolio w0 = (1/4, ..., 1/4), with the linear form: a vector t with
t_i >= w_i - w0_i and t_i >= w0_i - w_i has sum t_i >= sum |w_i - w0_i|, with equality at t_i = |w_i - w0_i|.

Money: none. CU is not used; the weights are fractions of wealth.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import pairwise, product

SEED = 2026
N_ASSETS = 4
GAMMAS = (Fraction(1), Fraction(2), Fraction(4), Fraction(8), Fraction(16))


class Lcg:
    """A linear congruential generator with fixed constants; integer output, so every run is identical."""

    def __init__(self, seed: int) -> None:
        self.state = seed

    def draw(self, modulus: int) -> int:
        """The next integer in 0 to modulus minus 1."""
        self.state = (1103515245 * self.state + 12345) % 2**31
        return self.state % modulus


def simulate_inputs() -> tuple[list[list[Fraction]], list[Fraction], list[list[Fraction]]]:
    """Return (M, mu, Sigma) generated from SEED: M has integers in -3..3, mu is in 0.02..0.14 per period."""
    gen = Lcg(SEED)
    m = [[Fraction(gen.draw(7) - 3) for _ in range(N_ASSETS)] for _ in range(N_ASSETS)]
    mu = [Fraction(gen.draw(13) + 2, 100) for _ in range(N_ASSETS)]
    sigma = [
        [
            sum((m[k][i] * m[k][j] for k in range(N_ASSETS)), Fraction(0)) / 16
            + (Fraction(1, 10) if i == j else Fraction(0))
            for j in range(N_ASSETS)
        ]
        for i in range(N_ASSETS)
    ]
    return m, mu, sigma


def solve(matrix: list[list[Fraction]], rhs: list[Fraction]) -> list[Fraction]:
    """Solve matrix x = rhs exactly by Gaussian elimination with row pivoting; raise ValueError if singular."""
    n = len(rhs)
    rows = [[*row, rhs[i]] for i, row in enumerate(matrix)]
    for col in range(n):
        pivot = next((r for r in range(col, n) if rows[r][col] != 0), None)
        if pivot is None:
            raise ValueError("the matrix is singular")
        rows[col], rows[pivot] = rows[pivot], rows[col]
        for r in range(n):
            if r != col and rows[r][col] != 0:
                factor = rows[r][col] / rows[col][col]
                rows[r] = [a - factor * b for a, b in zip(rows[r], rows[col], strict=True)]
    return [rows[i][n] / rows[i][i] for i in range(n)]


def dot(u: list[Fraction], v: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
    """The inner product u'v, exact."""
    return sum((a * b for a, b in zip(u, v, strict=True)), Fraction(0))


def matvec(a: list[list[Fraction]], v: list[Fraction]) -> list[Fraction]:
    """The product a v, exact."""
    return [dot(row, v) for row in a]


def submatrix(a: list[list[Fraction]], idx: list[int]) -> list[list[Fraction]]:
    """The rows and columns of a with indices in idx."""
    return [[a[i][j] for j in idx] for i in idx]


def equality_frontier_point(
    sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction, idx: list[int]
) -> tuple[list[Fraction], Fraction]:
    """Minimiser over the assets in idx with budget 1'w = 1, and its multiplier lambda, from the KKT system."""
    k = len(idx)
    sub_s = submatrix(sigma, idx)
    kkt = [[gamma * sub_s[i][j] for j in range(k)] + [Fraction(1)] for i in range(k)]
    kkt.append([Fraction(1)] * k + [Fraction(0)])
    rhs = [mu[i] for i in idx] + [Fraction(1)]
    sol = solve(kkt, rhs)
    return sol[:k], sol[k]


def closed_form(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction) -> list[Fraction]:
    """The same minimiser from the closed form (y - lambda z)/gamma, with y = Sigma^{-1} mu and z = Sigma^{-1} 1."""
    ones = [Fraction(1)] * N_ASSETS
    y = solve(sigma, mu)
    z = solve(sigma, ones)
    lam = (sum(y, Fraction(0)) - gamma) / sum(z, Fraction(0))
    return [(y[i] - lam * z[i]) / gamma for i in range(N_ASSETS)]


def risk(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], w: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
    """w'Sigma w, exact."""
    return dot(w, matvec(sigma, w))


def objective(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction, w: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
    """(gamma/2) w'Sigma w - mu'w, exact."""
    return gamma / 2 * risk(sigma, w) - dot(mu, w)


def nonnegative_candidates(
    sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction
) -> list[tuple[tuple[int, ...], list[Fraction]]]:
    """Every active set fixed at zero whose candidate passes primal and dual checks, as (zero set, weights)."""
    kept: list[tuple[tuple[int, ...], list[Fraction]]] = []
    for mask in product((0, 1), repeat=N_ASSETS):
        zeros = [i for i in range(N_ASSETS) if mask[i]]
        free = [i for i in range(N_ASSETS) if not mask[i]]
        if not free:
            continue
        w_free, lam = equality_frontier_point(sigma, mu, gamma, free)
        w = [Fraction(0)] * N_ASSETS
        for pos, value in zip(free, w_free, strict=True):
            w[pos] = value
        sw = matvec(sigma, w)
        nu = [gamma * sw[i] - mu[i] + lam for i in range(N_ASSETS)]
        if all(v >= 0 for v in w) and all(nu[i] >= 0 for i in zeros):
            kept.append((tuple(zeros), w))
    return kept


def main() -> None:
    """Print the simulated inputs, both frontiers, the monotonicity and inequality checks, and the turnover check."""
    _m, mu, sigma = simulate_inputs()
    print("lab M7 L06 1, SIMULATED inputs from a fixed seed by an integer recurrence (no library RNG), one period")
    print("mu " + " ".join(str(v) for v in mu))
    print("sigma_diagonal " + " ".join(str(sigma[i][i]) for i in range(N_ASSETS)))
    print(f"sigma_symmetric {all(sigma[i][j] == sigma[j][i] for i in range(N_ASSETS) for j in range(N_ASSETS))}")
    print("part_a equality_only gamma return risk objective budget_exact kkt_matches_closed_form")
    eq_vars: list[Fraction] = []
    eq_objs: list[Fraction] = []
    for gamma in GAMMAS:
        w, _ = equality_frontier_point(sigma, mu, gamma, list(range(N_ASSETS)))
        closed = closed_form(sigma, mu, gamma)
        budget = sum(w, Fraction(0)) == 1
        same = w == closed
        ret, var, obj = dot(mu, w), risk(sigma, w), objective(sigma, mu, gamma, w)
        eq_vars.append(var)
        eq_objs.append(obj)
        print(f"equality {float(gamma):g} {float(ret):.6f} {float(var):.6f} {float(obj):.6f} {budget} {same}")
        print("equality_weights " + " ".join(f"{float(v):.6f}" for v in w))

    print("part_b nonneg gamma zero_set candidates_passing distinct_points return risk objective")
    nn_vars: list[Fraction] = []
    nn_objs: list[Fraction] = []
    for gamma, eq_obj in zip(GAMMAS, eq_objs, strict=True):
        kept = nonnegative_candidates(sigma, mu, gamma)
        distinct = {tuple(w) for _, w in kept}
        best = min(kept, key=lambda item: objective(sigma, mu, gamma, item[1]))
        zeros, w = best
        ret, var, obj = dot(mu, w), risk(sigma, w), objective(sigma, mu, gamma, w)
        nn_vars.append(var)
        nn_objs.append(obj)
        label = "{" + ",".join(f"A{i + 1}" for i in zeros) + "}"
        print(
            f"nonneg {float(gamma):g} {label} {len(kept)} {len(distinct)} "
            f"{float(ret):.6f} {float(var):.6f} {float(obj):.6f}"
        )
        print("nonneg_weights " + " ".join(f"{float(v):.6f}" for v in w))
        print(f"nonneg_objective_at_least_equality {obj >= eq_obj}")

    def nonincreasing(values: list[Fraction]) -> bool:
        return all(later <= earlier for earlier, later in pairwise(values))

    print(f"risk_nonincreasing_in_gamma equality {nonincreasing(eq_vars)} nonneg {nonincreasing(nn_vars)}")

    w0 = [Fraction(1, N_ASSETS)] * N_ASSETS
    _, w = min(nonnegative_candidates(sigma, mu, GAMMAS[2]), key=lambda item: objective(sigma, mu, GAMMAS[2], item[1]))
    diff = [w[i] - w0[i] for i in range(N_ASSETS)]
    turnover = sum((abs(d) for d in diff), Fraction(0))
    t_star = [abs(d) for d in diff]
    feasible = all(t_star[i] >= diff[i] and t_star[i] >= -diff[i] for i in range(N_ASSETS))
    print(f"turnover_at_gamma 4 {float(turnover):.6f} linear_form_feasible {feasible}")
    print(f"linear_form_sum_equals_turnover {sum(t_star, Fraction(0)) == turnover}")
    gen = Lcg(SEED + 1)
    bigger = 0
    for _ in range(20):
        extra = [Fraction(gen.draw(5), 100) for _ in range(N_ASSETS)]
        t = [t_star[i] + extra[i] for i in range(N_ASSETS)]
        ok = all(t[i] >= diff[i] and t[i] >= -diff[i] for i in range(N_ASSETS))
        bigger += ok and sum(t, Fraction(0)) >= turnover
    print(f"feasible_perturbations_at_least_turnover {bigger} of 20")

4Where the canon breaks

  • The solution depends on the inputs. \(w(\gamma)\) is a function of \(\mu\) and \(\Sigma\), which this unit takes as given. This lesson does not measure how the weights move when the inputs change, and it does not estimate \(\mu\) or \(\Sigma\).
  • One period, one risk term. The objective uses one period and one quadratic risk term, \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\), which the unit does not identify with any risk measure. Lab 1 uses no costs, and it does not annualize its inputs.
  • \(\gamma\) is a parameter, not a calibrated quantity. Proposition 2.1 describes how the solution moves with \(\gamma\). It does not say which \(\gamma\) an investor should choose.
  • Negative weights are a model output. The equality-only solution is a legal point of the stated problem. Whether a short position is allowed is a constraint of the mandate. Misconception 2 shows that at \(\gamma = 1\) the constraint changes all four weights, not only the short one.
  • The turnover constraint is not used in the solver. Proposition 3.4 makes the constraint linear. Lab 1 checks the linearization, and does not solve a problem with the constraint.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A more risk-averse investor, with a larger \(\gamma\), earns a higher return.”

    Proposition 2.1 says the return \(R(\gamma)\) does not rise as \(\gamma\) rises. A larger \(\gamma\) puts more weight on the risk term and less on \(\mu^{\top}w\). In Lab 1 the return column of Part A falls from 0.113798 at \(\gamma = 1\) to 0.106241 at \(\gamma = 16\).

  2. “Adding \(w \ge 0\) changes only the weight of the asset that was short.”

    In Lab 1 at \(\gamma = 1\) the constraint changes all four weights. The first asset moves from 0.434783 to 0.388425, the second from 0.526836 to 0.203679, the third from 0.562535 to 0.407895, and the fourth from \(-0.524154\) to 0.000000. The constrained problem is solved jointly. The weights of A1 to A3 all change, and in the constrained solution none of them is zero.

  3. “The turnover constraint cannot be written in a quadratic program, because it contains an absolute value.”

    Proposition 3.4 writes it with auxiliary variables \(t_{i}\) as linear inequalities plus \(\sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\). Part D of Lab 1 checks the linearization at a solution. It prints True for the two linearization checks and 20 of 20 for the perturbation check.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · A two-asset closed form

Let \(\Sigma = 2I\), \(\mu = (1/10, 1/20)\), and \(\gamma = 1\). Find \(w(\gamma)\) from Theorem 1.3.

Solution

Here \(\Sigma^{-1} = I/2\), so \(y = \Sigma^{-1}\mu = (1/20, 1/40)\) and \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1} = (1/2, 1/2)\). Then \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}y = 3/40\) and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = 1\), so \(\lambda = (3/40 - 1)/1 = -37/40 = -0.925\). The weights are \(w = y - \lambda z = (1/20 + 0.4625, 1/40 + 0.4625) = (0.5125, 0.4875)\). They sum to one, and \(2w_{1} - 0.1 + \lambda = 1.025 - 1.025 = 0\), which is the first stationarity equation.

M2 · The objective at an equal-weight portfolio

With the data of M1 and \(\gamma = 1\), compute \(f_{1}(w)\) at \(w = (1/2, 1/2)\).

Solution

The risk term is \(w^{\top}\Sigma w = 2(1/4 + 1/4) = 1\). The return is \(\mu^{\top}w = (1/10)(1/2) + (1/20)(1/2) = 3/40\). So \(f_{1}(w) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 - 3/40 = 17/40 = 0.425\).

M3 · A single-asset turnover limit

Write \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le 0.1\) with auxiliary variables, as in Proposition 3.4.

Solution

Take \(w_{0,1} = 0.3\) and \(\tau = 0.1\). The linear form is \(t \ge w_{1} - 0.3\), \(t \ge 0.3 - w_{1}\), and \(t \le 0.1\). By Proposition 3.4 a value \(t\) exists exactly when \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le 0.1\). Feasibility requires \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le t \le 0.1\), which holds exactly when \(w_{1}\) is in \([0.2, 0.4]\).

Proof

P1 · The exact expansion of the mean-variance objective

Show that \(f_{\gamma}(w + d) - f_{\gamma}(w) - (\gamma\Sigma w - \mu)^{\top}d = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}d^{\top}\Sigma d\) for all \(w\) and \(d\).

Solution

This is Lemma 1.3 of L05 with \(Q = \gamma\Sigma\) and \(c = -\mu\). Its gradient is \(\gamma\Sigma w - \mu\), and its quadratic term is \(\tfrac{1}{2}d^{\top}(\gamma\Sigma)d = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}d^{\top}\Sigma d\). Lemma 1.3 gives the identity directly.

P2 · The budget multiplier denominator is positive

Let \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}\). Show that \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\).

Solution

From \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1}\), we get \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = (\Sigma z)^{\top}z = z^{\top}\Sigma z\). The vector \(z\) is not zero, because \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1} \ne 0\). Since \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, \(z^{\top}\Sigma z > 0\). So \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\).

P3 · Closed form with the identity matrix

Let \(\Sigma = I\) on \(\R^{n}\). Show that the minimiser of \(f_{\gamma}\) with \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) is \(w = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\).

Solution

Here \(y = \mu\) and \(z = \mathbf{1}\), so \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = n\) and \(\lambda = (\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu - \gamma)/n\). Then \(w = (\mu - \lambda\mathbf{1})/\gamma = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu - \gamma}{n\gamma}\mathbf{1}\). The second term equals \(\tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\), which is the stated form.

Research

R1 · The large-risk-aversion limit with \(\Sigma\) equal to the identity

With \(\Sigma = I\), show that \(w(\gamma) \to \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\) as \(\gamma \to \infty\).

Solution

By P3, \(w(\gamma) = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\). Each term has a limit as \(\gamma \to \infty\): \(\mu/\gamma \to 0\), and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma \to 0\), so the second term tends to \(\tfrac{-1}{n}\cdot(-1)\mathbf{1} = \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\). The limit is the equal-weight portfolio. With \(\mu = 0\) in P3, the same formula gives \(w = \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\), the unique minimiser of \(\tfrac12 w^{\top}w\) under the budget.

R2 · Nonnegative weights as a projection

With \(\Sigma = I\), \(\mu = (0.1, 0.02)\), \(\gamma = 1\), and \(w \ge 0\), find the minimiser.

Solution

Complete the square: \(f_{\gamma}(w) = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|w - \mu/\gamma\|^{2} - \|\mu\|^{2}/(2\gamma)\). So the minimiser over \(\{w \ge 0, \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\) is the point of that set nearest to \(v = \mu/\gamma = (0.1, 0.02)\). Among points with the budget, the nearest to \(v\) is \(v + t\mathbf{1}\) with \(t = (1 - \mathbf{1}^{\top}v)/2 = 0.44\), which is \((0.54, 0.46)\). Both coordinates are nonnegative, so this point is the minimiser.

R3 · The turnover region for two assets

Let \(w_{0} = (1/2, 1/2)\) and \(\tau = 0.2\). With \(w_{1} + w_{2} = 1\), find the set of \(w_{1}\) that satisfies the turnover constraint.

Solution

With the budget, \(w_{2} - 1/2 = 1/2 - w_{1}\), so the turnover is \(|w_{1} - 1/2| + |1/2 - w_{1}| = 2|w_{1} - 1/2|\). The constraint is \(2|w_{1} - 1/2| \le 0.2\), that is \(|w_{1} - 1/2| \le 0.1\). So \(w_{1}\) lies in \([0.4, 0.6]\).
