Doob's maximal inequality

A martingale that stays near its start on average can still wander far on some paths. Doob's maximal inequality bounds the chance that the largest value reached by a process up to a horizon is at least a level. The bound uses only the expectation at the horizon, and it needs the process to be nonnegative. Lesson L12 uses it to show that a martingale with bounded second moment settles down along almost every path. The proof here is short, because it reduces the question to the first time the level is reached, which is a stopping time by L09.

1Submartingales and the absolute value of a fair walk

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\Prob)\) be a probability space with a filtration \((\mathcal F_k)_{k\ge 0}\) (L08). A process \(X=(X_k)_{k\ge 0}\) is adapted when each \(X_k\) is known at time \(k\): for every real \(\lambda\), the event \(\{X_k\ge\lambda\}\) lies in \(\mathcal F_k\). A martingale has \(\E[X_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=X_k\). The next definition replaces equality with an inequality. The word "submartingale" is used here in plain words for this definition, and the concept is not one the curriculum assigns to another lesson.

Definition 11.1 (nonnegative submartingale)

An adapted process \(X\) is a nonnegative submartingale when \(X_k\ge 0\) and \(\E[X_k]<\infty\) for every \(k\), and \(\E[X_{k+1}\indic_A]\ge\E[X_k\indic_A]\) for every \(k\ge 0\) and every event \(A\in\mathcal F_k\). By the defining property of conditional expectation (L07), the last condition is the same as \(\E[X_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]\ge X_k\).

Lemma 11.2 (the absolute value of the fair walk)
  1. \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) are independent steps, each \(+1\) or \(-1\) with probability \(1/2\), with \(S_0=0\) and \(S_k=X_1+\cdots+X_k\).
  2. The filtration is the natural one, \(\mathcal F_k=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_k)\).

The process \(|S_k|\) is a nonnegative submartingale.

Proof

First a fact about real numbers: \(\tfrac12(|s+1|+|s-1|)=\max(1,|s|)\ge |s|\) for every real \(s\). If \(s\ge 1\), the left side is \(\tfrac12\bigl((s+1)+(s-1)\bigr)=s\). If \(s\le -1\), it is \(\tfrac12\bigl((-s-1)+(1-s)\bigr)=-s\). If \(|s|<1\), it is \(\tfrac12\bigl((s+1)+(1-s)\bigr)=1\). In each case the value is \(\max(1,|s|)\), which is at least \(|s|\).

Now fix \(k\) and an event \(A\in\mathcal F_k\). The random variable \(\indic_A|S_k|\) is determined by \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), while \(X_{k+1}\) is independent of those steps. Averaging over the two values of \(X_{k+1}\) with probability \(1/2\) each, \[ \begin{aligned} \E[\indic_A|S_{k+1}|]&=\E\Bigl[\indic_A\,\tfrac12\bigl(|S_k+1|+|S_k-1|\bigr)\Bigr]\\ &=\E\bigl[\indic_A\max(1,|S_k|)\bigr]\ge\E[\indic_A|S_k|]. \end{aligned} \] The process is adapted, since \(|S_k|\) is a function of \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), and it is nonnegative and bounded at each time, so integrable.

2Doob's maximal inequality

The inequality below is Doob's maximal inequality in its finite-horizon form. It is the tool that Lesson L12 applies.

Theorem 11.3 (Doob's maximal inequality, finite horizon)
  1. \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\Prob)\) is a probability space, and \((\mathcal F_k)_{k\ge 0}\) is a filtration on it.
  2. \(X=(X_k)_{k\ge 0}\) is a nonnegative submartingale on \((\mathcal F_k)\) in the sense of Definition 11.1.
  3. \(N\ge 0\) is a fixed whole number, and \(\lambda>0\) is a real number.

Let \(A=\{\max_{0\le k\le N}X_k\ge\lambda\}\). Then \[ \begin{aligned} \lambda\,\Prob(A)&\le \E[X_N\indic_A]\le \E[X_N],\\ \Prob\Bigl(\max_{0\le k\le N}X_k\ge\lambda\Bigr)&\le\frac{\E[X_N]}{\lambda}. \end{aligned} \]

Proof

Let \(\tau=\min\{k\le N: X_k\ge\lambda\}\) on \(A\), and \(\tau=\infty\) off \(A\). For \(0\le k\le N\), let \(A_k=\{\tau=k\}\). Then \[ A_k=\{X_k\ge\lambda\}\cap\bigcap_{j<k}\{X_j<\lambda\}\in\mathcal F_k, \] because each \(\{X_j\ge\lambda\}\) lies in \(\mathcal F_j\subseteq\mathcal F_k\) for \(j\le k\). The sets \(A_0,\ldots,A_N\) are disjoint and their union is \(A\). On \(A_k\), \(X_k\ge\lambda\), so \(\lambda\indic_{A_k}\le X_k\indic_{A_k}\).

Step 1. For \(B\in\mathcal F_k\) and \(k\le j\le N\), \(\E[X_j\indic_B]\ge\E[X_k\indic_B]\). Indeed, for each \(i\) with \(k\le i<j\) we have \(B\in\mathcal F_k\subseteq\mathcal F_i\), so the one-step inequality of Definition 11.1 gives \(\E[X_{i+1}\indic_B]\ge\E[X_i\indic_B]\). Chaining these from \(i=k\) to \(i=j-1\) gives the claim.

Step 2. Apply Step 1 with \(j=N\) and \(B=A_k\), which lies in \(\mathcal F_k\). This gives \(\E[X_N\indic_{A_k}]\ge\E[X_k\indic_{A_k}]\ge\lambda\,\Prob(A_k)\). Summing over \(k=0,\ldots,N\), and using that the \(A_k\) are disjoint with union \(A\), \[ \begin{aligned} \E[X_N\indic_A]&=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\E[X_N\indic_{A_k}]\\ &\ge\sum_{k=0}^{N}\lambda\,\Prob(A_k)=\lambda\,\Prob(A). \end{aligned} \]

Step 3. Since \(X_N\ge 0\), \(\E[X_N\indic_A]\le\E[X_N]\). Dividing the first and last inequalities by \(\lambda>0\) gives the bound.

Corollary 11.4 (the form to use)

Under the assumptions of Theorem 11.3, \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}X_k\ge\lambda)\le \E[X_N]/\lambda\). The probability on the left concerns the largest value up to the horizon, and the right side uses only the expectation at the horizon. The bound says nothing about \(\E[\max_{k\le N}X_k]\).

Lab 1 · Doob's bound for \(|S_k|\), checked on every atom and every horizon up to 12

For each horizon \(N\) from 1 to 12, the lab enumerates the \(2^N\) step sequences of the fair walk and computes \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}|S_k|\ge\lambda)\) and \(\E|S_N|\) exactly, for each \(\lambda\) in the grid \(\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots,N+1\}\). It predicts that the bound of Corollary 11.4 holds at every grid point, and that \(\lambda\Prob(A)\le\E[X_N\indic_A]\). Part B checks the one-step submartingale inequality of Lemma 11.2 on every atom of the first-\(k\)-steps partition for \(N=12\). Part C applies the same bound to the signed walk \(S_k\), which is not nonnegative, and predicts that the bound fails.

"""M6 L11 lab 1: Doob's maximal inequality for |S_k| of a fair walk, checked exactly.

The fair walk takes steps X_1, ..., X_N, each +1 or -1 with probability 1/2, and S_k = X_1 + ... + X_k with S_0 = 0.
The sample space is the 2**N step sequences, each with weight 2**(-N). Every probability and expectation is an
exact fractions.Fraction.

Part A: for N from 1 to 12, the process X_k = |S_k| (k = 0, ..., N) is checked against the inequality
P(max_{k<=N} X_k >= lambda) <= E[X_N] / lambda, for every lambda in the grid j/2 with j = 1, ..., 2(N+1). The lab
also checks the intermediate line lambda * P(A) <= E[X_N 1_A] <= E[X_N], where A = {max_{k<=N} X_k >= lambda}. It
prints the largest ratio lambda * P(A) / E[X_N] over the grid, the lambda where it is attained, and P(A) at that lambda.

Part B: the one-step submartingale inequality E[X_{k+1} 1_B] >= E[X_k 1_B] is checked for N = 12 on every atom B of
the first-k-steps partition. Each atom is a set of paths sharing their first k steps, and every event in that
partition is a union of atoms, so the atom check covers all of them.

Part C: the signed walk S_k is a martingale but not a nonnegative process. For N = 1 and N = 2 with lambda = 1, the
probability that max S_k >= 1 is compared with E[S_N] / lambda. The bound fails, so nonnegativity is needed.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
from fractions import Fraction

MAX_N = 12


def paths(length: int) -> list[tuple[int, ...]]:
    """All step sequences of the given length, each step +1 or -1."""
    return list(itertools.product((1, -1), repeat=length))


def partial_sums(path: tuple[int, ...]) -> list[int]:
    """S_0, S_1, ..., S_N for one step sequence, with S_0 = 0."""
    sums = [0]
    for step in path:
        sums.append(sums[-1] + step)
    return sums


def part_a() -> None:
    """Doob's inequality for X_k = |S_k| over the grid of lambda values, for each horizon N from 1 to 12."""
    print("Part A: P(max_{k<=N} X_k >= lambda) <= E[X_N] / lambda for X_k = |S_k|")
    for n in range(1, MAX_N + 1):
        weight = Fraction(1, 2**n)
        sample = paths(n)
        expected_last = sum((weight * abs(partial_sums(p)[n]) for p in sample), Fraction(0))
        worst_ratio = Fraction(0)
        worst_lambda = Fraction(0)
        worst_prob = Fraction(0)
        all_hold = True
        grid = [Fraction(j, 2) for j in range(1, 2 * (n + 1) + 1)]
        for lam in grid:
            hit = [p for p in sample if max(abs(s) for s in partial_sums(p)) >= lam]
            prob = weight * len(hit)
            e_hit = sum((weight * abs(partial_sums(p)[n]) for p in hit), Fraction(0))
            bound = expected_last / lam
            all_hold = all_hold and prob <= bound and lam * prob <= e_hit <= expected_last
            ratio = lam * prob / expected_last
            if ratio > worst_ratio:
                worst_ratio, worst_lambda, worst_prob = ratio, lam, prob
        print(
            f"N {n} lambda_count {len(grid)} all_bounds_hold {all_hold} "
            f"largest_ratio {float(worst_ratio):.6f} at_lambda {worst_lambda} "
            f"probability_at_lambda {worst_prob} "
            f"E_X_N {expected_last}"
        )


def part_b() -> None:
    """The one-step submartingale inequality for X_k = |S_k|, on every atom of the first-k-steps partition."""
    print("Part B: one-step submartingale inequality on every atom, N = 12")
    sample = paths(MAX_N)
    every_atom_holds = True
    atoms_checked = 0
    for k in range(MAX_N):
        atoms: dict[tuple[int, ...], int] = {}
        for p in sample:
            sums = partial_sums(p)
            gain = abs(sums[k + 1]) - abs(sums[k])
            atoms[p[:k]] = atoms.get(p[:k], 0) + gain
        atoms_checked += len(atoms)
        every_atom_holds = every_atom_holds and all(total >= 0 for total in atoms.values())
    print(f"atoms_checked {atoms_checked} one_step_submartingale_on_every_atom {every_atom_holds}")


def part_c() -> None:
    """The signed walk fails Doob's bound: its values are not nonnegative."""
    print("Part C: the signed walk S_k against the same bound, lambda = 1")
    for n in (1, 2):
        weight = Fraction(1, 2**n)
        sample = paths(n)
        prob = weight * sum(1 for p in sample if max(partial_sums(p)) >= 1)
        expected_last = sum((weight * partial_sums(p)[n] for p in sample), Fraction(0))
        print(f"N {n} P_max_signed_at_least_1 {prob} E_S_N {expected_last} bound_holds {prob <= expected_last / 1}")


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A, B and C."""
    part_a()
    part_b()
    part_c()

In Part A, every horizon from 1 to 12 prints all_bounds_hold True, so the bound holds at each grid point the lab tested. The largest ratio \(\lambda\Prob(A)/\E[X_N]\) equals 1 at \(N=1\), \(N=2\) and \(N=4\), so the bound is attained there: for \(N=1\) and \(\lambda=1\), \(|S_1|=1\) on every path, the probability is 1, and \(\E|S_1|=1\). At \(N=12\) the largest ratio is 0.845238 at \(\lambda=3\), where \(\Prob(A)=781/1024\) and \(\E|S_{12}|=693/256\), so the bound \(\E|S_{12}|/\lambda=231/256\) is not attained. Part B prints 4095 atoms, which is \(2^{12}-1\), the number of atoms of the first-\(k\)-steps partitions for \(k=0,\ldots,11\), and the one-step inequality holds on each. Part C prints \(\Prob=1/2\) and \(\E S_N=0\) for \(N=1\) and \(N=2\), so the bound \(1/2\le 0\) is false: the nonnegativity assumption is needed. The lab covers finitely many horizons and grid points, so it does not prove the bound for other values; Theorem 11.3 does.

3Where the canon breaks

  • Nonnegativity. Part C shows that the signed walk fails the bound at \(N=1\) and \(N=2\) with \(\lambda=1\). The proof uses \(X_N\ge 0\) in Step 3 only, and it fails without it.
  • The submartingale inequality, not the martingale equality. The proof uses \(\E[X_{i+1}\indic_B]\ge\E[X_i\indic_B]\) only. A nonnegative martingale is a submartingale, so the theorem applies to it.
  • A finite horizon. Theorem 11.3 fixes \(N\). The statement for an infinite horizon, which takes the supremum over all \(k\), is not proved on this page, and Lesson L12 needs only the finite-horizon form.
  • Tightness. Equality holds in Part A at \(N=1,2,4\). The constant 1 in front of \(\E[X_N]/\lambda\) cannot be lowered in general.

4Misconceptions

  1. “Doob's inequality bounds the largest value by the expectation at the horizon.”

    It bounds a probability: \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}X_k\ge\lambda)\le\E[X_N]/\lambda\), as in Corollary 11.4. It does not bound \(\E[\max_{k\le N}X_k]\). Part A of Lab 1 checks only tail probabilities at each level \(\lambda\), never an expected maximum.

  2. “The bound holds for any process with finite expectation, signed or not.”

    Nonnegativity is needed. For the signed walk with \(N=1\) and \(\lambda=1\), \(\Prob(\max_{k\le 1}S_k\ge 1)=1/2\), while \(\E[S_1]/1=0\). Part C of Lab 1 prints both values and bound_holds False.

  3. “The bound is always loose, since a maximum is rarely as large as the horizon value.”

    The bound is attained in some cases. For \(|S|\) of the fair walk with \(N=1\) and \(\lambda=1\), the probability is 1 and the bound is \(\E|S_1|/1=1\). Part A of Lab 1 prints a largest ratio of 1.000000 at \(N=1\), \(N=2\) and \(N=4\).

5Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · The bound for N = 2 and λ = 1

For the fair walk with \(N=2\) and \(X_k=|S_k|\), find \(\Prob(\max_{k\le 2}X_k\ge 1)\) and \(\E[X_2]\), and check Theorem 11.3.

Solution

Since \(|S_1|=1\) on every path, \(\max_{k\le 2}X_k\ge 1\) always, so the probability is 1. The value \(S_2\) is \(-2\), 0 or \(2\) with probabilities \(1/4\), \(1/2\) and \(1/4\), so \(\E|S_2|=2\cdot\tfrac14+0+2\cdot\tfrac14=1\). The bound reads \(1\le 1/1=1\), which holds with equality. Part A of Lab 1 prints the same values for \(N=2\): probability 1 and \(\E|S_2|=1\).

M2 · A horizon-3 check at λ = 2

For the fair walk with \(N=3\) and \(X_k=|S_k|\), find \(\Prob(\max_{k\le 3}X_k\ge 2)\), \(\E[X_3]\), and the bound at \(\lambda=2\).

Solution

The event \(\{\max_{k\le 3}|S_k|\ge 2\}\) occurs when \(|S_2|=2\), which means \(X_1=X_2\), or when \(|S_3|=3\), which also needs \(X_1=X_2\). So the event is \(\{X_1=X_2\}\), with probability \(1/2\). Also \(|S_3|\) takes the value 3 on the two paths where all three steps agree, and the value 1 on the other six paths, so \(\E|S_3|=\tfrac18(2\cdot 3+6\cdot 1)=\tfrac{12}{8}=\tfrac32\). The bound at \(\lambda=2\) is \(\tfrac32/2=\tfrac34\), and \(1/2\le 3/4\) holds.

M3 · The one-step inequality for a specific point

Verify the identity \(\tfrac12(|s+1|+|s-1|)=\max(1,|s|)\) at \(s=0\), \(s=2\) and \(s=-\tfrac12\).

Solution

At \(s=0\), the left side is \(\tfrac12(1+1)=1\), and \(\max(1,0)=1\). At \(s=2\), it is \(\tfrac12(3+1)=2\), and \(\max(1,2)=2\). At \(s=-\tfrac12\), it is \(\tfrac12(\tfrac12+\tfrac32)=1\), and \(\max(1,\tfrac12)=1\). In each case the two sides agree, as Lemma 11.2 requires.

Proof

P1 · The first-passage events are in F_k

Show that \(A_k=\{\tau=k\}\), with \(\tau\) the first index at which \(X\) reaches \(\lambda\), lies in \(\mathcal F_k\), and that \(\tau\) is a stopping time up to \(N\).

Solution

The event \(A_k\) is the set of outcomes where \(X_k\ge\lambda\) and \(X_j<\lambda\) for every \(j<k\). Each \(\{X_j<\lambda\}\) is the complement of \(\{X_j\ge\lambda\}\), which lies in \(\mathcal F_j\subseteq\mathcal F_k\). The event \(\{X_k\ge\lambda\}\) lies in \(\mathcal F_k\) by adaptedness. A finite intersection of events in \(\mathcal F_k\) is in \(\mathcal F_k\), so \(A_k\in\mathcal F_k\). This is the same argument as Lemma 9.7 of L09 for a stopping time, applied to a real-valued process.

P2 · Step 1 for a non-adjacent pair of times

Prove that \(\E[X_j\indic_B]\ge\E[X_k\indic_B]\) for \(B\in\mathcal F_k\) and \(j\ge k\), without applying the one-step inequality to a set that is not in the right \(\sigma\)-algebra.

Solution

Induct on \(j\ge k\). For \(j=k\) both sides are equal. Suppose \(\E[X_j\indic_B]\ge\E[X_k\indic_B]\) holds for some \(j\ge k\). Since \(B\in\mathcal F_k\subseteq\mathcal F_j\), the one-step inequality at time \(j\) with the event \(B\) gives \(\E[X_{j+1}\indic_B]\ge\E[X_j\indic_B]\). Combining, \(\E[X_{j+1}\indic_B]\ge\E[X_k\indic_B]\), which completes the induction. Each use of the one-step inequality is with an event in \(\mathcal F_j\), as Definition 11.1 requires.

P3 · The sum over the first-passage sets

Show that \(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\E[X_N\indic_{A_k}]=\E[X_N\indic_A]\), and explain why the sets \(A_k\) are disjoint.

Solution

Each outcome in \(A\) has a unique first index \(\tau\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\) with \(X_\tau\ge\lambda\), so it lies in exactly one \(A_k\). Each outcome outside \(A\) lies in none of them. Hence the \(A_k\) are disjoint with union \(A\). Since \(\indic_A=\sum_k\indic_{A_k}\), linearity of expectation gives \(\E[X_N\indic_A]=\sum_k\E[X_N\indic_{A_k}]\).

Empirical

E1 · The atoms in Part B

Part B of Lab 1 prints 4095 atoms. Explain the count, and state which horizon's partitions it covers.

Solution

The first-\(k\)-steps partition for \(k=0,\ldots,11\) has \(2^k\) atoms, one for each sequence of \(k\) steps. The total is \(\sum_{k=0}^{11}2^k=2^{12}-1=4095\). The lab's count is the sum over \(k\) from 0 to \(N-1\) with \(N=12\), which is the printed 4095. The partitions are those of the 12-step walk.

E2 · The bound at N = 12 and λ = 3

Using the printed values for \(N=12\), compute the bound \(\E[X_{12}]/\lambda\) at \(\lambda=3\), and the probability that the lab's largest ratio implies.

Solution

The printed \(\E|S_{12}|\) is \(693/256\), so the bound is \(693/768=231/256=0.90234375\). The printed probability at \(\lambda=3\) is \(781/1024=0.7626953125\), which is below the bound, as Theorem 11.3 requires. The printed ratio \(\lambda\Prob(A)/\E[X_{12}]\) is \(3\cdot(781/1024)/(693/256)=2343/2772=0.845238\), which is the largest ratio the lab prints.

E3 · The signed walk in Part C

Part C prints \(\Prob=1/2\) and \(\E S_N=0\) for \(N=2\) at \(\lambda=1\). Show that these values are correct, and say what the printed bound_holds False means.

Solution

For \(N=2\), \(\max_{k\le 2}S_k\ge 1\) requires \(S_1=1\) (since \(S_2\) is even and equals 2 only when \(S_1=1\)). The event \(\{X_1=1\}\) has probability \(1/2\). The mean \(\E S_2=0\) since each step has mean zero. The bound would say \(1/2\le 0\), which is false. The printed value says that the inequality fails for the signed walk, so the nonnegativity in Theorem 11.3 is needed.

Research

R1 · No smaller constant works

Show that no constant \(c<1\) satisfies \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}X_k\ge\lambda)\le c\,\E[X_N]/\lambda\) for all nonnegative submartingales.

Solution

Take \(X_k=|S_k|\) with \(N=1\) and \(\lambda=1\). Then \(|S_1|=1\) on every path, so \(\Prob(\max_{k\le 1}X_k\ge 1)=1\) and \(\E[X_1]/1=1\). The inequality with \(c\) would read \(1\le c\cdot 1\), which fails for \(c<1\). So the constant 1 is the best possible in the bound.

R2 · A nonnegative martingale is a submartingale

Let \(X\) be a nonnegative martingale. Show that \(X\) is a nonnegative submartingale, so Theorem 11.3 applies to it.

Solution

For \(A\in\mathcal F_k\), the martingale property gives \(\E[X_{k+1}\indic_A]=\E[X_k\indic_A]\), which is at least \(\E[X_k\indic_A]\). The hypothesis that \(X\) is nonnegative is given, and integrability is part of the martingale definition in L08, so \(X\) satisfies Definition 11.1 with equality in place of the inequality.

R3 · A second-moment bound for the fair walk

Use \(X_k=S_k^2\) for the fair walk. Show that \(X\) is a nonnegative submartingale and that \(\E[S_N^2]=N\). Deduce that \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}|S_k|\ge c)\le N/c^2\) for \(c>0\).

Solution

For \(s\) real, \(\tfrac12\bigl((s+1)^2+(s-1)^2\bigr)=s^2+1\ge s^2\). Conditioning on the first \(k\) steps as in Lemma 11.2 gives \(\E[S_{k+1}^2\indic_A]\ge\E[S_k^2\indic_A]\) for \(A\in\mathcal F_k\), so \(S_k^2\) is a nonnegative submartingale. For the second moment, \(\E[S_N^2]=\sum_{i,j}\E[X_iX_j]\), and \(\E[X_iX_j]=0\) for \(i\ne j\) by independence and zero mean, while \(\E[X_i^2]=1\), so \(\E[S_N^2]=N\). Corollary 11.4 with \(\lambda=c^2\) gives \(\Prob(\max_{k\le N}S_k^2\ge c^2)\le N/c^2\), and the event is the same as \(\max_{k\le N}|S_k|\ge c\).
