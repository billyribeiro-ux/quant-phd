Module M5 · Tier U · 130 hours · Part M, Mathematics

Real Analysis

Whether an iterative computation converges, and how far its output lies from the answer it approximates, are questions about completeness, limits and contraction. This module builds the analysis those questions need: the completeness of the real numbers, sequences and series, continuity in epsilon–delta form, metric spaces, uniform convergence, compactness, the Banach fixed-point theorem, and the Riemann–Stieltjes integral. Its project is a solver for a discounted savings problem. You prove that the Bellman operator is a contraction, implement value-function iteration with a certified error bound, and test the solver against an independent reference.

  • Prerequisites: M2 Single & Multivariable Calculus (the limit, the derivative and the definite integral, used as named prerequisites), and through M2, M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning (the discount factor and the geometric series). P0.1 is not yet released in this program, so its links open the concept index, which reads "not yet released".
  • Project: Contraction-mapping solver, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. The labs and the grader use generated model parameters and exact computation. No lab reads a data file, and no figure in this module is a real market's figure.

104 h lessons · 26 h project (20%)

Objectives

By the end of M5 you can:

  1. Prove that a sequence is convergent from the completeness of the real numbers, and that a series converges exactly when its partial sums satisfy the Cauchy condition (Lessons 1 and 2).
  2. Prove that a function is continuous at a point, with an explicit delta, and show that a delta valid at one point need not serve every point (Lesson 3).
  3. Test whether a candidate is a metric by checking its axioms, and prove that a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous (Lessons 4 and 5).
  4. Prove the Banach fixed-point theorem, with existence, uniqueness and an a priori error bound, for a contraction on a complete metric space (Lesson 7).
  5. Prove that the discounted Bellman operator is a contraction in the sup norm, and state the a posteriori bound that value-function iteration uses (Lessons 8 and 9).
  6. Define the Riemann–Stieltjes integral by partitions and tags, and evaluate one such integral by a closed form that you derive (Lesson 10).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on the prerequisites (M1 and M2), and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. With nine items that bar needs eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. What is the negation of "for every real \(x\), \(x^2 \ge 0\)"?

    Options

    The negation of "for every" is "there is", and the negation of \(x^2 \ge 0\) is \(x^2 < 0\) (M1, Lesson 1).

  2. Which proof technique assumes that the claim is false and derives a contradiction?

    Options

    Proof by contradiction assumes the negation of the claim (M1, Lesson 2).

  3. What is \(\ln(e^2)\)?

    The logarithm is the inverse of the exponential, so \(\ln(e^2) = 2\) (M1, Lesson 3).

  4. A payment is made one year from now. At an annual rate of 5 percent, compounded annually, what is its present value per unit paid? Give the discount factor to six decimal places.

    The discount factor for one year at an annual rate of \(r\) is \(1/(1 + r) = 1/1.05 = 0.952381\) (M1, Lesson 5).

  5. What is \(\sum_{k=0}^{3} 2^k\)?

    The sum is \(1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15\), which equals \(2^4 - 1\) by the closed form of M1, Lesson 6.

  6. What is \(\lim_{x \to 2} x^2\)?

    The limit of \(x^2\) as \(x\) approaches 2 is \(2^2 = 4\) (the M2 limit concept).

  7. What is the derivative of \(x^3\) at \(x = 1\)?

    The derivative of \(x^3\) is \(3x^2\), which equals 3 at \(x = 1\) (the M2 derivative concept).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): with \(\beta = 1/2\), what is the finite sum \(\sum_{n=0}^{4} \beta^n\)? It is the sum of the first five terms of a geometric decay with ratio 1/2.

    \(1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 = 31/16 = 1.9375\). This is the finite geometric sum that rung 1 evaluates in closed form (M1, Lesson 6).

  9. Skill check (rung 2): a map \(G\) on a space with distance \(d\) satisfies \(d(Gv, Gw) \le \beta\, d(v, w)\) with \(\beta = 1/2\). If \(d(v, w) = 4\), what bound does this give for \(d(Gv, Gw)\)?

    The bound is \(\beta \cdot 4 = 2\). This is the contraction inequality, the missing step of the completion problem in rung 2.

Lessons

Ten lessons. Six declare 10 study hours and four declare 11 (Lessons 7 to 10), so the lesson hours sum to 104. Each page declares its own hours in its data-hours attribute. Lessons 1 to 3 cover the completeness of the real numbers, sequences and series, and continuity. Lessons 4 to 6 cover metric spaces, uniform convergence and compactness. Lessons 7 to 9 cover contraction mappings, the Bellman operator and value-function iteration. Lesson 10 covers the Riemann–Stieltjes integral. The lesson pages share one structure: graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 10 hCompleteness of the Real NumbersThe least-upper-bound property as the defining axiom of the reals, the Archimedean property and nested intervals proved from it, and bisection that brackets a supremum. L02 · 10 hSequences, Cauchy Sequences and SeriesConvergence of sequences, Cauchy sequences, the proof that a real Cauchy sequence converges, and the Cauchy criterion for series. L03 · 10 hContinuity in Epsilon–Delta FormContinuity at a point and on a set, an explicit delta for \(x^2\), the sequential characterization, and the failure of uniform continuity for \(x^2\). L04 · 10 hMetric SpacesThe metric axioms, open and closed sets, the sup metric on bounded functions, and completeness of a metric space. L05 · 10 hUniform ConvergencePointwise against uniform convergence, the uniform limit of continuous functions, and a sequence of functions whose pointwise limit is discontinuous. L06 · 10 hCompactnessSequential compactness, Bolzano–Weierstrass in \(\R\) by bisection, and the extreme value theorem on a compact set. L07 · 11 hContraction Mappings and the Banach Fixed-Point TheoremContractions, the fixed-point theorem with uniqueness and an a priori error bound, and the modulus of a differentiable map. L08 · 11 hThe Discounted Bellman Operator Is a ContractionThe discounted savings model on a finite grid, the operator defined by its formula, and the proof that it contracts in the sup norm. L09 · 11 hValue-Function Iteration with a Certified Error BoundIteration of the operator, the a posteriori bound, and the stopping rule that certifies the error to the grid's fixed point. L10 · 11 hThe Riemann–Stieltjes IntegralThe integral of a continuous function against a continuously differentiable function, by partitions and tags, and a step integrator showing that a discontinuous integrand can have no integral.

The project ladder

The module ends in the contraction-mapping solver. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated case on a three-point grid: the contraction inequality checked step by step, one application of the Bellman operator, and the a posteriori bound, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same case with the modulus step and the bound line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement contraction_solver.py with the Bellman operator, value-function iteration and a certified error bound, and write the proof. Graded by the autograder against generated instances and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example Howard policy iteration with a certified bound, or a measured convergence rate against the discount factor over a family of instances, suitable for your portfolio.

Data status

No real data is used in M5. The labs and the grader compute from formulas and from generated model parameters. Where a page shows numbers from a generated case, the page labels them SIMULATED, and a generated case is not a data source. The savings model is dimensionless, so no currency value appears in the module.
