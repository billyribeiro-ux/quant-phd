The Riemann–Stieltjes integral

The ordinary integral weights a function by lengths. The Riemann–Stieltjes integral weights it by the increments of a second function \(G\), so one definition covers both. Its value can differ from the ordinary integral of the same function: \(\int_0^1 x\,d(x^{2}) = 2/3\), while \(\int_0^1 x\,dx = 1/2\). This lesson defines the integral by tagged partitions, proves that it exists when \(f\) is continuous and \(G\) has a continuous derivative, and gives an example showing that continuity of the integrand cannot be dropped. Part A of Lab 1 computes the sums exactly for \(f(x) = x\) and \(G(x) = x^{2}\).

1Tagged partitions and the Riemann–Stieltjes sum

Let \(a < b\). A partition \(P\) of \([a, b]\) is a finite list \(a = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n = b\). Its subintervals are \(I_i = [x_{i-1}, x_i]\), with lengths \(\Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}\), and its mesh is \(\lvert P \rvert = \max_i \Delta x_i\). A tagging of \(P\) is a list \(\xi = (\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n)\) with \(\xi_i \in I_i\). Call the pair \((P, \xi)\) a tagged partition.

Definition 1.1 (Riemann–Stieltjes sum)

Let \(f\) and \(G\) be real functions on \([a, b]\). The Riemann–Stieltjes sum of the tagged partition \((P, \xi)\) is \(S(P, \xi) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(\xi_i)\bigl(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1})\bigr)\).

Definition 1.2 (Riemann–Stieltjes integral)

A real number \(I\) is the Riemann–Stieltjes integral of \(f\) with respect to \(G\) on \([a, b]\), written \(\int_a^b f\,dG\), if for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that \(\lvert S(P, \xi) - I \rvert < \varepsilon\) for every tagged partition with \(\lvert P \rvert < \delta\). The condition is a limit as the mesh goes to zero, in the sense of the limit of M2. It does not refine partitions, and the tags are arbitrary.

Lemma 1.3 (the integral is unique)

Hypotheses: (1) \(f\) and \(G\) are real functions on \([a, b]\), \(a < b\); (2) \(I\) and \(I'\) both satisfy Definition 1.2 for \(f\) and \(G\). Conclusion: \(I = I'\).

Proof

Suppose \(I \ne I'\), and put \(\varepsilon = \lvert I - I' \rvert / 2 > 0\). Let \(\delta\) and \(\delta'\) be the numbers that Definition 1.2 gives for \(I\) and \(I'\) at this \(\varepsilon\). The uniform partition into \(n\) equal parts has mesh \((b - a)/n\), so choose \(n\) with \((b - a)/n < \min(\delta, \delta')\), and any tagging \(\xi\). Then \(\lvert S - I \rvert < \varepsilon\) and \(\lvert S - I' \rvert < \varepsilon\), so \(\lvert I - I' \rvert \le \lvert S - I \rvert + \lvert S - I' \rvert < 2\varepsilon = \lvert I - I' \rvert\), a contradiction.

2Continuous functions on a closed interval

The proofs below use the definite integral of M2 in its Darboux form. For a bounded \(h\) and a partition \(P\), the lower sum \(L(h, P)\) and the upper sum \(U(h, P)\) use the infimum and supremum of \(h\) on each \(I_i\). A function is integrable on \([a, b]\) when the supremum of its lower sums equals the infimum of its upper sums, and that common value is the integral (M2 Lesson 9, Definition 1.3). Three properties of the integral are used without proof, and they are named here as prerequisites. (P1) Every lower sum is at most every upper sum (M2 Lesson 9, Lemma 1.2). (P2) Integrals are linear, and integrable functions form a vector space (M2 Lesson 11, Lemma 1.1). (P3) The fundamental theorem: if \(F\) is continuous on \([c, d]\), differentiable on \((c, d)\), and \(F' = g\) there with \(g\) integrable, then \(\int_c^d g = F(d) - F(c)\) (M2 Lesson 10, Theorem 2.1). The further properties the proofs need, namely monotonicity of the integral, the bound \(\lvert \int_c^d h \rvert \le (d - c)\sup\lvert h \rvert\), and additivity over adjacent intervals, are not assumed. Lemma 2.4 below proves them from the Darboux definition and Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2 of M2 Lesson 9.

Lemma 2.1 (a continuous function is bounded and uniformly continuous)

Hypotheses: (1) \(a < b\); (2) \(f\) is continuous on \([a, b]\) in the epsilon–delta sense of Lesson 3. Conclusion: (i) \(f\) is bounded on \([a, b]\); (ii) for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that \(\lvert f(s) - f(t) \rvert < \varepsilon\) whenever \(s, t \in [a, b]\) and \(\lvert s - t \rvert < \delta\).

Proof

We use the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem of Lesson 6: every bounded sequence of reals has a convergent subsequence. Its limit lies in \([a, b]\), because every term does.

(i) If \(f\) were unbounded, choose \(x_n \in [a, b]\) with \(\lvert f(x_n) \rvert \ge n\). A subsequence \(x_{n_j}\) converges to some \(c \in [a, b]\). By continuity at \(c\), \(f(x_{n_j}) \to f(c)\), so \(\lvert f(x_{n_j}) \rvert\) is bounded. This contradicts \(\lvert f(x_{n_j}) \rvert \ge n_j \to \infty\).

(ii) Suppose (ii) fails for some \(\varepsilon > 0\). Then for each \(n\) there are \(s_n, t_n \in [a, b]\) with \(\lvert s_n - t_n \rvert < 1/n\) and \(\lvert f(s_n) - f(t_n) \rvert \ge \varepsilon\). A subsequence \(s_{n_j}\) converges to some \(c \in [a, b]\), and \(t_{n_j} \to c\) because \(\lvert s_{n_j} - t_{n_j} \rvert < 1/n_j\). Continuity at \(c\) gives \(f(s_{n_j}) - f(t_{n_j}) \to f(c) - f(c) = 0\), which contradicts \(\lvert f(s_{n_j}) - f(t_{n_j}) \rvert \ge \varepsilon\).

Lemma 2.2 (a continuous function is integrable)

Hypotheses: (1) \(a < b\); (2) \(h\) is continuous on \([a, b]\). Conclusion: \(h\) is integrable on \([a, b]\) in the Darboux sense of M2 Lesson 9.

Proof

Let \(\varepsilon > 0\), and let \(\delta\) be the number of Lemma 2.1(ii) for \(h\). Take a partition \(P\) with \(\lvert P \rvert < \delta\), and write \(M_i\) and \(m_i\) for the supremum and infimum of \(h\) on \(I_i\). Any two points of \(I_i\) are within \(\delta\), so \(M_i - m_i \le \varepsilon\). Hence \(U(h, P) - L(h, P) = \sum_i (M_i - m_i)\Delta x_i \le \varepsilon(b - a)\). By (P1), \(\sup_Q L(h, Q) \le \inf_Q U(h, Q)\). Also \(\inf_Q U(h, Q) \le U(h, P)\) and \(\sup_Q L(h, Q) \ge L(h, P)\), so \(0 \le \inf_Q U(h, Q) - \sup_Q L(h, Q) \le U(h, P) - L(h, P) \le \varepsilon(b - a)\). Since \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, the two bounds are equal, which is integrability.

Lemma 2.4 (monotonicity, estimate and additivity of the integral)

Hypotheses: (1) \(c < d\); (2) \(h\) and \(g\) are integrable on \([c, d]\); (3) \(M \ge 0\). Conclusion: (a) if \(h \le g\) on \([c, d]\), then \(\int_c^d h \le \int_c^d g\); (b) if \(\lvert h \rvert \le M\) on \([c, d]\), then \(\lvert \int_c^d h \rvert \le M(d - c)\); (c) if \(c < e < d\), then \(h\) is integrable on \([c, e]\) and on \([e, d]\), and \(\int_c^d h = \int_c^e h + \int_e^d h\).

Proof

An integrable function is bounded, because the definition requires boundedness (M2 Lesson 9, Definition 1.3). For a constant \(k\), every lower and upper sum on \([c, d]\) equals \(k(d - c)\), so \(\int_c^d k = k(d - c)\).

(a) Let \(P\) be a partition and \(I_i\) a subinterval. Since \(h \le g\), \(\inf_{I_i} h\) is a lower bound for \(g\) on \(I_i\), so \(\inf_{I_i} h \le \inf_{I_i} g\); likewise \(\sup_{I_i} h \le \sup_{I_i} g\). Hence \(L(h, P) \le L(g, P)\) for every \(P\), and \(\int_c^d h = \sup_P L(h, P) \le \sup_P L(g, P) = \int_c^d g\).

(b) The hypothesis gives \(-M \le h \le M\). Apply (a) to the constants \(-M\) and \(M\), whose integrals are \(-M(d - c)\) and \(M(d - c)\).

(c) Let \(I = \int_c^d h\), and let \(\varepsilon > 0\). Since \(I\) is both the supremum of the lower sums and the infimum of the upper sums (M2 Lesson 9, Definition 1.3), there are partitions \(P\) and \(Q\) of \([c, d]\) with \(L(h, P) > I - \varepsilon/2\) and \(U(h, Q) < I + \varepsilon/2\). The common refinement \(R\) of \(P\) and \(Q\) satisfies \(L(h, P) \le L(h, R)\) and \(U(h, R) \le U(h, Q)\) (M2 Lesson 9, Lemma 1.1), so \(U(h, R) - L(h, R) < \varepsilon\). Adding the point \(e\) to \(R\) does not increase \(U - L\), so there is a partition \(R' = R_1 \cup R_2\) with \(R_1\) a partition of \([c, e]\), \(R_2\) a partition of \([e, d]\), and \(U(h, R') - L(h, R') < \varepsilon\). This difference is the sum of \(U(h, R_1) - L(h, R_1)\) and \(U(h, R_2) - L(h, R_2)\), and both terms are nonnegative, so each is below \(\varepsilon\). By (P1), \(\sup L(h, \cdot) \le \inf U(h, \cdot)\) on \([c, e]\), and the gap between these two numbers is at most \(U(h, R_1) - L(h, R_1) < \varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, the gap is zero, so \(h\) is integrable on \([c, e]\). The same argument applies on \([e, d]\).

Now let \(J_1 = \int_c^e h\) and \(J_2 = \int_e^d h\). If \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) are partitions of \([c, e]\) and \([e, d]\), their union \(P\) is a partition of \([c, d]\), and \(L(h, P) = L(h, P_1) + L(h, P_2)\). Since \(L(h, P) \le I\), we get \(L(h, P_1) + L(h, P_2) \le I\), and taking suprema over \(P_1\) and then \(P_2\) gives \(J_1 + J_2 \le I\). Conversely, let \(P\) be any partition of \([c, d]\), and let \(P'\) be \(P\) with \(e\) added. Then \(L(h, P) \le L(h, P')\), and \(L(h, P') = L(h, P'_1) + L(h, P'_2)\) with \(L(h, P'_1) \le J_1\) and \(L(h, P'_2) \le J_2\). So \(L(h, P) \le J_1 + J_2\), and taking the supremum over \(P\) gives \(I \le J_1 + J_2\).

3Integrators with a continuous derivative

Say that \(G\) is continuously differentiable on \([a, b]\) when \(G\) has a derivative \(G'\) at every point of \([a, b]\) (one-sided at \(a\) and \(b\)), and \(G'\) is continuous on \([a, b]\).

Theorem 3.1 (the integral against a continuously differentiable integrator)

Hypotheses: (1) \(a < b\); (2) \(f\) is continuous on \([a, b]\); (3) \(G\) is continuously differentiable on \([a, b]\). Conclusion: \(\int_a^b f\,dG\) exists, and \(\int_a^b f\,dG = \int_a^b f(x)\,G'(x)\,dx\). The right side exists by Lemma 2.2, because \(fG'\) is continuous.

Proof

Put \(h = fG'\), which is continuous, and \(I = \int_a^b h\), which exists by Lemma 2.2. By Lemma 2.1(i), \(M = \sup_{[a, b]} \lvert G' \rvert\) is finite. Let \(\varepsilon > 0\), and put \(\eta = \varepsilon/(M(b - a) + 1)\). By Lemma 2.1(ii) for \(f\), there is \(\delta > 0\) with \(\lvert f(s) - f(t) \rvert < \eta\) whenever \(\lvert s - t \rvert < \delta\).

Let \((P, \xi)\) be a tagged partition with \(\lvert P \rvert < \delta\). On each \(I_i\), \(G\) is continuous and differentiable on the interior, and \(G'\) is continuous, hence integrable by Lemma 2.2. So (P3) gives \(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1}) = \int_{I_i} G'(t)\,dt\). By (P2), \(f(\xi_i)\bigl(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1})\bigr) = \int_{I_i} f(\xi_i) G'(t)\,dt\). By Lemma 2.4(c), applied repeatedly, \(I = \sum_i \int_{I_i} f(t) G'(t)\,dt\). Subtracting,

\[ S(P, \xi) - I = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \int_{I_i} \bigl(f(\xi_i) - f(t)\bigr) G'(t)\,dt. \]

For \(t \in I_i\), \(\lvert t - \xi_i \rvert \le \Delta x_i \le \lvert P \rvert < \delta\), so \(\lvert f(\xi_i) - f(t) \rvert < \eta\), and \(\lvert G'(t) \rvert \le M\). The integrand has absolute value at most \(\eta M\). By Lemma 2.4(b), applied on \(I_i\), \(\bigl\lvert \int_{I_i} (\cdots) \bigr\rvert \le \Delta x_i\,\eta M\). Summing over \(i\), \(\lvert S(P, \xi) - I \rvert \le (b - a)M\eta = \varepsilon M(b - a)/(M(b - a) + 1) < \varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon\) was arbitrary, Definition 1.2 holds with \(I\).

Corollary 3.2 (the example \(x\,d(x^{2})\))

Hypotheses: \(f(x) = x\) and \(G(x) = x^{2}\) on \([0, 1]\). Both satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1, since \(f\) is a polynomial and \(G'(x) = 2x\) is continuous. Conclusion: \(\int_0^1 x\,d(x^{2}) = 2/3\).

Proof

By Theorem 3.1, \(\int_0^1 x\,d(x^{2}) = \int_0^1 x \cdot 2x\,dx = 2\int_0^1 x^{2}\,dx\), using (P2) for the constant 2. Lesson 9 of M2 (Example 3.2) computes \(\int_0^1 x^{2}\,dx = 1/3\). So the integral is \(2 \cdot 1/3 = 2/3\).

Example 3.3 (the exact sums of Lab 1)

For \(G = x^{2}\) and \(f = x\) on a uniform partition into \(n\) parts, the right-tag sum is \((n+1)(4n-1)/(6n^{2})\) and the left-tag sum is \((4n^{2} - 3n - 1)/(6n^{2})\). Both differ from \(2/3\) by at most \(1/n\). Derivation: with \(x_i = i/n\) and \(\Delta x_i = 1/n\), \(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1}) = (2i - 1)/n^{2}\). The right-tag sum is \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} (i/n)(2i - 1)/n^{2} = (4n^{3} + 3n^{2} - n)/(6n^{3})\), and the left-tag sum is \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} ((i-1)/n)(2i - 1)/n^{2} = (4n^{3} - 3n^{2} - n)/(6n^{3})\). Using \(\sum i = n(n+1)/2\) and \(\sum i^{2} = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6\), these simplify to the two forms above. The proof of Theorem 3.1 with \(\eta\) replaced by the mesh gives \(\lvert S - 2/3 \rvert \le 2 \cdot \lvert P \rvert\), because \(M = 2\). Problem P2 proves the sharper bound \(\lvert S - 2/3 \rvert \le \lvert P \rvert\).

Lab 1 · Riemann–Stieltjes sums for \(x\,d(x^{2})\) and for a step integrator

The inputs are generated, not data: \(f(x) = x\) with \(G(x) = x^{2}\) on \([0, 1]\) in Part A; 200 seeded random tagged partitions in Part B; a step integrator at \(1/2\) (defined in Section 4) in Part C; and the identity integrator \(G(x) = x\) in Part D. Stated before running. Part A's exact sums should match the closed forms of Example 3.3 in every row, and should approach \(2/3\) as \(n\) grows (Corollary 3.2). Part B should satisfy \(\lvert S - 2/3 \rvert \le 2 \cdot \lvert P \rvert\) for every partition. Part C should give a sum of 0 with the left tag and 1 with the right tag, at each mesh that contains \(1/2\) (Proposition 4.1). Part D should give \((n+1)/(2n)\) and \((n-1)/(2n)\) for the right and left sums. The code uses a fixed seed and no network.

"""M5 L10 lab 1: Riemann-Stieltjes sums for the integral of f against dG on [0, 1].

Generated inputs, not data. Part A uses f(x) = x and G(x) = x^2. The integral is I = integral from 0 to 1 of
x * 2x dx = 2/3, the value that Corollary 3.2 of this lesson gives for a continuously differentiable integrator.
Part D uses the same f with the identity integrator G(x) = x, which gives the ordinary Riemann sums of x.

Part A: uniform partitions into n parts, with left tags and with right tags, computed exactly in Fraction
arithmetic. The lab checks the closed forms derived on the page:
  right-tag sum  S_right(n) = (n + 1)(4n - 1) / (6 n^2),  so S_right(n) - 2/3 = (3n - 1)/(6 n^2)
  left-tag sum   S_left(n)  = (4 n^2 - 3 n - 1) / (6 n^2), so S_left(n) - 2/3 = -(3n + 1)/(6 n^2)

Part B: 200 random tagged partitions of [0, 1], drawn with random.Random(20261028). For each one the lab checks
the bound |S - 2/3| <= 2 * mesh, where mesh is the largest subinterval length. The constant 2 is max |G'| = 2.

Part C: a step integrator G(x) = 0 for x < 1/2 and G(x) = 1 for x >= 1/2, with f(x) = 1 for x >= 1/2 and
f(x) = 0 otherwise. On uniform partitions that contain 1/2, the lab prints the sum with the left tag and the
sum with the right tag of the one interval on which G jumps. Both tagged partitions are the same partition.

Only Part B computes with floats; its checks allow 1e-12 for floating-point error. Parts A, C and D use exact
Fractions and integers. No network. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import random
from collections.abc import Callable
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261028
PARTS_A = (4, 10, 100, 1000)
PARTS_B = 200
MAX_INTERIOR = 30
SLACK = 1e-12
IDENTITY_N = 1000


def f(x: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The integrand f(x) = x."""
    return x


def g(x: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The integrator G(x) = x^2."""
    return x * x


def identity(x: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The integrator G(x) = x."""
    return x


def rs_sum(points: list[Fraction], tags: list[Fraction], integrator: Callable[[Fraction], Fraction]) -> Fraction:
    """Riemann-Stieltjes sum: the sum of f(tag_i) times the increment of the integrator over subinterval i."""
    total = Fraction(0)
    for i, tag in enumerate(tags, start=1):
        total += f(tag) * (integrator(points[i]) - integrator(points[i - 1]))
    return total


def uniform(n: int) -> list[Fraction]:
    """The uniform partition of [0, 1] into n parts."""
    return [Fraction(i, n) for i in range(n + 1)]


def part_a() -> None:
    """Exact left and right uniform sums for G = x^2, checked against the closed forms of the page."""
    print("part A: uniform partitions, exact, G(x) = x^2")
    target = Fraction(2, 3)
    for n in PARTS_A:
        pts = uniform(n)
        right = rs_sum(pts, pts[1:], g)
        left = rs_sum(pts, pts[:-1], g)
        right_formula = Fraction((n + 1) * (4 * n - 1), 6 * n * n)
        left_formula = Fraction(4 * n * n - 3 * n - 1, 6 * n * n)
        right_err = right - target
        left_err = left - target
        print(
            f"n {n} right {float(right):.12f} left {float(left):.12f} "
            f"right_err {float(right_err):.12f} left_err {float(left_err):.12f} "
            f"right_formula_matches {right == right_formula} left_formula_matches {left == left_formula} "
            f"right_err_formula_matches {right_err == Fraction(3 * n - 1, 6 * n * n)} "
            f"left_err_formula_matches {left_err == -Fraction(3 * n + 1, 6 * n * n)}"
        )


def part_b() -> None:
    """Random tagged partitions: the sum is within 2 times the mesh of 2/3."""
    print("part B: random tagged partitions, seeded")
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    worst_ratio = 0.0
    max_mesh = 0.0
    all_within = True
    for _ in range(PARTS_B):
        m = rng.randint(1, MAX_INTERIOR)
        interior = sorted(rng.random() for _ in range(m))
        bounds = [0.0, *interior, 1.0]
        tags = [lo + rng.random() * (hi - lo) for lo, hi in itertools.pairwise(bounds)]
        total = 0.0
        for i, tag in enumerate(tags, start=1):
            total += tag * (bounds[i] ** 2 - bounds[i - 1] ** 2)
        mesh = max(hi - lo for lo, hi in itertools.pairwise(bounds))
        err = abs(total - 2 / 3)
        all_within = all_within and err <= 2 * mesh + SLACK
        worst_ratio = max(worst_ratio, err / mesh)
        max_mesh = max(max_mesh, mesh)
    print(
        f"partitions {PARTS_B} largest_mesh {max_mesh:.6f} largest_error_over_mesh {worst_ratio:.6f} "
        f"all_within_2_mesh {all_within}"
    )


def part_c() -> None:
    """A step integrator: the sum depends on the tag at the jump, so the sums do not settle as the mesh shrinks."""
    print("part C: step integrator at 1/2, uniform partitions containing 1/2")
    half = Fraction(1, 2)

    def step(x: Fraction) -> int:
        return 1 if x >= half else 0

    for n in (10, 100):
        pts = uniform(n)
        k = n // 2
        left_total = 0
        right_total = 0
        jumps = 0
        for i in range(1, n + 1):
            d_g = step(pts[i]) - step(pts[i - 1])
            if d_g != 0:
                jumps += 1
            if i == k:
                left_total += step(pts[i - 1]) * d_g
                right_total += step(pts[i]) * d_g
        print(
            f"n {n} mesh {float(Fraction(1, n)):.6f} jumps {jumps} "
            f"left_tag_sum {left_total} right_tag_sum {right_total}"
        )


def part_d() -> None:
    """The identity integrator G(x) = x: the sums of f = x are the Riemann sums of x on [0, 1]."""
    print("part D: identity integrator, uniform partitions, exact")
    pts = uniform(IDENTITY_N)
    right = rs_sum(pts, pts[1:], identity)
    left = rs_sum(pts, pts[:-1], identity)
    print(
        f"n {IDENTITY_N} right {float(right):.12f} left {float(left):.12f} "
        f"right_formula_matches {right == Fraction(IDENTITY_N + 1, 2 * IDENTITY_N)} "
        f"left_formula_matches {left == Fraction(IDENTITY_N - 1, 2 * IDENTITY_N)}"
    )


def main() -> None:
    """Run the four parts in order."""
    part_a()
    part_b()
    part_c()
    part_d()

The largest ratio of error to mesh is 0.491631, so every error in Part B is below half of its partition's mesh. In Part C each partition has exactly one jump (jumps 1), and the sums are 0 with the left tag and 1 with the right tag, at meshes 0.1 and 0.01. The two tagged sums do not approach one value. In Part D the right sum at \(n = 1000\) is 0.500500000000 and the left sum is 0.499500000000, which are \(1/(2n)\) above and below \(1/2\). The lab checks these exact values for one function and one integrator each. It does not test a general theorem, and it does not show that the constant 2 in Part B is sharp.

4Proposition 4.1 and the step integrator

Theorem 3.1 needs continuity of \(f\) as well as of \(G'\). Proposition 4.1 shows what fails when \(f\) is discontinuous at the jump, and Proposition 4.2 shows that the integral can exist when \(G\) has no derivative at the jump. Both use the step integrator \(G(x) = 1\) for \(x \ge 1/2\) and \(G(x) = 0\) for \(x < 1/2\) on \([0, 1]\). It is nondecreasing, and it has no derivative at \(1/2\).

Proposition 4.1 (a discontinuous integrand has no integral against a step)

Hypotheses: (1) \(f(x) = 1\) for \(x \ge 1/2\) and \(f(x) = 0\) for \(x < 1/2\), on \([0, 1]\); (2) \(G\) is the step integrator above. Conclusion: no real number \(I\) satisfies Definition 1.2 for \(f\) and \(G\).

Proof

Suppose \(I\) satisfies Definition 1.2 with \(\varepsilon = 1/2\), and let \(\delta > 0\) be the number it gives. Choose an even \(n\) with \(1/n < \delta\). Take the uniform partition with \(x_i = i/n\), and let \(k = n/2\), so that \(x_k = 1/2\). Then \(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1}) = 0\) for \(i \ne k\), since both endpoints lie on the same side of \(1/2\). For \(i = k\), \(G(x_k) - G(x_{k-1}) = 1 - 0 = 1\). Take the tagging \(\xi_k = x_{k-1} < 1/2\), where \(f = 0\), which gives \(S = 0\). Take the tagging \(\xi_k = x_k = 1/2\), where \(f = 1\), which gives \(S = 1\). Both tagged partitions have mesh \(1/n < \delta\), so \(\lvert 0 - I \rvert < 1/2\) and \(\lvert 1 - I \rvert < 1/2\). These two distances add to at least 1, so they cannot both be below \(1/2\). This is a contradiction, so no such \(I\) exists.

Remark 4.2 (monotone is not enough)

Proposition 4.1 uses a nondecreasing integrator, so monotonicity of \(G\) does not rescue the integral when \(f\) is discontinuous at the jump. The step integrator also violates hypothesis (3) of Theorem 3.1, so Proposition 4.1 does not by itself show that hypothesis (2) is needed when \(G\) is continuously differentiable.

Proposition 4.2 (a continuous integrand picks up the value at a jump)

Hypotheses: (1) \(f(x) = x\) on \([0, 1]\), which is continuous; (2) \(G\) is the step integrator above. Conclusion: for every tagged partition, \(\lvert S(P, \xi) - 1/2 \rvert \le \lvert P \rvert\). Hence \(\int_0^1 f\,dG = 1/2\).

Proof

Let \(P\) have points \(x_0 < \cdots < x_n\). Since \(0 < 1/2 \le 1\), there is a unique index \(k\) with \(x_{k-1} < 1/2 \le x_k\). For \(i \ne k\), the endpoints of \(I_i\) lie on the same side of \(1/2\), so \(G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1}) = 0\). For \(i = k\), the increment is 1. Hence \(S(P, \xi) = f(\xi_k) = \xi_k\). Both \(\xi_k\) and \(1/2\) lie in \([x_{k-1}, x_k]\), because \(1/2\) lies in \((x_{k-1}, x_k]\). So \(\lvert \xi_k - 1/2 \rvert \le \Delta x_k \le \lvert P \rvert\), which gives the bound. Given \(\varepsilon > 0\), any \(\delta \le \varepsilon\) satisfies Definition 1.2 with \(I = 1/2\).

5Where the canon breaks

  • Continuity of the integrand. Proposition 4.1 shows that a discontinuous integrand can have no Riemann–Stieltjes integral, even against a nondecreasing step. Lab 1, Part C, prints the two tagged sums (0 and 1) at meshes 0.1 and 0.01 for the same step. The example violates hypothesis (3) of Theorem 3.1 as well as hypothesis (2), so it does not show that hypothesis (2) cannot be dropped when \(G\) is continuously differentiable.
  • Differentiability of the integrator. Proposition 4.2 shows, for \(f(x) = x\), that the integral against a jump integrator exists and equals the value of the integrand at the jump. Problem R2 proves the same for any continuous \(f\). The formula \(\int f\,dG = \int f G'\,dx\) of Theorem 3.1 cannot be applied at \(1/2\), because the step has no derivative there.
  • The integrator is not the variable. For \(G(x) = x^{2}\) the integral of \(f(x) = x\) is \(2/3\). The integral of the same \(f\) against \(G(x) = x\), the ordinary Riemann case, is \(1/2\) (Part D of Lab 1 prints sums that are \(1/(2n)\) from this value at \(n = 1000\)). A change of integrator can change the integral, so \(dG\) cannot be replaced by \(dx\) in general. For \(G(x) = x^{2}\) the value changes from \(1/2\) to \(2/3\), while for \(G(x) = x + 5\) it does not change (Problem P3 and R3).

6Misconceptions

  1. “When \(G\) is increasing, \(\int f\,dG\) equals \(\int f\,dx\).”

    Corollary 3.2 gives \(\int_0^1 x\,d(x^{2}) = 2/3\), and the ordinary integral \(\int_0^1 x\,dx = 1/2\) is different. Lab 1, Part D, prints the identity-integrator sums at \(n = 1000\) as 0.500500000000 and 0.499500000000, which are within 0.0005 of \(1/2\), not of \(2/3\). The integrator enters the sums, so \(G\) is part of the integral.

  2. “A discontinuous integrand is harmless when the mesh is small.”

    Proposition 4.1 shows that the sums of a discontinuous integrand against a step can take the values 0 and 1 at the same mesh. Lab 1, Part C, prints both sums on uniform partitions containing \(1/2\), at mesh 0.1 and at mesh 0.01. Shrinking the mesh does not remove the jump, because the tag at the jump decides the sum.

  3. “The formula \(\int f\,dG = \int f G'\,dx\) holds for every increasing \(G\).”

    Theorem 3.1 requires \(G\) to be continuously differentiable. The step integrator of Section 4 is nondecreasing but has no derivative at \(1/2\). Proposition 4.2 gives \(\int_0^1 x\,dG = 1/2\) for it, while the formula's right side needs a derivative that the step does not have. The hypothesis is needed to apply the formula.

7Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · A Riemann–Stieltjes sum on three points

Take \(f(x) = x\), \(G(x) = x^{3}\) on \([0, 1]\), the partition \(\{0, 1/2, 1\}\), and right tags. Compute \(S(P, \xi)\).

Solution

The tags are \(\xi_1 = 1/2\) and \(\xi_2 = 1\). The increments are \(G(1/2) - G(0) = 1/8\) and \(G(1) - G(1/2) = 7/8\). So \(S = (1/2)(1/8) + 1 \cdot (7/8) = 1/16 + 14/16 = 15/16\).

M2 · The integral over a longer interval

Compute \(\int_0^2 x\,d(x^{2})\).

Solution

Both hypotheses of Theorem 3.1 hold on \([0, 2]\), since \(G'(x) = 2x\) is continuous. So the integral equals \(\int_0^2 x \cdot 2x\,dx = 2\int_0^2 x^{2}\,dx\). Lesson 11 of M2 (Example 3.2) states \(\int_0^2 x^{2}\,dx = 8/3\). So the integral is \(16/3\).

M3 · The integral of the constant one

Let \(f \equiv 1\) on \([0, 1]\), and let \(G(x) = x^{3}\). Show that \(S(P, \xi) = G(1) - G(0)\) for every tagged partition, and find \(\int_0^1 dG\).

Solution

With \(f \equiv 1\), \(S(P, \xi) = \sum_{i} (G(x_i) - G(x_{i-1}))\). This sum telescopes to \(G(x_n) - G(x_0) = G(1) - G(0)\), whatever the partition and tags. So \(\int_0^1 dG = G(1) - G(0) = 1\). No continuity of \(G\) was used.

Proof

P1 · The Riemann–Stieltjes integral is unique

Use Definition 1.2 to prove that the integral, if it exists, is unique. This is Lemma 1.3, and the proof there uses only the definition and uniform partitions.

Solution

Let \(I\) and \(I'\) both satisfy Definition 1.2, and suppose \(I \ne I'\). Put \(\varepsilon = \lvert I - I' \rvert / 2\), and take \(\delta, \delta'\) from the definition for each value. A uniform partition with mesh below both \(\delta\) and \(\delta'\) exists, because its mesh is \((b - a)/n\). For any tagging of that partition, \(\lvert I - I' \rvert \le \lvert S - I \rvert + \lvert S - I' \rvert < 2\varepsilon = \lvert I - I' \rvert\), which is a contradiction. So \(I = I'\).

P2 · The sharper bound for x against x squared

For \(f(x) = x\), \(G(x) = x^{2}\) on \([0, 1]\), and any tagged partition with mesh \(m\), prove that \(\lvert S(P, \xi) - 2/3 \rvert \le m\).

Solution

As in the proof of Theorem 3.1, \(S - I = \sum_i \int_{I_i}(\xi_i - t)\,2t\,dt\), where \(I = 2/3\) by Corollary 3.2. For \(t \in I_i\), \(\lvert \xi_i - t \rvert \le \Delta x_i \le m\), and \(2t \ge 0\), so \(-m\,2t \le (\xi_i - t)2t \le m\,2t\) on \(I_i\). By Lemma 2.4(a) and linearity (P2), \(\bigl\lvert \int_{I_i}(\xi_i - t)2t\,dt \bigr\rvert \le m\int_{I_i} 2t\,dt\). Summing over \(i\) and using Lemma 2.4(c), \(\lvert S - I \rvert \le m\int_0^1 2t\,dt = m\). So the bound \(m\) holds.

P3 · The identity integrator gives the ordinary integral

Let \(f\) be continuous on \([a, b]\), and let \(G(x) = x\). Prove that \(\int_a^b f\,dG = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx\).

Solution

The function \(G(x) = x\) is continuously differentiable with \(G' \equiv 1\), which is continuous. Theorem 3.1 applies, so \(\int_a^b f\,dG = \int_a^b f(x) G'(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx\), and the right side exists by Lemma 2.2. This is the sense in which the Riemann–Stieltjes integral extends the ordinary integral.

E1 · Which tag is closer at n = 10

Read the \(n = 10\) row of Lab 1, Part A. Which tag gives the sum closer to \(2/3\), and by how much?

Solution

The right-tag sum is 0.715000000000, and the left-tag sum is 0.615000000000. The exact errors are \(29/600\) for the right tag and \(-31/600\) for the left tag, from the formulas of Example 3.3 with \(n = 10\). So the right tag is closer. The gap is \(31/600 - 29/600 = 1/300\), which the printed values give as 0.003333333334 from the printed digits of the two errors.

E2 · The error against the mesh in Part B

Read the Part B line. What does the printed largest ratio of error to mesh say about the bound of Problem P2?

Solution

The line prints largest_error_over_mesh 0.491631. This is the largest ratio of \(\lvert S - 2/3 \rvert\) to the mesh over the 200 partitions. It is below 1, which is the bound that Problem P2 proves: \(\lvert S - 2/3 \rvert \le m\). So every error in Part B is below half of its partition's mesh, which is consistent with the sharper bound.

E3 · The identity integrator at n = 1000

Read Part D of Lab 1. How far are the printed right and left sums from \(1/2\), and what is their average?

Solution

The printed right sum is 0.500500000000, which is \(1/(2n) = 0.0005\) above \(1/2\), and the left sum is 0.499500000000, which is \(0.0005\) below it. Their average is exactly \(1/2\), because \((n+1)/(2n) + (n-1)/(2n) = 1\). Both sums are the Riemann sums of \(x\) on \([0, 1]\), and their common limit is \(\int_0^1 x\,dx = 1/2\), in agreement with P3.

Research

R1 · Where the proof of Theorem 3.1 uses continuity of G'

Identify each place in the proof of Theorem 3.1 that uses the continuity of \(G'\), and state the weakest hypothesis on \(G'\) that the proof, as written, needs. Do not claim a result the proof does not show.

Solution

Continuity of \(G'\) is used in three places. First, it gives \(M = \sup \lvert G' \rvert < \infty\) through Lemma 2.1(i). Second, it makes \(G'\) integrable on each \(I_i\), which (P3) needs for the fundamental theorem step. Third, it makes \(fG'\) integrable through Lemma 2.2, so that \(I\) exists. The proof as written needs \(G'\) bounded and integrable on each subinterval, and G differentiable on each open subinterval with derivative \(G'\). The proof does not show that these are the weakest conditions, and it does not show that the theorem holds for a \(G'\) that is merely bounded.

R2 · A step at an interior point

Let \(f\) be continuous on \([0, 1]\), let \(c \in (0, 1)\), and let \(G\) be the step \(G(x) = 0\) for \(x < c\) and \(G(x) = 1\) for \(x \ge c\). Prove that \(\int_0^1 f\,dG = f(c)\).

Solution

Exactly as in Proposition 4.2, for a tagged partition there is a unique \(k\) with \(x_{k-1} < c \le x_k\), and \(S = f(\xi_k)\). Given \(\varepsilon > 0\), continuity of \(f\) at \(c\) gives \(\delta_0\) with \(\lvert f(s) - f(c) \rvert < \varepsilon\) for \(\lvert s - c \rvert < \delta_0\). For a partition of mesh \(m < \delta_0\), \(\xi_k\) lies within \(m\) of \(c\), because \(c\) and \(\xi_k\) both lie in \([x_{k-1}, x_k]\). So \(\lvert S - f(c) \rvert < \varepsilon\) and \(f(c)\) satisfies Definition 1.2.

R3 · Adding a constant to the integrator

Let \(f\) be continuous and \(G\) continuously differentiable on \([a, b]\), and let \(c\) be a constant. Show that \(\int_a^b f\,d(G + c) = \int_a^b f\,dG\). Check the claim for \(f(x) = x\) and \(G(x) = x^{2} + 5\) on \([0, 1]\).

Solution

For every tagged partition, the increments of \(G + c\) equal the increments of \(G\), because the constant cancels in \(\bigl(G(x_i) + c\bigr) - \bigl(G(x_{i-1}) + c\bigr)\). So the sums are identical, and the limits in Definition 1.2 are identical. Hence the integrals are equal. For the check, \(G + c\) has derivative \(2x\), which is continuous, so Theorem 3.1 applies with \(G' = 2x\), and the integral is \(\int_0^1 x \cdot 2x\,dx = 2/3\), the same value as in Corollary 3.2.
