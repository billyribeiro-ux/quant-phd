Sensitivity Engine

A desk reports how a bond price moves when its yield moves, to the cent, at first and second order. Each sensitivity can be computed by a formula, by a finite difference, or by automatic differentiation, and each method fails in its own way. You will write the module that computes all three, the Hessian of a function of two variables and the price in integer cents, and then write a note that shows where each numerical method is accurate and where it breaks.

Lab 1 · Worked example: one bond, step by step

The bond has face value 1000000 cents, a 5.00 % annual coupon, ten annual payments, and a yield of 4.00 %. Each year pays 50000 cents of coupon, and the last year adds the face value. Stated before running: the unrounded price is 1081108.96 cents, which rounds to 1081109 cents; the analytic \(dP/dy\) is about \(-8514667.40\) cents per unit of yield, and its central difference with step \(10^{-5}\) agrees with it to about \(2\times10^{-9}\) relative; the analytic \(d^2P/dy^2\) is about 83766485.12, and its central difference agrees with it to about \(10^{-7}\) relative; the duration \(-P'/P\) is 7.88 years. The derivatives use the chain rule of Lesson 5 on each discount factor \((1+y)^{-t}\), and the central differences are the finite-difference derivative of Lesson 13.

"""Rung 1 worked example: price and yield sensitivities of one SIMULATED bond, analytic and by central difference.

The bond is SIMULATED, not market data: face value 1000000 cents (10000.00), an annual coupon of 5.00 %, ten annual
payments, and a yield of 4.00 % compounded once a year. The program prints the unrounded price in cents, the rounded
price, the first and second derivatives in the yield by their cash-flow formulas and by central differences with
step 1e-5, the differences between each formula and its central difference, and the duration -P'/P in years.
Standard library only.
"""

Flow = tuple[int, float]


def price_cents(flows: list[Flow], y: float) -> float:
    """The unrounded price in cents at the annual yield y: the sum of cash_t / (1 + y)**t over the flows."""
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    return sum(cash * v**t for t, cash in flows)


def main() -> None:
    face_cents = 1_000_000  # SIMULATED face value, 10000.00 in cents
    coupon = face_cents * 0.05  # cents paid at the end of each year
    years = 10
    y = 0.04
    # Step 1. The cash flow of year t: the coupon, and the face value added in the last year.
    flows = [(t, coupon + (face_cents if t == years else 0)) for t in range(1, years + 1)]
    # Step 2. The discount factor of year t is v**t with v = 1 / (1 + y); the price is the sum of the discounted flows.
    price = price_cents(flows, y)
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    # Step 3. The derivative of v**t in y is -t * v**(t+1), so dP/dy = -sum t * cash * v**(t+1). The second derivative
    # of v**t is t(t+1) * v**(t+2), so d2P/dy2 = sum t(t+1) * cash * v**(t+2).
    first = -sum(t * cash * v ** (t + 1) for t, cash in flows)
    second = sum(t * (t + 1) * cash * v ** (t + 2) for t, cash in flows)
    # Step 4. The same two derivatives by central differences of the unrounded price, with step h = 1e-5.
    h = 1e-5
    up, down = price_cents(flows, y + h), price_cents(flows, y - h)
    first_numeric = (up - down) / (2 * h)
    second_numeric = (up - 2 * price + down) / (h * h)
    print("unrounded price (cents):", f"{price:.4f}")
    print("rounded price (cents, half to even):", round(price))
    print("dP/dy analytic (cents per unit yield):", f"{first:.4f}")
    print("dP/dy central difference:", f"{first_numeric:.4f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(first - first_numeric):.2e}")
    print("d2P/dy2 analytic (cents per squared unit yield):", f"{second:.4f}")
    print("d2P/dy2 central difference:", f"{second_numeric:.4f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(second - second_numeric):.2e}")
    print("duration -P'/P (years):", f"{-first / price:.6f}")

Lab 2 · Completion problem: the second difference and the dual product

Replace each ___ with the expression that the lesson gives. The central second difference is \((f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h))/h^2\), and the product rule of Lesson 14 says that the dot part of a product of two dual numbers (a value with its derivative, Lesson 14) is \(a\,\dot b + \dot a\,b\). When both lines are right, the second difference of the test cubic at \(x_0 = 0.8\) with step \(10^{-4}\) agrees with its analytic value 8.4 to better than \(10^{-7}\), and the forward-mode derivative agrees with the analytic value 3.76 to better than \(10^{-15}\).

"""Rung 2 completed solution: a central second difference, and forward-mode AD on a dual number.

The completion problem shows the two blanked lines as ___. Both are checked here on a cubic with coefficients
(1, -2, 3, 0.5), a test function and not a bond: the second difference at x0 = 0.8 against its analytic value 8.4, and
the forward-mode derivative at x0 against its analytic value 3.76. Each line is printed with its analytic value and the
difference. Standard library only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from collections.abc import Callable
from typing import Any


class Dual:
    """A dual number value + dot * eps, with eps squared equal to zero: the carrier of forward-mode AD."""

    def __init__(self, value: float, dot: float) -> None:
        self.value = value
        self.dot = dot

    def __add__(self, other: Any) -> Dual:
        o = lift(other)
        return Dual(self.value + o.value, self.dot + o.dot)

    __radd__ = __add__

    def __mul__(self, other: Any) -> Dual:
        o = lift(other)
        return Dual(self.value * o.value, ___)

    __rmul__ = __mul__


def lift(x: Any) -> Dual:
    """A dual number unchanged, or a real number as a dual number with zero derivative."""
    return x if isinstance(x, Dual) else Dual(float(x), 0.0)


def second_difference(f: Callable[[float], float], x: float, h: float) -> float:
    """The central second difference (f(x+h) - 2f(x) + f(x-h)) / h**2 of f at x with step h."""
    return ___


def ad_derivative(f: Callable[[Any], Any], x: float) -> float:
    """The derivative of f at x: f is evaluated on a dual number whose dot part is 1."""
    return float(lift(f(Dual(x, 1.0))).dot)


def cubic(x: Any) -> Any:
    """The test cubic 1 - 2x + 3x^2 + 0.5x^3 in Horner form, so that a dual number argument works."""
    return 1.0 + x * (-2.0 + x * (3.0 + x * 0.5))


def main() -> None:
    x0 = 0.8
    h = 1e-4
    second_numeric = second_difference(cubic, x0, h)
    second_analytic = 2 * 3.0 + 6 * 0.5 * x0
    print("second difference:", f"{second_numeric:.6f}", "| analytic:", f"{second_analytic:.6f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(second_numeric - second_analytic):.2e}")
    derivative = ad_derivative(cubic, x0)
    first_analytic = -2.0 + 2 * 3.0 * x0 + 3 * 0.5 * x0 * x0
    print("AD derivative:", f"{derivative:.6f}", "| analytic:", f"{first_analytic:.6f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(derivative - first_analytic):.2e}")

The central second difference of a cubic is exact in exact arithmetic, because its fourth derivative is zero, so the printed difference of 5.11e-08 is floating-point error alone. The dual number carries the derivative alongside the value, so the product line must add both product terms. The printed difference of 4.44e-16 is the floating-point error of the sum; the derivative is exact up to that error. The test cubic is written in Horner form, so the same function runs on a float and on a dual number.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A desk reports the price of each bond in integer cents and the sensitivity of that price to its yield, first and second order, so that a reader can see how far the price moves for a given move in the yield. The formulas are exact, but the desk also computes the same sensitivities numerically, and it needs to know where each numerical method is accurate. You write the module to the interface below, check it against the analytic values, and show in a note where each numerical method breaks.
Inputs
No data file. The autograder's instance comes from grader/instance.py, seeded by your workspace ID. It holds a SIMULATED bond (a face value in whole cents, a coupon in whole basis points, a maturity in whole years, and a yield in whole basis points), a cubic test function of one variable with a point x0, and a test function of two variables, a quadratic with a cubic cross term, with a point. All of it is SIMULATED. The note uses the bond of Lab 3: 2500000 cents face, a 6.00 % coupon, 12 years and a 5.00 % yield. It is an illustrative input and not market data.
Required outputs
In a repository of your own: sensitivity_engine.py at its root, with the five functions below, and a report/ folder beside it. Start from the starter file, whose signatures are complete. The functions are fd_derivative(f, x, h, scheme), returning a float; ad_derivative(f, x), returning a float; hessian_fd(f, x, h), returning a square list of lists of floats; bond_price_cents(instance), returning an int; and sensitivities(instance), returning a dictionary of floats with the keys dprice_dy, d2price_dy2, duration_analytic and convexity_analytic. For the note, commit three more files: report/run_report.py, which is Lab 3 with its calls replaced by calls to your module and which prints the same lines; report/error_analysis.md, the note described in the brief with its appendix; and report/self_assessment.md, the summary that the form in the self-assessment section produces.
Constraints
The standard library, or NumPy, only. Three conditions raise ValueError, and the conventions below name them: a step that is not above zero, a scheme other than "forward" or "central", and a yield at or below -1. No other exception is required, and no other input is specified. No function prints or warns. A run in your browser stops after 120 seconds. The conventions in the next subsection are binding.
Grading mode
Both modes apply. The code is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which runs 46 tests with warnings as errors. The tests cover the orders of the two finite differences from two steps, the forward-mode derivative and the Hessian against their analytic values, the symmetry of the Hessian, the price in integer cents against an exact rational calculation, the sensitivities against central differences of the unrounded price, the three named ValueError conditions in both directions, and the purity of the module. The same file runs in your browser and on your machine. It is not secret: its integrity comes from your seeded instance and from a second instance. The error-analysis note is graded by rubric: five criteria with four observable levels each, scored with the form in the self-assessment section and calibrated on two annotated exemplars. The note meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Conventions

  • Rates and yields. A rate is a decimal per period: 0.04 is 4 %. The yield is an annual rate compounded once a year. The instance gives it in whole basis points, so the yield is yield_bps / 10000, and the coupon rate is coupon_bps / 10000.
  • Cash flows. A bond with face value \(F\) cents, a coupon rate \(c\) and \(T\) whole years pays \(C_t = Fc\) cents at the end of each year \(t < T\), and \(C_T = Fc + F\) at the end of year \(T\). With \(F > 0\) and \(c \ge 0\), every cash flow is positive.
  • Unrounded price. The price is \(P(y) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} C_t\,(1+y)^{-t}\) in cents. It is not rounded. The derivatives are taken of this unrounded price.
  • Price in integer cents. bond_price_cents returns \(P(y)\) rounded to the nearest whole cent, an exact half cent going to the even cent, as an int. This is half-to-even rounding, and no other rounding rule is used.
  • Sensitivities. \(P'(y) = -\sum_{t} t\,C_t\,(1+y)^{-t-1}\) is dprice_dy, in cents per unit of yield. \(P''(y) = \sum_{t} t(t+1)\,C_t\,(1+y)^{-t-2}\) is d2price_dy2, in cents per squared unit of yield. Both are floats, not money.
  • Named ratios. duration_analytic is \(-P'(y)/P(y)\), in years, and convexity_analytic is \(P''(y)/P(y)\), in years squared. The two names are previews. The module uses only these formulas, and their later development is not part of this project.
  • Forward and central differences. For a step \(h > 0\), the forward estimate of \(f'(x)\) is \((f(x+h)-f(x))/h\), and the central estimate is \((f(x+h)-f(x-h))/(2h)\). Both are finite-difference derivatives of Lesson 13.
  • Hessian. For a step \(h > 0\), the diagonal entry \(i\) is \((f(x+he_i)-2f(x)+f(x-he_i))/h^2\), and the off-diagonal entry \((i,j)\) is \((f(x+he_i+he_j)-f(x+he_i-he_j)-f(x-he_i+he_j)+f(x-he_i-he_j))/(4h^2)\). The result is a list of lists, the matrix of second partial derivatives, the Hessian of Lesson 19.
  • Automatic differentiation. ad_derivative(f, x) evaluates \(f\) on an object of your own design that carries a value and a derivative together, a dual number, and returns the derivative at \(x\). The function \(f\) is written with numbers and the operators +, - and *. The result is the exact derivative of the function, not an approximation.
  • The three ValueError conditions. (a) A step \(h\) that is not above zero raises ValueError in fd_derivative and in hessian_fd. (b) A scheme other than "forward" or "central" raises ValueError in fd_derivative. (c) A yield at or below -1, that is yield_bps at or below -10000, raises ValueError in bond_price_cents and in sensitivities. A yield above -1 is accepted.
  • Tolerances. A comparison passes when its error is at most the tolerance times the larger of 1 and the magnitude of the expected value. The tolerances are: \(10^{-6}\) for the central difference of the cubic at step \(10^{-5}\); \(10^{-9}\) for the forward-mode derivative of the cubic; \(10^{-5}\) for each Hessian entry at step \(10^{-4}\); \(10^{-6}\) for symmetry, as the difference of the two off-diagonal entries against one plus the larger of them; \(10^{-6}\) for dprice_dy against the central difference of the unrounded price at step \(10^{-5}\); \(10^{-4}\) for d2price_dy2 against the second central difference at step \(10^{-5}\); and \(10^{-9}\) for the two named ratios. The order checks take the errors of the forward and the central estimates at steps \(10^{-2}\) and \(5 \times 10^{-3}\), and require the ratio of the two errors to lie from 1.9 to 2.1 for forward and from 3.8 to 4.2 for central.

What the suite does not check

The suite does not fix a default step, a particular algorithm, or an internal representation. An implementation may use a Richardson extrapolation, a complex step, a reverse-mode tape, a NumPy vector sum, or a closed-form cash-flow sum, and the same suite accepts each of them. A failing test names the property it checks and the idea to revisit, and it never prints the expected value.

"""
The bond is SIMULATED, not market data: face value 2500000 cents (25000.00), an annual coupon of 6.00 %, twelve
annual payments, and a yield of 5.00 %. The program prints the unrounded price, the analytic dP/dy with its forward
and central differences over a sweep of steps from 1e-1 to 1e-12, the step with the smallest central error, the
observed orders from a tenfold cut in the step, a kink case |x - 0.3| at x = 0.3 by forward, backward and central
differences, and a cancellation case 1e8 + sin(x) at x = 0.5 by the central difference. Standard library only.
"""

import math
from collections.abc import Callable

Flow = tuple[int, float]


def bond_price(flows: list[Flow], y: float) -> float:
    """The unrounded price in cents at the annual yield y."""
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    return sum(cash * v**t for t, cash in flows)


def difference(f: Callable[[float], float], x: float, h: float, scheme: str) -> float:
    """A forward or central difference estimate of f'(x) with step h."""
    if scheme == "forward":
        return (f(x + h) - f(x)) / h
    if scheme == "backward":
        return (f(x) - f(x - h)) / h
    return (f(x + h) - f(x - h)) / (2 * h)


def main() -> None:
    face_cents = 2_500_000  # SIMULATED face value, 25000.00 in cents
    coupon = face_cents * 0.06  # cents paid at the end of each year
    years = 12
    y = 0.05
    flows = [(t, coupon + (face_cents if t == years else 0)) for t in range(1, years + 1)]
    price = bond_price(flows, y)
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    exact = -sum(t * cash * v ** (t + 1) for t, cash in flows)
    print("unrounded price (cents):", f"{price:.4f}")
    print("rounded price (cents, half to even):", round(price))
    print("dP/dy analytic (cents per unit yield):", f"{exact:.6f}")

    def price_of(yield_rate: float) -> float:
        return bond_price(flows, yield_rate)

    steps = [10.0**-k for k in range(1, 13)]
    errors: dict[float, tuple[float, float]] = {}
    print("step h | forward error | central error")
    for h in steps:
        fwd = abs(difference(price_of, y, h, "forward") - exact)
        cen = abs(difference(price_of, y, h, "central") - exact)
        errors[h] = (fwd, cen)
        print(f"{h:.0e} | {fwd:.6e} | {cen:.6e}")
    best = min(steps, key=lambda h: errors[h][1])
    print("smallest central error at step:", f"{best:.0e}", "| error:", f"{errors[best][1]:.6e}")
    fwd_order = math.log10(errors[1e-2][0] / errors[1e-3][0])
    cen_order = math.log10(errors[1e-2][1] / errors[1e-3][1])
    print("observed order, forward (log10 of the error ratio, steps 1e-2 to 1e-3):", f"{fwd_order:.3f}")
    print("observed order, central (log10 of the error ratio, steps 1e-2 to 1e-3):", f"{cen_order:.3f}")

    def kink(x: float) -> float:
        return abs(x - 0.3)

    print("kink |x - 0.3| at x = 0.3, step 1e-3: forward", f"{difference(kink, 0.3, 1e-3, 'forward'):.1f}")
    print("kink at x = 0.3, step 1e-3: backward", f"{difference(kink, 0.3, 1e-3, 'backward'):.1f}")
    print("kink at x = 0.3, step 1e-3: central", f"{difference(kink, 0.3, 1e-3, 'central'):.1f}")
    print("kink at x = 0.3, step 1e-6: central", f"{difference(kink, 0.3, 1e-6, 'central'):.1f}")

    def offset(x: float) -> float:
        return 1e8 + math.sin(x)

    truth = math.cos(0.5)
    print("cancellation 1e8 + sin(x) at x = 0.5, exact f' =", f"{truth:.10f}")
    for h in (1e-4, 1e-8, 1e-12):
        cen = difference(offset, 0.5, h, "central")
        print(f"cancellation, central at step {h:.0e}:", f"{cen:.6f}", "| error:", f"{abs(cen - truth):.2e}")

The brief

Write for a reader who knows Lessons 4, 8, 13 and 14, not for a referee of this bond. Use these five sections, in this order and under these headings, followed by the appendix.

  1. Summary: the bond, its price, and the one number that shows where the central difference is most accurate, in at most 80 words.
  2. Setup and conventions: the bond and its cash flows, the unrounded and the rounded price, and which module function computes each number.
  3. Truncation and floating-point error: the expansion of each scheme with its power of \(h\), the step-size sweep, the step of smallest central error, and the observed orders.
  4. Where differences break: the kink and the cancellation case, each with the sweep rows that show it.
  5. Limitations: what the sweep covers, what it does not show, and what the two failure cases do not settle.

Appendix A · Derivations. Four results, each stated with its assumptions and derived in your own words: A1 the forward error, from the Taylor expansion of \(f\) at \(x\) with the remainder in Lagrange form; A2 the central error, from the same expansion to the third order; A3 the effect of floating-point error on a difference quotient, with the assumption about the size of the error in each value; A4 the one-sided slopes of the kink \(|x-0.3|\) at \(x = 0.3\).

Length. The body (the five sections) runs 400 to 900 words. The appendix has no limit. A word is a run of characters between spaces.

Numbers. Every number the note quotes is printed by report/run_report.py, with its unit, or is derived in the note from the bond's inputs. A derivative is quoted with its unit: cents per unit of yield, or cents per squared unit of yield. A cent amount is quoted as the integer the run prints.

Scoring. Each criterion scores the highest level whose every element the note shows. Each level contains the one below it: level 4 is level 3 plus the elements its cell names; level 2 is any attempt that misses an element of level 3 or makes an error its cell names; level 1 is the absence its cell names. The note meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Summary

The bond is SIMULATED: 2500000 cents face, a 6.00 % coupon, 12 years, a 5.00 % yield. Its unrounded price is 2721581.2909 cents, rounded to 2721581 cents. The smallest printed error of the central difference is 7.111207e-04 cents per unit of yield, at step 1e-6, against the analytic derivative of -23407826.805475 cents per unit of yield.

  • Meets C5 level 2: states the unit of the price, in cents.
Truncation and floating-point error

By Taylor's theorem with the remainder in Lagrange form, \(f(x+h) = f(x) + hf'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(\xi)\) for some \(\xi\) between \(x\) and \(x+h\). So the forward error is \(\tfrac{h}{2}f''(\xi)\), of order \(h\) (A1). To the third order, \(f(x\pm h) = f(x) \pm hf'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x) \pm \tfrac{h^3}{6}f'''(\xi_\pm)\), so the central error is of order \(h^2\) (A2). The sweep over steps from 1e-1 to 1e-12 shows the forward error at 1e-6 is 1.302673e+02, and the central error at 1e-6 is 7.111207e-04, the smallest central error printed. Below 1e-6 the central error is larger than its minimum at every printed step: 2.329569e-02 at 1e-7 and 5.004462e+02 at 1e-12. Each price is a float near 2.7 million, whose spacing is 2^-31, about 4.7e-10 cents; the difference of two prices divided by 2h then magnifies that error by 1/(2h), which is 5e11 at h = 1e-12 (A3). The lab prints the errors, not the spacing, so this is the stated mechanism and not a measured one.

  • Meets C1 level 3: states both truncation errors with their powers of \(h\), and gives a sweep over eleven decades that names the step of smallest central error.
  • Meets C1 level 4: states the floating-point error as proportional to \(1/h\), with its reason, and identifies the step where the sweep turns.
  • Meets C4 level 3: derives both orders from the expansions, with each step shown.
Observed orders and the tolerance

From steps 1e-2 and 1e-3 the sweep gives an observed order of 0.984 for the forward difference and 2.000 for the central difference. The derived orders are 1 for the forward and 2 for the central difference (A1, A2). The forward value 0.984 is below the derived 1, and the lab does not separate the two terms of its expansion, so the note does not attribute the gap to either term. The central value 2.000 matches the derived 2. The tolerance is 1e-6 relative. The central error at 1e-6 is 7.111207e-04, and relative to 23407826.805475 it is about 3e-11, which is below the tolerance.

  • Meets C3 level 4: states the relative tolerance and shows that the printed error at the chosen step is below it.
  • Misses C4 level 4: the remainders are stated in Lagrange form, but no bound on the error for a stated step is derived from them.
Where the differences break

For \(f(x) = |x-0.3|\) at \(x = 0.3\), the forward estimate is \((|h|)/h = +1\) for every step \(h > 0\), the backward estimate is \((0-|-h|)/h = -1\), and their average, which the central difference returns, is 0. The printed values are 1.0, -1.0 and 0.0. The derivative does not exist at the corner, and no step fixes the central value. For \(f(x) = 10^8 + \sin x\) at \(x = 0.5\), the float spacing near \(10^8\) is \(2^{-26}\), about 1.49e-8. At step \(10^{-8}\) the difference \(f(x+h)-f(x-h)\) spans \(2h = 2\times10^{-8}\), about 1.3 spacings, and the central error is 1.33e-01. At step \(10^{-4}\) the error is 5.32e-05, and at step \(10^{-12}\) the computed derivative is 0.000000, with error 8.78e-01.

  • Meets C2 level 4: derives the kink from the one-sided slopes, and the cancellation case from the float spacing, with the sweep rows that show each.
Units

The bond's price is 2721581 cents when rounded, and the derivatives use the unrounded 2721581.2909 cents. The first derivative is in cents per unit of yield, the second derivative is in cents per squared unit of yield, and the duration of the bond is in years. The rounding of the price does not enter any derivative.

  • Meets C5 level 4: states which price each derivative is computed from, the unrounded one, and the rounded integer that the function returns.
  • Meets C5 level 3: states the unit of the price, each derivative and the duration, and uses each consistently.
