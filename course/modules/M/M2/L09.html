Riemann Sums and the Definite Integral

  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

An integral is the area under a curve, and a sum of many thin rectangles is the way to define it without assuming what it is. Two sums bound it from above and below, and a function is integrable when the best upper bound and the best lower bound agree. This lesson defines the integral this way, proves that it exists for every monotone function (Section 2), and bounds the error of a Riemann sum (Section 1) of a monotone function by the mesh times the total change of the function (Corollary 2.3). The common value of the sums is what Lesson 10 turns into a formula.

1Partitions, sums and the definite integral

Let \([a, b]\) be a closed interval with \(a < b\). A partition \(P\) of \([a, b]\) is a finite list \(a = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n = b\). Its subintervals are \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), with widths \(\Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}\), and its mesh is \(\delta(P) = \max_i \Delta x_i\), the width of the widest subinterval.

Let \(f\) be bounded on \([a, b]\). On each subinterval set \(M_i = \sup\{f(x) : x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\}\) and \(m_i = \inf\{f(x) : x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\}\). The upper sum and the lower sum of \(f\) for \(P\) are \[ U(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} M_i\,\Delta x_i, \qquad L(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i\,\Delta x_i. \] Since \(m_i \le M_i\), always \(L(f, P) \le U(f, P)\).

For tags \(t_i \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\), the sum \(R = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(t_i)\,\Delta x_i\) is a Riemann sum of \(f\) for \(P\). Since \(m_i \le f(t_i) \le M_i\) for each \(i\), every Riemann sum lies between the lower and upper sums of its partition.

Lemma 1.1 (refinement)

Let \(Q\) contain every point of \(P\). Then \(L(f, P) \le L(f, Q)\) and \(U(f, Q) \le U(f, P)\).

Proof

It suffices to add one point \(t\) to \(P\), and then induct on the number of added points. Let \(t\) lie strictly inside the subinterval \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), so that it splits it into \([x_{i-1}, t]\) and \([t, x_i]\). The supremum over a smaller set is at most the supremum over the larger, so \(M' \le M_i\) and \(M'' \le M_i\), where \(M'\) and \(M''\) are the suprema on the two pieces. Hence \(M'(t - x_{i-1}) + M''(x_i - t) \le M_i (x_i - x_{i-1})\), and the upper sum does not increase. The same argument with infima shows that the lower sum does not decrease.

Lemma 1.2 (every lower sum is below every upper sum)

For any two partitions \(P\) and \(Q\) of \([a, b]\), \(L(f, P) \le U(f, Q)\).

Proof

Let \(R\) be the partition formed by all the points of \(P\) and \(Q\). Then \(R\) contains \(P\) and \(Q\), so Lemma 1.1 gives \(L(f, P) \le L(f, R)\) and \(U(f, R) \le U(f, Q)\). The middle inequality \(L(f, R) \le U(f, R)\) holds because \(m_i \le M_i\) on each subinterval of \(R\). Chaining the three gives \(L(f, P) \le U(f, Q)\).

Definition 1.3 (integrable function and the definite integral)

Let \(f\) be bounded on \([a, b]\). Set \(I_* = \sup_P L(f, P)\) and \(I^* = \inf_Q U(f, Q)\), over all partitions. By Lemma 1.2, \(I_* \le I^*\). The function \(f\) is integrable on \([a, b]\) when \(I_* = I^*\), and the common value is the definite integral, written \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\). Thus \(L(f, P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f, P)\) for every partition \(P\). The convention \(\int_b^a f = -\int_a^b f\) is used when the limits are reversed, and \(\int_a^a f = 0\).

Remark 1.4 (the definition is the Darboux form)

Definition 1.3 uses upper and lower sums only. The Riemann sums of Section 1 are the other common form, and Corollary 2.3 shows that for monotone \(f\) the two forms agree in the limit of small mesh. This lesson does not use any other class of functions.

2Monotone functions are integrable

A function \(f\) is nondecreasing on \([a, b]\) when \(s \le t\) implies \(f(s) \le f(t)\), and nonincreasing when \(s \le t\) implies \(f(s) \ge f(t)\). A monotone function is one of these two. A monotone function on \([a, b]\) is bounded, because \(\min(f(a), f(b)) \le f(x) \le \max(f(a), f(b))\) for every \(x\).

Theorem 2.2 (monotone integrands)

Let \(f\) be monotone on \([a, b]\). Then \(f\) is integrable on \([a, b]\), and for every partition \(P\) with mesh \(\delta\), \[ 0 \le U(f, P) - L(f, P) \le \delta\,|f(b) - f(a)|. \]

Proof

Suppose \(f\) is nondecreasing. On \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\) the supremum is at the right endpoint and the infimum at the left, so \(M_i = f(x_i)\) and \(m_i = f(x_{i-1})\). Therefore \[ U(f,P) - L(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\bigr)\Delta x_i \le \delta \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\bigr) = \delta\bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr), \] since each increment \(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\) is nonnegative and the increments telescope. If \(f\) is nonincreasing, the roles of the endpoints swap, and the same computation gives the bound \(\delta(f(a) - f(b)) = \delta|f(b) - f(a)|\). For integrability, let \(\varepsilon > 0\). Take the uniform partition into \(n\) equal parts, with \(n > (b - a)|f(b) - f(a)|/\varepsilon\). Its mesh is \((b-a)/n\), so \(0 \le I^* - I_* \le U(f, P) - L(f, P) \le \varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, \(I^* = I_*\).

Corollary 2.3 (Riemann sums converge at the mesh rate)

Let \(f\) be monotone on \([a, b]\), let \(P\) be a partition with mesh \(\delta\), and let \(R\) be any Riemann sum of \(f\) for \(P\). Then \(\bigl|R - \int_a^b f\bigr| \le \delta\,|f(b) - f(a)|\).

Proof

We have \(L(f, P) \le R \le U(f, P)\) because the tags satisfy \(m_i \le f(t_i) \le M_i\). We also have \(L(f, P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f, P)\) by Definition 1.3. Two numbers that both lie between \(L(f,P)\) and \(U(f,P)\) differ by at most \(U(f,P) - L(f,P)\), which Theorem 2.2 bounds by \(\delta|f(b) - f(a)|\).

3Example: the integral of \(x^2\)

Lemma 3.1 (sum of squares)

For every integer \(n \ge 1\), \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} i^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6\).

Proof

For \(n = 1\) both sides equal 1. If the formula holds for \(n\), then \(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} i^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 + (n+1)^2 = (n+1)\bigl(n(2n+1) + 6(n+1)\bigr)/6 = (n+1)(2n^2 + 7n + 6)/6 = (n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)/6\), which is the formula for \(n+1\).

Example 3.2 (\(x^2\) on \([0, 1]\))

Let \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0, 1]\), which is nondecreasing. For the uniform partition into \(n\) parts, \(U_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (i/n)^2 (1/n) = (n+1)(2n+1)/(6n^2)\) and \(L_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n}((i-1)/n)^2 (1/n) = (n-1)(2n-1)/(6n^2)\), by Lemma 3.1 applied with \(n - 1\) in place of \(n\), since \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} (i-1)^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} j^2\). Then \(U_n - L_n = 6n/(6n^2) = 1/n\), and \(U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2)\), \(1/3 - L_n = (3n-1)/(6n^2)\). Both differences tend to 0 by the limit laws of Lesson 2. Since \(L_n \le \int_0^1 x^2\,dx \le U_n\), the integral is \(1/3\).

Lab 1 · Upper and lower sums of \(x^2\), a nonuniform partition, and Riemann sums

Stated before running: for \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0, 1]\) and the uniform partitions with \(n = 1\) to \(128\) subintervals, \(U_n - L_n = 1/n\) exactly, and the integral \(1/3\) lies between \(L_n\) and \(U_n\) (part A). For a nonuniform partition with points \((i/10)^2\), \(U - L\) is at most the mesh times \(f(1) - f(0)\) (part B). For the uniform partitions with \(n = 8, 16, 32\), midpoint and right-endpoint Riemann sums lie within \(1/n\) of \(1/3\) (part C).

"""M2 L09 lab 1: upper and lower Darboux sums for x^2 on [0, 1], and the bound for monotone integrands.

Part A takes f(x) = x^2 on [0, 1], which is nondecreasing, and the uniform partitions with n = 1, 2, 4, ..., 128
subintervals. For a nondecreasing f the supremum on each subinterval is the value at its right endpoint and the
infimum is the value at its left endpoint, so U_n = sum of f(i/n)/n and L_n = sum of f((i-1)/n)/n. The program
checks in exact fractions that U_n - L_n = 1/n, that L_n <= 1/3 <= U_n, and that U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2) and
L_n - 1/3 = -(3n-1)/(6n^2). Part B takes a nonuniform partition with points (i/n)^2 and checks that U - L is at
most the mesh times f(1) - f(0). Part C takes the same uniform partitions with midpoint tags and right-endpoint tags
and checks |R - 1/3| <= 1/n for each Riemann sum R. Exact fractions throughout; standard library only; no
randomness and no network access.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction

N_VALUES = (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128)
TARGET = Fraction(1, 3)


def f(x: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """f(x) = x^2, nondecreasing on [0, 1]."""
    return x * x


def upper_uniform(n: int) -> Fraction:
    """U_n for f on [0, 1] with n equal subintervals; the supremum on [(i-1)/n, i/n] is f(i/n)."""
    return sum((f(Fraction(i, n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))


def lower_uniform(n: int) -> Fraction:
    """L_n for f on [0, 1] with n equal subintervals; the infimum on [(i-1)/n, i/n] is f((i-1)/n)."""
    return sum((f(Fraction(i - 1, n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))


def part_a() -> None:
    print("part A: f(x) = x^2 on [0, 1], uniform partitions into n subintervals")
    print("part A:    n         L_n          U_n        U_n - L_n")
    for n in N_VALUES:
        lo, up = lower_uniform(n), upper_uniform(n)
        if up - lo != Fraction(1, n):
            raise AssertionError(f"U - L is not 1/n at n = {n}")
        if not lo <= TARGET <= up:
            raise AssertionError(f"the integral 1/3 is not between L and U at n = {n}")
        if up - TARGET != Fraction(3 * n + 1, 6 * n * n):
            raise AssertionError(f"U - 1/3 formula fails at n = {n}")
        if lo - TARGET != -Fraction(3 * n - 1, 6 * n * n):
            raise AssertionError(f"L - 1/3 formula fails at n = {n}")
        print(f"part A: {n:4d}  {float(lo):12.8f}  {float(up):12.8f}  {float(up - lo):12.8f}")
    print("part A: U - L = 1/n exactly, and L <= 1/3 <= U, at every n in the table")


def part_b() -> None:
    n = 10
    points = [Fraction(i * i, n * n) for i in range(n + 1)]
    points_are_x_squared = [Fraction(i, n) ** 2 for i in range(n + 1)]
    if points != points_are_x_squared:
        raise AssertionError("partition points are not (i/n)^2")
    widths = [points[i] - points[i - 1] for i in range(1, n + 1)]
    mesh = max(widths)
    upper = sum((f(points[i]) * widths[i - 1] for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
    lower = sum((f(points[i - 1]) * widths[i - 1] for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
    gap = upper - lower
    bound = mesh * (f(Fraction(1)) - f(Fraction(0)))
    if gap > bound:
        raise AssertionError("U - L exceeds the mesh bound for a monotone f")
    print(f"part B: nonuniform partition of [0, 1] with n = {n} subintervals and points (i/n)^2")
    print(f"part B: mesh = {float(mesh):.8f}, U - L = {float(gap):.8f}, bound mesh*(f(1)-f(0)) = {float(bound):.8f}")
    print("part B: U - L is at most the mesh bound, as the monotone argument predicts")


def part_c() -> None:
    print("part C: Riemann sums of x^2 on [0, 1], error against 1/3 and the bound 1/n")
    for n in (8, 16, 32):
        right = sum((f(Fraction(i, n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
        midpoint = sum((f(Fraction(2 * i - 1, 2 * n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
        for label, value in (("right", right), ("midpoint", midpoint)):
            error = value - TARGET
            if abs(error) > Fraction(1, n):
                raise AssertionError(f"{label} Riemann sum error exceeds 1/n at n = {n}")
            print(
                f"part C: n = {n:3d} {label:>8s}  error = {float(error):+.8f}  bound 1/n = {float(Fraction(1, n)):.8f}"
            )
    print("part C: every Riemann sum in the table lies within 1/n of 1/3")


def main() -> None:
    part_a()
    part_b()
    part_c()

The program uses exact fractions for every sum, so the identities in part A are exact. The printed decimals display those fractions. The lab shows that the sums bracket \(1/3\) and that the bracket narrows at the rate \(1/n\), and it shows the mesh bound of Theorem 2.2 for one nonuniform partition. It does not show that the integral exists for functions other than monotone ones, and the midpoint sums in part C are a check on this one function, not a theorem.

4Where the canon breaks

  • Upper and lower sums bracket the integral. For \(x^2\) on \([0, 1]\) they do not equal it at any finite \(n\) (Example 3.2). At \(n = 128\), \(U_{128} - 1/3 = (3 \cdot 128 + 1)/(6 \cdot 128^2) = 385/98304\), and Lab 1, part A asserts this formula and prints \(U_{128} = 0.33724976\).
  • The error bound uses the mesh, not the count. Corollary 2.3 bounds the error by \(\delta|f(b) - f(a)|\). A partition with few points can have a large mesh, and a partition with many points can have a small one. Lab 1, part B has 10 subintervals (11 points) and mesh \(0.19\), which is larger than \(1/10\).
  • Monotonicity is a sufficient condition. Theorem 2.2 proves integrability for monotone functions, and this lesson proves nothing for the others. A function that is neither nondecreasing nor nonincreasing is not covered here; Lesson 11 handles the sums and differences of monotone functions by the linearity lemma there.

5Misconceptions

  1. “The upper sum with \(n\) rectangles is the integral.”

    For \(x^2\), \(U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2) > 0\) for every \(n\) (Example 3.2). At \(n = 128\), Lab 1 part A prints \(U_{128} = 0.33724976\), not \(0.33333333\). The upper sum is an upper bound, and it converges to the integral as \(n\) grows.

  2. “The left and right endpoint sums always bracket the integral.”

    For \(f(x) = x(1-x)\) on \([0, 1]\) with \(n = 2\), the left-endpoint sum is \((0 + 1/4)/2 = 1/8\) and the right-endpoint sum is \((1/4 + 0)/2 = 1/8\). Both lie below the integral. The integral is \(1/6\): \(x - x^2\) is a difference of two monotone functions, each integrable by Lesson 9, Theorem 2.2, so \(x - x^2\) is integrable by Lesson 11, Lemma 1.1, and Lesson 10, Theorem 2.1, with \(F(x) = x^2/2 - x^3/3\) (whose derivative is \(x - x^2\)) gives \(F(1) - F(0) = 1/2 - 1/3 = 1/6\). Two sums on the same side do not bracket the integral. The bracket in Lemma 1.2 uses the suprema and infima, not the endpoint values.

  3. “The error of a Riemann sum depends only on the number of subintervals.”

    Corollary 2.3 bounds the error by the mesh, so with a nonuniform partition the count does not control the error. Lab 1, part B uses 10 subintervals whose widest one has width \(0.19\), so the bound is \(0.19\), and it is larger than the \(0.1\) that a uniform partition with 10 subintervals would give.

Mechanics

M2-L09 · Upper and lower sums of \(x\) on \([0, 2]\)

For \(f(x) = x\) on \([0, 2]\) and the uniform partition with \(n = 4\), compute \(U\), \(L\), and \(U - L\).

Solution

The width is \(\Delta x = 1/2\), and \(f\) is nondecreasing, so the upper sum uses the right endpoints \(1/2, 1, 3/2, 2\) and the lower sum uses \(0, 1/2, 1, 3/2\). So \(U = (1/2 + 1 + 3/2 + 2)(1/2) = 5/2\) and \(L = (0 + 1/2 + 1 + 3/2)(1/2) = 3/2\). The difference is \(U - L = 1 = (f(2) - f(0))\,\Delta x = 2 \cdot 1/2\), as Theorem 2.2 gives with equality.

M2-L09 · Two-interval sums for \(x^3\)

For \(f(x) = x^3\) on \([0, 1]\) and \(n = 2\), compute \(U\), \(L\), and \(U - L\).

Solution

The subinterval width is \(1/2\), and \(f\) is nondecreasing. The upper sum is \((f(1/2) + f(1))/2 = (1/8 + 1)/2 = 9/16\), and the lower sum is \((f(0) + f(1/2))/2 = (0 + 1/8)/2 = 1/16\). The difference is \(1/2 = (f(1) - f(0))/2\), which matches the bound of Theorem 2.2 with equality.

M2-L09 · A decreasing integrand

For \(f(x) = 1/(1+x)\) on \([0, 1]\) and \(n = 2\), compute \(U\) and \(L\), and find the interval in which the integral lies.

Solution

The function is nonincreasing, so \(M_i\) is the value at the left endpoint and \(m_i\) at the right one. The values at \(0, 1/2, 1\) are \(1, 2/3, 1/2\). So \(U = (1 + 2/3)/2 = 5/6\) and \(L = (2/3 + 1/2)/2 = 7/12\). The integral lies in \([7/12, 5/6]\), and \(U - L = 1/4 = (f(0) - f(1))/2\), as Theorem 2.2 gives.

Proof

M2-L09 · The refinement lemma for one point

Prove that adding one point \(t\) to a partition does not decrease the lower sum and does not increase the upper sum, for any bounded \(f\).

Solution

Let \(t\) lie strictly inside \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\). The pieces \([x_{i-1}, t]\) and \([t, x_i]\) are subsets of \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), so their infima \(m'\) and \(m''\) are at least \(m_i\), and their suprema are at most \(M_i\). Then \(m'(t - x_{i-1}) + m''(x_i - t) \ge m_i(x_i - x_{i-1})\), so the lower sum does not decrease. The same argument with suprema shows the upper sum does not increase. Other subintervals are unchanged.

M2-L09 · The integrability argument

Let \(f\) be nondecreasing on \([a,b]\). Show that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a partition \(P\) with \(U(f,P) - L(f,P) \le \varepsilon\), and deduce that \(I_* = I^*\).

Solution

Choose an integer \(n > (b-a)(f(b) - f(a))/\varepsilon\) and the uniform partition \(P\) into \(n\) parts, with mesh \((b-a)/n\). Theorem 2.2 gives \(U(f,P) - L(f,P) \le (b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n < \varepsilon\). Since \(L(f,P) \le I_*\) and \(I^* \le U(f,P)\), we get \(0 \le I^* - I_* \le \varepsilon\). As \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, \(I^* = I_*\).

M2-L09 · The sum of squares by induction

Prove Lemma 3.1 without citing it, by induction on \(n\), and check the algebra in the step.

Solution

For \(n = 1\), the left side is 1 and the right side is \(1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3/6 = 1\). Assume the formula for \(n\). Adding \((n+1)^2\) gives \(\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1) + 6(n+1)^2}{6} = \frac{(n+1)\bigl(2n^2 + n + 6n + 6\bigr)}{6} = \frac{(n+1)(2n^2 + 7n + 6)}{6}\). Since \(2n^2 + 7n + 6 = (n+2)(2n+3)\), this is \((n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)/6\), the formula for \(n+1\).

Empirical

M2-L09 · The right-endpoint error at \(n = 8\)

Lab 1, part C prints a right-endpoint error of \(+0.06510417\) at \(n = 8\). Recompute it from Example 3.2.

Solution

The right-endpoint sum for the uniform partition is the upper sum \(U_8\), and Example 3.2 gives \(U_8 - 1/3 = (3 \cdot 8 + 1)/(6 \cdot 64) = 25/384\). Computing \(25/384\) from the exact fraction gives \(0.06510417\) to eight decimals, which matches the printed value.

M2-L09 · The midpoint error at \(n = 16\)

Lab 1, part C prints a midpoint error of \(-0.00032552\) at \(n = 16\). Compare it with \(-1/(12 \cdot 16^2)\) and state what this comparison does and does not establish.

Solution

\(1/(12 \cdot 256) = 1/3072 = 0.00032552\) to eight decimals, so the printed value equals \(-1/(12n^2)\) at \(n = 16\) to the digits shown. This lesson does not prove that formula for the midpoint sums, so the match is an observation about one function and one \(n\). It shows that the midpoint error is negative and of order \(1/n^2\) in this sample, and no more.

M2-L09 · How many parts for a tolerance

For \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0,1]\), how many uniform parts are needed so that the bound of Corollary 2.3 guarantees a Riemann sum within 0.01 of the integral?

Solution

For a uniform partition with \(n\) parts, the mesh is \(1/n\) and \(|f(1) - f(0)| = 1\). Corollary 2.3 gives the bound \(1/n\), which is at most 0.01 when \(n \ge 100\). So 100 uniform parts suffice. The bound is sufficient, not necessary. Every Riemann sum \(R\) of the uniform partition lies in \([L_n, U_n]\), and so does \(1/3\) (Section 1 and Example 3.2). Hence \(|R - 1/3| \le \max(U_n - 1/3, 1/3 - L_n) = (3n+1)/(6n^2)\), which is below 0.01 for \(n = 51\) (at \(n = 50\) it is \(151/15000 = 0.010067\), and at \(n = 51\) it is \(154/15606 = 0.009868\)). So 51 uniform parts keep every Riemann sum within 0.01, while the bound of Corollary 2.3 needs 100.

M2-L09 · A two-sided bound from a trivial partition

Let \(f\) be nondecreasing on \([a,b]\). Prove that \((b-a)f(a) \le \int_a^b f \le (b-a)f(b)\).

Solution

Take the trivial partition \(P = \{a, b\}\), with one subinterval. Its lower sum is \(L(f,P) = f(a)(b-a)\), because the infimum of a nondecreasing function on \([a,b]\) is \(f(a)\), and its upper sum is \(U(f,P) = f(b)(b-a)\). By Definition 1.3, \(L(f,P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f,P)\), which is the claim.

M2-L09 · Every Riemann sum converges to the integral

For \(f\) monotone on \([a,b]\), prove that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that every Riemann sum with mesh less than \(\delta\) lies within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\int_a^b f\).

Solution

Let \(\delta = \varepsilon/(|f(b) - f(a)| + 1)\). If a partition \(P\) has mesh below \(\delta\), then Corollary 2.3 gives \(|R - \int_a^b f| \le \delta|f(b) - f(a)| < \varepsilon\) for every Riemann sum \(R\) of \(P\), since \(\delta|f(b)-f(a)| \le \varepsilon|f(b)-f(a)|/(|f(b)-f(a)|+1) < \varepsilon\).

M2-L09 · The upper-sum error of the uniform partition

For \(f\) nondecreasing on \([a,b]\) and the uniform partition into \(n\) parts, show that \(0 \le U_n - \int_a^b f \le (b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n\).

Solution

The inequality \(\int_a^b f \le U_n\) is Definition 1.3, and \(\int_a^b f \ge L_n\) gives \(U_n - \int_a^b f \le U_n - L_n\). Theorem 2.2 bounds \(U_n - L_n\) by \((b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n\) because the mesh is \((b-a)/n\). Both bounds together give the claim.
