Single & Multivariable Calculus

Sensitivities are rates of change. A price moves with a yield, a payoff with an underlying, and a risk with a parameter, and the sensitivity of each to its input is a derivative. This module develops the calculus behind those derivatives from the definition of a limit. It covers limits of sequences and functions, the derivative and its rules, the mean value theorem, Taylor's theorem with remainder, the Riemann integral and the fundamental theorem, and then functions of several variables: partial derivatives, gradients, Jacobians, Hessians, multiple integrals, the implicit-function theorem and the envelope theorem. You leave able to prove a limit or a derivative from its definition, to bound a Taylor remainder, to state the hypotheses of the multivariable theorems, and to implement and test a sensitivity engine in Python.

  • Prerequisites: M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning, and P0.1 How to Study This Program. Secondary-school algebra is assumed.
  • Project: Sensitivity engine, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. The labs and the grader compute from formulas. A generated bond and a generated test function are labelled SIMULATED where they appear. The placement bar and the hours are design choices, not results.

240 h lessons · 60 h project (20%)

Objectives

By the end of M2 you can:

  1. Prove that a sequence or a function has a given limit from the \(\varepsilon\)-\(N\) or \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) definition, and apply the limit laws to sums, products and quotients, stating the hypothesis each law needs (Lessons 1 to 3).
  2. Compute a derivative from its definition as a limit, and apply the product, quotient and chain rules, each with its hypotheses (Lessons 4 and 5).
  3. Relate the monotonicity and extrema of a function to the sign and zeros of its derivative, using the mean value theorem (Lesson 6).
  4. Derive a Taylor polynomial and bound its remainder, and evaluate an integral by Riemann sums, the fundamental theorem, substitution and parts (Lessons 7 to 12).
  5. Compare analytic, finite-difference and automatic derivatives, and state where finite differences break (Lessons 13, 14 and 24).
  6. Compute partial derivatives, gradients, Jacobians and Hessians, and check the symmetry of a Hessian (Lessons 15 to 19).
  7. Apply the implicit-function and envelope theorems to a sensitivity, and implement and test a sensitivity engine (Lessons 20 to 23 and the project).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on M1 and P0.1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. For eight items that means at least seven correct: six of eight is 75 percent, and seven of eight is 87.5 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar on the P0.1 items is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. What is the negation of "for every \(x\) there is a \(y\) with \(R(x, y)\)"?

    Options

    M1, Lesson 1, Theorem 3.2 (a): the negation of \(\forall x\, \exists y\, R\) is \(\exists x\, \forall y\, \neg R\).

  2. Which technique proves that the square root of 2 is not rational?

    Options

    M1, Lesson 2: assume the root is a lowest-terms fraction and derive a contradiction.

  3. What is the sum \(1 + q + q^2 + \dots + q^{n-1}\) for \(q \ne 1\)?

    Options

    The finite geometric sum, M1, Lesson 6, proved by induction.

  4. Which function is the inverse of the exponential?

    Options

    M1, Lesson 3: the natural logarithm inverts the exponential.

  5. What grade does a theorem proved on the page carry in this program?

    Options

    Grade T is the theorem grade (P0.1, evidence grade).

  6. What may a page cite in this program?

    Options

    The citation registry holds the verified sources (P0.1, citation registry).

  7. Skill check (rung 1): for \(f(x) = x^2\), the difference quotient at \(a = 1\) is \((f(1 + h) - f(1))/h\). What is its limit as \(h \to 0\)?

    The quotient is \((2h + h^2)/h = 2 + h\), which tends to 2. This is the worked-example task of rung 1, taught in Lesson 4.

  8. Skill check (rung 2): for \(f(x) = x^3\) at \(x = 1\), the central difference \((f(1 + h) - f(1 - h))/(2h)\) equals \(3 + h^2\). What is its value at \(h = 0.1\)?

    \(3 + 0.1^2 = 3.01\). The missing step of the rung 2 completion problem is the expansion of \((1 + h)^3 - (1 - h)^3\), taught in Lesson 5.

Lessons

Twenty-four lessons of ten study hours each, which is 240 hours in total, and the hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Lessons 1 to 6 cover limits, continuity, the derivative and the mean value theorem. Lessons 7 to 12 cover Taylor's theorem, the Riemann integral and integration techniques. Lessons 13 and 14 cover numerical and automatic differentiation. Lessons 15 to 19 cover functions of several variables, and Lessons 20 to 22 cover multiple integrals and the implicit-function and envelope theorems. Lessons 23 and 24 apply the unit to a bond-price sensitivity and to the limits of numerical sensitivities. Lesson pages contain graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 10 hSequences and the Limit of a SequenceThe epsilon-N definition of a limit, uniqueness of the limit, the tail of a sequence, and divergence. L02 · 10 hContinuity and the Limit LawsProofs of the limit laws for sums, products and quotients, and continuity defined through sequences. L03 · 10 hLimits of Functions and One-Sided LimitsThe epsilon-delta limit of a function, its link to sequences, and a piecewise function with no two-sided limit at its break. L04 · 10 hThe Derivative as a LimitThe difference quotient, the derivative as its limit, differentiability implies continuity, and a kink with no derivative. L05 · 10 hDifferentiation Rules and the Chain RuleThe product, quotient and chain rules, each proved from the definition with its hypotheses. L06 · 10 hMean Value Theorem, Monotonicity and ExtremaRolle's theorem and the mean value theorem under Assumption 2.1 (the extreme value theorem, taken as an assumption), monotonicity from the sign of the derivative, and local extrema. L07 · 10 hHigher Derivatives and Taylor PolynomialsHigher derivatives, the Taylor polynomial of a function at a point, and its agreement with the function to a stated order. L08 · 10 hTaylor's Theorem with RemainderThe Lagrange remainder, its bound, and a numerical check of the bound at sampled points. L09 · 10 hRiemann Sums and the Definite IntegralUpper and lower Riemann sums, the definite integral as their common limit, and a convergence check. L10 · 10 hThe Fundamental Theorem of CalculusThe integral of a derivative, the derivative of an integral, and the recovery of an increment. L11 · 10 hIntegration Techniques: Substitution and PartsSubstitution and integration by parts, derived from the chain and product rules, and checked by quadrature. L12 · 10 hImproper IntegralsIntegrals over unbounded intervals and of unbounded functions, defined as limits, and their convergence rates. L13 · 10 hFinite-Difference Derivative ApproximationForward and central differences, their truncation errors, and the turn-up in error at small steps caused by rounding in arithmetic. L14 · 10 hAutomatic Differentiation: Forward and Reverse ModeDual numbers, forward-mode differentiation, and the reverse-mode idea, checked against the analytic derivative. L15 · 10 hEuclidean Space, Open Sets and Functions of Several VariablesPoints and distances in the plane and in higher dimensions, open sets, and continuity of functions of two variables. L16 · 10 hPartial DerivativesEach partial derivative as a one-variable derivative with the other variables held fixed, computed and checked. L17 · 10 hThe Gradient and the Directional DerivativeThe gradient, the directional derivative as its dot product with a unit vector, and the direction of steepest ascent. L18 · 10 hDifferentiability and the Jacobian MatrixDifferentiability of a map from the plane to the plane, and its Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives. L19 · 10 hSecond-Order Taylor Expansion and the Hessian MatrixThe Hessian of second partial derivatives, its symmetry, and the second-order expansion of a function of several variables. L20 · 10 hMultiple Integrals and Iterated IntegralsDouble integrals over a rectangle, iterated integrals, and an iterated Riemann sum checked numerically. L21 · 10 hThe Implicit-Function TheoremWhen an equation defines one variable as a differentiable function of another, and the implicit derivative. L22 · 10 hConstrained Optimization and the Envelope TheoremThe envelope theorem for an optimum that depends on a parameter, stated without multipliers, and checked numerically. L23 · 10 hSensitivities of a Bond Price: A Taylor PreviewThe Taylor expansion of a simulated bond price in its yield, with duration and convexity named as a preview of a later module. The project ladder

The module ends in the sensitivity engine. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated sensitivity of a simulated bond price in its yield, computed analytically and by central difference, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same task with the second-difference and automatic-differentiation lines removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement sensitivity_engine.py with analytic, finite-difference and automatic-differentiation sensitivities, the Hessian by finite differences, and the integer-cent bond price, and write the error-analysis note. Graded by the autograder against generated instances and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a sensitivity of a multivariable function with a stated hypothesis about where its finite differences break, suitable for your portfolio.
