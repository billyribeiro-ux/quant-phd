Regulatory-Impact Analysis: Dealer Capacity & Leverage Reform

A senior policy analyst at a financial regulatory agency must quantify the market footprint of banking leverage rules. You conduct a formal regulatory-impact analysis (RIA) evaluating how the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) affects primary dealer intermediation capacity in the U.S. Treasury market. You author a four-section policy memorandum—Problem Definition & Institutional Baseline, Quantitative Intermediation Model, Cost-Benefit Welfare Analysis, and Policy Recommendation & Implementation Plan—calibrated against an 8-regime simulation model and scored by rubric against two annotated exemplars.

Conventions and definitions

The labs, the simulation case, and the rubric use the following regulatory and microeconomic definitions:

Total Leverage Exposure (TLE)
The unweighted denominator of the Supplementary Leverage Ratio, comprising all on-balance-sheet assets without risk weights, counterparty credit risk on repo transactions, potential future exposure of OTC derivatives, and off-balance-sheet commitments. Measured in billions of dollars (\$B).
Dealer Treasury Intermediation Capacity
The maximum dollar volume of U.S. Treasury securities and sovereign repo a primary dealer can hold or finance given its Tier 1 capital \(E\) and non-Treasury assets \(A_{\mathrm{other}}\): \(C_T = \max\left(0, \frac{E}{\mathrm{SLR}_{\min}} - A_{\mathrm{other}}\right)\), in billions of dollars (\$B).
Leverage Hurdle Rate
The incremental net equity financing spread required per dollar of asset holding when the SLR binds: \(\text{Hurdle} = \mathrm{SLR}_{\min} \times r_E \times 10{,}000\) in basis points (bps), where \(r_E = 12.0\%\) is the annual cost of Tier 1 equity capital.
Secondary Market Liquidity Benefit
Annual transaction cost savings resulting from tighter secondary bid-ask spreads across annual trading volume \(V_{\mathrm{secondary}} = \$150\) trillion: \(\text{Benefit} = V_{\mathrm{secondary}} \times \frac{\max(0, 0.80 - \text{Spread})}{10{,}000}\), in billions of dollars per year (\$B/year).
Sovereign Debt Financing Savings
Annual interest expense reduction on annual gross marketable debt issuance \(D_{\mathrm{issuance}} = \$4\) trillion resulting from lower primary yield concessions: \(\text{Savings} = D_{\mathrm{issuance}} \times \frac{\max(0, 4.00 - \text{Concession})}{10{,}000}\), in billions of dollars per year (\$B/year).
Expected Systemic Risk Cost
The annualized expected societal deadweight loss of G-SIB distress: \(\text{Cost} = p_d \times L_{\mathrm{bailout}}\), where \(L_{\mathrm{bailout}} = \$500\) billion and \(p_d\) is the model-calibrated annual default probability, in billions of dollars per year (\$B/year).
Net Social Welfare
The overall annual net benefit of a regulatory regime: \(\text{Net Welfare} = \text{Liquidity Benefit} + \text{Sovereign Savings} - \text{Expected Risk Cost}\), in billions of dollars per year (\$B/year).
Capacity Elasticity
The arc elasticity of dealer Treasury capacity with respect to the regulatory SLR requirement: \(\epsilon = \frac{\Delta C / \bar{C}}{\Delta \mathrm{SLR} / \overline{\mathrm{SLR}}}\), dimensionless.

The simulation case

This case represents an industry-wide primary dealer sector under post-crisis regulatory capital standards. All parameters are SIMULATED for teaching and policy evaluation.

Institutional Sector Parameters
Aggregate primary dealer Tier 1 capital \(E = \$60.0\) billion; baseline non-Treasury loan and credit assets \(A_{\mathrm{other}} = \$750.0\) billion; annual cost of equity capital \(r_E = 12.0\%\); annual secondary Treasury volume \(V_{\mathrm{secondary}} = \$150{,}000\) billion; annual sovereign gross debt issuance \(D_{\mathrm{issuance}} = \$4{,}000\) billion; systemic crisis bailout loss \(L_{\mathrm{bailout}} = \$500\) billion.
Eight Policy Regimes
  1. Regime 1: Baseline G-SIB Holding Company (5.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.10\%\)).
  2. Regime 2: Insured Depository Institution Subsidiary (6.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.08\%\)).
  3. Regime 3: Recalibrated Buffer Proposal (4.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.12\%\)).
  4. Regime 4: Basel III International Minimum (3.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.16\%\)).
  5. Regime 5: 2020 Emergency Temporary Exemption (5.0% SLR, safe assets exempt, \(p_d = 0.13\%\)).
  6. Regime 6: Permanent Safe-Asset Exemption with Surcharge (5.5% SLR, safe assets exempt, \(p_d = 0.11\%\)).
  7. Regime 7: Stress Liquidity Shock (\$250B inbound selling, 5.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.18\%\)).
  8. Regime 8: Severe Crisis Shock (\$400B inbound selling, 5.0% SLR, non-exempt, \(p_d = 0.28\%\)).

"""Lab SR4-project-1: Project Rung 1 Worked Example.

SIMULATED. Quantitative dealer balance-sheet intermediation cost under the
Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR). Computes maximum total leverage exposure,
portfolio allocation, hurdle rate on zero-risk Treasuries, and break-even
market-making bid-ask spreads.
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def calculate_dealer_intermediation(
    equity: float,
    slr_ratio: float,
    non_treasury_assets: float,
    equity_cost_pct: float,
    annual_turnover: float,
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Calculate dealer Treasury capacity and market-making bid-ask spread requirements."""
    if equity <= 0 or slr_ratio <= 0:
        raise ValueError("equity and SLR ratio must be positive")
    if non_treasury_assets < 0:
        raise ValueError("non-treasury assets cannot be negative")

    # 1. Total Leverage Exposure (TLE) Capacity
    total_capacity = equity / slr_ratio

    # 2. Maximum Treasury Intermediation Capacity
    treasury_capacity = max(0.0, total_capacity - non_treasury_assets)

    # 3. Hurdle rate on balance-sheet expansion:
    # Each $1 of Treasuries requires $slr_ratio of equity.
    # At cost of equity r_e, the required return per dollar of assets is:
    hurdle_rate_bps = slr_ratio * equity_cost_pct * 10000.0  # in basis points (1 bp = 0.0001)

    # 4. Break-even bid-ask spread in market making:
    # If inventory turns over 'annual_turnover' times per year,
    # the required half-spread per turn is hurdle_rate / annual_turnover,
    # so the full round-trip bid-ask spread is 2 * (hurdle_rate / annual_turnover).
    breakeven_spread_bps = (2.0 * hurdle_rate_bps) / annual_turnover if annual_turnover > 0 else 0.0

    return {
        "equity": equity,
        "total_capacity": total_capacity,
        "treasury_capacity": treasury_capacity,
        "hurdle_rate_bps": hurdle_rate_bps,
        "breakeven_spread_bps": breakeven_spread_bps,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # Representative primary dealer instance (SIMULATED)
    equity = 50.0  # $50 billion Tier 1 capital
    slr_ratio = 0.05  # 5.0% enhanced SLR requirement
    non_treasury = 650.0  # $650 billion existing credit and mortgage loans
    equity_cost = 0.12  # 12.0% annual required return on equity
    turnover = 24.0  # 24x annual inventory turnover (holding period ~15 days)

    print("=== PROJECT RUNG 1: WORKED EXAMPLE (SIMULATED) ===")
    print(f"Inputs: Equity = ${equity:.1f}B, SLR = {slr_ratio * 100:.1f}%, Non-Treasury Assets = ${non_treasury:.1f}B")

    res = calculate_dealer_intermediation(
        equity=equity,
        slr_ratio=slr_ratio,
        non_treasury_assets=non_treasury,
        equity_cost_pct=equity_cost,
        annual_turnover=turnover,
    )

    print(f"Step 1: Total Balance-Sheet Capacity (TLE): ${res['total_capacity']:.1f}B")
    print(f"Step 2: Available Treasury Intermediation Capacity: ${res['treasury_capacity']:.1f}B")
    print(f"Step 3: Equity Hurdle Rate on Zero-Risk Treasuries: {res['hurdle_rate_bps']:.2f} bps")
    print(f"Step 4: Break-Even Market-Making Bid-Ask Spread: {res['breakeven_spread_bps']:.3f} bps")


main()

def calculate_treasury_capacity(equity: float, slr_ratio: float, non_treasury_assets: float) -> float:
    """Calculate Treasury capacity given equity, SLR ratio, and non-Treasury assets."""
    if equity <= 0 or slr_ratio <= 0:
        raise ValueError("equity and SLR ratio must be positive")
    total_capacity = equity / slr_ratio
    return max(0.0, total_capacity - non_treasury_assets)


def calibrate_slr_bisection(

    hi = 0.15

"""Lab SR4-project-3: Project Rung 3 Case Generator and Calibration.

SIMULATED. Comprehensive 8-regime Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA) model
quantifying dealer Treasury intermediation capacity, leverage shadow costs,
secondary market bid-ask spreads, and net social welfare across alternative
post-crisis regulatory designs (Federal Reserve Board, 2020, 2025; BCBS, 2014).
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def evaluate_regime(
    regime_id: int,
    name: str,
    equity: float,
    slr_ratio: float,
    non_treasury_assets: float,
    is_exempt: bool,
    cost_of_equity: float,
    annual_volume: float,
    annual_issuance: float,
    default_prob_annual: float,
    bailout_loss: float,
) -> dict[str, float | str | int]:
    """Evaluate dealer balance-sheet capacity, hurdle rates, and welfare for a single regime."""
    if equity <= 0 or slr_ratio <= 0:
        raise ValueError("equity and slr_ratio must be positive")

    # Total balance sheet capacity
    if is_exempt:
        # If Treasuries are exempt from SLR, dealer can expand up to risk-weighted capacity (~$1,200B)
        total_capacity = equity / slr_ratio + 400.0
        treasury_capacity = 600.0
        hurdle_bps = 0.0  # Zero shadow cost on zero-risk assets
        spread_bps = 0.20
        issuance_bps = 1.00
    else:
        total_capacity = equity / slr_ratio
        treasury_capacity = max(50.0, total_capacity - non_treasury_assets)
        hurdle_bps = slr_ratio * cost_of_equity * 10000.0  # in basis points (e.g. 0.05 * 0.12 * 10000 = 60.0 bps)
        capacity_factor = 400.0 / treasury_capacity
        spread_bps = 0.20 * capacity_factor
        issuance_bps = 1.00 * capacity_factor

    # Welfare components ($ billions / year)
    # Baseline comparison spread: 0.80 bps; baseline issuance concession: 4.00 bps
    spread_savings = max(0.0, 0.80 - spread_bps)
    liquidity_benefit = annual_volume * (spread_savings / 10000.0)

    issuance_savings = max(0.0, 4.00 - issuance_bps)
    debt_benefit = annual_issuance * (issuance_savings / 10000.0)

    expected_bailout = default_prob_annual * bailout_loss
    net_welfare = liquidity_benefit + debt_benefit - expected_bailout

    return {
        "regime_id": regime_id,
        "name": name,
        "slr_pct": slr_ratio * 100.0,
        "total_capacity": total_capacity,
        "treasury_capacity": treasury_capacity,
        "hurdle_bps": hurdle_bps,
        "spread_bps": spread_bps,
        "net_welfare": net_welfare,
    }

Two exemplars

Study the following two policy memoranda written to this specification at different performance levels.

Exemplar A (Level 4 · Exemplary)

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 4. The memorandum articulates the statutory problem with exact citations, quotes all numbers from Lab 3 Parts A through E with units, decomposes social welfare rigorously, and provides an actionable administrative implementation roadmap.

Section 1: Problem Definition & Institutional Baseline

Under Section 165 of the Dodd-Frank Act and the Basel III framework implemented by the Federal Reserve Board , U.S. Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) face an unweighted enhanced Supplementary Leverage Ratio (eSLR) requirement of 5.0% at the holding company level and 6.0% at the insured depository institution subsidiary level. In the baseline status quo (Lab 3 Part A, Regime 1), with aggregate Tier 1 capital of \$60.0B and existing non-Treasury assets of \$750.0B, primary dealer balance-sheet capacity is capped at \$1200.0B, leaving only \$450.0B available for Treasury intermediation. Because the SLR treats sovereign debt identically to risky loans, holding zero-risk Treasuries consumes Tier 1 capital at the rate of 5%, imposing an equity hurdle rate of 60.0 bps (Part A, Regime 1). This constraint was the primary driver of market dysfunction during the March 2020 dash-for-cash .

  • Meets C1 level 4: articulates the statutory tension, defines eSLR rules, and establishes baseline metrics quoting Regime 1.
Section 2: Quantitative Intermediation Model

Lab 3 Part A evaluates dealer capacity across eight regulatory regimes. At the subsidiary bank level (Regime 2, 6.0% SLR), capacity contracts to \$250.0B, while the leverage hurdle peaks at 72.0 bps (Part C). Under a recalibrated 4.0% SLR (Regime 3), Treasury capacity expands to \$750.0B, lowering the hurdle rate to 48.0 bps. In extreme regimes, the Basel III 3.0% floor (Regime 4) maximizes capacity at \$1250.0B (Part B), while safe-asset exemption regimes (Regimes 5 and 6) provide \$600.0B in capacity with a 0.0 bps leverage hurdle (Part C). Between Regimes 1 and 3, dealer capacity exhibits an arc elasticity of -2.2500 with respect to the SLR ratio (Part E), confirming that dealer intermediation capacity is strictly elastic to leverage requirements.

  • Meets C2 level 4: quotes exact figures from Lab 3 Parts A, B, C, and E, explaining capacity variations, hurdle rates, and elasticity.
Section 3: Cost-Benefit Welfare Analysis

Social welfare decomposes into secondary liquidity benefits, sovereign debt service savings, and expected systemic bailout costs. In the baseline status quo (Regime 1), net social welfare is \$10.08B/year (Part A). Loosening the SLR to the 3.0% Basel floor (Regime 4) maximizes net welfare at \$11.71B/year (Part D), delivering a \$1.63B/year gain over baseline, driven by \$12.00B in liquidity savings and \$1.60B in sovereign debt savings, despite higher annual bailout costs of \$0.80B. The recalibrated buffer proposal (Regime 3) delivers \$11.19B/year, balancing an \$11.43B liquidity benefit against a modest \$0.60B bailout cost. Safe-asset exemption with a surcharge (Regime 6) delivers \$9.65B/year, outperforming the uncompensated 2020 exemption (\$9.55B, Regime 5) due to lower default probability.

  • Meets C3 level 4: fully decomposes welfare components, quotes exact Part D figures, and compares trade-offs across all key regimes.
Section 4: Policy Recommendation & Implementation Plan

We recommend adopting the Recalibrated Buffer Proposal (Regime 3, 4.0% SLR), consistent with the Federal Reserve's 2025 regulatory direction . While Regime 4 yields slightly higher static welfare, it increases bank default probability by 60% over baseline. Regime 3 expands dealer Treasury capacity by \$300.0B (+66.7%) while maintaining robust solvency discipline. Implementation should proceed via an interagency Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) with a 90-day public comment period, a 12-month phased transition, and coordinated FICC Treasury clearing mandates to capture repo netting benefits.

  • Meets C4 level 4: clear policy recommendation balancing solvency and liquidity, accompanied by an actionable administrative rulemaking roadmap.
  • Meets C5 level 4: every regulatory claim is cited to verified registry keys with locators; every number cites Lab 3 parts; units explicit.

Exemplar B (Level 2 · Developing)

Official scoring: C1 2 · C2 2 · C3 2 · C4 2 · C5 2. The memorandum discusses bank leverage and Treasury liquidity but fails to cite verified registry keys, omits locators, quotes rounded numbers without Lab 3 Part letters, and offers vague policy recommendations without implementation steps.

Problem Definition & Baseline

Banks face the SLR which requires 5% equity. In our model, with \$60B equity, banks can hold \$1200B total assets, but other assets take up \$750B, leaving around \$450B for Treasuries. This creates a hurdle rate of about 60 bps, which makes it hard for dealers to trade Treasuries during market stress.

  • Misses C1: discusses the problem in general terms but omits statutory citations (Dodd-Frank, Basel III) and verified registry keys.
Model Results & Capacity

Under a 6% SLR, Treasury capacity drops to \$250B with a 72 bps hurdle. If we lower SLR to 4%, capacity goes up to \$750B. If we lower it to 3%, capacity reaches \$1250B. The elasticity is negative 2.25, showing that capacity changes a lot when the leverage ratio changes.

  • Misses C2: reports capacity numbers without citing Lab 3 Parts A, B, C, or E; omits explanation of why the hurdle rate arises from equity costs.
Cost-Benefit Analysis

Baseline welfare is \$10.08B. Regime 4 gives the highest welfare of \$11.71B, but bank default risk rises. Regime 3 gives \$11.19B. Overall, lowering the leverage ratio improves liquidity and saves money on government debt.

  • Misses C3: quotes net welfare figures but does not show the decomposition across liquidity benefits, debt issuance savings, and systemic bailout costs.
Policy Recommendation

Regulators should lower the SLR to 4% or exempt Treasuries so dealers can buy more bonds during panics. This will help market liquidity and make the Treasury market safer.

  • Misses C4: vague policy recommendation without administrative rulemaking steps, comment periods, or transition plans.
  • Misses C5: zero registry citations; no locators; numbers lack Part letter citations.
