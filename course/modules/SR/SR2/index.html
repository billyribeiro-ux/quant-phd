Leverage Cycles

Standard general equilibrium models treat credit as a frictionless conduit where interest rates alone balance supply and demand. In actual asset markets, loans require collateral, haircuts fluctuate endogenously over the business cycle, and financial intermediary balance sheets actively amplify volatility. In this module, you will master the economics of procyclical leverage, solve the Geanakoplos collateral equilibrium across heterogeneous beliefs, trace the transmission of margin spirals and forced fire sales, formalize multi-period leverage cycles, and explore continuous-time macroeconomic models where intermediary equity capital dictates aggregate stability and asset prices.

  • Prerequisites: SR1 Runs, Liquidity & Fire Sales and AP6 Intermediary Asset Pricing
  • Project: Leverage-Cycle Model: Collateral Equilibrium & Margin Shock Solver (Graded by both autograder and rubric)
  • Data: Model parameters and simulations are SIMULATED.

48 h lessons · 12 h project (20% of the 60 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of SR2 you can:

  1. Formalize the mark-to-market accounting mechanics of security broker-dealers and derive the Value-at-Risk capital constraint that produces procyclical leverage (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the collateral equilibrium of Geanakoplos and derive the marginal buyer cutoff and equilibrium asset price as closed-form functions of collateral haircuts and belief distributions (Lesson 2).
  3. Model the coupled dynamics of loss and margin spirals in wholesale funding markets and prove the feedback multiplier divergence condition (Lesson 3).
  4. Trace the complete multi-period leverage cycle across tranquil expansion, bad-news volatility shocks, collateral margin spikes, and wealth redistribution (Lesson 4).
  5. Formulate continuous-time dynamic macroeconomic models with an intermediary sector and explain the Volatility Paradox and non-linear crisis amplification (Lesson 5).
  6. Implement an autograded quantitative solver for the Geanakoplos collateral equilibrium model and quantify asset price crashes induced by exogenous margin shocks (Project).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions testing prerequisite knowledge across SR1, AP6, and core macro-finance foundations, alongside two skill checks testing the Geanakoplos equilibrium solver routines taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (7 of 8 items correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must satisfy the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In SR1, what mechanism drives the margin spiral in Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2008)?

    Options

    The margin spiral arises when falling asset prices increase measured risk, prompting lenders to demand higher margin haircuts, which forces further asset sales (SR1, liquidity-spiral).

  2. In AP6, what is the core thesis of intermediary asset pricing (He and Krishnamurthy 2013)?

    Options

    Intermediary asset pricing establishes that institutional intermediaries are the marginal price setters across modern securities markets (AP6, intermediary-asset-pricing).

  3. According to Gorton and Metrick (2012) in SR1, what constituted the "run on repo" during the 2007–2008 financial crisis?

    Options

    Gorton and Metrick (2012) showed that shadow banking was destabilized by dramatic haircut increases in wholesale repo agreements (SR1, run-on-repo).

  4. Under mark-to-market accounting, what happens to intermediary equity when an asset on the balance sheet rises in market value by \(\Delta A\)?

    Options

    From the balance sheet identity \(A = D + E\), holding debt \(D\) fixed implies \(\Delta E = \Delta A\) immediately under mark-to-market accounting.

  5. In a no-arbitrage two-state world with payoffs \(V_U > V_D > 0\), what is the maximum non-default loan collateralized solely by the asset when gross riskless rate is \(R_f\)?

    Options

    To guarantee no default in all states of nature without personal recourse, the promised loan repayment cannot exceed the worst-case liquidation value \(V_D\), yielding \(L = V_D / R_f\).

  6. If a financial institution holds $100M of assets funded by $10M of equity and $90M of debt, what is its leverage ratio (Assets / Equity)?

    Leverage is defined as \(A / E = 100 / 10 = 10.0\).

  7. [Skill Check 1] In a Geanakoplos two-state economy with \(V_U = 1.20\), \(V_D = 0.20\), \(R_f = 1.00\), and full non-default borrowing, what is the marginal buyer cutoff \(h^* = 1 / (1 + V_U - V_D)\)?

    \(h^* = 1 / (1 + 1.20 - 0.20) = 1 / 2.0 = 0.50\).

  8. [Skill Check 2] In the same economy (\(V_U = 1.20\), \(V_D = 0.20\), \(R_f = 1.00\), \(h^* = 0.50\)), what is the equilibrium asset price \(P = V_D + (1 - h^*)\)?

    \(P = 0.20 + (1 - 0.50) = 0.70\).

Lesson Map

  1. Lesson 1 · Procyclical Leverage & Mark-to-Market Accounting

    10 study hours. Empirical balance-sheet management of commercial banks versus broker-dealers (Adrian & Shin 2010), Value-at-Risk capital constraints, and mark-to-market feedback loops. Introduces procyclical leverage.

  2. Lesson 2 · Geanakoplos Collateral Equilibrium Mechanics

    10 study hours. General equilibrium with collateral constraints (Geanakoplos 2010), contract spaces, heterogeneous beliefs, the marginal buyer theorem, and endogenous margin determination. Introduces collateral equilibrium.

  3. Lesson 3 · Margin Spirals & Fire Sales

    10 study hours. The interaction of funding liquidity and market liquidity (Brunnermeier & Pedersen 2008), fire sales under cash-in-the-market pricing (Shleifer & Vishny 2011), and haircut spikes in wholesale repo markets.

  4. Lesson 4 · The Leverage Cycle Over Macroeconomic Cycles

    9 study hours. Multi-period leverage cycles over macroeconomic expansions and busts (Geanakoplos 2010), volatility compression during tranquil regimes, margin shocks, and wealth redistribution. Introduces leverage cycle.

  5. Lesson 5 · Macroeconomic Models with Intermediary Balance Sheets

    9 study hours. Continuous-time dynamic macro-financial models (Brunnermeier & Sannikov 2014, He & Krishnamurthy 2013), expert wealth share as the aggregate state variable, and the Volatility Paradox. Introduces macro model with a financial sector.

Project Ladder

The project guides you through the four rungs of professional mastery:

  1. 1 · Worked example

    Step-by-step mathematical derivation and numerical execution of a two-state Geanakoplos collateral equilibrium solver with verification of marginal buyer indifference and market-clearing conditions.

  2. 2 · Completion problem

    Complete the core equilibrium pricing and margin-shock routines by filling in the missing analytical expressions, verified instantly in Python.

  3. 3 · Independent project

    Author a production-grade collateral equilibrium and multi-period leverage cycle solver (solver.py) evaluated across 50 seeded test instances, alongside an executive quantitative research memo (writeup.md).

  4. 4 · Stretch extension

    Extend the solver to continuous optimism distributions (beta/normal distributions) or endogenous default under multi-state payoff trees.
