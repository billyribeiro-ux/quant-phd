Runs, Liquidity & Fire Sales

Commercial and shadow banks engage in structural maturity transformation by funding long-term illiquid assets with short-term demandable debt. While this intermediation architecture provides optimal liquidity insurance to households and businesses, it exposes institutions to strategic coordination failure, self-fulfilling panic runs, and systemic balance sheet contagion. In this module, you will master the foundational Diamond–Dybvig model of liquidity insurance, prove the multiplicity of equilibria under sequential service, analyze uninsured deposit runs under asset mark-to-market shocks in regional banking, formalize cash-in-the-market pricing during fire sales, and simulate coupled loss and margin spirals in wholesale repo funding markets. The module culminates in building an empirical run dynamics solver calibrated to the 2023 regional banking panic.

  • Prerequisites: BK1 Banking & Asset-Liability Management and E4 General Equilibrium & Information Economics
  • Project: Run Dynamics Model: Solvency Thresholds and Buffer Calibration (Graded by both autograder and rubric)
  • Data: Bank balance sheet parameters and calibration targets are SIMULATED.

40 h lessons · 10 h project (20% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of SR1 you can:

  1. Formulate the Diamond–Dybvig liquidity insurance problem and prove that demand deposit contracts implement the social planner's first-best consumption allocation when relative risk aversion exceeds unity (Lesson 1).
  2. Prove the multiplicity of pure-strategy Nash equilibria in the simultaneous withdrawal game under sequential service and demonstrate how government deposit insurance eliminates the run equilibrium (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the fundamental solvency run threshold \(\theta^*\) for uninsured depositors under mark-to-market asset shocks and evaluate bank run vulnerability across uninsured deposit concentrations (Lesson 3).
  4. Calculate equilibrium asset prices under cash-in-the-market pricing and quantify the secondary balance sheet impairment transmitted by fire-sale externalities (Lesson 4).
  5. Model the discrete-time coupled dynamics of loss and margin spirals and derive the exact feedback multiplier condition distinguishing convergent price adjustment from explosive collapse (Lesson 5).
  6. Calibrate an empirical run dynamics model to regional bank balance sheets and solve for the minimal regulatory liquidity buffer required to prevent uninsured deposit runs (Project).

Prerequisites

SR1 builds on the foundational concepts established in BK1 and E4, alongside core quantitative principles from E2, E3, FC1, and M4. Specifically, SR1 utilizes:

  • From BK1: maturity transformation, delegated monitoring, and deposit franchise.
  • From E2: Nash equilibrium and Bayesian–Nash equilibrium.
  • From E3: expected utility and CRRA utility.
  • From E4: rational-expectations equilibrium and market completeness.
  • From FC1: financial intermediary.
  • From M4: random variable, expectation, and independence.

The six new concepts introduced in SR1 are defined across the five lessons: Diamond–Dybvig model (L01), bank run (L02), uninsured-depositor runs (L03), fire sale (L04), and both liquidity spiral and run on repo (L05).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions testing prerequisite knowledge across BK1, E2, E3, and quantitative foundations, alongside two skill checks testing the numerical run threshold and buffer calibration routines taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (7 of 8 items correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must satisfy the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. What structural balance sheet feature characterizes commercial banking maturity transformation in BK1?

    Options

    Maturity transformation intermediates between savers seeking immediate liquidity and borrowers requiring long-term project capital (BK1, maturity transformation).

  2. In E2, what defines a pure-strategy Nash equilibrium in a normal-form game?

    Options

    In a Nash equilibrium, no player can strictly improve their payoff by unilaterally deviating from their chosen strategy (E2, Nash equilibrium).

  3. Under CRRA utility \(u(c) = \frac{c^{1-\gamma} - 1}{1 - \gamma}\), if \(\gamma = 2\), what is the Arrow–Pratt relative risk aversion coefficient \(-c \cdot u''(c) / u'(c)\)?

    For CRRA utility, relative risk aversion is constant and identically equal to parameter \(\gamma = 2.0\) (E3, CRRA utility).

  4. What does Townsend's costly state verification framework establish regarding optimal financial contracts?

    Options

    Costly state verification proves that standard debt with fixed promised repayment minimizes deadweight monitoring costs (BK1, delegated monitoring).

  5. If a social planner optimizes \(\frac{1}{2} u(c_1) + \frac{1}{2} u(c_2)\) subject to \(\frac{1}{2} c_1 + \frac{1}{8} c_2 = 1\) with \(\gamma = 2\) (\(c_2 = 2 c_1\)), what is the optimal early consumption \(c_1^*\)?

    \((1/2) c_1 + (1/8)(2 c_1) = (3/4) c_1 = 1 \implies c_1 = 4/3 \approx 1.3333\) (SR1, L01).

  6. In modern banking regulation, what is the statutory FDIC deposit insurance coverage limit per depositor per insured bank?

    Options

    Under U.S. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation rules established by the Dodd–Frank Act, the standard maximum deposit insurance amount is $250,000.

  7. Skill check 1: In the Run Dynamics Model, let uninsured deposit share \(\mu = 0.80\), liquid buffer \(\ell = 0.40\), and liquidation recovery \(\kappa = 0.70\). Calculate the theoretical run threshold \(\theta^* = \frac{\mu - \ell}{\kappa (1 - \ell)}\).

    \(\theta^* = (0.80 - 0.40) / (0.70 \times (1.0 - 0.40)) = 0.40 / 0.42 = 0.95238 \approx 0.9524\) (Rung 1 worked example).

  8. Skill check 2: In the Run Dynamics Model, for \(\mu = 0.80\), \(\kappa = 0.70\), and target threshold \(\theta_{\text{target}} = 0.50\), compute the calibrated liquid buffer \(\ell^* = \frac{\mu - \kappa \theta_{\text{target}}}{1 - \kappa \theta_{\text{target}}}\).

    \(\ell^* = (0.80 - 0.70 \times 0.50) / (1.0 - 0.70 \times 0.50) = 0.45 / 0.65 = 9/13 \approx 0.6923\) (Rung 2 completion problem).

Lesson Map

The module's 40 study hours are divided across five foundational lessons of 8 study hours each:

  1. The Diamond–Dybvig Architecture & Liquidity Insurance (8 h)

    Three dates, preference shocks, early versus late consumption, the social planner's first-best allocation, and the demand deposit contract with sequential service.

  2. Self-Fulfilling Panics & Multiplicity (8 h)

    The simultaneous withdrawal game, strategic complementarities, good versus run equilibria, suspension of convertibility, and government deposit insurance.

  3. Information, Solvency & Uninsured Deposit Runs (8 h)

    Statutory insurance ceilings, mark-to-market securities losses, fundamental run thresholds, and the dynamics of the 2023 regional banking panic.

  4. Fire Sales & Asset Liquidation (8 h)

    Asset illiquidity, cash-in-the-market pricing, liquidation discounts, balance sheet contagion, and systemic fire-sale externalities across peer institutions.

  5. Liquidity Spirals & Wholesale Funding Runs (8 h)

    The Brunnermeier–Pedersen loss and margin spiral framework, repurchase agreements, collateral haircuts, and the 2007–2008 run on repo.

Project Ladder

The module concludes with a 10-hour independent project: designing, validating, and calibrating a quantitative Run Dynamics Model. The project follows the standard four-rung instructional ladder:

  1. 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance of the Run Dynamics Model, demonstrating analytical run threshold evaluation, closed-form buffer calibration, and residual verification (Rung 1).

  2. 2 · Completion problem

    Implement the numerical bisection root search routine and bracket residual checks to solve for the calibrated liquid buffer to tolerance 1e-8 (Rung 2).

  3. 3 · Independent project

    Construct the complete autograded solver (solver.py) implementing solve_model and calibrate_buffer across edge cases, and author an executive policy memo (memo.md) evaluated by rubric (Rung 3).

  4. 4 · Stretch

    Extend the model to incorporate heterogeneous depositor beliefs, continuous asset fire-sale price impact, or tiered central bank discount window liquidity facilities (Rung 4).
