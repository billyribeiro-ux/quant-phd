Standard microprudential risk measures evaluate individual institutions in isolation. A bank can appear safely capitalized under its standalone Value-at-Risk while imposing catastrophic tail losses on the broader financial system when it fails. Adrian and Brunnermeier (2016) resolve this blindness by defining CoVaR: the Value-at-Risk of the financial system conditional on a specific institution being in distress. By isolating the marginal contribution \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) relative to a normal benchmark state via linear quantile regression, regulators can quantify systemic externalities, distinguish systemic contribution from systemic vulnerability, and price macroprudential capital surcharges.

1Microprudential Failures and the CoVaR Concept

Traditional banking regulation evaluates solvency through standalone Value-at-Risk (\(\text{VaR}_\alpha^i\)), requiring each institution \(i\) to hold sufficient capital to absorb its own tail loss at confidence level \(1 - \alpha\) over a given horizon. However, an institution's standalone risk measure fails to internalize two systemic channels: outward contagion (fire sales, margin spirals, and contractual defaults that depress peer balance sheets) and common factor exposure (joint exposure to macroeconomic downturns).

Definition 1.1 (CoVaR and \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\))

Let \(X_t^s\) denote the return or loss of the aggregate financial system and \(X_t^i\) denote the return or loss of financial institution \(i\). For quantile level \(q \in (0, 1)\), the CoVaR of the system conditional on institution \(i\) being in distress state \(\mathbb{C}(X^i) = \{X^i = \text{VaR}_q^i\}\) is defined implicitly as the \(q\)-quantile of the system's conditional distribution :

\[ \Prob\left(X_t^s \le \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|\mathbb{C}(X^i)} \;\middle|\; \mathbb{C}(X_t^i)\right) = q \]

The marginal systemic risk contribution, denoted \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i\), is the difference between the system's VaR conditional on institution \(i\) being in distress (\(X_t^i = \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i\)) and the system's VaR conditional on institution \(i\) operating in its median normal benchmark state (\(X_t^i = \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i\)) :

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|X^i = \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i} - \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|X^i = \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i} \]
Remark 1.2 (sign conventions in returns vs losses)

Adrian and Brunnermeier (2016) define \(X_t\) as asset or equity returns where losses are represented by negative values. In this convention, the 5% Value-at-Risk \(\text{VaR}_{0.05}\) is a negative number (e.g., \(-0.038\)), median \(\text{VaR}_{0.50}\) is near zero or slightly positive (e.g., \(+0.001\)), and \(\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^{s|i}\) is more negative than median \(\text{CoVaR}_{0.50}^{s|i}\). Consequently, \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i\) is a negative number (e.g., \(-0.029\) or \(-290\text{ bps}\)), indicating that the system's tail loss widens under institutional distress. When expressing \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) in positive loss units, authors report \(-\Delta\text{CoVaR}\). Throughout this lesson, returns are signed as economic returns.

2Estimation via Linear Quantile Regression

Because conditioning on the exact point event \(\{X_t^i = \text{VaR}_q^i\}\) yields zero probability under continuous return distributions, non-parametric conditioning requires density slicing or kernel smoothing. Adrian and Brunnermeier (2016) resolve this by estimating CoVaR parametrically through linear quantile regression.

Theorem 1.3 (Quantile Regression Equivalence and Closed-Form \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\))

Assume the conditional quantile of financial institution returns \(X_t^i\) given lagged macroeconomic state variables \(M_{t-1}\) is linear:

\[ X_t^i = \alpha^i + \gamma^i M_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t^i, \quad \text{with } \text{Quant}_q(\varepsilon_t^i \mid M_{t-1}) = 0 \]

Similarly, assume the conditional quantile of system returns \(X_t^s\) given institution returns \(X_t^i\) and state variables \(M_{t-1}\) is linear:

\[ X_t^s = \alpha^{s|i} + \beta^{s|i} X_t^i + \gamma^{s|i} M_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t^{s|i}, \quad \text{with } \text{Quant}_q(\varepsilon_t^{s|i} \mid X_t^i, M_{t-1}) = 0 \]

Then the conditional Value-at-Risk of institution \(i\) and the conditional system CoVaR satisfy :

\[ \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i = \hat{\alpha}_q^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^i M_{t-1} \] \[ \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|i} = \hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i} + \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1} \]

Furthermore, evaluating \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i\) yields the exact closed-form expression:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \left( \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i - \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i \right) \]
Proof

By definition, \(\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|X^i = \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i}\) evaluates the conditional system quantile at \(X_t^i = \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i\):

\[ \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|i} = \hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i} + \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1} \]

Similarly, evaluating the system quantile at the institution's median state \(X_t^i = \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i\) yields:

\[ \text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{s|\text{median}} = \hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i} + \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1} \]

Subtracting the median state from the distressed state:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \left(\hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i} + \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \text{VaR}_{q,t}^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1}\right) - \left(\hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i} + \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i + \hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1}\right) \] \[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \left(\text{VaR}_{q,t}^i - \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i\right) \]

Notice that the common intercept \(\hat{\alpha}_q^{s|i}\) and the state-variable contribution \(\hat{\gamma}_q^{s|i} M_{t-1}\) cancel out exactly. This establishes that \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) isolates the pure marginal impact of moving the institution from its median to its tail state.

Definition 1.4 (Pinball Loss Function and Linear Programming Formulation)

For quantile level \(q \in (0, 1)\), the asymmetric check function (pinball loss) \(\rho_q(u)\) is defined as:

\[ \rho_q(u) = u \left(q - \indic_{\{u < 0\}}\right) = \begin{cases} q u & \text{if } u \ge 0 \\ (q - 1) u = (1 - q)|u| & \text{if } u < 0 \end{cases} \]

Given observations \((y_t, x_t)_{t=1}^T\) with \(x_t \in \R^K\), the quantile regression estimator \(\hat{\beta}_q = \argmin_{\beta \in \R^K} \sum_{t=1}^T \rho_q(y_t - x_t^\top \beta)\) is equivalently formulated as a constrained linear program by decomposing residuals into positive and negative slack components \(u_t = u_t^+ - u_t^-\) where \(u_t^+, u_t^- \ge 0\):

\[ \min_{\beta \in \R^K, u^+, u^- \in \R_+^T} \sum_{t=1}^T \left(q u_t^+ + (1 - q) u_t^-\right) \quad \text{subject to } y_t - x_t^\top \beta = u_t^+ - u_t^- \quad \forall t \]

3Macroeconomic State Variables and Conditioning

Adrian and Brunnermeier (2016) stress that \(\text{CoVaR}\) must be conditioned on lagged macro-financial state variables \(M_{t-1}\) to prevent credit cycles and low-volatility regimes from masking systemic vulnerability . In tranquil pre-crisis periods, realized volatility \(\sigma_t\) drops, leading unconditioned \(\text{VaR}\) models to indicate minimal risk precisely when systemic leverage is highest .

The standard empirical state vector \(M_{t-1}\) comprises:

  1. Chicago Board Options Exchange VIX: Captures forward-looking equity market implied volatility and broad risk aversion.
  2. Short-term liquidity spread: The 3-month Libor-OIS spread or 3-month repo-Treasury spread, measuring counterparty distress in interbank funding markets.
  3. Term spread slope: The yield difference between 10-year Treasury bonds and 3-month Treasury bills, capturing term structure expectations and recession risk.
  4. Credit spread: The BofA Merrill Lynch or Moody's BAA-minus-AAA corporate bond yield spread, tracking default risk premia.
  5. Equity market returns: The lagged periodic return of the S&P 500 or broad total market index.

4Systemic Contribution versus Systemic Vulnerability

A critical econometric and policy distinction exists between an institution's systemic contribution and its systemic vulnerability:

  • Systemic Contribution (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{s|i}\)): Measures the externality that institution \(i\) imposes on the system. It conditions system distress on institution \(i\)'s distress. Highly interconnected dealer banks, money-center clearing brokers, and massive repo borrowers typically exhibit large \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{s|i}\).
  • Systemic Vulnerability (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{i|s}\)): Measures how severely institution \(i\) is impacted when the aggregate system experiences a systemic crisis. It conditions institution \(i\)'s return on system distress: \(\text{CoVaR}^{i|s} - \text{CoVaR}^{i|\text{median}}\). Small, regional, or undiversified banks may be highly vulnerable to systemic downturns despite having negligible systemic contribution.

Furthermore, to capture economic magnitude rather than percentage returns alone, regulators compute Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) by multiplying percentage \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_i\) by the total balance-sheet asset size or equity market capitalization \(W_i\) :

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^{\$, i} = W_{i,t} \cdot \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i \]
Example 1.5 (Quantile Regression vs OLS in CoVaR)

Suppose institution returns \(R_i\) and system returns \(R_s\) are generated with fat-tailed innovations. An OLS regression yields slope \(\beta_{\text{OLS}} = 0.6102\), while 5% quantile regression yields \(\beta_{0.05} = 0.7370\). If institution VaR is \(\text{VaR}_{0.05}^i = -0.0382\) and median return is \(\text{VaR}_{0.50}^i = +0.0010\), then:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i = 0.7370 \times (-0.0382 - 0.0010) = 0.7370 \times (-0.0392) = -0.0289 \quad (-289\text{ bps}) \]

Using the OLS slope would underestimate the systemic spillover as \(0.6102 \times (-0.0392) = -0.0239\) (\(-239\text{ bps}\)), failing to account for asymmetric tail dependence.

Lab 1 · CoVaR and Delta-CoVaR Estimation via Quantile Regression

Estimating CoVaR via 5% linear quantile regression reveals stronger tail sensitivity (\(\beta_{0.05} > \beta_{\text{OLS}}\)) and isolates a negative marginal systemic spillover (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR} < -250\text{ bps}\)) conditional on institution tail distress.

"""Lab SR3-L01-1: CoVaR and Delta-CoVaR Estimation via Quantile Regression.

SIMULATED: generates synthetic financial institution and systemic equity returns
to estimate conditional Value-at-Risk (CoVaR) and marginal systemic contribution
(Delta-CoVaR) following Adrian and Brunnermeier (2016).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import linprog


def fit_quantile_regression(y: np.ndarray, x: np.ndarray, q: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Solve linear quantile regression via linear programming."""
    t_obs, k_vars = x.shape
    c = np.concatenate([np.zeros(k_vars), q * np.ones(t_obs), (1.0 - q) * np.ones(t_obs)])
    eye_t = np.eye(t_obs)
    a_eq = np.hstack([x, eye_t, -eye_t])
    bounds = [(None, None)] * k_vars + [(0.0, None)] * (2 * t_obs)
    res = linprog(c, A_eq=a_eq, b_eq=y, bounds=bounds, method="highs")
    if not res.success:
        raise ValueError(f"Quantile optimization failed: {res.message}")
    return np.asarray(res.x[:k_vars], dtype=np.float64)


def main() -> None:
    # Deterministic synthetic sample calibration (T=500 daily observations)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(101)
    t_obs = 500

    # Macro state variable: market volatility index proxy (mean zero, AR(1))
    vol_state = np.zeros(t_obs)
    for t in range(1, t_obs):
        vol_state[t] = 0.65 * vol_state[t - 1] + rng.normal(0.0, 0.01)

    # Institution returns R_i (high beta financial institution)
    # R_i,t = alpha_i + gamma_i * M_t-1 + eps_i,t
    eps_i = rng.standard_t(df=4, size=t_obs) * 0.015
    r_inst = 0.0004 - 0.25 * vol_state + eps_i

    # System returns R_sys (financial sector aggregate)
    # R_sys,t = alpha_s + beta_s * R_i,t + gamma_s * M_t-1 + eps_s,t
    eps_s = rng.standard_t(df=4, size=t_obs) * 0.010
    r_sys = 0.0002 + 0.60 * r_inst - 0.15 * vol_state + eps_s

    # Design matrices
    ones = np.ones((t_obs, 1))
    x_inst = np.column_stack([ones, vol_state])
    x_sys = np.column_stack([ones, r_inst, vol_state])

    # 1. Institution VaR at q = 0.05 (crisis) and q = 0.50 (median)
    beta_inst_05 = fit_quantile_regression(r_inst, x_inst, q=0.05)
    beta_inst_50 = fit_quantile_regression(r_inst, x_inst, q=0.50)

    # Evaluate at sample average volatility (vol_mean = 0)
    eval_state = np.array([1.0, float(np.mean(vol_state))])
    var_inst_05 = float(np.dot(beta_inst_05, eval_state))
    var_inst_50 = float(np.dot(beta_inst_50, eval_state))

    # 2. System CoVaR quantile regression at q = 0.05
    beta_sys_05 = fit_quantile_regression(r_sys, x_sys, q=0.05)
    alpha_sys = beta_sys_05[0]
    beta_sys = beta_sys_05[1]
    gamma_sys = beta_sys_05[2]

    # CoVaR conditional on institution at 5% VaR vs median VaR
    covar_05 = float(alpha_sys + beta_sys * var_inst_05 + gamma_sys * eval_state[1])
    covar_50 = float(alpha_sys + beta_sys * var_inst_50 + gamma_sys * eval_state[1])
    delta_covar = covar_05 - covar_50

    # 3. OLS Benchmark for comparison
    ols_sys = np.linalg.lstsq(x_sys, r_sys, rcond=None)[0]

    print("=== CoVaR and Delta-CoVaR Estimation (Adrian & Brunnermeier 2016) ===")
    print(f"Observations (T): {t_obs}")
    print(f"Institution VaR(0.05): {var_inst_05:.4f} (-{abs(var_inst_05) * 100:.2f}%)")
    print(f"Institution VaR(0.50): {var_inst_50:.4f} (+{var_inst_50 * 100:.2f}%)")
    print(f"Quantile Regression beta(0.05): {beta_sys:.4f}")
    print(f"OLS Regression beta: {ols_sys[1]:.4f}")
    print(f"System CoVaR(0.05 | Inst VaR 0.05): {covar_05:.4f}")
    print(f"System CoVaR(0.05 | Inst Median):   {covar_50:.4f}")
    print(f"Delta-CoVaR(0.05):                  {delta_covar:.4f} ({delta_covar * 10000:.1f} bps)")


main()

The empirical quantile regression slope \(\hat{\beta}_{0.05} = 0.7370\) strictly exceeds the standard OLS slope of \(0.6102\), confirming tail dependence under leptokurtic return shocks. When the institution experiences its 5% Value-at-Risk loss (\(-3.82\%\)), the financial system's Value-at-Risk widens from \(-2.06\%\) to \(-4.96\%\), generating a marginal systemic risk contribution \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) of \(-289.3\text{ bps}\).

Where the canon breaks

While \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) represents a major conceptual advance over standalone \(\text{VaR}\), it possesses several well-documented theoretical and empirical limitations:

  1. Lack of Structural Directionality: Quantile regression estimates statistical conditioning, not causal counterfactuals. If institution \(i\) and the system are both driven by an unobserved common macro shock, institution \(i\) will exhibit a large \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) even if it possesses zero causal link to other financial institutions.
  2. Non-Subadditivity of Quantile Measures: Because \(\text{CoVaR}\) is based on quantiles rather than expectations, it inherits \(\text{VaR}\)'s mathematical violation of subadditivity. Merging two financial institutions can arbitrarily alter measured \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\).
  3. Quantile Crossing: In linear quantile regression with multi-dimensional macro state variables, estimated quantile hyperplanes at different quantile levels (e.g., \(q = 0.01\) vs \(q = 0.05\)) can cross outside the sample convex hull, violating monotonicity of the cumulative distribution function.

Misconceptions

  • A commercial bank with a very low standalone VaR is inherently safe and cannot impose systemic risk on the financial system.

    Standalone VaR measures only the probability distribution of the institution's own balance sheet. If a large bank holds massive short-term interbank repo liabilities or operates as a vital central counterparty clearing member, its sudden liquidation can spark severe market-wide fire sales and liquidity spirals even if its standalone insolvency probability appeared low in tranquil times.

  • Delta-CoVaR is mathematically symmetric: an institution's systemic contribution to the system equals the system's impact on the institution.

    \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{s|i}\) (systemic contribution) conditions the system on institution \(i\), while \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{i|s}\) (systemic vulnerability) conditions institution \(i\) on the system. A regional mortgage lender may suffer massive losses when the national housing market crashes (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{i|s} \ll 0\)) while having virtually zero outward impact on the broader banking sector (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{s|i} \approx 0\)).

  • Quantile regression coefficients are identical to ordinary least squares regression coefficients scaled by the standard normal distribution quantile.

    Under joint normality with constant covariance, quantile regression lines are parallel and slopes equal OLS slopes. However, financial asset returns exhibit non-linear tail dependence, skewness, and volatility clustering. In financial crises, correlation increases in the left tail, causing the quantile regression slope \(\hat{\beta}_q\) to strictly exceed the OLS slope.

Problems

Problem M.1: Given institution 5% VaR \(\text{VaR}_{0.05}^i = -0.0450\), median return \(\text{VaR}_{0.50}^i = 0.0020\), and estimated 5% quantile slope \(\hat{\beta}_{0.05}^{s|i} = 0.8500\), compute \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i\) in basis points.

Worked solution

Applying the closed-form Adrian & Brunnermeier formula:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i = \hat{\beta}_{0.05}^{s|i} \left(\text{VaR}_{0.05}^i - \text{VaR}_{0.50}^i\right) \] \[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i = 0.8500 \times (-0.0450 - 0.0020) = 0.8500 \times (-0.0470) = -0.03995 \]

Expressed in basis points, \(\Delta\text{CoVaR} = -399.5\text{ bps}\) (or an outward tail spillover of \(399.5\text{ bps}\)).

Problem M.2: An investment bank has an equity market capitalization of \(W = \$80\text{ billion}\) and an estimated \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05} = -0.0320\). Calculate the institution's Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{\$}\).

Worked solution

Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) scales the percentage systemic contribution by market capitalization:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^{\$} = W \cdot \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05} = \$80\text{B} \times (-0.0320) = -\$2.56\text{ billion} \]

The distress of this institution is projected to widen the aggregate tail loss of the financial system by \(\$2.56\text{ billion}\).

Problem M.3: In pinball loss optimization with quantile \(q = 0.05\), evaluate the loss \(\rho_{0.05}(u)\) for a positive residual \(u = +0.04\) and for a negative residual \(u = -0.04\).

Worked solution

For positive residual \(u \ge 0\):

\[ \rho_q(u) = q u = 0.05 \times 0.04 = 0.0020 \]

For negative residual \(u < 0\):

\[ \rho_q(u) = (1 - q)|u| = 0.95 \times 0.04 = 0.0380 \]

Negative residuals (under-predictions of losses) are penalized 19 times more severely than positive residuals (\((1-q)/q = 19\)), driving the optimal fitted line down to the 5th percentile.

Problem P.1: Prove that the sample quantile \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) of a sample \(\{y_t\}_{t=1}^T\) minimizes the unconstrained pinball loss \(\sum_{t=1}^T \rho_\alpha(y_t - c)\) over \(c \in \R\).

Worked solution

Let \(S(c) = \sum_{t=1}^T \rho_\alpha(y_t - c)\). The directional derivative of \(\rho_\alpha(u)\) with respect to \(c\) is:

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial c} \rho_\alpha(y_t - c) = -\left(\alpha - \indic_{\{y_t < c\}}\right) = \indic_{\{y_t < c\}} - \alpha \]

Summing across the sample:

\[ \frac{\partial S(c)}{\partial c} = \sum_{t=1}^T \indic_{\{y_t < c\}} - T \alpha \]

Setting the subgradient to contain zero requires:

\[ \sum_{t=1}^T \indic_{\{y_t < c\}} \le T \alpha \le \sum_{t=1}^T \indic_{\{y_t \le c\}} \]

Dividing by \(T\), the empirical distribution function \(\hat{F}(c)\) must satisfy \(\hat{F}(c) \ge \alpha\), where:

\[ \hat{F}(c) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \indic_{\{y_t \le c\}} \]

The infimum of such \(c\) is precisely the sample quantile \(\hat{q}_\alpha = \inf\{c : \hat{F}(c) \ge \alpha\}\).

Problem P.2: Prove that under bivariate normality of returns \((X^s, X^i) \sim \Normal(\mu, \Sigma)\) with correlation \(\rho\), the quantile regression slope \(\beta_q^{s|i}\) is identical for all quantiles \(q \in (0, 1)\) and equals the standard OLS slope.

Worked solution

Under joint normality, the conditional distribution of \(X^s\) given \(X^i = x\) is Gaussian:

\[ X^s \mid (X^i = x) \sim \Normal\left(\mu_s + \rho \frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_i}(x - \mu_i), \; \sigma_s^2 (1 - \rho^2)\right) \]

The \(q\)-quantile of this conditional normal distribution is:

\[ \text{Quant}_q(X^s \mid X^i = x) = \mu_s + \rho \frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_i}(x - \mu_i) + \sigma_s \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \Phi^{-1}(q) \]

Taking the partial derivative with respect to \(x\):

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \text{Quant}_q(X^s \mid X^i = x) = \rho \frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_i} = \frac{\Cov(X^s, X^i)}{\Var(X^i)} = \beta_{\text{OLS}} \]

Because \(\Phi^{-1}(q)\) enters only as an additive intercept term, the slope is independent of \(q\) and coincides with the linear conditional expectation OLS coefficient.

Problem P.3: Prove that \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i\) is strictly negative whenever the quantile slope \(\hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} > 0\) and the institution exhibits a non-degenerate tail (\(\text{VaR}_q^i < \text{VaR}_{0.5}^i\)).

Worked solution

Recall the closed-form theorem:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \left(\text{VaR}_{q,t}^i - \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i\right) \]

By definition of quantiles for continuous variables and any \(q < 0.50\), the distribution function is strictly increasing, which implies \(\text{VaR}_q^i - \text{VaR}_{0.5}^i < 0\).

Since \(\hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} > 0\), the product of a strictly positive slope and a strictly negative tail difference is strictly negative:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_{q,t}^i = \hat{\beta}_q^{s|i} \left(\text{VaR}_{q,t}^i - \text{VaR}_{0.5,t}^i\right) < 0 \]

This confirms that institutional distress unambiguously expands systemic Value-at-Risk under positive tail co-movement.

Problem E.1: In an empirical test on 2007–2009 crisis data, an econometrician finds that adding the VIX and Libor-OIS spread as conditioning variables increases the absolute magnitude of \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) by 45%. Explain the economic mechanism behind this amplification.

Worked solution

During crisis periods, elevated VIX and widened Libor-OIS spreads shift the conditional distribution of both the institution and the financial system downward. Because quantile regression parameters capture time-varying macroeconomic volatility, conditioning on stressed state variables shifts \(\text{VaR}_{0.05}^i\) further into the tail. Furthermore, state-contingent funding illiquidity intensifies fire-sale cross-holdings, increasing \(\hat{\beta}_{0.05}^{s|i}\) above its unconditional sample average.

Problem E.2: Using the Lab 1 output numbers, compare the standalone 5% Value-at-Risk of the institution (\(-3.82\%\)) with the system's conditional CoVaR (\(-4.96\%\)). Why is system CoVaR wider than standalone institution VaR?

Worked solution

System CoVaR (\(-4.96\%\)) evaluates the 5th percentile of the system's return distribution conditional on institution distress. When the institution experiences tail distress (\(-3.82\%\)), systemic market returns absorb both the institution's direct weight in the index and the compounding fire-sale and liquidity spillovers across peer institutions, depressing system returns below the individual bank's isolated threshold.

Problem E.3: Rank two institutions: Institution A has \(\Delta\text{CoVaR} = -400\text{ bps}\) and \(W_A = \$50\text{B}\). Institution B has \(\Delta\text{CoVaR} = -150\text{ bps}\) and \(W_B = \$800\text{B}\). Which bank creates greater total systemic damage?

Worked solution

Compute Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{\$}\) for both institutions:

\[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_A^{\$} = \$50\text{B} \times (-0.0400) = -\$2.00\text{ billion} \] \[ \Delta\text{CoVaR}_B^{\$} = \$800\text{B} \times (-0.0150) = -\$12.00\text{ billion} \]

Although Institution A has a higher percentage spillover per dollar of equity, Institution B's massive balance sheet creates \(\$12.00\text{B}\) in aggregate systemic loss, six times larger than Institution A. Macroprudential regulation must prioritize Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) for systemic capital surcharges.

Problem R.1: How can macroprudential bank capital surcharges (e.g., G-SIB surcharges) be directly calibrated to an institution's marginal systemic risk contribution \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{\$}\)?

Worked solution

Under a Pigouvian systemic taxation framework, each institution is assessed a capital buffer surcharge proportional to the expected negative externality it imposes on the system. By calculating:

\[ \tau_i = \kappa \cdot \frac{|\Delta\text{CoVaR}_i^{\$}|}{\sum_j |\Delta\text{CoVaR}_j^{\$}|} \cdot K_{\text{target}} \]

regulators allocate the macroprudential capital requirement such that institutions with higher interconnectedness, leverage, and tail spillover face higher capital costs, incentivizing internal balance sheet de-risking.

Problem R.2: What econometric strategy can resolve the endogeneity problem where simultaneous reverse causality exists between institution distress and system distress?

Worked solution

To identify the causal outward spillover from bank \(i\) to the system, researchers employ structural vector autoregressions (SVAR) identified via heteroskedasticity or high-frequency event studies around idiosyncratic corporate announcements (e.g., unexpected litigation losses or fraudulent trading disclosure unique to bank \(i\)). Alternatively, instrumental variables that shift bank \(i\)'s capital ratio without directly affecting the macroeconomy can trace the causal propagation of single-firm distress.

Problem R.3: Contrast the regulatory implications of \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) against the Basel III leverage ratio. Under what market conditions does \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) signal an emerging systemic crisis while the leverage ratio signals complete solvency?

Worked solution

The Basel III leverage ratio is a static accounting ratio of Tier 1 capital over unweighted total exposures. In credit booms, asset valuations are inflated, loan loss provisions are minimal, and asset book values mask growing tail correlations and shadow banking wholesale runs. Because \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) incorporates forward-looking market price tail dependence and macroeconomic risk premia (VIX, spreads), it detects widening tail co-movement and systemic fragility months before static book leverage ratios deteriorate.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What does \(\text{CoVaR}_q^{s|i}\) measure in quantitative systemic risk?

    Options

    CoVaR is defined as the Value-at-Risk of the financial system conditional on institution \(i\) being in its distressed \(\text{VaR}_q^i\) state (Adrian & Brunnermeier 2016).

  2. Why does \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) subtract the system's VaR conditional on the institution's median state rather than zero return?

    Options

    Subtracting the median state benchmarks the institution against its baseline normal operating conditions, neutralizing unconditional market trends.

  3. If \(\hat{\beta}_{0.05} = 0.60\), \(\text{VaR}_{0.05}^i = -0.042\), and \(\text{VaR}_{0.50}^i = -0.002\), compute \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_{0.05}^i\).

    \(\Delta\text{CoVaR} = 0.60 \times (-0.042 - (-0.002)) = 0.60 \times (-0.040) = -0.024\) (or \(-240\text{ bps}\)).

  4. What is the check function (pinball loss) penalty ratio for negative versus positive residuals when estimating a 1% tail quantile (\(q = 0.01\))?

    Options

    For \(q = 0.01\), negative residuals (underpredicting severe losses) receive weight \(1 - q = 0.99\), while positive residuals receive weight \(0.01\), yielding a 99:1 asymmetric penalty ratio.

  5. Which variable is NOT typically included in the Adrian & Brunnermeier (2016) macro state conditioning vector \(M_{t-1}\)?

    Options

    State variables represent broad macroeconomic and funding market conditions (VIX, liquidity spread, term spread, credit spread, equity returns), not firm-level executive compensation.

  6. What distinguishes systemic contribution (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{s|i}\)) from systemic vulnerability (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{i|s}\))?

    Options

    Contribution conditions system distress on institution failure (outward spillover); vulnerability conditions institution loss on system distress (inward exposure).

  7. A systemically important bank has an equity market capitalization of \(\$150\text{ billion}\) and \(\Delta\text{CoVaR} = -0.030\). Compute Dollar \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{\$}\) in billions of dollars.

    \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}^{\$} = \$150\text{B} \times (-0.030) = -\$4.5\text{ billion}\).

  8. Under what mathematical condition does the quantile regression slope \(\hat{\beta}_q\) strictly exceed the ordinary least squares slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}\)?

    Options

    Under asymmetric left-tail dependence, correlation increases during extreme market crashes, pulling the quantile regression slope strictly above the unconditional OLS slope.
