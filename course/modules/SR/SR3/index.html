Measuring Systemic Risk

Financial institutions operate in an interconnected macroeconomic ecosystem where individual insolvency risks cannot be evaluated in isolation. While traditional microprudential regulation focuses on individual institution Value-at-Risk, systemic crises emerge from macro-financial externalities, tail dependence, fire-sale spillovers, and contractual cascade dynamics across interbank funding networks. In this module, you will master the foundational quantitative econometrics and network models of systemic risk: CoVaR and quantile regression (Adrian & Brunnermeier 2016), Marginal Expected Shortfall and LRMES (Acharya et al. 2017), SRISK and conditional capital shortfall indices (Brownlees & Engle 2017), and the Eisenberg–Noe fixed-point clearing architecture and phase-transition theorems of financial networks (Acemoglu et al. 2015). The module culminates in building Atlas milestone A19: an enterprise systemic-risk monitor with automated alert thresholds.

  • Prerequisites: SR1 Runs, Liquidity & Fire Sales and PR4 Risk Measurement
  • Project: Atlas A19: Systemic-Risk Monitor (Graded by autograder across 50 seeds with mutant mutation testing)
  • Data: Market returns, macroeconomic state variables, and interbank nominal networks are SIMULATED for seed reproducibility.

40 h lessons · 10 h project (20% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of SR3 you can:

  1. Formulate the conditional quantile regression objective and estimate \(\text{CoVaR}\) and marginal systemic contribution \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) conditional on financial institution distress and macroeconomic state variables (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive Marginal Expected Shortfall (\(\text{MES}\)) from welfare economic foundations of capital shortfall externalities and project multi-month Long-Run MES (\(\text{LRMES}\)) under systemic crisis drawdowns (Lesson 2).
  3. Compute dollar-denominated conditional capital shortfalls and the aggregate \(\text{SRISK}\) systemic risk index, and decompose systemic risk contributions across leverage, market capitalization, and tail risk (Lesson 3).
  4. Solve Eisenberg–Noe network clearing payment vectors via monotone Picard contraction operators and quantify cascade propagation rounds across varying interbank network topologies (Lesson 4).
  5. Prove the Acemoglu–Ozdaglar–Tahbaz-Salehi phase transition theorem demonstrating how dense financial network interconnectedness shifts from risk diversification to systemic fragility under catastrophic shocks (Lesson 4).
  6. Construct an enterprise systemic-risk monitoring engine (Atlas A19) integrating CoVaR, MES, SRISK, and network clearing with calibrated multi-tier prudential alert thresholds (Project).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions testing prerequisite mastery across quantitative risk measurement (PR4), systemic liquidity spirals (SR1), and the core formulas of SR3 rungs 1 and 2. Mastery threshold is 80% (7 of 8 correct), including both designated data-skill checks.

  1. Why does microprudential Value-at-Risk (\(\text{VaR}\)) fail as an indicator of systemic risk during financial distress?

    Options

    VaR evaluates risk from the standpoint of an isolated institution and ignores externalities imposed on counterparties and the aggregate financial system (PR4, SR1).

  2. Which mathematical property prevents Value-at-Risk from being classified as a coherent risk measure under Artzner et al. (1999)?

    Options

    VaR violates subadditivity (\(\text{VaR}(X + Y) \le \text{VaR}(X) + \text{VaR}(Y)\) can fail for non-elliptical heavy tails), meaning merger or portfolio diversification can artificially increase measured VaR (PR4).

  3. In the Brunnermeier & Pedersen (2009) liquidity spiral framework, what mechanism couples loss spirals to margin spirals?

    Options

    Loss spirals erode intermediary equity, while margin spirals increase required funding haircuts, compounding fire sales (SR1).

  4. Skill check 1: Under the Brownlees & Engle (2017) SRISK framework, let prudential capital fraction \(k = 0.08\), book debt \(D = 900.0\), equity market capitalization \(W = 100.0\), and \(\text{LRMES} = 0.40\). Calculate the institution's conditional capital shortfall \(\text{SRISK} = \max(0, k D - (1 - k)(1 - \text{LRMES}) W)\).

    \(k D = 0.08 \times 900 = 72.0\). \((1 - k)(1 - \text{LRMES}) W = 0.92 \times 0.60 \times 100 = 55.2\). \(\text{SRISK} = 72.0 - 55.2 = 16.8\) (Rung 1 worked example).

  5. In Adrian & Brunnermeier (2016), how is \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}_t^i(q)\) defined relative to the institution's median state?

    Options

    \(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\) is the shift in the system's VaR when the institution moves from its median benchmark state to its distressed \(q\)-quantile state (Adrian & Brunnermeier 2016, Eq. 6).

  6. If a dealer bank has an estimated market beta \(\beta = 1.568\) and average daily return during 5% worst market crisis days of \(-5.52\%\), what is its Marginal Expected Shortfall expressed as a positive loss percentage (\(\text{MES} = -\E[R_i \mid R_{\text{sys}} \le \text{VaR}_{0.05}]\))?

    \(\text{MES} = -(-5.52\%) = 5.52\%\) (SR3, L02).

  7. Skill check 2: In a 5-node Eisenberg-Noe network where Node 0 promised \(\bar{p}_0 = 50.0\), outside senior debt \(b_0 = 15.0\), asset shock leaves liquid assets \(z_0 = 0.0\), and incoming interbank payments from solvent peers equal \(39.2\), what is Node 0's actual clearing payment \(p_0^* = \min(\bar{p}_0, \max(0, z_0 + \text{inflows} - b_0))\)?

    \(p_0^* = \min(50.0, \max(0, 0.0 + 39.2 - 15.0)) = \min(50.0, 24.2) = 24.2\) (Rung 2 completion problem).

  8. According to Acemoglu et al. (2015), what characterizes the phase transition of financial networks between complete and sparse connectivity?

    Options

    Dense networks diversify and absorb small shocks across many peer buffers, but act as transmission channels for catastrophic shocks that wipe out counterparty equity (Acemoglu et al. 2015, Theorem 1).

Lesson Map

The module's 40 lesson study hours are divided across four foundational lessons of 10 study hours each:

  1. CoVaR & Quantile Regression (10 h)

    Formulating conditional Value-at-Risk (\(\text{CoVaR}\)) and marginal systemic contribution (\(\Delta\text{CoVaR}\)), pinball loss optimization, macroeconomic state conditioning, and systemic contribution versus systemic vulnerability.

  2. Marginal Expected Shortfall & LRMES (10 h)

    Economic foundations of systemic risk taxation, bivariate tail dependence, non-parametric MES estimation, systemic expected shortfall (SES), and dynamic 6-month Long-Run MES (\(\text{LRMES}\)) crisis projections.

  3. SRISK & Capital Shortfall Index (10 h)

    Combining market-based tail risk with accounting leverage and balance-sheet debt, regulatory prudential capital ratios, aggregate systemic shortfall tracking, and NYU Stern V-Lab systemic risk rankings.

  4. Network Contagion & Cascade Dynamics (10 h)

    The Eisenberg–Noe clearing payment lattice operator, fixed-point Picard contraction algorithms, interbank default cascades, and the Acemoglu–Ozdaglar–Tahbaz-Salehi network phase-transition theorem.

  4. Stretch
