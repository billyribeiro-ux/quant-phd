Case Initiation & Financial Statement Reconstruction

An empirical valuation or corporate restructuring recommendation is only as credible as its underlying financial accounting engine. In professional practice, financial analysts frequently inherit distorted financial filings with non-articulating cash flows, disguised operating leases, and inconsistent working capital schedules. Mastering the rigorous reconstruction of three-statement financial models establishes the deterministic computational foundation required for dynamic valuation, debt capacity analysis, and board defense.

1. The Integrated Corporate Finance and Equity Case

A rigorous corporate finance valuation cannot treat operational modeling, capital structure choice, and investor communication as isolated silos. We formalize this unified investigative architecture:

Definition 1.1 (Integrated Corporate Finance and Equity Case)

An integrated corporate-finance and equity case is an exhaustive institutional evaluation of a single operating company that synthesizes: (1) historical financial statement reconstruction from audited regulatory filings (SEC Forms 10-K and 10-Q); (2) an integrated three-statement model enforcing exact balance sheet articulation; (3) forensic earnings quality and accrual audits; (4) dynamic valuation under time-varying cost of capital; (5) capital structure and liquidity optimization balancing tax shields against financial distress; (6) an operational and financial risk hedging architecture; and (7) an adversarial board-level strategic defense.

Following Penman (2013), financial statement analysis must separate operating activities (which generate customer value) from financing activities (which distribute that value among claimholders). Raw accounting trial balances must be systematically restated into net operating assets (\(\text{NOA}\)) and net financial obligations (\(\text{NFO}\)):

\[ \text{NOA}_t = \text{Operating Assets}_t - \text{Operating Liabilities}_t \] \[ \text{NFO}_t = \text{Financial Liabilities}_t - \text{Financial Assets}_t \] \[ \text{Common Equity}_t = \text{NOA}_t - \text{NFO}_t \]

Example 1.1 (Operating vs Financing Asset Restatement)

Consider a firm with Total Assets of $1,200M consisting of $100M operational cash, $80M excess short-term marketable securities, $320M accounts receivable, $250M inventory, and $450M net PP&E. Its liabilities consist of $180M accounts payable, $60M accrued wages, $100M short-term bank debt, and $350M long-term senior notes.

The operating assets total \(\$100\text{M} + \$320\text{M} + \$250\text{M} + \$450\text{M} = \$1,120\text{M}\). Operating liabilities total \(\$180\text{M} + \$60\text{M} = \$240\text{M}\). Hence, Net Operating Assets are \(\text{NOA} = \$1,120\text{M} - \$240\text{M} = \$880\text{M}\). Net Financial Obligations are \(\text{NFO} = (\$100\text{M} + \$350\text{M}) - \$80\text{M} = \$370\text{M}\). Common Shareholders Equity equals \(\text{NOA} - \text{NFO} = \$880\text{M} - \$370\text{M} = \$510\text{M}\).

2. Financial Statement Articulation Mechanics

A financial model articulates if and only if every transaction flows simultaneously through the Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Statement of Cash Flows without arbitrary plug variables.

Theorem 1.1 (Three-Statement Articulation Identity)

Let \(\Delta\text{Assets}_t\) denote the net change in total assets from period \(t-1\) to \(t\), and let \(\Delta\text{Liabilities}_t\) and \(\Delta\text{Equity}_t\) denote the changes in total liabilities and equity. Under double-entry accounting with clean surplus equity roll-forward, the articulation discrepancy:

\[ \delta_{\text{art}, t} = \Delta\text{Assets}_t - \left(\Delta\text{Liabilities}_t + \Delta\text{Equity}_t\right) \]

satisfies \(\delta_{\text{art}, t} \equiv 0\) for all \(t\) when cash balance is determined by the cash flow identity:

\[ \text{Cash}_t = \text{Cash}_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t \]

Proof

Total assets decompose into cash and non-cash assets: \(\text{Assets}_t = \text{Cash}_t + \text{AR}_t + \text{Inv}_t + \text{NPPE}_t\). Hence:

\[ \Delta\text{Assets}_t = \Delta\text{Cash}_t + \Delta\text{AR}_t + \Delta\text{Inv}_t + \Delta\text{NPPE}_t \]

From the fixed asset continuity schedule, \(\Delta\text{NPPE}_t = \text{CapEx}_t - \text{Depr}_t\). By definition of Cash Flow from Operations, Cash Flow from Investing, and Cash Flow from Financing:

\[ \text{CFO}_t = \text{Net Income}_t + \text{Depr}_t - (\Delta\text{AR}_t + \Delta\text{Inv}_t - \Delta\text{AP}_t) \] \[ \text{CFI}_t = -\text{CapEx}_t \] \[ \text{CFF}_t = -\text{Dividends}_t + \Delta\text{Debt}_t \]

Summing the three cash flow components gives \(\Delta\text{Cash}_t\):

\[ \Delta\text{Cash}_t = \text{Net Income}_t - \text{Dividends}_t - \Delta\text{AR}_t - \Delta\text{Inv}_t + \Delta\text{AP}_t - \text{CapEx}_t + \text{Depr}_t + \Delta\text{Debt}_t \]

Substituting \(\Delta\text{Cash}_t\) back into \(\Delta\text{Assets}_t\):

\[ \Delta\text{Assets}_t = (\text{Net Income}_t - \text{Dividends}_t) + \Delta\text{AP}_t + \Delta\text{Debt}_t \]

Under the clean-surplus relation, the change in equity is exactly retained earnings: \(\Delta\text{Equity}_t = \text{Net Income}_t - \text{Dividends}_t\). Total liabilities change by \(\Delta\text{Liabilities}_t = \Delta\text{AP}_t + \Delta\text{Debt}_t\). Therefore:

\[ \Delta\text{Liabilities}_t + \Delta\text{Equity}_t = \Delta\text{AP}_t + \Delta\text{Debt}_t + (\text{Net Income}_t - \text{Dividends}_t) = \Delta\text{Assets}_t \]

Subtracting yields \(\delta_{\text{art}, t} = 0\) identically.

3. Working Capital Cycles & Cash Conversion Dynamics

Operating working capital requirements dictate how top-line revenue expansion converts into real liquidity. We model trade credit and inventory velocity using the standard activity metrics from CF8:

\[ \text{DSO} = \frac{\text{Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Revenues}} \times 365 \] \[ \text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 \] \[ \text{DPO} = \frac{\text{Accounts Payable}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 \] \[ \text{Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} \]

When revenue grows at rate \(g\), working capital absorbs operating cash flows according to the investment rate \(\Delta\text{NWC} = (\text{DSO} + \text{DIO} \times (1 - \text{GrossMargin}) - \text{DPO} \times (1 - \text{GrossMargin})) \times \frac{\Delta\text{Rev}}{365}\).

Lab 1 · Three-statement financial reconstruction and articulation drill

We reconstruct a 3-year integrated financial model from a base trial balance and driver assumptions, verifying that the balance sheet articulation gap evaluates to zero across every projection year.

"""Lab L01-1: Three-statement model reconstruction and dynamic articulation."""

from __future__ import annotations


def main() -> None:
    # 3-year historical trial balance and 3-year projection
    years = [2024, 2025, 2026]
    rev_base = 1200.0
    growth = [0.08, 0.06, 0.05]
    gross_margin = 0.45
    ebitda_margin = 0.22
    tax_rate = 0.25
    interest_rate = 0.055
    capex_pct = 0.05
    depr_rate = 0.15
    dso = 45.0
    dio = 50.0
    dpo = 40.0
    payout = 0.30

    # Initial Balance Sheet (2023)
    cash = 120.0
    ar = rev_base * (dso / 365.0)
    inv = rev_base * (1.0 - gross_margin) * (dio / 365.0)
    nppe = 480.0
    ap = rev_base * (1.0 - gross_margin) * (dpo / 365.0)
    debt = 320.0
    equity = (cash + ar + inv + nppe) - (ap + debt)

    rev = rev_base
    print("Year | Revenue | EBITDA | Net Inc | Ending Cash | Articulation Gap")
    print("-" * 65)

    for i, yr in enumerate(years):
        rev = rev * (1.0 + growth[i])
        cogs = rev * (1.0 - gross_margin)
        ebitda = rev * ebitda_margin
        depr = nppe * depr_rate
        ebit = ebitda - depr
        interest = debt * interest_rate
        ebt = ebit - interest
        tax = max(0.0, ebt * tax_rate)
        ni = ebt - tax
        div = ni * payout

        # Working Capital
        ar_next = rev * (dso / 365.0)
        inv_next = cogs * (dio / 365.0)
        ap_next = cogs * (dpo / 365.0)
        delta_nwc = (ar_next - ar) + (inv_next - inv) - (ap_next - ap)

        capex = rev * capex_pct
        nppe = nppe + capex - depr
        equity = equity + (ni - div)

        cfo = ni + depr - delta_nwc
        cfi = -capex
        cff = -div
        delta_cash = cfo + cfi + cff
        cash = cash + delta_cash

        ar = ar_next
        inv = inv_next
        ap = ap_next

        tot_assets = cash + ar + inv + nppe
        tot_liab_eq = ap + debt + equity
        gap = tot_assets - tot_liab_eq

        print(f"{yr} | {rev:7.1f} | {ebitda:6.1f} | {ni:7.1f} | {cash:11.2f} | {gap:16.10f}")


main()

Where the canon breaks

Standard financial textbooks assume that the clean surplus relation (\(\text{Equity}_t = \text{Equity}_{t-1} + \text{Net Income}_t - \text{Dividends}_t\)) holds without exception. In corporate reporting under GAAP and IFRS, clean surplus fails repeatedly:

  • Other Comprehensive Income (OCI): Unrealized gains and losses on available-for-sale securities, foreign currency translation adjustments (CTA), and pension plan remeasurements bypass the Income Statement and flow directly into Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income (AOCI). Models that ignore OCI produce non-articulating equity bridges.
  • Stock-Based Compensation (SBC): Non-cash stock option and RSU compensation expenses reduce operating income on the income statement while simultaneously increasing additional paid-in capital (APIC) on the balance sheet, diluting share count without draining cash.
  • Operating Leases (ASC Topic 842): Operating leases are capitalized on the balance sheet as Right-of-Use (ROU) assets and operating lease liabilities. Simple models that treat leases solely as operating rental expenses distort enterprise value and leverage metrics.

Misconceptions

  • Balancing a financial model by inserting an arbitrary "plug" on the balance sheet is standard institutional practice.

    Arbitrary plug variables obscure fundamental accounting defects. If a model requires an artificial balancing line, an operational cash flow, working capital delta, or retained earnings schedule is modeled incorrectly. A professionally constructed model articulates through structural cash roll-forward or revolving debt facilities.

  • Depreciation expense is a source of operational cash for the firm.

    Depreciation is a non-cash accounting allocation of historical equipment costs. It appears as an addition on the Statement of Cash Flows exclusively to reverse the non-cash subtraction executed on the Income Statement. Depreciation generates cash only indirectly via the interest and depreciation tax shield (\(\tau \times \text{Depr}\)).

  • A firm with rapid top-line revenue growth will naturally experience expanding cash balances.

    Rapid revenue expansion often triggers severe cash drainage due to working capital expansion. As receivables and inventory surge ahead of cash collections (the working capital trap), operating cash flows turn negative, requiring external liquidity injections or credit facility draws.

Problem set

A corporation reports Year 1 Revenue of $800M and Year 2 Revenue of $960M. Its DSO is constant at 45 days, DIO is 60 days on COGS (COGS is 60% of revenue), and DPO is 40 days on COGS. Calculate the change in Net Operating Working Capital (\(\Delta\text{NWC}\)) from Year 1 to Year 2.

Solution

In Year 1: \(\text{Rev}_1 = \$800\text{M}\), \(\text{COGS}_1 = 0.60 \times \$800\text{M} = \$480\text{M}\). Accounts Receivable is \(\text{AR}_1 = 800 \times \frac{45}{365} = \$98.63\text{M}\). Inventory is \(\text{Inv}_1 = 480 \times \frac{60}{365} = \$78.90\text{M}\). Accounts Payable is \(\text{AP}_1 = 480 \times \frac{40}{365} = \$52.60\text{M}\). Total \(\text{NWC}_1 = 98.63 + 78.90 - 52.60 = \$124.93\text{M}\).

In Year 2: \(\text{Rev}_2 = \$960\text{M}\), \(\text{COGS}_2 = 0.60 \times \$960\text{M} = \$576\text{M}\). \(\text{AR}_2 = 960 \times \frac{45}{365} = \$118.36\text{M}\). \(\text{Inv}_2 = 576 \times \frac{60}{365} = \$94.68\text{M}\). \(\text{AP}_2 = 576 \times \frac{40}{365} = \$63.12\text{M}\). Total \(\text{NWC}_2 = 118.36 + 94.68 - 63.12 = \$149.92\text{M}\).

The change in Net Working Capital is \(\Delta\text{NWC} = 149.92 - 124.93 = \$24.99\text{M}\). Operating cash flow is reduced by $24.99M to fund this working capital build.

A company has beginning Net PP&E of $400M. During the year, it invests $65M in capital expenditures. The annual straight-line depreciation rate applied to beginning Net PP&E is 12.5%. Calculate the depreciation expense and ending Net PP&E.

Solution

Depreciation expense is calculated on beginning fixed assets: \(\text{Depr} = 0.125 \times \$400\text{M} = \$50.00\text{M}\).

Ending Net PP&E is calculated via the fixed asset roll-forward: \(\text{Ending PP\&E} = \text{Beginning PP\&E} + \text{CapEx} - \text{Depr} = \$400\text{M} + \$65\text{M} - \$50\text{M} = \$415.00\text{M}\).

A firm earns Net Income of $72M and pays out $25M in cash dividends. If beginning Common Equity was $310M and there are no other equity transactions, compute ending Common Equity and verify clean surplus accounting.

Solution

Under clean surplus accounting, retained earnings addition is \(\text{Net Income} - \text{Dividends} = \$72\text{M} - \$25\text{M} = \$47\text{M}\).

Ending Common Equity equals \(\text{Beginning Equity} + \text{Retained Earnings Addition} = \$310\text{M} + \$47\text{M} = \$357.00\text{M}\).

Prove that if a firm's Cash Conversion Cycle (\(\text{CCC}\)) is strictly negative, an increase in sales volume produces an immediate positive operational cash inflow prior to supplier payments.

Solution

The Cash Conversion Cycle is defined as \(\text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO}\). If \(\text{CCC} < 0\), then \(\text{DPO} > \text{DSO} + \text{DIO}\).

Let \(\tau_{\text{collect}} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO}\) denote the total time required to process inventory and collect cash from customer sales. Let \(\tau_{\text{pay}} = \text{DPO}\) denote the trade credit grace period granted by suppliers.

Since \(\tau_{\text{collect}} < \tau_{\text{pay}}\), the firm collects cash from customers at time \(\tau_{\text{collect}}\) before it is legally required to disburse cash to trade vendors at \(\tau_{\text{pay}}\). For incremental sales volume \(\Delta S\), the firm receives operating cash inflow \(\Delta S\) at \(\tau_{\text{collect}}\) and holds this float until \(\tau_{\text{pay}}\), providing a temporary negative-working-capital financing subsidy.

Prove that capitalizing research and development (R&D) expenditures rather than immediately expensing them increases reported operating profit (EBIT) and net operating assets, but leaves total net cash flow over the full asset life unchanged.

Solution

Under immediate expensing, \(\text{EBIT}_t = \text{Revenue}_t - \text{Opex}_t - \text{R\&D}_t\). Under capitalization, R&D expenditure \(I_{\text{R\&D}, t}\) is treated as an investing cash outflow (\(\text{CFI}\)), and amortized over useful life \(L\) with annual amortization \(A_t = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{s=t-L+1}^t I_{\text{R\&D}, s}\). In year \(t\), reported \(\text{EBIT}^*_t = \text{Revenue}_t - \text{Opex}_t - A_t\). If R&D is growing, \(I_{\text{R\&D}, t} > A_t\), so \(\text{EBIT}^*_t > \text{EBIT}_t\).

On the Statement of Cash Flows, under expensing: \(\text{CFO} = \text{NI} + \text{Depr}\) (with R&D already deducted in NI). Under capitalization: \(\text{CFO}^* = \text{CFO} + I_{\text{R\&D}}\), while \(\text{CFI}^* = \text{CFI} - I_{\text{R\&D}}\). The net cash flow \(\Delta\text{Cash} = \text{CFO}^* + \text{CFI}^* = \text{CFO} + \text{CFI}\) is identical in every period.

Prove that the choice between using Cash as the balance sheet plug versus using Revolving Debt as the balance sheet plug induces a structural feedback loop on net income through the interest expense schedule.

Solution

Suppose Revolving Debt \(D_t\) is used to balance the balance sheet: \(D_t = \text{Assets}_t - \text{OtherLiab}_t - \text{Equity}_t\). Interest expense is a function of debt: \(\text{Int}_t = r_d \cdot D_t\). However, Net Income depends on interest: \(\text{NI}_t = (\text{EBIT}_t - \text{Int}_t)(1 - \tau)\). Furthermore, Equity depends on Net Income through retained earnings: \(\text{Equity}_t = \text{Equity}_{t-1} + \text{NI}_t - \text{Div}_t\).

Substituting gives: \(D_t = \text{Assets}_t - \text{OtherLiab}_t - [\text{Equity}_{t-1} + (\text{EBIT}_t - r_d D_t)(1 - \tau) - \text{Div}_t]\). Solving for \(D_t\):

\[ D_t [1 - r_d(1 - \tau)] = \text{Assets}_t - \text{OtherLiab}_t - \text{Equity}_{t-1} - \text{EBIT}_t(1 - \tau) + \text{Div}_t \]

Thus, circularity is resolved analytically or through iterative Gauss-Seidel convergence.

In a sample of 500 manufacturing corporations, the average cash conversion cycle is 68 days with standard deviation 22 days. If a target firm operates with DSO = 52 days, DIO = 74 days, and DPO = 35 days, compute its CCC and standard z-score relative to the manufacturing cross-section.

Solution

The target firm's cash conversion cycle is \(\text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} = 52 + 74 - 35 = 91\text{ days}\).

The standardized cross-sectional z-score is \(z = \frac{91 - 68}{22} = \frac{23}{22} = +1.045\). The firm ties up significantly more liquidity in its operating cycle than peer benchmarks, indicating potential working capital optimization opportunities.

Empirical corporate finance studies document that working capital management affects firm value. A firm with $2.0B in revenues reduces its DSO from 55 days to 42 days and DIO from 60 days to 48 days (COGS = 65% of revenue). Compute the one-time permanent liquidity release generated by this operational efficiency program.

Solution

Revenue is \(\$2,000\text{M}\); daily revenue is \(\frac{\$2,000\text{M}}{365} = \$5.479\text{M/day}\). COGS is \(0.65 \times \$2,000\text{M} = \$1,300\text{M}\); daily COGS is \(\frac{\$1,300\text{M}}{365} = \$3.562\text{M/day}\).

DSO reduction of \(55 - 42 = 13\text{ days}\) releases: \(\Delta\text{AR} = 13 \times \$5.479\text{M} = \$71.23\text{M}\).

DIO reduction of \(60 - 48 = 12\text{ days}\) releases: \(\Delta\text{Inv} = 12 \times \$3.562\text{M} = \$42.74\text{M}\).

Total permanent cash generated from working capital reduction: \(\$71.23\text{M} + \$42.74\text{M} = \$113.97\text{M}\).

An equity analyst projects that a firm's capital expenditure will remain at 7.0% of revenue for the next 5 years, while annual depreciation equals 15% of beginning net PP&E. If Year 1 revenue is $1,000M growing at 6.0% annually and beginning PP&E is $450M, compute the 5-year total net cash outflow for fixed capital investment (CapEx minus Depreciation).

Solution

We trace the PP&E roll-forward over 5 years. Year 1: Rev = $1,060M, CapEx = $74.20M, Depr = \(0.15 \times 450 = \$67.50\text{M}\), Ending PP&E = $456.70M, Net outflow = $6.70M.

Year 2: Rev = $1,123.60M, CapEx = $78.65M, Depr = \(0.15 \times 456.70 = \$68.51\text{M}\), Ending PP&E = $466.85M, Net outflow = $10.15M.

Year 3: Rev = $1,191.02M, CapEx = $83.37M, Depr = \(0.15 \times 466.85 = \$70.03\text{M}\), Ending PP&E = $480.19M, Net outflow = $13.34M.

Year 4: Rev = $1,262.48M, CapEx = $88.37M, Depr = \(0.15 \times 480.19 = \$72.03\text{M}\), Ending PP&E = $496.53M, Net outflow = $16.34M.

Year 5: Rev = $1,338.23M, CapEx = $93.68M, Depr = \(0.15 \times 496.53 = \$74.48\text{M}\), Ending PP&E = $515.73M, Net outflow = $19.20M.

Total 5-year net cash outflow equals \(\$6.70\text{M} + \$10.15\text{M} + \$13.34\text{M} + \$16.34\text{M} + \$19.20\text{M} = \$65.73\text{M}\).

Discuss how the adoption of IFRS 16 and ASC Topic 842 (bringing operating leases onto corporate balance sheets as right-of-use assets and lease liabilities) altered empirical capital structure research and debt-to-EBITDA multiples.

Solution

Prior to ASC 842 / IFRS 16, operating lease commitments were disclosed off-balance-sheet in footnotes. Analysts capitalized them using rule-of-thumb multiples (e.g. \(8\times\) rent expense). Bringing leases onto the balance sheet recognized trillions in gross liabilities across retail, aviation, and logistics sectors, causing reported Debt/EBITDA ratios to jump significantly.

Empirical corporate finance studies (e.g. evaluating debt covenants and credit ratings) demonstrate that while credit rating agencies had already adjusted for off-balance-sheet debt, bank credit agreements had to be renegotiated with "frozen GAAP" clauses to prevent technical covenant default.

Analyze the structural implications of non-articulation in automated SEC EDGAR parsing pipelines. Why do simple differences between consecutive balance sheet line items fail to match Cash Flow Statement figures in 40% of public corporate filings?

Solution

Non-articulation between balance sheet deltas and cash flow statement lines occurs due to three structural factors: (1) Corporate mergers and acquisitions: an acquired company's assets and liabilities appear as balance sheet changes without flowing through operating working capital lines; (2) Foreign currency translation adjustments: balance sheet items are translated at closing spot rates whereas cash flows are translated at weighted-average exchange rates; (3) Non-cash asset divestitures, asset write-downs, and lease modifications.

Automated parsers that compute working capital cash flows as simple \(\Delta\text{AR}\) or \(\Delta\text{Inv}\) introduce severe measurement error, corrupting econometric panel studies.

Formulate an automated algorithmic reconciliation protocol that extracts trial balances from XBRL filings and flags balance sheet non-articulation discrepancies exceeding a 0.01% materiality tolerance.

Solution

The reconciliation protocol defines a strict validation DAG: (1) Ingest XBRL tags for Assets, Liabilities, and Equity; (2) Calculate balance sheet identity discrepancy \(\epsilon_{\text{BS}} = |\text{Assets} - (\text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity})|\); (3) Check clean surplus identity \(\epsilon_{\text{CS}} = |\text{Equity}_t - (\text{Equity}_{t-1} + \text{NetIncome}_t - \text{Dividends}_t + \text{OCI}_t + \Delta\text{APIC}_t)|\); (4) Reconcile cash roll-forward \(\epsilon_{\text{CF}} = |\text{Cash}_t - (\text{Cash}_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t)|\).

If \(\max(\epsilon_{\text{BS}}, \epsilon_{\text{CS}}, \epsilon_{\text{CF}}) / \text{Assets}_t > 10^{-4}\), the pipeline halts automated ingestion, dumps discrepancy waterfall tables, and queues the filing for forensic manual restatement.

Retrieval quiz

  1. In three-statement modeling, how does an increase in Accounts Receivable affect the Statement of Cash Flows?

    An increase in accounts receivable means revenues were recognized on the income statement that have not yet been collected in cash. To reconcile net income to true cash flow from operations, the increase in receivables must be subtracted.

  2. What is the formula for the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)?

    The cash conversion cycle measures the days elapsed between paying for inventory inputs and collecting cash from customer sales: $\text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO}$.

  3. A firm has annual COGS of $730M and average inventory of $24.0M. What is its Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) in days?

    \(\text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 = \frac{24.0}{730.0} \times 365 = 12.0\text{ days}\).

  4. Under double-entry articulation, if ending cash on the balance sheet is set equal to beginning cash plus the net cash flow from the cash flow statement, what must the balance sheet check (Total Assets - Total Liabilities - Equity) evaluate to?

    As proven in Theorem 1.1, the cash flow identity enforces that the change in total assets matches the change in total liabilities plus equity identically, yielding a zero balance sheet check.

  5. Why does the clean surplus relationship fail when a company reports significant Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)?

    Clean surplus requires that equity changes solely through net income less dividends. OCI bypasses the income statement and flows directly into Accumulated OCI in stockholders' equity, breaking clean surplus.

  6. A firm has Operating Assets of $500M and Operating Liabilities of $150M. Its Financial Liabilities are $250M and Financial Assets are $50M. What is Common Shareholders Equity in millions of dollars?

    \(\text{NOA} = 500 - 150 = 350\text{M}\). \(\text{NFO} = 250 - 50 = 200\text{M}\). Common Equity equals \(\text{NOA} - \text{NFO} = 350 - 200 = 150\text{M}\).

  7. What occurs when a financial model uses an artificial balance sheet "plug" variable rather than dynamic cash roll-forward?

    Artificial plugging forces the balance sheet to appear balanced by creating phantom assets or liabilities, hiding underlying errors and breaking cash-debt feedback mechanisms.

  8. A company has Year 1 Net PP&E of $200M. It records $50M of CapEx and $15M of depreciation in Year 2. By how many millions of dollars did Net PP&E increase?

    \(\Delta\text{NPPE} = \text{CapEx} - \text{Depr} = 50 - 15 = 35.0\text{M}\).
