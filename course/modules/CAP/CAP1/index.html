Reading, Refereeing & Replicating

Empirical financial economics moves forward through critical peer review and computational verification. In this doctoral capstone module, you master the formal referee report as a scholarly genre, build production-grade replication packages satisfying top-tier journal data policies, and systematically decompose empirical divergences between published anomalies and independent replications.

  • Prerequisites: F5, S7, QR2
  • Unlocks: CAP4
  • Project: Replication package plus referee report, autograder and rubric

Objectives

  1. Evaluate empirical finance papers using the institutional peer review architecture, distinguishing fatal identification defects from fixable specification limitations per Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017) guidelines.
  2. Construct reproducible replication packages meeting the Data and Code Availability Standards of the American Economic Association and the Journal of Finance.
  3. Diagnose and decompose empirical divergences between published empirical tables and independent replications using step-by-step waterfall accounting across sample windows, share code filters, delisting returns, and weighting schemes.
  4. Implement Shumway (1997) delisting return adjustments, correcting for delisting bias in CRSP performance-related cessations.
  5. Audit econometric inference across alternative covariance estimators, comparing homoskedastic OLS, White heteroskedasticity-consistent, Newey-West HAC, and Petersen one-way and two-way clustered standard errors.
  6. Formulate constructive, rigorous referee reports that isolate causal mechanisms, evaluate specification curves, and provide actionable guidance to journal editors.

Prerequisites

This doctoral capstone builds directly upon foundational results in cross-sectional asset pricing, multiple testing inference, and quantitative backtesting architecture. Students are expected to apply concepts established in prerequisite units without introductory review:

  • From F5 (The Factor Zoo & Replication): the factor zoo, publication bias, the anomaly replication rate, and replication methodology choices.
  • From S7 (Research Integrity & Multiple Testing): data snooping bias, p-hacking, t-statistic hurdles, false discovery rate, and deflated Sharpe ratio.
  • From QR2 (Backtesting Architecture): vectorized backtest, event-driven backtester, and walk-forward analysis.
  • From core foundations: delisting bias, survivorship bias, and the GRS test.

Placement diagnostic

Test your readiness for doctoral replication research. Learners meeting the mastery threshold may proceed directly to the capstone project specification.

  1. Under the canonical Shumway (1997) methodology, a NASDAQ-listed stock delisted for performance reasons (code 580) with a missing delisting return (DLRET is NaN) receives what imputed delisting return before compounding with its regular return?

    Options

    Shumway (1997) demonstrates that missing delisting returns on NASDAQ performance delistings average -55%, whereas NYSE and AMEX performance delistings average -30%.

  2. In a systematic replication divergence diagnosis, an original paper reports a monthly long-short anomaly spread of 0.85%, while an independent replication obtains 0.42%. Sequential pipeline steps isolate sample alignment (-0.07%), share code filtering (-0.11%), Shumway delisting (-0.12%), and winsorization (-0.01%). What is the exact step delta attributable to the final step of value-weighting?

    Options

    Total divergence is \(0.42\% - 0.85\% = -0.43\%\). By the articulation identity, the sum of step deltas must equal total divergence: \(-0.07\% - 0.11\% - 0.12\% - 0.01\% + \Delta_{\text{vw}} = -0.43\%\), implying \(-0.31\% + \Delta_{\text{vw}} = -0.43\%\), so \(\Delta_{\text{vw}} = -0.12\%\).

  3. According to Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017), what is the most critical guideline regarding reviewer recommendations in referee reports?

    Options

    Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017) emphasize constructive reviewing: separating fatal identification flaws from optional extensions and refraining from imposing idiosyncratic author preferences.

  4. What empirical finding does Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) document regarding the replication of 452 published asset pricing anomalies?

    Options

    Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) show that 65% of published anomalies fail at the 5% level under value-weighting because published equal-weighted returns were disproportionately driven by tiny, illiquid microcaps.

  5. In a panel with \(N = 2400\) total observations, \(K = 2\) regressors, and \(G = 50\) clusters, what is the finite-sample degrees-of-freedom correction multiplier \(\frac{G}{G-1} \times \frac{N-1}{N-K}\) applied to the cluster covariance matrix?

    Calculation: \(\frac{50}{49} \times \frac{2399}{2398} = 1.020408 \times 1.000417 \approx 1.02085\).

Lessons

L01 · 12 h The Peer Review Architecture & The Referee Report The institutional economics of journal review, anatomy of a referee report, constructive vs destructive criticism, and identification triage. L02 · 12 h Anatomy of Identification & Econometric Scrutiny The garden of forking paths, specification curve analysis, errors-in-variables, and Shanken (1992) factor risk premia corrections. L03 · 12 h Computational Reproducibility & The Replication Package AEA and JF code policies, data manifests, containerized deterministic pipelines, and cryptographic verification of publication tables. L04 · 12 h Taxonomy of Empirical Divergences: Data, Filtering & Adjustments The 5 operational divergence modes: sample windows, share code filters, Shumway delisting bias, winsorization discipline, and weighting schemes. L05 · 12 h Statistical Inference, Clustering & Standard Error Divergences White HC0, Newey-West HAC, Petersen one-way and two-way clustered standard errors, finite-sample degrees-of-freedom corrections, and exact GRS tests. L06 · 12 h Replication in Practice: Open-Source Asset Pricing & Meta-Science Large-scale factor replication initiatives, post-publication return decay, reconciling empirical optimism and pessimism, and synthesizing the final audit.

The project ladder

  1. Worked example

    Walk through an end-to-end replication audit of a cross-sectional anomaly table on sample panel data, computing CRSP universe filters, Shumway delisting imputations, and waterfall divergence decompositions.

  2. Completion problem

    Complete the core missing steps of two-way clustered covariance matrix estimation and articulation gap verification in the interactive completion lab.

  3. Independent project

    Replicate an assigned empirical paper from open financial data, build a fully verifiable replication package, compute step-by-step divergence diagnostics, and author a comprehensive referee report. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the replicated anomaly across global multi-country equity panels, audit machine-learning factor models, and package containerized pipelines under deterministic Nix environments.
