The Peer Review Architecture & The Referee Report

Peer review is the foundational gatekeeper of quantitative finance. A doctoral researcher must not only survive review but execute it with rigorous objectivity. Understanding the formal anatomy of the referee report enables you to diagnose fatal identification failures, protect the scientific record from statistical noise, and provide actionable, constructive guidance to editors.

1The Institutional Architecture of Financial Economics Peer Review

Scientific publication in top-tier finance journals (such as the Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, and Review of Financial Studies) operates under a delegated principal-agent hierarchy. Editors handle editorial triage, issuing desk rejections for manuscripts lacking theoretical grounding or empirical novelty. When a manuscript survives initial screening, the Editor assigns it to an Associate Editor or directly to external referees who serve as anonymous technical adjudicators.

Referees evaluate whether the empirical findings are credible, whether the economic mechanism is identified, and whether the paper makes an incremental contribution beyond existing canonical literature. The referee acts as an advisor to the editor rather than a final decision-maker; the ultimate editorial decision rests with the editor, who weighs multiple referee assessments against journal portfolio constraints. However, because editors face acute time constraints and information asymmetry regarding complex empirical codebases, the referee's evaluation exerts decisive influence.

The institutional structure of academic publishing also introduces systemic friction. Because career advancement and tenure depend heavily on top-tier publications, researchers face intense pressure to discover statistically significant results. This structural incentive generates severe publication bias, resulting in a pronounced discontinuity in published test statistics just above the canonical threshold of \(t = 1.96\). Referees must maintain vigilant scrutiny to distinguish robust economic discoveries from artifacts generated by specification searching and data p-hacking.

2Formal Anatomy of the Referee Report

Definition 1.1 (Referee Report)

A referee report is a formal, confidential scholarly critique commissioned by a journal editor to evaluate the technical validity, causal identification, econometric rigor, and scientific contribution of an unreleased research manuscript. It comprises two functionally distinct components: confidential remarks to the editor providing a clear publication recommendation and risk assessment, and a report to the authors detailing substantive major critiques and constructive minor refinements.

A professional referee report adheres strictly to a bipartite structure:

  • Part 1: Confidential Remarks to the Editor: Contains an unambiguous recommendation (Reject, Major Revision / Revise & Resubmit, Minor Revision, or Accept), an executive appraisal of whether the paper's core hypothesis is plausible, an assessment of whether the empirical results can be salvaged under alternative specifications, and a direct statement of any ethical or data-integrity concerns.
  • Part 2: Report to the Authors: Structured into three sequential sections:
    1. Executive Summary: A neutral, objective restatement of the paper's central research question, empirical design, proposed mechanism, and main empirical findings. This demonstrates to both the editor and the authors that the referee has fully comprehended the manuscript.
    2. Major Comments: Numbered, prioritized critiques addressing fundamental vulnerabilities. These encompass causal identification failure, look-ahead bias in signal formation, mechanical accounting artifacts, unaddressed omitted variables, and failure to account for multiple hypothesis testing.
    3. Minor Comments: Constructive observations regarding exposition, notation conventions, standalone table clarity, minor derivation steps, and missing citations.

3Constructive Refereeing & Identification Triage

In their influential treatise on scientific review, Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017) articulate the core ethical standard of peer review: the referee's role is to improve the scientific quality of research through objective, constructive evaluation, avoiding personal hobby-horses and excessive demands for peripheral robustness checks.

A vital intellectual skill of the referee is identification triage: distinguishing between fatal methodological flaws that cannot be cured by revision, and fixable empirical limitations that can be addressed in a revise-and-resubmit cycle.

Theorem 1.1 (Omitted Variable Bias in Factor Regressions)

Let an asset pricing test evaluate an empirical model where excess returns \(R_i\) are projected onto an observed characteristic or factor \(X_i\): \[ R_i = \alpha + \beta X_i + \epsilon_i \] Suppose the true data-generating process includes an unobserved systematic factor \(Z_i\) such that \(\E[\epsilon_i \mid X_i, Z_i] = 0\): \[ R_i = \alpha_0 + \gamma X_i + \delta Z_i + u_i \] Then the OLS estimator \(\hat{\beta}\) from the univariate regression converges in probability to: \[ \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta} = \gamma + \delta \frac{\Cov(X_i, Z_i)}{\Var(X_i)} \] In particular, if the omitted factor carries a non-zero risk premium (\(\delta \neq 0\)) and is correlated with the candidate signal (\(\Cov(X_i, Z_i) \neq 0\)), the estimated return premium \(\hat{\beta}\) is asymptotically biased and inconsistent.

Proof

Under OLS estimation of the univariate specification, the slope estimator is: \[ \hat{\beta} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})(R_i - \bar{R})}{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})^2} = \frac{\widehat{\Cov}(X_i, R_i)}{\widehat{\Var}(X_i)} \] Substituting the true structural relation \(R_i = \alpha_0 + \gamma X_i + \delta Z_i + u_i\) yields: \[ \widehat{\Cov}(X_i, R_i) = \gamma \widehat{\Var}(X_i) + \delta \widehat{\Cov}(X_i, Z_i) + \widehat{\Cov}(X_i, u_i) \] Dividing by \(\widehat{\Var}(X_i)\): \[ \hat{\beta} = \gamma + \delta \frac{\widehat{\Cov}(X_i, Z_i)}{\widehat{\Var}(X_i)} + \frac{\widehat{\Cov}(X_i, u_i)}{\widehat{\Var}(X_i)} \] By the weak law of large numbers, as \(N \to \infty\), sample covariances converge in probability to their population counterparts. Since \(\E[u_i \mid X_i] = 0\) by assumption, \(\text{plim} \, \widehat{\Cov}(X_i, u_i) = 0\). Thus: \[ \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta} = \gamma + \delta \frac{\Cov(X_i, Z_i)}{\Var(X_i)} \] The omitted variable bias term \(\delta \frac{\Cov(X_i, Z_i)}{\Var(X_i)}\) is non-zero whenever \(\delta \neq 0\) and \(\Cov(X_i, Z_i) \neq 0\).

Remark 1.2 (Fatal vs Fixable Flaws)

A fatal identification flaw occurs when \(\Cov(X_i, u_i) \neq 0\) due to simultaneity, mechanical accounting definitions (e.g. regressing earnings on components of earnings), or forward-looking information in signal formation. These cannot be cured by adjusting standard errors. Conversely, an omitted factor \(Z_i\) with observable proxies, or failure to cluster standard errors across panel entities, represents a fixable flaw that a constructive referee addresses by specifying exact orthogonalization tests.

Lab 1 · Automated referee specification audit and flaw classifier

This lab audits four candidate research paper specifications, scanning for look-ahead signal contamination, mechanical accounting artifacts, reverse causality, and unclustered standard errors, classifying each manuscript into an editorial recommendation.

"""Lab 1: Peer review specification scanner and identification flaw classifier."""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class PaperAudit:
    title: str
    has_reverse_causality: bool
    has_lookahead_leakage: bool
    has_mechanical_identity: bool
    has_clustered_se: bool
    n_sample_splits: int


def classify_referee_recommendation(audit: PaperAudit) -> tuple[str, list[str]]:
    fatal_flaws: list[str] = []
    major_flaws: list[str] = []

    if audit.has_lookahead_leakage:
        fatal_flaws.append("FATAL: Signal formation uses future information (look-ahead bias).")
    if audit.has_mechanical_identity:
        fatal_flaws.append("FATAL: Result is driven by an accounting identity rather than economic behavior.")
    if audit.has_reverse_causality:
        major_flaws.append("MAJOR: Endogeneity via reverse causality unaddressed by instrument or design.")
    if not audit.has_clustered_se:
        major_flaws.append("MAJOR: Standard errors fail to account for cross-sectional or serial correlation.")
    if audit.n_sample_splits < 2:
        major_flaws.append("MINOR: Lacks sub-period temporal stability checks.")

    if fatal_flaws:
        decision = "REJECT (Unsalvageable Identification Flaws)"
    elif len(major_flaws) >= 2:
        decision = "REVISE & RESUBMIT (Substantive Econometric Overhaul Required)"
    elif major_flaws:
        decision = "MINOR REVISION (Address Clustered Inference & Robustness)"
    else:
        decision = "ACCEPT (Empirically Credible)"

    return decision, fatal_flaws + major_flaws


def main() -> None:
    papers = [
        PaperAudit("Paper A (Lookahead Momentum)", False, True, False, True, 3),
        PaperAudit("Paper B (Accounting Artifact)", False, False, True, True, 2),
        PaperAudit("Paper C (Unclustered Panel)", True, False, False, False, 1),
        PaperAudit("Paper D (Clean Factor Design)", False, False, False, True, 4),
    ]

    for p in papers:
        decision, comments = classify_referee_recommendation(p)
        print(f"Audit: {p.title}")
        print(f"Recommendation: {decision}")
        for c in comments:
            print(f"  - {c}")
        print()


main()

  - FATAL: Signal formation uses future information (look-ahead bias).

Audit: Paper B (Accounting Artifact)
Recommendation: REJECT (Unsalvageable Identification Flaws)
  - FATAL: Result is driven by an accounting identity rather than economic behavior.

Audit: Paper C (Unclustered Panel)

Notice that fatal identification defects such as look-ahead signal contamination immediately trigger an outright rejection recommendation. Papers with unclustered standard errors or unaddressed endogeneity receive constructive Revise & Resubmit or Minor Revision guidance specifying the required econometric fixes.

4Where the Canon Breaks

The traditional canonical view portrays peer review as a dispassionate, truth-seeking filter that reliably admits valid scientific findings while rejecting spurious anomalies. In practice, this idealized mechanism breaks down due to structural agency conflicts:

  • Reviewer Risk Aversion: Referees often reject genuinely novel, paradigm-shifting papers because non-standard methodologies do not fit conventional templates, favoring incremental modifications of established factor models.
  • Hobby-Horse Proliferation: Referees frequently demand extensive battery testing and idiosyncratic robustness checks that serve the referee's research agenda rather than clarifying the paper's core hypothesis (Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer 2017).
  • The File-Drawer and Significance Filter: Null results and precise non-rejections are routinely rejected by journals, systematically compelling researchers to engage in specification search and p-hacking to clear the hurdle of \(t \ge 2.0\).

5Misconceptions

  1. “The primary objective of a referee report is to uncover reasons to reject the manuscript.”

    Peer review is not an adversarial execution; its primary scholarly function is constructive development. Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017) show that excessive referee hostility degrades the scientific literature by suppressing unconventional ideas and incentivizing authors to conceal limitations rather than transparently addressing them.

  2. “Any endogeneity or omitted variable bias can be resolved simply by adding a large vector of control variables.”

    Adding control variables blindly can induce collider stratification bias or bad controls that exacerbate rather than attenuate bias. If an added covariate is an outcome of the treatment variable, conditioning on it distorts the estimated causal effect.

  3. “A paper with a reported t-statistic exceeding 3.5 on its primary asset pricing spread is guaranteed to replicate.”

    Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) demonstrate that many published anomalies with high in-sample t-statistics fail to replicate under value-weighting or out-of-sample periods because the original significance was driven by microcap extremes and lookahead delisting omissions.

6Problem Set

Mechanics

M1 · Bipartite Referee Report Partitioning

A referee drafts a critique stating: "The author is an assistant professor whose tenure case depends on this paper; however, the empirical standard errors must be clustered by firm to account for persistent residuals." Classify each element of this statement into its proper structural component of a referee report.

Solution

The referee report must be strictly partitioned into two sections per Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017). Personal details regarding the author's academic rank, tenure status, or institutional affiliation belong strictly in the confidential remarks to the editor (if relevant to conflicts of interest), and must never appear in the report to the authors. The technical critique concerning clustered standard errors belongs in the major comments section of the report to the authors, accompanied by a constructive econometric citation such as Petersen (2009).

M2 · Omitted Variable Bias Magnitude

Suppose an asset pricing regression of excess return on an environmental disclosure index \(X\) yields \(\hat{\beta} = 0.40\%\) per month. An omitted firm governance factor \(Z\) has a true return loading \(\delta = 0.30\%\). The variance of \(X\) is \(1.5\) and the covariance between \(X\) and \(Z\) is \(1.0\). Calculate the true direct return premium \(\gamma\) of environmental disclosure.

Solution

By Theorem 1.1, \(\text{plim} \, \hat{\beta} = \gamma + \delta \frac{\Cov(X, Z)}{\Var(X)}\). Substituting the stated values: \[ 0.40\% = \gamma + 0.30\% \times \left( \frac{1.0}{1.5} \right) = \gamma + 0.30\% \times 0.6667 = \gamma + 0.20\% \] Solving for \(\gamma\): \[ \gamma = 0.40\% - 0.20\% = 0.20\% \text{ per month} \] Half of the observed empirical return spread was an artifact of omitted variable bias.

M3 · Delisting Impact on Short-Leg Portfolios

In a long-short distress anomaly, the short leg contains 100 stocks. During month \(t\), 5 stocks delist for poor performance. The reported unadjusted monthly return is \(-10.0\%\) for all 100 stocks. If the 5 delisting stocks actually suffer an additional \(-30.0\%\) delisting return, calculate the corrected equal-weighted short-leg return.

Solution

For the 95 surviving stocks, the return is \(-10.0\%\). For the 5 delisting stocks, the compounded return is: \[ R_{\text{adj}} = (1 - 0.10) \times (1 - 0.30) - 1 = 0.90 \times 0.70 - 1 = 0.63 - 1 = -37.0\% \] The corrected equal-weighted portfolio return is: \[ \bar{R} = \frac{95 \times (-10.0\%) + 5 \times (-37.0\%)}{100} = \frac{-950\% - 185\%}{100} = -11.35\% \] Omitting delisting returns causes the short leg return to be overstated (less negative) by 135 basis points.

Proof

P1 · Sign of Omitted Variable Bias

Prove that if an omitted factor \(Z\) carries a positive risk premium (\(\delta > 0\)) and is positively correlated with the candidate anomaly signal \(X\) (\(\Cov(X, Z) > 0\)), the univariate regression estimator \(\hat{\beta}\) always overstates the true direct characteristic effect (\(\hat{\beta} > \gamma\)).

Solution

From Theorem 1.1, the asymptotic bias is given by: \[ \text{Bias} = \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta} - \gamma = \delta \frac{\Cov(X, Z)}{\Var(X)} \] Since \(\Var(X) > 0\) for any non-degenerate variable, the sign of the bias is determined by the numerator \(\delta \Cov(X, Z)\). By assumption, \(\delta > 0\) and \(\Cov(X, Z) > 0\). Therefore, their product is strictly positive: \[ \delta \frac{\Cov(X, Z)}{\Var(X)} > 0 \implies \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta} > \gamma \] The univariate estimator asymptotically overstates the true effect \(\gamma\).

P2 · Invariance of Residual Orthogonality under Linear Transformations

Let \(X\) be an \(N \times K\) matrix of full column rank, and let \(M_X = I - X(X'X)^{-1}X'\) be the orthogonal projection matrix. Prove that for any invertible \(K \times K\) matrix \(A\), the projection matrix onto the column space of \(X^* = XA\) is identical to \(M_X\).

Solution

Let \(P_{X^*} = X^*(X^{*\prime} X^*)^{-1} X^{*\prime}\). Substituting \(X^* = XA\): \[ P_{X^*} = (XA) \left( (XA)' (XA) \right)^{-1} (XA)' = XA \left( A' X' X A \right)^{-1} A' X' \] Since \(A\) is invertible and square (\(K \times K\)), the inverse of the matrix product expands as \((A' (X'X) A)^{-1} = A^{-1} (X'X)^{-1} (A')^{-1}\). Substituting: \[ P_{X^*} = X A A^{-1} (X'X)^{-1} (A')^{-1} A' X' = X I (X'X)^{-1} I X' = X(X'X)^{-1}X' = P_X \] Therefore, \(M_{X^*} = I - P_{X^*} = I - P_X = M_X\). Linear transformations of regressors leave the residual space and projection matrices invariant.

P3 · Attenuation Bias under Classical Measurement Error

Suppose an observed firm signal \(X_i^* = X_i + \nu_i\), where \(X_i\) is the true latent signal and \(\nu_i \sim \iid (0, \sigma_\nu^2)\) is classical measurement error independent of \(X_i\) and the regression disturbance \(\epsilon_i\). Prove that the OLS slope estimator \(\hat{\beta}^*\) from regressing \(R_i = \alpha + \beta X_i^* + e_i\) is strictly attenuated toward zero.

Solution

The probability limit of \(\hat{\beta}^*\) is: \[ \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta}^* = \frac{\Cov(X_i^*, R_i)}{\Var(X_i^*)} \] Since \(X_i^* = X_i + \nu_i\) and \(\Cov(\nu_i, X_i) = \Cov(\nu_i, \epsilon_i) = 0\): \[ \Cov(X_i^*, R_i) = \Cov(X_i + \nu_i, \alpha + \beta X_i + \epsilon_i) = \beta \Var(X_i) \] The variance of the noisy regressor is: \[ \Var(X_i^*) = \Var(X_i + \nu_i) = \Var(X_i) + \sigma_\nu^2 \] Dividing covariance by variance yields: \[ \text{plim} \, \hat{\beta}^* = \beta \left( \frac{\Var(X_i)}{\Var(X_i) + \sigma_\nu^2} \right) = \beta \lambda_{\text{rel}} \] Where the reliability ratio \(\lambda_{\text{rel}} = \frac{\Var(X_i)}{\Var(X_i) + \sigma_\nu^2}\). For any \(\sigma_\nu^2 > 0\), \(\lambda_{\text{rel}} \in (0, 1)\), which guarantees that \(|\text{plim} \, \hat{\beta}^*| < |\beta|\), proving strict attenuation toward zero.

Empirical

E1 · Auditing a Cross-Sectional Momentum Table

A submitted manuscript reports a Fama-French 3-factor alpha of 92 basis points per month (\(t = 3.65\)) for a 12-month momentum strategy. However, the author constructs signals at month-end \(t\) and assumes trade execution at the month-end \(t\) closing price. Explain the empirical vulnerability and how a referee must instruct the author to rectify it.

Solution

The specification suffers from look-ahead leakage and unmodeled execution latency. Executing trades at month-end \(t\) using prices measured at month-end \(t\) requires instantaneous rebalancing without transaction latency. In practical asset pricing, signals formed at month-end \(t\) must be implemented at month-end \(t+1\) open or close, incorporating at least a 1-day or 1-month lag to prevent mechanical bid-ask bounce and look-ahead bias. The referee must require a 1-month skip between signal formation and portfolio formation (the standard 12-2 momentum formation period of Jegadeesh & Titman 1993).

E2 · Evaluating Survivorship in Hedge Fund Panels

An empirical study analyzes hedge fund alphas from 2000 to 2024 using a commercial database. The author filters the dataset by selecting only funds currently active in 2024 with at least 5 years of historical returns. Evaluate the severity of this survivorship filter from the referee's perspective.

Solution

This is a catastrophic survivorship bias defect that represents a fatal flaw. By conditioning the sample on survival until 2024, funds that liquidated due to catastrophic losses during the 2000 dot-com collapse, the 2008 financial crisis, or the 2020 pandemic are mechanically purged from the panel. This drastically inflates mean historical returns and alphas. A referee must flag this as an irremediable defect in the current draft and require reconstruction of the panel using both active and defunct graveyard files.

E3 · Reconciling T-Statistic Thresholds

A cross-sectional factor paper discovers a new ESG momentum anomaly with an OLS t-statistic of 2.15 over the 1995–2020 sample. Evaluate this finding against the multiple testing literature standards of Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016).

Solution

Under classical single-hypothesis testing, \(t = 2.15\) clears the two-tailed 5% critical value of 1.96. However, Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) show that over 400 factor anomalies have been published in top finance journals. In the presence of hundreds of investigated factors, the appropriate hurdle must be adjusted upward to account for data snooping, requiring \(t \ge 3.0\) to maintain an acceptable false discovery rate. A t-statistic of 2.15 has a high posterior probability of being a false discovery.

Research

R1 · Designing a Constructive R&R Plan

An author submits an innovative paper proposing that supply chain complexity predicts corporate bond yield spreads. The referee identifies that the complexity metric is correlated with firm size and credit rating, and the regressions use heteroskedasticity-consistent White standard errors rather than clustering. Outline the specific revisions necessary to transform this into an acceptable paper.

Solution

A constructive referee report should outline four specific steps: (1) Re-estimate the panel regressions including industry-by-year fixed effects and explicit controls for log market equity, leverage, interest coverage, and credit rating fixed effects. (2) Replace White standard errors with two-way clustered standard errors by firm and month per Petersen (2009). (3) Conduct an orthogonalization exercise where supply chain complexity is residualized against all observable balance sheet characteristics before testing predictive power. (4) Perform sub-sample splits examining investment-grade vs high-yield bonds to verify the economic mechanism.

R2 · Pre-Registration vs Retrospective Refereeing

How would the adoption of pre-registered empirical protocols (similar to registered reports in psychology and medicine) alter the primary responsibilities and failure modes of referees in financial economics?

Solution

Pre-registration fundamentally shifts peer review from outcome evaluation to design evaluation. Referees evaluate the conceptual soundness, statistical power, data cleansing pipeline, and identification strategy before empirical data is analyzed. This eliminates p-hacking, publication bias against null results, and retrospective storytelling. The failure mode of refereeing shifts from suppressing true nulls to potentially stifling post-hoc exploratory discovery if protocols are enforced too rigidly.

R3 · Structural Incentives of Open Code Policies

Analyze how mandatory code and data deposit policies (such as the AEA Data Editor audit guidelines) change author behavior during the initial submission phase.

Solution

Mandatory replication policies raise the expected reputational cost of sloppy or fraudulent empirical work. Authors invest significantly more upfront effort in modular software architecture, deterministic version control, and data provenance tracking. However, it also creates an incentive for authors to avoid proprietary or restricted-access datasets where third-party replication is legally prohibited, potentially biasing empirical research toward standardized public databases like CRSP and Compustat.

8Retrieval Quiz

  1. What are the two mandatory, structurally distinct components of a formal referee report?

    Options

    A referee report is partitioned into confidential remarks providing the editor with an unvarnished publication recommendation, and a constructive technical report delivered to the authors.

  2. According to Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017), what should a referee do when encountering a manuscript limitation that does not invalidate the paper's core hypothesis?

    Options

    Referees should rigorously distinguish fatal flaws from secondary limitations, avoiding scope creep and excessive demands for peripheral tests.

  3. Which of the following represents a fatal identification defect in an empirical cross-sectional asset pricing paper?

    Options

    Look-ahead bias mechanically leaks future information into historical signals, fatally contaminating all downstream pricing tests.

  4. What empirical phenomenon is documented by Chen & Zimmermann (2020) regarding published cross-sectional anomalies?

    Options

    Chen & Zimmermann (2020) show that publication bias leads to an unnatural surge in published t-statistics just clearing the 5% threshold.

  5. In a univariate regression, \(\hat{\beta} = 0.45\%\). The true model has an omitted variable \(Z\) with loading \(\delta = 0.50\%\). If \(\Cov(X, Z) = 0.6\) and \(\Var(X) = 1.0\), what is the true structural slope \(\gamma\) in percent?

    \(\gamma = \hat{\beta} - \delta \frac{\Cov(X, Z)}{\Var(X)} = 0.45\% - 0.50\% \times 0.6 = 0.45\% - 0.30\% = 0.15\%\).

  6. What is the recommended t-statistic hurdle proposed by Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) for novel factor discovery in financial economics?

    Options

    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) argue that due to extensive data snooping across hundreds of published factors, the cutoff should be raised to \(t \ge 3.0\).

  7. Why does a referee report open its report to the authors with an Executive Summary restating the paper?

    Options

    An accurate executive summary reassures authors and editors that critiques are grounded in a sound understanding of the paper.

  8. When an empirical finding is driven entirely by equal-weighted returns of microcaps, how should a referee evaluate the economic mechanism?

    Options

    Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) and Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015) show that anomalies concentrated in microcaps often fail after real-world transaction costs.

  9. What is collider stratification bias (conditioning on a post-treatment variable)?

    Options

    Conditioning on a collider variable opens a backdoor path that induces spurious correlation between otherwise independent variables.

  10. What minimum t-statistic hurdle does Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) suggest for a new empirical factor discovery?

    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) recommend raising the hurdle to \(t \ge 3.0\).
