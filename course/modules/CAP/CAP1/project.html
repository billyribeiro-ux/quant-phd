Replication Package & Referee Report

You receive an empirical cross-sectional asset pricing paper submitted to a premier finance journal. Your task is twofold: first, construct an automated, deterministic replication package that replicates the paper's core table from raw panel data, documenting and decomposing every empirical divergence; second, author a formal, constructive referee report evaluating identification, data filtering, and econometric inference.

The ladder

At the doctoral tier, rungs 1 and 2 are focused on core operational mechanics per the expertise reversal literature demonstrating that experienced learners gain maximum mastery through targeted problem-solving rather than extensive scaffolding. Complete the worked example and completion problem before beginning the independent capstone.

Rungs 1 and 2

Lab 1 · Worked example: End-to-end replication audit

This worked example demonstrates the 4-step pipeline: filtering ordinary US common shares, compounding Shumway delisting penalties, calculating value-weighted portfolio returns, and computing the divergence wedge against the published target.

"""Lab Project 1 (Worked Example): End-to-end replication audit and divergence decomposition."""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    # 5 stocks, 1 month
    permnos = np.array([10001, 10002, 10003, 10004, 10005])
    shrcd = np.array([11, 10, 31, 11, 12])  # 31 is REIT, 12 is foreign ADR
    exchcd = np.array([1, 2, 1, 3, 1])
    raw_ret = np.array([0.05, -0.02, 0.15, 0.08, -0.10])
    dlret = np.array([np.nan, np.nan, np.nan, -0.40, np.nan])
    dlstcd = np.array([0, 0, 0, 580, 0])  # Stock 4 delisted for performance
    me = np.array([500.0, 1500.0, 200.0, 300.0, 800.0])

    # 1. Share code filtering (SHRCD 10/11 only)
    valid_mask = np.isin(shrcd, [10, 11]) & np.isin(exchcd, [1, 2, 3])
    print(f"Step 1: Universe Filter: Retained {np.sum(valid_mask)} of {len(permnos)} stocks (excluded REIT and ADR).")

    # 2. Shumway Delisting Adjustment on filtered stocks
    adj_ret = np.copy(raw_ret)
    for i in range(len(permnos)):
        if dlstcd[i] > 0:
            d_ret = dlret[i]
            if np.isnan(d_ret):
                d_ret = -0.55 if exchcd[i] == 3 else -0.30
            adj_ret[i] = (1.0 + raw_ret[i]) * (1.0 + d_ret) - 1.0

    s4_raw = raw_ret[3]
    s4_adj = adj_ret[3]
    s4_dl = dlret[3]
    print(f"Step 2: Delist Adj on Stock 4: Raw {s4_raw:+.2f} -> Compounded {s4_adj:+.4f} (DLRET = {s4_dl:.2f})")

    # 3. Portfolio Weighting: Equal-weighted vs Value-weighted on valid stocks
    valid_ret = adj_ret[valid_mask]
    valid_me = me[valid_mask]

    ew_ret = float(np.mean(valid_ret))
    weights = valid_me / np.sum(valid_me)
    vw_ret = float(np.sum(weights * valid_ret))

    print(f"Step 3: Equal-Weighted Return: {ew_ret * 100:.3f}%")
    print(f"        Value-Weighted Return: {vw_ret * 100:.3f}%")
    print(f"        Weighting Wedge: {(vw_ret - ew_ret) * 100:+.3f}%")

    # 4. Articulation breakdown
    published_target = 0.040  # 4.0%
    divergence = vw_ret - published_target
    print(f"Step 4: Divergence from target ({published_target * 100:.1f}%): {divergence * 100:+.3f}%")


main()

Notice that filtering out REITs and ADRs removes extreme outliers, while compounding the -40% delisting return on Stock 4 transforms a raw +8% return into a severe -35.2% loss. Value-weighting further dampens microcap volatility, yielding a clean divergence breakdown.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A submitted research manuscript claims to discover a novel persistent anomaly yielding 85 basis points per month in long-short spread returns with a reported t-statistic of 3.84 under equal-weighting and unadjusted CRSP returns. As an independent referee commissioned by the journal, you must execute a computational replication audit of Table 1 using raw panel data, evaluate the credibility of the empirical claims, and author an authoritative referee report assessing whether the findings represent a genuine risk premium, an anomaly driven by microcap illiquidity, or an artifact of delisting survivorship bias.
Inputs
A panel of equity securities across \(T\) months containing stock identifiers (permnos), monthly timestamps (months), CRSP share codes (shrcd), exchange codes (exchcd), unadjusted monthly returns (raw_ret), delisting codes (dlstcd), delisting returns (dlret), market equity market capitalizations (me), predictive characteristic signals (signal), and benchmark market/factor series (mkt, smb, hml).
Required outputs
Two unified deliverables:
  1. An autograded Python module replication_engine.py implementing:
    • apply_share_code_filter: CRSP share code (10/11) and major exchange (1/2/3) filtering.
    • apply_delisting_adjustment: Shumway (1997) compounding and missing return penalty imputation (-30% NYSE/AMEX, -55% NASDAQ).
    • winsorize_cross_section: Monthly cross-sectional outlier clipping.
    • form_sorted_portfolios: Quintile portfolio sort and long-short spread construction under equal-weighting and value-weighting.
    • estimate_regression: Factor regression estimation with OLS, White HC0, Newey-West HAC, and clustered standard errors with degrees-of-freedom corrections.
    • decompose_replication_divergence: Sequential waterfall decomposition satisfying exact articulation.
    • verify_manifest: Cryptographic SHA-256 package verification.
  2. A formal 3-to-5 page written referee report formatted according to Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017), containing:
    • Part 1: Confidential cover letter to the Editor with recommendation (Reject, Major Revision, Minor Revision, Accept) and concise rationale.
    • Part 2: Report to the Authors with an objective Executive Summary, prioritized Major Comments (identification, data filters, inference), and constructive Minor Comments.
Constraints
Deterministic execution under Python 3.14 with NumPy; zero look-ahead bias across time; strict cross-sectional winsorization per period; exact articulation discrepancy zero; all claims in the referee report must cite exact table panels, equations, and literature registry keys.
Grading mode
The curriculum grades this project by both autograder and rubric: the computational module is graded automatically against deterministic pytest properties across 50 random seeds; the written referee report is graded by rubric across 5 criteria and four performance levels, calibrated against annotated exemplars.

Exemplar · Level 4 (Exemplary Referee Report)

Part 1: Confidential Remarks to the Editor

Recommendation: Reject.

Summary Rationale: While the paper addresses an interesting question regarding predictive signals in cross-sectional equity returns, the empirical findings do not survive basic data integrity checks. Specifically, as documented in my replication audit, the reported long-short spread of 85 bps/month (\(t = 3.84\)) is entirely an artifact of equal-weighting microcap stocks and omitting delisting return adjustments. When standard Shumway (1997) delisting corrections are applied and returns are value-weighted across NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ common stocks, the spread shrinks to 18 bps/month (\(t = 1.12\)), rendering the anomaly economically and statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Part 2: Report to the Authors

Executive Summary: The manuscript proposes a novel characteristic signal based on firm operating momentum and asserts that a zero-cost long-short decile portfolio generates 85 basis points per month in abnormal alpha. The authors claim this spread cannot be explained by standard multi-factor models and represents evidence of behavioral underreaction.

Major Comments:

  1. Delisting Return Bias: In Table 1, the authors compute monthly returns from the CRSP monthly stock file without compounding delisting returns (DLRET). Per Shumway (1997), missing delisting returns for performance-related delisting codes (500, 520, 551–574, 580, 584) average -30% for NYSE/AMEX and -55% for NASDAQ issues. In the short leg (Decile 1), approximately 4.2% of firm-months experience performance delistings. Imputing standard delisting penalties reduces the long-short spread by 17 basis points per month (\(\Delta_{\text{delist}} = -0.17\%\)).
  2. Microcap Domination & Portfolio Weighting: The reported results rely exclusively on equal-weighted returns without NYSE size breakpoints. Following Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020), microcaps represent over 60% of the cross-section by firm count but less than 3% of aggregate market value. Re-estimating Table 1 under value-weighting reduces the monthly spread from 0.65% to 0.18% (\(t = 1.12\)). The anomaly exists solely in illiquid securities where transaction costs exceed gross profits per Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015).
  3. Standard Error Clustering: The pooled panel regression in Table 3 reports OLS standard errors (\(t = 3.42\)). Estimating two-way clustered standard errors by firm and month per Petersen (2009) inflates the standard error from 0.0021 to 0.0048, dropping the t-statistic to 1.48.

Minor Comments: Table 1 lacks explicit units in the column headers; equation 4 omits time subscript indexing; the sample description does not state whether closed-end funds (SHRCD 12) were filtered.

Exemplar · Level 2 (Developing Referee Report)

Part 1: Confidential Remarks to the Editor

Recommendation: Major Revision.

Summary Rationale: The paper has some interesting empirical results but I could not replicate the exact numbers in Table 1. My numbers were smaller. The authors need to check their code and do more robustness tests.

Part 2: Report to the Authors

General Assessment: This paper investigates whether an equity signal predicts returns. The authors find positive results. However, I have several concerns with their methodology.

Comments:

  1. I tried replicating Table 1 using CRSP data and got a return spread of about 0.50% instead of 0.85%. The authors should explain why my replication does not match their published table.
  2. The paper uses equal-weighted portfolios. The authors should also show value-weighted returns because equal-weighting is sometimes criticized in the literature.
  3. The authors should run their regressions with different standard errors, including Newey-West and clustered standard errors, and see if the t-statistics stay above 2.0.
  4. There are several typos in Section 2, and Figure 1 is difficult to read. The authors should improve their figures and cite more papers on momentum.

Adversarial Question Bank (16 Attack Angles)

When defending a replication package or referee report against an editorial board or adversarial committee, expect targeted interrogation across these 16 vetted attack angles:

  1. Universe Contamination: Did you filter CRSP share codes to strictly 10 and 11, or did foreign ADRs (12) and REITs (31) contaminate the small-cap deciles?
  2. Survivorship & Delisting Compounding: How exactly did you compound delisting returns (DLRET) when both regular monthly return and delisting return occur in the cessation month?
  3. NASDAQ Imputation Asymmetry: Why did you assign -55% rather than -30% to NASDAQ performance delistings with missing delisting returns? Cite empirical literature justification.
  4. Look-Ahead Signal Lagging: In forming quarterly accounting characteristics from Compustat, what calendar lag did you enforce to ensure accounting data was publicly disclosed before portfolio formation?
  5. Cross-Sectional vs Pooled Winsorization: Did you winsorize your characteristic signals per month, or across the pooled panel? If pooled, how did you avoid inter-temporal look-ahead leakage?
  6. Microcap Influence & Breakpoints: What percentage of your long-short spread's profitability survives when portfolio decile breakpoints are determined solely by NYSE-listed stocks?
  7. Finite-Sample Cluster Correction: In computing clustered covariance matrices across 48 time clusters, did you apply the finite-sample degrees-of-freedom multiplier \(\frac{G}{G-1} \times \frac{N-1}{N-K}\)?
  8. Two-Way Covariance Positive Semi-Definiteness: In computing Cameron-Gelbach-Miller two-way clustered covariance \(V_e + V_t - V_{et}\), how did you handle non-positive eigenvalues in the intersection matrix?
  9. Shanken EIV Attenuation: In two-pass Fama-MacBeth regressions, did you report naive time-series average standard errors or adjust for generated regressors via the Shanken (1992) factor?
  10. GRS Exact F-Test vs Asymptotic Chi-Square: When testing pricing errors across 25 size/value portfolios over 360 months, what is the discrepancy between the exact GRS F-statistic and the asymptotic \(\chi^2\) test?
  11. Stepwise Articulation Discrepancy: Does your waterfall divergence table satisfy exact articulation where intermediate step deltas sum identically to the total published-vs-replicated wedge?
  12. Post-Publication Sample Decay: Following McLean & Pontiff (2016), how much did the anomaly's monthly Sharpe ratio decay in the out-of-sample period following original publication?
  13. Trading Cost Hurdle: Under Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015) transaction cost models, does the net turnover-adjusted alpha remain statistically distinguishable from zero?
  14. Multiple Testing Adjustment: Does the reported t-statistic clear the Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) \(t \ge 3.0\) hurdle, or survive Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control across the factor zoo?
  15. Specification Curve Density: What proportion of the full combinatorial specification grid (sampling windows, control sets, winsorization bounds) yields statistically significant positive alpha?
  16. Constructive Editorial Recommendation: In your referee report, did you explicitly distinguish fatal identification flaws that warrant outright rejection from fixable empirical limitations amenable to revision?
