Examination Architecture, Proof Standards & Timed Synthesis

Doctoral qualifying examinations evaluate whether a candidate possesses the technical rigor, mathematical fluency, and structural synthesis required to execute autonomous doctoral research. Under timed examination conditions, candidates cannot rely on open-ended literature review or ad hoc numerical approximations. Success requires mastering proof invariants, transversality conditions, martingale pricing kernels, and identification structures across asset pricing, econometrics, and macroeconomics. This lesson establishes the architectural foundation and proof discipline necessary to sit the three doctoral field prelims.

1. The Doctoral Qualifying Examination Framework

In quantitative finance and economic sciences, the transition from structured coursework to original dissertation inquiry is governed by comprehensive written milestone evaluations.

Definition 1.1 (Qualifying examination) A qualifying examination is a comprehensive doctoral examination administered after completion of core doctoral coursework to assess a candidate's advanced technical mastery, proof formulation capabilities, and autonomous analytical competence across foundational disciplines before advancing to dissertation research candidacy.

The examination evaluates whether the student can reconstruct foundational economic theories from primitive axioms, establish the mathematical limits of empirical estimation techniques, and diagnose structural failures in quantitative models without reliance on informal intuition or computational heuristics.

Definition 1.2 (Timed field examination) A timed field examination is a rigorous written preliminary examination conducted within a fixed time budget (typically three to four hours per subfield) where candidates solve complex analytical derivations, formulate structural econometric estimators, and verify general equilibrium dynamic systems under strict examination conditions without reference aids.

Timed field examinations assess not merely whether an answer can be found given infinite time, but whether the researcher has internalized mathematical representations to the point where derivation pathways, error detection, and boundary checks operate with fluid automaticity.

2. Mathematical Proof Standards & Invariant Verification

Doctoral prelim grading operates under uncompromising standards of mathematical completeness. A heuristic argument that states "taking limits yields the result" without demonstrating uniform integrability, dominated convergence, or boundary transversality receives substantial point deductions.

Theorem 1.1 (No-Arbitrage Pricing Invariant) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})\) be a filtered probability space supporting asset payoffs \(X \in L^2(\mathbb{P})\). Absence of arbitrage (No Free Lunch with Vanishing Risk) implies the existence of a strictly positive stochastic discount factor \(m > 0\) such that for every asset payoff \(X\) with market price \(P\): \[ P = \E[m X] \] Furthermore, for any vector of excess returns \(R^e\), \(\E[m R^e] = 0\), implying the fundamental volatility inequality: \[ \frac{\sigma(m)}{\E[m]} \ge \frac{|\E[R^e]|}{\sigma(R^e)} \]

Proof By the definition of covariance, for any excess return \(R^e\): \[ \E[m R^e] = \E[m] \E[R^e] + \Cov(m, R^e) = 0 \] Rearranging terms yields: \[ \E[R^e] = -\frac{\Cov(m, R^e)}{\E[m]} = -\frac{\rho_{m, R^e} \sigma(m) \sigma(R^e)}{\E[m]} \] Because the correlation coefficient is bounded in absolute value by unity, \(|\rho_{m, R^e}| \le 1\): \[ |\E[R^e]| = \frac{|\rho_{m, R^e}| \sigma(m) \sigma(R^e)}{\E[m]} \le \frac{\sigma(m) \sigma(R^e)}{\E[m]} \] Dividing both sides by \(\sigma(R^e) > 0\) establishes the Hansen-Jagannathan volatility inequality. This proof is complete.

Remark 1.1 (Transversality Conditions in Dynamic Control) In continuous-time stochastic control, solving the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation yields a candidate value function \(V(W, t)\). To complete a doctoral-grade proof via the verification theorem, the candidate must explicitly verify the transversality condition: \[ \lim_{T \to \infty} \E[e^{-\rho T} V(W_T)] = 0 \] Failure to verify transversality invalidates the proof because explosive bubble trajectories satisfy the HJB first-order conditions while violating intertemporal budget feasibility.

3. Timed Derivation Triage & Numerical Diagnostics

During a timed field examination, candidates encounter multi-part analytical questions requiring sequential derivation steps. Effective examination execution follows a three-stage protocol:

  1. Invariant Check: Before computing algebraic expansions, identify the core economic invariant (for example, Euler equation orthogonality \(\E_t[m_{t+1} R_{t+1} - 1] = 0\), matrix symmetry \(\Sigma = \Sigma^\top\), or boundary conditions \(V(0) = -\infty\)).
  2. Dimensional & Unit Consistency: Verify that intermediate algebraic expressions maintain correct physical dimensions. For instance, risk premia must have units of return per unit of time, while stochastic discount factors have units of inverse gross return.
  3. Degenerate Limit Evaluation: Check whether setting risk aversion \(\gamma \to 1\) recovers the logarithmic utility policy, or setting volatility \(\sigma \to 0\) recovers the deterministic Ramsey equilibrium.

Example 1.1 (Covariance Matrix Inversion & Tangency Invariant) Consider an exam question asking for the tangency portfolio weights \(w^* = \frac{\Sigma^{-1} \mu}{\mathbf{1}^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu}\) and maximum Sharpe ratio \(SR^* = \sqrt{\mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu}\). If the candidate computes an ill-conditioned covariance inversion, the numerical Sharpe ratio will deviate from \(\mu^\top w^* / \sqrt{w^{*\top} \Sigma w^*}\). Verifying that \(SR^*\) computed via the quadratic form equals the portfolio Sharpe ratio is a mandatory self-consistency check.

Lab 1 · Proof Invariant & Matrix Conditioning Diagnostic

Verifying positive definiteness, condition numbers, and tangency portfolio invariants confirms that quadratic Sharpe ratio calculations exactly equal the portfolio return-to-volatility ratio under exam conditions.

"""Lab CAP3-L01-1: Proof Invariant & Matrix Conditioning Diagnostic under Exam Conditions.

Evaluates numerical stability of covariance inversions, positive-definiteness bounds,
and verifies no-arbitrage proof invariants under timed doctoral prelim conditions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Construct a calibrated asset covariance matrix (4 test assets)
    np.random.seed(42)
    volatilities = np.array([0.15, 0.20, 0.18, 0.25])
    correlation = np.array(
        [
            [1.00, 0.45, 0.30, 0.55],
            [0.45, 1.00, 0.40, 0.60],
            [0.30, 0.40, 1.00, 0.35],
            [0.55, 0.60, 0.35, 1.00],
        ]
    )
    cov_matrix = np.outer(volatilities, volatilities) * correlation

    # 2. Condition number and spectral decomposition
    eigenvalues = np.linalg.eigvalsh(cov_matrix)
    cond_number = float(np.max(eigenvalues) / np.min(eigenvalues))
    is_positive_definite = bool(np.all(eigenvalues > 0.0))

    # 3. Mean excess returns
    mu_excess = np.array([0.06, 0.08, 0.05, 0.09])

    # 4. Tangency portfolio weights and maximum Sharpe ratio invariant
    inv_cov = np.linalg.inv(cov_matrix)
    tangency_weights = inv_cov @ mu_excess
    tangency_weights /= np.sum(tangency_weights)
    tangency_return = float(tangency_weights @ mu_excess)
    tangency_variance = float(tangency_weights @ cov_matrix @ tangency_weights)
    tangency_vol = float(np.sqrt(tangency_variance))
    sharpe_ratio = float(tangency_return / tangency_vol)

    # 5. Quadratic form verification: SR = sqrt(mu^T Sigma^{-1} mu)
    analytical_sr = float(np.sqrt(mu_excess @ inv_cov @ mu_excess))

    print(f"Condition Number: {cond_number:.2f}")
    print(f"Positive Definite: {is_positive_definite}")
    print(f"Tangency Weights: {[round(float(w), 4) for w in tangency_weights]}")
    print(f"Tangency Mean Return: {tangency_return:.4f}")
    print(f"Tangency Volatility: {tangency_vol:.4f}")
    print(f"Tangency Sharpe Ratio: {sharpe_ratio:.4f}")
    print(f"Quadratic Form SR: {analytical_sr:.4f}")
    print(f"Invariant Match Error: {abs(sharpe_ratio - analytical_sr):.2e}")


main()

Lab 2 · Mock Prelim Automated Timing & Scoring Engine

Executing an automated mock prelim grading pipeline verifies that multi-part mathematical solutions can be scored against stated tolerances and aggregated into a qualified pass verdict.

"""Lab CAP3-L01-2: Mock Prelim Automated Timing & Scoring Engine.

Simulates a doctoral prelim submission pipeline, checking mathematical proof steps,
numerical tolerance validation, and score aggregation across parts.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def evaluate_exam_step(
    candidate_answer: float,
    true_solution: float,
    tolerance: float = 1e-4,
    points_possible: float = 10.0,
) -> tuple[float, str]:
    error = abs(candidate_answer - true_solution)
    if error <= tolerance:
        return points_possible, f"PASS (error = {error:.2e} <= {tolerance:.2e})"
    partial = max(0.0, points_possible * (1.0 - min(1.0, error / (10.0 * tolerance))))
    return partial, f"FAIL (error = {error:.2e} > {tolerance:.2e})"


def main() -> None:
    exam_sections: list[dict[str, Any]] = [
        {"name": "Part A: SDF Variance Bound", "candidate": 0.4815, "truth": 0.48152, "tol": 1e-3, "points": 25.0},
        {"name": "Part B: Merton Fraction", "candidate": 0.5208, "truth": 0.52083, "tol": 1e-4, "points": 25.0},
        {"name": "Part C: Shanken Standard Error", "candidate": 0.0142, "truth": 0.01425, "tol": 1e-4, "points": 25.0},
        {
            "name": "Part D: Taylor Determinacy Metric",
            "candidate": 1.5833,
            "truth": 1.58333,
            "tol": 1e-4,
            "points": 25.0,
        },
    ]

    total_score = 0.0
    max_possible = 0.0

    print("--- Doctoral Prelim Mock Assessment ---")
    for sec in exam_sections:
        score, msg = evaluate_exam_step(sec["candidate"], sec["truth"], sec["tol"], sec["points"])
        total_score += score
        max_possible += sec["points"]
        print(f"{sec['name']}: {score:.1f}/{sec['points']:.1f} pts | {msg}")

    pct = (total_score / max_possible) * 100.0
    status = "QUALIFIED PASS" if pct >= 80.0 else "RETAKE REQUIRED"
    print(f"Final Aggregated Score: {total_score:.1f} / {max_possible:.1f} ({pct:.1f}%) -> {status}")

Where the canon breaks

Canonical doctoral textbooks frequently present linear-quadratic approximations and log-linearized systems as universally valid analytical representations. In reality, these approximations break down under non-linear state dynamics:

  • Zero Lower Bound (ZLB) Non-Linearities: Log-linearized New Keynesian models fail when nominal interest rates hit the effective lower bound, inducing equilibrium multiplicity and deflationary spirals that linear state-space solvers cannot capture.
  • High-Dimensional Factor Degeneracy: In cross-sectional asset pricing, when candidate factor portfolios exhibit near-collinearity, the sample covariance matrix \(\Sigma_f\) becomes singular, causing GRS test statistics and Shanken variance corrections to diverge.
  • Finite-Sample Size Distortions: While asymptotic GMM theory proves that \(J \sim \chi^2(m - k)\) as \(T \to \infty\), in finite samples with many instruments, the J-test suffers from severe over-rejection under the null hypothesis.

Misconceptions

  1. Memorizing canonical equations guarantees passing a doctoral qualifying examination.

    Prelim questions intentionally modify textbook assumptions (for example, introducing stochastic volatility, transaction costs, or non-separable preferences). Memorization without mastery of first-principles derivation techniques leads to immediate failure when boundary conditions shift.

  2. Omitting regularity conditions such as transversality or uniform integrability is a minor cosmetic defect.

    Doctoral examination committees deduct substantial points or fail candidate scripts when regularity conditions are omitted. Without transversality, explosive Ponzi bubble paths satisfy Euler first-order conditions, destroying the mathematical validity of the proof.

  3. A candidate should allocate equal time to every question on a timed field examination.

    Qualifying examinations feature asymmetric point allocations and variable difficulty. Optimal examination strategy requires triaging high-weight structural proofs and verifying core invariants before embarking on lengthy algebraic expansions.

Problem set

Mechanics problems

Calculate the maximum Sharpe ratio and Hansen-Jagannathan volatility bound on the stochastic discount factor given expected excess return \(\mu = 0.08\), return volatility \(\sigma = 0.20\), and risk-free rate \(r = 0.03\).

Solution

The Sharpe ratio of the single risky asset is \(\frac{\mu}{\sigma} = \frac{0.08}{0.20} = 0.40\). The mean stochastic discount factor is \(\E[m] = \frac{1}{1 + r} = \frac{1}{1.03} \approx 0.97087\). Applying the Hansen-Jagannathan bound: \(\sigma(m) \ge \E[m] \frac{\mu}{\sigma} = 0.97087 \times 0.40 \approx 0.38835\). Thus, any valid pricing kernel must exhibit standard deviation exceeding 0.3884.

Given an announcement day \(d = 12\) in a 30-day month and an observed Fed funds futures price jump \(\Delta f = -0.012\) (-1.2 bps), compute the Kuttner (2001) target rate surprise \(\Delta i^u\).

Solution

The remaining days in the month are \(M - d = 30 - 12 = 18\). The Kuttner scaling factor is \(\frac{M}{M - d} = \frac{30}{18} = \frac{5}{3} \approx 1.6667\). Multiplying by the futures jump yields: \(\Delta i^u = \frac{5}{3} \times (-0.012) = -0.020\) (-2.0 basis points). The unanticipated policy action is a 2 bps rate cut.

In a GMM estimation with \(m = 6\) moment conditions and \(k = 2\) estimated structural parameters, the minimized objective function yields \(T g_T^\top W g_T = 11.2\) across \(T = 250\) observations. Determine the degrees of freedom of the Hansen J-test and evaluate whether the model is rejected at the 5 percent significance level.

Solution

The degrees of freedom equal the number of overidentifying restrictions: \(df = m - k = 6 - 2 = 4\). The critical value for a \(\chi^2(4)\) distribution at \(\alpha = 0.05\) is 9.488. Because the test statistic \(J = 11.2 > 9.488\), the p-value is \(\approx 0.0244 < 0.05\). The null hypothesis of correct model specification is rejected at the 5 percent level.

Proof problems

Prove that in continuous-time portfolio choice under geometric Brownian motion, the HJB first-order condition for optimal portfolio share \(\alpha^*\) implies \(\alpha^* = -\frac{V_W}{W V_{WW}} \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2}\).

Solution

Let wealth dynamics be \(dW_t = [(\alpha (\mu - r) + r) W_t - c_t] dt + \alpha \sigma W_t dB_t\). The HJB equation is \(\rho V = \max_{c, \alpha} \{ u(c) + V_W [(\alpha (\mu - r) + r)W - c] + \frac{1}{2} V_{WW} \alpha^2 \sigma^2 W^2 \}\). Differentiating the objective inside the maximum with respect to \(\alpha\) yields: \(V_W (\mu - r)W + V_{WW} \alpha^* \sigma^2 W^2 = 0\). Dividing by \(W \ne 0\) and solving for \(\alpha^*\) gives \(\alpha^* = -\frac{V_W}{W V_{WW}} \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma^2}\). For CRRA utility \(V(W) = k \frac{W^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\), we have \(-\frac{W V_{WW}}{V_W} = \gamma\), establishing \(\alpha^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\).

Prove that the Shanken (1992) correction factor \(c = 1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) is strictly invariant to arbitrary non-singular linear transformations of the factor vector \(f_t^* = A f_t\).

Solution

Let \(f_t^* = A f_t\) for an invertible \(K \times K\) matrix \(A\). The transformed factor covariance matrix is \(\Sigma_{f^*} = A \Sigma_f A^\top\), with inverse \(\Sigma_{f^*}^{-1} = (A^\top)^{-1} \Sigma_f^{-1} A^{-1}\). The regression model \(R_t = \beta f_t + \epsilon_t = \beta^* f_t^* + \epsilon_t\) implies \(\beta^* = \beta A^{-1}\). In the cross-sectional equation \(\E[R] = \beta \lambda = \beta^* \lambda^*\), equating yields \(\beta \lambda = \beta A^{-1} \lambda^*\), which implies \(\lambda^* = A \lambda\). Computing the quadratic form: \(\lambda^{*\top} \Sigma_{f^*}^{-1} \lambda^* = (A \lambda)^\top [(A^\top)^{-1} \Sigma_f^{-1} A^{-1}] (A \lambda) = \lambda^\top A^\top (A^\top)^{-1} \Sigma_f^{-1} A^{-1} A \lambda = \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\). Thus, \(c = 1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) is invariant under all non-singular linear factor rotations.

Prove that in the 3-equation New Keynesian model with monetary policy rule \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t\), the Blanchard-Kahn condition for determinacy requires \(\phi_\pi > 1.0\).

Solution

Consider the system \(y_t = \E_t[y_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma}(i_t - \E_t[\pi_{t+1}])\) and \(\pi_t = \beta \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa y_t\). Substituting \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t\) into the IS curve: \(y_t = \E_t[y_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma}(\phi_\pi \pi_t - \E_t[\pi_{t+1}])\). Expressing in matrix form \(\begin{bmatrix} y_t \\ \pi_t \end{bmatrix} = A \begin{bmatrix} \E_t[y_{t+1}] \\ \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] \end{bmatrix}\). Because both \(y\) and \(\pi\) are forward-looking jump variables with no predetermined state variables, determinacy requires that both eigenvalues of \(A\) lie strictly inside the unit circle. Evaluating the characteristic polynomial \(\det(A - \mu I) = 0\) reveals that \(\operatorname{tr}(A) < 1 + \det(A)\) and \(\det(A) < 1\) hold if and only if \(\phi_\pi > 1.0\). When \(\phi_\pi \le 1.0\), at least one eigenvalue leaves the unit circle, generating indeterminacy.

Empirical problems

A researcher tests a two-factor pricing model across \(N = 25\) size and value portfolios over \(T = 360\) months. The estimated time-series intercepts yield \(\hat{\alpha}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_\epsilon^{-1} \hat{\alpha} = 0.45\), and the mean factor vector yields \(\bar{f}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \bar{f} = 0.12\). Compute the GRS test statistic and state the degrees of freedom.

Solution

The GRS test formula is \(GRS = \frac{T - N - K}{N} \frac{\hat{\alpha}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_\epsilon^{-1} \hat{\alpha}}{1 + \bar{f}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \bar{f}}\). Substituting \(T = 360, N = 25, K = 2\): \(\frac{T - N - K}{N} = \frac{360 - 25 - 2}{25} = \frac{333}{25} = 13.32\). The quadratic ratio is \(\frac{0.45}{1 + 0.12} = \frac{0.45}{1.12} \approx 0.401786\). Multiplying yields: \(GRS = 13.32 \times 0.401786 \approx 5.3518\). The test statistic follows an \(F(25, 333)\) distribution under the null hypothesis of zero pricing errors.

In a cross-sectional Fama-MacBeth regression, the estimated equity premium is \(\hat{\lambda} = 0.006\) with uncorrected OLS standard error \(SE_{\text{naive}} = 0.0018\). If \(\hat{\lambda}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \hat{\lambda} = 0.35\), compute the Shanken-corrected standard error and the resulting t-statistic.

Solution

The Shanken correction factor is \(c = 1 + \hat{\lambda}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \hat{\lambda} = 1 + 0.35 = 1.35\). The corrected standard error multiplies the residual covariance portion of the variance. In the standard approximation where residual estimation error dominates, \(SE_{\text{Shanken}} \approx \sqrt{1.35} \times SE_{\text{naive}} \approx 1.1619 \times 0.0018 \approx 0.00209\). The t-statistic adjusts from \(t_{\text{naive}} = \frac{0.006}{0.0018} = 3.33\) to \(t_{\text{Shanken}} = \frac{0.006}{0.00209} \approx 2.87\). Notice that the t-statistic drops below the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle of 3.0.

An empirical macro-finance study estimates the response of 10-year Treasury yields to Kuttner policy surprises using Jordà local projections. At horizon \(h = 4\) quarters, the estimated regression slope is \(\hat{\beta}_4 = 0.32\) with Newey-West standard error 0.08. Interpret the economic magnitude and test significance at \(\alpha = 0.01\).

Solution

The slope \(\hat{\beta}_4 = 0.32\) indicates that an unanticipated 100 basis point policy rate hike increases the 10-year Treasury yield by 32 basis points four quarters after the announcement. The t-statistic is \(t = \frac{0.32}{0.08} = 4.00\). The critical value for a two-tailed test at \(\alpha = 0.01\) is 2.576. Because \(4.00 > 2.576\) (p-value \(\approx 0.00006\)), the response is statistically significant at the 1 percent level, confirming persistent policy pass-through to long-term yields.

Research problems

Formulate a doctoral prelim defense answering how Campbell-Cochrane external habit formation reconciles smooth consumption growth with high SDF volatility without creating high risk-free rate volatility.

Solution

In the Campbell & Cochrane (1999) model, utility is defined over surplus consumption \(S_t = \frac{C_t - H_t}{C_t}\), where \(H_t\) is external habit. Effective relative risk aversion is \(\gamma_t = \frac{\gamma}{S_t}\). When consumption falls toward habit, \(S_t\) drops, causing effective risk aversion to surge during recessions and generating countercyclical risk premia and high SDF volatility. Crucially, Campbell and Cochrane reverse-engineer the sensitivity function \(\lambda(s_t)\) in the log surplus dynamics \(s_{t+1} = (1 - \phi) \bar{s} + \phi s_t + \lambda(s_t) v_{t+1}\) such that the intertemporal substitution effect (which encourages borrowing when consumption is low) exactly offsets the precautionary savings motive (which encourages saving due to higher uncertainty). This knife-edge cancellation keeps the equilibrium real risk-free rate perfectly constant, resolving the risk-free rate puzzle.

Critique the use of high-frequency Fed funds futures surprises as external instruments in proxy-SVARs under the Nakamura-Steinsson (2018) Delphic information effect.

Solution

The proxy-SVAR framework requires the external instrument \(Z_t\) to satisfy two core identifying assumptions: relevance (\(\E[Z_t \varepsilon_t^m] \ne 0\)) and exogeneity (\(\E[Z_t \varepsilon_t^j] = 0\) for all non-monetary structural shocks \(j \ne m\)). Nakamura and Steinsson (2018) demonstrate that FOMC policy announcements convey not only policy stance revisions but also private central bank assessments of economic fundamentals (the Delphic information effect). When the Federal Reserve raises rates unexpectedly, market participants infer that the central bank possesses favorable information regarding future output and inflation. Consequently, the high-frequency futures surprise \(Z_t\) correlates with macroeconomic news shocks, violating instrument exogeneity. Failure to purge the information effect leads to positive output and inflation responses to nominal tightening (an empirical information puzzle).

Develop an adversarial referee critique of a paper claiming to discover a new cross-sectional equity factor with in-sample t-statistic \(t = 2.45\) across 1972–2020.

Solution

The critique raises three fatal econometric objections. First, under the Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) multiple-testing framework, testing hundreds of potential anomalies inflates the false discovery rate; an anomaly requires \(t \ge 3.00\) to maintain a 5 percent significance level under data snooping. A t-statistic of 2.45 fails this hurdle. Second, under Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) and Chen & Zimmermann (2022), anomalies frequently disappear when microcap stocks (which constitute 60 percent of firms but only 3 percent of market capitalization) are excluded via NYSE breakpoints and value-weighting. Third, following McLean & Pontiff (2016), published factor returns experience an average 50 percent post-publication decay due to institutional arbitrage. Without an out-of-sample test or a structural economic model justifying why arbitrage capital cannot eliminate the premium, the factor represents empirical noise.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the primary evaluation objective of a doctoral qualifying examination?

    Options

    Qualifying examinations serve as the formal gatekeeper ensuring candidates possess the mathematical and econometric foundations necessary to execute autonomous research.

  2. Why must transversality conditions be satisfied in infinite-horizon dynamic optimization proofs?

    Options

    Without transversality, candidate policy functions may satisfy Euler first-order conditions while allowing wealth to explode to negative infinity through unbacked borrowing.

  3. What is the Hansen-Jagannathan lower bound on pricing kernel volatility \(\sigma(m)\)?

    Options

    The bound states that the coefficient of variation of any valid SDF \(\frac{\sigma(m)}{\E[m]}\) is bounded below by the Sharpe ratio of the tangency portfolio.

  4. If the tangency portfolio exhibits an expected excess return of 10 percent and a volatility of 20 percent, what is the maximum attainable Sharpe ratio (calculated as \(\mu / \sigma\))?

    Sharpe ratio = 0.10 / 0.20 = 0.50.

  5. How does Shanken (1992) modify naive Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional standard errors?

    Options

    Because first-pass betas are estimated with sampling error, the Shanken correction factor \(1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) inflates the second-pass variance appropriately.

  6. Why did Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) establish a t-statistic hurdle of \(t \ge 3.0\) for newly discovered factor anomalies?

    Options

    Under extensive data snooping and multi-testing of hundreds of candidate anomalies, a standard \(t \ge 1.96\) hurdle generates unacceptably high false discovery rates.

  7. What does the Blanchard-Kahn condition verify in linearized rational expectations models?

    Options

    The Blanchard-Kahn condition matches the count of unstable generalized eigenvalues to forward-looking jump variables, ensuring a unique stable saddle-path trajectory.

  8. In high-frequency event studies of FOMC rate decisions, why is the raw futures price jump scaled by \(\frac{M}{M - d}\)?

    Options

    Monthly average settlement mechanics require inverting the fraction of days remaining in the month \(\frac{M - d}{M}\) to recover the discrete target policy shock.
