Lesson L05 · 16 study hours · Tier D

Synthesis, Cross-Field Connections & Examination Execution

Doctoral qualifying examinations do not evaluate isolated disciplines in silos; they test the candidate's capacity to synthesize cross-field restrictions uniting asset pricing, econometrics, and macroeconomics. Affine term structure models connect monetary policy rules to pricing kernels under no-arbitrage constraints, while macro-financial equilibrium models connect intermediary capital constraints to aggregate demand and liquidity spirals. Sitting the three field prelims requires notation discipline, error-trapping protocols, and timed derivation triage. This lesson synthesizes cross-field theory and provides the execution strategy required to pass the qualifying examination.

1. The Macro-Finance Nexus: Affine Term Structure Modeling

A primary frontier where asset pricing and monetary macroeconomics converge is the term structure of interest rates under no-arbitrage restrictions.

Let \(X_t \in \mathbb{R}^K\) denote a state vector of macroeconomic factors (such as the output gap and inflation gap) evolving under the physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) as a VAR(1):

\[ X_{t+1} = \mu + \Phi X_t + \Sigma_v^{1/2} \varepsilon_{t+1}, \quad \varepsilon_{t+1} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, I_K) \]

The short-term nominal policy rate \(r_t\) is an affine function of macro factors: \(r_t = \delta_0 + \delta_1^\top X_t\). The nominal stochastic discount factor \(M_{t+1}\) is exponential-affine:

\[ M_{t+1} = \exp\left( -r_t - \frac{1}{2} \lambda_t^\top \lambda_t - \lambda_t^\top \varepsilon_{t+1} \right) \]

where the market prices of risk are affine in the macroeconomic state: \(\lambda_t = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 X_t\).

Theorem 5.1 (Affine Bond Pricing Recursions) Under no arbitrage, the price of an \(n\)-period zero-coupon bond is exponential-affine in the macroeconomic state vector: \(P_t^{(n)} = \exp(-A_n - B_n^\top X_t)\), and the continuously compounded yield is \(y_t^{(n)} = \frac{A_n}{n} + \frac{B_n^\top}{n} X_t\). The coefficients \(A_n \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(B_n \in \mathbb{R}^K\) satisfy the Riccati difference equations initialized at \(A_0 = 0, B_0 = \mathbf{0}\): \[ B_{n+1} = (\Phi - \Sigma_v^{1/2} \lambda_1)^\top B_n + \delta_1 \] \[ A_{n+1} = A_n + B_n^\top (\mu - \Sigma_v^{1/2} \lambda_0) - \frac{1}{2} B_n^\top \Sigma_v B_n + \delta_0 \]

Proof By the fundamental asset pricing equation, the price of an \((n+1)\)-period bond satisfies: \[ P_t^{(n+1)} = \E_t\left[ M_{t+1} P_{t+1}^{(n)} \right] \] Substituting the pricing kernel and the conjectured bond price formula: \[ P_t^{(n+1)} = \E_t\left[ \exp\left( -r_t - \frac{1}{2} \lambda_t^\top \lambda_t - \lambda_t^\top \varepsilon_{t+1} - A_n - B_n^\top X_{t+1} \right) \right] \] Substitute \(r_t = \delta_0 + \delta_1^\top X_t\) and \(X_{t+1} = \mu + \Phi X_t + \Sigma_v^{1/2} \varepsilon_{t+1}\): \[ P_t^{(n+1)} = \exp\left( -\delta_0 - \delta_1^\top X_t - A_n - B_n^\top (\mu + \Phi X_t) - \frac{1}{2} \lambda_t^\top \lambda_t \right) \E_t\left[ \exp\left( -(\lambda_t + \Sigma_v^{1/2 \top} B_n)^\top \varepsilon_{t+1} \right) \right] \] Because \(\varepsilon_{t+1} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, I)\), using the lognormal expectation formula \(\E[\exp(u^\top \varepsilon)] = \exp(\frac{1}{2} u^\top u)\): \[ \E_t\left[ \exp\left( -(\lambda_t + \Sigma_v^{1/2 \top} B_n)^\top \varepsilon_{t+1} \right) \right] = \exp\left( \frac{1}{2} (\lambda_t^\top \lambda_t + 2 B_n^\top \Sigma_v^{1/2} \lambda_t + B_n^\top \Sigma_v B_n) \right) \] The quadratic term \(-\frac{1}{2} \lambda_t^\top \lambda_t\) cancels. Expanding \(\lambda_t = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 X_t\) and matching coefficients against \(P_t^{(n+1)} = \exp(-A_{n+1} - B_{n+1}^\top X_t)\) verifies the Riccati recursions for \(A_{n+1}\) and \(B_{n+1}\). The proof is complete.

2. Intermediary Asset Pricing & Macroeconomic Liquidity Spirals

In modern macro-finance, the financial sector is not a passive veil. Intermediary balance sheet constraints transmit shocks between asset prices and the macroeconomy generating non-linear liquidity spirals.

Let financial intermediaries fund risky productive capital \(K_t\) by issuing deposits to households. Let \(\eta_t = \frac{E_t}{A_t}\) denote the intermediary equity capital ratio. When an adverse macroeconomic shock hits:

  1. Loss Spiral: Intermediary equity capital \(E_t\) is wiped out proportionally faster than assets due to balance sheet leverage, forcing intermediaries to deleverage.
  2. Margin Spiral: Falling asset prices trigger fire sales and higher haircut margins from lenders, further depressing market liquidity and collateral values.
  3. Credit Crunch: Real investment collapses as the shadow cost of intermediary capital surges, causing output and inflation to decline and transmitting financial distress into the dynamic IS curve.

Remark 5.1 (Cross-Field Equilibrium Synthesis) On a doctoral qualifying examination, a candidate may be asked to link the He-Krishnamurthy intermediary pricing kernel to a New Keynesian DSGE model. In such a model, the natural rate of interest \(r_t^n\) depends negatively on the intermediary credit spread: \(r_t^n = \rho - \sigma \E_t[\Delta y_{t+1}^n] - \chi \text{Spread}_t\). When financial constraints bind, the credit spread spikes, driving \(r_t^n\) deep into negative territory and pushing the nominal policy rate into the Zero Lower Bound (ZLB).

3. Doctoral Examination Execution Strategy & Error Trapping

Sitting a doctoral qualifying examination requires operational discipline. The candidate must avoid common failure modes:

  • Notation Discipline: Maintain consistent notation throughout multi-part proofs. Carefully distinguish between physical expectations \(\E^\mathbb{P}[\cdot]\) and risk-neutral expectations \(\E^\mathbb{Q}[\cdot]\). Confusing measures invalidates no-arbitrage proofs.
  • Triage and Time Budgeting: Allocate time proportionally to question weights. If a 4-part prelim question allocates [10, 20, 40, 30] points, spending 50 percent of the time on Part (a) guarantees an exam failure.
  • Sanity Invariant Trapping: After deriving any closed-form expression, verify limiting cases (e.g. \(\gamma \to 1, \sigma \to 0, \rho \to 0\)) and ensure that variances, Sharpe ratios, and Lagrange multipliers maintain proper non-negativity signs.

Lab 1 · Macro-Finance Affine Term Structure No-Arbitrage Estimation

Computing affine Riccati difference equations confirms that yield curve intercepts and factor loadings compound smoothly across 1Q, 4Q, and 12Q maturities.

"""Lab CAP3-L05-1: Macro-Finance Affine Term Structure No-Arbitrage SDF Estimation.

Estimates the cross-field pricing kernel connecting monetary policy rates,
macroeconomic output gaps, and the term structure of bond yields.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    # 2-factor macro-finance state vector X_t = [inflation_gap, output_gap]'
    np.random.seed(303)

    # Physical VAR(1) dynamics: X_{t+1} = mu + Phi * X_t + v_{t+1}
    phi_mat = np.array([[0.75, 0.10], [0.05, 0.85]])
    sigma_v = np.array([[0.0004, 0.0001], [0.0001, 0.0009]])
    chol_v = np.linalg.cholesky(sigma_v)

    # Affine short rate: r_t = delta_0 + delta_1' X_t
    delta_0 = 0.025
    delta_1 = np.array([0.65, 0.40])

    # Market prices of risk: lambda_t = lambda_0 + lambda_1 * X_t
    lambda_0 = np.array([-0.15, -0.25])

    # Recursive affine yield coefficients: A_n, B_n for n = 1, 4, 12 quarters
    # p_t^(n) = -A_n - B_n' X_t, y_t^(n) = (A_n + B_n' X_t) / n
    maturities = [1, 4, 12]
    yield_intercepts = []
    yield_loadings = []

    a_curr = 0.0
    b_curr = np.zeros(2)

    for n in range(1, max(maturities) + 1):
        # Recursion:
        # A_{n+1} = A_n + B_n' (mu - sigma_v lambda_0) - 0.5 * B_n' sigma_v B_n + delta_0
        # B_{n+1} = (Phi - sigma_v lambda_1)' B_n + delta_1
        # For simple homoskedastic model with lambda_1 = 0:
        a_next = a_curr - float(b_curr @ (chol_v @ lambda_0)) - 0.5 * float(b_curr @ sigma_v @ b_curr) + delta_0
        b_next = phi_mat.T @ b_curr + delta_1
        a_curr, b_curr = a_next, b_next
        if n in maturities:
            yield_intercepts.append(a_curr / float(n))
            yield_loadings.append(b_curr / float(n))

    print("--- Macro-Finance No-Arbitrage Term Structure Calibration ---")
    for idx, mat in enumerate(maturities):
        int_val = yield_intercepts[idx] * 100.0
        b_val = yield_loadings[idx]
        print(
            f"Maturity {mat:02d}Q: Yield Intercept = {int_val:.2f}% | "
            f"Loadings (pi, y) = [{b_val[0]:.4f}, {b_val[1]:.4f}]"
        )


main()

Where the canon breaks

Canonical doctoral macro-finance synthesis models often assume Gaussian homoskedastic shocks and constant risk premia parameters. In practice, these assumptions break down:

  • Regime Shifts in Macro-Finance Covariances: The correlation between equity returns and bond returns shifted from positive in the 1970s–1990s (stagflationary supply shocks) to negative in the 2000s–2010s (demand-driven recessions). Static affine models fail when the sign of \(\Cov(R_{\text{equity}}, R_{\text{bond}})\) flips.
  • Non-Linear Fire Sale Discontinuities: Linearized DSGE models assume that shocks are small perturbations around a steady state. During systemic runs, collateral constraints bind non-linearly, causing capital prices to bifurcate into multiple fire sale equilibria.
  • Structural Breaks in Monetary Policy Rules: Post-2008 unconventional policies (QE, forward guidance, balance sheet runoff) alter the transmission channel of policy rate rules, requiring shadow rate or balance sheet state variables.

Misconceptions

  1. Term structure models and dynamic general equilibrium macro models operate under independent stochastic discount factors.

    Under no arbitrage, all assets in an economy—equities, Treasury bonds, and corporate credit—must be priced by the exact same pricing kernel \(M_{t+1}\). Separating bond pricing from the macro SDF creates internal arbitrage contradictions.

  2. If an asset pricing model replicates the equity premium, it automatically generates realistic yield curves.

    Equity premia reflect long-run growth or habit risks, whereas bond yield curves reflect expected future short rates, term premia, and inflation uncertainty. Many models that fit equities produce inverted or wildly volatile yield curves.

  3. Writing lengthy, informal intuitive essays on qualifying exams compensates for incomplete mathematical proofs.

    Doctoral examination committees evaluate technical precision. Point deductions are severe when derivations contain algebraic leaps, undefined variables, or unverified boundary conditions, regardless of accompanying verbal commentary.

Problem set

Mechanics problems

In a 1-factor affine term structure model, the short rate is \(r_t = \delta_0 + \delta_1 x_t\) with \(\delta_0 = 0.03, \delta_1 = 0.8\). The state variable follows \(x_{t+1} = \phi x_t + \sigma \varepsilon_{t+1}\) with \(\phi = 0.85, \sigma = 0.02\). If the market price of risk is \(\lambda_t = \lambda_0 = -0.20\), compute the 2-period bond loading \(B_2\) and intercept \(A_2\).

Solution

For \(n = 1\): \(B_1 = \delta_1 = 0.8\), and \(A_1 = \delta_0 = 0.03\). For \(n = 2\): using the Riccati recursion \(B_2 = \phi B_1 + \delta_1 = 0.85 \times 0.8 + 0.8 = 0.68 + 0.8 = 1.48\). The intercept is \(A_2 = A_1 - B_1 \sigma \lambda_0 - \frac{1}{2} B_1^2 \sigma^2 + \delta_0\). Substitute: \(A_2 = 0.03 - 0.8 \times 0.02 \times (-0.20) - \frac{1}{2} (0.8)^2 (0.02)^2 + 0.03 = 0.03 + 0.0032 - \frac{1}{2} (0.64)(0.0004) + 0.03 = 0.06 + 0.0032 - 0.000128 = 0.063072\). The 2-period yield is \(y_t^{(2)} = \frac{0.063072}{2} + \frac{1.48}{2} x_t = 0.031536 + 0.74 x_t\).

In a macro-financial general equilibrium model, the natural rate of interest is \(r_t^n = 0.02 - 0.5 \text{Spread}_t\). If the corporate credit spread widens from 1.0 percent to 5.0 percent during a banking panic, compute the shift in \(r_t^n\) and state the policy implication if the central bank's nominal policy rate is constrained at zero.

Solution

Initially, \(r^n = 0.02 - 0.5(0.01) = 0.02 - 0.005 = 0.015\) (1.5 percent). When the spread widens to 5.0 percent, \(r^n = 0.02 - 0.5(0.05) = 0.02 - 0.025 = -0.005\) (-0.5 percent). The natural rate falls by 200 basis points into negative territory. With the nominal policy rate bounded at \(i_t \ge 0\), the central bank cannot lower the policy rate sufficiently to match \(r_t^n\), creating a contractionary real interest rate gap (\(i_t - \E[\pi] > r_t^n\)) that induces an economic recession unless unconventional quantitative easing is deployed.

Proof problems

Prove that in any affine term structure model where the state vector \(X_t\) is stationary with \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \Phi^n = \mathbf{0}\), the asymptotic long-term yield loading \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{B_n}{n}\) converges to zero.

Solution

The Riccati difference equation for \(B_n\) is \(B_n = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (\tilde{\Phi}^\top)^j \delta_1\), where \(\tilde{\Phi} = \Phi - \Sigma_v^{1/2} \lambda_1\). If the risk-neutral transition matrix \(\tilde{\Phi}\) has all eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle, the matrix geometric series converges: \(\lim_{n \to \infty} B_n = (I - \tilde{\Phi}^\top)^{-1} \delta_1 = B_\infty < \infty\). Now evaluate the asymptotic yield loading: \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{B_n}{n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{B_\infty}{n} = \mathbf{0}\). Thus, as bond maturity approaches infinity, the sensitivity of the long yield to stationary macroeconomic state fluctuations converges to zero. Long-term yields are governed entirely by the asymptotic constant intercept \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{A_n}{n}\) (the Dybvig-Ingersoll-Ross theorem).

Prove that under the expectations hypothesis of the term structure (zero term premia \(\lambda_t = \mathbf{0}\)), the forward rate \(f_t^{(n)} = p_t^{(n)} - p_t^{(n+1)}\) equals the expected future short rate \(\E_t[r_{t+n}]\).

Solution

Under the expectations hypothesis, the pricing kernel satisfies \(\E_t[M_{t+1} \dots M_{t+n}] = \exp(-\E_t[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} r_{t+j}])\). Bond prices satisfy \(P_t^{(n)} = \exp(-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \E_t[r_{t+j}])\), with log price \(p_t^{(n)} = -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \E_t[r_{t+j}]\). The forward rate between period \(t+n\) and \(t+n+1\) is defined as \(f_t^{(n)} = p_t^{(n)} - p_t^{(n+1)}\). Substituting log prices: \(f_t^{(n)} = \left( -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \E_t[r_{t+j}] \right) - \left( -\sum_{j=0}^n \E_t[r_{t+j}] \right) = \E_t[r_{t+n}]\). Hence, the forward rate is an unbiased predictor of the future short-term interest rate, proving the expectations hypothesis identity.

Prove that in the Brunnermeier-Sannikov (2014) macro-finance model, the equilibrium asset price volatility \(\sigma_q(\eta)\) strictly exceeds the fundamental cash-flow volatility \(\sigma\) whenever intermediary leverage constraints bind.

Solution

Let capital price be \(q(\eta)\), where \(\eta\) is the intermediary equity share. Capital evolves with fundamental shock \(dK_t / K_t = g dt + \sigma dB_t\). Applying Ito's lemma to \(q(\eta_t)\), the total return on capital is \(\frac{dq_t + dK_t}{q_t} = \mu_R dt + \sigma_R dB_t\), where total volatility is \(\sigma_R = \sigma + \sigma_q\). Intermediaries face equity wealth dynamics \(d\eta_t = \mu_\eta dt + \sigma_\eta dB_t\), with \(\sigma_\eta = \eta \left( \frac{\psi}{\eta} - 1 \right) \sigma_R\), where \(\psi > \eta\) is the share of productive capital held by intermediaries. By Ito's lemma, \(\sigma_q = \frac{q'(\eta)}{q(\eta)} \sigma_\eta\). Substituting \(\sigma_\eta\): \(\sigma_q = \frac{q'(\eta)}{q(\eta)} (\psi - \eta) (\sigma + \sigma_q)\). Solving for \(\sigma_q\): \(\sigma_q \left( 1 - \frac{q'(\eta)}{q(\eta)} (\psi - \eta) \right) = \frac{q'(\eta)}{q(\eta)} (\psi - \eta) \sigma\). When financial constraints bind, asset prices decline as equity drops (\(q'(\eta) > 0\)), and intermediaries hold leveraged assets (\(\psi > \eta\)). The amplification term \(\Lambda = \frac{q'(\eta)}{q(\eta)} (\psi - \eta) \in (0, 1)\) implies \(\sigma_q = \frac{\Lambda}{1 - \Lambda} \sigma > 0\). Total volatility is \(\sigma_R = \sigma + \sigma_q = \frac{1}{1 - \Lambda} \sigma > \sigma\), proving that endogenous balance sheet feedback strictly amplifies volatility.

Empirical problems

In estimating a Gaussian 3-factor affine term structure model on monthly Treasury yields (1985–2020), the first three principal components explain 99.8 percent of total yield variance (Level = 82.5%, Slope = 14.1%, Curvature = 3.2%). Explain how the risk price vector \(\lambda_t\) generates time-varying Cochrane-Piazzesi bond risk premia from the slope and curvature components.

Solution

Under constant risk prices (\(\lambda_1 = \mathbf{0}\)), expected excess bond holding returns are constant. Cochrane and Piazzesi (2005) prove that expected excess returns on 2- to 5-year Treasury bonds are strongly predictable using a single tent-shaped linear combination of forward rates (the CP factor). In affine models, this predictability is generated by specifying \(\lambda_1 \ne \mathbf{0}\): the market price of level risk covaries with the yield curve slope and curvature. When the yield curve is steep (slope is high), \(\lambda_t\) expands, requiring higher expected excess returns to hold long-term Treasuries and explaining the empirical failure of the expectations hypothesis.

An empirical study of covered interest parity (CIP) deviations post-2008 finds that the 3-month cross-currency basis \(\text{Basis}_t = r_t - (r_t^* + f_t - s_t)\) averages -25 basis points for EUR/USD and correlates at \(\rho = -0.68\) with US broker-dealer leverage. Explain how this empirical finding supports intermediary asset pricing over frictionless macro theory.

Solution

Under frictionless arbitrage, CIP is a pure no-arbitrage identity: borrowing in EUR, swapping to USD, and investing in Treasuries should earn zero excess profit (\(\text{Basis} = 0\)). Post-2008, post-crisis banking regulations (supplementary leverage ratio, Basel III capital requirements) require global banks to hold equity against gross balance sheet exposures. Exploiting CIP deviations requires expanding bank balance sheets. When broker-dealer leverage is constrained, banks ration arbitrage capital, causing the cross-currency basis to persist at -25 bps. The negative correlation with leverage confirms that CIP deviations widen when intermediary balance sheet capacity contracts, supporting intermediary asset pricing.

A mock qualifying examination committee grades a student's answer to a monetary VAR identification question. The student imposes a recursive Cholesky ordering with policy interest rate ordered first, followed by output and inflation. Critique why ordering the policy rate first is an econometric error in monthly data.

Solution

Ordering the policy rate first in a lower-triangular Cholesky decomposition implies the structural restriction that the central bank does not react to contemporaneous (within-month) shocks to output or inflation. While output and inflation data are released with lags (making sticky macro response plausible), ordering the interest rate first implies that all other variables respond instantaneously to monetary policy while monetary policy responds with a one-month lag to macroeconomic shocks. This contradicts central bank reaction functions and induces severe price puzzle distortions. Standard practice orders macro variables first (slow-moving) and the policy rate last (fast-moving), or uses high-frequency external instruments.

Research problems

Formulate a comprehensive doctoral research framework reconciling the failure of the expectations hypothesis in bond markets with the equity premium puzzle in a unified general equilibrium model.

Solution

The unified framework combines recursive Epstein-Zin preferences with non-neutral money and stochastic macroeconomic uncertainty (Bansal & Shaliastovich 2013). The equity premium puzzle requires large countercyclical compensation for real consumption growth risk. The expectations hypothesis failure requires bond risk premia to vary over time with predictable cyclical dynamics. The candidate proposes a model where inflation and real consumption growth share a common persistent state vector \(X_t\) with stochastic volatility: (1) real growth shocks carry positive market price of risk, generating a ~6 percent equity premium; (2) inflation uncertainty carries a time-varying price of risk that shifts with monetary policy transparency; and (3) when inflation is countercyclical (stagflation), nominal bonds covary positively with consumption drops, acting as risky assets with upward-sloping yield curves. This reconciles the cross-sectional equity premium and bond term premia within a single structural pricing kernel.

Design an oral prelim examination defense answering how digital central bank balance sheet expansions (QE) alter the transmission of monetary policy through the portfolio balance channel.

Solution

The defense outlines the portfolio balance channel formalized by Vayanos and Vila (2021) and Greenwood, Hanson & Stein (2015). Preferred-habitat investors (pension funds, insurance companies) have specialized demand for long-duration Treasury bonds. When the central bank conducts Quantitative Easing by purchasing hundreds of billions of long-term Treasuries and issuing short-term reserves, it extracts aggregate duration risk from private balance sheets. Risk-averse arbitrageurs absorb fewer long-term bonds, shrinking equilibrium duration risk premia across all long-term fixed income instruments. This flattens the yield curve, compresses corporate credit spreads, and stimulates capital expenditures even when the short-term policy rate is pinned at the zero lower bound.

Develop an adversarial doctoral critique of machine learning factor models (e.g. autoencoders or deep neural networks) claiming to supersede structural asset pricing models.

Solution

The critique delivers three foundational arguments. First, machine learning factor models are statistical curve-fitting architectures that maximize in-sample \(R^2\) without economic structural restrictions. They do not identify the economic mechanism driving risk premia (whether driven by habit, long-run risk, intermediary distress, or behavioral mispricing). Second, following Chen, Pelger & Zhu (2024), neural network factor representations are non-stationary and fragile to macroeconomic regime shifts (e.g., transition from low inflation 2010–2020 to high inflation 2021–2024). Third, pure statistical factor extraction fails the Lucas (1976) critique: because machine learning parameters do not represent structural deep preference parameters (\(\gamma, \beta, \psi\)), they cannot evaluate policy counterfactuals or forecast asset behavior under regulatory or monetary regime shifts.
