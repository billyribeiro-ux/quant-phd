Lab 1 · Worked example: Asset Pricing Prelim Analytical Derivation

Verifying the worked example confirms that the tangency portfolio weights, maximum Sharpe ratio, Hansen-Jagannathan volatility bound, and continuous-time Merton fraction are computed with exact analytical precision.

"""Lab CAP3-project-2: Worked Example: Field Prelim I Complete Analytical Verification.

Verifies the worked example solution of Hansen-Jagannathan SDF bounds,
tangency portfolio weights, and continuous-time Merton portfolio optimization.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    # Worked example parameters from project.html
    rf = 0.02
    mu = np.array([0.07, 0.09, 0.05])
    cov = np.array(
        [
            [0.040, 0.012, 0.008],
            [0.012, 0.060, 0.015],
            [0.008, 0.015, 0.030],
        ]
    )
    gamma = 5.0

    # Step 1: Invert covariance and compute tangency portfolio
    cov_inv = np.linalg.inv(cov)
    tangency_raw = cov_inv @ mu
    tangency_weights = tangency_raw / np.sum(tangency_raw)
    sr_tangency = float(np.sqrt(mu @ cov_inv @ mu))

    # Step 2: Hansen-Jagannathan bound for E[m] = 1 / (1 + rf)
    mean_sdf = 1.0 / (1.0 + rf)
    hj_lower_bound = mean_sdf * sr_tangency

    # Step 3: Merton continuous-time portfolio share
    sigma_first = float(np.sqrt(cov[0, 0]))
    merton_fraction = mu[0] / (gamma * (sigma_first**2))

    print(f"Worked Example Tangency Weights: {[round(float(w), 4) for w in tangency_weights]}")
    print(f"Tangency Portfolio Sharpe Ratio: {sr_tangency:.4f}")
    print(f"Hansen-Jagannathan Bound on Std(m): {hj_lower_bound:.4f}")
    print(f"Merton Optimal Stock Share: {merton_fraction:.4f}")


main()
Worked Example Tangency Weights: [0.4084, 0.3341, 0.2575]
Tangency Portfolio Sharpe Ratio: 0.4727
Hansen-Jagannathan Bound on Std(m): 0.4635
Merton Optimal Stock Share: 0.3500

The tangency portfolio achieves a Sharpe ratio of 0.4727, establishing an HJ lower bound of 0.4635 on pricing kernel volatility. The optimal Merton fraction allocates 35.0% to the risky stock under relative risk aversion gamma = 5.

Annotated exemplars

Exemplar 1 · Distinction Pass (Level 4)

Meets Level 4 across all criteria: fully verifies transversality, establishes exact Blanchard-Kahn eigenvalues, and applies Shanken corrections.

Field Prelim I Derivation (Asset Pricing): To establish the Hansen-Jagannathan (1991) bound on any admissible stochastic discount factor \(m\) pricing excess returns \(R^e\), project \(m\) onto the linear span of \(R^e\): \(m = \E[m] + R^e{}^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu + \varepsilon\), where \(\E[\varepsilon] = 0\) and \(\Cov(\varepsilon, R^e) = \mathbf{0}\). By orthogonality, \(\Var(m) = \mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu + \Var(\varepsilon) \ge \mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu\). Taking square roots yields \(\frac{\sigma(m)}{\E[m]} \ge \sqrt{\mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu} = SR^*\). In continuous-time Merton stochastic control, the candidate value function \(V(W) = (\theta^*)^{-\gamma} \frac{W^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\) satisfies the HJB first-order condition \(\alpha^* = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\). We explicitly verify the transversality condition \(\lim_{T \to \infty} \E[e^{-\rho T} V(W_T)] = 0\), which holds if and only if \(\theta^* = \frac{\rho + (\gamma - 1)[r + (\mu - r)^2 / (2 \gamma \sigma^2)]}{\gamma} > 0\).

Field Prelim II Derivation (Econometrics): In the second pass of Fama-MacBeth, \(\hat{\lambda} = (\hat{\beta}^\top \hat{\beta})^{-1} \hat{\beta}^\top \bar{R}\). Because \(\hat{\beta} = \beta + \Delta \beta\) contains first-pass estimation error, the naive variance \(\frac{1}{T} \Sigma_f\) is strictly biased downward. Applying delta-method asymptotics per Shanken (1992), the asymptotic variance is \(\frac{1}{T} [\Sigma_f + c_{\text{Shanken}} (\beta^\top \beta)^{-1} \beta^\top \Sigma_\varepsilon \beta (\beta^\top \beta)^{-1}]\), where the quadratic correction factor is \(c_{\text{Shanken}} = 1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\). Under the null of zero alphas, the GRS statistic \(GRS = \frac{T - N - K}{N} \cdot \frac{\hat{\alpha}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \hat{\alpha}}{1 + \bar{f}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \bar{f}}\) follows the exact finite-sample \(F(N, T - N - K)\) distribution.

Field Prelim III Derivation (Macroeconomics): In the 3-equation New Keynesian model, both \(y_t\) and \(\pi_t\) are forward-looking non-predetermined jump variables. Inverting the system into forward expectations \([y_t, \pi_t]^\top = A [\E_t y_{t+1}, \E_t \pi_{t+1}]^\top\), the characteristic polynomial roots lie strictly inside the unit disk if and only if \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y > 1.0\). This proves that output gap targeting relaxes the inflation coefficient requirement while preserving determinacy.

Exemplar 2 · Marginal Deficient (Level 2)

Fails Level 3: omits transversality verification, uses naive uncorrected Fama-MacBeth standard errors, and asserts the Taylor principle without proof.

Field Prelim I Derivation: The Hansen-Jagannathan bound says that \(\sigma(m)/\E[m]\) is greater than the Sharpe ratio. For Merton's problem, taking derivatives of the Bellman equation gives \(\alpha = \frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\) and consumption is a constant fraction of wealth. The transversality condition is standard and assumed to hold without proof.

Field Prelim II Derivation: We run Fama-MacBeth regressions by estimating betas in the first pass and then regressing returns on betas in the second pass. The standard errors are the sample standard deviation of the monthly lambdas divided by \(\sqrt{T}\). The GRS test is an F-test that alphas are zero.

Field Prelim III Derivation: The Taylor principle requires \(\phi_\pi > 1\) so that the central bank raises nominal interest rates more than one-for-one with inflation. If this holds, inflation is stable.

Exception handling & edge cases

Doctoral prelim scripts must handle structural and computational edge cases cleanly:

Scenario Failure Mode Prescribed Remediation
Singular Factor Covariance Matrix Inverting \(\hat{\Sigma}_f\) fails when factors are collinear or \(K \ge T\). Apply spectral truncation (PCA) or ridge shrinkage before computing the Shanken quadratic form \(\lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\).
Month-End FOMC Announcement Kuttner scaling denominator \(M - d \le 1\) approaches zero, magnifying noise. Switch to the unscaled next-month Fed funds futures contract jump \(\Delta f_{t+1}\) per Kuttner (2001) boundary protocol.
Negative Transversality Metric High expected returns with low risk aversion violate \(\theta^* > 0\). Recognize that infinite-horizon utility diverges; state that no bounded stationary value function exists under the stated parameters.
Weak Identification in GMM Jacobian rank deficiency produces flat objective surfaces and non-standard distributions. Report identification-robust Moreira (2003) conditional likelihood ratio or Kleibergen (2005) statistics alongside standard Wald tests.

Doctoral oral examination question bank

Doctoral candidates must be prepared to defend their qualifying examination scripts against hostile faculty attack angles:

  1. Why does the Hansen-Jagannathan bound establish an invariant that no preference perturbation can violate?
  2. In Merton's continuous-time problem, under what parameter values does the transversality condition fail despite positive risk aversion?
  3. Why does the Campbell-Cochrane external habit model require an engineered sensitivity function \(\lambda(s_t)\) rather than constant sensitivity?
  4. How does the Bansal-Yaron model explain why equity price-dividend ratios drop when macroeconomic uncertainty rises?
  5. In Fama-MacBeth two-pass regressions, under what conditions does the Shanken correction factor \(c_{\text{shanken}}\) equal 1.0 exactly?
  6. Why does the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken (GRS) test over-reject in the presence of conditional GARCH return heteroskedasticity?
  7. How does the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle of \(t \ge 3.0\) alter the empirical survival rate of published factor zoo anomalies?
  8. In the canonical 3-equation New Keynesian model, why does a strong policy response to output (\(\phi_y > 0\)) relax the Taylor requirement on \(\phi_\pi\)?
  9. What causes sunspot indeterminacy when the generalized Taylor principle is violated?
  10. How do month-end settlement mechanics in 30-day Fed funds futures necessitate switching to the next-month contract?
  11. Why do Nakamura-Steinsson Delphic information shocks violate instrument exogeneity in proxy-SVARs?
  12. How do Jordà local projections eliminate the compounding of autoregressive lag misspecification in structural VARs?
  13. Why does a model that fits the equity premium fail to price the term structure of interest rates under constant risk prices?
  14. How do intermediary leverage constraints in Brunnermeier-Sannikov create an endogenous non-linear liquidity spiral?
  15. Why is covered interest parity (CIP) violation post-2008 evidence of intermediary balance sheet constraints rather than currency risk?
  16. How does Heterogeneous-Agent New Keynesian (HANK) modeling resolve the forward guidance puzzle of standard representative-agent DSGE models?
