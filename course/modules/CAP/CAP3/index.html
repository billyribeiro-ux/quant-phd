Qualifying Examination

The doctoral qualifying examination serves as the formal gateway to independent research candidacy. Modeled on comprehensive PhD prelims at elite doctoral institutions, CAP3 integrates asset pricing, continuous-time stochastic control, cross-sectional and time-series econometrics, and dynamic general equilibrium macroeconomics into three timed written field examinations. You will master analytical proof execution under time constraints, formulate structural identification proofs, evaluate equilibrium stability invariants, and defend research methodologies against rigorous doctoral faculty standards.

  • Prerequisites: AP4 Resolving the Puzzles, AP6 Intermediary Asset Pricing, S5 GMM & Cross-Sectional Econometrics, E7 Monetary Economics, and SC6 Stochastic Control.
  • Project: Qualifying exam, graded by autograder and rubric (120 hours).
  • Hours: 200 study hours (80 h across 5 lessons, 120 h capstone qualifying exam project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formulate and solve continuous-time stochastic control and intertemporal asset pricing problems under state-variable uncertainty and recursive Epstein-Zin preferences.
  2. Estimate and diagnose cross-sectional and time-series asset pricing specifications using Generalized Method of Moments (GMM), Fama-MacBeth two-pass regressions with Shanken corrections, and GRS test statistics.
  3. Derive structural dynamic general equilibrium models, solve linearized New Keynesian state-space systems via Blanchard-Kahn conditions, and evaluate the Taylor principle.
  4. Identify monetary and macroeconomic policy shocks using high-frequency Fed funds futures surprises, narrative event restrictions, and local projections.
  5. Synthesize cross-field econometric and general-equilibrium restrictions into coherent, closed-form, and empirical research proofs.
  6. Execute timed doctoral qualifying examinations under strict professional and publication-grade proof standards.

Prerequisites & closure

CAP3 is the doctoral-tier capstone milestone. It assumes comprehensive mastery across five foundational advanced quantitative fields:

  • Long-run risk models and external habit formation from AP4.
  • Intermediary asset pricing and limits to arbitrage from AP6.
  • Generalized Method of Moments, Fama-MacBeth regressions, Shanken correction, and the GRS test from S5.
  • High-frequency monetary surprises, local projections, and time inconsistency from E7.
  • Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, verification theorems, and the Merton portfolio problem from SC6.

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic items testing prerequisite mastery across AP4, AP6, S5, E7, and SC6, along with two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In the Fama-MacBeth two-pass cross-sectional regression framework, why does the Shanken (1992) asymptotic standard error correction factor \(c = 1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) strictly exceed 1.0 when factor risk premia \(\lambda\) are non-zero?

    Options

    Because first-pass factor loadings (betas) are estimated rather than observed, sampling errors in beta contaminate the second-pass cross-sectional regression. Shanken (1992) proves that the asymptotic variance must be scaled by \(1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\). Rung 1 and 2 skill check.

  2. In a canonical 3-equation New Keynesian general equilibrium model with policy rule \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y y_t\), what analytical condition guarantees Blanchard-Kahn determinacy (a unique stable rational expectations equilibrium)?

    Options

    Per Bullard & Mitra (2002) and Woodford (2003), both forward-looking variables (output gap and inflation) are non-predetermined jump variables. Determinacy requires both eigenvalues of the expectations matrix to lie inside the unit circle, which holds if and only if \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y > 1.0\). Rung 2 skill check.

  3. According to Hansen and Jagannathan (1991), what is the minimum standard deviation of any valid stochastic discount factor \(m\) that prices a vector of excess returns with mean \(\mu\) and covariance \(\Sigma\)?

    Options

    The Hansen-Jagannathan bound follows directly from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the pricing equation \(\E[m R^e] = 0\), establishing \(\sigma(m)/\E[m] \ge \sqrt{\mu^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu}\).

  4. In Merton's (1969, 1971) continuous-time portfolio choice problem with constant relative risk aversion \(\gamma\), stock drift \(\mu\), volatility \(\sigma\), and risk-free rate \(r\), what is the optimal fraction of wealth invested in the risky asset?

    Options

    Maximizing the continuous-time HJB Hamiltonian yields the first-order condition \(\alpha^* W V_{WW} + (\mu - r) V_W = 0\). For CRRA utility \(V(W) \propto W^{1-\gamma}\), \(-W V_{WW} / V_W = \gamma\), producing the classical Merton fraction \(\frac{\mu - r}{\gamma \sigma^2}\).

  5. Under Hansen's (1982) Generalized Method of Moments (GMM), what is the asymptotic distribution of the overidentifying test statistic \(J = T g_T(\hat{\theta})^\top S_T^{-1} g_T(\hat{\theta})\) when \(m\) moments are used to estimate \(k\) structural parameters?

    Options

    Under the null hypothesis that the model is correctly specified, Hansen's J-statistic converges asymptotically to a chi-squared distribution with degrees of freedom equal to the number of overidentifying restrictions \(m - k\).

  6. What structural feature allows the Bansal-Yaron (2004) long-run risk model to explain the equity premium puzzle without requiring extreme risk aversion?

    Options

    Bansal and Yaron (2004) combine recursive Epstein-Zin preferences with a small, highly persistent predictable component in consumption growth and stochastic volatility, generating large equity premia with moderate risk aversion.

  7. In Kuttner's (2001) high-frequency identification framework, what scaling factor is applied to the spot Fed funds futures price jump \(\Delta f\) occurring on day \(d\) of a month with \(M\) calendar days?

    Options

    Because 30-Day Fed funds futures settle on the monthly average effective rate, a policy change on day \(d\) shifts only the remaining \(M - d\) daily rates. The spot futures jump is \(\Delta f = \frac{M - d}{M} \Delta i^u\), so inverting yields the scaling factor \(\frac{M}{M - d}\).

  8. What does the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken (GRS 1989) statistic test in multi-factor asset pricing models?

    Options

    The GRS test is a finite-sample F-test of the joint null hypothesis \(H_0: \alpha_1 = \dots = \alpha_N = 0\), testing whether the candidate factor portfolio spans the mean-variance frontier.

  9. In intermediary asset pricing models such as He and Krishnamurthy (2013), what drives spikes in asset risk premia during financial crises?

    Options

    He and Krishnamurthy (2013) demonstrate that the marginal investor in specialized asset markets is the financial intermediary. When negative shocks deplete intermediary equity capital, balance sheet constraints bind, sharply driving up the SDF volatility and required risk premia.

  10. Why are Jordà (2005) local projections increasingly preferred over structural vector autoregressions (SVARs) for empirical impulse response estimation?

    Options

    By estimating single-equation regressions for each forecast horizon \(h\), local projections avoid the compounding of specification errors that occurs when iterating forward a potentially misspecified VAR lag structure.

Lesson sequence

The qualifying examination curriculum covers 80 study hours distributed across five 16-hour doctoral preparation lessons:

  1. L01 · Examination Architecture, Proof Standards & Timed Synthesis (16 h)
  2. L02 · Field Prelim I: Asset Pricing & Continuous-Time Valuation (16 h)
  3. L03 · Field Prelim II: Cross-Sectional & Time-Series Econometrics (16 h)
  4. L04 · Field Prelim III: Dynamic General Equilibrium & Monetary Macro (16 h)
  5. L05 · Synthesis, Cross-Field Connections & Examination Execution (16 h)

Project ladder

The 120-hour qualifying examination project provides a four-rung progression culminating in sitting the three doctoral field examinations:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    End-to-end analytical derivation and numerical execution of a doctoral field prelim problem in Asset Pricing and Continuous-Time Valuation.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Complete the critical econometric and macroeconomic proofs: the Shanken standard error adjustment and the New Keynesian Blanchard-Kahn determinacy check.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Sit the three doctoral field examinations across Asset Pricing, Econometrics, and Macro/Monetary Economics, delivering verified solver engines and written proof scripts.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the examination engine to non-linear affine term structure estimation, regime-switching general equilibrium DSGE systems, and high-dimensional machine learning factor audits.
