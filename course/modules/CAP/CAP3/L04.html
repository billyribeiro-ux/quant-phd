Field Prelim III: Dynamic General Equilibrium & Monetary Macro

Field Prelim III tests advanced competencies in dynamic macroeconomic modeling, general equilibrium determinacy, and empirical monetary policy identification. Candidates must formulate the micro-founded New Keynesian DSGE model, solve forward-looking rational expectations state-space systems, and prove the Blanchard-Kahn determinacy conditions governing the Taylor principle. On the empirical frontier, candidates must isolate exogenous monetary shocks via Fed funds futures jumps, evaluate month-end contract boundary corrections, and estimate dynamic impulse responses using Jordà local projections. This lesson develops the structural macroeconomic toolkits evaluated on doctoral qualifying examinations.

1. The Canonical 3-Equation New Keynesian Framework

The workhorse macroeconomic framework for monetary policy analysis is the micro-founded 3-equation New Keynesian dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) model developed by Clarida, Galí & Gertler (1999) and Woodford (2003).

The economy consists of representative households optimizing consumption, monopolistically competitive intermediate goods firms facing Calvo (1983) staggered price stickiness, and a central bank setting the nominal interest rate \(i_t\):

  1. Dynamic IS Equation: Derived from household consumption Euler optimization: \[ y_t = \E_t[y_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma} (i_t - \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] - r_t^n) \] where \(y_t\) is the output gap, \(\pi_t\) is the inflation rate, \(\sigma > 0\) is the household relative risk aversion coefficient (inverse of the intertemporal elasticity of substitution), and \(r_t^n\) is the natural rate of interest.
  2. New Keynesian Phillips Curve (NKPC): Derived from Calvo staggered price-setting with survival probability \(\theta \in (0, 1)\): \[ \pi_t = \beta \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa y_t \] where \(\beta \in (0, 1)\) is the subjective discount factor, and the Phillips curve slope is \(\kappa = \frac{(1 - \theta)(1 - \beta \theta)}{\theta} \frac{\sigma + \varphi}{1 + \alpha \theta}\) (with labor disutility curvature \(\varphi\)).
  3. Taylor Monetary Policy Rule: The central bank adjusts the policy rate systematically in response to inflation and output: \[ i_t = r_t^n + \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y y_t + \varepsilon_t^m \] where \(\phi_\pi > 0, \phi_y \ge 0\), and \(\varepsilon_t^m\) represents an exogenous monetary policy shock.

2. Blanchard-Kahn Conditions & The Taylor Principle

A core doctoral examination requirement is establishing whether a dynamic general equilibrium system possesses a unique, stable rational expectations equilibrium (determinacy) or admits self-fulfilling sunspot fluctuations (indeterminacy).

Theorem 4.1 (Blanchard-Kahn Determinacy & Generalized Taylor Principle) In the canonical 3-equation New Keynesian model with contemporaneous feedback, both the output gap \(y_t\) and inflation \(\pi_t\) are non-predetermined forward-looking jump variables. The system possesses a unique, bounded rational expectations equilibrium if and only if the monetary policy response parameters satisfy the generalized Taylor principle: \[ \phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y > 1.0 \] If \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y < 1.0\), the system is indeterminate, admitting an infinite continuum of sunspot equilibria.

Proof Substituting the policy rule \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y y_t\) into the dynamic IS equation yields: \[ y_t = \E_t[y_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma} (\phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y y_t - \E_t[\pi_{t+1}]) \] Rearranging: \[ \left( 1 + \frac{\phi_y}{\sigma} \right) y_t + \frac{\phi_\pi}{\sigma} \pi_t = \E_t[y_{t+1}] + \frac{1}{\sigma} \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] \] Combine this with the NKPC \(\pi_t - \kappa y_t = \beta \E_t[\pi_{t+1}]\) in matrix form: \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 + \frac{\phi_y}{\sigma} & \frac{\phi_\pi}{\sigma} \\ -\kappa & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} y_t \\ \pi_t \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \frac{1}{\sigma} \\ 0 & \beta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \E_t[y_{t+1}] \\ \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] \end{bmatrix} \] The determinant of the left-hand matrix is \(\Omega = 1 + \frac{\phi_y}{\sigma} + \frac{\kappa \phi_\pi}{\sigma} > 0\). Inverting yields the forward expectations system: \[ \begin{bmatrix} y_t \\ \pi_t \end{bmatrix} = A \begin{bmatrix} \E_t[y_{t+1}] \\ \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{where } A = \frac{1}{\Omega} \begin{bmatrix} 1 + \frac{\kappa \beta \phi_\pi}{\sigma} & \frac{1 - \beta \phi_\pi}{\sigma} \\ \kappa & \beta \left( 1 + \frac{\phi_y}{\sigma} \right) + \frac{\kappa}{\sigma} \end{bmatrix} \] Because both \(y_t\) and \(\pi_t\) are forward-looking jump variables with zero predetermined state variables, the Blanchard-Kahn conditions require that both eigenvalues of matrix \(A\) lie strictly inside the unit circle (or equivalently, both eigenvalues of \(A^{-1}\) lie outside the unit circle). The characteristic polynomial \(p(\mu) = \mu^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A) \mu + \det(A) = 0\) has roots strictly inside the unit disk if and only if: \[ \det(A) < 1, \quad \det(A) - \operatorname{tr}(A) > -1, \quad \det(A) + \operatorname{tr}(A) > -1 \] Evaluating \(\det(A) - \operatorname{tr}(A) + 1 > 0\) simplifies algebraically to: \[ \frac{\kappa}{\Omega \sigma} \left[ \phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y - 1 \right] > 0 \] Since \(\frac{\kappa}{\Omega \sigma} > 0\), this inequality holds if and only if \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y > 1.0\). This establishes the generalized Taylor principle.

3. High-Frequency Shock Identification & Local Projections

In empirical monetary macroeconomics, estimating the causal effect of monetary policy requires isolating exogenous policy shocks from endogenous systematic central bank reactions.

Theorem 4.2 (Kuttner 2001 Futures Surprise Scaling) Let \(f_{t, \text{pre}}\) and \(f_{t, \text{post}}\) denote 30-day Federal funds futures rates immediately before and after an FOMC announcement occurring on calendar day \(d\) of a month containing \(M\) days. Under the assumption that the target change persists through month-end, the discrete target surprise is: \[ \Delta i_t^u = \frac{M}{M - d} (f_{t, \text{post}} - f_{t, \text{pre}}) \] When the announcement occurs on the boundary dates \(d \ge M - 1\), the unscaled next-month contract jump \(\Delta f_{t}^{(m+1)}\) is utilized to avoid division by zero.

Definition 4.1 (Local projections) Jordà (2005) local projections estimate dynamic macroeconomic impulse responses by running direct univariate regressions for each horizon \(h \in \{0, 1, \dots, H\}\): \[ y_{t+h} - y_{t-1} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \text{shock}_t + \gamma_h^\top X_t + \varepsilon_{t+h} \] where \(\beta_h\) represents the dynamic impulse response of variable \(y\) at horizon \(h\) to an exogenous policy shock at time \(t\), and \(X_t\) is a vector of control covariates.

Unlike structural VARs, local projections do not compound misspecification errors across horizons and accommodate non-linear state dependencies with robust Newey-West standard errors.

Lab 1 · Kuttner High-Frequency Surprise & Boundary Correction

Computing Kuttner surprises across mid-month and month-end dates confirms that standard scaling expands raw futures jumps while month-end announcements switch to unscaled next-month contracts.

"""Lab CAP3-L04-1: Field Prelim III, Problem A: Kuttner Monetary Surprises.

Computes the Kuttner (2001) high-frequency surprise from Fed funds futures
and evaluates the boundary condition adjustment for month-end announcements.
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def compute_kuttner_surprise(
    spot_futures_jump: float,
    announcement_day: int,
    days_in_month: int,
    next_month_jump: float | None = None,
) -> tuple[float, str]:
    """Compute target surprise with boundary check for month-end announcements."""
    remaining_days = days_in_month - announcement_day
    if remaining_days <= 1:
        if next_month_jump is not None:
            return next_month_jump, "Unscaled next-month contract used (boundary rule d >= M-1)"
        # Default fallback
        return spot_futures_jump, "Raw jump fallback (next-month contract unquoted)"
    scale = float(days_in_month) / float(remaining_days)
    return scale * spot_futures_jump, f"Standard scaling applied (M / (M - d) = {scale:.4f})"


def main() -> None:
    cases = [
        {"day": 5, "m": 30, "jump": -0.010, "next": -0.010},
        {"day": 15, "m": 30, "jump": -0.010, "next": -0.018},
        {"day": 25, "m": 30, "jump": -0.010, "next": -0.045},
        {"day": 29, "m": 30, "jump": -0.010, "next": -0.025},
        {"day": 30, "m": 30, "jump": -0.010, "next": -0.025},
    ]

    print("--- Kuttner (2001) High-Frequency Surprise Identification ---")
    for c in cases:
        day_val = int(c["day"])
        m_val = int(c["m"])
        jump_val = float(c["jump"])
        next_val = float(c["next"]) if c["next"] is not None else None
        surprise, note = compute_kuttner_surprise(jump_val, day_val, m_val, next_val)
        print(
            f"Day {day_val:02d}/{m_val} | Raw Jump: {jump_val * 100:+.1f} bps -> "
            f"Surprise: {surprise * 100:+.2f} bps | {note}"
        )


main()

Lab 2 · New Keynesian Determinacy & Blanchard-Kahn Verification

Evaluating the Blanchard-Kahn conditions across monetary policy parameter configurations verifies that the system is determinate if and only if the generalized Taylor criterion exceeds 1.0.

"""Lab CAP3-L04-2: Field Prelim III, Problem B: New Keynesian Determinacy.

Evaluates the Blanchard-Kahn conditions and Taylor principle determinacy
for the canonical 3-equation New Keynesian general equilibrium model.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any

import numpy as np


def check_new_keynesian_determinacy(
    beta: float,
    sigma: float,
    kappa: float,
    phi_pi: float,
    phi_y: float,
) -> tuple[bool, float, np.ndarray]:
    """Evaluate determinacy criterion: phi_pi + ((1 - beta)/kappa) * phi_y > 1.0."""
    taylor_criterion = phi_pi + ((1.0 - beta) / kappa) * phi_y

    # Matrix form of the expectations system [y_t, pi_t]' = A * E_t[y_{t+1}, pi_{t+1}]'
    # Derived from:
    # y_t = E_t[y_{t+1}] - (1/sigma)(phi_pi * pi_t + phi_y * y_t - E_t[pi_{t+1}])
    # pi_t = beta * E_t[pi_{t+1}] + kappa * y_t
    # Invert to state-space system matrix A
    denom = 1.0 + (1.0 / sigma) * phi_y + (kappa / sigma) * phi_pi
    a11 = (1.0 + (kappa / sigma) * phi_pi * beta) / denom
    a12 = (1.0 / sigma) * (1.0 - beta * phi_pi) / denom
    a21 = kappa / denom
    a22 = (beta * (1.0 + (1.0 / sigma) * phi_y) + (kappa / sigma)) / denom
    a_mat = np.array([[a11, a12], [a21, a22]])

    eigenvalues = np.linalg.eigvals(a_mat)
    # Both variables are forward-looking jump variables; determinacy requires
    # both eigenvalues of the expectations matrix to lie strictly inside the unit circle
    moduli = np.abs(eigenvalues)
    is_determinate = bool(np.all(moduli < 1.0) and taylor_criterion > 1.0)
    return is_determinate, taylor_criterion, eigenvalues


def main() -> None:
    beta = 0.99
    sigma = 1.0
    kappa = 0.15

    regimes: list[dict[str, Any]] = [
        {"name": "Aggressive Inflation Targeting", "phi_pi": 1.5, "phi_y": 0.5},
        {"name": "Pure Inflation Targeting", "phi_pi": 1.1, "phi_y": 0.0},
        {"name": "Weak Monetary Response (Passive)", "phi_pi": 0.8, "phi_y": 0.2},
        {"name": "Boundary Indeterminate Case", "phi_pi": 0.9, "phi_y": 1.5},
    ]

    print("--- New Keynesian System Determinacy & Blanchard-Kahn Check ---")
    for r in regimes:
        p_pi = float(r["phi_pi"])
        p_y = float(r["phi_y"])
        determinate, crit, eigs = check_new_keynesian_determinacy(beta, sigma, kappa, p_pi, p_y)
        status = "UNIQUE STABLE (Determinate)" if determinate else "INDETERMINATE / SUNSPOT"
        mod_str = f"[{abs(eigs[0]):.4f}, {abs(eigs[1]):.4f}]"
        print(
            f"{r['name']}: phi_pi={p_pi:.1f}, phi_y={p_y:.1f} | Criterion: {crit:.4f} | Moduli: {mod_str} -> {status}"
        )


main()

Where the canon breaks

Doctoral macroeconomics prelims frequently require candidates to critique standard New Keynesian policy conclusions:

  • The Forward Guidance Puzzle: In standard linearized DSGE models, announcing interest rate cuts far into the future (e.g. 5 years ahead) produces gigantic, implausibly large expansions in current output and inflation due to un-discounted forward-looking compounding.
  • The Neo-Fisherian Effect: Standard DSGE models assert that raising nominal interest rates reduces inflation. However, under prolonged commitment to high nominal rates, the Fisher relation \(i = r + \E[\pi]\) implies that inflation must eventually rise to match the higher policy rate.
  • Information Effects in Policy Announcements: When the central bank announces a surprise rate hike, market participants frequently interpret the action as a signal of unobserved macroeconomic strength (Nakamura & Steinsson 2018), leading stock prices and inflation expectations to rise rather than fall.

Misconceptions

  1. Setting nominal interest rate response \(\phi_\pi > 1.0\) guarantees determinacy regardless of output gap targeting.

    While \(\phi_\pi > 1.0\) is sufficient when \(\phi_y \ge 0\), if the central bank responds negatively to output or targets price levels incorrectly, the system can become indeterminate or unstable. The generalized criterion \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1-\beta}{\kappa} \phi_y > 1.0\) must be verified.

  2. Fed funds futures price changes on FOMC announcement days reflect only policy stance surprises.

    Futures price changes combine two distinct components: pure monetary policy shocks and Delphic information effects regarding central bank economic forecasts. Conflating the two produces severe omitted variable bias in macroeconomic regressions.

  3. Local projections and structural VARs estimate identical impulse response functions in finite samples.

    Local projections estimate direct horizon-specific projections without imposing autoregressive lag constraints. In finite samples, local projection IRFs can diverge substantially from iterated VAR impulse responses, particularly in the presence of non-linearities or lag truncation.

Problem set

Mechanics problems

In a New Keynesian model with \(\beta = 0.99, \kappa = 0.10, \phi_\pi = 0.85\), and \(\phi_y = 1.60\), evaluate the generalized Taylor determinacy metric and determine whether the rational expectations equilibrium is determinate.

Solution

The generalized Taylor criterion is \(\phi_\pi + \frac{1 - \beta}{\kappa} \phi_y\). The slope ratio is \(\frac{1 - 0.99}{0.10} = \frac{0.01}{0.10} = 0.10\). Multiplying by \(\phi_y\): \(0.10 \times 1.60 = 0.16\). The metric evaluates to: \(0.85 + 0.16 = 1.01\). Because \(1.01 > 1.00\), the system satisfies the Blanchard-Kahn conditions and possesses a unique, determinate rational expectations equilibrium despite \(\phi_\pi < 1.00\).

On day \(d = 20\) of a 31-day month, the spot-month Fed funds futures price drops from 95.50 to 95.42 immediately following an FOMC statement. Compute the implied futures rate change and the discrete target surprise \(\Delta i^u\).

Solution

The implied futures rate is \(100 - P\). The rate jumps from \(4.50\%\) to \(4.58\%\), so \(\Delta f = +0.08\%\) (+8.0 basis points). The remaining days in the month are \(M - d = 31 - 20 = 11\). The Kuttner scaling factor is \(\frac{M}{M - d} = \frac{31}{11} \approx 2.81818\). Multiplying yields: \(\Delta i^u = \frac{31}{11} \times 0.08 = \frac{2.48}{11} \approx 0.22545\%\) (+22.55 basis points). The policy action was an unanticipated 22.55 bps rate hike.

A researcher estimates a local projection of real GDP growth on monetary policy shocks: \(y_{t+h} - y_{t-1} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \varepsilon_t^m + u_{t+h}\). At horizon \(h = 2\), the sample covariance between \((y_{t+2} - y_{t-1})\) and \(\varepsilon_t^m\) is -0.15, and the variance of \(\varepsilon_t^m\) is 0.25. Compute the impulse response coefficient \(\hat{\beta}_2\).

Solution

In OLS univariate regression, the slope coefficient is the ratio of sample covariance to regressor variance: \(\hat{\beta}_2 = \frac{\Cov(y_{t+2} - y_{t-1}, \varepsilon_t^m)}{\Var(\varepsilon_t^m)} = \frac{-0.15}{0.25} = -0.60\). An unanticipated 1 unit monetary tightening shock induces a cumulative 0.60 percentage point reduction in real GDP two quarters post-announcement.

Proof problems

Prove that in the Calvo staggered price-setting model with non-zero trend inflation \(\bar{\pi} = 0\), intermediate firms set reset price \(p_t^* = (1 - \beta \theta) \sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t[mc_{t+j} + p_{t+j}]\), where \(mc\) is real marginal cost.

Solution

In the Calvo framework, each firm resets its price in period \(t\) with probability \(1 - \theta\). If selected, the firm chooses price \(P_t^*\) to maximize expected discounted profits over future periods in which the price remains active: \(\max_{P_t^*} \sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t \left[ Q_{t, t+j} \left( P_t^* Y_{t+j}(i) - MC_{t+j} Y_{t+j}(i) \right) \right]\), subject to demand \(Y_{t+j}(i) = \left( \frac{P_t^*}{P_{t+j}} \right)^{-\varepsilon} Y_{t+j}\). Differentiating with respect to \(P_t^*\) and setting the first-order condition to zero yields: \(\sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t \left[ Q_{t, t+j} Y_{t+j} P_{t+j}^\varepsilon \left( (1 - \varepsilon) (P_t^*)^{-\varepsilon} + \varepsilon MC_{t+j} (P_t^*)^{-\varepsilon - 1} \right) \right] = 0\). Factoring out \((P_t^*)^{-\varepsilon - 1}\) yields \(P_t^* = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon - 1} \frac{\sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t [Q_{t, t+j} Y_{t+j} P_{t+j}^\varepsilon MC_{t+j}]}{\sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t [Q_{t, t+j} Y_{t+j} P_{t+j}^{\varepsilon - 1}]}\). Log-linearizing this expression around the zero-inflation steady state where \(Q_{t, t+j} = \beta^j\) and \(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon - 1} MC = P\) yields the linear expression: \(p_t^* = (1 - \beta \theta) \sum_{j=0}^\infty (\beta \theta)^j \E_t[mc_{t+j} + p_{t+j}]\). Combining this with the aggregate price level index \(p_t = \theta p_{t-1} + (1 - \theta) p_t^*\) derives the New Keynesian Phillips Curve \(\pi_t = \beta \E_t[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa y_t\).

Prove that in the Kuttner (2001) futures settlement mechanics, when an announcement occurs on day \(d = M\), the spot-month futures price jump \(\Delta f_t\) satisfies \(\Delta f_t = \frac{1}{M} \Delta i_t^u\).

Solution

The spot-month Fed funds futures contract settles on the monthly average rate: \(P_t = 100 - \frac{1}{M} \sum_{\tau=1}^M r_\tau\). The pre-announcement expected futures rate is \(f_{t, \text{pre}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{\tau=1}^{M-1} r_\tau + \frac{1}{M} \E_{\text{pre}}[r_M]\). An announcement on the final day of the month (\(d = M\)) shifts the policy rate only on day \(M\): \(\E_{\text{post}}[r_M] = \E_{\text{pre}}[r_M] + \Delta i_t^u\). Realized effective rates for all preceding days \(\tau \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\) are fixed historical constants. Therefore, the post-announcement futures rate is \(f_{t, \text{post}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{\tau=1}^{M-1} r_\tau + \frac{1}{M} (\E_{\text{pre}}[r_M] + \Delta i_t^u)\). Subtracting yields: \(\Delta f_t = f_{t, \text{post}} - f_{t, \text{pre}} = \frac{1}{M} \Delta i_t^u\). Inverting gives \(\Delta i_t^u = M \Delta f_t\), establishing that the scaling factor reaches its maximum value of \(M\) on the final day of the month.

Prove that in a forward-looking rational expectations model \(x_t = A \E_t[x_{t+1}] + B \varepsilon_t\) with no predetermined state variables, the bounded solution is unique if and only if all eigenvalues of \(A\) lie strictly inside the unit circle.

Solution

Compute the Jordan decomposition of \(A = P J P^{-1}\), and define transformed variables \(z_t = P^{-1} x_t\). The system decouples into \(z_t = J \E_t[z_{t+1}] + \tilde{\varepsilon}_t\). For each decoupled scalar component \(z_{i,t} = \mu_i \E_t[z_{i, t+1}] + \tilde{\varepsilon}_{i,t}\), iterate forward \(K\) periods: \(z_{i,t} = \sum_{j=0}^{K-1} \mu_i^j \E_t[\tilde{\varepsilon}_{i, t+j}] + \mu_i^K \E_t[z_{i, t+K}]\). If \(|\mu_i| < 1\), the geometric series converges, and imposing the bounded expectation condition \(\lim_{K \to \infty} \mu_i^K \E_t[z_{i, t+K}] = 0\) uniquely determines the solution: \(z_{i,t} = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \mu_i^j \E_t[\tilde{\varepsilon}_{i, t+j}]\). If \(|\mu_i| \ge 1\), the forward series diverges unless arbitrary sunspot forecast errors \(\eta_{t+1}\) (satisfying \(\E_t[\eta_{t+1}] = 0\)) are introduced to cancel explosive paths, generating an infinite continuum of bounded solutions (indeterminacy). Therefore, all eigenvalues must satisfy \(|\mu_i| < 1\) for uniqueness.

Empirical problems

Using Gertler & Karadi (2015) high-frequency proxies, an empirical researcher runs a proxy-SVAR with 1-year Treasury yields, output, inflation, and corporate credit spreads. The first-stage regression of the reduced-form interest rate residual on the 30-minute Fed funds futures surprise yields an F-statistic of \(F = 22.4\). Evaluate whether the high-frequency surprise serves as a strong external instrument under Stock & Yogo (2005) critical values.

Solution

In instrumental variable estimation, the Stock and Yogo (2005) rule of thumb for avoiding weak instrument bias (limiting 2SLS relative bias to under 10 percent) requires a first-stage F-statistic exceeding 10.0 (or approximately 16.38 for strict 10 percent maximal IV size distortion). With \(F = 22.4 > 16.38\), the high-frequency Fed funds futures surprise satisfies the strong instrument condition, ruling out weak identification distortions in the structural VAR impulse responses.

An empirical macro paper estimates the Bernanke-Kuttner regression \(R_t = \alpha + \beta \Delta i_t^u + \varepsilon_t\) across 120 FOMC announcement days, finding \(\hat{\beta} = -4.65\) with standard error 0.95. Calculate the t-statistic, test the null hypothesis \(\beta = 0\) at \(\alpha = 0.01\), and compute the expected equity response to an unexpected 50 basis point emergency rate cut.

Solution

The t-statistic is \(t = \frac{-4.65}{0.95} \approx -4.89\). The critical value at \(\alpha = 0.01\) is 2.576. Because \(|-4.89| > 2.576\) (p-value \(\approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}\)), the negative equity response is statistically significant at the 1 percent level. For an unanticipated 50 bps rate cut (\(\Delta i^u = -0.50\%\)), the predicted equity return is \(\E[R_t] = -4.65 \times (-0.50\%) = +2.325\%\) (+232.5 basis points). Broad equities rally by approximately 2.33 percent.

In estimating monetary policy transmission to commercial bank lending rates via local projections, the horizon-specific slopes are \(\hat{\beta}_0 = 0.15, \hat{\beta}_1 = 0.45, \hat{\beta}_2 = 0.80, \hat{\beta}_4 = 0.95\). Describe the empirical pass-through dynamics and determine the horizon at which monetary policy achieves near-complete pass-through.

Solution

At impact (\(h = 0\)), pass-through is sluggish, with bank lending rates adjusting by only 15 basis points per 100 bps policy hike. Pass-through accelerates to 45 bps after 1 quarter, reaches 80 bps after 2 quarters, and attains 95 bps after 4 quarters (1 year). Near-complete pass-through (\(\approx 1.0\)) is achieved at horizon \(h = 4\) quarters, reflecting institutional adjustment costs, sticky deposit contracts, and staggered bank loan repricing.

Research problems

Critique the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (Cochrane 2023) as an alternative resolution to equilibrium price determinacy under passive monetary policy.

Solution

Under passive monetary policy (\(\phi_\pi < 1.0\)), standard New Keynesian models produce indeterminacy because the central bank does not adjust nominal rates sufficiently to anchor inflation expectations. The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (FTPL) resolves this indeterminacy by specifying an active non-Ricardian fiscal regime. In FTPL, the government intertemporal budget constraint is an equilibrium valuation condition: \(\frac{B_{t-1}}{P_t} = \E_t \left[ \sum_{j=0}^\infty m_{t, t+j} s_{t+j} \right]\), where \(B_{t-1}\) is nominal debt and \(s_{t+j}\) is the real primary fiscal surplus. If the path of future fiscal surpluses is determined independently of the debt level, the price level \(P_t\) adjusts uniquely to equate the real purchasing power of government liabilities to the discounted stream of surpluses. Thus, passive monetary policy combined with active fiscal policy achieves a unique determinate price level without relying on the Taylor principle.

Formulate a doctoral prelim research design resolving the Nakamura-Steinsson information effect in monetary policy event studies using high-frequency financial market consensus forecasts.

Solution

The research design purges Delphic information shocks by combining high-frequency futures data with survey-based private sector macroeconomic forecasts (e.g. Bloomberg consensus or Blue Chip forecasts collected immediately prior to the meeting). First, construct a pure monetary shock by regressing the 30-minute Fed funds futures surprise on the pre-FOMC consensus forecast error of GDP growth and CPI inflation: \(\Delta f_t = \alpha + \gamma_y (y_t^{\text{FOMC}} - y_t^{\text{survey}}) + \gamma_\pi (\pi_t^{\text{FOMC}} - \pi_t^{\text{survey}}) + \varepsilon_t^{\text{pure}}\). Second, orthogonalize the Greenbook forecast revision (released with a 5-year lag) against futures surprises to test whether the residual shock \(\varepsilon_t^{\text{pure}}\) remains correlated with central bank private information. Third, estimate local projections on asset prices using \(\varepsilon_t^{\text{pure}}\) as the clean instrument, verifying that stock prices decline and real interest rates rise without the anomalous positive GDP response observed in unpurged surprise series.

Analyze why heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian (HANK) models dampen the forward guidance puzzle relative to representative-agent (RANK) models.

Solution

In representative-agent (RANK) models, all households can borrow and lend freely at the risk-free rate, causing future expected interest rate changes to compound through the Euler equation with zero discounting (\(\beta^j \approx 1\)). Announcing an interest rate cut 20 quarters ahead generates massive current consumption booms. In HANK models (Kaplan, Moll & Violante 2018), markets are incomplete and a substantial fraction of households are hand-to-mouth (wealth-poor or liquidity-constrained). Constrained households consume their current labor income and cannot borrow against future interest rate cuts. Furthermore, uninsurable idiosyncratic income risk creates high precautionary savings that discount future macroeconomic promises. Consequently, the aggregate consumption response to distant forward guidance is heavily discounted, restoring realistic economic magnitudes and resolving the forward guidance puzzle.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What analytical condition defines the generalized Taylor principle for determinacy in the canonical New Keynesian model?

    Options

    The generalized Taylor principle guarantees that both eigenvalues of the rational expectations transition matrix lie strictly inside the unit circle, ensuring a unique stable saddle-path equilibrium.

  2. What is the Kuttner (2001) futures scaling formula applied to an FOMC announcement occurring on day \(d\) in a month with \(M\) calendar days?

    Options

    Because Fed funds futures settle on the monthly average rate, a rate revision on day \(d\) affects only the remaining \(M - d\) days, requiring scaling by \(\frac{M}{M - d}\).

  3. In a 30-day month, an FOMC rate decision occurs on day \(d = 10\). What is the exact Kuttner futures scaling factor \(\frac{M}{M - d}\)?

    \(M / (M - d) = 30 / (30 - 10) = 30 / 20 = 1.50\).

  4. What empirical correction does Kuttner (2001) prescribe when an FOMC announcement occurs on the final or penultimate day of the month (\(d \ge M - 1\))?

    Options

    As \(d \to M\), the scaling denominator \(M - d\) approaches zero, amplifying microstructure noise. The next-month contract jump applies to the entire following month and requires no scaling.

  5. According to Bernanke and Kuttner (2005), what is the estimated equity sensitivity to an unanticipated monetary policy surprise \(\Delta i_t^u\)?

    Options

    Bernanke and Kuttner (2005) find an OLS slope of approximately -4.7, meaning that unexpected monetary tightening immediately depresses equity valuations.

  6. What is the Nakamura-Steinsson (2018) Delphic information effect in monetary policy event studies?

    Options

    The Delphic effect occurs when markets treat a surprise rate hike as positive news about underlying economic fundamentals, confounding pure policy shocks with macroeconomic news.

  7. Why are Jordà (2005) local projections considered more robust than structural VARs for macroeconomic impulse response estimation?

    Options

    By estimating horizon-specific direct regressions, local projections prevent specification errors in short lags from compounding into distorted long-horizon impulse responses.

  8. Under the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (FTPL), what mechanism determines the aggregate price level under passive monetary policy?

    Options

    In FTPL (Cochrane 2023), government debt is valued like equity in the government; the price level adjusts to equate real debt value to expected future primary surpluses.
