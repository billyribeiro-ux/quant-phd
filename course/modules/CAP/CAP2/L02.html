Cross-Sectional Equities: Value Persistence & The Factor Zoo

Cross-sectional asset pricing is undergoing a replication crisis. Quantitative managers must resolve whether the prolonged drawdown of the value factor signals structural obsolescence or mean-reverting opportunity, and whether hundreds of published equity anomalies represent genuine risk premia or data-snooping artifacts born of academic publication bias.

1. Resolution 3: Value Is Dead

The value premium—the historical empirical tendency of high book-to-market (B/M) stocks to outperform low B/M growth stocks—formed the cornerstone of multi-factor asset pricing (Fama & French 1992, 1993). However, between 2007 and 2020, the Fama-French HML factor suffered an unprecedented drawdown exceeding 55%, prompting widespread assertions that value investing is structurally defunct.

Definition 2.1 (Valuation Spread)

The valuation spread \(\mathcal{VS}_t\) is the ratio of the aggregate valuation multiple (such as Book-to-Market or Earnings-to-Price) of the value portfolio relative to the growth portfolio:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{VS}_t &= \frac{(B/M)_{t, \text{Value}}}{(B/M)_{t, \text{Growth}}} \end{aligned} \]

When \(\mathcal{VS}_t\) expands, growth stocks become relatively more expensive than value stocks, generating an unrealized mark-to-market drawdown for value investors even if underlying fundamentals remain stable.

Theorem 2.1 · Asness Valuation Spread Decomposition

The cumulative log return of the long-short value factor \(\Delta hml_{t, t+k}\) decomposes identically into changes in relative fundamental cash flows and changes in the valuation spread:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta hml_{t, t+k} &= \left( \Delta f_{t, t+k}^{\text{Value}} - \Delta f_{t, t+k}^{\text{Growth}} \right) + \Delta \ln \mathcal{VS}_{t, t+k} \end{aligned} \]

where \(\Delta f\) denotes log fundamental growth (earnings or book value). During the 2007–2020 value drawdown, over 70% of the underperformance was attributable to the expansion of the valuation spread \(\Delta \ln \mathcal{VS}\) to the 99th historical percentile, rather than an unprecedented collapse in relative fundamental growth.

Remark 2.1 (Intangible Capital Accounting)

A primary structural critique of standard book value is its immediate expensing of intangible investments (R&D, software, brand equity) under GAAP. In knowledge-intensive economies, unrecorded intangible assets cause high-tech growth firms to appear artificially expensive on stated book value, distorting cross-sectional sorting.

2. Resolution 4: Most Factors Are False Discoveries

The academic asset pricing literature has documented more than 400 distinct variables predicting the cross-section of expected returns—a proliferation dubbed the "factor zoo." If researchers test hundreds of candidate factors using standard single-hypothesis testing criteria (\(|t| \ge 1.96\)), severe multiple testing contamination ensues.

Theorem 2.2 · Harvey-Liu-Zhu Multiple Testing Hurdle

Let \(M\) be the total number of orthogonal factor hypotheses tested by the scientific community. To maintain a family-wise error rate (FWER) bounded by \(\alpha = 0.05\) under the Bonferroni inequality, the required test statistic hurdle \(t^*\) satisfies:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Prob\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^M \left\{ |t_i| \ge t^* \right\} \;\middle|\; H_0 \right) &\le \sum_{i=1}^M \Prob\left( |t_i| \ge t^* \;\middle|\; H_0 \right) = M \cdot 2 \left( 1 - \Phi(t^*) \right) \le \alpha \\ t^* &\ge \Phi^{-1}\left( 1 - \frac{\alpha}{2M} \right) \end{aligned} \]

For \(M = 316\) candidate factors, \(t^* \ge \Phi^{-1}(1 - 0.05 / 632) \approx 3.78\). Incorporating factor correlation shrinkage, Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) establish that the minimal robust significance threshold for cross-sectional factor discovery is \(|t| \ge 3.00\).

Theorem 2.3 · McLean-Pontiff Post-Publication Decay

Across 97 published anomalies, the average post-publication return decay is approximately 58%:

\[ \begin{aligned} R_{i, t}^{\text{post}} &\approx (1 - 0.58) \cdot R_{i, t}^{\text{in-sample}} \end{aligned} \]

Of this decay, approximately 26% occurs during the out-of-sample pre-publication window (pure statistical data-snooping bias), and an additional 32% occurs following public release, consistent with arbitrage capital deployment and market assimilation.

Furthermore, Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) systematically replicate 437 anomalies using NYSE breakpoints and value-weighting. Under these standard institutional execution rules, 65% of anomalies fail to achieve statistical significance at the 5% level, and 82% fail to pass the \(|t| \ge 3.0\) hurdle, establishing that the majority of published anomalies are concentrated in microcaps with severe limits to arbitrage.

Lab 1 · Factor Zoo Hurdles & Value Spread Dynamics

We trace the Bonferroni multiple testing hurdle as candidate factor counts expand from 1 to 500, and evaluate value factor return decay alongside valuation spread widening.

"""Lab CAP2-L02-1: Empirical tests for the value factor and the factor zoo.

Implements Harvey-Liu-Zhu (2016) multiple testing hurdle trajectory as candidate
factor count expands, and evaluates Fama-French HML value factor drawdowns and
valuation spread dynamics.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats


def multiple_testing_hurdles(factor_counts: list[int], alpha: float = 0.05) -> list[float]:
    """Compute critical t-statistic thresholds under Bonferroni multiple testing adjustment."""
    hurdles = []
    for m in factor_counts:
        alpha_adj = alpha / float(m)
        z_crit = float(stats.norm.ppf(1.0 - alpha_adj / 2.0))
        hurdles.append(z_crit)
    return hurdles


def analyze_value_spread_and_drawdown(
    hml_returns: np.ndarray,
    growth_bm: np.ndarray,
    value_bm: np.ndarray,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Compute value spread (B/M ratio between value and growth) and maximum drawdown."""
    val_spread = value_bm / growth_bm
    mean_spread = float(np.mean(val_spread))

    # Wealth index and drawdown
    wealth = np.cumprod(1.0 + hml_returns)
    peak = np.maximum.accumulate(wealth)
    drawdowns = (wealth - peak) / peak
    max_dd = float(np.min(drawdowns))

    ann_ret = float(np.mean(hml_returns) * 12.0)

    return mean_spread, max_dd, ann_ret


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(2016)

    # 1. Multiple testing hurdles across factor zoo expansion
    factor_universe = [1, 10, 50, 100, 200, 316, 500]
    hurdles = multiple_testing_hurdles(factor_universe, alpha=0.05)

    print("--- Resolution 4: Factor Zoo Multiple Testing Hurdles ---")
    print(f"{'Candidate Factors (M)':<22} | {'Bonferroni t-Hurdle':<20} | {'Implied p-value'}")
    print("-" * 62)
    for m, h in zip(factor_universe, hurdles, strict=True):
        p_equiv = 2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(h))
        print(f"{m:<22} | {h:<20.3f} | {p_equiv:.6f}")

    # 2. Resolution 3: Value Factor (HML) Dynamics and Valuation Spread
    n_months = 180
    # Simulate pre-2010 positive alpha, post-2010 compression
    hml_pre = rng.normal(0.0035, 0.030, size=100)
    hml_post = rng.normal(-0.0010, 0.035, size=80)
    hml_all = np.concatenate([hml_pre, hml_post])

    # Valuation spread: B/M of value vs growth
    growth_bm = rng.normal(0.20, 0.02, size=n_months)
    value_bm = rng.normal(0.85, 0.08, size=n_months)
    # Stretch valuation spread during value drawdown
    value_bm[120:] += 0.35

    mean_spread, max_dd, ann_ret = analyze_value_spread_and_drawdown(hml_all, growth_bm, value_bm)

    print("\n--- Resolution 3: Is Value Dead? Factor Spread & Drawdowns ---")
    print(f"Full Sample Annualized HML Return: {ann_ret * 100.0:.2f}%")
    print(f"Pre-2010 Annualized HML Return:   {float(np.mean(hml_pre) * 12.0) * 100.0:.2f}%")
    print(f"Post-2010 Annualized HML Return:  {float(np.mean(hml_post) * 12.0) * 100.0:.2f}%")
    print(f"Average Value Spread (B/M Value / B/M Growth): {mean_spread:.2f}x")
    print(f"Maximum HML Drawdown:              {max_dd * 100.0:.2f}%")


main()

3. Where the Canon Breaks

In classical textbook factor models, anomaly premiums are assumed to represent stationary, tradeable alpha that persists indefinitely. In practice:

  • Microcap Distortion: Equal-weighted anomalies fail to scale to institutional capital because microcaps represent only 3% of aggregate market capitalization but account for over 80% of published anomaly profits.
  • Arbitrage Assimilation: Once an anomaly is publicized, quantitative hedge funds deploy capital, driving down mispricings and compressing post-publication returns.

4. Debate Briefs

Debate 3 · Resolution: "Value is dead."

Resolution: Value is dead.

Steelman For: Accounting metrics have broken down in knowledge-based economies. Traditional book value ignores intangible intellectual property, software, and human capital, incorrectly labeling modern technology monopolies as overvalued while classifying asset-heavy dying legacy firms as cheap. Over 2007–2020, value suffered a historic 13-year drawdown that broke standard risk models.

Steelman Against: Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013). Value underperformance was overwhelmingly driven by unprecedented valuation multiple divergence rather than catastrophic fundamental divergence. Value is a compensated financial distress risk factor (Fama & French 1993); the post-2020 violent inflation and interest rate resurgence delivered massive value outperformance, demonstrating cyclicality rather than death.

State of the Literature: Broad consensus that unadjusted book-to-market requires intangible asset adjustments, but strong evidence that valuation-based long-short strategies retain positive long-run expected premia.

What Would Settle It: A 20-year structural panel with intangibles-capitalized book equity where value stocks generate zero or negative alpha relative to the market and gross margins of value firms permanently fail to mean-revert.

Debate 4 · Resolution: "Most factors are false discoveries."

Resolution: Most factors are false discoveries.

Steelman For: Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) and Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020). Academic incentives encourage p-hacking and selective reporting. When tested against proper multiple-testing hurdles (\(|t| \ge 3.0\)) and evaluated on value-weighted institutional portfolios, over 65% of published anomalies fail to replicate.

Steelman Against: Chen & Zimmermann (2022). Cross-sectional anomalies are not statistical fictions: 82% of 205 replicated anomalies reproduce in direction and significance under independent open-source pipelines. Return decay is the expected outcome of market efficiency assimilating genuine insights, not proof of fraud or false discovery.

State of the Literature: General agreement that the classical \(|t| \ge 1.96\) standard is obsolete, and that institutional feasibility requires value-weighting and transaction cost hurdles.

What Would Settle It: A pre-registered 15-year forward evaluation of 200 published factors. If anomalies with in-sample \(t \in [2.0, 3.0]\) exhibit zero positive returns out-of-sample while \(t > 3.5\) factors persist, the false-discovery critique is definitively validated.

5. Misconceptions

  • A factor with an in-sample t-statistic of 2.1 has less than a 5% chance of being a false discovery.

    The 5% false positive rate applies only to a single pre-registered hypothesis. When researchers collectively test hundreds of potential predictor variables, the expected false discovery rate at \(t = 2.1\) exceeds 50% due to multiple hypothesis testing.

  • Value investing underperformance proves that fundamental cash flow valuation no longer matters.

    Underperformance can occur even when fundamental valuation is paramount: during speculative bubbles or multiple expansion regimes, prices diverge from fundamentals for prolonged horizons before violently mean-reverting.

  • An anomaly that fails to replicate under value-weighting was necessarily fabricated by the authors.

    Equal-weighted anomalies often capture real market phenomena that exist predominantly among small and illiquid stocks where limits to arbitrage prevent institutional capital from eliminating the mispricing.

6. Problem Set

Tier 1: Mechanics

Calculate the Bonferroni-adjusted critical t-hurdle for \(M = 500\) candidate factors at nominal significance \(\alpha = 0.01\).

Solution

The Bonferroni-adjusted significance level is \(\alpha^* = \frac{0.01}{500} = 0.00002\). The two-sided critical normal quantile is:

\[ t^* = \Phi^{-1}\left(1 - \frac{0.00002}{2}\right) = \Phi^{-1}(0.99999) \approx 4.265 \]

The required t-statistic hurdle is approximately 4.27.

If a factor generated an in-sample annualized return of 7.2%, calculate its expected post-publication return under the McLean-Pontiff (2016) 58% average decay benchmark.

Solution

Under McLean & Pontiff (2016), expected post-publication return decays by 58%:

\[ R_{\text{post}} = R_{\text{in-sample}} \times (1 - 0.58) = 7.2\% \times 0.42 = 3.024\% \]

The expected post-publication annualized return is approximately 3.02%.

Given value portfolio B/M of 1.20 and growth portfolio B/M of 0.25 at \(t=0\), and 1.50 and 0.20 at \(t=1\), compute the percentage change in the valuation spread \(\mathcal{VS}\).

Solution

The initial valuation spread is \(\mathcal{VS}_0 = 1.20 / 0.25 = 4.80\). The subsequent spread is \(\mathcal{VS}_1 = 1.50 / 0.20 = 7.50\). The percentage change is:

\[ \frac{7.50 - 4.80}{4.80} = \frac{2.70}{4.80} = 0.5625 \text{ (or } +56.25\% \text{)} \]

The valuation spread widened by 56.25%.

Tier 2: Proof

Prove that under the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure with independent test statistics, the False Discovery Rate (FDR) is bounded by \(\frac{m_0}{m} \alpha \le \alpha\).

Solution

Let \(m_0\) be the number of true null hypotheses out of \(m\) total tests. Order the p-values \(p_{(1)} \le \dots \le p_{(m)}\) and find \(k = \max \{i : p_{(i)} \le \frac{i}{m} \alpha\}\). Reject all \(H_{(i)}\) for \(i \le k\). The false discovery proportion is \(FDP = \frac{V}{R \vee 1}\), where \(V\) is false rejections and \(R = k\). Benjamini & Hochberg (1995) prove by induction on martingales that \(\E[FDP] = \sum_{i \in \text{Nulls}} \E\left[\frac{\indic\{p_i \le \frac{R}{m}\alpha\}}{R \vee 1}\right] = \frac{m_0}{m} \alpha \le \alpha\). \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that in the presence of positive correlation among test statistics, the Bonferroni hurdle remains a conservative upper bound on the family-wise error rate.

Solution

Let \(E_i\) denote the event that test \(i\) falsely rejects the null (\(|t_i| \ge t^*\)). By Boole's inequality (the union bound), \(\Prob(\bigcup_{i=1}^m E_i) \le \sum_{i=1}^m \Prob(E_i)\). Boole's inequality holds for arbitrary dependence structures, including positive correlation. When test statistics are positively correlated, \(\Prob(E_i \cap E_j) > \Prob(E_i)\Prob(E_j)\), meaning that \(\Prob(\bigcup E_i) < \sum \Prob(E_i)\). Hence, Bonferroni is strictly conservative. \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that capitalization of R&D expenditures increases stated book equity for growing firms.

Solution

Under GAAP, R&D is immediately expensed in period \(t\), reducing retained earnings by \(R\&D_t (1 - \tau)\). If capitalized and amortized over useful life \(L\) years with straight-line depreciation, current period expense is \(\frac{1}{L} \sum_{k=0}^{L-1} R\&D_{t-k}\). For a growing firm with \(R\&D_t > R\&D_{t-1}\), current amortization is strictly less than current R&D expenditure, leaving an asset \(Asset_t = \sum_{k=0}^{L-1} \left(1 - \frac{k}{L}\right) R\&D_{t-k} > 0\) on the balance sheet, which increases book equity by \((1-\tau) Asset_t\). \(\blacksquare\)

Tier 3: Empirical

In an empirical study of 100 anomaly factors, 30 factors have \(|t| \in [2.0, 2.5]\), 15 have \(|t| \in [2.5, 3.0]\), and 5 have \(|t| \ge 3.0\). How many factors pass the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle?

Solution

The Harvey-Liu-Zhu threshold requires \(|t| \ge 3.00\). Only the 5 factors with \(|t| \ge 3.0\) pass the hurdle, while the remaining 45 factors with \(|t| \in [2.0, 3.0)\) are rejected as likely false discoveries.

Explain why the replication rate of published anomalies in Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) drops from 80% under equal-weighting to 35% under value-weighting.

Solution

Equal-weighting allocates disproportionate influence to microcap stocks (the bottom 20% of NYSE market cap, which constitutes over 60% of all listed firms). Microcaps suffer severe liquidity constraints, high bid-ask spreads, and borrowing costs, creating apparent return anomalies that cannot be executed at scale in value-weighted institutional portfolios.

An asset management firm observes that its proprietary value strategy suffered a 40% drawdown while the value spread widened from 3.0x to 6.0x. What proportion of the drawdown is attributable to valuation multiple changes assuming equal fundamental growth?

Solution

If relative fundamental cash flows were unchanged, the return impact is given entirely by the change in the valuation spread: \(\Delta \ln \mathcal{VS} = \ln(6.0) - \ln(3.0) = \ln(2.0) \approx +0.693\). A doubling of growth multiples relative to value produces a price divergence of \((1/2 - 1) = -50\%\). Thus, 100% of the drawdown is explained by multiple expansion rather than fundamental deterioration.

Tier 4: Research

Propose a Bayesian hierarchical model to estimate true factor premia in the presence of publication bias.

Solution

Specify a prior distribution for true factor premia \(\mu_i \sim \pi_0 \delta_0 + (1-\pi_0) \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2)\) (spike-and-slab). The observed t-statistic reflects truncation: only studies with \(|t_i| \ge 1.96\) are submitted and published. Model the selection probability as \(P(\text{Publish} \mid t) = p_0 + (1-p_0)\indic\{|t| \ge 1.96\}\). Invert the truncated likelihood to recover unbiased posterior expectations \(\E[\mu_i \mid \text{Published}]\).

How does machine learning factor selection (e.g. Lasso, Elastic Net) compare with Bonferroni multiple testing correction?

Solution

Bonferroni tests factors marginally against a conservative univariate threshold, assuming worst-case non-cooperation. In contrast, Lasso penalization evaluates factors simultaneously in a joint multivariate regression, shrinking collinear redundant factors to zero and selecting an optimal sparse spanning basis.

Evaluate the economic rationale for why value investing might be a risk factor rather than a mispricing anomaly.

Solution

Value firms typically have high fixed capital, inflexible labor structures, and elevated financial leverage. In economic contractions, their operating leverage exacerbates earnings declines, making them unable to downsize capacity. Growth firms possess technological flexibility and growth options that can be deferred, making value assets intrinsically more exposed to aggregate distress shocks.

8. Retrieval Quiz

  1. What minimal t-statistic hurdle does Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) recommend for evaluating newly proposed equity factors?

    Options

    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) establish that accounting for hundreds of tested factors requires a t-statistic hurdle of at least 3.0.

  2. According to McLean & Pontiff (2016), what happens to anomaly returns following academic publication?

    Options

    Post-publication returns drop by approximately 58% on average across published equity anomalies.

  3. Why do Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) find that most published anomalies fail to achieve statistical significance?

    Options

    Value-weighting removes the undue influence of tiny, illiquid microcaps where equal-weighted anomaly profits are concentrated.

  4. What primarily accounted for the 2007–2020 drawdown in the value factor according to Asness (2020)?

    Options

    Value stocks became historically cheap relative to growth stocks as valuation multiples diverged to the 99th percentile.

  5. How does immediate expensing of intangible capital under GAAP distort standard book-to-market sorts?

    Options

    Expensing intangibles leaves knowledge capital off balance sheets, artificially lowering book equity for innovation-heavy firms.

  6. What do Chen & Zimmermann (2022) conclude regarding open-source replication of asset pricing factors?

    Options

    Chen & Zimmermann show that the vast majority of cross-sectional anomalies reproduce when faithfully implemented from open data.

  7. Why does the False Discovery Rate (FDR) differ from the Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER)?

    Options

    FDR bounds the proportion of discoveries that are spurious, providing greater statistical power than FWER in large exploratory universes.

  8. In quantitative equity research, why is winsorization preferred over raw data filtering?

    Options

    Winsorization limits the influence of erroneous data points or extreme microcap returns without removing stocks from the sample.
