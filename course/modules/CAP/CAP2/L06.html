Fiscal Macroeconomics & Microstructure: FTPL Inflation & High-Frequency Market Quality

The modern financial architecture operates across extreme timescales: macroeconomic price-level determination over multi-year fiscal cycles, and microsecond price discovery in electronic limit order books. Resolving whether post-pandemic inflation was a fiscal phenomenon and whether high-frequency trading genuinely improves market liquidity is essential for macro risk management and algorithmic execution design.

1. Resolution 11: FTPL Explains 2021–2023 Inflation

The post-pandemic inflation surge of 2021–2023 revived the fundamental debate between monetary and fiscal theories of price level determination. Classical New Keynesian economics attributes inflation to excess demand and cost-push supply shocks regulated by monetary policy interest rate rules. In contrast, the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (FTPL) treats the price level as the equilibrium market-clearing price of government debt.

Definition 6.1 (FTPL Government Valuation Equation)

Under the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (Cochrane 2023), the real market value of nominal government debt equals the present discounted value of expected future primary budget surpluses:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{B_{t-1}}{P_t} &= \E_t \sum_{j=0}^\infty \beta^j s_{t+j} \end{aligned} \]

where \(B_{t-1}\) is nominal public debt maturing at \(t\), \(P_t\) is the price level, \(\beta\) is the real discount factor, and \(s_{t+j}\) represents real primary surpluses (tax revenue minus government non-interest expenditures).

Theorem 6.1 · Unbacked Fiscal Expansion Price Impulse

Suppose the government distributes an unexpected nominal transfer \(\Delta B\) to households that is not accompanied by any expected increase in future primary surpluses (\(\Delta \E_t \sum \beta^j s_{t+j} = 0\)). The price level must jump immediately to equilibrate real debt with unchanged real backing:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\Delta P_t}{P_{t-1}} &= \frac{\Delta B}{B_{t-1}} = \frac{\Delta \text{Fiscal Deficit}}{\text{Initial Debt}} \end{aligned} \]

During 2020–2021, the US government enacted approximately \$5 trillion in cumulative fiscal transfers (about 20% of GDP) with debt/GDP initially near 100%. Under FTPL accounting, if households perceived these transfers as unbacked by future fiscal austerity, the cumulative required price level adjustment is \(\approx 20\%\), matching the realized cumulative US CPI surge from 2021 to 2023.

Remark 6.1 (The Supply-Chain and Phillips Curve Counter-Argument)

Bernanke & Blanchard (2023, 2025) counter that empirical decomposition of pandemic inflation reveals that initial 2021–2022 inflation was overwhelmingly driven by sector-specific supply bottlenecks (semiconductor shortages, shipping freight spikes) and global energy/food commodity shocks triggered by the Ukraine war, followed by labor market tightness (\(V/U\) vacancies-to-unemployment ratio). As supply bottlenecks resolved, inflation fell rapidly in 2023 without any corresponding primary fiscal surpluses, contradicting pure FTPL mechanics.

2. Resolution 12: HFT Improves Market Quality

In modern electronic financial markets, High-Frequency Trading (HFT) firms utilize proprietary low-latency infrastructure, automated market-making algorithms, and colocation facilities to execute millions of quotes and trades per second.

Definition 6.2 (Effective and Realized Spreads)

For a trade executed at price \(p_t\) with prevailing midpoint \(m_t\) and trade direction \(q_t \in \{-1, +1\}\), the effective spread \(ES_t\) and the \(\tau\)-period realized spread \(RS_t(\tau)\) are:

\[ \begin{aligned} ES_t &= 2 q_t (p_t - m_t) \\ RS_t(\tau) &= 2 q_t (p_t - m_{t+\tau}) \end{aligned} \]

The difference \(PI_t(\tau) = ES_t - RS_t(\tau) = 2 q_t (m_{t+\tau} - m_t)\) measures the permanent price impact, representing the adverse selection cost paid by market makers to informed counterparties.

Theorem 6.2 · Brogaard-Hendershott-Riordan Price Discovery Finding

Brogaard, Hendershott & Riordan (2014) analyze NASDAQ order-level data and demonstrate that HFTs facilitate price discovery by trading in the direction of permanent price changes:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Cov(\Delta m_{t+\tau}, q_t^{\text{HFT}}) &> 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Cov(\Delta m_{t+\tau}, q_t^{\text{non-HFT}}) < 0 \end{aligned} \]

HFT orders account for over 60% of price efficiency gains following macroeconomic and firm news releases. By deploying competitive automated quotes, HFTs compressed average quoted and effective spreads across large-cap US equities from over 15 basis points in 2000 to under 2.5 basis points by 2014.

Theorem 6.3 · Budish-Cramton-Shim Latency Arbitrage Tax

Budish, Cramton & Shim (2015) prove that continuous-time limit order books create an equilibrium market failure: whenever correlated asset prices jump on another exchange, symmetric speed races occur where HFT snipers attempt to pick off stale quotes before market makers can cancel. This generates a socially wasteful arms race where the latency arbitrage rent operates as a private tax, forcing liquidity providers to widen bid-ask spreads for regular institutional and retail investors.

Lab 1 · FTPL Inflation Accounting & High-Frequency Spread Analysis

We compute the theoretical cumulative price level impulse under FTPL unbacked fiscal transfers, and decompose high-frequency effective spreads into realized market-maker revenue and informed adverse selection.

"""Lab CAP2-L06-1: Empirical tests for the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level and HFT market quality.

Implements Cochrane (2023) fiscal surplus valuation equation inflation accounting,
and Hasbrouck (1991) / Brogaard et al. (2014) high-frequency quote-to-trade and
effective spread decomposition.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def ftpl_inflation_accounting(
    debt_gdp_ratio: float,
    fiscal_shock_pct_gdp: float,
    expected_surplus_response: float,
    real_rate: float = 0.015,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Decompose inflation shock under Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (Cochrane 2023).

    B_{t-1}/P_t = E_t sum beta^j s_{t+j}.
    Unbacked fiscal expansion Delta s_unbacked = Delta d - Delta E[sum s].
    Price level impulse: Delta P / P = Delta s_unbacked / (B/P).
    """
    unbacked_expansion = fiscal_shock_pct_gdp - expected_surplus_response
    implied_inflation = unbacked_expansion / debt_gdp_ratio
    return unbacked_expansion, implied_inflation, debt_gdp_ratio


def evaluate_hft_market_quality(
    quote_updates: np.ndarray,
    trade_counts: np.ndarray,
    effective_spreads_bps: np.ndarray,
    realized_spreads_bps: np.ndarray,
) -> tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    """Compute quote-to-trade ratio, effective spread, and adverse selection cost."""
    # Quote-to-trade ratio
    q_to_t = float(np.sum(quote_updates) / np.sum(trade_counts))

    mean_eff = float(np.mean(effective_spreads_bps))
    mean_real = float(np.mean(realized_spreads_bps))
    # Price impact / adverse selection: Effective spread - Realized spread
    adverse_selection = mean_eff - mean_real

    return q_to_t, mean_eff, mean_real, adverse_selection


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Resolution 11: FTPL and 2021-2023 Inflation
    # US Debt/GDP ~ 100% (1.0). Total pandemic fiscal transfers ~ $5T (20% of GDP).
    # If primary surpluses are expected to cover 40% of the stimulus, unbacked is 12% of GDP.
    debt_gdp = 1.00
    fiscal_shock = 0.20  # 20% of GDP
    surplus_backed = 0.08  # 8% of GDP backed by future surpluses

    unbacked, cum_inflation, _ = ftpl_inflation_accounting(debt_gdp, fiscal_shock, surplus_backed)

    print("--- Resolution 11: FTPL Explains 2021-2023 Inflation ---")
    print(f"Initial Debt-to-GDP Ratio:        {debt_gdp * 100.0:.1f}%")
    print(f"Total Fiscal Expansion:           {fiscal_shock * 100.0:.1f}% of GDP")
    print(f"Future Backed Primary Surpluses:  {surplus_backed * 100.0:.1f}% of GDP")
    print(f"Unbacked Fiscal Expansion:        {unbacked * 100.0:.1f}% of GDP")
    print(f"FTPL Predicted Cumulative Inflation: {cum_inflation * 100.0:.2f}%")

    # 2. Resolution 12: HFT and Market Quality
    # 100 trading sessions across modern electronic limit order books
    rng = np.random.default_rng(2014)
    trades = rng.integers(5000, 20000, size=100)
    # HFT quote-to-trade ratio typically 20:1 to 60:1
    quote_mult = rng.uniform(25.0, 50.0, size=100)
    quotes = (trades * quote_mult).astype(np.int64)

    eff_spreads = rng.normal(3.2, 0.4, size=100)  # bps
    real_spreads = rng.normal(1.1, 0.3, size=100)  # bps

    q_ratio, eff_s, real_s, adv_sel = evaluate_hft_market_quality(quotes, trades, eff_spreads, real_spreads)

    print("\n--- Resolution 12: Does HFT Improve Market Quality? ---")
    print(f"Aggregate Quote-to-Trade Ratio: {q_ratio:.1f} : 1")
    print(f"Average Effective Spread:      {eff_s:.2f} bps")
    print(f"Average Realized Spread:       {real_s:.2f} bps")
    print(f"Informed Adverse Selection:    {adv_sel:.2f} bps")
    print(f"Net Liquidity Assessment:      {'LIQUIDITY ENHANCING' if eff_s < 5.0 else 'TOXIC'}")


main()

3. Where the Canon Breaks

Textbook models assume frictionless Walrasian auctioneers and purely Ricardian fiscal regimes:

  • Phantom Liquidity & Flash Crashes: HFT market makers provide massive quote depth during calm conditions, but withdraw quotes within milliseconds when volatility or toxic flow spikes (Easley, Lopez de Prado & O'Hara 2012), triggering structural flash crashes.
  • Fiscal-Monetary Policy Coordination Breakdown: When monetary authorities aggressively raise interest rates to combat inflation while the government runs multi-trillion deficits, higher interest expense on public debt increases fiscal deficits, fueling the very inflation the central bank seeks to extinguish.

4. Debate Briefs

Debate 11 · Resolution: "FTPL explains 2021–2023 inflation."

Resolution: FTPL explains 2021–2023 inflation.

Steelman For: Cochrane (2023). Decades of quantitative easing with zero interest rates produced zero inflation because bank reserves were backed by assets. In 2020–2021, the government directly sent trillions in stimulus checks and transfers to households without any corresponding plan for future tax increases or entitlement reforms. This unbacked fiscal injection inflated the price level until the real value of government debt dropped to match expected future surpluses.

Steelman Against: Bernanke & Blanchard (2025). Inflation surged globally across nations with vastly different fiscal policies, including countries with balanced budgets. The timing coincides exactly with energy supply shocks and freight shipping bottlenecks. The subsequent disinflation of 2023 occurred while US fiscal deficits actually widened to 6% of GDP, completely refuting the claim that inflation was driven by primary surplus expectations.

State of the Literature: Active intellectual conflict between New Keynesian economists attributing the episode to supply shocks and loose Fed policy, and fiscal theorists asserting that monetary policy is impotent without fiscal backing.

What Would Settle It: A natural policy experiment in a large developed economy featuring a massive fiscal cash disbursement during tranquil supply chains with an independent central bank holding short rates constant; if inflation surges proportionally to the unbacked debt shock, FTPL is confirmed.

Debate 12 · Resolution: "HFT improves market quality."

Resolution: HFT improves market quality.

Steelman For: Brogaard, Hendershott & Riordan (2014). Empirical bid-ask spreads have collapsed by over 80% since the advent of electronic HFT market making. Retail investors receive price improvement on virtually all retail equity orders. HFT algorithmic market makers absorb transitory imbalances and eliminate stale pricing across fragmented global venues within milliseconds.

Steelman Against: Budish, Cramton & Shim (2015). The continuous-time market design creates an unceasing arms race where high-frequency snipers pick off stale quotes. This imposes an adverse selection tax on passive limit orders, causing displayed market depth to be hollow "phantom liquidity." Large institutional orders suffer significant market impact, and markets become vulnerable to sudden liquidity blackouts.

State of the Literature: Broad agreement that HFT dramatically lowered trading costs for small retail orders, but consensus that latency arbitrage creates negative externalities that could be mitigated via frequent batch auctions.

What Would Settle It: A randomized control trial or mandatory exchange transition where an active exchange switches from continuous limit order books to 100-millisecond frequent batch auctions; if institutional execution slippage declines while displayed depth doubles, the latency arbitrage tax hypothesis is established.

5. Misconceptions

  • The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level assumes that governments intentionally default on their debt.

    FTPL assumes that nominal sovereign debt is always paid in full in nominal terms; adjustment occurs through the price level (inflation), which deflates the real purchasing power of debt to match expected real primary surpluses.

  • High-frequency trading firms make profits by front-running retail orders in the broker order flow.

    Wholesale market makers internalize uninformed retail flow legally through payment for order flow, offering price improvement. The primary battleground for latency races is cross-venue arbitrage on public exchanges (sniping stale displayed quotes).

  • Tightening monetary policy by raising interest rates always reduces inflation under all fiscal conditions.

    Under fiscal dominance, raising interest rates increases government debt servicing costs. If the fiscal authority does not raise taxes to cover higher interest expense, the newly issued debt increases fiscal transfers to bondholders, generating additional inflationary pressure.

6. Problem Set

Tier 1: Mechanics

Under the FTPL valuation equation, if outstanding debt is \$20 trillion, the initial price level index is \(P_0 = 100\), and households revise expected discounted future primary surpluses from \$20 trillion to \$16 trillion, compute the new equilibrium price level \(P_1\).

Solution

Under FTPL, \(\frac{B}{P_1} = \text{Surpluses}\). Substituting:

\[ \frac{\$20 \text{T}}{P_1} = \frac{\$16 \text{T}}{P_0} \implies P_1 = P_0 \times \frac{\$20}{\$16} = 100 \times 1.25 = 125 \]

The price level rises by 25% to \(P_1 = 125\).

A trader buys at ask price \$50.05 when the midpoint is \$50.02. Five minutes later, the midpoint is \$50.04. Calculate the effective spread and the 5-minute realized spread in cents.

Solution

The buy trade has direction \(q = +1\). The effective spread is:

\[ ES = 2 \times (+1) \times (\$50.05 - \$50.02) = 2 \times \$0.03 = \$0.06 \text{ (6.0 cents)} \]

The 5-minute realized spread is:

\[ RS = 2 \times (+1) \times (\$50.05 - \$50.04) = 2 \times \$0.01 = \$0.02 \text{ (2.0 cents)} \]

The effective spread is 6 cents, and the market-maker's realized revenue is 2 cents (4 cents lost to adverse selection).

An exchange executes 40,000 trades during a trading day while processing 1,600,000 quote updates. Calculate the quote-to-trade ratio.

Solution

The quote-to-trade ratio is:

\[ \text{Ratio} = \frac{1,600,000}{40,000} = 40.0 \]

The quote-to-trade ratio is 40 to 1.

Tier 2: Proof

Prove that in the Budish, Cramton & Shim (2015) continuous-time limit order book, a perfectly competitive market maker must widen their bid-ask spread in proportion to the arrival rate of latency arbitrage snipers.

Solution

Let \(\lambda_{\text{invest}}\) be the arrival rate of non-toxic institutional/retail order flow, and let \(\lambda_{\text{snipe}}\) be the arrival rate of correlated price jumps where latency snipers race to pick off stale quotes. On each retail trade, the market maker earns half the spread \(s/2\). On each sniping event, the market maker loses \(L\), where \(L\) is the magnitude of the jump. Zero expected profit under competitive market making requires:

\[ \E[\Pi] = \lambda_{\text{invest}} \frac{s}{2} - \lambda_{\text{snipe}} L = 0 \implies s = \frac{2 \lambda_{\text{snipe}} L}{\lambda_{\text{invest}}} \]

The equilibrium spread \(s\) is strictly proportional to the sniping arrival rate \(\lambda_{\text{snipe}}\) and jump size \(L\). \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that under the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level, monetary policy cannot achieve price stability if the fiscal authority follows an active, non-Ricardian fiscal policy.

Solution

In an active fiscal regime (Leeper 1991), future primary surpluses \(\{s_t\}\) are determined exogenously by political constraints and do not adjust to satisfy the intertemporal government budget constraint. By the government debt valuation equation, \(\frac{B_{t-1}}{P_t} = \E_t \sum_{j=0}^\infty \beta^j s_{t+j}\). If the central bank attempts to peg the price level \(\bar{P}\) through interest rate policy, the left-hand side is fixed. However, any shock to expected surpluses \(\Delta \E_t \sum \beta^j s_{t+j}\) alters the right-hand side, violating equality unless \(P_t\) adjusts. Thus, monetary policy cannot stabilize \(P_t\) without fiscal backing. \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that the permanent price impact \(PI_t(\tau) = ES_t - RS_t(\tau)\) reflects the private information component of the order.

Solution

Let price dynamics follow the Glosten-Milgrom / Hasbrouck framework: \(m_{t+\tau} = m_t + \lambda q_t + \eta_{t+\tau}\), where \(\lambda q_t\) is the permanent information content of the trade direction \(q_t\), and \(\E_t[\eta_{t+\tau}] = 0\). The effective spread is \(ES_t = 2 q_t (p_t - m_t)\). The realized spread is \(RS_t(\tau) = 2 q_t (p_t - m_{t+\tau}) = 2 q_t (p_t - (m_t + \lambda q_t + \eta_{t+\tau}))\). Taking the difference:

\[ ES_t - RS_t(\tau) = 2 q_t \left( p_t - m_t - (p_t - m_t - \lambda q_t - \eta_{t+\tau}) \right) = 2 q_t (\lambda q_t + \eta_{t+\tau}) \]

Taking expectations, \(\E[ES_t - RS_t(\tau)] = 2 \lambda q_t^2 = 2 \lambda\), which is identically twice the permanent information footprint \(\lambda\). \(\blacksquare\)

Tier 3: Empirical

In an empirical analysis of US Treasury market making, high-frequency quote cancellations account for 96% of all order messages. Interpret this metric in terms of inventory management vs latency defense.

Solution

A 96% cancellation rate (quote-to-trade ratio > 25:1) reflects both dynamic inventory shading and defensive cancellation. Market makers continuously update quotes to avoid adverse selection from latency snipers who monitor correlated Chicago futures (e.g. 10-year Treasury futures at CME). The high cancellation rate represents the cost of defending passive limit orders in continuous time.

In 2023, US inflation declined from 9.1% to 3.1% while the federal budget deficit widened from \$1.38 trillion to \$1.70 trillion. How do critics of FTPL use this observation?

Solution

Under FTPL, widening fiscal deficits without credible future tax increases should expand inflation pressure. The sharp drop in inflation concurrent with widening deficits strongly supports the New Keynesian / supply-side view that 2021–2022 inflation was driven by transient supply-chain snarls and energy spikes that subsequently unwound, rather than fiscal surplus expectations.

An empirical study of an equity stock measures an effective spread of 2.4 bps and a 5-minute realized spread of 0.6 bps. Calculate the adverse selection price impact in bps.

Solution

Adverse selection is the difference between effective and realized spreads:

\[ PI = ES - RS = 2.4 \text{ bps} - 0.6 \text{ bps} = 1.8 \text{ bps} \]

Informed adverse selection accounts for 1.8 basis points (or 75%) of the total effective spread.

Tier 4: Research

Design an empirical test to evaluate whether frequent batch auctions eliminate latency arbitrage in live foreign exchange markets.

Solution

Exploit an exchange transition (such as EBS or Euronext adopting 100ms batch auctions). Measure the cross-market correlation of price updates across venues, the volume of cancel orders executed within 1 millisecond of price jumps on secondary exchanges, and the average effective spread for institutional parent orders. If sniping cancels collapse to zero and institutional effective spreads decline significantly post-transition, the Budish-Cramton-Shim hypothesis is confirmed.

How does the maturity structure of government debt affect the inflation impulse under the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level?

Solution

If government debt is exclusively short-term (floating rate), an unbacked fiscal shock forces an immediate jump in the price level \(P_t\). If the government has long-duration nominal debt, the price of long-term bonds can decline instead (term premium and yield rise), spreading the inflation impulse over multiple years. Long-term debt cushions immediate inflation by absorbing part of the fiscal shock via bondholder capital losses.

Evaluate the systemic risk implications of high-frequency market making under extreme market volatility.

Solution

Because HFT market makers operate with minimal proprietary capital and tight internal risk limits, they do not have affirmative obligations to maintain orderly markets. In extreme tail events, automated risk-management algorithms simultaneously shut down quoting across all venues, producing sudden catastrophic drops in market depth (liquidity black holes) and triggering severe price dislocations.
