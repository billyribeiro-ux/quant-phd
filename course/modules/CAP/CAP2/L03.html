International & Central Banking: Quantitative Easing & The UIP Puzzle

Modern monetary economics and global macro investing hinge upon two contested transmission mechanisms: whether central bank balance sheet expansion depresses yields by altering the market supply of duration, and whether the persistent profitability of foreign exchange carry trades reflects compensation for tail risk or systematic behavioral market failure.

1. Resolution 5: QE Works via Portfolio Balance

Following the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, central banks engaged in large-scale asset purchases (Quantitative Easing, or QE) at the zero lower bound. The central macroeconomic controversy is whether QE operates primarily via a portfolio balance channel (removing duration risk from private hands) or a signaling channel (committing to lower future short rates).

Definition 3.1 (Portfolio Balance Channel)

The portfolio balance channel posits that due to market segmentation and preferred habitats, imperfect substitution exists across asset maturities and credit classes. When a central bank purchases long-term Treasury bonds and agency mortgage-backed securities, it reduces the net duration supply held by private investors, compressing term and duration premia across all close substitute assets.

Theorem 3.1 · Krishnamurthy-Vissing-Jorgensen Yield Decomposition

Let an asset's nominal yield \(y_{i, t}^{(\tau)}\) decompose into expectations of short rates, a duration term premium, a safety premium, and a default risk premium:

\[ \begin{aligned} y_{i, t}^{(\tau)} &= \frac{1}{\tau} \sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1} \E_t\left[ r_{t+j} \right] + TP_t^{(\tau)} + SP_{i, t}^{(\tau)} + DP_{i, t}^{(\tau)} \end{aligned} \]

Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2011) demonstrate using high-frequency event study windows around QE1 announcements that 10-year Treasury yields fell by 107 basis points, while Baa-rated corporate bonds fell by only 40 basis points, and lower-rated credit yields barely responded. This differential response confirms distinct safety and preferred habitat channels rather than a uniform reduction in policy rate expectations.

Remark 3.1 (The Signaling Counter-Perspective)

Critics, notably Woodford (2004), argue that in a frictionless benchmark satisfying Wallace (1981) neutrality, open market purchases of government bonds for central bank reserves have zero effect on equilibrium allocations because the private sector internalizes the government's consolidated balance sheet. Therefore, QE functions primarily as forward guidance signaling that short rates will remain near zero for longer than previously anticipated.

2. Resolution 6: UIP Failure Is a Risk Premium

Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP) states that the expected rate of depreciation of a domestic currency against a foreign currency should equal the nominal interest rate differential between the two countries. Formally, let \(S_t\) be the spot exchange rate (domestic units per foreign currency unit), and \(s_t = \ln S_t\). Let \(F_{t, t+1}\) be the one-period forward exchange rate, with \(f_t = \ln F_{t, t+1}\). By Covered Interest Parity (CIP), \(f_t - s_t = i_t - i_t^*\).

Theorem 3.2 · Fama Forward Discount Regression & UIP Puzzle

Consider the empirical regression of realized currency depreciation on the forward premium:

\[ \begin{aligned} s_{t+1} - s_t &= \alpha + \beta (f_t - s_t) + \epsilon_{t+1} \end{aligned} \]

Under UIP and rational expectations, \(\alpha = 0\) and \(\beta = 1\). Fama (1984) established empirically across G10 currencies that \(\hat{\beta}\) is consistently negative, averaging \(\hat{\beta} \approx -0.8\) to \(-1.2\). High-interest-rate currencies tend to appreciate rather than depreciate, generating persistent excess returns for the foreign exchange carry trade.

Proof

Let the expected excess return (currency risk premium) be \(p_t = \E_t[s_{t+1}] - f_t\). Under rational expectations, realized depreciation is \(s_{t+1} - s_t = (f_t - s_t) + p_t + \eta_{t+1}\), where \(\E_t[\eta_{t+1}] = 0\). The OLS slope estimator in Fama's regression is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta &= \frac{\Cov(s_{t+1} - s_t, f_t - s_t)}{\Var(f_t - s_t)} = \frac{\Var(f_t - s_t) + \Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)}{\Var(f_t - s_t)} = 1 + \frac{\Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)}{\Var(f_t - s_t)} \end{aligned} \]

A negative slope estimate \(\beta < 0\) strictly requires:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t) &< -\Var(f_t - s_t) < 0 \end{aligned} \]

and consequently, \(\Var(p_t) > \Var(f_t - s_t)\). Thus, for UIP failure to reflect a risk premium, the currency risk premium must be time-varying, negatively correlated with the interest differential, and more volatile than the interest differential itself. \(\blacksquare\)

Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen (2008) and Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011) demonstrate that currency carry trades exhibit pronounced negative skewness and systemic crash risk: carry returns collapse during global liquidity freezes when funding constraints bind, compensating investors for non-diversifiable volatility risk.

Conversely, Burnside et al. (2010) argue against a pure risk explanation by demonstrating that the Sharpe ratio of hedged carry trade portfolios remains high even after hedging crash risk with out-of-the-money currency options, pointing toward delayed overshooting and central bank monetary frictions.

Lab 1 · QE Announcement Decompositions & Fama UIP Regressions

We analyze event-study yield reactions across Treasuries and corporate credit to isolate QE transmission channels, and estimate Fama's forward discount regression to evaluate the empirical failure of UIP.

"""Lab CAP2-L03-1: Empirical tests for QE transmission channels and UIP failure.

Implements Krishnamurthy-Vissing-Jorgensen (2011) event-study yield response
decomposition across safe and credit assets, and Fama (1984) forward premium
regressions for uncovered interest parity.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats


def evaluate_qe_announcement_effects(
    event_yield_changes: dict[str, np.ndarray],
) -> dict[str, tuple[float, float, float]]:
    """Compute mean 2-day announcement yield changes, t-statistics, and p-values."""
    results = {}
    for asset, changes in event_yield_changes.items():
        m = float(np.mean(changes))
        se = float(np.std(changes, ddof=1) / np.sqrt(len(changes)))
        t_stat = m / se if se > 1e-12 else 0.0
        p_val = float(2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(t_stat))))
        results[asset] = (m, t_stat, p_val)
    return results


def fama_uip_regression(
    spot_depreciation: np.ndarray,
    forward_premium: np.ndarray,
) -> tuple[float, float, float, float, float]:
    """Estimate Fama (1984) forward discount regression: Delta s_{t+1} = alpha + beta (f_t - s_t) + e."""
    x = forward_premium
    y = spot_depreciation
    n = len(x)

    x_mean = float(np.mean(x))
    y_mean = float(np.mean(y))

    ss_xx = float(np.sum((x - x_mean) ** 2))
    ss_xy = float(np.sum((x - x_mean) * (y - y_mean)))

    beta = ss_xy / ss_xx
    alpha = y_mean - beta * x_mean

    resid = y - (alpha + beta * x)
    s_sq = float(np.sum(resid**2) / (n - 2))
    se_beta = float(np.sqrt(s_sq / ss_xx))
    t_beta_zero = float(beta / se_beta)

    # Test H0: beta == 1 (Uncovered Interest Parity)
    t_beta_one = float((beta - 1.0) / se_beta)
    p_uip = float(2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(t_beta_one))))

    return alpha, beta, se_beta, t_beta_zero, p_uip


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(2011)

    # 1. Resolution 5: QE Announcement Window Yield Responses (bps)
    # 5 major QE1 announcements (Nov 2008 - Mar 2009)
    # Long Treasury, Agency MBS, Baa Corporate
    qe_events = {
        "10Y_Treasury": np.array([-38.0, -18.0, -14.0, -47.0, -11.0]),
        "Agency_MBS": np.array([-44.0, -29.0, -20.0, -52.0, -15.0]),
        "Baa_Corporate": np.array([-15.0, -8.0, -12.0, -28.0, -7.0]),
    }

    qe_res = evaluate_qe_announcement_effects(qe_events)

    print("--- Resolution 5: QE Transmission & Portfolio Balance ---")
    print(f"{'Asset Class':<16} | {'Mean Change (bps)':<18} | {'t-stat':<10} | {'p-value'}")
    print("-" * 56)
    for asset, (mean_chg, t_val, p_val) in qe_res.items():
        print(f"{asset:<16} | {mean_chg:<18.1f} | {t_val:<10.2f} | {p_val:.4f}")

    # 2. Resolution 6: UIP Failure and Forward Discount Puzzle
    # Simulate 120 monthly currency observations
    fp = rng.normal(0.002, 0.006, size=120)  # interest rate differential (f - s)
    # Under empirical puzzle: beta is negative (~ -0.80), carry currency appreciates
    noise = rng.normal(0.0, 0.025, size=120)
    delta_s = 0.001 - 0.85 * fp + noise

    alpha, beta, se_beta, t_zero, p_uip = fama_uip_regression(delta_s, fp)

    print("\n--- Resolution 6: UIP Failure & Currency Risk Premia ---")
    print(f"Fama Regression Intercept (alpha): {alpha:.5f}")
    print(f"Fama Regression Slope (beta):     {beta:.3f} (SE: {se_beta:.3f})")
    print(f"t-statistic against zero (beta=0): {t_zero:.3f}")
    print(f"UIP Hypothesis Test (H0: beta=1):  p-value = {p_uip:.5f}")
    print(f"UIP Rejection Status:              {'REJECTED' if p_uip < 0.05 else 'ACCEPTED'}")


main()

3. Where the Canon Breaks

Standard open-economy macroeconomic models assume frictionless financial intermediation and pure rational expectations:

  • Balance Sheet Constraints: When primary dealer banks face regulatory leverage constraints (such as the Supplementary Leverage Ratio), they cannot act as unconstrained arbitrageurs, causing Covered Interest Parity (CIP) deviations to open up alongside UIP breakdowns.
  • Crash Asymmetry: Currency carry returns resemble selling out-of-the-money puts: long periods of small steady gains punctuated by sudden, violent devaluations.

4. Debate Briefs

Debate 5 · Resolution: "QE works via portfolio balance."

Resolution: QE works via portfolio balance.

Steelman For: Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2011) and Bernanke (2020). By absorbing duration and prepayment risk, the central bank frees up risk capacity in the private financial sector. Asset price responses are heterogeneous across asset classes, with mortgage and long-duration assets gaining the most, which is impossible if QE were merely forward guidance on the short rate.

Steelman Against: Woodford (2004). Wallace neutrality implies that central bank swaps of bonds for reserves have zero effect on consumption or pricing unless they signal future short-rate paths. In empirical studies, term premia declines are often confounded with simultaneous revisions to policy rate path expectations.

State of the Literature: Empirical consensus supports both channels: portfolio balance operates strongly during crisis periods with severe intermediary constraints, while signaling dominates during tranquil market operations.

What Would Settle It: A policy event in which a central bank conducts massive asset purchases while simultaneously and credibly raising its path of policy rates; if long-term yields decline despite rising short-rate paths, the portfolio balance mechanism is decisively proven.

Debate 6 · Resolution: "UIP failure is a risk premium."

Resolution: UIP failure is a risk premium.

Steelman For: Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011) and Brunnermeier et al. (2008). High-interest currencies systematically collapse during global market crashes, precisely when marginal utility is high. The excess return is compensation for bearing negative skewness and liquidity disaster risk.

Steelman Against: Burnside et al. (2010). Hedging the crash risk of carry trades using deep out-of-the-money currency put options does not erase the strategy's Sharpe ratio. Furthermore, the volatility of stochastic discount factors required to rationalize negative Fama beta coefficients exceeds any plausible macroeconomic pricing kernel.

State of the Literature: Broad recognition of non-linear risk exposure, but persistent behavioral anomalies (investor inattention and delayed overshooting) remain necessary to explain the magnitude of the puzzle.

What Would Settle It: Evaluation of synthetic digital currencies with automated interest rates and zero sovereign risk; if forward discount regressions on synthetic tokens still yield negative beta coefficients, behavioral frictions are proven.

5. Misconceptions

  • Quantitative Easing is pure money printing that mechanically causes high inflation immediately.

    QE is an asset swap of government bonds for interest-bearing central bank reserves. If banks hold these reserves rather than expanding broad credit, aggregate broad money supply and consumer prices remain unimpacted.

  • A negative beta in Fama's regression proves that currency markets violate no-arbitrage conditions.

    Uncovered Interest Parity is not an arbitrage relationship because exchange rate risk is unhedged. A violation of UIP merely indicates that currency risk premia are non-zero and time-varying, fully consistent with absence of arbitrage.

  • Covered Interest Parity (CIP) and Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP) are the same concept.

    CIP involves buying a currency forward to hedge exchange rate risk, representing a pure no-arbitrage condition. UIP leaves exchange rate risk unhedged, representing an economic hypothesis about expected returns.

6. Problem Set

Tier 1: Mechanics

If the 1-year US nominal interest rate is 5.0% and the 1-year Eurozone rate is 2.0%, calculate the forward premium \((F - S) / S\) under Covered Interest Parity.

Solution

Under Covered Interest Parity:

\[ \frac{F - S}{S} = \frac{1 + i_{\text{US}}}{1 + i_{\text{EUR}}} - 1 = \frac{1.05}{1.02} - 1 \approx 0.02941 \text{ (or } +2.94\% \text{)} \]

The forward dollar trades at an approximate 2.94% premium to compensate for the higher US interest rate.

In a Fama regression, \(\hat{\alpha} = 0.002\) and \(\hat{\beta} = -0.80\). If the forward premium is \(f_t - s_t = 0.03\), what is the expected currency depreciation \(\E[s_{t+1} - s_t]\)?

Solution

Using the estimated Fama regression:

\[ \E[s_{t+1} - s_t] = 0.002 + (-0.80) \times 0.03 = 0.002 - 0.024 = -0.022 \text{ (or } -2.20\% \text{)} \]

The high-interest currency is expected to appreciate by 2.20%, intensifying the carry trade profit.

During a central bank QE announcement, 10-year Treasury yields fall by 30 bps, of which 18 bps is estimated to be term premium reduction. What percentage is attributable to the portfolio balance channel?

Solution

The proportion attributable to term premium compression is:

\[ \frac{18 \text{ bps}}{30 \text{ bps}} = 0.60 \text{ (or } 60\% \text{)} \]

60% of the yield decline is attributable to duration risk removal via portfolio balance.

Tier 2: Proof

Prove that a negative slope in the Fama regression (\(\beta < 0\)) implies that the variance of the currency risk premium must exceed the variance of the forward premium.

Solution

From Theorem 3.2, \(\beta = 1 + \frac{\Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)}{\Var(f_t - s_t)}\). If \(\beta < 0\), then:

\[ \frac{\Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)}{\Var(f_t - s_t)} < -1 \implies \Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t) < -\Var(f_t - s_t) \]

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, \(|\Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)| \le \sigma_p \sigma_{f-s}\). Combining these:

\[ \sigma_p \sigma_{f-s} \ge |\Cov(p_t, f_t - s_t)| > \sigma_{f-s}^2 \implies \sigma_p > \sigma_{f-s} \]

Squaring both sides yields \(\Var(p_t) > \Var(f_t - s_t)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that under log-normality, the stochastic discount factors in two countries \(M_t\) and \(M_t^*\) uniquely determine the exchange rate depreciation under complete markets.

Solution

Under complete markets and absence of arbitrage, the pricing kernel for foreign currency returns must articulate with domestic returns: \(S_{t+1} / S_t = M_{t+1}^* / M_{t+1}\). Taking natural logarithms:

\[ s_{t+1} - s_t = m_{t+1}^* - m_{t+1} \]

The exchange rate change is identically the difference between the foreign and domestic log stochastic discount factors. \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that in the Vayanos & Vila (2021) preferred habitat model, central bank purchases of a specific bond maturity lower yields across adjacent maturities.

Solution

In the Vayanos-Vila model, risk-averse arbitrageurs intermediate between preferred-habitat clientele across the yield curve. Arbitrageurs hold a continuum of maturities, pricing bonds via a no-arbitrage cross-maturity factor model. A supply reduction \(\Delta S_\tau < 0\) at maturity \(\tau\) reduces arbitrageurs' net duration exposure. Because arbitrageurs smooth exposure across maturities through curve trading, the shadow price of duration risk falls along the entire curve, lowering yields for neighboring maturities \(\tau \pm \epsilon\). \(\blacksquare\)

Tier 3: Empirical

In an empirical test of 10 currency pairs over 20 years, an FX carry trade strategy yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.82 with a skewness of -1.45. Interpret this risk profile in light of Brunnermeier et al. (2008).

Solution

The high Sharpe ratio combined with pronounced negative skewness (-1.45) confirms the "eating steamroller pennies" profile documented by Brunnermeier et al. (2008). Carry trades deliver steady returns in tranquil regimes but experience severe left-tail drawdowns when global funding liquidity freezes, consistent with disaster risk compensation.

An event study of a 100-billion central bank bond purchase finds that 10-year yields dropped 8 bps upon announcement, but 3-month policy rate expectations derived from OIS swaps were unchanged. Which channel is supported?

Solution

Because short-rate expectations (OIS swaps) were completely unchanged, the signaling channel is ruled out. The 8 bps yield compression reflects pure term premium reduction, providing direct empirical evidence in favor of the portfolio balance channel.

Suppose a researcher estimates a Fama regression using robust Newey-West standard errors and finds \(\hat{\beta} = -0.75\) with a standard error of 0.35. Test the null hypothesis of UIP (\(\beta = 1\)) at the 5% level.

Solution

The test statistic for \(H_0: \beta = 1\) is:

\[ t = \frac{\hat{\beta} - 1.0}{\text{SE}} = \frac{-0.75 - 1.00}{0.35} = \frac{-1.75}{0.35} = -5.00 \]

Since \(|t| = 5.00 > 1.96\), the p-value is less than \(10^{-6}\). The null hypothesis of Uncovered Interest Parity is decisively rejected.

Tier 4: Research

How does post-2008 Covered Interest Parity (CIP) failure complicate empirical tests of Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP)?

Solution

Prior to 2008, CIP held almost perfectly due to non-arbitrage in FX swaps, meaning the forward premium \(f - s\) equaled the risk-free interest differential \(i - i^*\). Post-2008, bank balance sheet constraints (Basel III, SLR) introduced persistent cross-currency basis spreads. A regression using forward contracts now tests both UIP and the cross-currency basis friction simultaneously.

Formulate a structural model in which Quantitative Easing operates simultaneously through liquidity, default, and duration channels.

Solution

Specify a three-factor pricing model where bond yields depend on duration risk (aggregate market maturity), liquidity risk (bid-ask or Treasury-OIS spread), and credit risk. Central bank purchases of Treasuries reduce aggregate duration; purchases of commercial paper or corporate debt reduce liquidity and default spreads. Event studies of announcement surprises across different asset classes isolate the relative weights of these three channels.

Evaluate whether the peso problem explanation for UIP failure can be tested using currency option smiles.

Solution

Under a peso problem, the market anticipates a rare devaluation that has not yet occurred in-sample. If rational, the option-implied risk-neutral density from out-of-the-money puts must price this catastrophe, generating a steep volatility smile. Comparing the option-implied disaster probability with historical currency crashes tests whether the carry premium is fair insurance or an unhedged arbitrage.
