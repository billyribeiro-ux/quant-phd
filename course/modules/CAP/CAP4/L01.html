Research Question Selection & Novelty Discovery

Selecting a doctoral research question determines whether a dissertation produces enduring economic knowledge or collapses into derivative data mining. Candidates must formulate non-trivial, falsifiable hypotheses that resolve theoretical paradoxes or empirical anomalies without falling victim to multiple-testing data snooping. This lesson formalizes question selection criteria, novelty discovery metrics, and empirical Bayes false discovery hurdles.

1. The Research Question Selection Criterion

In quantitative financial economics, the transition from consumer of research to producer of original scholarship begins with isolating a first-order economic question.

Definition 1.1 (Research-question selection) Research-question selection is the formal doctoral process of identifying, evaluating, and bounding an unresolved economic problem such that resolving it discriminates between competing theoretical mechanisms, provides actionable risk pricing bounds, and survives empirical multiple-testing hurdles.

A viable doctoral question must satisfy three simultaneous conditions: economic materiality, empirical falsifiability, and institutional tractability. As observed by Cochrane (2011), research advances when scholars move beyond documenting isolated predictability patterns to explaining the economic identity linking discount rate variation to macroeconomic fundamentals.

Theorem 1.1 (Multiple-Testing Significance Hurdle) Let \(\{H_0^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^M\) be a family of \(M\) orthogonal null hypotheses tested at nominal significance level \(\alpha\). To bound the Family-Wise Error Rate (\(\FWER\)) such that \(\Prob(\text{at least one false rejection}) \le \alpha\), the individual test hurdle under the Bonferroni inequality must satisfy: \[ \alpha_M = \frac{\alpha}{M} \] Equivalently, for two-tailed Gaussian test statistics \(z_i \sim \Normal(0, 1)\), the critical value \(z_M^* = \Phi^{-1}(1 - \frac{\alpha}{2M})\) grows asymptotically as \(\sqrt{2 \ln M}\).

Proof By Boole's inequality (the union bound), for any collection of events \(E_i = \{|z_i| \ge z_M^*\}\): \[ \Prob\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^M E_i\right) \le \sum_{i=1}^M \Prob(E_i) = \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_M = M \left(\frac{\alpha}{M}\right) = \alpha \] For Gaussian tails, using the standard Mills ratio bound \(\Prob(|z| \ge x) \sim \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi} x} e^{-x^2/2}\), setting \(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi} x} e^{-x^2/2} = \frac{\alpha}{M}\) and taking logarithms yields \(-\frac{1}{2} x^2 - \ln x \sim -\ln M\), establishing that \(x \sim \sqrt{2 \ln M}\). This proof is complete.

Remark 1.1 (The Factor Zoo and Hurdle Calibration) Testing hundreds of candidate anomalies without multiplicity correction guarantees false discovery. Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) demonstrate that because over 300 factors have been published in top finance journals, the appropriate significance hurdle for newly discovered asset pricing factors must be raised from \(t \ge 1.96\) to at least \(t \ge 3.00\).

2. Novelty Discovery vs. Derivative Gap-Filling

A frequent failure mode in doctoral dissertations is "derivative gap-filling"—applying an existing regression to a minor sub-sample or tweaking an established parameter by a small epsilon without changing the underlying economic mechanism.

Example 1.1 (Genuine Novelty vs Derivative Tweaking) Consider a candidate studying equity momentum.
Derivative Proposal: "I will test the Jegadeesh-Titman 12-month momentum strategy on Brazilian mid-cap consumer stocks from 2012 to 2022." (Fails novelty: merely verifies whether an established empirical regularity holds in a regional sub-sample).
Genuine Doctoral Contribution: "I will test whether cross-sectional momentum profits are generated by intermediary leverage constraints rather than investor underreaction, constructing an instrumented proxy-SVAR that isolates dealer balance sheet shocks and proves that momentum crashes coincide with shadow banking funding runs." (Passes novelty: discriminates between behavioral and financial intermediation theories).

Novelty discovery requires establishing an empirical boundary where the existing canon breaks, followed by deriving an explicit economic mechanism that reconciles the divergence through reproducible, out-of-sample identification.

Lab 1 · Multiple Testing Hurdle & Empirical Bayes False Discovery Diagnostic

Testing 400 factor hypotheses with 10% true signals demonstrates that unadjusted t-tests generate high false discovery rates, whereas the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle (t ≥ 3.0) and Benjamini-Hochberg control preserve posterior discovery validity.

"""Lab CAP4-L01-1: Multiple Testing Hurdle & Empirical Bayes False Discovery Diagnostic.

Evaluates research question selection by computing Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle adjustments,
Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, and empirical Bayes posterior probabilities of true discovery.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats  # type: ignore[import-untyped]


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Calibrated distribution of t-statistics from factor research literature
    np.random.seed(42)
    n_hypotheses = 400
    prop_true = 0.10  # 10% true signals, 90% nulls

    # Generate synthetic t-statistics: nulls ~ N(0, 1), non-nulls ~ N(3.2, 1)
    is_true = np.random.rand(n_hypotheses) < prop_true
    t_stats = np.zeros(n_hypotheses)
    t_stats[~is_true] = np.random.normal(0.0, 1.0, size=int(np.sum(~is_true)))
    t_stats[is_true] = np.random.normal(3.2, 1.0, size=int(np.sum(is_true)))

    # 2. Two-tailed p-values
    p_values = 2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(np.abs(t_stats)))

    # 3. Multiple testing hurdles
    # Classical unadjusted hurdle (alpha = 0.05)
    unadjusted_hurdle = float(stats.norm.ppf(1.0 - 0.05 / 2.0))
    bonferroni_alpha = 0.05 / n_hypotheses
    bonferroni_hurdle = float(stats.norm.ppf(1.0 - bonferroni_alpha / 2.0))

    # Harvey-Liu-Zhu empirical hurdle for cross-sectional anomalies (~ 3.0)
    hlz_hurdle = 3.00

    # 4. Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR = 0.05)
    sorted_indices = np.argsort(p_values)
    sorted_p = p_values[sorted_indices]
    bh_threshold = (np.arange(1, n_hypotheses + 1) / n_hypotheses) * 0.05
    significant_bh = sorted_p <= bh_threshold
    if np.any(significant_bh):
        max_idx = int(np.max(np.where(significant_bh)[0]))
        bh_discoveries = max_idx + 1
        bh_cutoff_t = float(np.abs(t_stats[sorted_indices[max_idx]]))
    else:
        bh_discoveries = 0
        bh_cutoff_t = bonferroni_hurdle

    # 5. Empirical Bayes posterior probability for a discovery with t = 2.8 vs t = 3.5
    def posterior_prob_true(t_val: float) -> float:
        f_null = float(stats.norm.pdf(t_val, 0.0, 1.0))
        f_alt = float(stats.norm.pdf(t_val, 3.2, 1.0))
        num = prop_true * f_alt
        denom = num + (1.0 - prop_true) * f_null
        return float(num / denom)

    post_t28 = posterior_prob_true(2.8)
    post_t35 = posterior_prob_true(3.5)

    print(f"Total Hypotheses Tested: {n_hypotheses}")
    print(f"Unadjusted Hurdle (p=0.05): {unadjusted_hurdle:.4f}")
    print(f"Bonferroni Hurdle: {bonferroni_hurdle:.4f}")
    print(f"Harvey-Liu-Zhu Hurdle: {hlz_hurdle:.2f}")
    print(f"Benjamini-Hochberg Discoveries: {bh_discoveries}")
    print(f"BH Effective Hurdle t: {bh_cutoff_t:.4f}")
    print(f"Posterior P(True | t=2.8): {post_t28:.4f}")
    print(f"Posterior P(True | t=3.5): {post_t35:.4f}")


main()
Total Hypotheses Tested: 400
Unadjusted Hurdle (p=0.05): 1.9600
Bonferroni Hurdle: 3.8361
Harvey-Liu-Zhu Hurdle: 3.00
Benjamini-Hochberg Discoveries: 28
BH Effective Hurdle t: 2.9450
Posterior P(True | t=2.8): 0.8379
Posterior P(True | t=3.5): 0.9798

The unadjusted hurdle of 1.96 produces dozens of false discoveries, whereas the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle of 3.00 aligns closely with the Benjamini-Hochberg effective cutoff (2.9450), ensuring that discovered anomalies have a posterior probability of being true exceeding 97%.

Where the canon breaks

Traditional research question formulation often fails due to three systemic distortions:

  • Publication Bias Filter: Journals historically published papers with \(t > 2.0\), discarding null findings into file drawers. Consequently, replicating published canonical anomalies often reveals substantial effect attenuation due to initial sample selection truncation.
  • Data Mining Snooping Contamination: When researchers test hundreds of variable transformations, the true test statistic distribution shifts from \(\Normal(0, 1)\) to extreme value distributions, rendering standard p-values uninformative.
  • Microcap Liquidity Illusions: Anomalies that appear highly profitable in equal-weighted portfolios frequently disappear when institutional transaction costs and NYSE-size decile breakpoints are applied.

Misconceptions

  1. A doctoral research question must examine an asset class that has never been studied before.

    Novelty does not require inventing obscure datasets; the most impactful doctoral dissertations answer central, long-standing economic questions about core asset classes by providing cleaner causal identification or resolving conflicting theoretical mechanisms.

  2. A t-statistic of 2.10 on a newly constructed factor portfolio constitutes sufficient evidence of an asset pricing anomaly.

    Under multiple-testing adjustments across the extensive literature, Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) prove that the significance hurdle must exceed 3.00 to control false discoveries; a t-statistic of 2.10 carries a high probability of representing empirical noise.

  3. Executing dozens of regression variations until finding a statistically significant specification is standard empirical exploratory practice.

    Unregistered exploratory specification searches represent data snooping and p-hacking. Without pre-registering the research protocol or applying multiple testing corrections, reported standard errors and p-values are invalid.

Problem set

Mechanics problems

Calculate the Bonferroni-adjusted significance level \(\alpha_M\) and the two-tailed Gaussian critical value \(z_M^*\) for family-wise error control at \(\alpha = 0.05\) across \(M = 500\) candidate factor anomalies.

Solution

The Bonferroni-adjusted significance level is \(\alpha_M = \frac{0.05}{500} = 0.00010\). For a two-tailed test, the tail area is \(\frac{\alpha_M}{2} = 0.00005\). The standard normal quantile is \(z_M^* = \Phi^{-1}(1 - 0.00005) = \Phi^{-1}(0.99995) \approx 3.8906\). Any candidate factor must achieve a t-statistic exceeding 3.89 to survive family-wise error rate control.

In an empirical literature testing \(M = 200\) factors where the prior probability of a factor being true is \(\pi_0 = 0.08\), a researcher observes an anomaly with \(t = 2.40\). Assuming null factors produce \(t \sim \Normal(0, 1)\) and true factors produce \(t \sim \Normal(3.0, 1)\), calculate the posterior probability that the discovered anomaly is true.

Solution

Compute the likelihoods under both hypotheses: \(f(t \mid \text{null}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-2.40^2 / 2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-2.88} \approx 0.02239\). \(f(t \mid \text{true}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-(2.40 - 3.0)^2 / 2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-0.18} \approx 0.33314\). Applying Bayes' rule: \(\Prob(\text{true} \mid t = 2.40) = \frac{0.08 \times 0.33314}{0.08 \times 0.33314 + 0.92 \times 0.02239} = \frac{0.026651}{0.026651 + 0.020603} = \frac{0.026651}{0.047254} \approx 0.5640\). The posterior probability of true discovery is only 56.4%, meaning there is a 43.6% probability that the result is a false positive.

A researcher sorts equities into deciles based on a candidate anomaly. The raw equal-weighted long-short spread is 72 basis points per month (\(t = 2.85\)). If applying the Shumway delisting adjustment reduces the monthly spread by 18 basis points, calculate the adjusted spread and evaluate whether it maintains statistical significance at \(\alpha = 0.01\) (critical value 2.576).

Solution

The unadjusted standard error is \(SE = \frac{72}{2.85} \approx 25.263\) bps. The delisting-adjusted spread is \(72 - 18 = 54\) basis points. Assuming standard error stability, the adjusted t-statistic is \(t_{\text{adj}} = \frac{54}{25.263} \approx 2.138\). Because \(2.138 < 2.576\), the anomaly is no longer statistically significant at the 1 percent level.

Proof problems

Prove that under the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure with independent test statistics, the False Discovery Rate (\(\FDR = \E[\FDP]\)) is bounded above by \(\frac{M_0}{M} q \le q\), where \(M_0\) is the number of true null hypotheses.

Solution

Let \(P_{(1)} \le \dots \le P_{(M)}\) be the ordered p-values. The BH procedure rejects \(H_{(i)}\) for all \(i \le k\), where \(k = \max \{ i : P_{(i)} \le \frac{i}{M} q \}\). Let \(V\) be the number of false rejections. The false discovery proportion is \(\FDP = \frac{V}{\max(k, 1)}\). By conditioning on the p-values of the non-null hypotheses, Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) establish that \(\E\left[\frac{\indic\{P_i \le \frac{k}{M} q\}}{k}\right] = \frac{q}{M}\) for each true null \(i \in I_0\). Summing over all \(M_0\) true null hypotheses: \(\FDR = \sum_{i \in I_0} \E\left[\frac{\indic\{i \text{ is rejected}\}}{k}\right] = \sum_{i \in I_0} \frac{q}{M} = \frac{M_0 q}{M} \le q\). This completes the proof.

Prove that in a regression with generated regressors \(Y_t = \gamma \hat{X}_t + u_t\), where \(\hat{X}_t = X_t + v_t\) with \(\E[X_t v_t] = 0\), the OLS estimator \(\hat{\gamma}_{\text{OLS}}\) suffers from attenuation bias towards zero.

Solution

Let the true structural relationship be \(Y_t = \beta X_t + \epsilon_t\), with \(\Cov(X_t, \epsilon_t) = 0\). The observed regressor is \(\hat{X}_t = X_t + v_t\), where \(v_t\) is classical measurement error with variance \(\sigma_v^2 > 0\) uncorrelated with \(X_t\) and \(\epsilon_t\). The OLS regression of \(Y\) on \(\hat{X}\) yields: \[ \hat{\gamma}_{\text{OLS}} = \frac{\Cov(Y_t, \hat{X}_t)}{\Var(\hat{X}_t)} = \frac{\Cov(\beta X_t + \epsilon_t, X_t + v_t)}{\Var(X_t + v_t)} = \frac{\beta \Var(X_t)}{\Var(X_t) + \Var(v_t)} = \beta \left( \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_v^2} \right) \] Because \(\sigma_v^2 > 0\), the reliability ratio \(\frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_v^2} < 1\), proving that \(|\hat{\gamma}_{\text{OLS}}| < |\beta|\) asymptotically.

Prove that the Hansen-Jagannathan distance \(HJ = \sqrt{\min_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \E[(m - m^*)^2]}\) on an affine candidate SDF \(m(b) = 1 - b^\top (f - \E[f])\) is uniquely minimized when \(b = \Cov(f, f)^{-1} \Cov(f, R^e) \Sigma_{R^e}^{-1} \E[R^e]\).

Solution

Let \(\mathcal{M}\) be the set of valid SDFs satisfying \(\E[m R^e] = 0\). The Hansen-Jagannathan distance measures the quadratic distance from candidate SDF \(m(b)\) to the set \(\mathcal{M}\). In payoff space, the pricing error vector is \(\alpha(b) = \E[m(b) R^e] = \E[R^e] - \Cov(R^e, f) b\). The HJ distance is given by the quadratic form \(HJ(b)^2 = \alpha(b)^\top \E[R^e R^{e\top}]^{-1} \alpha(b)\). Differentiating with respect to \(b\) and setting the gradient to zero yields: \(-2 \Cov(R^e, f)^\top \E[R^e R^{e\top}]^{-1} (\E[R^e] - \Cov(R^e, f) b) = 0\). Solving for \(b\) gives \(b^* = [\Cov(R^e, f)^\top \Omega^{-1} \Cov(R^e, f)]^{-1} \Cov(R^e, f)^\top \Omega^{-1} \E[R^e]\). When the factor spans the test asset space, this reduces to the projection formula \(b = \Cov(f, f)^{-1} \Cov(f, R^e) \Sigma_{R^e}^{-1} \E[R^e]\).

Empirical problems

A doctoral candidate tests 100 accounting anomalies. Ten anomalies exhibit t-statistics between 2.0 and 2.5, five exhibit t-statistics between 2.5 and 3.0, and two exhibit t-statistics exceeding 3.2. Apply the Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle and evaluate how many discoveries survive.

Solution

Under the Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) empirical multiple testing standard, factors with \(t < 3.0\) fail to survive after accounting for historical data snooping. The 10 anomalies with \(t \in [2.0, 2.5]\) and the 5 anomalies with \(t \in [2.5, 3.0]\) are classified as false rejections / unverified discoveries. Only the 2 anomalies with \(t > 3.2\) exceed the hurdle, reducing the discovery count from 17 down to 2 (an 88.2% attrition rate).

An empirical study examines cross-sectional momentum in US equities across 1970–2024. In the raw sample, equal-weighted decile 10-1 spread is 1.15% per month (\(t = 4.10\)). When excluding microcaps below the NYSE 20th percentile size cutoff, the spread drops to 0.65% per month (\(t = 2.45\)). Calculate the percentage of anomaly return attributable to microcaps and evaluate its economic feasibility.

Solution

The return contribution of microcaps is \(1.15\% - 0.65\% = 0.50\%\) per month. The proportion of raw spread attributable to microcaps is \(\frac{0.50}{1.15} \approx 43.48\%\). Because microcaps represent less than 3% of aggregate market capitalization and face massive bid-ask spreads and borrowing constraints, an anomaly that loses 43.5% of its return and drops to \(t = 2.45\) upon microcap exclusion is largely an illiquidity artifact.

Using the Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) q-factor model benchmark, an empirical researcher finds that an anomaly's alpha has a t-statistic of 2.75 against the Fama-French 3-factor model, but drops to \(t = 1.20\) against the q-5 model. Interpret this econometric finding for research question selection.

Solution

The anomaly's apparent excess return against the Fama-French 3-factor model is fully absorbed by the investment and profitability factors of the q-5 model (\(t\) drops from 2.75 to 1.20). This indicates that the candidate anomaly does not represent a new independent dimension of risk; rather, it is a linear combination of firm investment and return-on-equity characteristics. Selecting this anomaly as an original doctoral contribution would result in immediate rejection by dissertation committees.

Research problems

Critique the use of machine learning feature importance scores as a justification for research question novelty in asset pricing.

Solution

High feature importance in an opaque non-linear model (e.g., gradient boosted trees or neural networks) establishes statistical correlation with returns but does not constitute an economic question. Without a theoretical model explaining which economic agent faces the risk, why arbitrageurs cannot trade it away, or what macroeconomic state variable drives the pricing kernel, feature importance reflects complex curve fitting. A doctoral thesis must specify the economic mechanism and testable counterfactuals rather than relying on predictive black-box rankings.

Formulate a doctoral dissertation research proposal that investigates the impact of central bank digital currency (CBDC) on commercial bank deposit funding, detailing the economic mechanism, falsifiable hypothesis, and causal identification strategy.

Solution

Economic Mechanism: CBDC introduces a risk-free digital alternative to uninsured commercial bank deposits, flattening the deposit supply curve and attenuating banks' market power in deposit pricing.
Falsifiable Hypothesis: Banks with higher shares of uninsured commercial deposits experience greater deposit outflows and contract lending more aggressively during stress regimes when CBDC interest rate spreads widen.
Identification Strategy: Exploit cross-sectional variation in bank branching exposure to pilot CBDC rollout jurisdictions or quasi-experimental regulatory deposit fee thresholds in a difference-in-differences framework with bank and time fixed effects.

Explain how Cochrane's (2011) presidential address reframed the central question of modern asset pricing from cash-flow news to discount-rate variation, and state the econometric challenge this poses for thesis question selection.

Solution

Cochrane (2011) showed via dividend-growth and price-dividend variance decompositions that nearly 100% of aggregate stock market price-dividend fluctuations are driven by time-varying discount rates rather than revisions in expected future cash flows. For doctoral research question selection, this implies that finding predictability in returns is no longer sufficient; the thesis must explain why discount rates fluctuate. The core econometric challenge is separating time-varying risk aversion from time-varying economic risk, which requires structural general equilibrium modeling or non-linear state-space identification.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the primary objective of doctoral research-question selection?

    Options

    Research-question selection focuses on identifying high-impact, falsifiable economic mechanisms rather than mechanical curve-fitting or minor data tweaking.

  2. Why do Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) recommend raising the asset pricing discovery hurdle to \(t \ge 3.0\)?

    Options

    Multiple testing across hundreds of candidate anomaly factors requires strict family-wise or false discovery rate control, lifting the necessary t-statistic hurdle to at least 3.0.

  3. What distinguishes genuine doctoral novelty from derivative gap-filling?

    Options

    Doctoral scholarship requires advancing economic understanding by explaining mechanisms, resolving canon breakdowns, or providing structural identification.

  4. What is the minimum empirical t-statistic hurdle recommended by Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) for new factor discovery?

    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) calibrate the multiple testing hurdle for cross-sectional anomaly factors to \(t \ge 3.0\).

  5. According to Cochrane (2011), what drives nearly 100% of price-dividend ratio fluctuations in aggregate equity markets?

    Options

    Cochrane (2011) showed through variance decompositions that discount rate variation accounts for aggregate price-dividend volatility.

  6. Why do Chen & Zimmermann (2020) find that published anomaly returns decay post-publication?

    Options

    Initial published returns reflect in-sample selection bias, followed by institutional trading that partially or fully attenuates genuine mispricings.

  7. How does Boole's inequality establish the Bonferroni critical value?

    Options

    The union bound \(\Prob(\bigcup E_i) \le \sum \Prob(E_i)\) ensures that testing \(M\) hypotheses at \(\alpha / M\) bounds the family-wise error rate at \(\alpha\).

  8. What role does economic theory play in empirical research question selection?

    Options

    Economic theory disciplines question selection by defining which state variables can plausibly price risk and providing testable counterfactual implications.
