Research Thesis & Defense

The doctoral dissertation thesis represents the definitive capstone milestone of the quantitative finance doctoral program. Candidates execute autonomous, original scholarship extending a replicated empirical or theoretical asset pricing finding. Through rigorous question selection, methodological pre-registration, institutional data architecture, causal econometric identification, the Atlas reproducible computational pipeline, and manuscript engineering, you will produce a publication-grade journal article accompanied by an end-to-end replication package, defended orally against an adversarial dissertation committee.

  • Prerequisites: CAP1 Reading, Refereeing & Replicating and CAP3 Qualifying Examination.
  • Project: Thesis, graded by autograder and rubric (1080 hours).
  • Hours: 1200 study hours (120 h across 10 lessons, 1080 h doctoral dissertation thesis project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formulate and pre-register an autonomous doctoral research thesis extending a replicated empirical asset pricing result.
  2. Implement institutional sample hygiene and delisting return adjustments under Shumway and NYSE size breakpoint protocols.
  3. Derive structural Euler equation moment conditions and formulate causal identification strategies using instrumental variables.
  4. Execute end-to-end reproducible research pipelines with cryptographically verified artifact DAGs on Atlas.
  5. Engineer publication-grade academic manuscripts conforming to the Cochrane introduction standard and premier finance journal conventions.
  6. Defend doctoral thesis contributions orally against an adversarial dissertation committee across sixteen core attack angles.

Prerequisites & closure

CAP4 is the terminal doctoral capstone of the quantitative finance program. It assumes complete doctoral mastery across the prerequisite closure, specifically building on:

  • Referee reports, replication packages, and replication divergence diagnosis from CAP1.
  • Doctoral qualifying examinations and timed field examinations from CAP3.
  • Fama-MacBeth two-pass regressions, Shanken asymptotic variance corrections, and Gibbons-Ross-Shanken (GRS) tests from S5.
  • Data-snooping bias, false discovery rate controls, and backtest overfitting from S7.
  • Generalized Method of Moments (GMM) and Hansen-Jagannathan SDF bounds from AP1 and S5.

Placement diagnostic

Ten diagnostic items testing prerequisite mastery across CAP1, CAP3, econometric identification, and data hygiene, along with two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the dissertation project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Why does Shumway (1997) demonstrate that failing to adjust for delisting returns in CRSP induces severe survivorship and performance bias in cross-sectional equity anomaly returns?

    Options

    Shumway (1997) showed that performance-related delistings typically experience severe negative returns (-30% on NYSE/AMEX, -55% on NASDAQ) that are missing from raw monthly CRSP files. Treating missing values as zero artificially inflates anomaly returns, especially in high-turnover distress portfolios. Rung 1 and 2 skill check.

  2. In cross-sectional asset pricing regressions, under what mathematical condition does the Shanken (1992) correction factor \(c = 1 + \lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) equal 1.0 exactly?

    Options

    The quadratic term \(\lambda^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \lambda\) measures the variance inflation stemming from estimating rather than observing factor loadings. When \(\lambda = 0\), the quadratic form vanishes, and \(c = 1.0\). Rung 2 skill check.

  3. What constitutes an original doctoral contribution in empirical asset pricing beyond mechanical factor replication?

    Options

    Doctoral scholarship requires advancing economic understanding by explaining why a premium exists, identifying who holds the risk, or proving that standard models misprice assets due to institutional or friction constraints.

  4. What is the central purpose of formal pre-registration in financial economics research?

    Options

    Pre-registration defines hypotheses, data transformations, exclusion filters, and statistical loss functions in advance, eliminating researcher degrees of freedom that generate spurious anomalies.

  5. Why do Hou, Xue, and Zhang (2020) and Chen and Zimmermann (2022) advocate using NYSE size breakpoints rather than pooled exchange breakpoints in anomaly portfolios?

    Options

    Microcaps represent ~60% of all public firms but only ~3% of market capitalization. NYSE decile breakpoints prevent microcap contamination from dominating portfolio spreads.

  6. In instrumental variables (2SLS) identification, what condition does the Montiel Olea-Pflueger / Stock-Yogo rule test?

    Options

    A weak instrument (F < 10) causes 2SLS estimates to be biased toward OLS and produces distorted asymptotic hypothesis test sizes.

  7. What does the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken (GRS) statistic evaluate in multi-factor asset pricing models?

    Options

    The GRS test evaluates whether the candidate factor portfolio spans the mean-variance efficient frontier of the test assets, formalized as \(\alpha_1 = \dots = \alpha_N = 0\).

  8. According to Cochrane (2005) and Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017), what should the first four pages of an academic finance manuscript accomplish?

    Options

    Premier finance articles state their main empirical result, economic significance, and identification strategy immediately without rhetorical filler or meandering historical introductions.

  9. When an adversarial referee or committee member demands robustness tests that weaken statistical significance, what is the proper doctoral response protocol?

    Options

    Professional doctoral scholarship treats referee critiques with transparency, establishing boundary conditions and explaining where and why economic mechanisms attenuate.

  10. In an oral dissertation defense, what is the primary indicator of successful candidate performance?

    Options

    Dissertation committees test whether the candidate understands the exact conditions under which their empirical claims hold or fail, assessing scholarly maturity and intellectual honesty.

Lesson roadmap

The 120 lesson hours are structured into ten 12-hour units guiding candidates through every phase of doctoral thesis execution:

  1. L01 · Research Question Selection & Novelty Discovery

    12 hours · Disciplined question selection: balancing economic significance against econometric tractability, mapping the research frontier, rejecting derivative gap-filling, and multiple testing hurdles.

  2. L02 · Literature Synthesis & Theoretical Framing

    12 hours · Formulating the structural economic model behind empirical hypotheses: SDF restrictions, Euler equations, state-variable hedging demands, and equilibrium pricing conditions.

  3. L03 · Study Pre-Registration & Methodological Protocol

    12 hours · Pre-registration protocols: binding hypotheses, sample selection freezes, specification curve analysis, and eliminating researcher degrees of freedom.

  4. L04 · Institutional Data Architecture & Sample Hygiene

    12 hours · Sample hygiene: CRSP/Compustat merging, survivorship bias, Shumway delisting corrections, look-ahead restatements, and NYSE size breakpoint filters.

  5. L05 · Identification Strategy & Causal Econometrics

    12 hours · Causal econometrics: instrumental variables (2SLS), first-stage F-diagnostics, proxy-SVARs, and Shanken variance corrections for generated regressors.

  6. L06 · The Original Contribution & Structural Validation

    12 hours · Validating an original doctoral contribution: extending a replicated baseline with non-linear or structural representations, GRS efficiency, and out-of-sample pricing power.

  7. L07 · The Atlas Research Pipeline & End-to-End Reproducibility

    12 hours · Pipeline architecture: deterministic DAG execution, frozen container environments, data lineage hashing, and one-click end-to-end reproducibility.

  8. L08 · Academic Manuscript Engineering & Journal Formatting

    12 hours · Manuscript engineering: the Cochrane four-page introduction formula, tabular accounting integrity, mathematical articulation, and journal guidelines.

  9. L09 · Mock Referee Process, Peer Review & Revision Protocol

    12 hours · Peer review: triaging referee reports, point-by-point response matrices, non-defensive robustness testing, and parameter sensitivity bounds.

  10. L10 · Doctoral Thesis Defense & Committee Examination

    12 hours · Oral dissertation defense: presentation structure, handling hostile attack angles, stress-testing net economic returns, and committee evaluation.

Project ladder

The capstone thesis project follows the mandatory four-rung doctoral mastery ladder:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance of an empirical asset pricing replication and structural extension pipeline, demonstrating delisting corrections, Fama-MacBeth regressions with Shanken corrections, and GRS tests. Annotated with rationale at every econometric stage.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    The identical econometric pipeline with core calculation steps removed. Evaluates automated implementation of Shanken corrections, GRS curvature normalizations, and NYSE-breakpoint capitalization weighting under instant autograding.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The definitive doctoral dissertation: an original, pre-registered contribution extending a replicated empirical result, delivered as a journal-format manuscript with a full Atlas replication package and defended orally against the rubric (1080 hours).

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Formal submission to a top-tier finance journal (Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, or Review of Financial Studies) accompanied by an open-source public replication repository and reproducible data release.
