Mock Referee Process, Peer Review & Revision Protocol

The peer review process at premier finance journals is an adversarial stress test of econometric resilience. Receiving a Revise and Resubmit decision requires navigating complex referee critiques, executing exhaustive sensitivity grids, and demonstrating that empirical findings do not hinge on arbitrary sample filters. A doctoral dissertation must be prepared with the same rigor required for a major journal revision. By engineering an exhaustive referee response matrix, running cross-sectional sensitivity batteries, and adjusting standard errors for multi-way clustering, doctoral researchers transform skeptical referee scrutiny into an engine of empirical proof.

1. Peer Review Mechanics & The Response Protocol

In top-tier academic journals, the editorial workflow proceeds through four distinct hurdles: desk screening by the Editor-in-Chief, associate editor assignment, double-blind or single-blind referee evaluation, and post-review editorial decision as analyzed by Berk, Harvey & Hirshleifer (2017).

Definition 9.1 (Referee Response Matrix) A referee response matrix is an exhaustive, point-by-point rebuttal document where every referee comment \(C_i\) is paired with: (1) a direct summary of the authors' conceptual response, (2) an exact verbatim quote of the revised manuscript text, (3) line and page references to the revision, and (4) an accompanying empirical sensitivity table directly testing the referee's alternative hypothesis.

The cardinal rule of academic revisions is constructive compliance: authors must never dismiss a referee's concern as trivial or merely argue verbally. Every empirical objection must be answered with new data, alternative econometric specifications, or formal analytical derivations.

Theorem 9.1 (Two-Way Cluster Covariance Decomposition) Let residuals \(\hat{\epsilon}_{it}\) in a panel regression be subject to both persistent firm-level clustering and time-specific macro shocks. Under the Cameron, Gelbach & Miller (2011) two-way clustering estimator, the asymptotic covariance matrix of OLS estimates is: \[ \hat{V}_{2\text{-way}} = \hat{V}_{\text{firm}} + \hat{V}_{\text{time}} - \hat{V}_{\text{white}} \] where \(\hat{V}_{\text{firm}}\) clusters by firm, \(\hat{V}_{\text{time}}\) clusters by time period, and \(\hat{V}_{\text{white}}\) is the standard White heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance matrix. If both dimensions exhibit positive intra-cluster correlation, standard errors clustered along a single dimension underestimate the true sampling variance: \[ \operatorname{SE}_{2\text{-way}}(\hat{\beta}) \ge \max\Big(\operatorname{SE}_{\text{firm}}(\hat{\beta}), \; \operatorname{SE}_{\text{time}}(\hat{\beta})\Big) \]

Proof Let the score vector for observation \((i, t)\) be \(s_{it} = X_{it} \epsilon_{it}\). The panel variance is the sum of expectations \(\sum_{i,t} \sum_{j,s} \mathbb{E}[s_{it} s_{js}^\top]\). By inclusion-exclusion of non-zero error correlations: \[ \mathcal{S}_{2\text{-way}} = \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \sum_{t=1}^T s_{it} \right)\left( \sum_{s=1}^T s_{is}^\top \right) + \sum_{t=1}^T \left( \sum_{i=1}^N s_{it} \right)\left( \sum_{j=1}^N s_{jt}^\top \right) - \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T s_{it} s_{it}^\top \] Multiplying by the projection sandwich \((X^\top X)^{-1}\) yields \(\hat{V}_{\text{firm}} + \hat{V}_{\text{time}} - \hat{V}_{\text{white}}\). When intra-firm correlation \(\rho_{\text{firm}} > 0\) and intra-time correlation \(\rho_{\text{time}} > 0\), the cross-products in both firm and time sums exceed the diagonal White variance. Subtracting \(\hat{V}_{\text{white}}\) eliminates double-counted diagonal terms, leaving the sum of distinct off-diagonal positive covariances. Hence \(\hat{V}_{2\text{-way}} \ge \hat{V}_{\text{firm}}\) and \(\hat{V}_{2\text{-way}} \ge \hat{V}_{\text{time}}\), completing the proof.

Remark 9.1 (The "Agree and Execute" Revision Protocol) When a referee requests an additional control variable, sample split, or alternative winsorization rule, defensive arguing increases rejection probabilities. The optimal revision protocol executes the requested check, inserts the result into an online appendix or robustness section, and highlights that the primary empirical conclusion survives the referee's specification.

2. Robustness Sensitivity Grids

A central demand in modern empirical finance revisions per Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) is demonstrating that asset pricing anomalies are not artifacts of outlier trimming or sample selection.

Example 9.1 (Winsorization & Subsample Stability) An empirical researcher discovers a cross-sectional predictive coefficient \(\hat{\beta} = 0.35\) with \(t = 5.2\). A skeptical referee suspects the result is driven by microcap penny stocks during market crises. The author responds by constructing a \(3 \times 3\) sensitivity grid crossing three winsorization cutoffs (0.5%, 1.0%, 2.5%) against three sample periods (Full sample, Pre-2008, and Crisis-excluded sample). If the coefficient remains stable (\(\hat{\beta} \in [0.33, 0.37]\)) and statistically significant across all 9 cells, the referee's objection is definitively refuted.

Every doctoral revision package must include five standard robustness batteries:

  1. Outlier Sensitivity: Comparing 0.5%, 1%, and 2.5% winsorization versus truncation.
  2. Crisis Subsample Trimming: Excluding the 2008–2009 Global Financial Crisis and March 2020 COVID shock to confirm that findings do not depend solely on systemic crashes.
  3. Breakpoint Disaggregation: Evaluating NYSE-only breakpoints versus all-exchange breakpoints.
  4. Clustering Robustness: Comparing White standard errors, firm clustering, time clustering, and two-way clustering.
  5. Lag Structure Perturbations: Varying sorting formation periods (e.g. 1-month, 3-month, 6-month, 12-month) and holding periods.

Lab 1 · Referee Response Matrix & Robustness Sensitivity Diagnostic

Executing referee-demanded robustness checks—winsorization threshold perturbations, crisis trimming, and double-clustering variance inflation—quantifies empirical parameter stability across adversarial specifications.

"""Lab CAP4-L09-1: Referee Response Matrix & Robustness Sensitivity Diagnostic.

Evaluates the peer review revision protocol by executing referee-demanded robustness checks:
sample window trimming, alternative clustering, and outlier winsorization thresholds (0.5% vs 1% vs 2.5%),
computing parameter sensitivity metrics across the response matrix.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    np.random.seed(42)
    n_obs = 360

    # 1. Baseline empirical specification: Y = alpha + beta * X + eps
    # True beta = 0.35
    x = np.random.normal(0.0, 1.0, size=n_obs)
    # Add fat-tailed shocks to simulate real financial outliers
    eps = np.random.standard_t(df=4, size=n_obs) * 0.5
    y = 0.05 + 0.35 * x + eps

    # Baseline OLS (1% winsorization)
    def run_ols(x_arr: np.ndarray, y_arr: np.ndarray) -> tuple[float, float]:
        n = len(x_arr)
        x_mat = np.column_stack([np.ones(n), x_arr])
        b = np.linalg.inv(x_mat.T @ x_mat) @ (x_mat.T @ y_arr)
        res = y_arr - x_mat @ b
        s2 = float(np.sum(res**2) / (n - 2))
        var_b = s2 * np.linalg.inv(x_mat.T @ x_mat)[1, 1]
        return float(b[1]), float(np.sqrt(var_b))

    def winsorize_pct(arr: np.ndarray, pct: float) -> np.ndarray:
        low = float(np.percentile(arr, pct))
        high = float(np.percentile(arr, 100.0 - pct))
        clipped = np.clip(arr, low, high)
        return np.asarray(clipped, dtype=np.float64)

    # 2. Referee Demands:
    # Demand A: Winsorization sensitivity (0.5%, 1.0%, 2.5%)
    beta_base, se_base = run_ols(winsorize_pct(x, 1.0), winsorize_pct(y, 1.0))
    beta_w05, _se_w05 = run_ols(winsorize_pct(x, 0.5), winsorize_pct(y, 0.5))
    beta_w25, _se_w25 = run_ols(winsorize_pct(x, 2.5), winsorize_pct(y, 2.5))

    # Demand B: Subsample trimming (excluding Great Financial Crisis 2008-2009, e.g. 24 middle months)
    mask_trimmed = np.ones(n_obs, dtype=bool)
    mask_trimmed[160:184] = False
    beta_trimmed, _se_trimmed = run_ols(winsorize_pct(x[mask_trimmed], 1.0), winsorize_pct(y[mask_trimmed], 1.0))

    # Demand C: Cluster-robust standard error inflation (simulated double clustering)
    cluster_inflation = 1.28
    se_double_cluster = se_base * cluster_inflation

    # 3. Sensitivity Bounds & Response Matrix Metrics
    all_betas = [beta_base, beta_w05, beta_w25, beta_trimmed]
    max_beta_dev = float(np.max(np.abs(np.array(all_betas) - beta_base)))
    pct_max_dev = (max_beta_dev / beta_base) * 100.0
    min_t_stat = float(np.min([b / se_double_cluster for b in all_betas]))

    print(f"Baseline Coefficient (1.0% Winsor): {beta_base:.4f} (se={se_base:.4f})")
    print(f"0.5% Winsor Coefficient: {beta_w05:.4f}")
    print(f"2.5% Winsor Coefficient: {beta_w25:.4f}")
    print(f"Subsample Trimmed Coefficient: {beta_trimmed:.4f}")
    print(f"Double-Clustered Standard Error: {se_double_cluster:.4f}")
    print(f"Maximum Coefficient Deviation: {pct_max_dev:.2f}%")
    print(f"Worst-Case Referee t-Statistic: {min_t_stat:.2f}")


main()

The baseline specification yields \(\hat{\beta} = 0.3807\) with standard error 0.0267. Under 0.5% winsorization \(\hat{\beta} = 0.3794\) and under 2.5% winsorization \(\hat{\beta} = 0.3805\). Where the canon breaks

Revision strategies collapse when authors adopt flawed interaction approaches:

  • Adversarial Tone: Attacking the referee's competence or questioning their motivation in the response memo immediately alienates editors and guarantees rejection.
  • Cosmetic Compliance: Promising revisions in the response letter while failing to modify the manuscript prose or insert empirical tables invites desk rejection upon subsequent rounds.
  • Selective Subsample Reporting: Reporting only the single winsorization cutoff that preserves statistical significance while concealing that the result vanishes under alternative thresholds violates academic integrity.

Misconceptions

  1. If a referee is factually mistaken about an econometric theorem, the author should write a sharp rebuttal telling the referee they are wrong.

    An aggressive rebuttal guarantees rejection. When a referee misunderstands a mathematical point, the proper response assumes responsibility for unclear exposition: politely explain the derivation, provide a step-by-step appendix proof, and revise the main text to prevent future reader confusion.

  2. Running dozens of robustness checks in response to a referee increases the risk of multiple testing contamination.

    Robustness checks requested by referees test whether a pre-existing finding is fragile to specification changes, not whether new anomalies can be discovered. Demonstrating that an existing point estimate is stable across alternative filters confirms structural validity.

  3. Clustering standard errors by firm is always conservative and sufficient for panel asset pricing regressions.

    Firm clustering accounts for autocorrelation within firms, but it assumes cross-sectional independence across firms in any given month. Asset returns exhibit strong contemporaneous market-wide correlation; failing to cluster by time or two-way can underestimate standard errors by 30% to 50%.

Problem set

Mechanics problems

In a panel regression, the baseline standard error under OLS is \(\operatorname{SE}_{\text{OLS}} = 0.0250\). Clustered standard errors by firm yield \(\operatorname{SE}_{\text{firm}} = 0.0290\), and clustering by time yields \(\operatorname{SE}_{\text{time}} = 0.0310\). If the White standard error is \(\operatorname{SE}_{\text{white}} = 0.0240\), calculate the two-way clustered standard error using Cameron-Gelbach-Miller variance decomposition.

Solution

By Theorem 9.1, the variance decomposes as: \[ V_{2\text{-way}} = V_{\text{firm}} + V_{\text{time}} - V_{\text{white}} \] Computing variances: \[ V_{\text{firm}} = 0.0290^2 = 0.000841, \quad V_{\text{time}} = 0.0310^2 = 0.000961, \quad V_{\text{white}} = 0.0240^2 = 0.000576 \] Summing: \[ V_{2\text{-way}} = 0.000841 + 0.000961 - 0.000576 = 0.001226 \] Taking the square root: \[ \operatorname{SE}_{2\text{-way}} = \sqrt{0.001226} \approx 0.03501 \] The two-way clustered standard error is approximately 0.0350.

A regression coefficient \(\hat{\beta} = 0.420\) has a baseline \(t\)-statistic of \(4.20\) (\(\operatorname{SE} = 0.100\)). Under two-way clustering requested by a referee, the standard error increases by 35%. Calculate the new \(t\)-statistic and evaluate whether the finding remains statistically significant at the \(\alpha = 0.01\) critical threshold (\(t_{\text{crit}} = 2.576\)).

Solution

Inflated standard error: \[ \operatorname{SE}_{\text{new}} = 0.100 \times 1.35 = 0.135 \] New \(t\)-statistic: \[ t_{\text{new}} = \frac{0.420}{0.135} \approx 3.111 \] Because \(3.111 > 2.576\), the finding remains statistically significant at the 1% significance level despite the 35% variance penalty.

An author evaluates an anomaly across three winsorization cutoffs: \(\hat{\beta}_{0.5\%} = 0.38\), \(\hat{\beta}_{1.0\%} = 0.35\), \(\hat{\beta}_{2.5\%} = 0.32\). Calculate the maximum percentage parameter deviation relative to the baseline 1.0% specification.

Solution

Baseline coefficient = \(0.35\).
Absolute deviations: \[ |0.38 - 0.35| = 0.03, \quad |0.32 - 0.35| = 0.03 \] Maximum percentage deviation: \[ \frac{0.03}{0.35} \times 100\% \approx 8.57\% \] Parameter estimates vary by at most 8.57% across winsorization cutoffs.

Proof problems

Prove that in the presence of positive contemporaneous cross-sectional correlation \(\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_{it}, \epsilon_{jt}) = \rho \sigma^2 > 0\) for all \(i \neq j\), the conventional OLS standard error estimator that assumes cross-sectional independence understates the true sampling variance by a factor of \(\mathcal{O}(N)\).

Solution

Consider the sample mean \(\bar{y}_t = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N y_{it}\) where \(y_{it} = \mu + \epsilon_{it}\) with \(\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_{it}) = \sigma^2\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_{it}, \epsilon_{jt}) = \rho \sigma^2\).
Under the independence assumption, the conventional variance is: \[ \operatorname{Var}_{\text{iid}}(\bar{y}_t) = \frac{\sigma^2}{N} \] The true sampling variance accounting for cross-sectional correlation is: \[ \operatorname{Var}_{\text{true}}(\bar{y}_t) = \frac{1}{N^2} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N \operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_{it}) + \sum_{i \neq j} \operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_{it}, \epsilon_{jt}) \right] = \frac{1}{N^2} \left[ N \sigma^2 + N(N - 1)\rho \sigma^2 \right] = \frac{\sigma^2}{N} \left[ 1 + (N - 1)\rho \right] \] The ratio of true to assumed variance is: \[ \frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\text{true}}(\bar{y}_t)}{\operatorname{Var}_{\text{iid}}(\bar{y}_t)} = 1 + (N - 1)\rho = \mathcal{O}(N) \quad (\text{as } N \to \infty) \] Even for small positive \(\rho = 0.05\), with \(N = 1000\) stocks, \(1 + 999 \times 0.05 \approx 50.95\), meaning true variance is 50 times larger than reported under i.i.d. assumptions. This proves that clustering or Fama-MacBeth time aggregation is mandatory in cross-sectional equity panels.

Prove that winsorizing symmetric outliers at percentile \(\alpha\) on a heavy-tailed distribution with finite mean reduces the asymptotic variance of the sample mean relative to raw truncation when extreme values contain non-zero location information.

Solution

Let \(X\) follow a distribution with symmetric density \(f(x)\) around \(\mu\). Let \(c_1 = F^{-1}(\alpha)\) and \(c_2 = F^{-1}(1 - \alpha)\).
Under truncation, observations outside \([c_1, c_2]\) are discarded, reducing effective sample size from \(N\) to \(N_{\text{eff}} = N(1 - 2\alpha)\).
Under winsorization, observations outside the interval are clamped to \(c_1\) and \(c_2\). The winsorized estimator preserves the entire sample size \(N\).
The asymptotic variance of the trimmed mean is \(\sigma_{\text{trim}}^2 / (N(1 - 2\alpha)^2)\), whereas the winsorized mean has influence function: \[ \psi(x) = \begin{cases} c_1 - \mu & x < c_1 \\ x - \mu & c_1 \le x \le c_2 \\ c_2 - \mu & x > c_2 \end{cases} \] Because \(\psi(x)\) is bounded, the asymptotic variance \(\frac{1}{N} \mathbb{E}[\psi(X)^2]\) remains finite even for distributions without fourth moments (such as Student-\(t\) with \(\nu = 3\)). Preserving sample points at the boundary retains Fisher information regarding location while eliminating extreme variance inflation. This completes the proof.

Prove that the Fama-MacBeth (1973) two-step standard error is numerically equivalent to clustering standard errors by time period in a balanced panel with identical cross-sectional regressors across periods.

Solution

In a balanced panel with \(T\) periods and \(N\) assets, Fama-MacBeth runs \(T\) cross-sectional regressions \(\hat{\beta}_t = (X_t^\top X_t)^{-1} X_t^\top y_t\).
If regressors are constant across time (\(X_t = X\)), then \(\hat{\beta}_t = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y_t\).
The aggregate Fama-MacBeth estimate is \(\bar{\beta} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \hat{\beta}_t = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top \bar{y}\), which equals the pooled OLS estimate.
The Fama-MacBeth sampling variance is: \[ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{\text{FM}}(\bar{\beta}) = \frac{1}{T(T - 1)} \sum_{t=1}^T (\hat{\beta}_t - \bar{\beta})(\hat{\beta}_t - \bar{\beta})^\top \] Now consider the time-clustered sandwich estimator: \[ \hat{V}_{\text{time}} = (X_{\text{pool}}^\top X_{\text{pool}})^{-1} \left( \sum_{t=1}^T X_t^\top \hat{\epsilon}_t \hat{\epsilon}_t^\top X_t \right) (X_{\text{pool}}^\top X_{\text{pool}})^{-1} \] Since \(X_{\text{pool}}^\top X_{\text{pool}} = T(X^\top X)\) and \(X_t = X\), this simplifies to: \[ \hat{V}_{\text{time}} = \frac{1}{T^2} (X^\top X)^{-1} \left( \sum_{t=1}^T X^\top \hat{\epsilon}_t \hat{\epsilon}_t^\top X \right) (X^\top X)^{-1} \] Noting that \((X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top \hat{\epsilon}_t = \hat{\beta}_t - \bar{\beta}\), the middle product becomes \(\sum_{t=1}^T (\hat{\beta}_t - \bar{\beta})(\hat{\beta}_t - \bar{\beta})^\top\).
Adjusting for the small-sample degrees-of-freedom factor \(\frac{T}{T - 1}\), \(\hat{V}_{\text{time}} = \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{\text{FM}}(\bar{\beta})\). This completes the proof.

Empirical & practical problems

Draft a professional, point-by-point response entry for a referee who writes: "The author's result appears entirely driven by microcap stocks that are untradeable by institutional asset managers."

Solution

A professional response entry:

Referee Comment 2: "The author's result appears entirely driven by microcap stocks that are untradeable by institutional asset managers."

Response: We thank the referee for raising this crucial institutional concern. In response, we have conducted an extensive microcap sensitivity analysis following the methodology of Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020). Specifically, we have re-estimated our primary asset pricing tests under three distinct screening protocols:

  1. NYSE Breakpoints with Value-Weighted Portfolios: Eliminating microcaps from determining portfolio boundaries;
  2. Explicit Market-Cap Truncation: Dropping all stocks falling in the bottom 20% of NYSE market capitalization;
  3. Liquidity Screening: Restricting the sample to stocks with share prices above $5.00 and positive trading volume for 12 consecutive months.

As reported in new Table 4 (Panel B, page 19) and discussed on pages 18–20 of the revised manuscript:

"Under value weighting with NYSE breakpoints, the factor spread portfolio generates an annualized return of 5.84% (\(t = 3.42\)), compared to 6.12% (\(t = 3.88\)) in the baseline specification. Truncating the bottom 20% of market capitalization leaves the premium virtually unchanged at 5.71% (\(t = 3.29\)). These results demonstrate that the economic mechanism is robust among large- and mid-cap institutional equities."

Explain the operational role of an Associate Editor in resolving conflicting referee recommendations (e.g. Referee 1 recommends "Accept"; Referee 2 recommends "Reject").

Solution

When referees disagree:
1. Weighing Arguments vs. Voting: Associate Editors do not simply average referee scores. They evaluate the substantive merits of the critiques. If Referee 2 identifies a fatal identification breakdown or mathematical error while Referee 1 writes a superficial praise report, the AE prioritizes the substantive objection.
2. Editorial Synthesis Memo: The AE drafts a confidential recommendation to the Editor-in-Chief synthesizing the conflict, identifying whether Referee 2's concerns are remediable, and outlining specific conditions required for a revision.
3. Guidance Letter: If the Editor grants an R&R, the editor's cover letter explicitly instructs the authors which referee points are paramount, providing a roadmap for successful revision.

Construct a Python script that automates the generation of a LaTeX robustness table crossing three winsorization levels against two subsamples.

Solution

Automated LaTeX robustness table generator:

def generate_robustness_latex(results_grid: dict[tuple[str, str], tuple[float, float]], filename: str) -> None:
    lines = [
        r"\begin{table}[htbp]",
        r"\centering",
        r"\caption{Parameter Sensitivity Across Winsorization and Subperiods}",
        r"\label{tab:robustness_grid}",
        r"\begin{tabular}{lccc}",
        r"\toprule",
        r"Sample & 0.5\% Winsor & 1.0\% Winsor & 2.5\% Winsor \\",
        r"\midrule"
    ]
    for sub in ["Full Sample (1974--2023)", "Excluding Crisis (No 2008)"]:
        row_str = sub
        for w in ["0.5%", "1.0%", "2.5%"]:
            b, t = results_grid[(sub, w)]
            row_str += f" & {b:.3f} [{t:.2f}]"
        lines.append(row_str + r" \\")
    lines.extend([
        r"\bottomrule",
        r"\end{tabular}",
        r"\end{table}"
    ])
    with open(filename, "w", encoding="utf-8") as f:
        f.write("\n".join(lines) + "\n")

Research & defense problems

During an oral defense examination, a committee member acts as a hostile referee, stating: "Your model works only because you included the 2008 financial crisis, which was a once-in-a-century anomaly." Provide the complete econometric defense.

Solution

Defense: "We have explicitly tested this hypothesis in our robustness battery (Table 6, Model 4). When the entire 2008–2009 Global Financial Crisis (24 months) is excised from the estimation window, the predictive coefficient changes from 0.3807 to 0.3860—a deviation of only 1.38%—and the \(t\)-statistic remains highly statistically significant at 11.08 under two-way clustering. Furthermore, estimating the model exclusively on the post-crisis period (2010–2024) yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.74 (\(t = 2.89\)). This proves that our findings are not driven by Lehman-era liquidity fire sales but reflect a persistent structural risk premium."

How does Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) establish that referee evaluation standards must incorporate multiple-testing adjustments rather than the traditional \(t = 2.0\) significance threshold?

Solution

Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) show that the finance discipline has collectively tested hundreds of candidate anomaly factors on the same historical CRSP/Compustat dataset. Under classical hypothesis testing with \(\alpha = 0.05\) (\(t = 1.96\)), five out of every 100 tested factors will appear statistically significant purely by chance. Using Bonferroni, Holm, and false discovery rate (FDR) adjustments, they establish that the hurdle \(t\)-statistic for a newly proposed factor must be raised to at least \(t \ge 3.00\) (corresponding to \(p < 0.0027\)) to maintain an overall family-wise error rate below 5%."

Synthesize the strategic considerations governing when an author should appeal an editorial rejection versus submitting to an alternative journal.

Solution

Strategic Guidelines:
1. Grounds for Appeal: Appeals are justified only if the rejection was explicitly predicated on an objective, verifiable factual or mathematical error by the referee (e.g. the referee asserted a proof was missing when it appeared in Section 3, or miscalculated an empirical identity).
2. Discretionary Judgments Are Non-Appealable: If the rejection was based on subjective evaluations of novelty, economic interest, or editorial fit, appeals are virtually never successful and damage professional relationships.
3. Opportunity Cost: Journal appeals typically take 3 to 6 months to resolve. In doctoral job market contexts, immediately revising the manuscript to address legitimate referee critiques and submitting to another top journal is almost always the superior strategy.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the central operational component of an effective referee response document?

    Options

    A structured response matrix demonstrates constructive compliance by addressing every critique with revised text and empirical evidence.

  2. Why is one-way firm clustering often insufficient in cross-sectional equity panel regressions?

    Options

    Equities share contemporaneous market and macroeconomic factors; failing to account for time correlation severely deflates standard errors.

  3. What is the recommended multiple-testing hurdle \(t\)-statistic established by Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) for new asset pricing factor discoveries?

    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) establish that family-wise error control across hundreds of tested factors requires a \(t\)-statistic threshold of at least \(t \ge 3.0\).

  4. Under Cameron, Gelbach & Miller (2011), how is the two-way clustered variance matrix calculated?

    Options

    Two-way clustering adds firm and time cluster variances and subtracts the White diagonal variance to avoid double-counting.

  5. What is the optimal response when a referee requests a robustness test that the authors believe is economically irrelevant?

    Options

    Constructive compliance requires executing the test to demonstrate empirical transparency and show that findings are uncompromised.

  6. Why is Fama-MacBeth standard error estimation numerically equivalent to time-clustering in a balanced panel?

    Options

    Both methods aggregate cross-sectional estimates over time, allowing contemporaneous shocks to correlate arbitrarily across assets.

  7. What distinguishes an empirical sensitivity grid from illicit p-hacking?

    Options

    Transparency is the dividing line: sensitivity grids reveal parameter stability across a pre-specified grid, whereas p-hacking conceals non-significant variations.

  8. When is an author formally justified in appealing an editorial rejection decision?

    Options

    Appeals are considered only for objective, demonstrable errors of fact or mathematical proof, not subjective disagreements over contribution.
