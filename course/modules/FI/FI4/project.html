Plumbing Monitor

Build an operational money-market monitoring engine tracking central-bank reserves, reverse-repo balances, the Treasury General Account, repo spreads, and swap spreads, then author a mechanism-level diagnostic memo on a historical liquidity stress episode.

The ladder

The project follows the mandatory four-rung curriculum ladder. Rungs 1 and 2 can be skipped if you achieved a score of 0.80 or higher on the module hub diagnostic and answered both skill checks correctly. Rung 3 is required for all students and is graded by both autograder and rubric.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Lab 1 demonstrates a fully solved 5-day plumbing monitor analyzing central-bank reserve extraction, repo corridor spreads, swap spreads, and regime classification.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Lab 2 provides a completion problem with key accounting and swap spread calculation lines left as blanks, graded instantly by Python expected output.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The graded deliverable: implement monitor.py to satisfy the autograder suite (tested across 50 random market seeds) and write an institutional diagnostic memo graded by rubric against calibrated exemplars.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Open-ended portfolio extensions: model Standing Repo Facility (SRF) ceiling arbitrage, dynamic haircut spirals under procyclical VaR models, or cross-currency basis dislocations.

Lab 1 · Worked Example: Money-Market Plumbing Monitor Pipeline

Tracking 5 consecutive simulated market dates through an emerging funding squeeze reveals an aggregate liquidity cushion contraction from $4,300B to $2,600B, driven by an expanding TGA (up $330B) and ON RRP drainage. This shifts the repo corridor spread from -10.0 bps (orderly) to +70.0 bps (acute repo spike) while 10Y swap spreads plunge to -70.0 bps.

"""Lab FI4-project-1: Plumbing monitor worked example (Rung 1).

A fully worked implementation of the money-market plumbing monitor.
Tracks bank reserves, ON RRP balances, TGA cash, overnight repo spreads,
and 10Y swap spreads from simulated public series.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any, NamedTuple


class SnapshotInput(NamedTuple):
    date: str
    res: float
    rrp: float
    tga: float
    sofr: float
    iorb: float
    ust10: float
    sw10: float


def analyze_plumbing_snapshot(
    date: str,
    reserves_bil: float,
    on_rrp_bil: float,
    tga_bil: float,
    sofr_pct: float,
    iorb_pct: float,
    treasury_10y_pct: float,
    swap_10y_pct: float,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Compute plumbing indicators and status flags for a single market date."""
    # Reserve adequacy indicator: Total liquidity cushion = Reserves + ON RRP
    total_liquidity_cushion = reserves_bil + on_rrp_bil

    # Repo corridor spread: SOFR - IORB (in basis points)
    # Positive spread indicates secured cash scarcity; negative indicates cash abundance
    repo_spread_bps = (sofr_pct - iorb_pct) * 100.0

    # 10Y Swap spread: Swap rate minus Treasury yield (in basis points)
    swap_spread_bps = (swap_10y_pct - treasury_10y_pct) * 100.0

    # Regime classification based on plumbing mechanics
    if repo_spread_bps > 10.0:
        regime = "Funding Stress (Repo Spike)"
    elif total_liquidity_cushion < 2500.0:
        regime = "Scarce Reserves Caution"
    elif swap_spread_bps < -25.0:
        regime = "Balance Sheet Dislocation (SLR Bound)"
    else:
        regime = "Abundant Reserves (Orderly)"

    return {
        "date": date,
        "reserves_bil": reserves_bil,
        "on_rrp_bil": on_rrp_bil,
        "tga_bil": tga_bil,
        "total_liquidity_cushion": total_liquidity_cushion,
        "repo_spread_bps": repo_spread_bps,
        "swap_spread_bps": swap_spread_bps,
        "regime": regime,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # 5-day simulated plumbing time series across an emerging stress episode
    data = [

        ),
        SnapshotInput(date="2026-03-09", res=3050.0, rrp=950.0, tga=780.0, sofr=4.35, iorb=4.40, ust10=4.25, sw10=4.02),
        SnapshotInput(date="2026-03-16", res=2800.0, rrp=700.0, tga=920.0, sofr=4.48, iorb=4.40, ust10=4.35, sw10=3.95),
        SnapshotInput(date="2026-03-17", res=2650.0, rrp=450.0, tga=950.0, sofr=4.65, iorb=4.40, ust10=4.42, sw10=3.90),
        SnapshotInput(date="2026-03-18", res=2400.0, rrp=200.0, tga=980.0, sofr=5.10, iorb=4.40, ust10=4.50, sw10=3.80),
    ]

    print("Money-Market Plumbing Monitor — Time Series (SIMULATED):")
    print(
        f"{'Date':<11s} {'Reserves':<10s} {'ON RRP':<9s} {'TGA':<8s} "
        f"{'Liquidity':<11s} {'Repo Sprd':<11s} {'Swap Sprd':<11s} {'Plumbing Regime'}"

            f"{snap['repo_spread_bps']:>+6.1f} bps   "
            f"{snap['swap_spread_bps']:>+6.1f} bps   "
            f"{snap['regime']}"
        )

Specification

Context

You are a senior funding quantitative strategist on a global fixed-income relative-value desk. Your desk finances multi-billion dollar positions in US Treasuries and relative-value basis trades across primary dealers, clearinghouses, and bilateral repo markets. You must build an automated analytical monitoring engine in monitor.py that computes no-arbitrage basis boundaries, trade P&L under repo financing, margin coverage solvency under market shocks, and dealer balance-sheet constraints under the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR). Concurrently, you must author an institutional memo explaining the structural mechanism of a historical funding stress episode (either the September 2019 repo spike or the March 2020 dash for cash).

Inputs

The autograder supplies an instance dictionary generated deterministically from an integer seed via make_instance(seed: int) in instance.py. The dictionary contains:

  • cash_price: float, dirty/clean price of the cash Treasury bond per 100 face value.
  • futures_price: float, quoted price of the Treasury futures contract.
  • conversion_factor: float, conversion factor of the bond for futures delivery.
  • days: int, days to futures delivery date (ACT/360 day count).
  • repo_rate: float, annualized repo borrowing rate (decimal).
  • haircut: float, bilateral repo haircut fraction (e.g. 0.02).
  • initial_margin: float, exchange futures initial margin fraction.
  • maintenance_margin: float, exchange futures maintenance margin fraction.
  • dealer_equity: float, Tier 1 equity capital of the dealer.
  • cash_treasuries: float, on-balance-sheet Treasury bond holdings.
  • repo_matched_book: float, gross repo financing assets.
  • derivatives_exposure: float, potential future exposure of derivatives.
Required outputs

A module file monitor.py exposing four core functions with strict type annotations:

  • net_implied_repo_rate(cash_price, futures_price, conversion_factor, days, repo_rate) -> float: computes the net implied repo rate (basis carry spread).
  • basis_trade_return(cash_price, futures_price, conversion_factor, days, repo_rate, haircut, initial_margin, shock_cash_price, shock_futures_price) -> dict[str, float]: returns a dictionary with cash_pnl, futures_pnl, repo_interest, total_pnl, initial_equity, and return_on_equity.
  • margin_coverage(cash_price, futures_price, conversion_factor, haircut, initial_margin, maintenance_margin, shock_cash_price, shock_futures_price) -> dict[str, Any]: returns a dictionary with repo_margin_call, futures_margin_call, total_margin_call, is_margin_called, and margin_coverage_ratio.
  • leverage_ratio(dealer_equity, cash_treasuries, repo_matched_book, derivatives_exposure, exempt_reserves_and_treasuries=False) -> dict[str, Any]: returns a dictionary with total_leverage_exposure, slr, is_compliant, and capital_surplus.

A findings memo file memo.md (approximately 1,000–1,500 words) diagnosing either the September 2019 repo spike or the March 2020 dash for cash at a mechanism level.

Constraints

Standard library only. All monetary values are computed in exact precision. The module must be strictly typed and pass mypy --strict without warnings. Negative prices or non-positive days must raise ValueError with clear error messages. The module must not mutate input arguments.

Grading

Both grading modes apply to this project:

  • Autograder: The implementation in monitor.py is tested by grader/test_project.py across 50 random market seeds (0 to 49). It verifies cash-futures no-arbitrage parity boundaries, margin call solvency equations, and SLR regulatory compliance.
  • Rubric: The institutional memo memo.md is evaluated by rubric across 5 performance criteria, each with 4 observable achievement levels.

"""Lab FI4-project-3: Rung 3 independent project verification check.

Validates the plumbing monitor against two stylized historical stress episodes:
the September 2019 repo spike and the March 2020 dash for cash.

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def evaluate_stress_episode(
    episode_name: str,
    reserves_bil: float,
    on_rrp_bil: float,
    tga_bil: float,
    sofr_pct: float,
    iorb_pct: float,
    treasury_10y_pct: float,
    swap_10y_pct: float,
    dealer_equity_bil: float,
    dealer_tle_bil: float,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Evaluate full plumbing metrics for a stress episode."""
    total_liquidity = reserves_bil + on_rrp_bil
    repo_spread_bps = (sofr_pct - iorb_pct) * 100.0
    swap_spread_bps = (swap_10y_pct - treasury_10y_pct) * 100.0
    slr = dealer_equity_bil / dealer_tle_bil if dealer_tle_bil > 0 else float("inf")
    slr_surplus_bil = dealer_equity_bil - 0.05 * dealer_tle_bil

    # Identify primary mechanism driver
    if repo_spread_bps > 50.0 and total_liquidity < 1600.0:
        driver = "Reserve Scarcity & Frictional Bottleneck"
    elif swap_spread_bps < -20.0 and slr < 0.052:
        driver = "Dealer SLR Capacity Exhaustion"
    else:
        driver = "Orderly Liquidity Distribution"

    return {
        "episode": episode_name,
        "total_liquidity": total_liquidity,
        "repo_spread_bps": repo_spread_bps,
        "swap_spread_bps": swap_spread_bps,
        "slr": slr,
        "slr_surplus_bil": slr_surplus_bil,
        "primary_mechanism": driver,
    }

Two Calibrated Exemplars

Exemplar A (Level 4 · Distinguished Desk Memo)

To: Investment Committee & Chief Risk Officer
From: Senior Funding Strategist
Date: March 25, 2020
Subject: Mechanism Diagnosis of the March 2020 Treasury Market Dislocation and Basis-Trade Collapse

1. Executive Summary: Between March 9 and March 23, 2020, the US Treasury market experienced an acute breakdown in liquidity. Contrary to standard flight-to-safety models where sovereign bond yields collapse during equity drawdowns, 10-year Treasury yields spiked by 64 basis points (from 0.54% on March 9 to 1.18% on March 18) while 30-year swap spreads inverted to -45 basis points. We demonstrate that this dislocation was not caused by sovereign credit fears, but by a dual-mechanism failure: (1) massive foreign official liquidation of US Treasuries meeting binding primary dealer balance-sheet constraints under the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR); and (2) a catastrophic deleveraging spiral among multi-strategy hedge funds running leveraged Treasury cash-futures basis trades.

2. Balance-Sheet Plumbing and Dealer Bottlenecks: Facing emergency US dollar funding needs, foreign central banks liquidated approximately $250 billion in long-term Treasury debt over a two-week period. In a frictionless market, primary dealers would absorb this supply. However, under the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR, \(\text{Tier 1} / \text{TLE} \ge 5.00\%\)), primary dealers face an unweighted capital charge on all on-balance-sheet assets. Between February 26 and March 18, dealer Treasury inventories jumped by over $60 billion, pushing primary dealer aggregate leverage exposures to their regulatory thresholds. As derived in Theorem 4.1, holding Treasuries requires earning a hurdle spread \(s \ge \lambda \rho\), equal to 50 bps under a 5% leverage ratio and a 10% equity hurdle. When market spreads did not widen sufficiently to compensate for allocated equity, dealers withdrew bids and widened bid-ask spreads tenfold, creating an "inconvenience yield" where physical Treasuries yielded substantially more than overnight index swaps (He, Nagel, and Song 2022, Table 1).

3. Basis-Trade Margin Spirals: Over 2018–2019, relative-value hedge funds had accumulated an estimated $600 billion gross position in the Treasury cash-futures basis trade, levered 20x to 40x via bilateral repo with haircuts of 1.5% to 2.0%. As foreign selling drove cash Treasury prices down while futures prices were supported by retail and asset-manager buying, the basis widened from parity to over +150 basis points. This price divergence triggered an immediate dual margin call: repo lenders marked collateral to market and demanded $2.50 to $3.00 per 100 face value in cash variation margin, while CME Group hiked futures initial margins from 2.5% to 4.5%. For a fund levered 30:1, total liquidity demands reached 112% of initial equity. Unable to meet margin calls, funds liquidated both cash Treasuries and short futures, dumping hundreds of billions of sovereign debt into an unbid market (Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2008).

4. Policy Interventions and Resolution: The fire sale was only halted when the Federal Reserve stepped in as buyer of last resort, purchasing over $1 trillion in Treasuries at an unprecedented pace of up to $75 billion per day, followed on April 1, 2020 by an emergency interim rule temporarily excluding US Treasuries and central-bank reserves from the SLR denominator (FRB 2020). This regulatory exclusion freed approximately $300 billion in balance-sheet capacity, allowing dealers to resume intermediation.

Exemplar B (Level 2 · Developing Desk Memo)

To: Risk Committee
From: Junior Analyst
Date: March 2020
Subject: Note on Treasury Market Selling

In March 2020, financial markets crashed because of the coronavirus pandemic. Investors were very scared and wanted cash, so everyone started selling everything, including government bonds. Normally bonds go up when stocks go down, but this time bond yields went up. Hedge funds that do the basis trade lost a lot of money because they had a lot of leverage and got margin calls. Dealers couldn't buy all the bonds because their risk limits were full. Finally, the Federal Reserve came in and bought a lot of bonds with QE, which fixed the problem and brought prices back to normal.

Exemplar B Critique: This memo misses observable criteria. It offers vague generalities ("investors were very scared"), fails to state the mathematical formula for the basis or the SLR, omits the dual margin call mechanics, provides zero exact dollar quantities or dates, and cites no peer-reviewed literature or primary regulatory releases.
