Interest rate benchmarks anchor trillions of dollars in floating-rate mortgages, corporate debt, interest rate swaps, and futures contracts worldwide. Following the structural decline of interbank unsecured lending and the manipulation scandals that discredited LIBOR, global financial regulators transitioned the bedrock of dollar financial contracts to the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR). Understanding how SOFR is constructed from transaction-level repo data is essential for pricing derivative cash flows, managing basis risk, and modeling money-market plumbing.

1From Unsecured LIBOR to Secured Transaction Benchmarks

The London Interbank Offered Rate (LIBOR) historically represented the estimated interest rate at which leading banks could borrow unsecured funds in the London interbank market. Following post-2008 regulatory capital reforms (which penalized unsecured short-term interbank wholesale lending), trading volume in unsecured interbank cash collapsed. As a result, LIBOR submissions increasingly relied on subjective expert judgment rather than executed market transactions.

The Alternative Reference Rates Committee (ARRC) selected the Secured Overnight Financing Rate as the preferred replacement for US dollar LIBOR. Rather than relying on subjective dealer surveys or unsecured exposures, SOFR is grounded in over one trillion dollars of daily observable, secured repurchase agreement transactions.

Definition 2.1 (Secured Overnight Financing Rate — SOFR)

The Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) is a broad measure of the general cost of financing cash overnight collateralized by US Treasury securities. Administered and published daily by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at approximately 8:00 AM ET, SOFR reflects transaction-level data across three segments: tri-party repo settled at Bank of New York Mellon, General Collateral Finance (GCF) repo cleared by FICC, and bilateral Treasury repo cleared through the FICC Delivery-versus-Payment (DVP) service.

The New York Fed publishes a hierarchy of Treasury repo reference rates:

  • Tri-Party General Collateral Rate (TGCR): Reflects tri-party repo transactions collateralized solely by Treasuries (excluding Federal Reserve transactions).
  • Broad General Collateral Rate (BGCR): Encompasses TGCR plus GCF repo transactions backed by Treasuries.
  • SOFR: Encompasses BGCR plus bilateral cleared Treasury repo from the FICC DVP service (filtering out specific-issue trades executed at rates below general collateral).

2The Volume-Weighted Median Algorithm

To ensure robustness against extreme outliers and transaction misreporting, SOFR is calculated as a volume-weighted median rather than a simple arithmetic average.

Theorem 2.1 (Volume-Weighted Median Formulation)

Let a set of \(N\) eligible transaction buckets be observed on business day \(t\), where each transaction \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) has executed interest rate \(r_i \in \mathbb{R}\) and trading volume \(v_i > 0\). Order the transactions such that rates are strictly non-decreasing:

\[ r_{(1)} \le r_{(2)} \le \dots \le r_{(N)} \]

Define total market volume as \(V_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{i=1}^N v_{(i)}\) and the cumulative volume function \(C(k) = \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)}\) for \(k = 1, \dots, N\) with \(C(0) = 0\). Then the volume-weighted median rate \(r_{\text{med}}\) is uniquely defined as:

\[ r_{\text{med}} = r_{(k^*)} \quad \text{where} \quad k^* = \min \left\{ k \in \{1, \dots, N\} : C(k) \ge \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}} \right\} \]
Proof

Because every volume \(v_{(i)} > 0\), the cumulative volume sequence \(C(k)\) is strictly increasing in \(k\), satisfying \(C(0) = 0 < \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}} < V_{\text{tot}} = C(N)\). The set \(S = \{k \in \{1, \dots, N\} : C(k) \ge \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}}\}\) is non-empty and bounded below by 1. By the well-ordering principle of the natural numbers, \(S\) contains a unique minimum element \(k^*\). Therefore, the rate \(r_{(k^*)}\) is uniquely determined.

Example 2.1 (Volume-Weighted Median vs. Volume-Weighted Mean)

Suppose three repo transactions occur: $300M at 4.30%, $500M at 4.35%, and $200M at 5.50% (a distressed outlier). Total volume is $1,000M. The 50th percentile threshold is $500M. Cumulative volumes are: at 4.30%, $300M (< $500M); at 4.35%, $300M + $500M = $800M (≥ $500M). The volume-weighted median is exactly 4.35%. In contrast, the volume-weighted mean is:

\[ \bar{r} = \frac{300 \times 4.30 + 500 \times 4.35 + 200 \times 5.50}{1000} = \frac{1290 + 2175 + 1100}{1000} = 4.565\% \]

The median is completely immune to the 5.50% outlier rate, whereas the mean shifts by +21.5 bps.

Lab 1 · SOFR Volume-Weighted Median Calculation

Given simulated transaction-level repo clusters totaling over $1 trillion across tri-party, GCF, and bilateral cleared repo, the volume-weighted median rate algorithm identifies the exact 50th volume percentile at 4.350% with an interquartile spread of 7.0 bps.

"""Lab FI4-L02-1: Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) calculation methodology.

Implements the New York Fed volume-weighted median algorithm across simulated
tri-party, GCF, and bilateral cleared Treasury repo transactions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from typing import NamedTuple


class RepoTrade(NamedTuple):
    rate_pct: float
    volume_millions: float
    segment: str


def compute_volume_weighted_percentiles(trades: list[RepoTrade], percentiles: list[float]) -> dict[float, float]:
    """Compute volume-weighted percentiles (including 50th for SOFR).

    Sorts trades by rate in ascending order, calculates cumulative volume,
    and returns the interest rate where cumulative volume crosses each percentile.
    """
    sorted_trades = sorted(trades, key=lambda t: t.rate_pct)
    total_volume = sum(t.volume_millions for t in sorted_trades)

    results: dict[float, float] = {}
    for p in percentiles:
        threshold = total_volume * (p / 100.0)
        cum_vol = 0.0
        selected_rate = sorted_trades[-1].rate_pct
        for t in sorted_trades:
            cum_vol += t.volume_millions
            if cum_vol >= threshold:
                selected_rate = t.rate_pct
                break
        results[p] = selected_rate
    return results


def main() -> None:
    # SIMULATED transaction dataset representing one business day across three repo segments
    rng = random.Random(42)

    segments = ["Tri-party", "GCF Repo", "FICC DVP Bilateral"]
    trades: list[RepoTrade] = []

    # Generate 15 representative transaction clusters
    base_rates = [4.30, 4.31, 4.32, 4.33, 4.33, 4.34, 4.34, 4.35, 4.35, 4.36, 4.37, 4.38, 4.40, 4.45, 4.50]
    for rate in base_rates:
        vol = round(rng.uniform(25_000, 120_000), 2)  # $25B to $120B per bucket
        seg = rng.choice(segments)
        trades.append(RepoTrade(rate_pct=rate, volume_millions=vol, segment=seg))

    total_volume = sum(t.volume_millions for t in trades)
    vw_mean = sum(t.rate_pct * t.volume_millions for t in trades) / total_volume

    percentiles = compute_volume_weighted_percentiles(trades, [25.0, 50.0, 75.0])
    sofr_rate = percentiles[50.0]
    p25_rate = percentiles[25.0]
    p75_rate = percentiles[75.0]

    print("SOFR Daily Calculation Summary (SIMULATED Market Day):")
    print(f"  Total Eligible Volume: ${total_volume:,.2f} million (${total_volume / 1_000:,.2f} billion)")
    print(f"  Total Transaction Count: {len(trades)} trade clusters")
    print("\nBenchmark Rates:")
    print(f"  Volume-Weighted Median (SOFR): {sofr_rate:.3f}%")
    print(f"  25th Percentile: {p25_rate:.3f}%")
    print(f"  75th Percentile: {p75_rate:.3f}%")
    print(f"  Interquartile Range: {p75_rate - p25_rate:.3f}% ({(p75_rate - p25_rate) * 100:.1f} bps)")
    print(f"  Volume-Weighted Mean: {vw_mean:.4f}%")

    print("\nVolume Breakdown by Segment:")
    for seg in segments:
        seg_vol = sum(t.volume_millions for t in trades if t.segment == seg)
        seg_share = seg_vol / total_volume
        print(f"  {seg:20s}: ${seg_vol:,.2f}M ({seg_share:.1%})")


main()

3Where the canon breaks

Standard macroeconomic models assume that the central bank maintains absolute control over short-term interest rates through administered policy rates. In reality, secured overnight rates frequently experience acute supply-demand spikes. On September 16–17, 2019, corporate tax payment deadlines coincided with a massive settlement of newly auctioned Treasury debt, draining over $100 billion of reserves from the banking system. SOFR spiked from 2.43% to 5.25% in a single day, with bilateral repo trading as high as 10.0% intraday, forcing the Federal Reserve to reinstitute overnight repo market interventions.

4Misconceptions

  1. “SOFR and the Effective Federal Funds Rate (EFFR) measure the exact same interest rate.”

    EFFR measures unsecured overnight borrowing among commercial banks, whereas SOFR measures secured Treasury repo borrowing across banks and non-bank financial institutions including money market funds and broker-dealers.

  2. “Because SOFR is secured by US Treasuries, it must always trade strictly below unsecured rates like EFFR.”

    During collateral absorption bottlenecks (such as large Treasury auction settlement dates), massive dealer financing needs can drive repo rates strictly above EFFR, as occurred dramatically in September 2019.

  3. “SOFR includes term borrowing rates such as 1-month and 3-month loans.”

    SOFR is strictly an overnight rate. Term SOFR rates used in loan agreements are derivative rates calculated from SOFR futures contracts traded on the CME, not cash borrowing rates.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Volume-weighted median from discrete table

Compute the volume-weighted median interest rate for four repo transactions: $400M at 5.20%, $350M at 5.22%, $500M at 5.25%, and $250M at 5.30%.

Solution

Total volume is \(V = 400 + 350 + 500 + 250 = \$1{,}500\text{M}\). The 50th percentile threshold is \(0.50 \times 1500 = \$750\text{M}\). Cumulative volume at 5.20% is $400M. Cumulative volume at 5.22% is \(400 + 350 = \$750\text{M}\). Since cumulative volume at 5.22% reaches exactly $750M, the volume-weighted median rate is 5.22%.

M2 · Volume-weighted interquartile range

Using the transactions in M1, compute the 25th percentile rate, the 75th percentile rate, and the interquartile range in basis points.

Solution

The 25th percentile threshold is \(0.25 \times 1500 = \$375\text{M}\). Cumulative volume at 5.20% is $400M (≥ 375M), so \(P_{25} = 5.20\%\). The 75th percentile threshold is \(0.75 \times 1500 = \$1{,}125\text{M}\). Cumulative volume at 5.22% is $750M; at 5.25% it is \(750 + 500 = \$1{,}250\text{M}\) (≥ 1125M), so \(P_{75} = 5.25\%\). The interquartile range is \(5.25\% - 5.20\% = 0.05\% = 5.0\) basis points.

M3 · SOFR compound daily average

Over a three-day weekend (Friday to Monday), Friday's published SOFR is 5.30%. If an interest rate swap accrues on compounded SOFR for those 3 days, compute the gross compounding factor.

Solution

Over the weekend, Friday's rate applies for 3 days. Under money-market conventions, the simple interest factor is \(1 + r \times \frac{d}{360} = 1 + 0.0530 \times \frac{3}{360} = 1 + 0.00044167 = 1.00044167\).

Proof

P1 · Breakdown point of volume-weighted median

Prove that the volume-weighted median has a breakdown point of 50%, meaning that an arbitrary manipulation of transaction rates cannot shift the median unless the corrupted volume strictly exceeds \(\frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}}\).

Solution

Suppose an adversary controls volume \(V_{\text{bad}}\) and attempts to drive the median to an arbitrary rate \(M \to \infty\). The honest volume is \(V_{\text{good}}\), with total volume \(V_{\text{tot}} = V_{\text{good}} + V_{\text{bad}}\). For the median to lie in the adversary's manipulated set, the cumulative volume of the adversary's transactions must satisfy \(V_{\text{bad}} \ge \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{2} (V_{\text{good}} + V_{\text{bad}})\), which simplifies to \(V_{\text{bad}} \ge V_{\text{good}}\), or \(V_{\text{bad}} / V_{\text{tot}} \ge 0.50\). If \(V_{\text{bad}} < 0.50 V_{\text{tot}}\), the cumulative volume threshold \(\frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}}\) must fall within honest trades, bounding the median within the range of honest transactions.

P2 · Monotonicity of median in rate shifts

Prove that if every transaction rate \(r_i\) increases by a non-negative constant \(\Delta \ge 0\) while all volumes \(v_i\) remain constant, the resulting volume-weighted median increases by exactly \(\Delta\).

Solution

Let the original sorted rates be \(r_{(1)} \le \dots \le r_{(N)}\). Adding \(\Delta \ge 0\) shifts each rate to \(r'_{(i)} = r_{(i)} + \Delta\). Because \(r_{(i)} \le r_{(j)} \iff r_{(i)} + \Delta \le r_{(j)} + \Delta\), the relative ordering of all transactions is preserved. Cumulative volume \(C(k)\) is unchanged. Hence \(k^* = \min\{k : C(k) \ge \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}}\}\) is identical. The new median is \(r'_{(k^*)} = r_{(k^*)} + \Delta\), establishing shift equivariance.

P3 · Existence of volume-weighted median

Prove that for any finite collection of positive volumes \(v_i > 0\) and real rates \(r_i\), a volume-weighted median always exists and is unique.

Solution

Because the number of transactions \(N\) is finite and each volume \(v_i > 0\), the cumulative volume sequence \(C(k) = \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)}\) forms a finite, strictly increasing sequence on \(\{1, \dots, N\}\) with \(C(N) = V_{\text{tot}}\). The target threshold \(T = \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{tot}}\) satisfies \(0 < T \le C(N)\). The set \(K = \{k \in \{1, \dots, N\} : C(k) \ge T\}\) is finite, non-empty, and possesses a unique least element \(k^* = \min K\). The corresponding rate \(r_{(k^*)}\) uniquely defines the volume-weighted median.

Empirical

E1 · September 2019 repo spike dynamics

On September 17, 2019, SOFR jumped to 5.25% while the Federal Reserve's target range was 2.00%–2.25%. Analyze the balance-sheet plumbing factors (corporate tax dates and Treasury auction settlements) that drained reserve liquidity.

Solution

Quarterly corporate tax payments drained approximately $35 billion from bank reserves into the Treasury General Account (TGA). Concurrently, $78 billion in net Treasury debt settled, requiring primary dealers to fund massive Treasury inventories via repo. With reserves already depleted by prior Quantitative Tightening, cash providers hit internal liquidity limits, leading to an extreme supply-demand mismatch that spiked repo rates to 10% intraday.

E2 · GCF vs. Tri-party repo spread

Explain why GCF repo rates typically trade slightly above tri-party general collateral rates (TGCR) during periods of balance-sheet constraint.

Solution

Tri-party repo primarily involves cash-rich non-banks (money market funds) lending to primary dealers. GCF repo is an interdealer market where dealers reallocate collateral and funding amongst each other. When primary dealer balance sheets become constrained, interdealer borrowing costs rise faster than cash pool deposit rates, opening a positive GCF–TGCR spread.

E3 · SOFR filtering of specific-collateral specials

Describe the specific algorithmic filter the Federal Reserve Bank of New York applies to bilateral DVP repo trades before including them in the SOFR median calculation.

Solution

In bilateral repo, trades can occur at below-market rates because the borrower specifically needs a scarce CUSIP ("on special"). To prevent these trades from distorting SOFR, the NY Fed sorts bilateral trades and removes transactions executed at rates substantially below the prevailing general collateral rate, ensuring SOFR reflects pure funding costs rather than security-borrowing premia.

Research

R1 · Credit sensitivity and the BSBY controversy

Critique why bank lenders resisted SOFR during periods of bank stress (favoring credit-sensitive benchmarks like BSBY) and evaluate why regulators forced BSBY's cessation.

Solution

Unlike LIBOR, SOFR is a secured rate that contains zero bank credit risk. In a banking panic, flight-to-safety drives Treasury repo rates down while bank marginal funding costs surge, squeezing bank net interest margins. Banks created credit-sensitive rates (like BSBY) to mimic LIBOR's credit spread. Regulators pushed back because credit-sensitive indices relied on the same low-volume, fragile commercial paper markets that failed in 2008, threatening systemic stability.

R2 · Standing Repo Facility as an interest rate ceiling

In July 2021, the Federal Reserve established the Standing Repo Facility (SRF). Formulate the arbitrage mechanism through which the SRF caps spikes in SOFR.

Solution

The SRF allows primary dealers and eligible banks to borrow cash overnight against Treasuries and Agency debt directly from the Fed at an administered minimum bid rate \(r_{\text{SRF}}\). If market repo rates rise above \(r_{\text{SRF}}\), dealers borrow from the Fed rather than private lenders, capping private market repo rates and establishing a credible ceiling on SOFR.

R3 · Convexity bias between SOFR futures and forward rates

Derive why CME 3-month SOFR futures prices trade at a convexity bias discount relative to forward rate agreements in a stochastic interest rate environment.

Solution

Futures contracts settle variation margin daily, whereas forward contracts settle cash flows at maturity. Because positive mark-to-market gains can be reinvested at higher interest rates when rates rise, long futures contracts have positive correlation with rates. By standard Cox-Ingersoll-Ross futures-forward parity, futures rates trade below forward rates, creating a convexity bias adjustment \(\text{Bias} \approx \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 t_1 t_2\).
