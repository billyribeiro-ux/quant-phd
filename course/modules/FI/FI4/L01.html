Modern wholesale financial markets do not run on unsecured bank deposits. They run on collateralized funding. Every day, trillions of dollars of securities are financed through repurchase agreements, providing leverage to market makers, funding to relative-value hedge funds, and high-quality liquid investments to cash pools. The terms of these trades—specifically the interest rate and the haircut buffer—dictate available leverage and systemic liquidity across the global financial architecture.

1Repurchase Agreement Mechanics and Economic Structure

Legally, a repurchase agreement is structured as a simultaneous sale and forward commitment to repurchase securities at a specified future date and price. Economically, however, it functions as a secured loan of cash backed by high-quality collateral.

Definition 1.1 (Repurchase Agreement)

A repurchase agreement (repo) is a financial transaction in which a party (the cash borrower / collateral seller) transfers eligible financial securities to a counterparty (the cash lender / collateral buyer) in exchange for cash principal \(P\), while simultaneously agreeing to repurchase equivalent securities on a specified settlement date \(t + d\) for an agreed repurchase price \(C_{\text{due}}\).

From the perspective of the cash receiver, the transaction is a repo. From the perspective of the cash provider, who buys the securities with an agreement to resell them, the exact same contract is designated a reverse repo.

Definition 1.2 (Repo Haircut)

Let \(V\) be the market invoice value (dirty price including accrued interest) of the collateral securities delivered at settlement date \(t\). The repo haircut \(h \in [0, 1)\) is the fraction of collateral market value withheld by the cash lender as an equity cushion against collateral price decline. The cash principal lent is given by:

\[ P = (1 - h) V \]

The implied initial leverage available to the collateral holder is \(\frac{1}{h}\).

Theorem 1.1 (Repurchase Cash-Due Formula under ACT/360)

Let the repo financing terms satisfy the following explicit conditions:

  1. Assumption 1: Initial cash principal \(P > 0\) in integer cents.
  2. Assumption 2: Annualized repo financing rate \(r_{\text{repo}} \ge 0\).
  3. Assumption 3: Financing term \(d \in \mathbb{N}\) days, where \(d \ge 1\).
  4. Assumption 4: Standard money-market day-count convention of ACT/360.

Then the total repurchase cash due \(C_{\text{due}}\) at maturity date \(t + d\) is uniquely given by:

\[ C_{\text{due}} = P \left(1 + r_{\text{repo}} \frac{d}{360}\right) \]

where the total interest accrued over the period is \(I = P \cdot r_{\text{repo}} \cdot \frac{d}{360}\).

Proof

Under the ACT/360 money-market convention, simple interest accrues linearly at periodic rate \(r_{\text{periodic}} = r_{\text{repo}} \frac{d}{360}\). The cash borrower is contractually obligated to return the full borrowed principal \(P\) plus accrued interest \(I = P \cdot r_{\text{periodic}}\). Factoring \(P\) yields \(C_{\text{due}} = P (1 + r_{\text{repo}} \frac{d}{360})\). By Assumptions 1–4, each factor is strictly positive and finite, proving uniqueness.

Remark 1.1 (Collateral Protection Buffer)

The haircut creates an immediate equity buffer of \(V - P = h V\). In tri-party Treasury repo, haircuts historically remained stable at 1.5% to 2.0% even during severe stress, whereas in non-agency securitized debt repo, haircuts jumped to 45% during the 2007–2008 financial crisis, forcing rapid deleveraging. If collateral value falls by \(\Delta V\), the cash lender remains fully collateralized without loss provided \(\Delta V \le h V\).

2Market Structures: Bilateral vs. Tri-Party vs. Centrally Cleared Repo

The US repo market functions across distinct institutional clearing mechanisms:

Segment Clearing Mechanism Primary Collateral Typical Participants
Tri-Party Repo Bank of New York Mellon (clearing agent) provides collateral allocation and custody. General Collateral (Treasuries, Agency MBS). Cash lenders (MMFs, corporate treasuries) lending to primary dealers.
Bilateral Repo Direct counterparty delivery-versus-payment (DVP) without a clearing agent. Specific collateral ("specials") and bespoke securities. Hedge funds financing positions with primary dealers.
Centrally Cleared (FICC DVP) Fixed Income Clearing Corporation (FICC) interposes as central counterparty. Treasuries and Agency debt. Interdealer brokers and sponsoring primary dealers.

Lab 1 · Repurchase Agreement Settlement and Haircut Sizing

Given simulated collateral with market value $10,000,000, a 2.0% haircut, a 4.50% repo rate, and a 14-day term, the exact cash lent is $9,800,000.00 and repurchase cash due under ACT/360 is exactly $9,817,150.00.

"""Lab FI4-L01-1: Repurchase agreement settlement, interest calculation, and haircut sizing.

Standard library only. All monetary values are computed in exact integer cents using Fraction.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction


def round_cents(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """Round Fraction to nearest whole cent, half-up: floor(x + 1/2)."""
    return math.floor(x + Fraction(1, 2))


def repo_cash_lent(collateral_cents: int, haircut: Fraction) -> int:
    """Cash lent against collateral with haircut h: P = (1 - h) * V."""
    if collateral_cents <= 0 or not (Fraction(0, 1) <= haircut < Fraction(1, 1)):
        raise ValueError("Invalid collateral or haircut")
    return round_cents(Fraction(collateral_cents) * (Fraction(1, 1) - haircut))


def repo_cash_due(cash_lent_cents: int, repo_rate: Fraction, days: int) -> int:
    """Repurchase cash due on ACT/360 day-count: C_due = P * (1 + r * d / 360)."""
    if cash_lent_cents <= 0 or days <= 0 or repo_rate < Fraction(0, 1):
        raise ValueError("Invalid repo financing parameters")
    interest_fraction = Fraction(cash_lent_cents) * repo_rate * Fraction(days, 360)
    return cash_lent_cents + round_cents(interest_fraction)


def main() -> None:
    # SIMULATED collateral values and terms
    collateral_value_usd = 10_000_000
    collateral_cents = collateral_value_usd * 100  # 1,000,000,000 cents ($10,000,000.00)
    haircut = Fraction(2, 100)  # 2.0% haircut
    repo_rate = Fraction(45, 1000)  # 4.50% annualized repo rate
    term_days = 14  # 14-day term repo

    print("Repo Contract Parameters (SIMULATED):")
    print(f"  Collateral market value: ${collateral_value_usd:,.2f} ({collateral_cents} cents)")
    print(f"  Haircut: {float(haircut):.2%}")
    print(f"  Repo rate: {float(repo_rate):.2%} (ACT/360)")
    print(f"  Term: {term_days} days")

    # Step 1: Compute cash lent at settlement (T=0)
    cash_lent = repo_cash_lent(collateral_cents, haircut)
    haircut_buffer_cents = collateral_cents - cash_lent
    print("\nSettlement Cash Flow (T=0):")
    print(f"  Cash lent by buyer: ${cash_lent / 100:,.2f} ({cash_lent} cents)")
    print(f"  Collateral buffer: ${haircut_buffer_cents / 100:,.2f} ({haircut_buffer_cents} cents)")

    # Step 2: Compute repurchase cash due at maturity (T=14)
    cash_due = repo_cash_due(cash_lent, repo_rate, term_days)
    interest_paid = cash_due - cash_lent
    print("\nRepurchase Cash Flow (T=14):")
    print(f"  Repurchase cash due: ${cash_due / 100:,.2f} ({cash_due} cents)")
    print(f"  Total repo interest: ${interest_paid / 100:,.2f} ({interest_paid} cents)")

    # Step 3: Haircut sensitivity table
    print("\nHaircut Sensitivity Analysis on $10,000,000 Collateral:")
    for h_pct in [0, 1, 2, 5, 10]:
        h_frac = Fraction(h_pct, 100)
        lent = repo_cash_lent(collateral_cents, h_frac)
        due = repo_cash_due(lent, repo_rate, term_days)
        interest = due - lent
        print(f"  Haircut {h_pct:2d}%: Cash Lent = ${lent / 100:,.2f} | Interest = ${interest / 100:,.2f}")


main()

3Where the canon breaks

Standard textbook finance treats repo as a risk-free transaction because it is fully secured by US government debt. In real financial panics, however, repo is subject to severe rollover and funding liquidity risk. As demonstrated by Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2008), when volatility rises or collateral values drop, lenders increase haircuts dynamically. A fund levered 50:1 on a 2% haircut that faces a haircut increase to 4% must immediately double its equity or liquidate half of its assets, triggering a margin-spiral fire sale.

4Misconceptions

  1. “Because a repurchase agreement is collateralized by US Treasuries, the cash lender bears zero counterparty credit risk.”

    If the borrower defaults simultaneously with a sharp drop in Treasury prices exceeding the haircut buffer, the lender incurs immediate principal losses upon liquidating the collateral into an illiquid market.

  2. “A repo haircut of 2% means that the borrower pays 2% interest on the borrowed funds.”

    The haircut is an equity deduction applied to collateral valuation to determine loan principal; the interest cost is determined independently by the annualized repo rate \(r_{\text{repo}}\).

  3. “In a repo transaction, title to the securities remains with the borrower while the lender merely holds a lien.”

    In standard repo master agreements (such as the SIFMA MRA), legal title transfers fully to the cash lender, granting the lender the legal right to rehypothecate or sell the securities during the term.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Overnight repo interest calculation

A dealer borrows cash against $50,000,000 face value of Treasuries valued at par with an overnight repo rate of 5.40% and a 2.0% haircut. Compute the cash borrowed and the total cash due at settlement the following day under ACT/360.

Solution

The collateral value is \(V = \$50{,}000{,}000\). Cash principal borrowed is \(P = (1 - 0.02) \times 50{,}000{,}000 = \$49{,}000{,}000\). The term is \(d = 1\) day. Accrued interest is \(I = 49{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0540 \times \frac{1}{360} = \$7{,}350\). Total cash due at repurchase the following morning is \(C_{\text{due}} = 49{,}000{,}000 + 7{,}350 = \$49{,}007{,}350\) (4,900,735,000 cents).

M2 · Implied leverage from haircut

A hedge fund finances $100,000,000 market value of Treasury bonds in the bilateral repo market. Compute the initial equity required and the resulting balance-sheet leverage for haircuts of (a) 1.0%, (b) 2.0%, and (c) 5.0%.

Solution

Initial equity is \(E = h \times V\) and leverage is \(\frac{V}{E} = \frac{1}{h}\). (a) At \(h = 1.0\%\), equity required is \(\$1{,}000{,}000\) and leverage is \(1 / 0.01 = 100\times\). (b) At \(h = 2.0\%\), equity required is \(\$2{,}000{,}000\) and leverage is \(1 / 0.02 = 50\times\). (c) At \(h = 5.0\%\), equity required is \(\$5{,}000{,}000\) and leverage is \(1 / 0.05 = 20\times\).

M3 · 30-day term repo interest

Compute the total interest due on a 30-day term repo for $25,000,000 principal at an annualized repo rate of 4.80% under ACT/360.

Solution

Under ACT/360, the interest is \(I = P \cdot r_{\text{repo}} \cdot \frac{d}{360} = 25{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0480 \times \frac{30}{360} = 25{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0040 = \$100{,}000\) (10,000,000 cents).

Proof

P1 · Invariance of haircut buffer to rate changes

Prove that under Theorem 1.1, the dollar haircut buffer \(V - P\) established at settlement date \(t\) is mathematically invariant to subsequent fluctuations in the repo interest rate \(r_{\text{repo}}\).

Solution

By Definition 1.2, cash lent is \(P = (1 - h)V\). The dollar haircut buffer is \(B = V - P = V - (1 - h)V = h V\). Differentiating \(B\) with respect to \(r_{\text{repo}}\) yields \(\frac{\partial B}{\partial r_{\text{repo}}} = \frac{\partial (h V)}{\partial r_{\text{repo}}} = 0\), since neither the contractual haircut fraction \(h\) nor the initial spot invoice value \(V\) depends on the financing rate \(r_{\text{repo}}\). Hence the initial dollar equity buffer is completely invariant to interest rates.

P2 · Maximum tolerable price drop without variation margin

Prove that if collateral market value drops from \(V\) to \(V'\), the cash lender's principal \(P\) remains fully secured (\(V' \ge P\)) if and only if the percentage price decline satisfies \(\frac{V - V'}{V} \le h\).

Solution

Full collateralization requires \(V' \ge P\). Substituting \(P = (1 - h)V\) gives \(V' \ge (1 - h)V = V - h V\). Rearranging terms yields \(h V \ge V - V'\), which upon dividing both sides by \(V > 0\) gives \(\frac{V - V'}{V} \le h\). Thus the haircut fraction \(h\) is exactly the maximum percentage price decline tolerable before collateral value falls below cash principal.

P3 · Linearity of repo cash flows across multi-collateral baskets

Prove that for a basket of \(K\) securities with values \(V_k\) and individual haircuts \(h_k\), the total cash lent equals \(P_{\text{tot}} = (1 - \bar{h}) V_{\text{tot}}\) where \(\bar{h} = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k h_k\) is the value-weighted average haircut.

Solution

Total collateral value is \(V_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{k=1}^K V_k\). Portfolio weights are \(w_k = \frac{V_k}{V_{\text{tot}}}\). Cash lent against each security is \(P_k = (1 - h_k) V_k\). Total cash lent is \(P_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{k=1}^K (1 - h_k) V_k = \sum_{k=1}^K V_k - \sum_{k=1}^K h_k V_k = V_{\text{tot}} - V_{\text{tot}} \sum_{k=1}^K w_k h_k = (1 - \bar{h}) V_{\text{tot}}\), proving linearity.

Empirical

E1 · Collateral price shock and margin call sizing

A fund finances $50,000,000 of Treasuries at a 2.0% haircut. If collateral value drops by 3.5% overnight due to an unexpected inflation report, calculate the variation margin call required to restore the 2.0% haircut on the new collateral value.

Solution

Initial collateral is \(V_0 = \$50{,}000{,}000\) and initial loan is \(P_0 = 50{,}000{,}000 \times 0.98 = \$49{,}000{,}000\). The new collateral value is \(V_1 = 50{,}000{,}000 \times (1 - 0.035) = \$48{,}250{,}000\). The maximum permissible loan against \(V_1\) at a 2% haircut is \(P_1 = 48{,}250{,}000 \times 0.98 = \$47{,}285{,}000\). The lender issues a variation margin call of \(P_0 - P_1 = 49{,}000{,}000 - 47{,}285{,}000 = \$1{,}715{,}000\). The fund must post $1,715,000 in cash immediately.

E2 · Haircut escalation during a funding crisis

Suppose a dealer financing $20,000,000 in structured debt sees its lender raise the haircut from 5.0% to 25.0% during a liquidity squeeze. If the dealer has only $1,000,000 in free liquid reserves, calculate how many dollars of collateral it is forced to liquidate.

Solution

Initially, loan is \(P_0 = 20{,}000{,}000 \times 0.95 = \$19{,}000{,}000\) and equity is \(\$1{,}000{,}000\). Total available equity with free cash is \(\$1{,}000{,}000 + \$1{,}000{,}000 = \$2{,}000{,}000\). At a 25% haircut, \(\$2{,}000{,}000\) equity can support a maximum position of \(V_{\text{max}} = \frac{2{,}000{,}000}{0.25} = \$8{,}000{,}000\). The dealer must liquidate \(\$20{,}000{,}000 - \$8{,}000{,}000 = \$12{,}000{,}000\) in collateral into the market.

E3 · Rehypothecation velocity

Explain how a single Treasury bond can settle multiple repo obligations through rehypothecation and calculate the net liquidity created if a bond is re-pledged across a 3-dealer chain with haircuts of 2.0%, 2.5%, and 3.0%.

Solution

Under rehypothecation, Dealer 1 receives the bond against $98 cash from Client A. Dealer 1 re-pledges the bond to Dealer 2 against \(\$100 \times (1 - 0.025) = \$97.50\). Dealer 2 re-pledges to Dealer 3 against \(\$100 \times (1 - 0.03) = \$97.00\). Total cumulative cash financed across the three transactions is \(\$98.00 + \$97.50 + \$97.00 = \$292.50\) on a single $100 bond, creating substantial funding velocity.

Research

R1 · Procyclical leverage and the margin spiral

Using the framework of Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2008), formulate how endogenous haircuts generate destabilizing fire-sale spirals when asset market liquidity and funding liquidity interact.

Solution

When market volatility \(\sigma_t\) rises, risk-averse lenders expand haircuts \(h_t \propto \text{VaR}(\sigma_t)\). Because maximum leverage is \(1 / h_t\), rising haircuts force leveraged traders to de-lever by selling assets. The resulting collective selling depresses market prices \(V_t\) and increases illiquidity and volatility, triggering another round of haircut increases in a self-reinforcing loss spiral.

R2 · General collateral vs. special repo rates

Define the concept of a "special" repo rate and derive why a high demand to borrow a specific Treasury bond drives its repo financing rate strictly below general collateral repo rates.

Solution

In general collateral (GC) repo, the lender accepts any Treasury security. When short sellers aggressively seek a specific CUSIP (e.g. on-the-run 10-year note), they are willing to lend cash at below-market rates to obtain that exact security. The special repo rate \(r_{\text{special}} < r_{\text{GC}}\) gives the bondholder a financing subsidy \(\text{specialness} = r_{\text{GC}} - r_{\text{special}} > 0\), which can even drive repo rates negative.

R3 · Central clearing and multilateral netting efficiency

Evaluate how the introduction of central clearing (such as the SEC Treasury clearing mandate) transforms bilateral counterparty exposures into multilateral novation and quantify its impact on gross balance-sheet exposures.

Solution

In bilateral repo, Dealer A lending $100M to B and borrowing $100M from C reports $200M in gross balance-sheet assets and liabilities. Under central clearing (FICC), both trades novate to the central counterparty. The offsetting positions net multilaterally to zero net exposure, dramatically reducing Total Leverage Exposure and freeing regulatory capital.

7Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the standard day-count convention used to calculate interest on US dollar repurchase agreements?

    Options

    US money-market repo transactions accrue simple interest on the actual number of elapsed days divided by a 360-day year (ACT/360).

  2. If a dealer pledges $10,000,000 market value of Treasuries at a 2.0% haircut, how many dollars of cash principal are lent?

    \(P = (1 - 0.02) \times 10{,}000{,}000 = 9{,}800{,}000\) dollars.

  3. From the perspective of a cash provider who buys collateral and agrees to sell it back, what is the contract called?

    Options

    A cash lender buys the security and agrees to resell it, which is the reverse repo leg of the trade.

  4. What is the theoretical maximum balance-sheet leverage permitted by a 2.0% repo haircut?

    \(\text{Leverage} = 1 / h = 1 / 0.02 = 50\times\).

  5. Compute the interest accrued on $20,000,000 cash principal borrowed for 1 day at an annualized repo rate of 4.41% under ACT/360, rounded to the nearest dollar.

    \(I = 20{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0441 \times (1 / 360) = 2{,}450\) dollars.

  6. What primary clearing institution acts as the tri-party clearing agent for the US repo market?

    Options

    Bank of New York Mellon is the sole surviving clearing bank providing tri-party collateral administration and settlement in the US.

  7. When a Treasury bond is trading "on special" in the repo market, how does its repo rate compare to the general collateral (GC) rate?

    Options

    High demand to borrow a specific bond allows holders to finance cash at cheaper rates, driving special repo rates below general collateral.

  8. What is the dollar size of the haircut equity buffer on $10,000,000 of collateral with a 2.0% haircut?

    \(h \times V = 0.02 \times 10{,}000{,}000 = 200{,}000\) dollars.
