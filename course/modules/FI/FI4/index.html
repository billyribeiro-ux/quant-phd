Module FI4 · Tier U · 60 hours · Part 3, Fixed Income & Markets

Money Markets & Plumbing

Master the institutional mechanics, settlement identities, dealer balance-sheet constraints, and arbitrage dislocations that govern the modern dollar funding market.

Prerequisites: FI1, E5
Introduces: 10 concepts across money markets, reference rates, central-bank plumbing, and basis trading
Project: Plumbing monitor (autograder and rubric)

42 h lessons · 18 h project (30.0% of the 60 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of FI4 you can:

  1. Calculate exact settlement cash flows, collateral haircut buffers, and repurchase cash due across bilateral, tri-party, and centrally cleared repo contracts.
  2. Derive the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) volume-weighted median from transaction-level distribution data and evaluate rate dispersion against policy benchmarks.
  3. Formulate the Federal Reserve balance-sheet accounting identity and trace how Treasury General Account (TGA) and Overnight Reverse Repo (ON RRP) flows absorb commercial bank reserves.
  4. Evaluate primary dealer intermediation capacity under the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) and calculate economic returns on allocated capital.
  5. Model the structural drivers of negative swap spreads and prove why balance-sheet capital charges prevent arbitrage capital from restoring parity.
  6. Simulate the Treasury cash-futures basis trade under leverage, margin calls, haircut hikes, and fire-sale spirals during market stress episodes such as the March 2020 dash for cash.

Prerequisites

FI4 assumes FI1 (Bond Math & Curve Construction) and E5 (Macroeconomics: Aggregate Demand, Supply & Monetary Policy), alongside their prerequisite closure {AC1, CF1, E1, M1, M2, P0.1}. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From FI1: bond price-yield relation, day-count convention, clean and dirty price, duration, and dv01.
  • From E5: policy rate, Taylor rule, and national accounts.
  • From AC1: balance sheet and double-entry bookkeeping.
  • From M1: present value, discount factor, and compounding frequency.
  • From M2: derivative and Taylor's theorem.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight testing prerequisite mastery across FI1, E5, AC1, and M1, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the US money-market standard day-count convention ACT/360, what is the exact year fraction for a 14-day borrowing term?

    Options

    US money markets (including Treasury repo and commercial paper) quote rates on the ACT/360 day-count convention (FI1, day-count convention).

  2. A Treasury note with face value $100,000 has a clean price of $100,250 and has accrued $750 in interest since the last coupon. What is its dirty invoice price in dollars?

    Dirty price = Clean price + Accrued interest = $100,250 + $750 = $101,000 (FI1, clean and dirty price).

  3. What does the DV01 of a fixed-income security measure?

    Options

    DV01 is the dollar value of a basis point, \(\text{DV01} = -\frac{dP}{dy} \times 0.0001\) (FI1, dv01).

  4. In modern central banking under an abundant-reserves framework, how does the central bank primarily establish a floor for overnight interest rates?

    Options

    Under the floor system, administered rates (IORB for banks and ON RRP for non-bank financial institutions) anchor overnight market rates (E5, policy rate).

  5. According to fundamental double-entry accounting, if a commercial bank buys $100 million in Treasuries from the central bank, paying from its reserve account, how is the commercial bank's balance sheet affected?

    Options

    This is an asset composition swap on the bank's balance sheet: cash reserves decrease and securities increase with zero net change in total assets (AC1, balance sheet).

  6. What is the present value of $10,000 received in exactly 1 year assuming an annual discount rate of 5.0% with annual compounding, rounded to the cent?

    \(PV = 10{,}000 / (1 + 0.05) = 9{,}523.8095...\) dollars (M1, present value).

  7. Under Taylor's theorem, what is the linear approximation of a bond price change \(\Delta P\) for a small yield change \(\Delta y\)?

    Options

    First-order Taylor expansion yields \(\Delta P \approx \frac{dP}{dy} \Delta y = -D_{\text{mod}} P \Delta y\) (FI1, duration; M2, Taylor's theorem).

  8. When the Treasury sells new debt to the public and deposits the proceeds into the Treasury General Account (TGA) at the Federal Reserve, what happens to commercial bank reserve balances?

    Options

    Because the TGA is a liability on the Fed balance sheet, an increase in TGA drains commercial bank reserves dollar-for-dollar (E5, policy rate).

  9. Skill check (rung 1). On a Federal Reserve balance sheet with total assets of $7,500 billion, currency of $2,320 billion, reverse repo balances of $1,250 billion, TGA of $900 billion, and other liabilities of $210 billion, calculate total bank reserves in billions of dollars.

    By the central bank balance-sheet identity: \(\text{Reserves} = 7500 - (2320 + 1250 + 900 + 210) = 2{,}820\) billion dollars (FI4 project rung 1).

  10. Skill check (rung 2). If the 10-year fixed SOFR swap rate is 4.00% and the 10-year benchmark Treasury yield is 4.25%, what is the 10-year swap spread in basis points?

    \(\text{Swap Spread} = (4.00\% - 4.25\%) \times 10{,}000 = -25.0\) basis points (FI4 project rung 2).

Lessons

Six lessons of seven study hours each (42 study hours total). Each lesson includes verified claims, a Python lab twin, common misconceptions, a tiered problem set with complete worked solutions, a key-results table, and an active retrieval quiz.

L01 · 7 h Repurchase Agreements and Collateral Haircuts Mechanics of repo lending, collateral valuation, haircut sizing, cash due on ACT/360 day-count, and tri-party vs. bilateral execution. L02 · 7 h Reference Rates and Overnight Funding (SOFR) The transition from unsecured LIBOR to secured overnight rates, the NY Fed volume-weighted median algorithm, and repo rate volatility. L03 · 7 h Central Bank Plumbing and Balance Sheet Dynamics Federal Reserve balance-sheet accounting, the reserve liability identity, TGA cash swings, and the Overnight Reverse Repo (ON RRP) facility. L04 · 7 h Dealer Intermediation and Balance Sheet Constraints Primary dealer market making, the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR), allocated equity costs, and shadow balance-sheet frictions. L05 · 7 h Swap Spreads and Negative Spreads Interest rate swap pricing, the economic emergence of negative 30-year swap spreads, and why dealer balance-sheet costs prevent arbitrage convergence. L06 · 7 h The Treasury Cash-Futures Basis Trade and the Dash for Cash Hedge fund basis-trade mechanics, conversion factor delivery, margin calls, fire-sale unwinds, and the March 2020 Treasury market dislocation.

Project ladder

The module concludes with an 18-hour capstone project: building an automated money-market plumbing monitor and authoring an institutional analytical memo on a historical funding stress episode.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A complete reference implementation analyzing 5-day money-market plumbing time series, computing reserve balances, corridor spreads, and dealer SLR capacity.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    A partially completed pipeline where you implement reserve identity extraction and 10Y swap spread analytics, checked instantly in Python.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Build the full plumbing monitor (autograded on simulated market seeds) and write an institutional memo explaining the mechanics of the September 2019 repo spike or March 2020 dash for cash (graded by rubric).

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch extensions

    Extend the monitor to simulate central-bank standing repo facility (SRF) backstops, dynamic haircut schedules, or multi-curve basis trades.
