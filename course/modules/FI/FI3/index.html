Bond Risk Premia & the Expectations Hypothesis

Empirical breakdown of the expectations hypothesis, time-varying bond risk premia, affine term structure modeling, and quantitative relative-value curve analytics.

Prerequisites: FI1, S3
Unlocks: GM2, FI5, FI6, STUDIO-FI
Project: Atlas A13: term-premium and rates-RV module (autograder)

56 h lessons across 8 lessons @ 7 h · 24 h project (30.0% of the 80 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of FI3 you can:

  1. Formulate the expectations hypothesis across yield, return, and forward-rate specifications and prove their mutual non-equivalence under Jensen's inequality.
  2. Estimate Campbell-Shiller and Fama-Bliss predictive regressions to test the empirical failure of constant term premia.
  3. Construct 1-year holding-period excess return panels and analyze their cross-sectional covariance and duration scaling.
  4. Extract the Cochrane-Piazzesi return-forecasting factor from forward rates and evaluate its characteristic tent-shaped coefficient geometry.
  5. Estimate model-based affine term premia using the Adrian, Crump and Moench three-step linear regression framework.
  6. Assess econometric challenges in term-structure models, including small-sample persistence bias and out-of-sample predictability decay.
  7. Calculate horizon carry, roll-down appreciation, and break-even yield shifts on static and dynamic yield curves.
  8. Attribute multi-leg relative-value curve trade P&L into carry, roll-down, level, slope, curvature, and convexity components.

Prerequisites

FI3 assumes FI1 (Bond Math & Curve Construction), S3 (Time Series & State-Space Models), and their prerequisite closure {M1, M2, M3, M4, S1, S2, CS1, CS3, CF1}. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From FI1: spot rate, forward rate, duration, convexity, DV01, and term spread.
  • From S3: vector autoregression, stationarity, Kalman filter, and spurious regression.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite concepts from FI1 and S3, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. If the 1-year spot rate is \(y(1) = 3.0\%\) and the 2-year spot rate is \(y(2) = 4.0\%\) (continuously compounded), what is the 1-year forward rate between years 1 and 2?

    Options

    \(f(1, 2) = 2 \cdot y(2) - 1 \cdot y(1) = 2(0.04) - 0.03 = 0.05\) or 5.0% (FI1, forward rate).

  2. For a 2-year zero-coupon bond with continuously compounded yield of 4.0%, what is its modified duration in years (\(D_{\text{mod}} = \tau / (1 + y)\))?

    \(D_{\text{mod}} = 2.0 / (1 + 0.04) \approx 1.9231\) years (FI1, duration).

  3. What does the term spread typically measure in Treasury market analysis?

    Options

    The term spread is the slope of the yield curve between two maturities (FI1, term-spread).

  4. For a stationary bivariate VAR(1) process \(X_{t+1} = \Phi X_t + \epsilon_{t+1}\), what condition must the eigenvalues of \(\Phi\) satisfy?

    Options

    Stationarity of a VAR(1) requires all characteristic roots to have modulus strictly less than 1 (S3, stationarity).

  5. What occurs when two independent unit-root non-stationary series are regressed on each other in OLS?

    Options

    Spurious regression results in non-standard distributions where conventional t-ratios reject the null of no relationship with high probability (S3, spurious-regression).

  6. If the 10-year yield is 4.50% and the 2-year yield is 3.50%, what is the 10y-2y term spread in basis points?

    \(4.50\% - 3.50\% = 1.00\% = 100\) basis points (FI1, term-spread).

  7. What is the role of the measurement equation in a linear Gaussian state-space model estimated via Kalman filter?

    Options

    The measurement equation \(y_t = A + B X_t + v_t\) links observed variables to latent state factors (S3, kalman-filter).

  8. What is the dollar convexity of a 2-year zero-coupon bond per unit face value (\(C \approx \tau^2\))?

    For a continuously compounded zero-coupon bond, convexity is \(\tau^2 = 2^2 = 4\) years squared (FI1, convexity).

  9. Skill check 1 (Rung 1): Given 1-year spot rate \(y(1) = 3.50\%\) and 2-year spot rate \(y(2) = 4.20\%\), what is the 1y1y forward rate \(f(1, 2)\) in percent?

    \(f(1, 2) = 2(4.20) - 1(3.50) = 8.40 - 3.50 = 4.90\%\) (Rung 1 task).

  10. Skill check 2 (Rung 2): On a static yield curve with \(y(1) = 3.50\%\) and \(y(2) = 4.20\%\), what is the 1-year roll-down return in percent for a 2-year zero-coupon bond, given by \((\tau - 1)\) \([y(\tau) - y(\tau - 1)]\)?

    \(\text{Roll-down} = (2 - 1) \cdot (0.042 - 0.035) = 0.007 = 0.70\%\) (70.0 bps) (Rung 2 missing step).

Lessons

  1. L01 · The Expectations Hypothesis & Its Classical Tests

    Formulations of the expectations hypothesis, Jensen's inequality, and Campbell-Shiller regression tests.

  2. L02 · Excess Bond Returns & Log Pricing Dynamics

    Log bond prices, 1-year holding period excess returns, duration scaling, and return covariance structure.

  3. L03 · The Term Premium: Definition, Decomposition & Properties

    Decomposing long yields into expected short-rate paths and term premia, and countercyclical macroeconomic drivers.

  4. L04 · Forward-Rate Regressions: Fama–Bliss & Campbell–Shiller

    Predictive regressions of excess bond returns on forward-spot spreads and the maturity structure of risk premia.

  5. L05 · The Cochrane–Piazzesi Factor & Tent-Shaped Predictability

    Single-factor return forecasting, the tent-shaped forward-rate weights, and out-of-sample predictability debates.

  6. L06 · Model-Based Term Premia & Affine Pricing Models

    Adrian-Crump-Moench 3-step linear regression model, no-arbitrage restrictions, and small-sample persistence bias.

  7. L07 · Carry, Roll-Down & Horizon Return Breakdown

    Instantaneous carry, static curve roll-down, break-even yield moves, and horizon scenario analysis.

  8. L08 · Relative Value Curve Trades & P&L Attribution

    Barbells, bullets, and butterflies: DV01-neutral weights, twist risk, cross-gamma, and linear factor P&L attribution.
