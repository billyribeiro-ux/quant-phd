Relative-value fixed income hedge funds and sovereign bond trading desks generate alpha by constructing curve trades—such as steepeners, flatteners, and butterflies—designed to be immune to parallel yield shifts. When a portfolio executes a 2s-5s-10s butterfly, rigorous risk management requires continuous performance attribution. Determining whether profits arose from net carry, roll-down aging, curve flattening, butterfly twist, or cross-gamma convexity is crucial for diagnosing trade health and separating repeatable skill from unhedged systematic factor exposures.

1Relative-Value Portfolio Construction: The Butterfly

A classic yield curve trade is the butterfly, which positions against the curvature of the term structure. A long belly butterfly buys the intermediate tenor (e.g., 5-year) and sells the surrounding "wings" (e.g., 2-year and 10-year).

To eliminate first-order exposure to parallel curve shifts, positions are weighted so the portfolio dollar duration (DV01) is zero:

\[ \sum_{i \in \{\text{wings, body}\}} w_i D_i = 0 \]

Under 50/50 duration weighting, each wing provides half of the offsetting duration: \(w_2 D_2 = -\frac{1}{2} w_5 D_5\) and \(w_{10} D_{10} = -\frac{1}{2} w_5 D_5\).

2Analytical P&L Attribution Framework

Let a portfolio hold weights \(w \in \mathbb{R}^m\) across bond maturities \(\tau \in \mathbb{R}^m\) over elapsed time \(\Delta t\). Over the holding period, the yield curve shifts from \(y_0\) to \(y_1 = y_0 + \Delta y\).

Definition 8.1 (Curve Trade P&L Attribution)

A curve trade PnL attribution is the mathematical decomposition of total portfolio return \(\Pi\) into deterministic time-accrual components and orthogonal yield-curve factor shocks via Taylor expansion:

\[ \Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{carry}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{roll}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{level}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{slope}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{curvature}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{convexity}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{res}} \]

where:

  • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{carry}} \equiv \sum_{i} w_i (y_{0, i} - r_{\mathrm{repo}}) \Delta t\)
  • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{roll}} \equiv \sum_{i} w_i (\tau_i - \Delta t)\left(y_{0, i} - y_{0}(\tau_i - \Delta t)\right)\)
  • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{level}} \equiv -\left(\sum_{i} w_i \tau_i\right) \Delta L\)
  • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{slope}} \equiv -\sum_{i} w_i \tau_i (\Delta y_i - \Delta L)\)
  • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{convexity}} \equiv \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i} w_i \tau_i^2 (\Delta y_i)^2\)
Theorem 8.1 (Taylor Series P&L Attribution Invariant)

For any discrete bond portfolio, the exact repricing return \(\Pi = \sum_i w_i \frac{P_{1, i} - P_{0, i}}{P_{0, i}} - r_{\mathrm{repo}} \Delta t\) articulates identically with the explained components up to third-order truncation error:

\[ \Pi \equiv \Pi_{\mathrm{carry}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{roll}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{level}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{slope}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{convexity}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{res}} \]

When the portfolio is duration-neutral (\(\sum_i w_i \tau_i = 0\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{level}} \equiv 0\), isolating the portfolio completely from parallel yield shocks.

Proof

Log price changes expand as \(\Delta p_i = -\tau_i \Delta y_i + \frac{1}{2}\tau_i^2 (\Delta y_i)^2 + \mathcal{O}((\Delta y)^3)\). Combining with the static curve aging decomposition \(\mathrm{Carry} + \mathrm{RollDown}\) and summing over weights \(w_i\) produces the decomposition. By construction, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{level}} = -\Delta L \sum_i w_i \tau_i = 0\) when \(\sum_i w_i \tau_i = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

3Macroeconomic Drivers & Literature Connections

As proved by Campbell and Shiller (1991) and Cochrane and Piazzesi (2005), curve trade profitability is heavily modulated by macroeconomic cycles. In economic downturns, central bank easing triggers steepening and butterfly cheapening (the belly underperforms), creating large negative slope and curvature attribution unless dynamically hedged.

Lab 1 · Relative value curve trade P&L attribution (2s5s10s butterfly)

Under a DV01-neutral 2s5s10s butterfly weighting, net level P&L is identically zero under parallel shifts, and total portfolio return decomposes into carry, roll-down, curve slope/curvature twist, and second-order convexity.

"""Lab FI3-L08-1: Relative value curve trade P&L attribution (2s5s10s butterfly).

SIMULATED: evaluates a duration-weighted 2s5s10s butterfly portfolio across
a combined shift in yield curve level, slope, and curvature.
Standard library and NumPy only; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def attribute_pnl(
    weights: np.ndarray,
    maturities: np.ndarray,
    y0: np.ndarray,
    y1: np.ndarray,
    dt: float,
    repo_rate: float,
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Decompose curve trade P&L into carry, roll, level, slope, convexity, and residual."""
    m_legs = len(weights)
    delta_y = y1 - y0
    delta_level = float(np.mean(delta_y))

    # Carry P&L
    carry_pnl = float(np.sum(weights * (y0 - repo_rate) * dt))

    # Roll-down P&L
    roll_vals = np.zeros(m_legs, dtype=np.float64)
    for i in range(m_legs):
        tau = maturities[i]
        if tau > dt:
            tau_rem = tau - dt
            y_rem = float(np.interp(tau_rem, maturities, y0))
            roll_vals[i] = tau_rem * (y0[i] - y_rem)
    rolldown_pnl = float(np.sum(weights * roll_vals))

    # Factor P&L
    level_pnl = float(-np.sum(weights * maturities * delta_level))
    slope_pnl = float(-np.sum(weights * maturities * (delta_y - delta_level)))
    curvature_pnl = 0.0
    convexity_pnl = float(0.5 * np.sum(weights * (maturities**2) * (delta_y**2)))

    # Exact bond repricing total return
    p0 = np.exp(-maturities * y0)
    p1 = np.exp(-(maturities - dt) * y1)
    ret_bonds = (p1 - p0) / p0 - repo_rate * dt
    total_pnl = float(np.sum(weights * ret_bonds))

    residual_pnl = float(total_pnl - (carry_pnl + rolldown_pnl + level_pnl + slope_pnl + curvature_pnl + convexity_pnl))

    return {
        "carry_pnl": carry_pnl,
        "rolldown_pnl": rolldown_pnl,
        "level_pnl": level_pnl,
        "slope_pnl": slope_pnl,
        "curvature_pnl": curvature_pnl,
        "convexity_pnl": convexity_pnl,
        "residual_pnl": residual_pnl,
        "total_pnl": total_pnl,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # 2s-5s-10s Butterfly Trade: Long 5y belly, short 2y and 10y wings
    maturities = np.array([2.0, 5.0, 10.0], dtype=np.float64)
    y0 = np.array([0.035, 0.042, 0.048], dtype=np.float64)

    # Fifty-fifty duration weighting:
    # w_2 * 2 + w_10 * 10 = w_5 * 5. With w_5 = 2.0:
    # w_2 * 2 = 5.0 => w_2 = -2.5; w_10 * 10 = 5.0 => w_10 = -0.5.
    # Total DV01: (-2.5)*2 + 2.0*5 + (-0.5)*10 = -5 + 10 - 5 = 0 (DV01 neutral!)
    weights = np.array([-2.5, 2.0, -0.5], dtype=np.float64)

    # Market shift over 3 months (dt = 0.25y):
    # Level shift +25 bps, flattening (10y drops 10 bps vs 2y), curvature drops 15 bps (belly outperformance)
    delta_y = np.array([0.0030, 0.0015, 0.0020], dtype=np.float64)
    y1 = y0 + delta_y
    dt = 0.25
    repo_rate = 0.032

    res = attribute_pnl(weights, maturities, y0, y1, dt, repo_rate)

    print("2s5s10s Butterfly P&L Attribution (DV01-Neutral):")
    print(f"  Weights: [2y: {weights[0]:.1f}, 5y: {weights[1]:.1f}, 10y: {weights[2]:.1f}]")
    print(f"  Net DV01 exposure = {np.sum(weights * maturities):.4f} (strictly zero)")

    print("\nP&L Decomposition (cents per dollar notional of wings):")
    for key in [
        "carry_pnl",
        "rolldown_pnl",
        "level_pnl",
        "slope_pnl",
        "convexity_pnl",
        "residual_pnl",
        "total_pnl",
    ]:
        val = res[key]
        print(f"  {key:15s}: {val:9.5f} ({val * 10000:7.2f} bps)")

    # Articulation invariant
    explained = sum(res[k] for k in ["carry_pnl", "rolldown_pnl", "level_pnl", "slope_pnl", "convexity_pnl"])
    print(f"\nExplained P&L (Linear + Convexity + Carry/Roll): {explained:9.5f} ({explained * 10000:.2f} bps)")
    print(f"Residual unexplained (higher-order Taylor error): {res['residual_pnl']:9.5f}")
    assert abs(res["total_pnl"] - (explained + res["residual_pnl"])) < 1e-12
    print("Exact articulation verified.")


main()

The attribution confirms that the duration-neutral butterfly is completely insulated from parallel level shifts (\(\Pi_{\text{level}} = 0.0\)). Total P&L of +97.3 bps was driven by positive carry (+11.3 bps), roll-down (+40.8 bps), and curve slope twist (+100.0 bps).

Where the canon breaks

Textbook fixed income assumes that a DV01-neutral butterfly trade has zero risk to yield curve movements. In active portfolio management, the canon breaks decisively:

  • Non-Parallel Twist Risk: DV01 neutrality protects only against pure parallel shifts. A flattening or steepening twist (the 2y moving differently from the 10y) introduces massive directional slope risk unless the wings are regression-weighted.
  • Convexity Mismatch: In large market moves, long-duration wings gain more convexity than the intermediate belly, causing net portfolio convexity to alter the trade payoff non-linearly.

Misconceptions

  1. A DV01-neutral butterfly is a risk-free arbitrage strategy.

    DV01 neutrality protects only against first-order parallel shifts. Butterfly trades are exposed to significant curvature risk, slope twists, and negative roll-down if curve dynamics move unfavorably.

  2. Fifty-fifty duration weighting guarantees that the portfolio has zero slope risk.

    Fifty-fifty weighting assumes that 2-year and 10-year yields shift symmetrically. In reality, short yields are much more volatile than long yields, requiring regression weighting (\(\beta\)-weighting) or PCA weighting to achieve slope neutrality.

  3. Taylor series P&L attribution with duration and convexity explains 100% of price changes.

    Taylor expansions are polynomial approximations. Large discrete yield jumps or severe curve deformations generate non-zero residual errors from higher-order derivatives and cross-gammas.

Problem set

Mechanics

Construct a 50/50 duration-neutral butterfly across 2-year, 5-year, and 10-year zero-coupon bonds with modified durations 2, 5, and 10 years respectively. If the portfolio is long $10 million of the 5-year note (\(w_5 = +10\)), determine the position sizes in the 2-year and 10-year wings.

Solution

The total belly duration contribution is \(w_5 \times D_5 = 10 \times 5 = 50\). Under 50/50 weighting, each wing must offset half of this duration (25 each): \(w_2 \times 2 = -25 \implies w_2 = -12.5\) million (short 2-year note), and \(w_{10} \times 10 = -25 \implies w_{10} = -2.5\) million (short 10-year note). This achieves net zero DV01.

A curve trade has weights \(w = [+2, -1]\) on bonds with durations \(D = [7, 10]\). If both bond yields rise by a parallel shift of 20 basis points, calculate the level P&L in basis points.

Solution

Portfolio net duration is \(\sum w_i D_i = 2(7) + (-1)(10) = 14 - 10 = +4\) years. For a parallel shift of \(\Delta L = +0.0020\) (20 bps), the level P&L is \(-\text{Net Duration} \times \Delta L = -4 \times 0.0020 = -0.0080\) or -80 basis points. The positive duration exposure drives the loss.

Calculate the second-order convexity P&L contribution for a $100 bond with duration 10 years and convexity 120 when yields jump by 100 basis points (\(\Delta y = 0.01\)).

Solution

The convexity formula is \(\frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(120)(0.01)^2 = 60 \times 0.0001 = 0.0060\) or 60 basis points ($0.60 on a $100 face amount). This positive value mitigates the linear duration loss.

Proof

Prove that under a two-factor term structure model \(y(\tau) = L + S \cdot e^{-\lambda \tau}\), a butterfly trade is simultaneously level-neutral and slope-neutral if and only if \(\sum_i w_i \tau_i = 0\) and \(\sum_i w_i \tau_i e^{-\lambda \tau_i} = 0\).

Solution

Log bond price change is \(\Delta p_i = -\tau_i \Delta y_i = -\tau_i (\Delta L + \Delta S e^{-\lambda \tau_i})\). Total portfolio change is \(\Pi = \sum_i w_i \Delta p_i = -\Delta L (\sum_i w_i \tau_i) - \Delta S (\sum_i w_i \tau_i e^{-\lambda \tau_i})\). For \(\Pi\) to vanish for all arbitrary shocks \(\Delta L\) and \(\Delta S\), the coefficients must vanish independently: \(\sum_i w_i \tau_i = 0\) and \(\sum_i w_i \tau_i e^{-\lambda \tau_i} = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that for any three-bond butterfly portfolio with maturities \(\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \tau_3\), it is impossible to simultaneously achieve dollar neutrality (\(\sum w_i = 0\)), duration neutrality (\(\sum w_i \tau_i = 0\)), and convexity neutrality (\(\sum w_i \tau_i^2 = 0\)) with non-zero weights.

Solution

The system of equations \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ \tau_1 & \tau_2 & \tau_3 \\ \tau_1^2 & \tau_2^2 & \tau_3^2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \\ w_3 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{0}\) is governed by a Vandermonde matrix. The determinant is \(\prod_{1 \le i < j \le 3} (\tau_j - \tau_i) = (\tau_2 - \tau_1)(\tau_3 - \tau_1)(\tau_3 - \tau_2)\). Because all three maturities are strictly distinct, the determinant is strictly non-zero. Hence the only solution is the trivial solution \(w_1 = w_2 = w_3 = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that a duration-neutral long butterfly (long belly, short wings) always has strictly positive net convexity (\(\sum w_i \tau_i^2 > 0\)) under 50/50 weighting.

Solution

Let belly maturity be \(\tau_2\) with weight \(w_2 > 0\). Wings are \(\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \tau_3\) with \(w_1 = -\frac{\tau_2}{2\tau_1} w_2\) and \(w_3 = -\frac{\tau_2}{2\tau_3} w_2\). The portfolio convexity is \(\sum w_i \tau_i^2 = w_1 \tau_1^2 + w_2 \tau_2^2 + w_3 \tau_3^2 = -\frac{\tau_2}{2} w_2 \tau_1 + w_2 \tau_2^2 - \frac{\tau_2}{2} w_2 \tau_3 = w_2 \tau_2 \left[\tau_2 - \frac{\tau_1 + \tau_3}{2}\right]\). If \(\tau_2\) is placed at or below the midpoint \(\frac{\tau_1+\tau_3}{2}\), duration convexity scaling creates net positive convexity due to quadratic convexity curvature. \(\blacksquare\)

Empirical

Why do empirical rates traders prefer regression weighting (\(\beta\)-weighting) over 50/50 duration weighting in 2s5s10s butterflies?

Solution

In historical US Treasury data, 2-year yields have higher empirical volatility than 10-year yields, and the regression slope of 10-year yield changes on 2-year yield changes is significantly less than 1.0 (typically ~0.65). 50/50 weighting leaves the portfolio overexposed to 2-year yield movements; \(\beta\)-weighting balances empirical risk exposures.

How does a curve steepener (long 2y, short 10y duration-neutral) perform during the onset of a recession according to Campbell and Shiller (1991)?

Solution

As the central bank aggressively cuts policy rates in response to recessionary indicators, 2-year yields plunge rapidly while 10-year yields fall more slowly due to long-run inflation expectations. The curve steepens dramatically, generating large positive slope P&L for the duration-neutral steepener.

During a market liquidity crisis (such as March 2020), what happens to the residual attribution \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{res}}\) in off-the-run vs. on-the-run relative value curve trades?

Solution

Liquidity premia blow out, creating large pricing divergences between on-the-run benchmark bonds and older off-the-run issues. Factor models based purely on smooth yield curves fail to explain these microstructure dislocations, causing residual P&L to spike and dominate trade performance.

Research

Formulate a PCA-based dynamic hedging algorithm for a multi-asset sovereign bond portfolio across 10 distinct maturity tenors.

Solution

Extract the first three eigenvectors \(v_1, v_2, v_3\) of the yield change covariance matrix \(\Sigma\). For a target portfolio with exposures \(w\), compute factor beta exposures \(\beta_k = w^\top (D \odot v_k)\) for \(k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). Select three liquid benchmark instruments (e.g., 2y, 5y, 10y futures) and solve the \(3 \times 3\) linear system to find hedge weights \(h\) such that \(h^\top (D_{\text{bench}} \odot V_{\text{bench}}) = -\beta\), immunizing the portfolio against level, slope, and curvature shocks simultaneously.

Analyze how the cross-gamma term \(\sum_{i \ne j} w_i w_j \operatorname{Cov}(\Delta y_i, \Delta y_j)\) impacts the risk of levered curve trades under non-Gaussian jump distributions.

Solution

While first-order attribution assumes continuous smooth yield changes, discrete central bank announcement surprises generate jump discontinuities. Positive cross-gamma across legs can turn sharply negative during liquidity evaporation when correlations break down toward +1 or -1, causing catastrophic drawdown in highly levered relative-value arbitrage books.

How does the Cochrane-Piazzesi factor forecast the cyclical performance of duration-neutral curve flattener and butterfly strategies?

Solution

Because the CP factor loads heavily on curve curvature (the tent shape), a high CP factor indicates that the belly is cheap relative to wings and expected risk premia are elevated. A quantitative strategy conditioning on high CP signals by going long the butterfly belly and short the wings systematically captures the reversion of curvature back to neutral, generating robust risk-adjusted alpha across cycles.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What condition must a relative-value bond portfolio satisfy to be duration-neutral (DV01-neutral)?

    Options

    Duration neutrality requires \(\sum w_i D_i = 0\), which immunizes the portfolio against first-order parallel curve shifts.

  2. In a perfectly duration-neutral butterfly portfolio, what is the level P&L under an exact parallel shift of +50 basis points?

    \(\Pi_{\text{level}} = -\Delta L \sum w_i D_i = -0.0050 \times 0 = 0\).

  3. In a standard long 2s-5s-10s butterfly trade, what positions does the trader take?

    Options

    A long butterfly buys the intermediate body (5y) and sells the outer wings (2y and 10y).

  4. If a portfolio has net duration of +4 years and yields rise by 20 basis points across all tenors, what is the level P&L loss in basis points?

    \(4 \times 20\text{ bps} = 80\text{ bps}\) loss.

  5. Why does fifty-fifty duration weighting often fail to eliminate curve slope risk in practice?

    Options

    Short yields have higher variance, requiring empirical beta or PCA weighting to achieve true slope immunity.

  6. What is the sign of the convexity P&L contribution \(\frac{1}{2}\sum w_i D_i^2 (\Delta y_i)^2\) for a single long bond position?

    Options

    Because \((\Delta y)^2 \ge 0\) and duration squared is positive, convexity always adds positive value for a long bond position.

  7. If a bond has convexity 120 and yields move by 100 basis points (\(\Delta y = 0.01\)), what is the convexity gain in basis points?

    \(\frac{1}{2}(120)(0.01)^2 = 60 \times 0.0001 = 0.0060 = 60\text{ bps}\).

  8. In a comprehensive P&L attribution, what causes the non-zero residual term \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{res}}\)?

    Options

    The residual represents third-order and higher Taylor series truncation errors from large discrete price changes.
