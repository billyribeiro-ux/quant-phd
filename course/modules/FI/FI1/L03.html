Spot Rates, Forward Rates, and the No-Arbitrage Identity

Coupon bonds bundle cash flows across multiple future maturities, obscuring the price of time. Zero-coupon spot rates isolate the pure cost of borrowing for individual horizons, forming the elementary building blocks of all fixed-income valuation. Implied forward rates encode the marginal borrowing costs between future dates, enforcing strict no-arbitrage relationships that anchor term structure models, interest rate derivatives, and risk management engines.

1Zero-Coupon Bonds and Spot Rates

A zero-coupon bond pays a single cash flow equal to its face value \(F = 1\) at future maturity \(\tau\) with no intermediate coupon payments. Let \(Z(\tau)\) denote the market price today of this zero-coupon bond.

Definition 3.1 (Spot Rate)

The spot rate (or zero-coupon yield) \(s(\tau)\) for maturity \(\tau > 0\) is the annualized rate of return earned on a zero-coupon bond from settlement today until maturity \(\tau\). Under annual compounding:

\[ Z(\tau) = \frac{1}{(1 + s(\tau))^\tau} \iff s(\tau) = Z(\tau)^{-1/\tau} - 1 \]

Under continuous compounding, the continuous spot rate \(s_{\text{cont}}(\tau)\) satisfies:

\[ Z(\tau) = e^{-s_{\text{cont}}(\tau) \tau} \iff s_{\text{cont}}(\tau) = -\frac{\ln Z(\tau)}{\tau} \]

The curve mapping maturity \(\tau\) to spot yield \(s(\tau)\) is the zero curve (or spot curve).

Theorem 3.1 (No-Arbitrage Discount Factor Monotonicity)

Assume the following conditions hold:

  1. Assumption 1: Frictionless, arbitrage-free capital markets without transaction costs or short-sale constraints.
  2. Assumption 2: Nominal interest rates are non-negative across all holding periods (\(s(\tau) \ge 0\)).
  3. Assumption 3: Investors can store cash without physical holding costs or decay.

Then the discount factor curve \(Z(\tau)\) is monotonically non-increasing in maturity: for any \(0 < \tau_1 < \tau_2\), \(Z(\tau_2) \le Z(\tau_1) \le 1\).

Proof

Suppose to the contrary that \(Z(\tau_2) > Z(\tau_1)\). An arbitrageur can buy the \(\tau_1\) zero for \(Z(\tau_1)\) and short the \(\tau_2\) zero for \(Z(\tau_2)\), generating an immediate cash inflow of \(Z(\tau_2) - Z(\tau_1) > 0\) at time 0. At time \(\tau_1\), the purchased bond matures, providing $1 of cash. By Assumption 3, the arbitrageur stores this $1 cash until time \(\tau_2\) with zero holding cost. At time \(\tau_2\), the stored $1 satisfies the short liability of $1 on the \(\tau_2\) zero. All future liabilities are covered with zero net outflow, locking in guaranteed riskless profit. To preclude arbitrage under Assumption 1, we must have \(Z(\tau_2) \le Z(\tau_1)\).

2Forward Rates and Replication

Definition 3.2 (Forward Rate)

The forward rate \(f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\) is the annualized interest rate contracted today for an investment spanning the future interval from time \(\tau_1\) to time \(\tau_2\) with \(0 < \tau_1 < \tau_2\). Under annual compounding:

\[ (1 + s(\tau_2))^{\tau_2} = (1 + s(\tau_1))^{\tau_1} (1 + f(\tau_1, \tau_2))^{\tau_2 - \tau_1} \] \[ f(\tau_1, \tau_2) = \left[\frac{(1 + s(\tau_2))^{\tau_2}}{(1 + s(\tau_1))^{\tau_1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\tau_2 - \tau_1}} - 1 = \left[\frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_2)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\tau_2 - \tau_1}} - 1 \]

For an instantaneous forward rate \(f(\tau)\) starting at future date \(\tau\), the continuous limit is:

\[ f(\tau) = \lim_{\Delta \tau \to 0} f(\tau, \tau + \Delta \tau) = -\frac{d \ln Z(\tau)}{d\tau} = s(\tau) + \tau \frac{ds(\tau)}{d\tau} \]
Theorem 3.2 (No-Arbitrage Forward Rate Replication)

Under Assumption 1, the unique forward rate preventing cash-and-carry arbitrage for 1-period borrowing from \(\tau_1\) to \(\tau_2 = \tau_1 + 1\) is given by the exact ratio:

\[ 1 + f(\tau_1, \tau_1 + 1) = \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)} \]
Proof

Consider the portfolio formed at time 0 by purchasing 1 unit of a zero maturing at \(\tau_1\) (costing \(Z(\tau_1)\)) and shorting \(k = \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)}\) units of a zero maturing at \(\tau_1 + 1\) (proceeds \(k Z(\tau_1 + 1) = Z(\tau_1)\)). The initial net cost at time 0 is \(Z(\tau_1) - Z(\tau_1) = 0\). At time \(\tau_1\), the purchased zero delivers $1 in cash. At time \(\tau_1 + 1\), the short zero requires paying \(k = \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)}\). This synthetic portfolio guarantees borrowing $1 at \(\tau_1\) and repaying \(\frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)}\) at \(\tau_1 + 1\). The effective gross borrowing rate is \(\frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)}\). To prevent arbitrage between this synthetic loan and a forward contract on the same horizon, the forward rate must satisfy \(1 + f(\tau_1, \tau_1 + 1) = \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_1 + 1)}\).

Remark 3.1 (Slope Relationship Between Spot and Forward Curves)

The continuous relationship \(f(\tau) = s(\tau) + \tau s'(\tau)\) establishes three fundamental geometric rules:

  1. If the spot curve is upward sloping (\(s'(\tau) > 0\)), the forward curve lies strictly above the spot curve: \(f(\tau) > s(\tau)\).
  2. If the spot curve is downward sloping / inverted (\(s'(\tau) < 0\)), the forward curve lies strictly below the spot curve: \(f(\tau) < s(\tau)\).
  3. If the spot curve is flat (\(s'(\tau) = 0\)), the forward rate equals the spot rate: \(f(\tau) = s(\tau)\).

Lab 1 · Spot Curves, Forward Rates, and the No-Arbitrage Identity

SIMULATED: Evaluates a 4-year spot curve, derives 1-year forward rates, verifies the exact rational no-arbitrage identity (1+s_2)^2 = (1+s_1)(1+f) using Fraction, and prints the 4y-1y term spread.

"""Lab FI1-L03-1: Spot rates, forward rates, and the no-arbitrage forward identity.

SIMULATED: annual zero-coupon spot yield curve and derived 1-year forward rates.
Rates are represented as exact rational Fraction instances.
Standard library only; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Spot rates for maturities tau = 1, 2, 3, 4 years
    spot_rates: dict[int, Fraction] = {
        1: Fraction(4, 100),  # 4.0%
        2: Fraction(9, 200),  # 4.5%
        3: Fraction(5, 100),  # 5.0%
        4: Fraction(27, 500),  # 5.4%
    }

    # 2. Discount factors: Z(tau) = 1 / (1 + s(tau))^tau
    discount_factors: dict[int, Fraction] = {}
    for tau, s in spot_rates.items():
        discount_factors[tau] = Fraction(1, 1) / ((1 + s) ** tau)

    print("Spot Rates and Zero-Coupon Discount Factors:")
    for tau in sorted(spot_rates):
        s = spot_rates[tau]
        z = discount_factors[tau]
        print(f"  tau = {tau} yr: s({tau}) = {float(s):.4%} ({s}), Z({tau}) = {float(z):.6f}")

    # 3. 1-year forward rates f(tau-1, tau) satisfying:
    # (1 + s(tau))^tau = (1 + s(tau-1))^(tau-1) * (1 + f(tau-1, tau))
    forward_rates: dict[tuple[int, int], Fraction] = {}
    for tau in range(2, 5):
        t_prev = tau - 1
        # Z(t_prev) / Z(tau) = 1 + f(t_prev, tau)
        one_plus_f = discount_factors[t_prev] / discount_factors[tau]
        f = one_plus_f - 1
        forward_rates[(t_prev, tau)] = f
        print(f"\nForward Rate f({t_prev}, {tau}):")
        print(f"  f({t_prev}, {tau}) = {float(f):.6%} ({f})")

        # Verify no-arbitrage forward compounding identity exactly
        lhs = (1 + spot_rates[tau]) ** tau
        rhs = ((1 + spot_rates[t_prev]) ** t_prev) * (1 + f)
        print(f"  Identity check (1 + s_{tau})^{tau} == (1 + s_{t_prev})^{t_prev} * (1 + f): {lhs == rhs}")
        assert lhs == rhs

    # 4. Term spread: s(4) - s(1)
    spread_4_1 = spot_rates[4] - spot_rates[1]
    spread_bps = float(spread_4_1) * 10_000
    print("\nTerm Spread:")
    print(f"  4y - 1y Spot Spread = {float(spread_4_1):.4%} = {spread_bps:.2f} bps")


main()

3Where the canon breaks

The classical Pure Expectations Hypothesis posits that the forward rate \(f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\) equals the mathematical expectation of the future spot rate: \(f(\tau_1, \tau_2) = \E[s(\tau_2 - \tau_1)]\). Decades of empirical asset pricing research decisively refute this hypothesis. Forward rates systematically exceed realized spot rates on average because long-term bondholders demand a risk premium (the term premium) for bearing duration and inflation risk. Consequently, forward curves cannot be interpreted as unbiased expectations of future monetary policy.

4Misconceptions

  1. “The forward rate is the market's forecast of what the future spot rate will actually be.”

    The forward rate is an arbitrage-free contract rate derived from current spot prices. It bundles both expected future spot rates and an unobserved term premium. Conflating the forward rate with an expected value ignores compensation for interest-rate risk.

  2. “Forward rates can never be lower than the spot rate.”

    Whenever the spot curve is inverted (downward sloping, \(s'(\tau) < 0\)), the forward rate \(f(\tau) = s(\tau) + \tau s'(\tau)\) must lie strictly below the spot rate to pull down the multi-period geometric average.

  3. “Spot rates and forward rates trade in separate markets with independent prices.”

    Spot rates and forward rates are algebraically coupled by no-arbitrage replication. Any divergence between a forward market quote and the ratio of zero-coupon discount factors triggers immediate riskless cash-and-carry arbitrage.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Discrete forward rate computation

Given 1-year and 2-year annual spot rates \(s(1) = 3.0\%\) and \(s(2) = 4.0\%\), compute the 1-year forward rate \(f(1, 2)\) under annual compounding.

Solution

Under annual compounding: \((1 + s(2))^2 = (1 + s(1)) (1 + f(1, 2))\). Substitute \(s(1) = 0.03\) and \(s(2) = 0.04\): \((1.04)^2 = (1.03) (1 + f(1, 2))\). Compute \((1.04)^2 = 1.0816\). Divide by \(1.03\): \(1 + f(1, 2) = \frac{1.0816}{1.03} \approx 1.050097\). Therefore, \(f(1, 2) \approx 0.050097\) or 5.0097% (500.97 bps).

M2 · Continuous forward rate from discount factors

Discount factors for maturities 3 and 5 years are \(Z(3) = 0.863838\) and \(Z(5) = 0.783526\). Compute the continuous forward rate \(f_{\text{cont}}(3, 5)\).

Solution

Under continuous compounding, \(Z(5) = Z(3) e^{-f_{\text{cont}}(3, 5) \times (5 - 3)}\). Rearranging gives \(f_{\text{cont}}(3, 5) = -\frac{\ln(Z(5) / Z(3))}{5 - 3} = -\frac{\ln(0.783526 / 0.863838)}{2} = -\frac{\ln(0.907029)}{2} \approx -\frac{-0.097581}{2} = 0.04879\) or 4.879% per annum.

M3 · Spot rate from sequential forward rates

One-year forward rates for years 1, 2, and 3 are \(f(0, 1) = 4.0\%\), \(f(1, 2) = 5.0\%\), and \(f(2, 3) = 6.0\%\). Compute the 3-year spot rate \(s(3)\) under annual compounding.

Solution

The spot compounding identity is \((1 + s(3))^3 = (1 + f(0, 1))(1 + f(1, 2))(1 + f(2, 3)) = (1.04)(1.05)(1.06) = 1.15752\). Taking the cube root: \(1 + s(3) = (1.15752)^{1/3} \approx 1.04996\). Thus, \(s(3) \approx 0.04996\) or 4.996% per annum.

Proof

P1 · Continuous forward rate identity

Prove that the continuous instantaneous forward rate \(f(\tau)\) satisfies \(f(\tau) = s(\tau) + \tau \frac{ds(\tau)}{d\tau}\).

Solution

By Definition 3.1 under continuous compounding, \(Z(\tau) = e^{-s(\tau) \tau}\). Taking the natural logarithm gives \(\ln Z(\tau) = -s(\tau) \tau\). By Definition 3.2, the instantaneous forward rate is \(f(\tau) = -\frac{d \ln Z(\tau)}{d\tau}\). Applying the product rule of differentiation: \(-\frac{d}{d\tau}[-s(\tau) \tau] = \frac{d}{d\tau}[s(\tau) \tau] = s(\tau) \frac{d\tau}{d\tau} + \tau \frac{ds(\tau)}{d\tau} = s(\tau) + \tau s'(\tau)\). This proves the identity.

P2 · Cash-and-carry forward arbitrage

Construct the explicit cash flows for an arbitrage strategy when a traded forward rate \(F_{\text{mkt}}\) satisfies \(1 + F_{\text{mkt}} > \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_2)}\).

Solution

At time 0: Short 1 forward contract agreeing to borrow $1 at \(\tau_1\) and repay \(1 + F_{\text{mkt}}\) at \(\tau_2\). Concurrently, buy 1 unit of zero-coupon bond maturing at \(\tau_1\) (costing \(Z(\tau_1)\)) and short \(\frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_2)}\) units of zero maturing at \(\tau_2\) (proceeds \(Z(\tau_1)\)). Net cash flow at time 0 is \(Z(\tau_1) - Z(\tau_1) = 0\). At time \(\tau_1\): Receive $1 from the maturing zero and lend it out via the forward contract. At time \(\tau_2\): Receive \(1 + F_{\text{mkt}}\) from the forward investment and repay the short zero liability of \(\frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_2)}\). The net cash flow at \(\tau_2\) is \((1 + F_{\text{mkt}}) - \frac{Z(\tau_1)}{Z(\tau_2)} > 0\). Because initial outlay is zero and terminal cash flow is strictly positive without risk, an arbitrage is realized.

P3 · Forward rate as a marginal rate

Show that the forward rate \(f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\) is a weighted marginal rate and prove that \(s(\tau_2)\) is a convex combination of \(s(\tau_1)\) and \(f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\) in log returns.

Solution

In continuous compounding, \(\tau_2 s(\tau_2) = \tau_1 s(\tau_1) + (\tau_2 - \tau_1) f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\). Dividing both sides by \(\tau_2\): \(s(\tau_2) = \frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2} s(\tau_1) + \frac{\tau_2 - \tau_1}{\tau_2} f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\). Let \(w = \tau_1 / \tau_2 \in (0, 1)\). Then \(1 - w = (\tau_2 - \tau_1) / \tau_2\). Thus \(s(\tau_2) = w s(\tau_1) + (1 - w) f(\tau_1, \tau_2)\), proving that the longer spot rate is a convex combination of the shorter spot rate and the forward rate.

Empirical

E1 · US Treasury forward curve inversion

During late 2023, the US Treasury 3-month yield was 5.40% while the 10-year yield was 4.20%. Compute the average 9.75-year forward rate starting in 3 months and explain the curve shape.

Solution

Using continuous compounding: \(\tau_1 = 0.25\), \(s(\tau_1) = 0.0540\), \(\tau_2 = 10.0\), \(s(\tau_2) = 0.0420\). \(\tau_2 s(\tau_2) = 10.0 \times 0.0420 = 0.420\). \(\tau_1 s(\tau_1) = 0.25 \times 0.0540 = 0.0135\). Marginal term is \(\tau_2 s(\tau_2) - \tau_1 s(\tau_1) = 0.420 - 0.0135 = 0.4065\). The forward rate is \(f(0.25, 10.0) = \frac{0.4065}{9.75} \approx 0.04169\) or 4.169%. Because the spot curve was inverted (\(s(10) < s(0.25)\)), the forward rate sits below the 10-year spot yield.

E2 · Term premium extraction

If the 5-year forward rate for borrowing 5 years forward is 4.80%, while survey forecasts of the 5-year spot rate in 5 years average 3.50%, estimate the 5-year forward term premium.

Solution

The forward rate decomposes as \(f(5, 10) = \E_0[s_5(5)] + TP(5, 10)\), where \(TP\) is the term premium. Substituting the survey forecast: \(4.80\% = 3.50\% + TP(5, 10) \implies TP(5, 10) = 4.80\% - 3.50\% = 1.30\%\) (130 basis points). Investors demand a 130 bp risk premium to lock in a 5-year loan 5 years forward rather than rolling over shorter debt.

E3 · Negative forward rates

In Swiss sovereign debt in 2016, 2-year spot rate was -0.80% and 3-year spot rate was -0.70%. Calculate the 1-year forward rate \(f(2, 3)\).

Solution

Under annual compounding: \(1 + f(2, 3) = \frac{(1 - 0.0070)^3}{(1 - 0.0080)^2} = \frac{(0.993)^3}{(0.992)^2} = \frac{0.979148}{0.984064} \approx 0.995004\). The forward rate is \(f(2, 3) = 0.995004 - 1 = -0.004996\) or -0.500%. Negative forward rates arise when sovereign short rates and deposit rates are negative.

Research

R1 · Fama-Bliss forward rate predictability

Describe the classic Fama-Bliss (1987) regression of excess bond returns on the forward-spot spread. How does the empirical rejection of the expectations hypothesis manifest in the regression slope?

Solution

Fama and Bliss regressed the 1-year holding period excess return on an \(n\)-year Treasury bond against the forward-spot spread: \(r_{x, t+1}^{(n)} = \alpha + \beta [f_t(n-1, n) - s_t(1)] + \epsilon_{t+1}\). Under the pure expectations hypothesis, expected excess returns are zero, implying \(\beta = 0\). Empirically, Fama and Bliss found \(\beta > 0\) (often near or above 1 for longer maturities), indicating that high forward spreads predict high future bond excess returns rather than rising future spot rates, confirming time-varying term premia.

R2 · Heath-Jarrow-Morton forward rate formulation

In modern term structure theory, forward rates are modeled directly rather than spot rates. State the advantage of the forward rate framework and write the HJM drift condition under the risk-neutral measure.

Solution

The primary advantage of the Heath-Jarrow-Morton (HJM) framework is that by taking the initial forward curve \(f(0, \tau)\) as an input, the model automatically fits the observed market term structure without requiring inversion of short-rate parameters. Under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), the absence of arbitrage dictates that the forward rate drift \(\alpha(t, \tau)\) is completely determined by its volatility structure \(\sigma(t, \tau)\): \(\alpha(t, \tau) = \sigma(t, \tau) \int_t^\tau \sigma(t, u) du\). No free drift parameter remains.

R3 · Convexity bias in forward vs futures rates

Explain why forward rates and futures rates diverge in stochastic interest rate economies. Formulate the convexity adjustment between Eurodollar/SOFR futures and forward rate agreements.

Solution

Forward contracts settle once at maturity \(\tau\), whereas futures contracts are marked to market daily with cash variation margin. When interest rates are positively correlated with futures prices, the daily cash inflows can be reinvested at higher rates, while cash outflows occur when rates are lower. This daily settlement advantage makes futures prices strictly higher than forward prices (and futures yields lower than forward rates): \(f_{\text{forward}} = f_{\text{futures}} - \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \tau_1 \tau_2\), where \(\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \tau_1 \tau_2\) is the convexity adjustment.
