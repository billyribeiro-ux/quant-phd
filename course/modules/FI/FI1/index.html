Module FI1 · Tier U · 90 hours · Part 3, Fixed Income & Markets

Bond Math & Curve Construction

Construct, price, and calibrate fixed-income term structures from market instruments to smooth parametric curves.

Prerequisites: M2, CF1
Unlocks: FI2, FI3, STUDIO-FI
Project: Atlas A5: curve engine (autograder)

72 h lessons · 18 h project (20.0% of the 90 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of FI1 you can:

  1. Price fixed-coupon bonds on coupon and non-coupon settlement dates using exact rational day-count fractions.
  2. Compute the yield to maturity of a coupon bond by bisection and prove the monotonicity of price in yield.
  3. Convert between money-market discount yields, bond-equivalent yields, and continuously compounded rates.
  4. Calculate Macaulay duration, modified duration, convexity, and DV01, verifying them against central finite differences.
  5. Extract zero-coupon spot rates from par bond quotes using iterative curve bootstrapping under no-arbitrage.
  6. Derive exact forward rates from spot rates and verify the absence of cash-and-carry arbitrage.
  7. Calibrate Nelson-Siegel and Svensson parametric models to market curves using variable projection.
  8. Evaluate empirical curve-fitting models against the official Federal Reserve Gürkaynak-Sack-Wright specification.

Prerequisites

FI1 assumes M2 (Calculus & Optimization), CF1 (Corporate Finance: Valuation & Capital Budgeting), and their prerequisite closure {M1, AC1, P0.1}. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From M1: present value, discount factor, annuity, perpetuity, and compounding frequency.
  • From M2: derivative and Taylor's theorem.
  • From CF1: internal rate of return and net present value.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite concepts from M1, M2, and CF1, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 items correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and correctly answer both skill checks.

  1. What is the present value of $1,000 received in 2 years at an annual interest rate of 5%, compounded annually?

    Options

    \(PV = 1000 / (1.05)^2 = 907.0294...\) dollars (M1, present value).

  2. A level perpetuity pays $50 at the end of each year. At a discount rate of 5% per annum, what is its present value in dollars?

    \(PV = C / r = 50 / 0.05 = 1{,}000\) dollars (M1, perpetuity).

  3. What is the present value of a 3-year ordinary annuity paying $1,000 annually at an interest rate of 10% per annum, rounded to the cent?

    \(1000 \times [1 - (1.10)^{-3}] / 0.10 = 2{,}486.85\) dollars (M1, annuity).

  4. What is the effective annual rate (EAR) corresponding to a nominal annual rate of 6% compounded monthly?

    Options

    \((1 + 0.06/12)^{12} - 1 = (1.005)^{12} - 1 \approx 0.061678\) or 6.168% (M1, compounding frequency).

  5. For a differentiable function \(f(x) = (1+x)^{-n}\), what is the first derivative \(f'(x)\)?

    Options

    By the power and chain rules, \(\frac{d}{dx}(1+x)^{-n} = -n(1+x)^{-n-1}\) (M2, derivative).

  6. Under Taylor's theorem, what is the second-order expansion of \(f(x + \Delta x)\) about \(x\)?

    Options

    Second-order Taylor expansion contains the \(\frac{1}{2}\) coefficient on the second derivative term (M2, Taylor's theorem).

  7. What is the internal rate of return (IRR) of a project with initial outlay $100 and a single cash flow of $110 at year 1?

    Options

    \(-100 + 110 / (1 + r) = 0 \implies 1 + r = 1.10 \implies r = 10\%\) (CF1, internal rate of return).

  8. If the net present value of a cash flow stream is strictly decreasing in the discount rate, how many internal rates of return can exist on \((-1, \infty)\)?

    Options

    A strictly monotonic function can cross zero at most once (CF1, internal rate of return).

  9. Skill check (rung 1). A bond with face value $1,000 (100,000 cents) and 5.0% annual coupon paid semiannually (coupon rate Fraction(25, 1000) per period) has 20 semiannual periods remaining. If the semiannual yield is Fraction(45, 2000) (2.25% per period), what is the dirty price on a coupon date in cents?

    The exact price is \(\lfloor 100000 \times [0.025 \times \frac{1 - (1.0225)^{-20}}{0.0225} + (1.0225)^{-20}] + 0.5 \rfloor = 103{,}991\) cents ($1,039.91) (FI1 project rung 1).

  10. Skill check (rung 2). Under the 30/360 day-count convention, a bond with face value $1,000 paying 5.0% annual coupon semiannually ($25.00 coupon per period) has accrued 45 days since the last coupon in a 180-day coupon period. What is the accrued interest in dollars?

    Accrued interest is \(25.00 \times \frac{45}{180} = 6.25\) dollars ($6.25 = 625 cents) (FI1 project rung 2).

Lessons

Eight lessons of nine study hours each. Every lesson includes verified claims, a Python lab twin, common misconceptions, a tiered problem set with complete worked solutions, a key-results table, and an active retrieval quiz.

L01 · 9 h Bond Cash Flows, Price-Yield Relation, and Yield to Maturity Fixed-coupon cash flows, exact pricing on coupon dates, monotonicity theorem, and yield to maturity by bisection. L02 · 9 h Yield Quotation Conventions, Day Counts, and Clean and Dirty Price ACT/360, ACT/365, and 30/360 fractions, accrued interest calculations, invoice price, and money-market conversions. L03 · 9 h Spot Rates, Forward Rates, and the No-Arbitrage Identity Zero-coupon discount factors, implied forward curves, no-arbitrage forward compounding identities, and term spreads. L04 · 9 h Par Yields, Curve Shapes, and Term Spreads Par bond coupon rates, par repricing theorem, relationships between spot, par, and forward curves, and term spread slopes. L05 · 9 h Macaulay Duration, Modified Duration, Convexity, and DV01 First- and second-order price sensitivities, dollar duration, portfolio DV01 aggregation, and central finite differences. L06 · 9 h Bootstrapping the Zero Curve Iterative extraction of discount factors from sequential coupon instruments and exact par bond repricing verification. L07 · 9 h Parametric Curves: Nelson-Siegel and Svensson Models Nelson-Siegel 4-parameter and Svensson 6-parameter functional forms, variable projection fitting, and the nesting theorem. L08 · 9 h The GSW Treasury Curve and Empirical Curve Evaluation The Federal Reserve Gürkaynak-Sack-Wright specification, filtering rules, RMSE comparison across curve construction methods.
