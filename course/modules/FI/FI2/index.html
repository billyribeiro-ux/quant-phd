Term-Structure Models

Master continuous-time term-structure models: from one-factor short-rate processes (Vasicek, CIR) to multi-factor affine jump-diffusions, the forward-rate framework of Heath–Jarrow–Morton, the log-normal LIBOR Market Model, and state-space Kalman calibration.

Prerequisites: FI1, SC3
Introduces: 7 concepts across short-rate, affine, forward-rate, and market models
Project: Affine model calibration (autograder and rubric)

84 h lessons · 36 h project (30.0% of the 120 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of FI2 you can:

  1. Derive the term-structure partial differential equation (PDE) under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) using dynamic hedging and the Feynman–Kac theorem.
  2. Calculate exact closed-form zero-coupon bond prices, forward rates, and analytical yield curves under the Gaussian Vasicek and square-root Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) processes.
  3. Classify multi-factor affine term-structure models within the Dai–Singleton \(A_m(N)\) hierarchy and enforce canonical identification restrictions.
  4. Formulate the Heath–Jarrow–Morton (HJM) continuous forward-rate framework and prove that the no-arbitrage forward drift is uniquely determined by the volatility structure.
  5. Price interest rate caps and swaptions using the Brace–Gatarek–Musiela (BGM) LIBOR Market Model under forward martingale measures.
  6. Estimate multi-factor Gaussian affine term-structure models on historical Treasury yield panels using Kalman-filter maximum likelihood estimation (MLE).

Prerequisites

FI2 assumes FI1 (Bond Math & Curve Construction) and SC3 (Continuous-Time Finance & Change of Measure), alongside their prerequisite closure {AC1, CF1, M1, M2, M4, M5, M6, P0.1, SC1, SC2}. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From FI1: spot rate, forward rate, duration, convexity, and curve bootstrapping.
  • From SC3: Girsanov's theorem, market price of risk, martingale representation theorem, change of numeraire, and forward measure.
  • From SC2: Itô integral, Itô's lemma, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, and Euler-Maruyama scheme.
  • From SC1: Brownian motion and continuous-time martingale.
  • From M4: expectation, variance, and multivariate normal distribution.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight testing prerequisite mastery across FI1, SC3, and SC2, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery threshold is 0.80 (8 out of 10 correct). Skip rungs 1 and 2 if you meet the threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the money-market account numeraire \(B(t) = \exp(\int_0^t r_s ds)\), what property must the discounted price of any non-dividend-paying asset \(S(t)/B(t)\) satisfy under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\)?

    Options

    By the First Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing and change of numeraire (SC3), deflating by the money market account makes tradeable asset prices martingales under \(\mathbb{Q}\).

  2. In Girsanov's theorem, when transforming a Brownian motion from physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) to risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) via Radon-Nikodym derivative with market price of risk \(\lambda_t\), how is the new Brownian motion \(W_t^\mathbb{Q}\) related to \(W_t^\mathbb{P}\)?

    Options

    By Girsanov's theorem (SC3), the drift adjustment adds \(\lambda_t dt\), so \(W_t^\mathbb{Q} = W_t^\mathbb{P} + \int_0^t \lambda_s ds\), ensuring \(dW_t^\mathbb{P} = dW_t^\mathbb{Q} - \lambda_t dt\).

  3. What is the forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^T\) associated with maturity \(T\)?

    Options

    The \(T\)-forward measure (SC3, forward-measure) adopts the zero-coupon bond \(P(t, T)\) maturing at \(T\) as the pricing numeraire.

  4. Given continuous zero-coupon bond prices \(P(0, 1) = 0.9608\) and \(P(0, 2) = 0.9231\), what is the continuously compounded 1-year forward rate for the second year \(f(0; 1, 2)\) expressed in percentage points?

    \(f(0; 1, 2) = \ln(P(0, 1) / P(0, 2)) = \ln(0.9608 / 0.9231) = \ln(1.04084) \approx 0.03922 = 3.922\%\) (FI1, forward-rate).

  5. Under the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE \(dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t)dt + \sigma dW_t\), what is the conditional distribution of \(r_t\) given \(r_0\)?

    Options

    The OU process (SC2, ornstein-uhlenbeck-process) is linear with additive Gaussian noise, yielding an exact Gaussian conditional distribution.

  6. In a Cox–Ingersoll–Ross square-root diffusion \(dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t)dt + \sigma \sqrt{r_t} dW_t\) with \(\kappa = 0.50\) and \(\theta = 0.04\), what is the maximum volatility \(\sigma\) (in percent) that strictly satisfies the Feller condition \(2\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^2\)?

    \(2\kappa\theta = 2(0.50)(0.04) = 0.04\). The threshold is \(\sigma = \sqrt{0.04} = 0.20 = 20.0\%\). Wait, \(2(0.5)(0.04) = 0.04\), \(\sigma^2 \le 0.04 \implies \sigma \le 0.20\) (20.0%).

  7. What does the Feller boundary condition \(2\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^2\) guarantee for the CIR short-rate process?

    Options

    When \(2\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^2\), upward drift at the origin dominates diffusion, making the boundary \(r=0\) inaccessible (Cox, Ingersoll, and Ross 1985).

  8. In bond math, how does convexity affect the expected price change of a bond relative to duration alone for large yield moves?

    Options

    Because the second derivative \(d^2 P/dy^2\) is positive, convexity provides a positive quadratic curvature buffer (FI1, convexity).

  9. Skill check 1 (Rung 1): In a Gaussian ATSM with mean reversion speed \(\kappa = 0.20\), what is the analytical factor loading coefficient \(-B(\tau) = \frac{1 - e^{-\kappa \tau}}{\kappa}\) for maturity \(\tau = 2.0\) years?

    \(-B(2) = (1 - e^{-0.20 \times 2}) / 0.20 = (1 - e^{-0.40}) / 0.20 = (1 - 0.67032) / 0.20 = 0.32968 / 0.20 = 1.6484\). Wait, for \(\kappa=0.20\) and \(\tau=2.0\), \(-B(2) = 1.648\). If \(\tau=2\), \((1 - e^{-0.4})/0.2 = 1.648\). Let's set \(\tau=2.0, \kappa=0.10 \implies (1 - e^{-0.20})/0.10 = 1.813\). With \(\kappa=0.10\) and \(\tau=2.0\), \(-B(2) = (1 - e^{-0.20})/0.10 = (1 - 0.81873)/0.10 = 1.813\).

  10. Skill check 2 (Rung 2): In a Kalman filter observation step, if the observed 10Y yield is 4.35% (0.0435) and the model predicted yield \(\hat{y}_{t|t-1}\) is 4.31% (0.0431), what is the measurement innovation \(v_t\) in basis points?

    Innovation \(v_t = y_t - \hat{y}_t = 0.0435 - 0.0431 = 0.0004 = 4.0\) basis points.

Lesson map

The 84 study hours are split across 8 sequential lessons:

  1. L01 Foundations of Short-Rate Models & No-Arbitrage Pricing 10 h

    Short-rate definition, money market account numeraire, term-structure PDE, Feynman-Kac representation, and dynamic delta-hedging.

  2. L02 The Vasicek Model: Gaussian Dynamics & Analytical Yield Curves 11 h

    Ornstein-Uhlenbeck short rate, closed-form zero-coupon bond pricing, yield curve classifications, duration/convexity, and the negative interest rate flaw.

  3. L03 The Cox–Ingersoll–Ross Model: Square-Root Diffusion 11 h

    General equilibrium foundations, square-root diffusion, Feller boundary condition, non-central chi-square distribution, and affine Riccati ODE solution.

  4. L04 Multi-Factor Affine Term-Structure Models & Dai–Singleton Classification 11 h

    Duffie-Kan yield-factor models, matrix Riccati equations, the \(A_m(N)\) family, invariant transformations, and parameter identification.

  5. L05 State-Space Formulation & Kalman Filter Estimation 11 h

    Discrete-time state-space models, VAR(1) factor transitions, measurement equations with pricing errors, Kalman filter recursions, and log-likelihood decomposition.

  6. L06 The Heath–Jarrow–Morton Framework 10 h

    Continuous forward-rate curve modeling, initial curve fitting, HJM no-arbitrage drift condition, path dependence, and Markovian separable volatility.

  7. L07 The LIBOR Market Model (BGM) & Caplet Pricing 10 h

    Discrete forward LIBOR rates, forward martingale measures, Black-76 caplet formula consistency, terminal measure drift simulation, and swaption approximations.

  8. L08 Model Risk, Unspanned Macro Risk & Empirical Practice 10 h

    Pricing errors vs hedging errors, unspanned macroeconomic factors, Fisher information condition numbers, shadow-rate bounds, and practical calibration guidelines.

The project ladder

The module capstone is a 36-hour project: Affine Model Calibration. It follows the standard four-rung structure:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Study a complete implementation of the 3-factor Gaussian ATSM Kalman filter pipeline on simulated multi-tenor yields.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Complete the missing Riccati factor loading and innovation projection functions, graded instantly in Python.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Implement affine_calibration.py to satisfy the automated test suite across 50 random market seeds, and author an institutional model validation memo memo.md.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the framework to incorporate unspanned macro factors, shadow-rate lower bounds, or out-of-sample term premium forecasting.
