European Swaptions & the Bachelier Normal Model

European swaptions are among the highest-volume non-linear derivatives in global capital markets, enabling mortgage servicers, liability-driven pension funds, and sovereign debt managers to hedge interest-rate duration and convexity. While the Black-76 framework served as the classical standard under positive interest rates, the emergence of negative policy rates in Europe and Japan forced the global interbank market to adopt Louis Bachelier's 1900 arithmetic Brownian motion model. Understanding swaption annuity measures, normal volatility quotation, and Bachelier Greeks is essential for fixed income risk management.

1Swaption Payoffs & the Swap Annuity Measure

A swaption is an option to enter into an interest rate swap. Market conventions distinguish between payer and receiver swaptions based on the fixed-rate cash flow.

Definition 4.1 (European Swaption)

A swaption is an option granting the holder the right, but not the obligation, to enter into an underlying interest-rate swap at a specified future expiry date \(T_{\text{exp}}\) with pre-agreed fixed strike rate \(K\) and tenor spanning \([T_{\text{exp}}, T_M]\). A payer swaption gives the right to pay fixed \(K\) and receive floating, yielding terminal payoff at \(T_{\text{exp}}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{payer}}(T_{\text{exp}}) &= A(T_{\text{exp}}) \max\left( S(T_{\text{exp}}) - K, \, 0 \right) \end{aligned} \]

A receiver swaption gives the right to receive fixed \(K\) and pay floating, with payoff:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{receiver}}(T_{\text{exp}}) &= A(T_{\text{exp}}) \max\left( K - S(T_{\text{exp}}), \, 0 \right) \end{aligned} \]

where \(A(t) = \sum_{k=1}^M \tau_k P^D(t, T_k)\) is the swap annuity (basis point value) and \(S(t)\) is the forward par swap rate.

Theorem 4.1 (Swap Annuity Numéraire and Martingale Property)

Let \(A(t) = \sum_{k=1}^M \tau_k P^D(t, T_k)\) serve as the numéraire asset. Under the swap annuity probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}^A\), the forward par swap rate \(S(t)\) is a driftless martingale:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^A} \left[ S(T_{\text{exp}}) \right] &= S(t), \quad \forall t \le T_{\text{exp}} \end{aligned} \]

Proof. The present value of the floating leg of the forward swap is \(V_{\text{flt}}(t) = S(t) A(t)\). Since the floating leg is a portfolio of tradable interest rate instruments (or replicable forward contracts), its relative price deflated by the annuity numéraire is \(\frac{V_{\text{flt}}(t)}{A(t)} = \frac{S(t) A(t)}{A(t)} = S(t)\). By the change-of-numéraire theorem, any tradable asset deflated by the numéraire is a local martingale under the associated measure. Assuming bounded variance, \(S(t)\) is a true \(\mathbb{Q}^A\)-martingale.

2The Bachelier Normal Model

In his 1900 doctoral dissertation, Louis Bachelier formulated the first continuous-time mathematical theory of option pricing using arithmetic Brownian motion. Following the collapse of interest rates into negative territory in the 2010s, interdealer swaption brokerages transitioned from Black-76 to the Bachelier model.

Definition 4.2 (The Bachelier Normal Model)

The Bachelier model (or normal interest-rate model) specifies that under the swap annuity measure \(\mathbb{Q}^A\), the forward par swap rate \(S(t)\) evolves according to arithmetic Brownian motion without drift:

\[ \begin{aligned} dS(t) &= \sigma_N \, dW_t^A \end{aligned} \]

where \(\sigma_N > 0\) is the absolute (normal) volatility parameter, typically quoted in basis points per year (bp/y), and \(W_t^A\) is a standard Brownian motion under \(\mathbb{Q}^A\).

Theorem 4.2 (Bachelier Swaption Closed-Form Pricing Formula)

The present value at time \(t \le T_{\text{exp}}\) of a European payer swaption with strike \(K\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{payer}}(t) &= A(t) \left[ (S(t) - K) \Phi(d) + \sigma_N \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t} \, \phi(d) \right] \end{aligned} \]

and the receiver swaption value is:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{receiver}}(t) &= A(t) \left[ (K - S(t)) \Phi(-d) + \sigma_N \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t} \, \phi(d) \right] \end{aligned} \]

where \(\Phi(\cdot)\) and \(\phi(\cdot)\) are the standard normal CDF and PDF, and the standardized moneyness \(d\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} d &= \frac{S(t) - K}{\sigma_N \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t}} \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Under \(\mathbb{Q}^A\), the terminal swap rate is normally distributed: \(S(T_{\text{exp}}) \sim \mathcal{N}(S(t), \sigma_N^2 (T_{\text{exp}} - t))\). Let \(\omega = \sigma_N \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t}\). We express \(S(T_{\text{exp}}) = S(t) + \omega Z\) where \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\). The expectation is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^A} \left[ (S(T_{\text{exp}}) - K)^+ \right] &= \int_{-d}^\infty (S(t) - K + \omega z) \phi(z) dz \\ &= (S(t) - K) \int_{-d}^\infty \phi(z) dz + \omega \int_{-d}^\infty z \phi(z) dz \\ &= (S(t) - K) \Phi(d) + \omega \phi(d) \end{aligned} \]

Multiplying by the numéraire \(A(t)\) yields the payer swaption price. Subtracting \(A(t)(S(t) - K)\) directly yields the receiver swaption formula via put-call parity.

Bachelier swaption prices exhibit clean analytical sensitivities (Greeks):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Delta} &= \frac{\partial V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial S} = A(t) \Phi(d), \quad \text{Gamma} = \frac{\partial^2 V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial S^2} = A(t) \frac{\phi(d)}{\sigma_N \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t}} \\ \text{Vega} &= \frac{\partial V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial \sigma_N} = A(t) \sqrt{T_{\text{exp}} - t} \, \phi(d) \end{aligned} \]

3Black-76 vs. Bachelier Volatility Conversion

When interest rates are positive, traders frequently convert between Black lognormal volatility \(\sigma_B\) and Bachelier normal volatility \(\sigma_N\). Equating at-the-money option values (\(S = K\)):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{ATM}}^{\text{Bachelier}} &= A(t) \sigma_N \sqrt{T} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \\ V_{\text{ATM}}^{\text{Black-76}} &= A(t) S_0 \left[ 2 \Phi\left(\frac{1}{2} \sigma_B \sqrt{T} \right) - 1 \right] \approx A(t) S_0 \sigma_B \sqrt{T} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \end{aligned} \]

To first order, the relationship is \(\sigma_N \approx S_0 \sigma_B\). A second-order expansion yields the Choi-Fabozzi-de Wet approximation:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_N &\approx S_0 \sigma_B \left( 1 - \frac{1}{24} \sigma_B^2 T \right) \end{aligned} \]

For example, if \(S_0 = 4.00\%\) and \(\sigma_B = 20\%\), the equivalent normal volatility is \(\sigma_N \approx 0.04 \times 0.20 = 0.0080\) or \(80\) bps/year.

Lab 4.1 · Swaptions: Black-76 Breakdown vs. Bachelier Normal Model

Hypothesis: Black-76 swaption pricing fails catastrophically under negative forward swap rates, whereas the Bachelier normal model prices swaptions robustly across positive, zero, and negative rate regimes while maintaining exact put-call parity and closed-form Greeks matching finite differences.

"""Lab FI6-L04-1: European Swaptions: Black-76 vs. Bachelier Normal Model.

Compares European swaption valuation under Black-76 and the Bachelier (1900) normal model.
Demonstrates the breakdown of lognormal Black-76 under negative interest rates
and verifies Bachelier put-call parity and analytical Greeks against finite differences.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

from scipy.stats import norm


def black76_swaption(
    fwd_swap: float,
    strike: float,
    annuity: float,
    texp: float,
    vol_black: float,
    is_payer: bool = True,
) -> float:
    """Price European swaption using Black-76 formula. Raises ValueError if fwd_swap or strike <= 0."""
    if fwd_swap <= 0.0 or strike <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError(f"Black-76 requires strictly positive rates. Got S={fwd_swap}, K={strike}")

    std_dev = vol_black * math.sqrt(texp)
    d1 = (math.log(fwd_swap / strike) + 0.5 * std_dev**2) / std_dev
    d2 = d1 - std_dev

    if is_payer:
        val = fwd_swap * float(norm.cdf(d1)) - strike * float(norm.cdf(d2))
    else:
        val = strike * float(norm.cdf(-d2)) - fwd_swap * float(norm.cdf(-d1))

    return annuity * val


def bachelier_swaption(
    fwd_swap: float,
    strike: float,
    annuity: float,
    texp: float,
    vol_normal: float,
    is_payer: bool = True,
) -> float:
    """Price European swaption using Bachelier normal model: dS = sigma_N * dW."""
    if texp <= 0.0 or vol_normal <= 0.0:
        intrinsic = max(fwd_swap - strike, 0.0) if is_payer else max(strike - fwd_swap, 0.0)
        return annuity * intrinsic

    std_dev = vol_normal * math.sqrt(texp)
    d = (fwd_swap - strike) / std_dev

    pdf_d = float(norm.pdf(d))
    cdf_d = float(norm.cdf(d))

    if is_payer:
        call_val = (fwd_swap - strike) * cdf_d + std_dev * pdf_d
        return annuity * call_val
    else:
        cdf_minus_d = float(norm.cdf(-d))
        put_val = (strike - fwd_swap) * cdf_minus_d + std_dev * pdf_d
        return annuity * put_val


def bachelier_greeks(
    fwd_swap: float,
    strike: float,
    annuity: float,
    texp: float,
    vol_normal: float,
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Analytical Greeks for Bachelier payer swaption."""
    std_dev = vol_normal * math.sqrt(texp)
    d = (fwd_swap - strike) / std_dev
    pdf_d = float(norm.pdf(d))
    cdf_d = float(norm.cdf(d))

    delta = annuity * cdf_d
    gamma = annuity * pdf_d / std_dev
    vega = annuity * math.sqrt(texp) * pdf_d  # dPrice / dSigma_N
    return {"delta": delta, "gamma": gamma, "vega": vega}


def run_lab() -> None:
    print("=== European Swaptions: Black-76 vs. Bachelier Normal Model ===")

    # Scenario 1: Standard positive rate environment
    # 1Y into 5Y swaption (texp = 1.0, tenor = 5.0)
    annuity_pos = 4.520  # 5-year swap annuity
    texp = 1.0
    fwd_swap_pos = 0.0400  # 4.00%
    strike_pos = 0.0400  # ATM
    vol_black = 0.20  # 20% lognormal vol
    # Normal vol ATM approximation: sigma_N approx S * sigma_B = 0.04 * 0.20 = 0.0080 (80 bps/year)
    vol_normal_pos = fwd_swap_pos * vol_black

    p_black = black76_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_black, is_payer=True)
    r_black = black76_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_black, is_payer=False)
    p_bach = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos, is_payer=True)
    r_bach = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos, is_payer=False)

    print("--- Positive Rates (ATM S = 4.00%, K = 4.00%, 1Yx5Y) ---")
    print(f"Black-76 Payer Swaption   : {p_black:.6f}")
    print(f"Bachelier Payer Swaption  : {p_bach:.6f} (Diff: {(p_bach - p_black) * 10000.0:.2f} bps of annuity)")
    print(f"Black-76 Put-Call Parity  : {(p_black - r_black) - annuity_pos * (fwd_swap_pos - strike_pos):.2e}")
    print(f"Bachelier Put-Call Parity : {(p_bach - r_bach) - annuity_pos * (fwd_swap_pos - strike_pos):.2e}")

    # Scenario 2: Negative rate environment (EUR/CHF market conditions, e.g., 2015-2021)
    fwd_swap_neg = -0.0035  # -35 bps
    strike_neg = -0.0020  # -20 bps (OTM payer)
    annuity_neg = 4.950
    vol_normal_neg = 0.0055  # 55 bps/year normal vol

    print("\n--- Negative Rates Environment (S = -0.35%, K = -0.20%) ---")
    try:
        black76_swaption(fwd_swap_neg, strike_neg, annuity_neg, texp, vol_black, is_payer=True)
    except ValueError as exc:
        print(f"Black-76 Execution Result : FAILED -> {exc}")

    p_bach_neg = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_neg, strike_neg, annuity_neg, texp, vol_normal_neg, is_payer=True)
    r_bach_neg = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_neg, strike_neg, annuity_neg, texp, vol_normal_neg, is_payer=False)
    parity_neg = (p_bach_neg - r_bach_neg) - annuity_neg * (fwd_swap_neg - strike_neg)

    print(f"Bachelier Payer Swaption  : {p_bach_neg:.6f}")
    print(f"Bachelier Receiver Swapt  : {r_bach_neg:.6f}")
    print(f"Bachelier Put-Call Parity : {parity_neg:.2e} (Exact Parity Maintained)")

    # Greeks and Finite Difference Verification
    print("\n--- Bachelier Greeks vs. Numerical Finite Differences ---")
    greeks = bachelier_greeks(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos)
    h_s = 1e-5
    p_up = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos + h_s, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos, is_payer=True)
    p_dn = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos - h_s, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos, is_payer=True)
    num_delta = (p_up - p_dn) / (2.0 * h_s)
    num_gamma = (p_up - 2.0 * p_bach + p_dn) / (h_s**2)

    h_v = 1e-5
    p_v_up = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos + h_v, is_payer=True)
    p_v_dn = bachelier_swaption(fwd_swap_pos, strike_pos, annuity_pos, texp, vol_normal_pos - h_v, is_payer=True)
    num_vega = (p_v_up - p_v_dn) / (2.0 * h_v)

    print(
        f"Delta (Analytic vs. Num)  : {greeks['delta']:.6f} vs. {num_delta:.6f} "
        f"(Diff: {abs(greeks['delta'] - num_delta):.2e})"
    )
    print(
        f"Gamma (Analytic vs. Num)  : {greeks['gamma']:.4f} vs. {num_gamma:.4f} "
        f"(Diff: {abs(greeks['gamma'] - num_gamma):.2e})"
    )
    print(
        f"Vega  (Analytic vs. Num)  : {greeks['vega']:.6f} vs. {num_vega:.6f} "
        f"(Diff: {abs(greeks['vega'] - num_vega):.2e})"
    )


run_lab()

4Where the Canon Breaks: Normal Tail Risk and the Flat Smile Fallacy

Although the Bachelier model elegantly accommodates negative interest rates, its underlying stochastic assumption—unbounded arithmetic Brownian motion—creates severe practical limitations:

  • Absence of Lower Bound: Under Bachelier dynamics \(dS = \sigma_N dW\), the forward swap rate can drift to \(-\infty\). In reality, physical currency storage costs impose an effective lower bound (typically around \(-100\) to \(-75\) bps) on nominal deposit rates.
  • Flat Smile Implication: A constant \(\sigma_N\) implies that implied normal volatility is completely flat across all strike rates. Real swaption markets exhibit pronounced skew (OTM payers trading at different volatilities than OTM receivers) and smile curvature.
  • Distributional Mismatch: As interest rates rise high above zero, market rate distributions behave more lognormally than normally, causing Bachelier with constant \(\sigma_N\) to misprice deep out-of-the-money options.

5Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: A payer swaption is a put option because paying fixed interest rate feels like paying a liability.

    Correction: A payer swaption is a call option on the swap rate. As market interest rates rise (\(S > K\)), the right to pay a lower fixed rate \(K\) becomes more valuable, generating payoff \((S - K)^+\). Conversely, a receiver swaption is a put option on the swap rate with payoff \((K - S)^+\).

  • Misconception: Physically settled swaptions and cash-settled swaptions have identical economic value.

    Correction: Physically settled swaptions deliver a live swap discounted along the collateralized OIS curve. Cash-settled swaptions settle using the standard ISDA par yield formula, which discounts cash flows using the swap rate itself rather than the multi-curve OIS discount curve, creating a systematic valuation discrepancy known as the cash-settlement basis.

  • Misconception: Bachelier normal volatility is quoted as a percentage of the interest rate (e.g. 20%).

    Correction: Normal volatility is an absolute rate change, quoted in basis points per year (e.g. 80 bp/year or 0.0080). Lognormal (Black) volatility is relative and quoted as a percentage (e.g. 20%). Conflating the two units leads to errors of two orders of magnitude.

6Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 4.1 (Bachelier ATM Swaption Valuation): An at-the-money 1-year into 5-year European payer swaption has forward swap rate \(S = 3.50\%\) and strike \(K = 3.50\%\). The 5-year swap annuity is \(A(0) = 4.40\), expiry is \(T_{\text{exp}} = 1.0\) year, and normal volatility is \(\sigma_N = 75\) bps/year (\(0.0075\)). Calculate the price of the swaption.

Solution

For an at-the-money swaption (\(S = K\)), standardized moneyness \(d = 0\). Thus \(\Phi(0) = 0.5\) and \(\phi(0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \approx 0.398942\).

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{payer}} &= A(0) \sigma_N \sqrt{T} \phi(0) \\ &= 4.40 \times 0.0075 \times 1.0 \times 0.398942 \\ &= 4.40 \times 0.00299207 = 0.013165 \quad (131.65 \text{ bps of notional}) \end{aligned} \]

On a \$100M notional contract, the dollar price is \$1,316,510.00.

Problem 4.2 (Bachelier Put-Call Parity): For the swaption in Problem 4.1, if the strike is set to \(K = 3.80\%\) (OTM payer) and the payer swaption is priced at \(85.20\) bps of notional, compute the price of the corresponding receiver swaption without re-evaluating normal integrals.

Solution

By swaption put-call parity: \(V_{\text{payer}} - V_{\text{receiver}} = A(0) (S - K)\). Compute the underlying forward swap value:

\[ \begin{aligned} A(0) (S - K) &= 4.40 \times (0.0350 - 0.0380) = 4.40 \times (-0.0030) \\ &= -0.013200 \quad (-132.00 \text{ bps}) \end{aligned} \]

Solving for the receiver swaption:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{receiver}} &= V_{\text{payer}} - A(0)(S - K) = 85.20 \text{ bps} - (-132.00 \text{ bps}) \\ &= 217.20 \text{ bps of notional} \end{aligned} \]

Problem 4.3 (Analytical Vega Calculation): Calculate the analytical Vega \(\frac{\partial V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial \sigma_N}\) for the ATM swaption in Problem 4.1, and verify the price change for a 1 bp/year increase in normal volatility.

Solution

Analytical Bachelier Vega is \(\text{Vega} = A(0) \sqrt{T} \phi(d)\). With \(d = 0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Vega} &= 4.40 \times 1.0 \times 0.398942 = 1.75535 \end{aligned} \]

For a 1 bp/year (\(0.0001\)) change in normal volatility, the price change is \(\Delta V = 1.75535 \times 0.0001 = 0.0001755\) or \(1.76\) bps of notional (\$17,554 per \$100M notional).

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 4.4 (Bachelier Payer Delta and Gamma Derivation): Derive the analytical expressions for Bachelier swaption Delta \(\frac{\partial V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial S}\) and Gamma \(\frac{\partial^2 V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial S^2}\), and prove that Gamma is strictly positive for all \(\sigma_N > 0\).

Solution

Let \(\omega = \sigma_N \sqrt{T}\). Differentiating \(V = A [ (S-K)\Phi(d) + \omega \phi(d) ]\) with \(d = \frac{S-K}{\omega}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} &= A \left[ \Phi(d) + (S-K)\phi(d)\frac{1}{\omega} + \omega \phi'(d)\frac{1}{\omega} \right] \end{aligned} \]

Using the identity \(\phi'(d) = -d\phi(d) = -\frac{S-K}{\omega}\phi(d)\), the last two terms cancel identically: \((S-K)\phi(d)/\omega - (S-K)\phi(d)/\omega = 0\). Thus \(\text{Delta} = A \Phi(d)\). Differentiating again with respect to \(S\): \(\text{Gamma} = A \phi(d)\frac{1}{\omega} = \frac{A \phi(d)}{\sigma_N \sqrt{T}}\). Because \(A > 0, \sigma_N > 0, \sqrt{T} > 0\), and \(\phi(d) > 0\), Gamma is strictly positive.

Problem 4.5 (Equivalence of Bachelier ATM Formula): Prove that the ATM Bachelier swaption formula \(V_{\text{ATM}} = A \sigma_N \sqrt{T} / \sqrt{2\pi}\) is the exact expectation of a folded half-normal distribution.

Solution

At \(S = K\), the payoff is \((S(T) - S_0)^+ = \max(\omega Z, 0)\) where \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) and \(\omega = \sigma_N \sqrt{T}\). The expectation is \(\mathbb{E}[\max(\omega Z, 0)] = \omega \int_0^\infty z \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2} dz\). Substituting \(u = z^2/2, du = z dz\), the integral evaluates to \(\int_0^\infty e^{-u} du = 1\). Thus \(\mathbb{E}[\max(\omega Z, 0)] = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2\pi}} = \frac{\sigma_N \sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\). Multiplying by numéraire \(A\) yields the exact ATM Bachelier formula.

Problem 4.6 (Choi-Fabozzi-de Wet Expansion): Prove the leading-order relationship between Black volatility \(\sigma_B\) and normal volatility \(\sigma_N\) by expanding \(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_B \sqrt{T} \right)\) in a Taylor series about zero.

Solution

In the Black-76 ATM formula, \(d_1 = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_B \sqrt{T}\) and \(d_2 = -d_1\). The ATM price is \(V_B = A S_0 [2\Phi(x) - 1]\) where \(x = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_B \sqrt{T}\). The Taylor series for the standard normal CDF is \(\Phi(x) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left( x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \mathcal{O}(x^5) \right)\). Substituting this into \(V_B\):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_B &= A S_0 \left[ \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\frac{\sigma_B \sqrt{T}}{2} - \frac{\sigma_B^3 T^{3/2}}{48} \right) \right] = \frac{A S_0 \sigma_B \sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left( 1 - \frac{\sigma_B^2 T}{24} \right) \end{aligned} \]

Equating this to \(V_{\text{Bach}} = \frac{A \sigma_N \sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\) directly implies \(\sigma_N = S_0 \sigma_B \left(1 - \frac{1}{24}\sigma_B^2 T \right)\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 4.7 (The 2015 Swiss Negative Rate Shock): On January 15, 2015, the Swiss National Bank abandoned its 1.20 EUR/CHF peg and lowered deposit rates to \(-0.75\%\). Swiss swap rates dropped to \(-0.50\%\). Document the operational disruption that occurred in Swiss swaption trading desks and how dealers adopted Bachelier quoting.

Solution

Automated trading systems running Black-76 crashed due to negative rate domain errors (\(\ln(S/K)\)). Interdealer quote screens went blank, and electronic execution venues halted swaption trading. Dealers held emergency working groups and unanimously transitioned from Black-76 lognormal quotation to Bachelier normal volatility (quoted in bp/year). Within three weeks, the entire Swiss franc and euro swaption markets operated exclusively in Bachelier normal volatility.

Problem 4.8 (Mortgage Refinancing Convexity Hedging): US mortgage servicers hold massive portfolios of residential mortgages with prepayment call options. When interest rates drop, prepayments accelerate, shortening asset duration. Explain how mortgage desks utilize payer and receiver swaptions to manage negative convexity.

Solution

As rates decline, homeowners refinance, forcing mortgage servicers to reinvest cash at lower yields (negative convexity). To hedge against rate drops, servicers purchase receiver swaptions (which gain value when rates fall). Conversely, if rates rise, prepayment slows and asset duration extends; servicers purchase payer swaptions to pay fixed and hedge extension risk. The swaption market serves as the primary macro liquidity provider for aggregate US mortgage convexity hedging.

Problem 4.9 (Cash Settlement Basis in European Swaptions): European swaptions settled in cash use the standard ISDA formula discounting cash flows at the swap rate \(S(T_{\text{exp}})\). In contrast, physically settled swaptions deliver a swap discounted at collateralized OIS rates. Analyze the sign and economic magnitude of the physical-to-cash settlement basis.

Solution

Under upward-sloping yield curves, the single-rate annuity \(A_{\text{par}}(S) = \frac{1 - (1+S)^{-n}}{S}\) uses a higher discount rate than multi-curve collateralized discount factors \(P^D(0, T_k)\). Consequently, cash-settled swaptions deliver a lower payoff than physically settled swaptions. Dealers quote physical swaptions at a systematic premium over cash-settled swaptions, with the cash-settlement basis reaching 2 to 6 bps of notional depending on tenor and curve steepness.

Problem 4.10 (Shifted Lognormal vs. Bachelier Normal Fit): Compare the empirical performance of a shifted lognormal Black model (with calibrated shift parameter \(s\)) versus the pure Bachelier model across deep out-of-the-money swaptions during the 2016-2020 negative rate era.

Solution

A shifted Black model preserves proportional volatility dynamics above \(-s\), fitting positive interest rate skews more naturally than Bachelier. However, the shift parameter \(s\) is an arbitrary tuning parameter without structural economic meaning; choosing \(s\) too small causes volatility explosion near \(-s\), while choosing \(s\) too large distorts moneyness. Bachelier provides parameter-free robustness across all strikes and clean analytical Greeks, making it the industry standard despite minor tail fit imperfections.

Problem 4.11 (Swaption Pricing under Multi-Curve Annuities): Formulate the exact drift adjustment required when the collateral discount curve \(P^D\) and the floating projection curve \(P^F\) are driven by distinct stochastic factors under a two-factor HJM interest-rate model.

Solution

Under a multi-factor HJM framework, the floating swap leg involves forward rate expectations under \(\mathbb{Q}^A\) that depend on the instantaneous covariation between the forward rate process \(dF(t)\) and the annuity bond portfolio \(dA(t)\). Specifically, \(dS(t) = \sigma_S(t) dW_t^A\) requires an explicit forward-discount correlation drift correction \(\int_t^T \text{Cov}(d\ln P^D(t, u), dF(t)) du\) if the multi-curve basis is stochastic, generating an endogenous basis convexity adjustment.

Problem 4.12 (Bachelier Implied Volatility Asymptotics): Derive the leading-order asymptotic behavior of Bachelier implied normal volatility \(\sigma_N(K)\) as strike \(K \to \infty\) when the true underlying distribution of forward swap rates is lognormal.

Solution

Under a true lognormal distribution, the upper tail decays as \(e^{-(\ln x)^2 / (2\sigma_B^2 T)}\), which is much fatter than Gaussian decay \(e^{-x^2 / (2\sigma_N^2 T)}\). To match the slower lognormal tail decay using a Gaussian density, the Bachelier implied normal volatility must increase with strike: \(\sigma_N(K) \approx K \sigma_B / \sqrt{2\ln(K/S_0)}\) as \(K \to \infty\). This proves that fitting Gaussian models to lognormal reality produces a steep upward-sloping normal volatility smile for high strikes.

8Retrieval Practice

  1. What option right does a payer swaption grant?

    Options

    A payer swaption gives the holder the right to pay the fixed strike rate K and receive the floating benchmark rate.

  2. What is the appropriate numéraire asset for pricing European swaptions?

    Options

    The swap annuity A(t) = sum tau_k P^D(t, T_k) serves as numéraire, making the forward swap rate a driftless martingale.

  3. What diffusion process governs the swap rate in the Bachelier model?

    Options

    Bachelier specifies arithmetic Brownian motion with constant absolute volatility, admitting negative rates.

  4. In what units is Bachelier normal volatility quoted in interest rate markets?

    Options

    Normal volatility reflects absolute rate changes and is quoted in basis points per year (e.g. 75 bp/y = 0.0075).

  5. What is the at-the-money approximation relating normal volatility sigma_N to Black volatility sigma_B?

    Options

    To first order, equating ATM options yields sigma_N = S_0 * sigma_B.

  6. What is the analytical Bachelier Delta for a payer swaption?

    Options

    Differentiating with respect to S yields Delta = A(t) * Phi(d), where terms involving phi(d) cancel identically.

  7. How does Bachelier Gamma depend on the volatility parameter sigma_N?

    Options

    Gamma is given by A * phi(d) / (sigma_N * sqrt(T)), which is inversely proportional to normal volatility.

  8. Why do physically settled and cash-settled European swaptions differ in price?

    Options

    The standard ISDA cash-settlement formula uses single-rate par yield discounting, creating a systematic valuation wedge against multi-curve OIS discounting.

  9. What theoretical flaw does the Bachelier model have regarding negative interest rates?

    Options

    Arithmetic Brownian motion is unbounded below, assigning non-zero probability to arbitrarily large negative rates.

  10. Why do mortgage servicers purchase swaptions?

    Options

    Mortgage prepayment options shorten duration when rates fall and extend duration when rates rise, requiring swaption convexity hedges.
