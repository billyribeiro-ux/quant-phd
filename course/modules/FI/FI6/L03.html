Caps, Floors, and the Black-76 Model

Interest-rate caps and floors are the foundational option contracts of fixed income markets, providing asymmetric insurance against floating rate spikes or declines for debt issuers and lenders. Fischer Black's 1976 model established the standard benchmark for pricing European options on forward contracts by casting forward rates as martingales under forward measure numéraires. Stripping caplet volatilities from quoted flat cap surfaces is the core prerequisite for calibrating term-structure and stochastic volatility models across trading desks.

1Interest-Rate Caps, Floors, and Cap-Floor Parity

A borrower issuing floating-rate debt faces cash flow uncertainty from rising interest rates. To hedge this risk, the borrower purchases an interest rate cap, which provides a series of European call options on successive floating forward rates.

Definition 3.1 (Interest-Rate Caps, Floors, and Caplets)

An interest-rate caps and floors contract is a strip of European call or put options (caplets or floorlets) on successive floating forward interest rates. For a tenor structure \(T_0 < T_1 < \dots < T_M\) with accrual fractions \(\tau_k\), the payoff of the \(k\)-th caplet at payment date \(T_k\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{Cpl}_k(T_k) &= N \tau_k \max\left( F(T_{k-1}; T_{k-1}, T_k) - K, \, 0 \right) \end{aligned} \]

and the payoff of the corresponding floorlet is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{Flt}_k(T_k) &= N \tau_k \max\left( K - F(T_{k-1}; T_{k-1}, T_k), \, 0 \right) \end{aligned} \]

where \(N\) is the contract notional, \(K\) is the strike rate, and \(F(T_{k-1}; T_{k-1}, T_k)\) is the floating rate reset at \(T_{k-1}\).

Theorem 3.1 (Cap-Floor-Swap Parity)

Under multi-curve discounting with collateralized discount factor \(P^D(t, T_k)\), a portfolio long a cap and short an otherwise identical floor replicates a payer interest-rate swap with fixed rate \(K\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Cap}(t; K) - \text{Floor}(t; K) &= \text{Swap}_{\text{payer}}(t; K) \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^M P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k [F_k(t) - K] \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Consider the cash flow of caplet \(k\) minus floorlet \(k\) at payment date \(T_k\). By elementary algebra, \(\max(F_k - K, 0) - \max(K - F_k, 0) = F_k - K\). Discounting to time \(t\) under the risk-neutral collateralized measure yields:

\[ P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}}[F_k(T_{k-1}) - K] = P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k [F_k(t) - K] \]

which is identically the present value of the forward rate agreement (FRA). Summing across all periods \(k = 1, \dots, M\) produces the payer swap valuation formula.

2The Black-76 Forward Lognormal Model

In 1976, Fischer Black adapted the Black-Scholes equity option framework to forward and futures contracts . In interest rate markets, Black's formulation is applied by choosing the zero-coupon bond maturing at payment date \(T_k\) as the numéraire.

Definition 3.2 (The Black-76 Model)

The Black-76 model assumes that under the \(T_k\)-forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\), the forward interest rate \(F_k(t) \equiv F(t; T_{k-1}, T_k)\) follows a driftless geometric Brownian motion:

\[ \begin{aligned} dF_k(t) &= \sigma_k F_k(t) \, dW_t^{T_k} \end{aligned} \]

where \(\sigma_k > 0\) is the constant lognormal (relative) volatility parameter and \(W_t^{T_k}\) is a standard Brownian motion under \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\) .

Theorem 3.2 (Black-76 Caplet and Floorlet Pricing Formulas)

The present value at time \(t < T_{k-1}\) of a European caplet with strike \(K\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{Cpl}_k(t) &= P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \left[ F_k(t) \Phi(d_1) - K \Phi(d_2) \right] \end{aligned} \]

and the floorlet value is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{Flt}_k(t) &= P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \left[ K \Phi(-d_2) - F_k(t) \Phi(-d_1) \right] \end{aligned} \]

where \(\Phi(\cdot)\) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function, and:

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1 &= \frac{\ln(F_k(t) / K) + \frac{1}{2} \sigma_k^2 (T_{k-1} - t)}{\sigma_k \sqrt{T_{k-1} - t}} \\ d_2 &= d_1 - \sigma_k \sqrt{T_{k-1} - t} \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Under the forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\), the present value of the payoff is \(\mathbf{Cpl}_k(t) = P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}}[(F_k(T_{k-1}) - K)^+]\). Since \(F_k(t)\) is a geometric martingale under \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\), \(\ln F_k(T_{k-1})\) is normally distributed with conditional variance \(\sigma_k^2(T_{k-1}-t)\). Evaluating the standard lognormal expectation \(\int_{\ln K}^\infty (e^x - K) f(x) dx\) directly yields the Black formula terms \(F_k(t)\Phi(d_1) - K\Phi(d_2)\).

3Caplet Volatility Stripping

In OTC derivatives markets, brokers quote flat cap volatilities \(\Sigma_M\) for standard maturities \(M \in \{1\text{Y}, 2\text{Y}, 3\text{Y}, 5\text{Y}, 10\text{Y}\}\). A flat cap volatility is a single model parameter inserted into all caplets of the cap to match total market price:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Cap}^{\text{mkt}}(t; T_M) &= \sum_{k=1}^M \mathbf{Cpl}_k(t; \Sigma_M) \end{aligned} \]

Because different caplets reset at different future dates \(T_{k-1}\), the forward caplet volatilities \(\sigma_k\) are not constant. To construct a consistent term structure of volatility, quants strip spot caplet volatilities using an iterative bootstrapping procedure:

  1. For the first caplet (\(M=1\)), the stripped volatility equals the quoted flat volatility: \(\sigma_1 = \Sigma_1\).
  2. For subsequent maturities \(M > 1\), the previous \(M-1\) caplets are priced using their previously stripped volatilities \(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{M-1}\).
  3. The remaining residual market value: \[ \Delta \mathbf{Cpl}_M = \text{Cap}^{\text{mkt}}(t; T_M) - \sum_{k=1}^{M-1} \mathbf{Cpl}_k(t; \sigma_k) \] is matched to the final caplet to solve for \(\sigma_M\) via numerical root-finding (e.g. bisection or Brent's method).

Lab 3.1 · Black-76 Caplet Pricing, Parity & Volatility Stripping

Hypothesis: Black-76 caplet and floorlet prices satisfy exact put-call parity against floating rate agreements, and iterative volatility stripping recovers a term structure of forward caplet volatilities that exactly reprices quoted market flat caps.

"""Lab FI6-L03-1: Black-76 Caplet Pricing, Stripping, and Parity.

Evaluates Black-76 caplets and floorlets under multi-curve discounting,
verifies exact put-call parity against floating rate agreements,
and implements a caplet volatility stripping algorithm from flat cap quotes.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

from scipy.stats import norm


def black76_caplet(
    fwd_rate: float,
    strike: float,
    discount_factor: float,
    tau: float,
    texp: float,
    vol: float,
) -> float:
    """Price a European caplet using Black-76 formula under the T-forward measure."""
    if texp <= 0.0 or vol <= 0.0:
        return discount_factor * tau * max(fwd_rate - strike, 0.0)

    std_dev = vol * math.sqrt(texp)
    d1 = (math.log(fwd_rate / strike) + 0.5 * std_dev**2) / std_dev
    d2 = d1 - std_dev

    call_price = fwd_rate * float(norm.cdf(d1)) - strike * float(norm.cdf(d2))
    return discount_factor * tau * call_price


def black76_floorlet(
    fwd_rate: float,
    strike: float,
    discount_factor: float,
    tau: float,
    texp: float,
    vol: float,
) -> float:
    """Price a European floorlet using Black-76 formula under the T-forward measure."""
    if texp <= 0.0 or vol <= 0.0:
        return discount_factor * tau * max(strike - fwd_rate, 0.0)

    std_dev = vol * math.sqrt(texp)
    d1 = (math.log(fwd_rate / strike) + 0.5 * std_dev**2) / std_dev
    d2 = d1 - std_dev

    put_price = strike * float(norm.cdf(-d2)) - fwd_rate * float(norm.cdf(-d1))
    return discount_factor * tau * put_price


def solve_caplet_vol(
    target_caplet_pv: float,
    fwd_rate: float,
    strike: float,
    df: float,
    tau: float,
    texp: float,
) -> float:
    """Solve for implied caplet volatility using bisection root-finding."""
    low, high = 1e-4, 3.0
    for _ in range(60):
        mid = 0.5 * (low + high)
        pv = black76_caplet(fwd_rate, strike, df, tau, texp, mid)
        if pv < target_caplet_pv:
            low = mid
        else:
            high = mid
    return 0.5 * (low + high)


def run_lab() -> None:
    print("=== Black-76 Caplet Pricing & Parity Validation ===")

    # Setup: 2-year cap on semi-annual forward rates (reset at 0.5, 1.0, 1.5; pay at 1.0, 1.5, 2.0)
    strike = 0.0400  # 4.00% strike
    tau = 0.5
    resets = [0.5, 1.0, 1.5]
    payments = [1.0, 1.5, 2.0]
    fwd_rates = [0.0380, 0.0410, 0.0435]
    # OIS discount factors to payment dates
    dfs = [0.9610, 0.9410, 0.9205]

    # Test single caplet parity on caplet 2 (texp = 1.0, pay = 1.5)
    fwd2 = fwd_rates[1]
    df2 = dfs[1]
    texp2 = resets[1]
    test_vol = 0.22  # 22% lognormal volatility

    caplet_val = black76_caplet(fwd2, strike, df2, tau, texp2, test_vol)
    floorlet_val = black76_floorlet(fwd2, strike, df2, tau, texp2, test_vol)
    fra_pv = df2 * tau * (fwd2 - strike)
    parity_diff = (caplet_val - floorlet_val) - fra_pv

    print(f"Caplet 2 PV (Black-76)   : {caplet_val:.6f}")
    print(f"Floorlet 2 PV (Black-76) : {floorlet_val:.6f}")
    print(f"FRA Underlying PV        : {fra_pv:.6f}")
    print(f"Caplet - Floorlet - FRA  : {parity_diff:.2e} (Exact Parity Verified)")

    print("\n--- Caplet Stripping from Quoted Flat Cap Volatilities ---")
    # Quoted flat cap volatilities across market maturities:
    # 1Y Cap (1 caplet at 0.5y reset): flat vol = 24.0%
    # 1.5Y Cap (2 caplets at 0.5y, 1.0y): flat vol = 22.5%
    # 2Y Cap (3 caplets at 0.5y, 1.0y, 1.5y): flat vol = 21.5%
    cap_maturities = [1.0, 1.5, 2.0]
    flat_vols = [0.240, 0.225, 0.215]

    # Compute quoted flat cap market PVs:
    # Cap(T_M) = sum_{k=1}^M Caplet(F_k, K, DF_k, tau, texp_k, flat_vol_M)
    cap_market_pvs = []
    for m_idx, _mat in enumerate(cap_maturities):
        n_caplets = m_idx + 1
        pv = sum(
            black76_caplet(fwd_rates[i], strike, dfs[i], tau, resets[i], flat_vols[m_idx]) for i in range(n_caplets)
        )
        cap_market_pvs.append(pv)

    # Strip forward caplet volatilities iteratively:
    # Caplet 1 PV is exactly Cap 1Y PV
    # Caplet 2 PV = Cap 1.5Y PV - Caplet 1 PV(using stripped vol 1)
    # Caplet 3 PV = Cap 2.0Y PV - Caplet 1 PV - Caplet 2 PV
    stripped_caplet_vols: list[float] = []
    accum_caplets_pv = 0.0

    print(
        f"{'Caplet':<8} {'Reset(y)':<10} {'Pay(y)':<8} {'Fwd Rate':<10} "
        f"{'Flat Vol':<10} {'Stripped Vol':<14} {'Caplet PV':<12}"
    )
    for k in range(len(resets)):
        target_cap_pv = cap_market_pvs[k]
        target_caplet_pv = target_cap_pv - accum_caplets_pv
        c_vol = solve_caplet_vol(target_caplet_pv, fwd_rates[k], strike, dfs[k], tau, resets[k])
        stripped_caplet_vols.append(c_vol)
        # Verify caplet PV with stripped vol
        c_pv = black76_caplet(fwd_rates[k], strike, dfs[k], tau, resets[k], c_vol)
        accum_caplets_pv += c_pv
        row_str = (
            f"{k + 1:<8} {resets[k]:<10.1f} {payments[k]:<8.1f} {fwd_rates[k] * 100.0:<9.2f}% "
            f"{flat_vols[k] * 100.0:<9.1f}% {c_vol * 100.0:<13.2f}% {c_pv:<12.6f}"
        )
        print(row_str)

    print(f"\nFinal Cumulative Cap PV  : {accum_caplets_pv:.6f}")
    print(f"Target 2Y Flat Cap PV    : {cap_market_pvs[-1]:.6f}")
    print(f"Stripping Residual Error : {abs(accum_caplets_pv - cap_market_pvs[-1]):.2e}")

4Where the Canon Breaks: Negative Rates and the Log-Singularity

The foundational assumption of the Black-76 model is that forward interest rates follow a lognormal diffusion \(dF_k(t) = \sigma_k F_k(t) dW_t\). Under lognormality, rates are strictly positive: \(F_k(t) > 0\) for all \(t\) with probability 1.

Following the 2014-2015 introduction of negative deposit facility rates by the European Central Bank and the Swiss National Bank , benchmark market forward rates across Europe and Japan plummeted below zero (e.g. -50 bps on EURIBOR). At negative rates, the Black-76 model suffers fatal breakdown:

  • Undefined Moneyness: The logarithmic term \(\ln(F_k / K)\) in \(d_1\) becomes mathematically undefined when \(F_k \le 0\) or \(K \le 0\).
  • Implied Volatility Explosion: As rates approach zero from above, lognormal percentage volatility \(\sigma_{\text{log}} \approx \frac{\sigma_{\text{abs}}}{F}\) diverges to infinity, destroying all numerical calibration stability.
  • Absence of Zero-Bound Floor: Real interest rates can be negative when central banks charge negative rates on excess reserves, invalidating the geometric Brownian motion assumption.

5Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: A cap is priced as a single European option on the average swap rate.

    Correction: A cap is a strip of independent European call options (caplets) on individual forward rates maturing at different dates. An option on a swap rate is a swaption, which has fundamentally different convexity, exercise mechanics, and correlation sensitivity.

  • Misconception: Flat cap volatilities quoted by interdealer brokers represent the actual volatility of forward rates resetting at that maturity.

    Correction: Flat cap volatility is a weighted blended average volatility across all caplets contained in the contract. The marginal forward volatility of the final caplet (the stripped caplet volatility) often differs substantially from the quoted flat cap figure, especially when the volatility term structure is steep.

  • Misconception: In Black-76 caplet pricing, cash flows are discounted using the forward rate itself.

    Correction: Under the modern multi-curve framework, the forward rate \(F_k\) determines only the projected payoff expectation under \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\). Discounting to present value is performed strictly using the collateralized OIS discount factor \(P^D(t, T_k)\).

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 3.1 (Black-76 Caplet Calculation): A 6-month caplet resets at \(T_1 = 1.0\) year and pays at \(T_2 = 1.5\) years (\(\tau = 0.5\)). The forward rate is \(F = 4.50\%\), strike is \(K = 4.00\%\), Black lognormal volatility is \(\sigma = 20\%\), and the OIS discount factor is \(P^D(0, 1.5) = 0.9400\). Notional is \$10,000,000. Calculate the dollar price of the caplet.

Solution

Step 1: Compute \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) with \(texp = 1.0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sigma \sqrt{T} &= 0.20 \times 1.0 = 0.20 \\ \ln(F / K) &= \ln(0.0450 / 0.0400) = \ln(1.125) = 0.117783 \\ d_1 &= \frac{0.117783 + 0.5 \times (0.20)^2}{0.20} = \frac{0.137783}{0.20} = 0.6889 \\ d_2 &= 0.6889 - 0.20 = 0.4889 \end{aligned} \]

Step 2: Normal CDFs: \(\Phi(0.6889) \approx 0.7545\), \(\Phi(0.4889) \approx 0.6875\).

Step 3: Call price in rate units:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Call} &= 0.0450 \times 0.7545 - 0.0400 \times 0.6875 \\ &= 0.033953 - 0.027500 = 0.006453 \end{aligned} \]

Step 4: Discounted dollar value:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{PV} &= 10{,}000{,}000 \times 0.9400 \times 0.5 \times 0.006453 = \$30{,}329.10 \end{aligned} \]

Problem 3.2 (Floorlet Parity Application): For the exact caplet in Problem 3.1, determine the market value of the corresponding floorlet with strike \(K = 4.00\%\) using cap-floor-swap parity without recomputing normal integrals.

Solution

By cap-floor parity, \(\text{Cpl} - \text{Flt} = P^D(0, T_2) \tau (F - K)\). Compute the forward rate agreement value:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{FRA} &= 10{,}000{,}000 \times 0.9400 \times 0.5 \times (0.0450 - 0.0400) \\ &= 4{,}700{,}000 \times 0.0050 = \$23{,}500.00 \end{aligned} \]

Solving for the floorlet value:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Flt} &= \text{Cpl} - \text{FRA} = \$30{,}329.10 - \$23{,}500.00 = \$6{,}829.10 \end{aligned} \]

Problem 3.3 (Stripping Marginal Caplet Volatility): A 1-year cap composed of a single caplet has market value \$50,000 at flat vol \(20.0\%\). A 2-year cap covering that first caplet plus two additional identical caplets has total market price \$135,000. If the second and third caplets have identical sensitivity and forward rate profiles, estimate the stripped forward volatility required for the remaining caplets.

Solution

The total market price of the 2-year cap is \$135,000. Subtracting the market value of the first caplet (\$50,000) leaves \$85,000 of target value for the second and third caplets combined (\$42,500 each). Because \$42,500 is less than the first caplet's \$50,000 despite longer maturity and equal strike moneyness, the implied forward caplet volatility must be lower than the original 20.0% flat volatility, falling to approximately 17.5%.

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 3.4 (Forward Measure Martingale Property): Prove that under the forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{T}\) with numéraire \(P(t, T)\), the forward rate \(F(t; T_1, T)\) spanning \([T_1, T]\) is a true martingale.

Solution

By definition of the forward rate, \(1 + \tau F(t; T_1, T) = \frac{P(t, T_1)}{P(t, T)}\). Dividing by numéraire \(P(t, T)\), the relative price of the zero-coupon bond \(P(t, T_1)\) is \(\frac{P(t, T_1)}{P(t, T)} = 1 + \tau F(t; T_1, T)\). By the fundamental theorem of asset pricing, any tradable asset deflated by the numéraire is a martingale under \(\mathbb{Q}^T\). Since \(\frac{P(t, T_1)}{P(t, T)}\) is a martingale, subtracting 1 and dividing by deterministic constant \(\tau\) preserves the martingale property. Hence \(\mathbb{E}_s^{\mathbb{Q}^T}[F(t; T_1, T)] = F(s; T_1, T)\) for all \(s < t\).

Problem 3.5 (Black-76 Caplet Vega Formula): Derive the analytical formula for caplet Vega \(\frac{\partial \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial \sigma}\) and prove it is strictly non-negative.

Solution

Differentiating the Black-76 caplet formula with respect to \(\sigma\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial \sigma} &= P^D \tau \left[ F \phi(d_1) \frac{\partial d_1}{\partial \sigma} - K \phi(d_2) \frac{\partial d_2}{\partial \sigma} \right] \end{aligned} \]

Using the identity \(F \phi(d_1) = K \phi(d_2)\) and noting \(\frac{\partial d_1}{\partial \sigma} - \frac{\partial d_2}{\partial \sigma} = \sqrt{T}\), we obtain \(\text{Vega} = P^D(t, T) \tau F \phi(d_1) \sqrt{T - t}\). Because \(P^D > 0, \tau > 0, F > 0, \sqrt{T-t} > 0\), and the standard normal density \(\phi(d_1) > 0\), caplet Vega is strictly positive for all \(\sigma > 0\).

Problem 3.6 (Asymptotic Moneyness Limits): Prove that as strike \(K \to 0\), the Black-76 caplet price converges to the discounted present value of the forward rate, and as \(K \to \infty\), the price converges monotonically to zero.

Solution

As \(K \to 0\), \(\ln(F / K) \to +\infty\), so \(d_1, d_2 \to +\infty\). Thus \(\Phi(d_1) \to 1\) and \(\Phi(d_2) \to 1\). The term \(K \Phi(d_2) \to 0 \times 1 = 0\). Hence \(\mathbf{Cpl} \to P^D \tau F\), which is the full forward payment. Conversely, as \(K \to \infty\), \(d_1, d_2 \to -\infty\), so \(\Phi(d_1), \Phi(d_2) \to 0\). Using L'Hôpital's rule on \(K \Phi(d_2)\), the entire bracket vanishes, proving \(\lim_{K \to \infty} \mathbf{Cpl} = 0\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 3.7 (Downward Sloping Volatility Term Structure): Historical interest-rate cap quotes show flat volatilities of \(32\%\) for 1-year caps, \(26\%\) for 2-year caps, and \(22\%\) for 5-year caps. Explain the macroeconomic and monetary policy causes behind downward-sloping cap volatility curves.

Solution

Downward-sloping cap volatility curves occur when the market perceives high near-term monetary policy uncertainty (e.g. during an aggressive Federal Reserve hiking or cutting campaign). Over longer horizons, central bank interest rate rules mean-revert toward long-term neutral rates, dampening forward rate volatility. Additionally, institutional pension and insurance demand for long-duration fixed-rate cash flows compresses long-dated swaption and cap implied volatilities.

Problem 3.8 (Negative Rate Breakdowns in Euro Markets): In 2016, 6-month EURIBOR forward rates fell to \(-0.25\%\). A European bank holding a legacy cap portfolio tried to calculate Black-76 implied volatilities. Detail the exact numerical error produced by analytics libraries and identify the market-standard patch implemented by dealers.

Solution

Standard analytics libraries evaluating \(\ln(F / K)\) returned a floating-point domain error (NaN) when \(F = -0.0025 < 0\) or for zero-strike caps. To preserve Black-76 infrastructure without complete system rewrites, dealers introduced the Shifted Black-76 model: rates are translated by a constant shift \(s > 0\) (typically \(s = 100\) to \(200\) bps) so that \(F + s > 0\) and \(K + s > 0\), allowing standard lognormal routines to process shifted inputs.

Problem 3.9 (Caplet Smile Asymmetry): In US cap markets, out-of-the-money caps (\(K > F\)) trade at significantly higher implied volatilities than at-the-money caps. Explain why rate caps exhibit positive volatility skew whereas equity options exhibit negative skew.

Solution

Equity options exhibit negative skew because stock price drops correlate with heightened volatility (the leverage effect and panic selling). In contrast, interest rate caps exhibit positive skew because interest rates spike during sudden inflationary shocks or aggressive central bank tightening cycles. Corporate borrowers rush to purchase OTM caps for debt protection during rate surges, bidding up the right tail of the implied volatility distribution.

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 3.10 (Shifted Lognormal vs Normal Bachelier): Contrast the shifted Black-76 model with the Bachelier normal model in their mathematical treatment of negative interest rates. Specifically evaluate how each model handles the probability of rates reaching arbitrarily negative values.

Solution

The shifted Black model enforces a strict hard floor at \(-s\): rates can never fall below \(-s\) because \(F(t) + s > 0\) almost surely. In contrast, the Bachelier normal model assumes Gaussian diffusion \(dF = \sigma_N dW\), which assigns positive probability to all real numbers on \((-\infty, \infty)\). While Bachelier requires no arbitrary shift parameter \(s\), it permits interest rates to drift to \(-\infty\), whereas shifted Black imposes a rigid lower bound that may distort deep out-of-the-money floorlet pricing.

Problem 3.11 (Breeden-Litzenberger Risk-Neutral Density Extraction): Formulate the continuous strike second derivative \(\frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial K^2}\) for a stripped caplet and show how it extracts the risk-neutral probability density of the forward rate \(F(T_{k-1})\).

Solution

By the Breeden-Litzenberger theorem, the present value of a call option is:

\[ \mathbf{Cpl}(K) = P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \int_K^\infty (f - K) q(f) \, df \]

where \(q(f)\) is the risk-neutral density under \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\). Differentiating once with respect to \(K\) yields:

\[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial K} = -P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k \int_K^\infty q(f) \, df \]

Differentiating a second time yields \(\frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial K^2} = P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k q(K)\). Thus, the risk-neutral density is recovered directly:

\[ q(K) = \frac{1}{P^D(t, T_k) \tau_k} \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{Cpl}}{\partial K^2} \]

Problem 3.12 (Caplet Stripping Ill-Posedness and Regularization): Explain why caplet stripping from a finite set of market cap maturities is an ill-posed inverse problem, and describe how Tikhonov smoothness regularization resolves instability in stripped forward volatility curves.

Solution

Quoted cap maturities are sparse (e.g. 1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y), leaving intermediate semi-annual caplets unobserved. Fitting piece-wise constant volatilities produces saw-tooth oscillations and negative forward variances when small bid-ask quote perturbations occur. Tikhonov regularization resolves this ill-posedness by minimizing a penalized objective function:

\[ \min_{\sigma} \sum_M (\text{Cap}_M^{\text{model}} - \text{Cap}_M^{\text{mkt}})^2 + \lambda \int (\sigma''(t))^2 \, dt \]

balancing market re-pricing fidelity with curve smoothness.

8Retrieval Practice

  1. What does an interest rate caplet represent?

    Options

    A caplet is an individual European call option on a forward interest rate resetting at the period start and paying at period end.

  2. What portfolio replicates a payer interest-rate swap?

    Options

    By cap-floor-swap parity, max(F-K, 0) - max(K-F, 0) = F-K, exactly matching the cash flows of a payer swap.

  3. Under which probability measure is the forward rate F(t; T_{k-1}, T_k) a martingale in Black-76?

    Options

    Using the zero-coupon bond maturing at payment date T_k as numéraire establishes the forward rate as a driftless martingale.

  4. What is the difference between a quoted flat cap volatility and a stripped caplet volatility?

    Options

    Flat volatility is a blended quote for the whole cap package, whereas stripped volatility is the marginal volatility of an individual caplet.

  5. Why does the Black-76 model fail when interest rates become negative?

    Options

    The natural logarithm requires strictly positive arguments; negative forward rates or strikes produce domain errors.

  6. How does caplet Vega depend on the forward rate under Black-76?

    Options

    Differentiating Black-76 yields Vega = P^D * tau * F * phi(d_1) * sqrt(T), directly scaling with the forward rate level.

  7. In modern multi-curve pricing, what discount factors are applied to caplet payoffs?

    Options

    All collateralized derivative cash flows must be discounted along the OIS discount curve under CSA agreements.

  8. What practical shift technique was used in European markets to price negative-rate caplets under Black-76?

    Options

    Shifted Black translates both forward rate and strike by a constant s, ensuring positive inputs for the logarithm.

  9. What does the second derivative of caplet prices with respect to strike K yield?

    Options

    By the Breeden-Litzenberger result, d^2 Cpl / dK^2 recovers the state price density and risk-neutral distribution of the forward rate.

  10. Why are caplet stripping algorithms susceptible to numerical instability without regularization?

    Options

    Small pricing discrepancies in cumulative flat caps compound into violent swings in stripped forward volatilities across discrete buckets.
