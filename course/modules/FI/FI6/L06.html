The Swaption Volatility Cube & Surface Engineering

In equity options, the implied volatility surface spans two dimensions: option strike and time to expiry. In interest-rate swaptions, an option specifies not only when it expires, but also the tenor of the underlying swap delivered upon exercise. This introduces a third independent financial dimension, transforming the volatility surface into a three-dimensional swaption volatility cube. Building a robust swaption cube requires calibrating slices with SABR models, interpolating parameters across off-grid expiries and tenors, and enforcing static no-arbitrage conditions.

1Geometry & Coordinate Architecture of the Cube

A swaption quote depends upon three independent coordinates: option maturity, underlying swap duration, and strike price.

Definition 6.1 (The Swaption Volatility Cube)

The swaption volatility cube is the continuous three-dimensional manifold of implied volatilities parameterizing European swaptions across three coordinates:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sigma(T_{\text{exp}}, T_{\text{tenor}}, K) \end{aligned} \]

where \(T_{\text{exp}}\) is the option expiration date, \(T_{\text{tenor}}\) is the length of the underlying interest-rate swap, and \(K\) is the swap strike rate (or relative moneyness \(K - S\) in basis points) .

In market practice, broker data is structured in two layers:

  1. The ATM Swaption Matrix: A two-dimensional grid of at-the-money volatilities spanning standard expiries \(T_{\text{exp}}\) (from 1M to 30Y) and swap tenors \(T_{\text{tenor}}\) (from 1Y to 30Y).
  2. Out-of-the-Money Skew Quotes: Volatility spreads quoted at standardized basis point offsets from the ATM forward swap rate (e.g. from −200 to +200 bps).

A production swaption engine does not store raw volatility points across a fine 3D mesh. Instead, it parameterizes each discrete \((T_{\text{exp}}, T_{\text{tenor}})\) grid slice using the SABR model parameter vector \(\boldsymbol{\theta}(T_{\text{exp}}, T_{\text{tenor}}) = (\alpha, \rho, \nu)\) with fixed \(\beta = 0.50\). This reduces the high-dimensional cube to smooth 2D parameter surfaces.

2Parametric 2D Interpolation for Off-Grid Swaptions

Exotic derivatives and risk management buckets frequently require pricing swaptions whose expiry and tenor do not coincide with standard market grid pillars (e.g. a 3-year into 5-year swaption when market quotes exist only at 1Y and 5Y expiries, and 2Y and 10Y tenors).

Theorem 6.1 (SABR Parameter Surface Interpolation)

Let an off-grid swaption have expiry \(T^* \in [T_1, T_2]\) and tenor \(\tau^* \in [\tau_1, \tau_2]\). Defining normalized weights:

\[ \begin{aligned} w_{\text{exp}} &= \frac{T^* - T_1}{T_2 - T_1}, \quad w_{\text{ten}} = \frac{\tau^* - \tau_1}{\tau_2 - \tau_1} \end{aligned} \]

the interpolated parameter vector \(\mathbf{\theta}(T^*, \tau^*)\) is computed via bilinear blending across the four bounding grid nodes:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{\theta}(T^*, \tau^*) &= (1 - w_{\text{exp}}) (1 - w_{\text{ten}}) \mathbf{\theta}_{11} + (1 - w_{\text{exp}}) w_{\text{ten}} \mathbf{\theta}_{12} \\ &\quad + w_{\text{exp}} (1 - w_{\text{ten}}) \mathbf{\theta}_{21} + w_{\text{exp}} w_{\text{ten}} \mathbf{\theta}_{22} \end{aligned} \]

where \(\mathbf{\theta} = (\alpha, \rho, u)\).

Proof. The bilinear basis functions \(\phi_{ij}(T, \tau)\) form the unique continuous tensor product piecewise-linear interpolation operator on rectangular cells. At each grid corner \((T_i, \tau_j)\), the interpolant recovers the calibrated node value \(\mathbf{\theta}_{ij}\) identically. Evaluating the Hagan formula using interpolated parameters guarantees that the off-grid strike smile inherits the proper asymptotic curvature and skewness properties of neighboring market slices.

3Static Arbitrage & Breeden-Litzenberger Density Verification

Interpolating parameters across space can inadvertently generate prices that violate fundamental no-arbitrage bounds. A valid swaption cube must satisfy three static arbitrage conditions:

  1. Strike Monotonicity: Payer swaption prices must be strictly monotonically decreasing in strike \(K\): \(\frac{\partial V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial K} \le 0\).
  2. Calendar Monotonicity: For swaps with identical maturity dates, swaption prices must be non-decreasing in expiry.
  3. Convexity (Absence of Butterfly Arbitrage): Payer swaption prices must be strictly convex in strike: \(\frac{\partial^2 V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial K^2} \ge 0\).
Theorem 6.2 (Breeden-Litzenberger Density Non-Negativity)

Under the swap annuity measure \(\mathbb{Q}^A\), the risk-neutral probability density function \(q(K)\) of the forward swap rate \(S(T_{\text{exp}})\) is given by:

\[ \begin{aligned} q(K) &= \frac{1}{A(0)} \frac{\partial^2 V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial K^2} \end{aligned} \]

A swaption surface is free of butterfly arbitrage if and only if \(q(K) \ge 0\) for all strikes \(K \in \mathbb{R}\).

Proof. By risk-neutral pricing under numéraire \(A\), \(V_{\text{payer}}(K) = A(0) \int_K^\infty (s - K) q(s) ds\). Differentiating once using Leibniz's rule yields \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial K} = -A(0) \int_K^\infty q(s) ds\). Differentiating a second time yields \(\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial K^2} = A(0) q(K)\). Since a probability density cannot be negative, \(q(K) \ge 0\) is necessary and sufficient for the absence of butterfly arbitrage.

Lab 6.1 · Swaption Cube Construction & Arbitrage Verification

Hypothesis: Calibrating SABR parameter slices across a grid of expiries and tenors and performing bilinear surface interpolation produces smooth off-grid swaption smiles that satisfy Breeden-Litzenberger convexity with non-negative risk-neutral density across all market strikes.

"""Lab FI6-L06-1: The Swaption Volatility Cube and Breeden-Litzenberger Arbitrage Verification.

Constructs a 3D swaption volatility cube across option expiry, swap tenor, and strike.
Calibrates SABR slices, performs 2D surface parameter interpolation for off-grid swaptions,
and verifies absence of butterfly arbitrage by computing the Breeden-Litzenberger density.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

from scipy.optimize import minimize
from scipy.stats import norm


def sabr_vol(
    fwd: float,
    strike: float,
    texp: float,
    alpha: float,
    beta: float,
    rho: float,
    nu: float,
) -> float:
    """Hagan et al. (2002) SABR Black implied volatility."""
    if abs(fwd - strike) < 1e-8:
        term1 = alpha / (fwd ** (1.0 - beta))
        g1 = (1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0 * (alpha**2 / (fwd ** (2.0 - 2.0 * beta)))
        g2 = rho * beta * nu * alpha / (4.0 * (fwd ** (1.0 - beta)))
        g3 = (2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) / 24.0 * (nu**2)
        return float(term1 * (1.0 + (g1 + g2 + g3) * texp))

    log_fk = math.log(fwd / strike)
    denom_base = (fwd * strike) ** ((1.0 - beta) / 2.0)
    denom_exp = 1.0 + ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (log_fk**2) + ((1.0 - beta) ** 4 / 1920.0) * (log_fk**4)
    denom = denom_base * denom_exp

    z = (nu / alpha) * denom_base * log_fk
    if abs(z) < 1e-6:
        z_over_xz = 1.0 - 0.5 * rho * z + (1.0 / 12.0) * (2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) * (z**2)
    else:
        rad = max(1.0 - 2.0 * rho * z + z**2, 0.0)
        xz = math.log((math.sqrt(rad) + z - rho) / (1.0 - rho))
        z_over_xz = z / xz

    t_term = (
        ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (alpha**2 / ((fwd * strike) ** (1.0 - beta)))
        + (rho * beta * nu * alpha) / (4.0 * denom_base)
        + ((2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) / 24.0) * (nu**2)
    )
    return float((alpha / denom) * z_over_xz * (1.0 + t_term * texp))


def black_payer_price(fwd: float, strike: float, texp: float, vol: float, annuity: float = 1.0) -> float:
    """Black-76 payer swaption price."""
    std = vol * math.sqrt(texp)
    d1 = (math.log(fwd / strike) + 0.5 * std**2) / std
    d2 = d1 - std
    return float(annuity * (fwd * norm.cdf(d1) - strike * norm.cdf(d2)))


def calibrate_slice(fwd: float, texp: float, strikes: list[float], vols: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Calibrate (alpha, rho, nu) for a single expiry x tenor slice with beta=0.5."""
    atm_vol = vols[len(vols) // 2]
    init_alpha = atm_vol * math.sqrt(fwd)

    def obj(p: list[float]) -> float:
        a, r, n = p
        if a <= 1e-4 or n <= 1e-4 or r <= -0.99 or r >= 0.99:
            return 1e6
        return float(sum((sabr_vol(fwd, k, texp, a, 0.5, r, n) - v) ** 2 for k, v in zip(strikes, vols, strict=False)))

    res = minimize(obj, [init_alpha, -0.2, 0.3], bounds=[(1e-4, 1.0), (-0.95, 0.95), (1e-4, 2.0)], method="L-BFGS-B")
    return float(res.x[0]), float(res.x[1]), float(res.x[2])

4Where the Canon Breaks: Static Surfaces vs Dynamic Term Structure Consistency

While the swaption cube provides an accurate representation of marginal European swaption prices across the financial coordinate space \((T_{\text{exp}}, T_{\text{tenor}}, K)\), it is a purely static pricing object. It suffers from fundamental theoretical limitations when applied to path-dependent derivatives:

  • Absence of Joint Dynamics: The cube independently describes the terminal distribution of each forward swap rate \(S(t; T_{\text{exp}}, T_{\text{tenor}})\). It does not specify the joint covariance or instantaneous correlation between forward rates of different tenors.
  • Bermudan Swaption Inconsistency: A Bermudan swaption allows exercise on any reset date. Pricing Bermudans requires evaluating early-exercise boundaries that depend on the entire path of the yield curve. A static cube cannot price Bermudan swaptions without being mapped into a multi-factor term structure model (e.g. Cheyette or Libor Market Model).
  • CMS Convexity Adjustments: Constant Maturity Swap (CMS) products depend on integrals over the swaption smile across multiple observation dates. Small inconsistencies in cube interpolation can cause substantial pricing errors in CMS spread options.

5Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Off-grid swaption volatilities should be calculated by directly interpolating implied volatilities linearly across strikes and tenors.

    Correction: Directly interpolating implied volatilities across coordinates creates severe risk of calendar and butterfly arbitrage (e.g. kinks and negative risk-neutral densities). Best practice interpolates the underlying calibrated SABR parameter surfaces \((\alpha, \rho, u)\), evaluating the Hagan model on the interpolated parameters to preserve smooth, arbitrage-free smile geometry.

  • Misconception: If each grid slice has a positive risk-neutral density, the entire 3D interpolated cube is automatically free of calendar spread arbitrage.

    Correction: Verifying strike convexity on individual slices guarantees only the absence of butterfly arbitrage within each slice. It does not prevent calendar spread arbitrage, which occurs if option prices for identical underlying swaps do not grow monotonically with expiry.

  • Misconception: Swaption cubes can be constructed using pure Black-76 lognormal volatilities in all global markets.

    Correction: Following the multi-year presence of negative interest rates in Europe and Japan, swaption cubes in EUR, CHF, and JPY are constructed entirely in Bachelier normal volatility (bp/year). Attempting to build a lognormal cube in these markets causes algorithmic collapse at negative forward swap rates.

6Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 6.1 (Bilinear Parameter Weight Calculation): An off-grid swaption has expiry \(T^* = 2.5\) years and tenor \(\tau^* = 7.0\) years. The bounding grid pillar expiries are \(T_1 = 1.0, T_2 = 5.0\), and tenors are \(\tau_1 = 2.0, \tau_2 = 10.0\). Calculate the bilinear interpolation weights \((w_{11}, w_{12}, w_{21}, w_{22})\) assigned to the four corner nodes.

Solution

Compute normalized coordinate fractions:

\[ \begin{aligned} w_{\text{exp}} &= \frac{2.5 - 1.0}{5.0 - 1.0} = \frac{1.5}{4.0} = 0.375 \\ w_{\text{ten}} &= \frac{7.0 - 2.0}{10.0 - 2.0} = \frac{5.0}{8.0} = 0.625 \end{aligned} \]

The four bilinear corner weights are:

\[ \begin{aligned} w_{11} &= (1 - 0.375)(1 - 0.625) = 0.625 \times 0.375 = 0.234375 \\ w_{12} &= (1 - 0.375)(0.625) = 0.625 \times 0.625 = 0.390625 \\ w_{21} &= (0.375)(1 - 0.625) = 0.375 \times 0.375 = 0.140625 \\ w_{22} &= (0.375)(0.625) = 0.234375 \end{aligned} \]

Verification: \(0.234375 + 0.390625 + 0.140625 + 0.234375 = 1.000000\).

Problem 6.2 (Off-Grid Alpha Interpolation): Using the weights from Problem 6.1, compute the interpolated parameter \(\alpha^*\) given calibrated corner alphas: \(\alpha_{11} = 0.0380, \alpha_{12} = 0.0320, \alpha_{21} = 0.0340, \alpha_{22} = 0.0290\).

Solution

Evaluate the linear combination:

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^* &= 0.234375(0.0380) + 0.390625(0.0320) + 0.140625(0.0340) + 0.234375(0.0290) \\ &= 0.00890625 + 0.01250000 + 0.00478125 + 0.00679688 \\ &= 0.032984 \quad (0.03298) \end{aligned} \]

Problem 6.3 (Numerical Butterfly Arbitrage Check): Three payer swaptions with identical 1Y into 5Y structure and strike spacing \(\Delta K = 25\) bps are priced at: \(V(3.75\%) = 142.50\) bps, \(V(4.00\%) = 118.00\) bps, and \(V(4.25\%) = 95.00\) bps. Verify whether this market quote slice contains butterfly arbitrage.

Solution

A butterfly spread long at \(3.75\%\), short two at \(4.00\%\), and long at \(4.25\%\) has cost:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Butterfly Price} &= V(3.75\%) - 2 V(4.00\%) + V(4.25\%) \\ &= 142.50 - 2(118.00) + 95.00 \\ &= 142.50 - 236.00 + 95.00 = +1.50 \text{ bps} \end{aligned} \]

Since the butterfly price is strictly positive (\(+1.50\) bps \(> 0\)), the discrete second derivative is positive, confirming the absence of butterfly arbitrage across this strike interval.

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 6.4 (Breeden-Litzenberger Density Normalization): Prove that integrating the Breeden-Litzenberger second derivative \(\frac{1}{A(0)} \frac{\partial^2 V_{\text{payer}}}{\partial K^2}\) over all strikes \(K \in (-\infty, \infty)\) yields exactly 1.

Solution

Let \(q(K) = \frac{1}{A(0)} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial K^2}\). By the fundamental theorem of calculus:

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty q(K) \, dK = \frac{1}{A(0)} \left[ \lim_{K \to \infty} \frac{\partial V}{\partial K} - \lim_{K \to -\infty} \frac{\partial V}{\partial K} \right] \]

For a payer swaption, as \(K \to \infty\), the option never exercises, so \(V \to 0\) and \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial K} \to 0\). As \(K \to -\infty\), the option always exercises, so \(V \to A(0)(S - K)\) and \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial K} \to -A(0)\). Substituting these limits:

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty q(K) \, dK = \frac{1}{A(0)} [0 - (-A(0))] = \frac{A(0)}{A(0)} = 1 \]

Problem 6.5 (Calendar Spread Monotonicity Condition): Formulate the mathematical no-arbitrage condition governing European swaptions on underlying swaps that share an identical terminal maturity date \(T_M\), and prove that call prices must decrease as option expiry approaches \(T_M\).

Solution

Consider swaption 1 expiring at \(T_1\) and swaption 2 expiring at \(T_2 > T_1\), both delivering an underlying swap that matures at fixed terminal date \(T_M\). Swaption 1 delivers a longer swap (\(T_M - T_1\)) with higher annuity \(A(T_1)\) than swaption 2 (\(T_M - T_2\)). Furthermore, the holder of swaption 1 can enter the swap at \(T_1\) and hedge forward to \(T_2\). Because the underlying cash flows of the shorter swap are a strict subset of the longer swap, no-arbitrage mandates that \(V(0; T_1, T_M - T_1) \ge V(0; T_2, T_M - T_2)\).

Problem 6.6 (Continuity of Bilinear Parameter Manifolds): Prove that bilinear parameter interpolation across rectangular grid cells produces a \(C^0\) continuous global surface that is differentiable everywhere except along the cell boundary edges.

Solution

Across any boundary edge between cell \(A\) and cell \(B\) (say along constant \(T = T_2\)), the interpolation formula depends only on the coordinate \(\tau\) and the two node values \(\mathbf{\theta}(T_2, \tau_1)\) and \(\mathbf{\theta}(T_2, \tau_2)\). Since both cell \(A\) and cell \(B\) share these exact two corner values, their boundary traces coincide identically: \(\mathbf{\theta}_A(T_2, \tau) = \mathbf{\theta}_B(T_2, \tau)\). Hence the surface is \(C^0\) continuous everywhere. The partial derivatives \(\frac{\partial \mathbf{\theta}}{\partial T}\) exhibit jump discontinuities across lines \(T = T_i\), verifying piecewise differentiability.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 6.7 (The Diagonal Co-Terminal Swaption Axis): In mortgage convexity and Bermuda swaption desks, traders focus heavily on "co-terminal" swaptions (swaptions where \(T_{\text{exp}} + T_{\text{tenor}} = 10\text{Y}\) or \(30\text{Y}\)). Explain why liquidity and hedging flows cluster along co-terminal diagonals of the swaption cube.

Solution

Co-terminal swaptions share the identical final fixed-rate cash flow date \(T_M\) (e.g. a 1Yx9Y, 2Yx8Y, and 5Yx5Y all terminate in 10 years). Agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) have 30-year final maturities; hedging mortgage duration changes as time progresses requires shifting along the 30-year co-terminal diagonal. Additionally, Bermudan swaptions can be decomposed into strips of co-terminal European swaptions, making the co-terminal diagonal the most actively traded and liquid sub-manifold of the entire cube.

Problem 6.8 (The Lehman Swaption Cube Crisis): In autumn 2008, broker quotes for out-of-the-money receiver swaptions diverged wildly across dealers, with bid-ask spreads widening by 10x. Document how major investment banks patched inconsistent cube data during the liquidity freeze.

Solution

During the panic, trading in deep OTM wings halted, leaving quotes stale or contradictory. Standard least-squares SABR calibration crashed due to outlier points. Quantitative risk teams implemented robust M-estimators with Huber loss functions, capped vol-of-vol \(\nu \le 0.80\), and locked correlation \(\rho\) to historical rolling averages. Slices with non-positive Breeden-Litzenberger densities were automatically rejected and replaced by synthetic parametric wings to avoid posting negative risk margins.

Problem 6.9 (CMS Spread Option Valuation on the Cube): A Constant Maturity Swap (CMS) spread option pays \(\max(S_{10\text{Y}}(T) - S_{2\text{Y}}(T) - K, 0)\). Explain why pricing CMS spread options requires knowledge of the joint correlation between two distinct tenors on the swaption cube.

Solution

The payoff depends simultaneously on the 10-year swap rate and the 2-year swap rate at future time \(T\). The variance of the spread is:

\[ \text{Var}(S_{10} - S_2) = \sigma_{10}^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2 \rho_{10, 2} \sigma_{10} \sigma_2 \]

While the swaption cube provides \(\sigma_{10}\) and \(\sigma_2\) individually, it contains no information about \(\rho_{10, 2}\). Pricing CMS spread options requires calibrating a dynamic multi-factor HJM model to the cube to extract the cross-tenor correlation matrix.

Problem 6.10 (Bicubic Spline vs SABR Parameter Interpolation): Contrast the mathematical stability of direct 2D bicubic spline interpolation of Black volatilities \(\sigma(T, \tau)\) against bilinear interpolation of calibrated SABR parameters \((\alpha(T, \tau), \rho(T, \tau), \nu(T, \tau))\).

Solution

Direct bicubic spline interpolation of volatilities produces smooth derivatives in \((T, \tau)\) space, but spline overshooting between sparse market nodes routinely creates artificial humps and negative second derivatives \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} < 0\), introducing butterfly arbitrage. In contrast, interpolating SABR parameters ensures that at every off-grid point, the strike smile is generated by a valid structural stochastic volatility model, preserving structural skew and wing properties without spline oscillation artifacts.

Problem 6.11 (Global Arbitrage-Free SVI-SABR Formulation): Formulate the Gatheral Surface Quasi-Explicit (SSVI) condition on a swaption slice \(\theta(k) = \sigma^2 T\) and derive the necessary and sufficient inequality constraints on total implied variance that eliminate calendar spread arbitrage.

Solution

Let \(w(k, T) = \sigma^2(k, T) T\) be total implied variance at log-moneyness \(k = \ln(K/f)\). By Dupire's local volatility formulation, absence of calendar arbitrage requires \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial T} \ge 0\) for all \(k\). Under SSVI parameterization:

\[ w(k, \theta_T) = \frac{\theta_T}{2} \left[ 1 + \rho \phi(\theta_T) k + \sqrt{(\phi(\theta_T) k + \rho)^2 + (1 - \rho^2)} \right] \]

calendar monotonicity is guaranteed if \(\theta_T\) is non-decreasing in \(T\) and \(\frac{d}{d\theta} (\theta \phi(\theta)) \ge 0\) with \(\phi(\theta)(1 + |\rho|) \le 4\).

Problem 6.12 (Mapping the Swaption Cube into Cheyette Models): Detail how a calibrated static swaption cube is projected into a Markovian Cheyette quasi-Gaussian term-structure model to price Bermudan swaptions without full Monte Carlo simulation.

Solution

Cheyette models parameterize the HJM forward rate volatility as \(\sigma(t, T) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i(t) e^{-\kappa_i (T-t)}\). This separable exponential kernel allows the infinite-dimensional yield curve dynamics to collapse into a finite-dimensional Markovian state vector \((x_t, y_t)\). The deterministic volatility weights \(\lambda_i(t)\) are calibrated to reprice the ATM diagonal of the swaption cube, while state-dependent local volatility scalings \(g(x_t)\) match SABR skewness, enabling fast finite-difference PDE pricing of Bermudan exercise boundaries.

8Retrieval Practice

  1. What are the three coordinate dimensions of the swaption volatility cube?

    Options

    A swaption requires three coordinates: option expiration, underlying swap length, and strike (moneyness).

  2. Why do production swaption desks interpolate SABR parameters rather than raw volatilities?

    Options

    Direct volatility interpolation often creates kinks that violate convexity; interpolating model parameters preserves structural smile geometry.

  3. What mathematical condition ensures the absence of butterfly arbitrage on a swaption slice?

    Options

    Convexity in strike (d^2 V / dK^2 >= 0) guarantees non-negative butterfly spread payoffs and non-negative risk-neutral probabilities.

  4. What is a co-terminal swaption?

    Options

    Co-terminal swaptions share the identical swap end date (e.g. 1Yx9Y and 5Yx5Y both terminate in 10 years).

  5. Why can't a static swaption cube price Bermudan swaptions directly?

    Options

    Bermudan swaptions require evaluating multi-period early-exercise decisions driven by joint yield curve dynamics not contained in static marginal quotes.

  6. What formula is used to extract the risk-neutral density from payer swaption prices?

    Options

    The Breeden-Litzenberger result proves that the second derivative of call prices with respect to strike yields the risk-neutral density.

  7. What is the sum of bilinear interpolation weights assigned to the four bounding corner nodes?

    Options

    Bilinear basis functions form a partition of unity, ensuring that their sum identically equals 1.0.

  8. What risk arises when pricing CMS spread options using individual swaption cube slices?

    Options

    The spread variance depends heavily on the correlation between two different rate tenors, which is not captured by individual slice quotes.

  9. Why must swaption payer prices decrease monotonically with strike K?

    Options

    Because the cash payoff is max(S-K, 0), increasing K strictly reduces or preserves the payoff in every state of the world.

  10. How are European swaptions in negative-rate currencies parameterized in the cube?

    Options

    Normal SABR (beta = 0) parameterizes the cube in Bachelier normal volatility, ensuring stable calculations across negative rates.
