Multi-Curve Framework & OIS Discounting

Before August 2007, fixed income pricing relied on a single yield curve where forward rates and discount factors shared an identical term structure. When interbank credit and liquidity risk widened tenor basis spreads, single-curve valuation collapsed. Multi-curve discounting separates the discount curve, determined by collateralized overnight index swap (OIS) rates under Credit Support Annexes, from forwarding curves for floating reference rates. This dual-curve architecture governs all modern linear and non-linear interest rate derivatives.

1. The Collapse of Single-Curve Valuation

In classical textbook theory, a zero-coupon bond \(P(t, T)\) served both to discount future cash flows and to define simple forward rates:

\[ \begin{aligned} F(t; T_1, T_2) &= \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \frac{P(t, T_1)}{P(t, T_2)} - 1 \right) \end{aligned} \]

This formulation assumed that an investor could borrow or lend unsecured funds at the risk-free rate. In August 2007, the spread between 3-month interbank offer rates and 3-month overnight index swaps expanded from historical levels of 5–10 basis points to over 150 basis points due to acute bank counterparty credit risk and term liquidity hoarding.

Definition 1.1 (Collateralized Discount Curve). Under a standardized Credit Support Annex (CSA) with zero minimum transfer amounts and daily cash variation margining in currency \(C\), the cash flows of a derivative position are discounted at the overnight collateral rate \(r_c(t)\). For USD derivatives, this benchmark discount factor is:

\[ \begin{aligned} P_D(t, T) &= \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}} \left[ \exp\left( -\int_t^T r_c(u) du \right) \right] \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 1.1 (Funding Replication Theorem). If a derivative is fully collateralized with daily cash margining earning overnight rate \(r_c(t)\), absence of arbitrage implies that the unique risk-neutral pricing measure \(\mathbb{Q}^D\) uses the collateralized bank account \(B_D(t) = \exp\left(\int_0^t r_c(u) du\right)\) as numeraire.

Proof. Consider a self-financing replication portfolio \(\Pi(t)\) hedging derivative \(V(t)\). Daily cash collateral \(C(t) = V(t)\) is posted by the out-of-the-money counterparty to the in-the-money counterparty. The collateral receiver pays overnight interest at rate \(r_c(t)\). Any cash invested in the hedging strategy earns or costs \(r_c(t)\). By no-arbitrage dynamic replication, the drift of \(V(t)\) under the pricing measure equals \(r_c(t) V(t)\). Hence, discounted asset prices \(V(t)/B_D(t)\) are \(\mathbb{Q}^D\)-martingales. A formal derivation of collateral-adjusted discounting confirms that unsecured borrowing rates cannot serve as discount numeraires.

2. Dual-Curve Bootstrapping & Forward Projection

Under multi-curve discounting, market pricing distinguishes two distinct curves:

  • The Discount Curve \(P_D(0, T)\): Bootstrapped from liquid OIS contracts (SOFR swaps in USD, \(\text{ESTR}\) swaps in EUR).
  • The Projection Curves \(P_F^x(0, T)\): Bootstrapped for each floating index tenor \(x \in \{1\text{M}, 3\text{M}, 6\text{M}\}\) using basis swaps and standard fixed-for-floating swaps.

Definition 1.2 (Tenor Forward Rate). For an accrual period \([T_{k-1}, T_k]\) with day-count fraction \(\tau_k\), the forward floating rate \(F_k(t) = F(t; T_{k-1}, T_k)\) is projected from the fictitious zero curve \(P_F(t, T)\) as:

\[ \begin{aligned} F_k(t) &= \frac{1}{\tau_k} \left( \frac{P_F(t, T_{k-1})}{P_F(t, T_k)} - 1 \right) \end{aligned} \]

The forward rate \(F_k(t)\) is a martingale under the forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}\) associated with the collateral discount bond \(P_D(t, T_k)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} F_k(t) &= \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{T_k}} \left[ F_k(T_{k-1}) \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 1.2 (Multi-Curve Swap Par Rate). In a fixed-for-floating interest rate swap paying fixed coupon \(S\) on dates \(T_1, \dots, T_n\) and receiving floating rate \(F_k\) resetting at \(T_{k-1}\), the par swap rate under multi-curve discounting is:

\[ \begin{aligned} S(t) &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(t, T_k) F_k(t)} {\sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(t, T_k)} = \frac{V_{\text{flt}}(t)}{A(t)} \end{aligned} \]

where \(A(t) = \sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(t, T_k)\) is the swap annuity under OIS discounting.

Proof. The present value of the floating leg is the sum of discounted floating payments:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{flt}}(t) &= \sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(t, T_k) F_k(t) \end{aligned} \]

The present value of the fixed leg paying coupon \(S\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{fix}}(t) &= S \sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(t, T_k) = S A(t) \end{aligned} \]

Equating \(V_{\text{flt}}(t) = V_{\text{fix}}(t)\) yields the unique par swap rate \(S(t)\). In contrast to single-curve theory, \(V_{\text{flt}}(t)\) does not telescope to \(P_D(t, T_0) - P_D(t, T_n)\) because \(F_k(t)\) is generated by \(P_F\) rather than \(P_D\).

Lab 1 · Multi-Curve Bootstrapping and Swap Pricing

Evaluating a 3-year receiver swap under multi-curve discounting will reveal a systematic valuation discrepancy compared to naive single-curve discounting proportional to the tenor basis spread.

"""Lab FI6-L01-1: Multi-Curve Bootstrapping and Swap Pricing.

Demonstrates dual-curve pricing: OIS discount curve vs. floating projection curve.
Compares multi-curve swap valuation with naive single-curve discounting,
quantifying the pricing discrepancy introduced by basis spreads.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def discount_factor(zero_rate: float, tenor: float) -> float:
    """Continuous compounding discount factor P(0, T) = exp(-r * T)."""
    return math.exp(-zero_rate * tenor)


def forward_rate_from_factors(df_start: float, df_end: float, tau: float) -> float:
    """Simple forward rate F(0; T_start, T_end) = (P(0, T_start)/P(0, T_end) - 1) / tau."""
    return (df_start / df_end - 1.0) / tau


def run_lab() -> None:
    print("=== Multi-Curve Discounting & Swap Valuation ===")

    # Market curves: OIS (SOFR) discounting curve vs. Forwarding curve (Term reference)
    # Tenors: 0.5y, 1.0y, 1.5y, 2.0y, 2.5y, 3.0y (semi-annual fixed leg, quarterly or semi float)
    tenors = [0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0]
    tau = 0.5  # semi-annual accrual

    # OIS zero rates (discount curve) and Reference zero rates (projection curve)
    # Basis spread is ~25-35 bps across tenors
    ois_rates = [0.0380, 0.0395, 0.0405, 0.0415, 0.0422, 0.0428]
    proj_rates = [0.0410, 0.0428, 0.0440, 0.0450, 0.0457, 0.0463]

    p_ois = [discount_factor(r, t) for r, t in zip(ois_rates, tenors, strict=False)]
    p_proj = [discount_factor(r, t) for r, t in zip(proj_rates, tenors, strict=False)]

    print(
        f"{'Tenor (y)':<10} {'OIS Zero':<10} {'P_OIS(0,T)':<12} "
        f"{'Proj Zero':<10} {'P_Proj(0,T)':<12} {'Basis (bps)':<12}"
    )
    for t, ro, do, rp, dp in zip(tenors, ois_rates, p_ois, proj_rates, p_proj, strict=False):
        basis_bps = (rp - ro) * 10000.0
        print(f"{t:<10.1f} {ro:<10.4f} {do:<12.5f} {rp:<10.4f} {dp:<12.5f} {basis_bps:<12.1f}")

    # Compute forward rates on projection curve
    fwd_rates = []
    for i in range(len(tenors)):
        df_start = 1.0 if i == 0 else p_proj[i - 1]
        df_end = p_proj[i]
        f = forward_rate_from_factors(df_start, df_end, tau)
        fwd_rates.append(f)

    # Swap Annuity under OIS discounting: A(0) = sum(tau * P_OIS(0, T_k))
    annuity_multicurve = sum(tau * do for do in p_ois)

    # Floating leg PV under multi-curve: sum(tau * P_OIS(0, T_k) * F_k)
    pv_float_multicurve = sum(tau * do * f for do, f in zip(p_ois, fwd_rates, strict=False))

    # Multi-curve par swap rate: S_multi = pv_float / annuity
    s_multi = pv_float_multicurve / annuity_multicurve

    # Naive single-curve calculation (using projection curve for both discounting and forwarding)
    annuity_single = sum(tau * dp for dp in p_proj)
    # In single curve, floating leg telescopes to 1 - P(0, T_n)
    pv_float_single = 1.0 - p_proj[-1]
    s_single = pv_float_single / annuity_single

    # Also compute single curve when using OIS for both:
    annuity_ois_single = sum(tau * do for do in p_ois)
    pv_float_ois_single = 1.0 - p_ois[-1]
    s_ois_single = pv_float_ois_single / annuity_ois_single

    print("\n--- 3-Year Par Swap Rate Analysis ---")
    print(f"Multi-Curve Par Swap Rate  : {s_multi * 100.0:.4f}% ({s_multi * 10000.0:.1f} bps)")
    print(f"Single-Curve (Proj only)   : {s_single * 100.0:.4f}% ({s_single * 10000.0:.1f} bps)")
    print(f"Single-Curve (OIS only)    : {s_ois_single * 100.0:.4f}% ({s_ois_single * 10000.0:.1f} bps)")
    print(f"Dual-Curve Annuity A(0)    : {annuity_multicurve:.5f}")

    # Valuation of fixed-rate receiver swap with fixed coupon K = 4.50% on $100M notional
    notional = 100_000_000.0
    k_fixed = 0.0450
    pv_swap_multicurve = notional * (k_fixed * annuity_multicurve - pv_float_multicurve)
    pv_swap_single = notional * (k_fixed * annuity_single - pv_float_single)

    pricing_diff = pv_swap_multicurve - pv_swap_single
    print("\nReceiver Swap PV (Fixed = 4.50%, Notional = $100M):")
    print(f"Multi-Curve PV             : ${pv_swap_multicurve:,.2f}")
    print(f"Single-Curve PV            : ${pv_swap_single:,.2f}")
    print(
        f"Pricing Error (Naive - Dual): ${-pricing_diff:,.2f} "
        f"({(-pricing_diff / notional) * 10000.0:.2f} bps of notional)"
    )


run_lab()

Where the Canon Breaks

The multi-curve framework assumes that cash collateral is fixed in a single domestic currency without embedded options. In institutional cross-border trading, this assumption breaks down:

  • Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD) Collateral Options: Multi-currency CSAs permit the posting party to choose between USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY cash. The effective discount rate becomes the minimum across collateral yield options, introducing non-linear optionality into linear discounting documented across OTC derivatives clearing.
  • Cross-Currency Basis Spreads: The presence of non-zero cross-currency basis spreads violates covered interest parity for funding currencies, requiring currency-adjusted discounting curves establishing persistent post-crisis CIP deviations.

4Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Overnight index swaps (OIS) carry zero credit risk and represent the theoretical risk-free rate of economic textbook models.

    Correction: While OIS rates represent daily compounded overnight transactions between major creditworthy financial institutions, they reflect secured or near-risk-free interbank liquidity. They are not pure default-free sovereign yields; however, because ISDA Credit Support Annexes mandate daily cash collateralization with interest remunerated at the OIS rate, OIS is the exact economic discounting rate for fully collateralized derivative cash flows.

  • Misconception: Forward floating reference rates can still be extracted directly from ratios of discount factors as \(F(t; T_{k-1}, T_k) = \frac{1}{\tau_k}\left(\frac{P^D(t, T_{k-1})}{P^D(t, T_k)} - 1 \right)\).

    Correction: This relationship holds if and only if the underlying loan or floating rate can be funded and borrowed at the discount rate. For term reference rates with credit and term liquidity premia, forward rates must be projected using a tenor-specific forwarding curve \(P^F(t, T)\), while discounting occurs along \(P^D(t, T)\). The single-curve formula yields severe mispricings under dual-curve reality.

  • Misconception: Bootstrapping dual curves requires joint simultaneous non-linear optimization across all OIS and floating swap quotes.

    Correction: Dual curve bootstrapping is strictly hierarchical and sequential. First, the discount curve \(P^D(t, T)\) is bootstrapped entirely independently using OIS instruments (fed funds or SOFR swaps). Second, given the frozen discount curve, each floating forwarding curve (e.g. 1M, 3M, 6M) is bootstrapped sequentially from standard interest rate swaps or basis swaps referencing that specific tenor.

Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 1.1. Given 1-year and 2-year OIS discount factors \(P_D(0, 1) = 0.9600\) and \(P_D(0, 2) = 0.9180\), and 1-year into 1-year forward projection factor \(P_F(0, 1) = 0.9550, P_F(0, 2) = 0.9100\), calculate the multi-curve 2-year annual par swap rate.

Solution

Step 1: Calculate forward rates on the projection curve:

\[ \begin{aligned} F_1 &= \frac{1.0}{0.9550} - 1 = 0.04712 \\ F_2 &= \frac{0.9550}{0.9100} - 1 = 0.04945 \end{aligned} \]

Step 2: Compute floating leg PV using OIS discounting:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{flt}} &= 0.9600 \times 0.04712 + 0.9180 \times 0.04945 \\ &= 0.04524 + 0.04540 = 0.09064 \end{aligned} \]

Step 3: Compute swap annuity:

\[ \begin{aligned} A(0) &= P_D(0, 1) + P_D(0, 2) = 0.9600 + 0.9180 = 1.8780 \end{aligned} \]

Step 4: Par swap rate \(S = \frac{0.09064}{1.8780} = 0.04826\) (4.826%).

Problem 1.2. A financial institution enters a \$50M notional 1-year FRA paying fixed rate \(4.50\%\) and receiving floating rate \(F(0; 0.5, 1.0)\). If \(P_D(0, 1.0) = 0.9580\) and projected forward rate is \(4.90\%\), determine the market value of the FRA.

Solution

The FRA payoff at \(T_2 = 1.0\) is \(\tau \cdot (F - K)\) with \(\tau = 0.5\). Discounted present value:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{FRA}} &= 50{,}000{,}000 \times 0.9580 \times 0.5 \times (0.0490 - 0.0450) \\ &= 50{,}000{,}000 \times 0.9580 \times 0.0020 = \$95{,}800.00 \end{aligned} \]

Problem 1.3. Compute the basis spread in basis points between 6-month projected forward rate and 6-month OIS implied forward rate if \(P_D(0, 0.5) = 0.9800, P_D(0, 1.0) = 0.9600\) and \(P_F(0, 0.5) = 0.9780, P_F(0, 1.0) = 0.9560\).

Solution \[ \begin{aligned} F_{\text{OIS}} &= \frac{1}{0.5}\left(\frac{0.9800}{0.9600} - 1\right) = 4.167\% \\ F_{\text{Proj}} &= \frac{1}{0.5}\left(\frac{0.9780}{0.9560} - 1\right) = 4.603\% \\ \text{Spread} &= (0.04603 - 0.04167) \times 10^4 = 43.6 \text{ bps} \end{aligned} \]

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 1.4. Prove that under single-curve discounting (\(P_F \equiv P_D\)), the floating leg of a spot-starting interest rate swap telescopes to \(1 - P(0, T_n)\).

Solution

Expanding the floating leg cash flows under \(P_F \equiv P_D\):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{flt}} &= \sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P(0, T_k) \left[ \frac{1}{\tau_k}\left(\frac{P(0, T_{k-1})}{P(0, T_k)} - 1\right) \right] \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^n [P(0, T_{k-1}) - P(0, T_k)] \end{aligned} \]

The sum telescopes: \([P(0, T_0) - P(0, T_1)] + [P(0, T_1) - P(0, T_2)] + \dots + [P(0, T_{n-1}) - P(0, T_n)]\). For spot-starting swaps, \(P(0, T_0) = P(0, 0) = 1.0\), leaving \(1 - P(0, T_n)\).

Problem 1.5. Prove that the weights \(w_k = \frac{\tau_k P_D(0, T_k)}{A(0)}\) in the multi-curve swap rate representation sum to 1.

Solution

By definition of the swap annuity \(A(0) = \sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(0, T_k)\). Summing the weights:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=1}^n w_k &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n \tau_k P_D(0, T_k)}{A(0)} = \frac{A(0)}{A(0)} = 1.0 \end{aligned} \]

Because \(P_D(0, T_k) > 0\) and \(\tau_k > 0\), every \(w_k \in (0, 1)\), establishing that the swap rate is a strict convex combination of forward rates.

Problem 1.6. Prove that the Radon-Nikodym derivative between the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}^D\) and forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^T\) is \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^T}{d\mathbb{Q}^D} = \frac{P_D(T, T) B_D(0)}{P_D(0, T) B_D(T)} = \frac{1}{P_D(0, T) B_D(T)}\).

Solution

By change of numeraire, for two numeraires \(N_1(t) = P_D(t, T)\) and \(N_2(t) = B_D(t)\), the Radon-Nikodym derivative on \(\mathcal{F}_t\) is \(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^T}{d\mathbb{Q}^D}\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_t} = \frac{P_D(t, T) B_D(0)}{P_D(0, T) B_D(t)}\). Setting \(t = T\) with \(P_D(T, T) = 1\) and \(B_D(0) = 1\) yields the result.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 1.7. In post-2008 USD markets, the 3M vs 6M tenor basis swap spread traded at a median of 18 bps. Explain the economic risk factors that generate a positive basis spread for longer tenors.

Solution

A 6-month interbank loan entails 6 months of continuous counterparty credit risk and locks up bank balance sheet liquidity twice as long as a 3-month loan. To induce lenders to offer 6-month funds, borrowers must pay a liquidity term premium and higher default compensation, creating a persistent positive basis spread.

Problem 1.8. Examine the impact on swap DV01 when discounting is switched from 3-month LIBOR discounting to SOFR discounting in a rising rate environment where SOFR is 30 bps below LIBOR.

Solution

Because SOFR is lower than LIBOR, SOFR discount factors \(P_D(0, T) = \exp(-r_{\text{sofr}} T)\) are higher than legacy discount factors. Consequently, the swap annuity \(A(0)\) is larger, increasing the dollar DV01 of the swap by 2–4% across intermediate and long tenors.

Problem 1.9. Quantify the mark-to-market error incurred by a bank pricing a 10-year \$100M fixed-receiver swap (annuity = 7.80) if the forwarding curve shifts upward by 25 basis points while the discount curve remains constant.

Solution

The floating leg present value increases by approximately \(\Delta V_{\text{flt}} = \text{Notional} \times A(0) \times \Delta F = 100{,}000{,}000 \times 7.80 \times 0.0025 = \$1{,}950{,}000\). For a fixed receiver, the swap value decreases by \$1.95M.

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 1.10. Formulate the bootstrapping linear programming problem to simultaneously extract smooth zero curves from overlapping OIS and basis swap quotes while penalizing curve curvature.

Solution

Minimize \(\int_0^{T_{\max}} [r''(t)]^2 dt + \lambda \sum_{i} \epsilon_i^2\) subject to linear repricing constraints \(\sum \tau_k P(0, T_k) (F_k - S_i) = \epsilon_i\). The cubic spline parameterization converts this into a quadratic program with linear equality constraints.

Problem 1.11. Under Basel III capital rules, banks must hold regulatory capital against credit valuation adjustment (CVA). How does collateralized OIS discounting interact with bilateral CVA calculations?

Solution

Perfect zero-threshold daily variation margining reduces bilateral net exposure to the 10-day margin period of risk (MPOR). Consequently, CVA is reduced by over 90% relative to uncollateralized trades, isolating the residual risk to gap jumps during liquidation.

Problem 1.12. Contrast multi-curve pricing under physical collateral delivery with negative interest rates: what happens when the collateral rate \(r_c(t) < 0\)?

Solution

When \(r_c(t) < 0\), the collateral receiver must pay negative interest (i.e. receives a fee from the poster). The discount factor \(P_D(0, T) = \exp(-\int_0^T r_c dt) > 1.0\), meaning future sure cash flows have a present value greater than par.

7Retrieval Practice

  1. Why did single-curve interest rate pricing collapse after August 2007?

    Options

    Substantial counterparty risk and liquidity hoarding widened interbank basis spreads, making single-curve discounting obsolete.

  2. What determines the discount curve for a fully collateralized interest rate swap under a daily cash CSA?

    Options

    The contractual interest paid on cash variation margin represents the effective funding cost of the position, requiring OIS discounting.

  3. Under multi-curve discounting, what is the swap annuity \(A(0)\)?

    Options

    The swap annuity is the present value of receiving 1 basis point per period, discounted along the collateral curve.

  4. Why does the floating leg present value fail to telescope under multi-curve discounting?

    Options

    Telescoping requires \(P_F \equiv P_D\). Distinct curves prevent the algebraic cancellation of intermediate discount factors.

  5. Under which probability measure is the forward floating rate \(F(t; T_1, T_2)\) a martingale?

    Options

    A forward rate is a martingale under the forward measure associated with the zero-coupon bond maturing on its payment date.

  6. What is a tenor basis swap?

    Options

    Tenor basis swaps exchange e.g. 3M LIBOR + spread for 6M LIBOR in the same currency, establishing the tenor basis curve.

  7. If the projection zero rate is strictly greater than the OIS zero rate across all maturities, which statement is true?

    Options

    Higher forward rates on the projection curve increase floating cash flows, driving the par swap rate above the pure OIS rate.

  8. What does a positive cross-currency basis spread in EUR/USD indicate?

    Options

    A cross-currency basis spread reflects dollar funding scarcity and deviations from covered interest parity.

  9. In a multi-curve bootstrap, which curve is stripped first?

    Options

    OIS swaps depend only on the discount curve. Once stripped, OIS discount factors discount the cash flows of tenor basis instruments.

  10. What is the Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD) collateral option in a bilateral CSA?

    Options

    If a CSA allows multiple collateral currencies, the poster posts the currency offering the highest interest relative to FX funding costs.
