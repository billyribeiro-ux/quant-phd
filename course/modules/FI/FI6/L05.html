In interest rate options, market implied volatilities vary strongly across strikes, forming pronounced asymmetric smiles and skews that flat models cannot reproduce. The SABR (Stochastic Alpha, Beta, Rho) model developed by Patrick Hagan and collaborators in 2002 transformed fixed income quantitative finance by providing an accurate, closed-form asymptotic formula for implied volatility. Today, SABR serves as the universal interpolation and smile-risk management language across global swaption and cap/floor trading desks.

1SABR Continuous-Time Stochastic Dynamics

Prior to SABR, local volatility models such as Constant Elasticity of Variance (CEV) predicted that when interest rates rose, the volatility smile would shift in the opposite direction of market reality (the "backbone problem"). Hagan, Kumar, Lesniewski, and Woodward resolved this paradox by coupling CEV local volatility with stochastic volatility .

Definition 5.1 (The SABR Stochastic Volatility Model)

The SABR model is a two-factor continuous-time diffusion where the forward rate \(F(t)\) and its stochastic volatility state variable \(\alpha(t)\) satisfy the stochastic differential equations:

\[ \begin{aligned} dF(t) &= \alpha(t) F(t)^\beta \, dW_1(t), \quad F(0) = f \\ d\alpha(t) &= \nu \alpha(t) \, dW_2(t), \quad \alpha(0) = \alpha \\ d\langle W_1, W_2 \rangle_t &= \rho \, dt \end{aligned} \]

under the forward measure numéraire \(\mathbb{Q}^T\), where \(\alpha > 0\) is initial volatility, \(\beta \in [0, 1]\) is the CEV elasticity exponent, \(\rho \in (-1, 1)\) is the correlation between rate and volatility shocks, and \(\nu > 0\) is the volatility of volatility (vol-of-vol) .

The four parameters govern distinct economic dimensions of the implied volatility surface:

  • Alpha (\(\alpha\)): Sets the overall height and level of the volatility smile.
  • Beta (\(\beta\)): Determines the local volatility backbone. For \(\beta = 1\), the rate dynamics are lognormal; for \(\beta = 0\), the dynamics are normal (Bachelier); for \(\beta = 0.5\), the dynamics follow CIR square-root scaling.
  • Rho (\( \rho\)): Controls the asymmetry and skew of the smile. Negative correlation (\( \rho < 0\)) creates a downward-sloping skew (high volatility for low strikes), whereas positive correlation (\( \rho > 0\)) creates an upward slope.
  • Nu (\( u\)): Controls the smile curvature and convexity (the wings). Higher \( u\) deepens the U-shaped smile.

2The Hagan et al. (2002) Asymptotic Formula

The primary reason for SABR's global adoption is that option prices do not require numerical PDE grids or Monte Carlo simulation. Using singular perturbation techniques on the backward Kolmogorov equation, Hagan et al. derived an asymptotic expansion expressing Black-76 implied volatility \(\sigma_{\text{B}}(K, f)\) in closed form.

Theorem 5.1 (Hagan et al. Implied Volatility Expansion)

Under SABR dynamics, for an option with expiry \(T\) and strike \(K\), the Black-76 implied volatility \(\sigma_{\text{B}}(K, f)\) is given to order \(\mathcal{O}(T)\) by:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_{\text{B}}(K, f) &= \frac{\alpha}{(f K)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}} \left[ 1 + \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24} \ln^2\left(\frac{f}{K} \right) + \frac{(1-\beta)^4}{1920} \ln^4\left(\frac{f}{K} \right) \right]} \\ &\quad \times \left( \frac{z}{x(z)} \right) \cdot \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24} \frac{\alpha^2}{(f K)^{1-\beta}} + \frac{ \rho \beta u \alpha}{4 (f K)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}} + \frac{2 - 3 \rho^2}{24} u^2 \right) T \right] \end{aligned} \]

where the moneyness transformation variable \(z\) and function \(x(z)\) are defined by:

\[ \begin{aligned} z &= \frac{\nu}{\alpha} (f K)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}} \ln\left(\frac{f}{K} \right) \\ x(z) &= \ln\left( \frac{\sqrt{1 - 2 \rho z + z^2} + z - \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) \end{aligned} \]

In the limit as strike approaches the at-the-money forward rate (\(K \to f\)), the ratio \(\frac{z}{x(z)} \to 1\), yielding the exact at-the-money formula:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_{\text{ATM}} &= \frac{\alpha}{f^{1-\beta}} \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24} \frac{\alpha^2}{f^{2-2\beta}} + \frac{ \rho \beta u \alpha}{4 f^{1-\beta}} + \frac{2 - 3 \rho^2}{24} u^2 \right) T \right] \end{aligned} \]

3Model Calibration & The Parameter Backbone

Simultaneously calibrating all four parameters \((\alpha, \beta, \rho, u)\) across a single maturity slice is mathematically ill-conditioned because \(\beta\) and \( \rho\) are highly collinear in their impact on the slope of the smile at the money. Differentiating the Hagan formula at ATM reveals:

\[ \begin{aligned} \left. \frac{\partial \sigma_{\text{B}}}{\partial K} \right|_{K=f} &\approx -\frac{1-\beta}{2 f} \sigma_{\text{ATM}} + \frac{ \rho u}{2 f^{1-\beta}} \end{aligned} \]

Both lower \(\beta\) and more negative \( \rho\) steepen the downward skew. Consequently, standard industry practice fixes \(\beta\) a priori based on market convention or historical time-series regressions:

  • US Dollar Swaptions: \(\beta = 0.50\) is the canonical market convention.
  • Euro / Negative Rate Markets: \(\beta = 0.0\) (Normal SABR) to natively accommodate rates near zero.

With \(\beta\) fixed, the remaining three parameters \((\alpha, \rho, u)\) are calibrated to market quotes via nonlinear least-squares optimization: \(\min_{\alpha, \rho, u} \sum_{i} (\sigma_{\text{SABR}}(K_i) - \sigma_{\text{market}}(K_i))^2\).

Lab 5.1 · The SABR Implied Volatility Engine & Calibration

Hypothesis: The Hagan et al. (2002) asymptotic expansion provides a flexible three-parameter parameterization capable of fitting empirical swaption smiles to within 1 basis point of market volatility while maintaining stable parametric sensitivities to skew and curvature.

"""Lab FI6-L05-1: The SABR Stochastic Volatility Model.

Implements the Hagan et al. (2002) asymptotic implied volatility expansion,
analyzes the impact of correlation rho (skew) and vol-of-vol nu (smile curvature),
and performs least-squares calibration of (alpha, rho, nu) to market swaption strikes.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

from scipy.optimize import minimize


def sabr_black_vol(
    fwd: float,
    strike: float,
    texp: float,
    alpha: float,
    beta: float,
    rho: float,
    nu: float,
) -> float:
    """Evaluate Black-76 implied volatility under the SABR model via Hagan et al. (2002)."""
    if fwd <= 0.0 or strike <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError(f"SABR lognormal expansion requires F > 0, K > 0. Got F={fwd}, K={strike}")

    # Check ATM condition
    if abs(fwd - strike) < 1e-8:
        # Hagan ATM expansion
        f_mid = fwd
        term1 = alpha / (f_mid ** (1.0 - beta))
        gamma1 = (1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0 * (alpha**2 / (f_mid ** (2.0 - 2.0 * beta)))
        gamma2 = rho * beta * nu * alpha / (4.0 * (f_mid ** (1.0 - beta)))
        gamma3 = (2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) / 24.0 * (nu**2)
        return float(term1 * (1.0 + (gamma1 + gamma2 + gamma3) * texp))

    # General OTM formula
    log_fk = math.log(fwd / strike)
    math.sqrt(fwd * strike)

    denom_base = (fwd * strike) ** ((1.0 - beta) / 2.0)
    denom_expansion = 1.0 + ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (log_fk**2) + ((1.0 - beta) ** 4 / 1920.0) * (log_fk**4)
    denom = denom_base * denom_expansion

    z = (nu / alpha) * denom_base * log_fk

    # Numerical safeguard for z near 0
    if abs(z) < 1e-6:
        z_over_xz = 1.0 - 0.5 * rho * z + (1.0 / 12.0) * (2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) * (z**2)
    else:
        radicand = 1.0 - 2.0 * rho * z + z**2
        if radicand < 0.0:
            radicand = 0.0
        num_arg = math.sqrt(radicand) + z - rho
        if num_arg <= 0.0:
            num_arg = 1e-12
        xz = math.log(num_arg / (1.0 - rho))
        z_over_xz = z / xz

    t_term = (
        ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (alpha**2 / ((fwd * strike) ** (1.0 - beta)))
        + (rho * beta * nu * alpha) / (4.0 * denom_base)
        + ((2.0 - 3.0 * rho**2) / 24.0) * (nu**2)
    )

    vol = (alpha / denom) * z_over_xz * (1.0 + t_term * texp)
    return float(vol)


def calibrate_sabr(
    fwd: float,
    texp: float,
    strikes: list[float],
    market_vols: list[float],
    fixed_beta: float = 0.5,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Calibrate (alpha, rho, nu) to market implied volatilities with beta fixed."""
    atm_vol = market_vols[len(market_vols) // 2]
    # Initial guess: alpha approx atm_vol * fwd^(1-beta)
    init_alpha = atm_vol * (fwd ** (1.0 - fixed_beta))
    init_params = [init_alpha, -0.2, 0.4]

    def objective(params: list[float]) -> float:
        a, r, n = params
        if a <= 1e-5 or n <= 1e-5 or r <= -0.999 or r >= 0.999:
            return 1e6
        err = 0.0
        for k, v_mkt in zip(strikes, market_vols, strict=False):
            try:
                v_model = sabr_black_vol(fwd, k, texp, a, fixed_beta, r, n)
                err += (v_model - v_mkt) ** 2
            except Exception:
                return 1e6
        return err

    bounds = [(1e-4, 2.0), (-0.99, 0.99), (1e-4, 3.0)]
    res = minimize(objective, init_params, bounds=bounds, method="L-BFGS-B")
    return float(res.x[0]), float(res.x[1]), float(res.x[2])

4Where the Canon Breaks: Extreme Strike Asymptotics & Negative Densities

Despite its ubiquitous industry status, the original Hagan et al. asymptotic expansion contains mathematical flaws in extreme tails (the "wing problem"):

  • Perturbative Failure in the Wings: The formula is derived under the assumption that \( u^2 T \ll 1\) and \(|f - K| \ll 1\). For deep out-of-the-money strikes (\(K \ll f\) or \(K \gg f\)) or long expiries, the expansion breaks down.
  • Negative Risk-Neutral Density: Applying the Breeden-Litzenberger relation \(q(K) = \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\) to the Hagan formula often produces regions where \(q(K) < 0\) in the deep out-of-the-money put tail, implying negative state probabilities and butterfly arbitrage.
  • Arbitrage Bounds Violation: In the right tail (\(K \to \infty\)), call prices can fail to decay monotonically or violate Lee's moment formulas, requiring asymptotic wing-blending or numerical PDE solutions in production engines.

5Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: All four SABR parameters (\(\alpha, \beta, \rho, u\)) should be fitted simultaneously to a single market swaption smile.

    Correction: Simulating joint calibration of \(\beta\) and \( \rho\) on a single smile produces severe over-fitting and parameter instability due to their strong numerical collinearity. \(\beta\) must be chosen economically based on the historical rate-volatility relation or market convention, leaving only \((\alpha, \rho, u)\) to be calibrated to option quotes.

  • Misconception: The Hagan SABR formula prices options directly in dollar units.

    Correction: The Hagan formula outputs an implied volatility (\(\sigma_{\text{B}}\) or \(\sigma_N\)), not a dollar price. That volatility must be fed into the corresponding Black-76 or Bachelier pricing formula alongside the swap annuity numéraire to obtain the present value of the contract.

  • Misconception: The SABR model is a full term-structure model capable of pricing Bermudan swaptions.

    Correction: SABR is a single-rate option model designed for European options. It does not specify correlation between rates across different forward tenors or the joint evolution of the entire yield curve. Pricing path-dependent or Bermudan derivatives requires embedding SABR into a multi-factor Libor Market Model (LMM-SABR) or Cheyette framework.

6Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 5.1 (SABR ATM Implied Volatility): Let \(f = 4.00\%\), \(\alpha = 0.038\), \(\beta = 0.50\), \( \rho = -0.25\), \( u = 0.35\), and expiry \(T = 2.0\) years. Compute the leading-order at-the-money Black-76 implied volatility \(\sigma_{\text{ATM}}\).

Solution

Step 1: Compute baseline factor \(\frac{\alpha}{f^{1-\beta}}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} f^{1-\beta} &= (0.04)^{0.5} = 0.20 \\ \frac{\alpha}{f^{1-\beta}} &= \frac{0.038}{0.20} = 0.1900 \quad (19.00\%) \end{aligned} \]

Step 2: Evaluate the three second-order correction terms in \([\dots]\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \gamma_1 &= \frac{(1-0.5)^2}{24} \frac{(0.038)^2}{(0.04)^1} = \frac{0.25}{24} \frac{0.001444}{0.04} = 0.010417 \times 0.03610 = 0.000376 \\ \gamma_2 &= \frac{(-0.25)(0.5)(0.35)(0.038)}{4 (0.20)} = \frac{-0.0016625}{0.80} = -0.002078 \\ \gamma_3 &= \frac{2 - 3(-0.25)^2}{24} (0.35)^2 = \frac{2 - 0.1875}{24} \times 0.1225 = 0.075521 \times 0.1225 = 0.009251 \\ \text{Sum} &= \gamma_1 + \gamma_2 + \gamma_3 = 0.000376 - 0.002078 + 0.009251 = 0.007549 \end{aligned} \]

Step 3: Multiply by \(T = 2.0\): \(\sigma_{\text{ATM}} = 0.1900 \times [1 + 0.007549 \times 2.0] = 0.1900 \times 1.015098 = 0.19287\) (\(19.29\%\)).

Problem 5.2 (Moneyness Variable Calculation): For a 1-year swaption with \(f = 3.00\%\), strike \(K = 4.00\%\), \(\beta = 0.50\), \(\alpha = 0.030\), and \( u = 0.40\), calculate the dimensionless variable \(z\) in the Hagan formula.

Solution

Compute each term in \(z = \frac{\nu}{\alpha} (f K)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}} \ln(f / K)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\nu}{\alpha} &= \frac{0.40}{0.030} = 13.3333 \\ f K &= 0.03 \times 0.04 = 0.0012 \\ (f K)^{\frac{1-0.5}{2}} &= (0.0012)^{0.25} = 0.186121 \\ \ln(f / K) &= \ln(0.03 / 0.04) = \ln(0.75) = -0.287682 \end{aligned} \]

Multiplying these components:

\[ \begin{aligned} z &= 13.3333 \times 0.186121 \times (-0.287682) = -0.7139 \end{aligned} \]

Problem 5.3 (ATM Volatility Sensitivity to Alpha): Prove that to leading order, \(\frac{\partial \sigma_{\text{ATM}}}{\partial \alpha} = f^{\beta - 1}\), and compute the change in ATM implied volatility for a \(\Delta \alpha = 0.002\) shift when \(f = 4.00\%\) and \(\beta = 0.50\).

Solution

From the leading-order expansion \(\sigma_{\text{ATM}} \approx \alpha f^{\beta - 1}\), differentiating with respect to \(\alpha\) yields \(\frac{\partial \sigma_{\text{ATM}}}{\partial \alpha} = f^{\beta - 1}\). With \(f = 0.04\) and \(\beta = 0.5\), \(f^{0.5 - 1} = (0.04)^{-0.5} = \frac{1}{0.20} = 5.0\). For \(\Delta \alpha = 0.002\), the change in implied volatility is \(\Delta \sigma = 5.0 \times 0.002 = 0.0100\) or \(1.00\%\) (100 bps of Black volatility).

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 5.4 (Derivation of the x(z) Function): Prove that the transformation \(x(z) = \ln\left( \frac{\sqrt{1 - 2 \rho z + z^2} + z - \rho}{1 - \rho} \right)\) is the exact integral of the hyperbolic distance metric on the Poincaré half-plane representation of SABR.

Solution

Under geometric Riemannian geometry, the geodesic distance between the current state and strike across the SABR manifold satisfies the integral \(x(z) = \int_0^z \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 2 \rho s + s^2}} ds\). Using standard trigonometric/hyperbolic substitution \(s - \rho = \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \sinh u\), the integrand becomes \(du\). Integrating from \(0\) to \(z\) yields \(\ln\left(\frac{z - \rho + \sqrt{1 - 2 \rho z + z^2}}{1 - \rho} \right)\). This proves that \(x(z)\) is the exact geodesic distance governing the heat kernel expansion.

Problem 5.5 (At-the-Money Limit of z / x(z)): Prove using L'Hôpital's rule that \(\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z}{x(z)} = 1\) and determine the first-order derivative \(\left. \frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{z}{x(z)} \right) \right|_{z=0}\).

Solution

Since \(x(0) = \ln\left(\frac{1- \rho}{1- \rho} \right) = 0\), the limit is indeterminate of form \(0/0\). Differentiating \(x(z)\): \(x'(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 2 \rho z + z^2}}\). At \(z = 0\), \(x'(0) = 1\). Thus \(\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z}{x(z)} = \frac{1}{x'(0)} = 1\). Expanding in Taylor series: \(x'(z) = (1 - 2 \rho z + z^2)^{-1/2} = 1 + \rho z + \mathcal{O}(z^2)\). Integrating gives \(x(z) = z + \frac{1}{2} \rho z^2 + \mathcal{O}(z^3)\). Inverting yields \(\frac{z}{x(z)} = 1 - \frac{1}{2} \rho z + \mathcal{O}(z^2)\). The first derivative at \(z = 0\) is \(-\frac{1}{2} \rho\).

Problem 5.6 (CEV Limiting Case of SABR): Prove that when vol-of-vol \( u \to 0\), the SABR implied volatility formula reduces identically to the classical Constant Elasticity of Variance (CEV) implied volatility formula.

Solution

As \( u \to 0\), \(z = \frac{\nu}{\alpha}(fK)^{(1-\beta)/2}\ln(f/K) \to 0\). From Problem 5.5, \(\frac{z}{x(z)} \to 1\). In the time bracket, all terms containing \( u\) vanish (\(\gamma_2 \to 0, \gamma_3 \to 0\)). The formula reduces to \(\sigma_{\text{B}}(K, f) = \frac{\alpha}{(fK)^{(1-\beta)/2}[1 + \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\ln^2(f/K)]} [1 + \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\frac{\alpha^2}{(fK)^{1-\beta}}T]\), which matches the singular perturbation expansion of the pure CEV diffusion \(dF = \alpha F^\beta dW\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 5.7 (Empirical Skew Shift During Market Panics): During market flight-to-safety episodes (e.g. October 2008 or March 2023), US interest-rate swaption desks observed calibrated SABR \( \rho\) shifting from \(-0.25\) to \(-0.65\). Explain the macro-financial mechanism causing swaption skews to tilt more negatively during banking stress.

Solution

During banking panics, investors rush into safe-haven government bonds, driving swap rates sharply down. Concurrently, emergency demand for downside duration protection creates panic buying of out-of-the-money receiver swaptions, sending low-strike implied volatilities soaring. This intense negative covariation between rate drops and volatility spikes drives the empirical parameter \( \rho\) deeply negative, steepening the left wing of the swaption smile.

Problem 5.8 (Fixing Beta Across Currency Regimes): Examine why Japanese yen (JPY) and Swiss franc (CHF) swaption desks standardized on \(\beta = 0.0\) (Normal SABR) while US dollar desks retained \(\beta = 0.50\). Document the operational failure that occurs when calibrating \(\beta = 0.50\) to negative rates.

Solution

In JPY and CHF markets, policy rates spent years below zero. If \(\beta > 0\), the rate term \(f^\beta\) requires taking fractional powers of negative numbers (e.g. \((-0.0025)^{0.5}\)), which is mathematically non-real and crashes pricing algorithms. Setting \(\beta = 0.0\) transforms the driftless rate equation into arithmetic Brownian motion \(dF = \alpha dW\), enabling unbroken calculation across negative, zero, and positive strikes.

Problem 5.9 (Vol-of-Vol Term Structure): In swaption markets, calibrated \( u\) is typically very high for 1-month and 3-month expiries (\( u \approx 0.80 - 1.20\)) and decays monotonically toward \( u \approx 0.20\) for 10-year and 30-year expiries. Explain the structural economic rationale for decaying vol-of-vol.

Solution

Near-term implied volatility is subject to discrete, high-frequency shocks from macroeconomic data releases (CPI, payrolls) and central bank policy announcements, creating extreme short-term volatility variance. Over long horizons, volatility mean-reverts toward its long-run ergodic mean, dampening the variance of integrated variance. By the central limit theorem, multi-year rate distributions become smoother, requiring lower vol-of-vol \( u\).

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 5.10 (The Hagan Wing Problem and Butterfly Arbitrage): Explain how the Breeden-Litzenberger relation detects butterfly arbitrage in the deep wings of the Hagan SABR formula, and describe the Ballotta-Bonfiglioli or PDE cure.

Solution

A butterfly spread consists of long calls at \(K-\Delta\), short two calls at \(K\), and long a call at \(K+\Delta\). In absence of arbitrage, the payoff is non-negative, requiring \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} \ge 0\) everywhere. For deep OTM strikes or high \( u^2 T\), the asymptotic Hagan expansion violates this condition, yielding negative implied probability densities. To eliminate butterfly arbitrage, modern trading desks solve the forward Kolmogorov PDE numerically or graft asymptotic arbitrage-free wing splines onto the core smile.

Problem 5.11 (Normal vs Lognormal SABR Expansion Accuracy): Compare the accuracy of the Hagan lognormal expansion versus the Hagan normal expansion when pricing near-the-money swaptions in a high-rate regime (\(f = 5.50\%\)) versus a zero-rate regime (\(f = 0.05\%\)).

Solution

At \(f = 5.50\%\), rates are far from the zero bound, and lognormal SABR provides accurate asymptotic fits with error under 0.1 bps. However, as \(f \to 0\), the lognormal expansion denominator \((fK)^{(1-\beta)/2}\) becomes singular, and \(\ln(f/K)\) exhibits numerical instability. Normal SABR (\(\beta = 0\)) maintains completely uniform convergence across zero, displaying zero singularity and superior error control in low-rate regimes.

Problem 5.12 (Free Boundary SABR at Zero): When \(\beta < 1\), the rate process \(dF = \alpha F^\beta dW\) can reach zero with positive probability. Formulate the boundary conditions (absorbing vs reflecting) at \(F = 0\) and analyze their impact on European swaption prices.

Solution

Feller's boundary classification shows that for \(0 < \beta < 1/2\), the origin is an regular boundary requiring an explicit boundary condition. Under an absorbing boundary condition, rates hitting zero stay at zero forever, extinguishing option volatility. Under a reflecting boundary, rates bounce back into positive territory. An absorbing boundary produces lower option prices and a steeper downward smile near zero compared to a reflecting boundary, with market quotes generally aligning closer to reflection.

8Retrieval Practice

  1. What does the acronym SABR stand for?

    Options

    SABR represents the parameters of the Hagan et al. (2002) model: Stochastic Alpha (volatility), Beta (CEV exponent), and Rho (correlation).

  2. How does parameter rho affect the SABR implied volatility smile?

    Options

    Correlation rho dictates whether the smile slopes downwards (rho < 0) or upwards (rho > 0).

  3. What aspect of the smile does parameter nu control?

    Options

    Volatility of volatility nu controls smile curvature; higher nu increases implied volatility in both out-of-the-money wings.

  4. Why is beta typically fixed a priori rather than fitted to the smile?

    Options

    Because changing beta or rho produces nearly identical changes in smile slope, calibrating both simultaneously causes extreme parameter instability.

  5. What value of beta corresponds to the Bachelier normal swaption model?

    Options

    When beta = 0, F^beta = 1, reducing the rate diffusion to arithmetic normal Brownian motion.

  6. What value of beta is the standard market convention for US dollar swaptions?

    Options

    USD swaption markets conventionally set beta = 0.50, balancing normal and lognormal empirical properties.

  7. What mathematical pathology can occur in deep out-of-the-money strikes under the Hagan formula?

    Options

    The asymptotic approximation can yield negative second derivatives d^2 C / dK^2 in deep wings, admitting butterfly arbitrage.

  8. What output does the Hagan et al. formula produce directly?

    Options

    The Hagan formula computes implied volatility, which must then be converted to a dollar price using standard option pricers.

  9. What limit does z / x(z) approach as strike K approaches the forward rate f?

    Options

    By L'Hôpital's rule, z / x(z) converges smoothly to 1 as z approaches 0 at the money.

  10. Why does vol-of-vol nu decline with option expiry in market swaption cubes?

    Options

    Mean-reversion smooths out short-term variance spikes over multi-year periods, causing long-dated vol-of-vol to decay.
