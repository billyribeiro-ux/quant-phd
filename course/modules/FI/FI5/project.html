Inflation-Expectations Decomposition

Build an operational inflation-linked analytics engine in decomposition.py to decompose breakeven inflation into expected inflation, the inflation risk premium, and the TIPS liquidity premium, and author an institutional research memo on the TIPS-Treasury puzzle.

The ladder

The project follows the mandatory four-rung curriculum ladder. Rungs 1 and 2 can be skipped if you achieved a score of 0.80 or higher on the module hub diagnostic and answered both skill checks correctly. Rung 3 is required for all students and is graded by both autograder and rubric.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Lab 1 demonstrates a fully solved end-to-end inflation analytics pipeline: TIPS cash flow construction with deflation floor, synthetic replication, puzzle basis, and tripartite breakeven decomposition.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Lab 2 provides a completion problem with key indexation and tripartite decomposition lines left as blanks, graded instantly by Python expected output.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The graded deliverable: implement decomposition.py to satisfy the autograder suite (tested across 50 random market seeds) and write an institutional diagnostic memo graded by rubric against calibrated exemplars.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Open-ended portfolio extensions: estimate a multi-factor no-arbitrage affine term structure model with inflation survey anchors (D'Amico, Kim & Wei 2018), or backtest a relative-value basis trading strategy on the TIPS-Treasury puzzle with dynamic repo haircut constraints.

Lab 1 · Worked Example: Inflation Expectations Decomposition Pipeline

Executing the end-to-end pipeline on a 10-year market state (nominal yield 4.25%, TIPS real yield 1.95%, survey expected inflation 2.05%, TIPS liquidity premium 20.0 bps) calculates a breakeven inflation rate of 230.0 bps, an inflation risk premium of 45.0 bps, and a Fleckenstein puzzle basis of $0.817 per $100 par (9.6 bps implied yield basis).

"""Lab FI5-project-1: Inflation-expectations decomposition worked example (Rung 1).

A fully worked implementation of the inflation-expectations decomposition pipeline.
Analyzes TIPS mechanics, computes breakevens, replicates synthetic Treasuries,
measures the Fleckenstein puzzle basis, and decomposes breakevens into expected inflation,
the inflation risk premium, and the TIPS liquidity premium.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def run_pipeline(
    nominal_yield: float,
    real_yield: float,
    survey_inflation: float,
    liquidity_proxy: float,
    beta_liq: float,
    base_cpi: float,
    ref_cpi: float,
    real_coupon: float,
    maturity_years: int,
    cash_price: float,
    swap_rate: float,
    mod_duration: float,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Execute end-to-end inflation expectations decomposition pipeline."""
    # 1. Breakeven yield
    bei = nominal_yield - real_yield

    # 2. TIPS cash flows with deflation floor
    index_ratio = ref_cpi / base_cpi
    face = 100.0
    adjusted_coupon = face * (real_coupon / 2.0) * index_ratio
    adjusted_principal = face * max(1.0, index_ratio)
    floor_benefit = face * max(0.0, 1.0 - index_ratio)

    # 3. Synthetic Treasury replication & puzzle basis
    synth_yield = (1.0 + real_yield) * (1.0 + swap_rate) - 1.0
    # Approximate synthetic price via yield difference from nominal
    synth_price = cash_price * (1.0 - mod_duration * (synth_yield - nominal_yield))
    price_basis = cash_price - synth_price
    yield_basis_bps = (price_basis / (cash_price * mod_duration)) * 10_000.0

    # 4. Tripartite breakeven decomposition
    tlp = beta_liq * liquidity_proxy
    irp = bei - survey_inflation + tlp

    return {
        "bei_bps": bei * 10_000.0,
        "index_ratio": index_ratio,
        "adjusted_coupon": adjusted_coupon,
        "adjusted_principal": adjusted_principal,
        "floor_benefit": floor_benefit,
        "synth_yield": synth_yield,
        "price_basis": price_basis,
        "yield_basis_bps": yield_basis_bps,
        "expected_inflation_bps": survey_inflation * 10_000.0,
        "tlp_bps": tlp * 10_000.0,
        "irp_bps": irp * 10_000.0,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # Stylized inputs for 10-year maturity
    res = run_pipeline(
        nominal_yield=0.0425,
        real_yield=0.0195,
        survey_inflation=0.0205,
        liquidity_proxy=0.0020,
        beta_liq=1.0,
        base_cpi=260.00,
        ref_cpi=299.00,
        real_coupon=0.0150,
        maturity_years=10,
        cash_price=101.50,
        swap_rate=0.0235,
        mod_duration=8.40,
    )

    print("Inflation Expectations Decomposition Pipeline (Worked Example):")
    print("=" * 68)
    print(f"  Breakeven Inflation (BEI):         {res['bei_bps']:>6.1f} bps")
    print(f"  TIPS Index Ratio:                  {res['index_ratio']:>8.4f}")
    print(f"  Adjusted Semiannual Coupon:       ${res['adjusted_coupon']:>6.2f} per $100 par")
    p_adj = res["adjusted_principal"]
    fl_ben = res["floor_benefit"]
    print(f"  Adjusted Principal (Maturity):    ${p_adj:>6.2f} (Floor: ${fl_ben:.2f})")
    print(f"  Synthetic Treasury Yield:          {res['synth_yield']:.2%}")
    print(f"  Puzzle Mispricing Price Basis:    ${res['price_basis']:>6.3f} per $100 par")
    print(f"  Puzzle Implied Yield Basis:        {res['yield_basis_bps']:>6.1f} bps")
    print("-" * 68)
    print("  Tripartite Decomposition of Breakeven:")
    print(f"    Expected Inflation E[pi]:        {res['expected_inflation_bps']:>6.1f} bps")
    print(f"    TIPS Liquidity Premium (TLP):    {res['tlp_bps']:>6.1f} bps")
    print(f"    Inflation Risk Premium (IRP):    {res['irp_bps']:>6.1f} bps")
    print(
        f"    Identity Check: E[pi] + IRP - TLP = "
        f"{res['expected_inflation_bps'] + res['irp_bps'] - res['tlp_bps']:.1f} bps"
    )
    print("=" * 68)

Specification

Context

You are a quantitative fixed-income researcher and macro portfolio strategist at an institutional asset manager. Central banks, corporate treasurers, and sovereign wealth funds rely on market-implied breakeven inflation rates to evaluate inflation expectations and term structures. However, market breakevens are contaminated by both time-varying inflation risk premia and persistent TIPS liquidity frictions. You must build an automated analytical library in decomposition.py that implements the tripartite decomposition of breakevens and models the synthetic Treasury replication package behind the TIPS-Treasury puzzle. Concurrently, you must author an institutional research memo diagnosing a historical market dislocation episode (the 2008 Lehman collapse, the 2017 secular stagnation regime, or the 2022 post-pandemic inflation shock).

Inputs

The autograder supplies an instance dictionary generated deterministically from an integer seed via make_instance(seed: int) in instance.py. The dictionary contains:

  • nominal_yield: float, annualized nominal Treasury yield (decimal).
  • real_yield: float, annualized TIPS real yield (decimal).
  • survey_inflation: float, survey-based expected inflation (decimal).
  • tips_liquidity_proxy: float, observable liquidity penalty proxy (decimal).
  • beta_liquidity: float, regression sensitivity factor for liquidity proxy.
  • maturity_years: int, maturity in years (5, 10, 20, or 30).
  • real_coupon_rate: float, annual real coupon rate (decimal).
  • base_cpi: float, base CPI level for indexation.
  • ref_cpi: float, reference CPI level at calculation date.
  • face_value: float, par value (default 100.0).
  • mod_duration: float, modified duration of the bond in years.
  • swap_rate: float, zero-coupon inflation swap rate (decimal).
  • cash_nominal_price: float, clean market price of cash nominal Treasury.
Required outputs

A module file decomposition.py exposing five core functions with strict type annotations:

  • breakeven_yield(...): computes the nominal-real yield spread.
  • tips_cash_flows(...): computes index ratio, adjusted coupon, and deflation-protected principal.
  • replicate_synthetic_treasury(...): computes the exact synthetic nominal Treasury yield under geometric Fisher compounding.
  • fleckenstein_puzzle_basis(...): computes the price basis and implied yield basis in basis points.
  • decompose_breakeven(...): performs the tripartite decomposition into BEI, expected inflation, TIPS liquidity premium, and inflation risk premium.

An institutional research memo file memo.md (approximately 1,000–1,500 words) diagnosing the empirical decomposition of breakevens and the TIPS-Treasury puzzle during a historical market regime.

Constraints

Standard library only. All monetary values are computed in exact precision. The module must be strictly typed and pass mypy --strict without warnings. Non-positive CPI levels or face values must raise ValueError with clear error messages. The module must not mutate input arguments.

Grading

Both grading modes apply to this project:

  • Autograder: The implementation in decomposition.py is tested by grader/test_project.py across 50 random market seeds (0 to 49). It verifies breakeven identities, statutory deflation floor guarantees, Fisher synthetic replication, and tripartite decomposition invariants.
  • Rubric: The institutional research memo memo.md is evaluated by rubric across 5 performance criteria, each with 4 observable achievement levels.

Two Calibrated Exemplars

Exemplar A (Level 4 · Distinguished Desk Memo)

To: Asset Allocation Committee & Chief Investment Officer
From: Senior Fixed Income Quantitative Strategist
Date: November 14, 2008
Subject: Quantitative Diagnosis of the Breakeven Inflation Collapse and TIPS-Treasury Mispricing Dislocation

1. Executive Summary: Between September 15 and November 10, 2008, 10-year US breakeven inflation collapsed precipitously from 2.15% to an unprecedented low of 0.45%, while 5-year breakevens briefly traded below zero (-0.12%). Naive interpretations by financial media suggested market participants were pricing an imminent and devastating Great Depression-style deflation. We demonstrate that this interpretation is fundamentally erroneous. Utilizing the tripartite decomposition framework of D'Amico, Kim, and Wei (2018) (D'Amico, Kim & Wei 2018), we show that long-term survey-expected inflation remained firmly anchored at 1.75% to 1.90%. Instead, the breakeven collapse was driven almost entirely by an unprecedented explosion in the TIPS liquidity premium to 185–220 basis points, compounded by a $4.70 per $100 par overpricing of cash nominal Treasuries relative to synthetic inflation-swapped TIPS packages (Fleckenstein, Longstaff & Lustig 2014, Table 1).

2. Tripartite Breakeven Decomposition: The fundamental no-arbitrage identity links nominal yields, real yields, inflation expectations, and risk premia:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{BEI}_t^{(10)} &= y_{N,t}^{(10)} - y_{R,t}^{(10)} \\ &= \mathbb{E}_t\left[\pi_{t,t+10}\right] + \text{IRP}_t^{(10)} - \text{TLP}_t^{(10)} \end{aligned} \]

In October 2008, the 10-year nominal Treasury yield fell to 3.80% in an aggressive flight-to-safety, while the 10-year TIPS yield surged to 3.35%, driven by frantic liquidation by leveraged hedge funds facing prime-broker margin calls. While expected inflation \(\mathbb{E}[\pi]\) remained near 1.75% and the inflation risk premium \(\text{IRP}\) stood at +55 bps, the TIPS liquidity premium \(\text{TLP}\) exploded from its historical average of 15 bps to 185 bps. Because \(\text{TLP}\) enters with a negative sign, this 170-basis-point liquidity shock mechanically compressed breakeven inflation to 45 bps.

3. The TIPS-Treasury Puzzle and Limits to Arbitrage: Simultaneously, the Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) synthetic Treasury basis widened to historical extremes. A synthetic 10-year nominal Treasury replicated by bundling 10-year TIPS with zero-coupon inflation swaps traded at $98.80 per $100 par, while actual cash 10-year Treasuries traded at $103.50. Under textbook no-arbitrage, an arbitrageur would purchase the synthetic package and short the cash Treasury, harvesting a 55.4 bps annualized yield premium. However, this arbitrage was completely unviable for institutional capital due to three binding frictions: (1) primary dealers faced acute balance-sheet exhaustion under regulatory leverage constraints; (2) bilateral repo lenders raised TIPS collateral haircuts from 2% to over 8%; and (3) cash nominal Treasuries were on special in the repo market, imposing a 25–40 bps financing drag to maintain the short leg.

4. Strategic Recommendations: The current breakeven level represents an extraordinary relative-value buying opportunity for unconstrained, unlevered capital with a 3- to 5-year investment horizon. We recommend establishing an overweight position in 10-year TIPS funded by short nominal Treasuries, locking in an unhedged liquidity rebound of 150+ basis points as market plumbing normalizes.

Exemplar B (Level 2 · Developing Desk Memo)

To: Investment Committee
From: Junior Fixed Income Analyst
Date: November 2008
Subject: Note on Low TIPS Breakevens

In the last few months, inflation expectations have dropped sharply. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate fell below 1% and is currently around 0.50%, which shows that the bond market is worried about a severe deflation like Japan or the 1930s. TIPS yields went way up because investors panicked after Lehman Brothers went bankrupt and wanted cash instead of inflation bonds. Also, TIPS bonds are cheaper than regular Treasuries if you swap them with inflation swaps, which is a puzzle that shouldn't happen in efficient markets. We think inflation will eventually come back once the government prints money, so buying TIPS makes sense.

Exemplar B Critique: This memo fails on observable criteria. It treats breakeven inflation naively as pure expected inflation, failing to state the mathematical tripartite identity \(\text{BEI} = \mathbb{E}[\pi] + \text{IRP} - \text{TLP}\). It provides no exact dollar basis for the synthetic replication puzzle, mentions no specific dates or numbers for yields and risk premia, ignores dealer balance-sheet and repo haircut frictions, and cites no peer-reviewed literature or verified registry keys.
