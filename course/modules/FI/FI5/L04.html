The TIPS-Treasury Puzzle & Limits to Arbitrage

In frictionless asset pricing, sovereign bonds sharing identical cash flows must command identical market prices. Yet Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) uncovered an extraordinary anomaly: synthetic nominal Treasuries replicated by combining TIPS with zero-coupon inflation swaps trade at persistent, multi-billion-dollar discounts to actual cash Treasuries. This persistent mispricing, known as the TIPS-Treasury puzzle, challenges the fundamental law of one price. Understanding how dealer balance-sheet constraints under the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR), repo haircuts, and collateral specialness prevent arbitrageurs from closing this basis is essential for quantitative relative-value fixed-income investing.

1Synthetic Treasury Replication and the Law of One Price

The Law of One Price states that two portfolios delivering identical cash flows in all future states of nature must sell for identical prices today. An investor can construct a synthetic nominal Treasury bond by bundling a physical inflation-indexed bond (TIPS) with a strip of Zero-Coupon Inflation Swaps (ZCIS).

Definition 4.1 (Synthetic Nominal Treasury Replication Portfolio)

Consider an \(n\)-year TIPS bond paying semiannual real coupons \(c_R / 2\) and par value \(F\) at maturity \(T\). The promised cash flow at coupon date \(t_k\) is \((F c_R / 2) \cdot (\text{CPI}_{t_k} / \text{CPI}_0)\). An investor constructs a synthetic nominal Treasury by:

  1. Purchasing 1 unit of the TIPS bond.
  2. Entering a strip of zero-coupon inflation swaps with maturities \(t_k\) and notionals \(N_k = F c_R / 2\) as the inflation payer (paying \(\text{CPI}_{t_k} / \text{CPI}_0 - 1\) and receiving fixed \((1 + S_{t_k})^{t_k} - 1\)).
  3. Entering a final ZCIS of maturity \(T\) and notional \(F\) paying terminal inflation and receiving fixed \((1 + S_T)^T - 1\).

This synthetic package converts every inflation-indexed coupon and principal payment into a guaranteed, fixed nominal cash flow: \(\text{CF}_{t_k}^{\text{synth}} = (F c_R / 2) \cdot (1 + S_{t_k})^{t_k}\) and \(\text{CF}_T^{\text{synth}} = (F c_R / 2) \cdot (1 + S_T)^T + F \cdot (1 + S_T)^T\).

Theorem 4.1 (Law of One Price for Synthetic Sovereign Debt)

Under frictionless markets with zero borrowing constraints, zero haircuts, zero transaction costs, and no counterparty risk, the price of a cash nominal Treasury \(P_{\text{nom}}\) and its maturity- and coupon-matched synthetic replication package \(P_{\text{synth}}\) must be strictly identical:

\[ P_{\text{nom}} = P_{\text{synth}} = P_{\text{TIPS}} + \sum_{k=1}^N \text{NPV}(\text{ZCIS}_k) \]

Because inflation swaps have zero net present value at inception (\(\text{NPV} = 0\)), the initial price of the synthetic bond is simply the market price of the TIPS bond.

Proof

Both the cash nominal Treasury and the synthetic package are issued by or backed by the US sovereign, pay cash flows on identical dates, and deliver deterministic nominal dollars. If \(P_{\text{nom}} > P_{\text{synth}}\), an arbitrageur sells short the cash Treasury and buys the synthetic package, locking in a riskless upfront profit with zero net cash flows at all future dates. If \(P_{\text{nom}} < P_{\text{synth}}\), the reverse trade locks in an arbitrage profit.

2The TIPS-Treasury Bond Puzzle

Despite the mathematical purity of Theorem 4.1, empirical data reveals a massive, persistent violation of the Law of One Price in the world's most liquid sovereign debt market.

Definition 4.2 (TIPS–Treasury Bond Puzzle)

The TIPS–Treasury bond puzzle is the empirical phenomenon documented by Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) whereby actual cash nominal Treasury bonds trade at a persistent, statistically significant, and economically large price premium relative to exactly replicated synthetic nominal Treasuries formed from TIPS and inflation swaps:

\[ \text{Basis}_t = P_{\text{nom}, t} - P_{\text{synth}, t} > 0 \]

Equivalently, in yield terms, synthetic nominal Treasuries trade at an excess yield (discount price) relative to cash nominal Treasuries: \(y_{\text{synth}, t} - y_{\text{nom}, t} > 0\).

Theorem 4.2 (Empirical Magnitude of the Puzzle Basis)

Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) analyze 29 matched pairs of nominal Treasuries and TIPS over the 2004–2009 period and establish that:

  1. The mispricing is almost uniformly in one direction: cash nominal Treasuries are systematically overpriced relative to synthetic packages on more than 99% of trading days.
  2. The average price mispricing is $1.00 to $4.00 per $100 par value, corresponding to an average yield basis of 20 to 55 basis points across tenors.
  3. During the height of the 2008 financial crisis, the basis widened to over $10 to $14 per $100 par on seasoned long-term bonds (exceeding 200 basis points in yield).
  4. The aggregate dollar mispricing across the US sovereign debt curve exceeded $56 billion at its peak in late 2008.

3Limits to Arbitrage: Balance-Sheet Constraints, Haircuts, and Specialness

Why did hedge funds, sovereign wealth funds, and primary dealers fail to arbitrage away a $50-billion pricing discrepancy in plain-vanilla sovereign bonds? In modern financial markets, arbitrage is not costless; it requires capital, leverage, and dealer balance-sheet capacity.

Theorem 4.3 (Net Arbitrage Hurdle Condition)

To exploit the puzzle, an arbitrageur must buy the synthetic package (long TIPS, pay fixed in inflation swaps) and short the cash nominal Treasury. Let \(\text{Basis}_{\text{bps}}\) denote the annualized gross basis spread. The trade is viable if and only if the gross spread exceeds the sum of repo financing costs, haircut drag, and regulatory balance-sheet charges:

\[ \text{Basis}_{\text{bps}} > \underbrace{(r_{\text{GC}} - r_{\text{special}})}_{\text{Special Repo Drag}} + \underbrace{h_{\text{TIPS}} \cdot r_{\text{borrow}}}_{\text{Haircut Financing Cost}} + \underbrace{\lambda_{\text{SLR}} \cdot \rho_E}_{\text{Dealer Balance-Sheet Hurdle}} \]

where \(r_{\text{GC}} - r_{\text{special}}\) is the repo fee to borrow the cash Treasury to short, \(h_{\text{TIPS}}\) is the haircut on financing the long TIPS, and \(\lambda_{\text{SLR}} \cdot \rho_E\) is the shadow cost of equity capital under the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (He, Nagel & Song 2022).

Proof

Shorting a specific cash Treasury requires borrowing it in the bilateral repo market. When market demand to short Treasuries is high, the bond trades "on special" at a financing rate \(r_{\text{special}} < r_{\text{GC}}\), forcing the short seller to pay a financing concession of \(r_{\text{GC}} - r_{\text{special}}\). Second, financing the long TIPS position via repo requires equity capital equal to the haircut \(h_{\text{TIPS}} \approx 2\%\) to \(8\%\). Third, under the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR), primary dealers face an unweighted capital requirement (\(\text{Tier 1} / \text{Total Assets} \ge 5\%\)). Warehousing gross long TIPS and gross short Treasuries inflates dealer balance sheets, demanding an equity return hurdle of \(\rho_E \approx 10\%\). If the hurdle exceeds the gross basis, capital cannot enter, and the mispricing persists.

Lab 1 · The TIPS-Treasury Puzzle: Synthetic Replication, Basis, and Arbitrage Limits

Constructing a synthetic 5-year nominal Treasury from TIPS (2.0% real coupon) and flat inflation swaps (2.40%) yields a synthetic package price of $100.986 per $100 par. When actual cash 5-year Treasuries trade at $102.500, the puzzle mispricing price basis is $1.514 per $100 par (33.2 bps implied yield basis). After factoring in repo specialness financing drag (15.0 bps) and dealer capital charges under SLR (25.0 bps), the net annualized arbitrage return is -10.5 bps, proving the basis cannot be profitably closed by constrained dealers.

"""Lab FI5-L04-1: The TIPS-Treasury puzzle: synthetic replication, basis, and arbitrage limits.

Standard library only. Constructs synthetic nominal Treasury bonds from TIPS and inflation swaps,
computes the Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) mispricing puzzle basis,
and evaluates financing and balance-sheet limits to arbitrage.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def price_synthetic_treasury(
    real_cash_flows: list[dict[str, float]],
    inflation_swap_rates: list[float],
    nominal_discount_factors: list[float],
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Price a synthetic nominal Treasury created by bundling TIPS with inflation swaps.

    For each payment period t:
    TIPS cash flow = CF_real * (CPI_t / CPI_0)
    Inflation swap exchanges (CPI_t / CPI_0) for (1 + S_t)^t
    Synthesized nominal cash flow = CF_real * (1 + S_t)^t
    Present value = sum synthesized nominal cash flow * DF_nominal_t
    """
    if len(real_cash_flows) != len(inflation_swap_rates) or len(real_cash_flows) != len(nominal_discount_factors):
        raise ValueError("Cash flows, swap rates, and discount factors must have identical length")

    pv_synthetic = 0.0
    total_nominal_cf = 0.0

    for cf_item, s_t, df_t in zip(real_cash_flows, inflation_swap_rates, nominal_discount_factors, strict=True):
        t = cf_item["time_years"]
        cf_real = cf_item["cf_real"]
        synth_cf_nominal = cf_real * ((1.0 + s_t) ** t)
        pv_synthetic += synth_cf_nominal * df_t
        total_nominal_cf += synth_cf_nominal

    return {
        "pv_synthetic": pv_synthetic,
        "total_nominal_cf": total_nominal_cf,
    }


def compute_puzzle_basis(
    cash_treasury_price: float,
    synthetic_treasury_price: float,
    modified_duration: float,
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Compute the Fleckenstein-Longstaff-Lustig TIPS-Treasury puzzle basis.

    Price basis (cents per $100 par): P_Treasury - P_Synthetic
    Yield basis approximation (basis points): [P_Treasury - P_Synthetic] / [P * ModDur] * 10,000
    """
    price_basis = cash_treasury_price - synthetic_treasury_price
    yield_basis_bps = (price_basis / (cash_treasury_price * modified_duration)) * 10_000.0

    return {
        "cash_treasury_price": cash_treasury_price,
        "synthetic_treasury_price": synthetic_treasury_price,
        "price_basis": price_basis,
        "yield_basis_bps": yield_basis_bps,
    }


def evaluate_arbitrage_viability(
    price_basis: float,
    cash_treasury_price: float,
    holding_period_years: float,
    repo_haircut: float,
    repo_financing_spread_bps: float,
    dealer_capital_hurdle_bps: float,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Evaluate whether the TIPS-Treasury mispricing basis can be profitably exploited.

    Net arbitrage return = Yield Basis - Repo Financing Spread - Dealer Capital Hurdle
    """
    gross_annual_carry_bps = (price_basis / cash_treasury_price) / holding_period_years * 10_000.0
    net_annual_carry_bps = gross_annual_carry_bps - repo_financing_spread_bps - dealer_capital_hurdle_bps

    is_profitable = net_annual_carry_bps > 0.0

    return {
        "gross_annual_carry_bps": gross_annual_carry_bps,
        "repo_financing_spread_bps": repo_financing_spread_bps,
        "dealer_capital_hurdle_bps": dealer_capital_hurdle_bps,
        "net_annual_carry_bps": net_annual_carry_bps,
        "is_profitable": is_profitable,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Synthetic 5-Year Nominal Treasury Bond Construction (SIMULATED)
    # Real coupon = 2.0% ($1.00 semiannual), par = $100
    times = [0.5 * k for k in range(1, 11)]
    real_cfs = []
    for t in times:
        cf = 1.0 if t < 5.0 else 101.0
        real_cfs.append({"time_years": t, "cf_real": cf})

    # Flat inflation swap rate = 2.40% across tenors
    swap_rates = [0.0240 for _ in times]

    # Nominal discount factors at 4.20% flat yield
    discount_factors = [(1.0 + 0.0420 / 2.0) ** (-2.0 * t) for t in times]

    synth_res = price_synthetic_treasury(real_cfs, swap_rates, discount_factors)
    synth_price = synth_res["pv_synthetic"]

    # 2. Compare against Cash Nominal Treasury
    # Stylized market price reflecting Fleckenstein et al. (2014) overpricing of nominals
    cash_price = 102.50  # Cash Treasury trades at a premium over synthetic package ($1.51 per $100 par)
    mod_duration = 4.45

    basis_res = compute_puzzle_basis(cash_price, synth_price, mod_duration)

    print("TIPS-Treasury Puzzle Replication (SIMULATED):")
    print(f"  Cash Nominal Treasury Price:       ${basis_res['cash_treasury_price']:.3f}")
    print(f"  Synthetic Treasury Package Price:  ${basis_res['synthetic_treasury_price']:.3f}")
    print(f"  Mispricing Price Basis:            ${basis_res['price_basis']:.3f} per $100 par")
    print(f"  Implied Yield Mispricing Basis:    {basis_res['yield_basis_bps']:.1f} bps")

    # 3. Arbitrage Limits Evaluation
    # Repo financing cost (special vs GC spread) = 15 bps
    # Dealer SLR capital hurdle rate = 25 bps
    arb_res = evaluate_arbitrage_viability(
        price_basis=basis_res["price_basis"],
        cash_treasury_price=cash_price,
        holding_period_years=5.0,
        repo_haircut=0.03,
        repo_financing_spread_bps=15.0,
        dealer_capital_hurdle_bps=25.0,
    )

    print("\nLimits to Arbitrage Friction Analysis:")
    print(f"  Gross Annualized Basis Spread:     {arb_res['gross_annual_carry_bps']:.1f} bps")
    print(f"  Repo Financing Drag (Special/GC):  {arb_res['repo_financing_spread_bps']:.1f} bps")
    print(f"  Dealer Capital Charge Hurdle (SLR):{arb_res['dealer_capital_hurdle_bps']:.1f} bps")
    print(f"  Net Annualized Arbitrage Return:   {arb_res['net_annual_carry_bps']:+.1f} bps")
    print(f"  Arbitrage Viable After Frictions:  {arb_res['is_profitable']}")


main()

The lab confirms the central empirical conclusion of Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014): the TIPS-Treasury puzzle persists not because market participants are unaware of it, but because institutional financing frictions and regulatory leverage constraints prevent arbitrage capital from deploying at scale.

Where the canon breaks

The classical theory of efficient markets posits that sovereign debt curves are linked by frictionless no-arbitrage. In practice, three real-world mechanics invalidate this paradigm:

  1. Slow-moving capital: Arbitrage capital is held by specialized fixed-income relative-value hedge funds and dealer desks. When systemic shocks occur, margin calls and investor redemptions force funds to delever, widening the basis rather than closing it.
  2. Asymmetric shorting friction: Shorting cash Treasuries requires borrowing collateral through primary dealers. When dealers are balance-sheet constrained, they widen repo haircut spreads, turning a theoretical arbitrage profit into a severe cash drain.
  3. Convenience yield asymmetry: Cash nominal Treasuries serve as pristine, universally accepted margin collateral across global financial plumbing (clearinghouses, central banks, triparty repo). TIPS cannot be reused as easily, conferring a distinct monetary convenience yield on nominal Treasuries that synthetic packages cannot replicate (He, Nagel & Song 2022).

Misconceptions

  • The price difference between cash nominal Treasuries and synthetic Treasuries reflects sovereign default risk.

    Both nominal Treasuries and TIPS are direct general obligations of the United States Treasury, backed by the full faith and credit of the US government. They share identical sovereign default risk. The puzzle basis stems entirely from market microstructure, liquidity, and dealer balance-sheet warehousing frictions.

  • A persistent arbitrage opportunity in sovereign bonds proves that quantitative models are flawed.

    The persistence of the basis proves that frictionless textbook models omit real-world constraints. As documented by Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014), once margin requirements, repo financing costs, and regulatory capital charges are incorporated, the apparent arbitrage profit disappears for market participants.

  • Buying TIPS and selling nominal Treasuries is a pure arbitrage trade that carries zero mark-to-market risk.

    The trade is exposed to severe mark-to-market and financing risk. If the basis widens further (as occurred between August and November 2008), the arbitrageur faces margin calls on the short Treasury leg and hair-cut expansions on the long TIPS leg, risking forced liquidation before maturity convergence.

Problem set

Problem M.1: A 10-year cash nominal Treasury trades at a clean price of $103.20 per $100 par. An exactly replicated synthetic nominal Treasury created from TIPS and zero-coupon inflation swaps trades at $101.40. Calculate the price basis and the implied yield mispricing basis assuming a modified duration of 8.5 years.

Solution

The price basis is \(P_{\text{nom}} - P_{\text{synth}} = \$103.20 - \$101.40 = \$1.80\) per $100 par. The implied yield mispricing basis is \((1.80 / (103.20 \times 8.5)) \times 10,000 = (1.80 / 877.20) \times 10,000 \approx 20.52\) basis points.

Problem M.2: An arbitrage fund considers entering a 5-year TIPS-Treasury relative-value trade with a gross basis of 35 basis points. Financing the short Treasury leg requires paying a special repo concession of 12 basis points, and repo haircut funding costs add 8 basis points. Calculate the net annualized carry spread before regulatory capital costs.

Solution

Net carry before regulatory charges equals \(\text{Gross Basis} - \text{Specialness} - \text{Haircut Cost} = 35 - 12 - 8 = 15.0\) basis points.

Problem M.3: In late 2008, the aggregate par value of outstanding TIPS was approximately $500 billion, and the average price mispricing basis across the curve reached $4.50 per $100 par. Calculate the total aggregate dollar mispricing across the TIPS market.

Solution

Total dollar mispricing equals \(\$500\text{B} \times (4.50 / 100) = \$22.5\) billion. Across all paired nominal and TIPS bonds, Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014) documented aggregate peak mispricing exceeding $56 billion.

Problem P.1: Prove that if an arbitrageur faces mark-to-market margin requirements with finite liquid capital \(W_0\), an arbitrarily wide basis can force premature liquidation even when default risk is zero.

Solution

Let the basis at inception be \(B_0 > 0\). The fund shorts 1 unit of cash Treasury at \(P_{\text{nom},0}\) and buys 1 unit of synthetic Treasury at \(P_{\text{synth},0} = P_{\text{nom},0} - B_0\). Initial liquid margin capital is \(W_0\). Suppose at time \(t < T\), liquidity shocks widen the basis to \(B_t = B_0 + \Delta B\). The mark-to-market loss on the position is \(-\Delta B\). The exchange and repo lender issue variation margin calls equal to \(\Delta B\). If \(\Delta B > W_0\), liquid capital is exhausted (\(W_t < 0\)), triggering mandatory liquidation regardless of guaranteed convergence at \(T\).

Problem P.2: Prove that under the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (\(\text{Tier 1} / \text{Total Assets} \ge \rho_{\text{SLR}}\)), an arbitrage trade that doubles gross balance-sheet assets requires an annualized hurdle return strictly proportional to dealer return on equity.

Solution

Let dealer leverage ratio be \(\text{SLR} = E / A \ge \rho_{\text{SLR}}\). Entering a relative-value trade with gross long asset \(A_{\text{long}}\) and gross repo financing adds \(2 A_{\text{long}}\) to total leverage exposure (TLE). To maintain compliance, the dealer must allocate equity capital \(\Delta E = 2 \rho_{\text{SLR}} A_{\text{long}}\). If the cost of dealer equity capital is \(r_E\), the annual hurdle cost per dollar of trade notional is \(\Delta E \cdot r_E / A_{\text{long}} = 2 \rho_{\text{SLR}} r_E\). For \(\rho_{\text{SLR}} = 5\%\) and \(r_E = 10\%\), the hurdle spread is \(2 \times 0.05 \times 0.10 = 0.010\) (100 bps).

Problem P.3: Prove that if cash nominal Treasuries possess a non-monetary convenience yield \(\delta_{\text{convenience}} > 0\) from collateral rehypothecation that synthetic packages lack, the equilibrium basis \(P_{\text{nom}} - P_{\text{synth}} > 0\) is consistent with competitive equilibrium.

Solution

Let investors maximize expected utility over wealth and collateral liquidity services. Holding physical nominal Treasuries yields flow liquidity benefit \(\delta_t\), modifying the pricing Euler equation to \(P_{\text{nom}, t} = \mathbb{E}_t [ M_{t+1} (P_{\text{nom}, t+1} + C_{t+1}) ] + \delta_t\). Because synthetic packages cannot be rehypothecated in general triparty repo or posted as pristine regulatory liquidity (HQLA), \(\delta_{\text{synth}} = 0\). In competitive equilibrium, \(P_{\text{nom}, t} - P_{\text{synth}, t} = \mathbb{E}_t [ \sum_{k=1}^T M_{t, t+k} \delta_{t+k} ] > 0\). The price premium reflects the capitalized present value of collateral convenience services.

Problem E.1: In Fleckenstein, Longstaff, and Lustig (2014), how did the puzzle basis correlate with systemic measures of financial market stress (such as repo spreads, VIX, and off-the-run Treasury spreads)?

Solution

Fleckenstein et al. (2014, Table 4 and Section IV) show that the puzzle basis correlates strongly and positively with measures of systemic financial stress: widening when the VIX spikes, when overnight repo spreads widen, when corporate bond CDS spreads expand, and when dealer aggregate balance-sheet leverage contracts. This empirical link demonstrates that the basis is driven by intermediary balance-sheet constraints.

Problem E.2: Why did the Federal Reserve's Large-Scale Asset Purchase (LSAP) programs in 2009–2011 reduce the TIPS-Treasury puzzle basis?

Solution

By purchasing over $1 trillion in Treasuries and hundreds of billions in TIPS directly from primary dealers, the Federal Reserve absorbed vast amounts of inventory from private balance sheets. This unburdened dealer balance-sheet capacity, injected aggregate central-bank reserves, lowered repo financing haircuts, and allowed relative-value arbitrageurs to narrow the synthetic mispricing basis.

Problem E.3: How does the TIPS-Treasury puzzle basis compare to the Treasury cash-futures basis during funding liquidity crises?

Solution

Both bases reflect dealer balance-sheet limits and margin frictions. In both cases, physical cash bonds trade at a discount to derivative-replicated alternatives when dealers are constrained (or cash Treasuries command a premium when investors scramble for specific collateral). However, while the futures basis converges every quarter at contract delivery, the TIPS-Treasury basis persists over multi-year horizons because TIPS cannot be delivered into nominal futures contracts.

Problem R.1: Formulate an optimal portfolio sizing rule for a relative-value basis fund operating under dynamic Value-at-Risk (VaR) constraints and procyclical repo haircuts.

Solution

Let fund wealth be \(W_t\). The fund chooses trade notional \(X_t\) to maximize expected return \(\mu_B X_t\) subject to a VaR constraint \(\alpha \sigma_B X_t \le W_t\) and a margin constraint \(h(VIX_t) X_t \le W_t\). Because margin haircuts \(h(VIX_t)\) increase sharply during volatility spikes (\(h' > 0\)), optimal position size is \(X_t^* = W_t / \max(\alpha \sigma_B, h(VIX_t))\). When volatility spikes, the margin constraint binds, forcing procyclical position liquidation and driving the basis wider.

Problem R.2: Evaluate whether sovereign debt managers should opportunistically alter the issuance ratio between TIPS and nominal Treasuries to exploit the puzzle basis.

Solution

Because cash nominal Treasuries systematically trade at a price premium relative to synthetic replication packages (nominal yields trade below synthetic yields), the US Treasury incurs higher financing costs when issuing TIPS relative to issuing nominal debt. Fleckenstein et al. (2014) calculate that issuing TIPS cost US taxpayers tens of billions of dollars in excess interest expense. Suspending or reducing TIPS issuance during severe basis dislocations would minimize sovereign borrowing costs.

Problem R.3: Design an empirical econometric test to differentiate between the collateral convenience yield hypothesis and the dealer balance-sheet capacity hypothesis for the TIPS-Treasury puzzle.

Solution

Run a time-series regression of the puzzle basis \(\text{Basis}_t\) on both (1) an unweighted dealer leverage constraint proxy (such as aggregate broker-dealer SLR surplus or CDS spread) and (2) collateral convenience proxies (such as Treasury general collateral repo spreads over OIS and T-bill convenience yields). A significant loading on dealer SLR variables holding collateral spreads constant identifies intermediary warehousing frictions as the dominant driver beyond pure moneyness.
