Inflation erodes the purchasing power of fixed nominal cash flows, exposing conventional bondholders to severe real capital losses during inflationary shocks. Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) eliminate purchasing-power risk by indexing principal and coupon payments directly to headline consumer prices. Understanding the 2.5-month indexation lag, reference CPI interpolation, semiannual cash flow adjustments, and the statutory deflation floor is essential for evaluating real interest rates, pricing inflation-linked derivatives, and executing sovereign relative-value curve strategies.

1Real versus Nominal Debt and TIPS Indenture Mechanics

A standard nominal sovereign bond promises fixed dollar coupons and principal at pre-specified dates. While default-free in legal tender, nominal bonds leave the holder exposed to inflation uncertainty. In contrast, an inflation-indexed bond is a debt contract whose principal balance and periodic coupon distributions are adjusted over time in direct proportion to a specified price index, providing guaranteed real purchasing power.

Definition 1.1 (Inflation-Indexed Bond and Index Ratio)

Let \(\text{CPI}_t\) denote the non-seasonally adjusted Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U). An inflation-indexed bond (specifically a US Treasury Inflation-Protected Security, or TIPS) is defined by a base index level \(\text{CPI}_{\text{base}}\) established at issuance and an annual real coupon rate \(c_{\text{real}}\). At any settlement date \(t\), the index ratio \(I_t\) is:

\[ I_t = \frac{\text{Ref CPI}_t}{\text{CPI}_{\text{base}}} \]

where \(\text{Ref CPI}_t\) is the reference CPI interpolated according to the Treasury's 2.5-month indexation convention.

Theorem 1.1 (Reference CPI Daily Interpolation)

Let settlement date \(t\) fall on day \(d\) of month \(m\), where month \(m\) contains \(D\) calendar days. Because the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes CPI with a lag, the Treasury defines the reference CPI by linear daily interpolation between the headline CPI of the third preceding month (\(m-3\)) and second preceding month (\(m-2\)):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Ref CPI}_t &= \text{CPI}_{m-3} \\ &\quad + \frac{d - 1}{D} \left(\text{CPI}_{m-2} - \text{CPI}_{m-3}\right) \end{aligned} \]
Proof

On the first day of month \(m\) (\(d=1\)), the interpolation weight \((d-1)/D = 0\), yielding \(\text{Ref CPI}_t = \text{CPI}_{m-3}\). On the first day of the subsequent month (\(m+1\), \(d=1\)), the formula evaluates to \(\text{CPI}_{(m+1)-3} = \text{CPI}_{m-2}\). For all intermediate days \(1 \le d \le D\), the expression is the unique linear interpolant on \([1, D+1)\) mapping day \(d\) continuously between \(\text{CPI}_{m-3}\) and \(\text{CPI}_{m-2}\), as stipulated in 31 CFR Part 356, Appendix B.

2Cash Flow Valuation and the Deflation Floor Option

TIPS pay semiannual coupons. Unlike Canadian or European capital-indexed bonds that adjust coupons while keeping principal unhedged against deflation, US TIPS feature a statutory deflation floor on principal redemption.

Theorem 1.2 (TIPS Cash Flow Indenture with Deflation Floor)

Let a TIPS bond have par value \(F\), annual real coupon rate \(c_{\text{real}}\), semiannual payment dates \(t_1, t_2, \dots, t_N = T\), and settlement index ratios \(I_{t_k}\). The promised cash flows are:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{CF}_{t_k} &= F \cdot \frac{c_{\text{real}}}{2} \cdot I_{t_k}, \quad k = 1, \dots, N-1 \\ \text{CF}_T &= F \cdot \frac{c_{\text{real}}}{2} \cdot I_T + F \cdot \max(1.0, I_T) \end{aligned} \]

The final principal redemption can be decomposed into an unfloored indexed principal plus a European deflation put option:

\[ F \cdot \max(1.0, I_T) = F \cdot I_T + F \cdot \max(0, 1.0 - I_T) \]
Proof

The identity \(\max(a, b) = b + \max(0, a - b)\) applied with \(a = 1.0\) and \(b = I_T\) establishes the decomposition. The term \(F \cdot \max(0, 1.0 - I_T)\) is isomorphic to the payoff of a European put option on the price level index ratio with strike \(K = 1.0\) expiring at maturity \(T\).

Example 1.1 (Deflation Floor Payout)

Consider a 5-year TIPS with \(F = \$1,000\) and \(c_{\text{real}} = 2.0\%\). Suppose cumulative deflation occurs such that at maturity \(I_T = 0.90\). The unfloored principal would be \(\$1,000 \times 0.90 = \$900\). The deflation floor triggers, ensuring principal redemption of \(\$1,000 \times \max(1.0, 0.90) = \$1,000\). The semiannual coupon at maturity is \(\$1,000 \times (0.02 / 2) \times 0.90 = \$9.00\). Total cash flow received at maturity is \(\$1,009.00\).

3Real Duration and Quoting Conventions

TIPS trade in secondary markets on real yield to maturity. The quoted real clean price represents the unadjusted price per 100 dollars of face value. Settlement cash paid (dirty price) scales by the index ratio:

\[ \text{Dirty Price} = (\text{Clean Price} + \text{Accrued Real Interest}) \times I_t \]
Definition 1.2 (Real Duration)

The real duration of a TIPS bond measures its percentage price sensitivity to a parallel shift in the real yield curve \(y_{\text{real}}\), holding the path of future inflation constant:

\[ D_{\text{real}} = -\frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y_{\text{real}}} \]

Because TIPS cash flows scale with inflation, their sensitivity to changes in expected inflation is negligible (ignoring the deflation floor option delta and indexation lag), leaving duration risk concentrated in real rate movements.

"""Lab FI5-L01-1: TIPS cash flow construction, indexation mechanics, and deflation floor.

Standard library only. Computes Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)
reference CPI interpolation, index ratio, inflation-adjusted coupon and principal
schedules under both inflation and deflation scenarios, and real duration metrics.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def interpolate_reference_cpi(
    cpi_m_minus_3: float,
    cpi_m_minus_2: float,
    day_of_month: int,
    days_in_month: int,
) -> float:
    """Compute interpolated reference CPI according to US Treasury TIPS indexation rules.

    The 2.5-month indexation lag interpolates between CPI from 3 months prior
    and CPI from 2 months prior:
    Ref_CPI(d) = CPI_{m-3} + [(d - 1) / D] * (CPI_{m-2} - CPI_{m-3})
    """
    if day_of_month < 1 or day_of_month > days_in_month:
        raise ValueError("day_of_month must be within [1, days_in_month]")
    if cpi_m_minus_3 <= 0 or cpi_m_minus_2 <= 0:
        raise ValueError("CPI values must be positive")
    alpha = (day_of_month - 1) / days_in_month
    return cpi_m_minus_3 + alpha * (cpi_m_minus_2 - cpi_m_minus_3)


def compute_tips_cash_flows(
    par_value: float,
    real_coupon_rate: float,
    maturity_years: float,
    index_ratios: list[float],
) -> list[dict[str, Any]]:
    """Generate semiannual TIPS coupon and principal cash flow schedule.

    Parameters:
        par_value: Base principal (e.g. 1000.00 USD).
        real_coupon_rate: Annual real coupon rate (decimal, e.g. 0.02 for 2.0%).
        maturity_years: Time to maturity in years (e.g. 5.0).
        index_ratios: List of index ratios (I_t) for each semiannual period.

    Returns:
        List of cash flow dictionaries containing period, time, index ratio,
        unadjusted coupon, adjusted coupon, principal, and total cash flow.
    """
    num_periods = round(maturity_years * 2)
    if len(index_ratios) != num_periods:
        raise ValueError(f"Expected {num_periods} index ratios, got {len(index_ratios)}")

    semi_coupon_rate = real_coupon_rate / 2.0
    schedule = []

    for t_idx, index_ratio in enumerate(index_ratios, start=1):
        time_years = t_idx * 0.5
        unadjusted_coupon = par_value * semi_coupon_rate
        adjusted_coupon = unadjusted_coupon * index_ratio
        principal_redemption = 0.0

        if t_idx == num_periods:
            # Deflation floor guarantees at least par redemption: max(1.0, index_ratio) * par
            redemption_ratio = max(1.0, index_ratio)
            principal_redemption = par_value * redemption_ratio

        total_cf = adjusted_coupon + principal_redemption
        schedule.append(
            {
                "period": t_idx,
                "time_years": time_years,
                "index_ratio": index_ratio,
                "adjusted_coupon": adjusted_coupon,
                "principal_redemption": principal_redemption,
                "total_cash_flow": total_cf,
            }
        )

    return schedule


def compute_real_duration(
    schedule: list[dict[str, Any]],
    real_yield_annual: float,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Compute TIPS present value, Macaulay real duration, and modified real duration.

    Discounting uses semiannual compounding: DF_t = (1 + y/2)^(-2t).
    """
    semi_yield = real_yield_annual / 2.0
    pv_total = 0.0
    weighted_time = 0.0

    for item in schedule:
        t = item["time_years"]
        cf = item["total_cash_flow"]
        df = (1.0 + semi_yield) ** (-2.0 * t)
        pv = cf * df
        pv_total += pv
        weighted_time += t * pv

    mac_duration = weighted_time / pv_total if pv_total > 0 else 0.0
    mod_duration = mac_duration / (1.0 + semi_yield)
    return pv_total, mac_duration, mod_duration

The lab verifies that the deflation floor is a one-sided guarantee. While inflation accrual raises coupons and principal symmetrically, deflation reduces coupons but cannot depress principal redemption below original face value.

Where the canon breaks

Textbook treatments describe TIPS as pure real zero-beta inflation hedges. In practice, three institutional frictions disrupt this theoretical property:

  1. The 2.5-month indexation lag: Because of publication delays in CPI-U, TIPS do not track contemporaneous price changes. A sudden surge in monthly inflation takes 2.5 months to reflect in the index ratio. During high-volatility inflation regimes, the short-horizon correlation between monthly TIPS returns and headline monthly CPI inflation is under 0.40.
  2. Seasoning and the deflation put wedge: Freshly issued TIPS trade at \(I_t \approx 1.0\), meaning their embedded deflation put option is at the money. Seasoned TIPS that have accumulated 20% inflation have an index ratio of 1.20, rendering their deflation put deeply out of the money. Consequently, on-the-run and off-the-run TIPS of similar duration do not trade at identical real yields.
  3. Taxation of phantom inflation accrual: Under US federal income tax rules, the inflation accretion on TIPS principal is taxed in the year it accrues, long before cash redemption at maturity. This creates a negative tax carry for taxable accounts during elevated inflation.

Misconceptions

  • TIPS coupon payments are fixed dollar amounts determined by the real coupon rate at issuance.

    TIPS coupon payments are variable dollar amounts. The real coupon rate \(c_{\text{real}}\) is fixed, but it is multiplied by the inflation-adjusted principal \(F \cdot I_t\). As cumulative inflation rises, periodic dollar coupon payments increase proportionately.

  • TIPS perfectly immunize an institutional portfolio against unexpected monthly inflation spikes.

    Due to the statutory 2.5-month indexation lag, TIPS prices reflect historical inflation with a two- to three-month delay. An immediate unexpected inflation shock affects nominal bond yields instantaneously but takes months to flow into TIPS principal adjustments.

  • If cumulative inflation over a TIPS bond's life is negative, the investor loses principal at maturity.

    The US TIPS indenture embeds an explicit deflation floor: at maturity, the Treasury redeems the bond at \(\max(1.0, I_T) \times \text{par}\). Investors are legally guaranteed to receive at least 100% of their initial nominal face value at maturity.

Problem set

Problem M.1: On September 15, the reference CPI for a TIPS bond is 305.25. The base CPI established at issuance is 265.00. Calculate the current index ratio to 5 decimal places.

Solution

The index ratio is defined as \(I_t = \text{Ref CPI}_t / \text{CPI}_{\text{base}} = 305.25 / 265.00 = 1.1518868 \approx 1.15189\). This indicates that consumer prices have accumulated 15.189% inflation since the base date.

Problem M.2: A 10-year TIPS with $10,000 par value has a real annual coupon rate of 1.75%. The settlement index ratio is 1.1200. What is the dollar amount of the upcoming semiannual coupon payment?

Solution

The semiannual coupon payment equals \(\text{Par} \times (c_{\text{real}} / 2) \times I_t = \$10,000 \times (0.0175 / 2) \times 1.1200 = \$10,000 \times 0.00875 \times 1.1200 = \$98.00\).

Problem M.3: A 5-year TIPS has a par value of $1,000. At maturity, cumulative deflation has reduced the index ratio to 0.9400. Calculate the final coupon payment (real coupon 2.50% annual) and the final principal redemption payment.

Solution

The final semiannual coupon is \(\$1,000 \times (0.025 / 2) \times 0.9400 = \$11.75\). The principal redemption is protected by the deflation floor: \(\$1,000 \times \max(1.0, 0.9400) = \$1,000.00\). Total cash flow received at maturity is \(\$1,011.75\).

Problem P.1: Prove that the deflation floor in a TIPS bond represents a non-negative convex option payoff with respect to the final index ratio \(I_T\).

Solution

The deflation floor benefit function is \(g(I_T) = \max(0, 1.0 - I_T)\). For any \(I_T \in \mathbb{R}\), \(g(I_T) \ge 0\). For any two values \(I_1, I_2\) and \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\), \(\max(0, 1.0 - (\lambda I_1 + (1-\lambda) I_2)) \le \lambda \max(0, 1.0 - I_1) + (1-\lambda) \max(0, 1.0 - I_2)\) by the subadditivity of the supremum operator. Hence, \(g\) is non-negative and convex, representing a standard European put option with strike 1.0.

Problem P.2: Prove that for a zero-coupon inflation-indexed bond without deflation floor, the real duration with respect to real yield equals the nominal duration with respect to nominal yield for a comparable zero-coupon nominal bond.

Solution

For an \(n\)-year zero-coupon TIPS, the real present value is \(P_{\text{real}} = F e^{-n y_{\text{real}}}\). Its real duration is \(- \partial \ln P_{\text{real}} / \partial y_{\text{real}} = n\). For an \(n\)-year nominal zero-coupon bond, \(P_{\text{nom}} = F e^{-n y_{\text{nom}}}\), and nominal duration is \(- \partial \ln P_{\text{nom}} / \partial y_{\text{nom}} = n\). Both durations equal time to maturity \(n\), confirming that real duration of real bonds matches nominal duration of nominal bonds.

Problem P.3: Prove that an unexpected increase in the reference CPI strictly increases the dollar duration (DV01) of a coupon-bearing TIPS bond.

Solution

Dollar duration is \(\text{DV01} = - \partial P_{\text{dirty}} / \partial y_{\text{real}} \times 0.0001\). Since \(P_{\text{dirty}} = P_{\text{clean, real}} \times I_t\), we have \(\text{DV01} = I_t \times \text{DV01}_{\text{real, base}}\). Because \(I_t = \text{Ref CPI}_t / \text{CPI}_{\text{base}}\), taking the derivative with respect to \(\text{Ref CPI}_t\) gives \(\partial \text{DV01} / \partial \text{Ref CPI}_t = \text{DV01}_{\text{real, base}} / \text{CPI}_{\text{base}} > 0\). Thus, higher inflation scales up all future cash flows and increases the dollar sensitivity to real yields.

Problem E.1: Empirical studies find that the daily correlation between TIPS returns and changes in headline inflation is close to zero. Explain the econometric reason for this finding based on indexation mechanics.

Solution

Daily TIPS returns are dominated by shifts in the real discount rate \(y_{\text{real}}\), while daily inflation prints do not exist because CPI-U is only released monthly. Furthermore, the 2.5-month indexation lag implies that the published monthly CPI was already largely priced into market forward breakevens prior to release date, leaving daily TIPS price movements driven almost exclusively by real rate duration fluctuations.

Problem E.2: During the 2008 financial crisis, why did on-the-run 5-year TIPS trade at lower real yields than seasoned 10-year TIPS with 5 years of remaining maturity?

Solution

In late 2008, severe deflation fears emerged. Newly issued 5-year TIPS had an index ratio near 1.0, making their deflation put option at the money and highly valuable. In contrast, seasoned 10-year TIPS with 5 years remaining had accumulated substantial past inflation (\(I_t \approx 1.25\)), rendering their deflation floor far out of the money. The option value embedded in the newly issued TIPS elevated their price and depressed their quoted real yield relative to seasoned bonds.

Problem E.3: How does phantom income taxation affect the required pre-tax real yield of TIPS held in taxable accounts during a 7% inflation regime?

Solution

In a 7% inflation environment, the principal accretion of 7% per year is taxed immediately at ordinary income rates (e.g. 35%), generating a cash tax liability of 2.45% of face value before any principal is received. To deliver a positive post-tax real return, taxable investors demand a higher pre-tax real yield, creating downward price pressure relative to tax-exempt institutional buyers.

Problem R.1: Formulate a no-arbitrage replicating strategy for the embedded deflation floor in a TIPS bond using zero-coupon inflation swaps and options on CPI.

Solution

To replicate the deflation floor \(\max(0, 1.0 - I_T) \times F\), an investor holds a long position in a European CPI put option with strike \(K = \text{CPI}_{\text{base}}\) expiring at maturity \(T\). In the inter-dealer derivatives market, inflation floorlets on zero-coupon inflation swaps directly deliver \(\max(0, 1.0 - \text{CPI}_T / \text{CPI}_0)\), providing exact payoff replication for the statutory Treasury put.

Problem R.2: How does the indexation lag introduce seasonal volatility into short-dated TIPS real yields?

Solution

Because headline CPI-U exhibits strong monthly seasonality (rising in spring and decelerating in autumn), the 2.5-month indexation lag forces reference CPI to follow a predictable seasonal oscillation. For short-dated TIPS with duration under 1 year, seasonal inflation accrual represents a substantial fraction of total horizon return, causing quoted real yields to fluctuate with calendar seasonality.

Problem R.3: Evaluate whether sovereign debt managers should issue TIPS with or without a statutory deflation floor from a debt-sustainability perspective.

Solution

From the sovereign's perspective, issuing with a deflation floor sells an out-of-the-money put option to investors, lowering the required coupon at auction. However, during systemic debt-deflation episodes when tax revenues contract, the floor prevents government debt service from declining, exacerbating fiscal fragility. Unfloored indexation provides superior macroeconomic counter-cyclical risk sharing.

    Both the clean price and the accrued real interest are scaled by the settlement index ratio.

  8. If cumulative deflation reduces the index ratio to 0.85 at maturity for a $1,000 par TIPS, what is the final principal redemption payment in dollars?

    The deflation floor guarantees \(\max(1.0, 0.85) \times \$1,000 = \$1,000.00\).

  9. What is 'phantom income' in the context of TIPS taxation?

    Options

    The IRS taxes annual inflation adjustments to principal as current income, even though cash is not distributed until maturity.

  10. To which economic factor is the real duration of a TIPS bond primarily exposed?

    Options

    Real duration isolates percentage price sensitivity with respect to real yield shifts.
