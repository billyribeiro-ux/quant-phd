Scenario P&L, Tail Stress & Execution Architecture

Traditional risk management relies heavily on local Greek linearizations and historical Value-at-Risk, both of which fatally underestimate portfolio damage during regime breaks. In complex macroeconomic portfolios combining cash bonds, foreign exchange forwards, interest rate swaps, and non-linear options, linear approximations fail to capture curvature, volatility shocks, and cross-asset correlation breakdowns. True resilience demands full non-linear scenario revaluation across historical and hypothetical macroeconomic stress shocks, paired with execution models that account for Almgren-Chriss market impact drag, dealer balance sheet constraints, and margin spiral stop-out boundaries.

1. Scenario P&L: Full Non-Linear Revaluation vs Greek Approximations

In quantitative macro management, evaluating trade expressions solely through infinitesimal Greeks (\(\Delta, \Gamma, \text{DV01}, \mathcal{V}\)) introduces massive blind spots when markets experience non-marginal macro shocks (Pedersen 2015; Hull 2021).

Definition 6.1 (scenario P&L) The scenario P&L of a multi-asset macro portfolio under a discrete macroeconomic scenario \(\mathbf{s} \in \mathcal{S}\) is the exact change in net portfolio market value computed via full non-linear revaluation of every underlying contract under the joint state shift \(\mathbf{s} = (\Delta \mathbf{S}, \Delta \mathbf{y}, \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma}, \Delta t)\):

\[ \Pi(\mathbf{s}) = \sum_{i=1}^M N_i \cdot \left[ V_i\big(\mathbf{S}_0 + \Delta \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{s}), \mathbf{y}_0 + \Delta \mathbf{y}(\mathbf{s}), \boldsymbol{\sigma}_0 + \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{s}), T_i - \Delta t\big) - V_i(\mathbf{S}_0, \mathbf{y}_0, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_0, T_i) \right] - \text{Financing}(\mathbf{s}) \]

where \(V_i(\cdot)\) is the exact contract pricing function (Black-Scholes-Merton formula, discounted cash flow curve, or forward pricing model), \(N_i\) is the contract position quantity, and \(\text{Financing}(\mathbf{s})\) represents accrued repo financing drag and margin borrow costs over scenario horizon \(\Delta t\).

Theorem 6.1 (The Convexity Error of Linear Greek Approximations) Let \(\Delta \Pi_{\text{linear}}(\mathbf{s})\) be the first-order Greek approximation of portfolio P&L:

\[ \Delta \Pi_{\text{linear}}(\mathbf{s}) = \boldsymbol{\Delta}^T \Delta \mathbf{S} - 10^4 \cdot \mathbf{DV01}^T \Delta \mathbf{y} + \boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^T \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma} + \Theta \Delta t \]

The discrepancy between true scenario P&L and the linear approximation is the non-linear convexity gap \(\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{s}) = \Pi(\mathbf{s}) - \Delta \Pi_{\text{linear}}(\mathbf{s})\). For a portfolio with positive net gamma (\(\boldsymbol{\Gamma} \succ 0\)) and positive vega (\(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}} > 0\)), the linear approximation systematically understates portfolio gains under large symmetric market moves (\(\|\Delta \mathbf{S}\| \gg 0\)):

\[ \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{s}) \approx \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{S}^T \boldsymbol{\Gamma} \Delta \mathbf{S} + \Delta \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{Vanna} \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma} + \frac{1}{2} \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma}^T \mathbf{Volga} \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma} > 0 \]

Conversely, for short-option expressions (such as unhedged currency carry trades or short variance swaps), \(\boldsymbol{\Gamma} \prec 0\), causing linear models to catastrophically underestimate downside tail losses during volatility spikes.

2. Designing Canonical Macro Stress Scenarios

A rigorous macro stress testing framework does not merely simulate historical 99% VaR quantiles. It evaluates portfolio behavior across canonical macroeconomic regime breaks (Pedersen 2015; Brunnermeier et al. 2008):

  • 2008 Lehman Liquidity Freeze: Massive bull steepening in sovereign curves (front-end rates collapse), violent FX carry unwind (JPY and USD surge, high-yield FX plummets 25%), equity markets drop 35%, and implied volatility explodes (+30 to +50 points).
  • 2020 Covid Flash Crash: Extreme global equity liquidation (-30%), flight-to-safety sovereign curve rally, credit spread blowout, and severe basis disruptions between cash Treasuries and futures.
  • 2022 Fed Hawkish Inflation Shock: Rapid bear flattening and inversion of sovereign curves (-100 to -150 bps in 2s10s slope), simultaneous drop in both equities (-20%) and long-duration bonds (-25%), positive equity-bond correlation destroying classical 60/40 diversification.
  • 2024 JPY Carry Trade Unwind: Sudden Bank of Japan hawkish rate hike triggering an abrupt +15% surge in the Japanese Yen over 3 trading sessions, forcing global margin liquidations in technology equities and high-beta assets.

3. Execution Architecture, Market Impact, and Stop-Out Boundaries

Paper macroeconomic strategies often fail in live institutional deployment because of transaction costs, dealer balance sheet constraints, and price impact during liquidation (Almgren and Chriss 2001; Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2009).

Definition 6.2 (Almgren-Chriss Optimal Liquidation Impact) When unwinding a macro position of \(X_0\) shares or contracts over horizon \(T\) divided into intervals \(t_k\), trading at rate \(v_k = \frac{x_k - x_{k-1}}{\tau}\), the total expected execution cost \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{exec}}\) combines permanent price impact \(\gamma\) and temporary price impact \(\eta\):

\[ \mathcal{C}_{\text{exec}} = \frac{1}{2} \gamma X_0^2 + \tau \sum_{k=1}^K \eta \left( \frac{x_k - x_{k-1}}{\tau} \right)^2 \]

In stressed macro environments, market depth \(\frac{1}{\eta}\) contracts by 80% to 95%. Attempting to liquidate a multi-billion dollar relative-value position in illiquid sovereign bonds or emerging market FX creates self-inflicted execution drag that exceeds the original trade margin. Consequently, quantitative macro risk engines enforce strict stop-out boundaries calibrated to position liquidation horizons rather than instantaneous mark-to-market prices.

Lab 1 · Macro Portfolio Tail Stress Revaluation Engine

Hypothesis: Across four canonical macroeconomic crisis scenarios, linear Greek approximations fail to capture option convexity, resulting in multi-million dollar convexity gaps where non-linear options protect capital during market crashes.

"""Lab GM4-L06-1: Scenario P&L and Non-Linear Tail Stress Testing.

Evaluates a multi-asset macro portfolio across four canonical macroeconomic
stress scenarios, comparing linear Greek approximations with full non-linear revaluation.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Scenario:
    name: str
    rates_slope_shock_bps: float
    fx_carry_shock_pct: float
    equity_spot_shock_pct: float
    vol_shock_pts: float


def bsm_call_pnl(s0: float, s1: float, k: float, v0: float, v1: float, t: float, r: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Calculate (full_nonlinear_pnl, linear_delta_approx_pnl)."""

    # Helper for analytical price
    def price(s: float, vol: float) -> float:
        sqt = vol * math.sqrt(t)
        d1 = (math.log(s / k) + (r + 0.5 * vol * vol) * t) / sqt
        d2 = d1 - sqt
        c1 = 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(d1 / math.sqrt(2.0)))
        c2 = 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(d2 / math.sqrt(2.0)))
        return s * c1 - k * math.exp(-r * t) * c2

    p0 = price(s0, v0)
    p1 = price(s1, v1)
    nonlinear_pnl = p1 - p0

    # Initial Delta
    sqt = v0 * math.sqrt(t)
    d1 = (math.log(s0 / k) + (r + 0.5 * v0 * v0) * t) / sqt
    delta = 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(d1 / math.sqrt(2.0)))
    linear_pnl = delta * (s1 - s0)

    return nonlinear_pnl, linear_pnl


def main() -> None:
    # Portfolio composition:
    # 1. 2s10s Curve Steepener ($10M notional, DV01 neutrality, pays off when slope widens)
    # 2. FX Carry: Long AUD, Short JPY ($5M notional)
    # 3. Long Equity Volatility Straddle ($2M notional in options)

    scenarios = [
        Scenario(
            name="2008 Lehman Liquidity Freeze",
            rates_slope_shock_bps=120.0,
            fx_carry_shock_pct=-0.22,
            equity_spot_shock_pct=-0.35,
            vol_shock_pts=0.30,
        ),
        Scenario(
            name="2020 Covid Flash Crash",
            rates_slope_shock_bps=65.0,
            fx_carry_shock_pct=-0.14,
            equity_spot_shock_pct=-0.28,
            vol_shock_pts=0.45,
        ),
        Scenario(
            name="2022 Fed Hawkish Inflation Shock",
            rates_slope_shock_bps=-110.0,
            fx_carry_shock_pct=0.08,
            equity_spot_shock_pct=-0.18,
            vol_shock_pts=0.06,
        ),
        Scenario(
            name="2024 JPY Carry Trade Unwind",
            rates_slope_shock_bps=30.0,
            fx_carry_shock_pct=-0.16,
            equity_spot_shock_pct=-0.10,
            vol_shock_pts=0.25,
        ),
    ]

    print("=== Macro Portfolio Tail Stress Revaluation ($17M Gross Exposure) ===")
    print(f"{'Scenario Name':<34}{'Non-Linear P&L':<18}{'Linear Approx P&L':<18}{'Convexity Gap':<14}")
    print("-" * 84)

    for sc in scenarios:
        slope_shock = sc.rates_slope_shock_bps
        fx_shock = sc.fx_carry_shock_pct
        eq_shock = sc.equity_spot_shock_pct
        vol_shock = sc.vol_shock_pts

        # 1. Steepener P&L: DV01 of $10M spread is ~$3,500 per bp
        # Slope change in bps * DV01
        pnl_steepener = slope_shock * 3500.0

        # 2. FX Carry P&L: delta-one cash return
        pnl_carry = 5_000_000.0 * fx_shock

        # 3. Equity Option Straddle P&L: Non-linear revaluation vs linear delta
        s0 = 100.0
        s1 = s0 * (1.0 + eq_shock)
        v0 = 0.18
        v1 = v0 + vol_shock
        t = 0.25
        r = 0.045
        nl_call, lin_call = bsm_call_pnl(s0, s1, 100.0, v0, v1, t, r)
        # Put via parity / symmetry
        nl_put, lin_put = bsm_call_pnl(s0, 2 * s0 - s1, 100.0, v0, v1, t, r)
        straddle_nl_pct = (nl_call + nl_put) / 20.0  # Normalized to option budget
        straddle_lin_pct = (lin_call + lin_put) / 20.0

        pnl_straddle_nl = 2_000_000.0 * straddle_nl_pct
        pnl_straddle_lin = 2_000_000.0 * straddle_lin_pct

        total_nonlinear = pnl_steepener + pnl_carry + pnl_straddle_nl
        total_linear = pnl_steepener + pnl_carry + pnl_straddle_lin
        convexity_gap = total_nonlinear - total_linear

        print(f"{sc.name:<34}${total_nonlinear:>12,.0f}     ${total_linear:>12,.0f}     ${convexity_gap:>10,.0f}")


main()

4. Where the Canon Breaks

Standard risk management frameworks often presume that scenario stress testing can be conducted in a static, friction-free environment where all contracts can be settled at marked mid-market prices. In severe financial crises, this assumption breaks down completely (Pedersen 2015; Almgren and Chriss 2001).

First, market liquidity is endogenously dependent on fund solvency (Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2009). When multiple macro hedge funds hold overlapping relative-value positions (such as the Treasury basis or short volatility), an initial price shock forces simultaneous margin calls. The collective liquidation attempts overwhelm dealer absorption capacity, widening bid-ask spreads by orders of magnitude.

Second, correlation structures do not merely shift; markets suffer complete asset class decoupling. In March 2020, even US Treasury bonds—the world's deepest safe haven—experienced sudden intraday sell-offs alongside equities as foreign central banks and levered basis funds rushed to raise USD cash.

Third, counterparty risk and exchange circuit breakers freeze trade execution. When futures markets limit-up or limit-down, hedging cannot occur, and dynamic delta-hedging algorithms fail to execute, converting intended market-neutral books into unhedged tail liabilities.

5. Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Portfolio delta and duration provide an accurate forecast of crisis P&L under a 30% market drop.

    Why it is wrong: First-order Greeks are local linear derivatives accurate only for infinitesimal price moves (\(\Delta S \to 0\)). Under a 30% drop, option delta shifts radically due to gamma, vega expands during panic, and bond duration changes as yields plunge, creating enormous non-linear convexity gaps.

  • Misconception: If a macro portfolio passes historical VaR at 99% confidence, it is protected against future market crises.

    Why it is wrong: Historical VaR only reflects shocks observed in the backward-looking lookback window. Novel macroeconomic shocks (such as 2022's stagflationary bond-equity crash) fall outside historical tranquil distributions and inflict catastrophic losses on VaR-compliant books.

  • Misconception: Liquidating a portfolio during a market panic can be achieved at quoted screen bid prices.

    Why it is wrong: Liquidating large institutional notional consumes multiple tiers of the limit order book, generating severe temporary and permanent market impact (Almgren and Chriss 2001) that severely degrades realized exit prices.

Mechanics

Problem 6.1 · Convexity Gap in a Single Call Option

A portfolio holds 10,000 European call options on an equity index with current spot \(S_0 = 100\), strike \(K = 100\), initial delta \(\Delta = 0.50\), and gamma \(\Gamma = 0.03\). The market experiences a sudden 10% upward shock (\(\Delta S = +10\)). Compute the linear delta P&L, the second-order Delta-Gamma P&L, and the resulting convexity gain.

Worked Solution

1. Linear Delta P&L per option: \(\Delta P_{\text{lin}} = \Delta \times \Delta S = 0.50 \times 10.0 = \$5.00\).

2. Second-order term per option: \(\frac{1}{2} \Gamma (\Delta S)^2 = 0.5 \times 0.03 \times (10.0)^2 = 0.5 \times 0.03 \times 100 = \$1.50\).

3. Delta-Gamma P&L per option: \(\$5.00 + \$1.50 = \$6.50\).

4. Total portfolio convexity gain (for 10,000 options): \(10,000 \times \$1.50 = \$15,000\).

The linear model misses $15,000 of positive P&L by ignoring the option's expanding delta as spot rallies.

Problem 6.2 · Almgren-Chriss Execution Cost Calculation

A macro fund needs to liquidate \(X_0 = 500,000\) shares of an ETF over \(T = 5\) days in equal daily blocks (\(x_k = 100,000\) shares/day). Permanent impact parameter is \(\gamma = 2.5 \times 10^{-7}\) $/share, and temporary impact parameter is \(\eta = 5.0 \times 10^{-6}\) $/share/day. Calculate the total expected execution price impact drag.

Worked Solution

1. Permanent price impact cost: \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{perm}} = \frac{1}{2} \gamma X_0^2 = 0.5 \times (2.5 \times 10^{-7}) \times (500,000)^2 = 0.5 \times (2.5 \times 10^{-7}) \times (2.5 \times 10^{11}) = 0.5 \times 62,500 = \$31,250\).

2. Temporary price impact cost: For constant daily rate \(v = 100,000\), daily temporary cost is \(\eta v^2 = (5.0 \times 10^{-6}) \times (100,000)^2 = (5.0 \times 10^{-6}) \times 10^{10} = \$50,000\) per day. Over 5 days: \(5 \times \$50,000 = \$250,000\).

3. Total execution drag: \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{total}} = \$31,250 + \$250,000 = \$281,250\).

Problem 6.3 · Multileg Scenario P&L Aggregation

A macro portfolio contains: (1) Long $20M 10Y Treasury note (\(\text{DV01} = \$16,000/\text{bp}\)), (2) Short $15M S&P 500 futures (\(\Delta = -1.0\)), and (3) Long 1,000 ATM S&P 500 straddles (vega \(\mathcal{V} = \$2,500/\text{vol pt}\), net gamma \(\Gamma = 0.05\)). Under a "Flight to Quality" scenario, 10Y yields drop 40 bps, S&P 500 falls 8%, and implied volatility rises 12 points. Compute the net scenario P&L (assume S&P spot = 100).

Worked Solution

1. Bond P&L: Yield falls 40 bps \(\implies \Delta \Pi_{\text{bond}} = - \text{DV01} \times (-40) = \$16,000 \times 40 = +\$640,000\).

2. Equity Short Futures P&L: S&P falls 8% on $15M notional \(\implies \Delta \Pi_{\text{eq}} = -1.0 \times (-0.08) \times \$15,000,000 = +\$1,200,000\).

3. Straddle Vega P&L: \(\mathcal{V} \times \Delta \sigma = \$2,500 \times 12 = +\$30,000\). Gamma P&L: \(0.5 \times 0.05 \times (-8)^2 \times 1,000 = 0.025 \times 64 \times 1,000 = +\$1,600\). Total straddle = \(+\$31,600\).

4. Net Scenario P&L: \(\$640,000 + \$1,200,000 + \$31,600 = +\$1,871,600\).

Proof

Problem 6.4 · Non-Negative Convexity Gap of Long Vanilla Options

Prove that for any standard European call or put option under the Black-Scholes model with constant implied volatility, the convexity gap \(\mathcal{G}(\Delta S) = V(S_0 + \Delta S) - [V(S_0) + \Delta \cdot \Delta S]\) is strictly non-negative for all price shocks \(\Delta S \in (-S_0, \infty)\), and strictly positive for all \(\Delta S \neq 0\).

Worked Solution

By Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder, for any twice continuously differentiable function \(V(S)\), there exists \(\xi\) strictly between \(S_0\) and \(S_0 + \Delta S\) such that:

\[ V(S_0 + \Delta S) = V(S_0) + V'(S_0) \Delta S + \frac{1}{2} V''(\xi) (\Delta S)^2 \]

Under the Black-Scholes model, \(V'(S_0) = \Delta\) and \(V''(S) = \Gamma(S)\). Thus, the convexity gap is:

\[ \mathcal{G}(\Delta S) = \frac{1}{2} \Gamma(\xi) (\Delta S)^2 \]

In Problem 3.4, it was proven that Black-Scholes gamma \(\Gamma(S) = \frac{\phi(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}} > 0\) strictly for all \(S > 0\). Since \(\xi > 0\), \(\Gamma(\xi) > 0\) strictly.

Furthermore, for any \(\Delta S \neq 0\), \((\Delta S)^2 > 0\). Therefore, \(\mathcal{G}(\Delta S) = \frac{1}{2} \Gamma(\xi) (\Delta S)^2 > 0\) strictly, proving that linear approximations strictly underestimate the value of long options under any non-zero price shock. \(\blacksquare\)

Problem 6.5 · Optimality of Linear Liquidation Trajectories under Quadratic Impact

Prove that under the Almgren-Chriss framework with risk neutrality (\(\lambda = 0\)) and pure quadratic temporary price impact, the trading schedule that minimizes total execution cost is strictly constant-rate linear liquidation: \(x_k = X_0 \left(1 - \frac{k}{K}\right)\).

Worked Solution

Let \(X_0\) be the total shares to liquidate over \(K\) identical time intervals \(\tau = T / K\). Let \(n_k = x_{k-1} - x_k\) be the shares sold in step \(k\), subject to the conservation constraint \(\sum_{k=1}^K n_k = X_0\).

The total temporary impact cost is \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{temp}} = \eta \sum_{k=1}^K \left( \frac{n_k}{\tau} \right) n_k = \frac{\eta}{\tau} \sum_{k=1}^K n_k^2\). Permanent impact depends only on \(X_0\): \(\frac{1}{2} \gamma X_0^2\).

Form the Lagrangian for minimizing temporary cost subject to \(\sum_{k=1}^K n_k = X_0\):

\[ \mathcal{L}(n_1, \dots, n_K, \lambda) = \frac{\eta}{\tau} \sum_{k=1}^K n_k^2 - \lambda \left( \sum_{k=1}^K n_k - X_0 \right) \]

Taking the first-order condition with respect to each \(n_k\): \(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial n_k} = \frac{2 \eta}{\tau} n_k - \lambda = 0 \implies n_k = \frac{\lambda \tau}{2 \eta}\).

Since the right-hand side is independent of \(k\), all trade sizes must be identical: \(n_k = n\) for all \(k\). Summing gives \(K n = X_0 \implies n = \frac{X_0}{K}\).

Thus, remaining position is \(x_k = X_0 - k n = X_0 \left(1 - \frac{k}{K}\right)\), proving that a uniform linear execution trajectory strictly minimizes price impact under quadratic friction. \(\blacksquare\)

Problem 6.6 · Reverse Stress Testing Invariance Principle

Prove that for a linear portfolio with factor exposures \(\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \R^K\) and a loss threshold \(L > 0\), the minimum Mahalanobis distance factor shock \(\mathbf{f}^* \in \R^K\) satisfying \(\boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{f} \le -L\) is collinear with \(\mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta}\), and find the exact formula for \(\mathbf{f}^*\).

Worked Solution

The reverse stress testing problem seeks the most plausible factor shock (minimizing Mahalanobis distance \(\mathbf{f}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \mathbf{f}\)) that causes a portfolio loss of at least \(L\):

\[ \min_{\mathbf{f}} \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{f}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \mathbf{f} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{f} = -L \]

Set up the Lagrangian: \(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{f}, \lambda) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{f}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \mathbf{f} + \lambda (\boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{f} + L)\).

First-order condition: \(\nabla_{\mathbf{f}} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \mathbf{f} + \lambda \boldsymbol{\beta} = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{f} = -\lambda \mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta}\).

Multiply by \(\boldsymbol{\beta}^T\): \(\boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{f} = -\lambda \boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta} = -L \implies \lambda = \frac{L}{\boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta}}\).

Substituting \(\lambda\) back gives the unique optimal shock:

\[ \mathbf{f}^* = -\frac{L}{\boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta}} \mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta} \]

This proves that the most likely ruin scenario is perfectly aligned with the covariance-projected factor exposure vector \(\mathbf{\Sigma}_f \boldsymbol{\beta}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Empirical

Problem 6.7 · Empirical Liquidity Breakdown in March 2020 Treasury Market

During normal market conditions, the bid-ask spread on the on-the-run 10-year US Treasury note is approximately 1/64th of 1% (1.56 bps of price, or ~$150 per $1M). On March 16, 2020, bid-ask spreads blew out to 8/64ths (12.5 bps of price). For a $500,000,000 Treasury liquidation, compute the additional transaction friction incurred solely from bid-ask widening.

Worked Solution

1. Normal half-spread cost: \(0.5 \times \frac{1.5625}{10,000} \times \$500,000,000 = 0.5 \times 0.00015625 \times \$500,000,000 = \$39,062.50\).

2. Crisis half-spread cost: \(0.5 \times \frac{12.5}{10,000} \times \$500,000,000 = 0.5 \times 0.00125 \times \$500,000,000 = \$312,500\).

3. Additional transaction friction: \(\$312,500 - \$39,062.50 = \$273,437.50\).

Even in the world's most liquid sovereign debt instrument, emergency liquidation during dealer inventory congestion imposed over a quarter-million dollars in immediate crossing penalty.

Problem 6.8 · August 2024 Japanese Yen Carry Unwind Dynamics

On August 5, 2024 ("Black Monday" in Tokyo), the Nikkei 225 plummeted 12.4% while USD/JPY dropped from 153.0 to 142.0 as leveraged currency carry trades unwound. Analyze why an unhedged long USD/JPY trade experienced a multi-sigma drawdown that wiped out 4 years of accumulated carry in 72 hours.

Worked Solution

Over 2021–2024, the USD/JPY rate differential was roughly +5.0% per annum, accumulating ~20% in nominal interest carry over 4 years.

When the Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 0.25% alongside weak US payroll data, USD/JPY plunged from 153 to 142, a spot drop of \(\frac{142 - 153}{153} = -7.19\%\) in 3 days, following an earlier drop from 161 (-11.8%). Leveraged macro accounts faced margin calls from retail brokers and prime brokers simultaneously.

For a typical 5x levered carry position, a 7.2% currency decline translates into a -36% loss of equity capital in 72 hours, perfectly illustrating the extreme negative return skewness of unhedged carry trades.

Problem 6.9 · Stagflation Tail Scenario: 2022 Performance Decomposition

In 2022, the S&P 500 declined 18.1% while the Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index dropped 13.0%. For a classical 60/40 balanced portfolio ($60M equity, $40M bond), compute the annual dollar P&L and explain why linear diversification models failed to protect institutional investors.

Worked Solution

1. Equity dollar loss: \(0.60 \times \$100,000,000 \times (-0.181) = -\$10,860,000\).

2. Bond dollar loss: \(0.40 \times \$100,000,000 \times (-0.130) = -\$5,200,000\).

3. Total portfolio loss: \(-\$10,860,000 - \$5,200,000 = -\$16,060,000\) (-16.06%).

Linear risk models relied on the 20-year sample correlation of \(\rho(\text{stocks}, \text{bonds}) \approx -0.30\) characteristic of the disinflationary Great Moderation. In a supply-driven inflation shock, central bank rate hikes depress both equity multiples (via discount rates) and bond prices simultaneously, shifting correlation to +0.60 and invalidating classical duration hedging.

Research

Problem 6.10 · Dynamic Liquidation Horizons under Balance Sheet Constraints

Synthesize Gârleanu and Pedersen's (2011) model of margin-based asset pricing. How do dealer regulatory capital constraints (such as the Supplementary Leverage Ratio, SLR) restrict liquidity provision and alter optimal execution trajectories?

Worked Solution

Gârleanu and Pedersen (2011) demonstrate that assets requiring higher margin or balance sheet space trade at a discount, offering higher expected returns to compensate for margin friction. Under Basel III SLR rules, bank-affiliated primary dealers face non-risk-weighted capital charges on total unweighted assets, including risk-free Treasuries and reverse repos.

During macroeconomic crises, when funds seek to borrow cash or dealers must warehouse customer inventory, dealer balance sheet limits bind. Dealers widen bid-ask spreads and refuse repo financing. In an Almgren-Chriss liquidation model, this causes temporary price impact \(\eta\) to become a convex function of cumulative dealer inventory, forcing optimal liquidation horizons to lengthen significantly to avoid catastrophic price slippage.

Problem 6.11 · Reverse Stress Testing via Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)

Critique how generative neural architectures (such as Wasserstein GANs or Variational Autoencoders) can be used to generate plausible, high-dimensional macroeconomic stress scenarios that violate Gaussian copula assumptions.

Worked Solution

Traditional parametric stress scenarios rely on linear factor models that fail to capture tail dependencies, volatility clustering, and asymmetry. WGANs trained on multidecade macro time series learn the non-linear manifold of joint market returns without restrictive distributional priors.

To perform reverse stress testing, the generator is coupled with an adversarial loss function penalized by portfolio P&L: \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{WGAN}} + \lambda \max(0, \Pi(\mathbf{s}) + L_{\text{threshold}})\). The generator synthesizes synthetic crisis states that look statistically indistinguishable from historical market regimes while specifically targeting the portfolio's idiosyncratic vulnerabilities, revealing multi-factor blind spots that traditional historical replays miss.

Problem 6.12 · Automated Stop-Out Boundary Calibration under Jump Discontinuities

Formulate the optimal dynamic liquidation threshold (stop-out boundary) for a leveraged macro relative-value fund subject to discrete jump risk and margin margin-call triggers.

Worked Solution

Let fund equity \(E_t\) follow a jump-diffusion process \(d E_t = \mu E_t dt + \sigma E_t d W_t + (Y - 1) E_{t-} d q_t\), where \(q_t\) is a Poisson counter with intensity \(\lambda\) and \(Y\) is the jump distribution. The fund faces an external regulatory or prime broker liquidation threshold \(E_{\min}\).

To avoid fire-sale liquidation at \(E_{\min}\), the manager establishes an internal voluntary stop-out boundary \(E^* > E_{\min}\). The optimal boundary solves an optimal stopping problem balancing the expected opportunity cost of abandoning positive-alpha positions against the catastrophic cost of bankruptcy slippage:

\[ E^* = \arg\min_E \left\{ \E\left[\int_0^{\tau_E} e^{-r t} \text{Carry}_t dt + e^{-r \tau_E} \mathcal{C}_{\text{liquidation}}(E_{\tau_E})\right] \right\} \]

Under discrete jump risk, the boundary \(E^*\) must include a substantial buffer: \(E^* - E_{\min} \ge \text{VaR}_\alpha(\text{Jump Magnitude})\) to prevent discrete overnight price jumps from skipping past the stop-loss order directly into liquidation default.

8. Spaced Retrieval Quiz

  1. What defines the "scenario P&L" of a multi-asset macro portfolio?

    Options

    Scenario P&L revalues all underlying options, bonds, and derivatives through exact pricing formulas rather than first-order Greek approximations.

  2. Why does a linear Greek approximation significantly underestimate the payoff of a long straddle during a crisis?

    Options

    An ATM straddle has delta near zero. Linear approximations predict zero P&L, missing the large gains generated by positive gamma and surging implied volatility.

  3. In the Almgren-Chriss market impact model, what distinguishes permanent impact from temporary impact?

    Options

    Permanent impact permanently moves the market price due to net order flow, whereas temporary impact reflects the temporary price concession demanded by liquidity providers.

  4. Under risk neutrality and quadratic temporary impact, what is the optimal execution trajectory to liquidate a position?

    Options

    Because temporary impact is quadratic in trade velocity, Jensen's inequality implies that spreading execution uniformly minimizes total quadratic drag.

  5. What unique macroeconomic dynamic caused both equities and sovereign bonds to collapse simultaneously in 2022?

    Options

    Unexpected inflation forced central banks to raise rates aggressively, driving discount rates higher and simultaneously depressing equity valuations and bond prices.

  6. What is "reverse stress testing" in institutional risk architecture?

    Options

    Reverse stress testing starts from a predefined catastrophic loss threshold and identifies the exact combination of market moves most likely to trigger it.

  7. Why do dealer regulatory leverage ratio constraints (SLR) exacerbate liquidity crises in sovereign bond markets?

    Options

    Under non-risk-weighted leverage ratios, low-risk Treasury inventory consumes precious balance sheet equity, forcing dealers to withdraw from market making during stress.

  8. Why must a voluntary stop-out boundary include a safety buffer above the prime broker liquidation threshold?

    Options

    Discrete price jumps and market illiquidity cause execution slippage; without a buffer, a jump shock pushes equity directly into forced liquidation default.
