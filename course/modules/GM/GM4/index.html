Macro Strategy Construction

Translating macroeconomic views into profitable, risk-controlled institutional trading books is the central craft of global macro. Even a correct fundamental forecast generates catastrophic losses if expressed through an inefficient instrument, an unhedged systematic risk factor, a negative-carry structure, or an oversized bet. GM4 develops the mathematical principles and quantitative architectures required to design, hedge, size, and stress-test trade expressions across rates, foreign exchange, equities, and volatility. You will master duration-neutral curve structures, options versus delta-one convexity engineering, multi-asset carry and roll-down attribution, fractional Kelly sizing under covariance uncertainty, and full non-linear scenario P&L evaluation delivered in Atlas milestone A20.

  • Prerequisites: GM2 Macro Regimes & Forecasting, FI3 Bond Risk Premia, X2 Currency Factors, DV2 Volatility Surface Models, and PR3 Kelly Criterion & Portfolio Growth.
  • Project: Atlas A20: Macro Trade-Construction Engine, autograded milestone (28 hours).
  • Hours: 80 study hours (52 h across 6 lessons, 28 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formulate the trade-expression optimization problem, balancing capital efficiency, basis risk, financing drag, and regulatory margin constraints across asset classes.
  2. Construct duration-neutral and regression-weighted relative-value curve spreads (steepeners, flatteners, butterflies) that isolate term premia from parallel shifts.
  3. Evaluate the structural trade-off between options and delta-one expressions, quantifying analytical gamma convexity, vega exposure, and theta decay drag.
  4. Decompose holding-period returns into instantaneous coupon/dividend carry, yield curve roll-down gains, and collateral repo financing drag.
  5. Derive fractional Kelly capital allocations and volatility-targeting constraints under parameter estimation error and stressed covariance breakdowns.
  6. Implement full non-linear scenario revaluation grids for multi-asset macro portfolios under stagflation, monetary tightening, and liquidity spiral regimes in Atlas A20.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite concepts from GM2, FI3, X2, DV2, and PR3, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In constructing a duration-neutral US Treasury 2Y/10Y yield curve steepener trade, leg 1 (2Y note) has a DV01 of $42.50 per contract, while leg 2 (10Y note) has a DV01 of $85.00 per contract. If the manager purchases 100 contracts of the 2Y note, how many contracts of the 10Y note must be sold short to establish exact DV01 neutrality?

    Options

    Duration neutrality requires \(N_1 \cdot \text{DV01}_1 + N_2 \cdot \text{DV01}_2 = 0\). With \(N_1 = 100\) long contracts, \(N_2 = -100 \times \frac{42.50}{85.00} = -50\) contracts short. Rung 1 skill check.

  2. A 5-year benchmark Treasury note has a spot yield of 4.50%, a modified duration of 4.40 years, an expected roll-down slide along an upward-sloping yield curve of 15 basis points over a 1-year holding period, and general collateral repo financing cost of 3.80%. What is the net expected holding-period carry in basis points?

    Options

    Net carry equals gross coupon yield (450 bps) plus roll-down gain (\(\text{Duration} \times \Delta y_{\text{roll}} = 4.40 \times 15 = 66\) bps) minus repo financing drag (380 bps): \(450 + 66 - 380 = 136\) bps. Rung 2 skill check.

  3. Under the expectations hypothesis of the term structure (FI3), what does a steep upward-sloping yield curve forecast?

    Options

    The pure expectations hypothesis asserts that forward rates equal expected future spot rates plus a zero (or constant) term premium, implying an upward slope reflects expected rising short rates.

  4. In empirical fixed-income term premium modeling (FI3, Adrian et al. 2013), why does the expectations hypothesis fail systematically?

    Options

    Term premia are strongly time-varying and countercyclical: investors demand higher compensation for holding duration during economic downturns, producing significant predictive power in forward rate spreads.

  5. According to Brunnermeier, Nagel, and Pedersen (2008) in X2, what is the defining statistical return distribution characteristic of currency carry trades?

    Options

    Currency carry trades harvest steady positive carry in quiet regimes but suffer abrupt, violent drawdowns during global liquidity squeezes, generating marked negative return skewness.

  6. In Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density extraction (DV2), how is the risk-neutral probability density function \(q(K)\) recovered from the European call option price surface \(C(K)\)?

    Options

    Breeden and Litzenberger (1978) proved that butterfly spreads in the limit isolate Arrow-Debreu state prices, establishing \(q(K) = e^{r T} \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\).

  7. Under the continuous Kelly criterion (PR3), what is the optimal fraction \(k^*\) of capital allocated to an asset with expected excess return \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\)?

    Options

    Maximizing the expected compound growth rate \(\E[\ln(W_T)]\) for a log-normal diffusion yields the unconstrained Kelly leverage fraction \(k^* = \frac{\mu}{\sigma^2}\).

  8. Why do institutional quantitative macro funds employ fractional Kelly sizing (e.g., quarter-Kelly \(f = 0.25\)) rather than full Kelly (PR3)?

    Options

    Because parameters are estimated with error, overestimating \(\mu\) under full Kelly pushes leverage into the negative-growth zone. Fractional Kelly trades a modest reduction in growth for a dramatic collapse in drawdown depth and ruin probability.

  9. In macroeconomic regime modeling (GM2), what empirical signal historically provides the highest pseudo-\(R^2\) in probit models for forecasting US recessions 12 months ahead?

    Options

    Estrella and Mishkin (1998) established that the 10Y minus 3M term spread is the single most powerful out-of-sample predictor of recessions across 2-to-6 quarter horizons.

  10. When evaluating macroeconomic trade expressions, what is "basis risk"?

    Options

    Basis risk occurs when an imperfect proxy (such as a liquid index future or cross-currency swap) decouples from the targeted economic exposure due to structural frictions, credit spreads, or supply-demand imbalances.

Lesson sequence

Six lessons totaling 52 hours of instruction develop the complete trade-construction pipeline:

  1. L01 · From Macro View to Market Expression 9 hours

    The anatomy of macro views. Mapping fundamental economic forecasts to tradeable instruments. The Trilemma of Expression: Capital Efficiency, Basis Risk, and Financing Drag. Introduces trade expression.

  2. L02 · Relative-Value Trades & Curve Structures 9 hours

    Principles of relative value. Isolating pure alpha and term premia from parallel factor shocks. DV01-neutral steepeners, flatteners, butterflies, and sovereign spreads. Introduces relative-value trade.

  3. L03 · Options vs. Delta-One: Convexity & Asymmetry 9 hours

    Payoff geometry. Delta-one linearity versus options non-linearity. The cost of path independence: theta decay versus delta hedging. Convexity of expression and payoff asymmetry ratios. Introduces options vs. delta-one expression, convexity of a trade expression, and risk–reward asymmetry.

  4. L04 · Carry, Roll-Down & Financing Drag in Expressions 8 hours

    Multi-asset carry theory (Koijen et al. 2018, Ilmanen 2011). Instantaneous yield, curve roll-down, and repo financing drag. Negative-carry traps, funding liquidity spirals, and collateral haircuts.

  5. L05 · Sizing & Cross-Asset Allocation under Scenarios 9 hours

    Capital allocation architectures. The Kelly criterion, parameter estimation noise, and fractional Kelly rules. Risk parity, marginal risk contribution, and covariance matrix breakdown in crisis regimes.

  6. L06 · Scenario P&L, Tail Stress & Execution Architecture 8 hours

    Full non-linear scenario revaluation versus linear Greek approximations. Designing canonical macro stress tests. Market impact models (Almgren-Chriss), liquidity decay, and stop-out monitoring. Introduces scenario P&L.

Capstone project: Atlas A20

The 28-hour capstone project delivers Atlas A20: Macro Trade-Construction Engine (atlas.strategy), implementing the complete quantitative pipeline: View → Expression → Sizing → Scenario P&L. The project utilizes a mandatory four-rung pedagogical ladder:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance constructing a US Treasury 2Y/10Y duration-neutral steepener trade and an option-straddle volatility expression, calculating exact hedge ratios, carry attribution, and scenario grids.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    The same task with core numerical functions removed (DV01 hedge ratio, carry roll-down, fractional Kelly leverage clamping, payoff asymmetry). Graded instantly in-browser.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project deliverable

    Implement the complete production module in atlas/strategy.py fulfilling Atlas A20 Contract, verified by an automated test suite across 50 seeds (0–49) and killing 5 adversarial mutants.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch goal

    Extend Atlas A20 to simulate dynamic cross-asset delta hedging with Almgren-Chriss market impact and endogenous margin spiral boundaries during rapid volatility spikes.
