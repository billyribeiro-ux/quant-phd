Funding Shocks, Bank Runs & Policy Backstops

Modern financial intermediaries transform liquid short-term liabilities into illiquid, long-duration assets. This structural maturity mismatch creates systemic vulnerability to sudden funding runs. When short-term creditors refuse to roll over funding or uninsured depositors execute coordinated digital withdrawals, institutions are forced into catastrophic asset fire sales. Because private market liquidity evaporates during crises, only an unconstrained public balance sheet can truncate the spiral. This lesson formalizes run dynamics, dealer capacity bottlenecks, and central bank backstops across the 2023 SVB run, March 2020 Treasury cash crunch, and 2012 Eurozone crisis.

1. Maturity Mismatch, Bank Runs & Market-Based Intermediation

Financial intermediaries perform two vital economic functions: maturity transformation and liquidity creation. In the seminal Diamond & Dybvig (1983) framework, a bank finances long-term productive investments yielding \(R > 1\) at \(T=2\) with demand deposits redeemable at par \(r_1 = 1\) at \(T=1\). If depositors panic, an early liquidation of assets yields a fire-sale liquidation value \(L < 1\). This creates strategic complementarity: if depositor \(i\) expects other depositors to withdraw at \(T=1\), their rational best response is to withdraw immediately, precipitating an inefficient run equilibrium.

In modern financial systems, funding fragility extends beyond commercial bank retail deposits into wholesale repo, commercial paper, and prime brokerage financing (Adrian & Shin 2010). Non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) fund levered portfolios of sovereign bonds, mortgage-backed securities, and corporate credit using short-term secured repo. A funding run manifests as an abrupt spike in repo haircuts or a refusal by money market funds to roll over overnight financing, forcing massive asset liquidations (Shleifer & Vishny 2011).

Definition 5.1 (policy backstop in a crisis) A policy backstop in a crisis is an explicit, non-discretionary or state-contingent commitment by an unconstrained sovereign authority or central bank to provide emergency liquidity, purchase distressed collateral, or guarantee liabilities at non-punitive valuations, truncating strategic-run complementarities, capping asset fire-sale discounts, and preventing systemic solvency collapse.

2. Theoretical Mechanics: Bagehot's Rule & Equilibrium Selection

The classical prescription for lender-of-last-resort policy dates to Walter Bagehot's Lombard Street (1873): lend freely at a high rate against good collateral. In modern mathematical finance, a policy backstop alters the strategic payoff matrix between market participants, eliminating bad run equilibria.

Theorem 5.1 (Run Elimination via Unconstrained Collateral Backstop) Consider \(N\) depositors holding claims \(D\) against a bank holding assets with fundamental hold-to-maturity value \(V_{\text{HTM}}\) and secondary fire-sale market value \(V(w) = V_{\text{HTM}}(1 - \kappa w)\), where \(w \in [0, 1]\) is the fraction of depositors withdrawing. If \(V_{\text{HTM}} \ge ND\) but \(V(1) < ND\), the unregulated game possesses two pure-strategy Nash equilibria: a Pareto-efficient benign equilibrium (\(w^* = 0\)) and a destructive run equilibrium (\(w^* = 1\)). An unconstrained central bank facility that pledges to advance liquidity against collateral valued at fundamental par \(V_{\text{HTM}}\) uniquely eliminates the run equilibrium, making \(w^* = 0\) the strictly dominant strategy.

Proof Without a backstop, let \(u_i(w)\) denote depositor \(i\)'s payoff from withdrawing early versus waiting until maturity \(T=2\). If depositor \(i\) withdraws early, they receive \(D\) if assets cover claims, or a prorated fraction \(\frac{V(w)}{w ND} D\) if the bank is liquidated. If depositor \(i\) waits, their payoff at \(T=2\) is \(\max\left(0, \frac{V_{\text{HTM}} - w ND}{1 - w}\right)\). When \(w = 1\), late depositors receive \(0\), while early depositors receive \(\min(D, V(1)/N) > 0\). Thus \(w = 1\) is a Nash equilibrium. When the central bank offers emergency lending against collateral at \(V_{\text{HTM}}\), the bank satisfies all withdrawals \(w ND\) using central bank cash loans without liquidating collateral at fire-sale discounts (\(\kappa = 0\)). The bank's remaining assets at \(T=2\) have value \(V_{\text{HTM}} - w ND\). Each remaining depositor receives: \[ \frac{V_{\text{HTM}} - w ND}{(1 - w) N} = \frac{V_{\text{HTM}} - w ND}{N - w N} \ge D \quad \text{since } V_{\text{HTM}} \ge ND \] Because the late payoff is at least \(D\) regardless of the withdrawal choices of other depositors (\(\forall w \in [0, 1]\)), waiting strictly weakly dominates withdrawing early (and strictly dominates if waiting yields interest \(R > 1\)). Consequently, withdrawing is an iteratively strictly dominated strategy, uniquely selecting the efficient equilibrium \(w^* = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 5.1 (Silicon Valley Bank Run & The BTFP) On March 9, 2023, uninsured tech depositors withdrew $42 billion from Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) in ten hours, following an announced $1.8 billion realized loss on its Available-for-Sale (AFS) securities. SVB held $120 billion in long-duration Treasuries and Agency MBS whose market value had fallen by $21 billion due to Fed rate hikes (FRB 2023). With an additional $100 billion of withdrawals scheduled for March 10, California regulators closed the bank. On March 12, the Federal Reserve, Treasury, and FDIC invoked the Systemic Risk Exception and established the Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP), lending up to one year against eligible collateral valued at par rather than market value. By neutralizing duration mark-to-market haircuts, the policy backstop halted contagion across regional US lenders.

Remark 5.1 (The Solvency vs. Liquidity Boundary) Bagehot's rule requires lending only to solvent institutions. In practice, during an acute liquidity panic, market prices incorporate massive fire-sale discounts and liquidity premia. An institution may appear deeply insolvent at temporary market prices while remaining fundamentally solvent if held to maturity. Policy backstops that evaluate collateral at par or hold-to-maturity value resolve this identification problem, but introduce moral hazard if the insolvency is structural rather than purely liquidity-driven (Acharya et al. 2016).

3. Landmark Backstop Interventions

March 2020: The US Treasury "Dash for Cash"

In mid-March 2020, the onset of COVID-19 triggered an unprecedented global scramble for dollar cash. Leveraged hedge funds running Treasury cash-futures basis trades were hit by margin calls, while foreign central banks sold off hundreds of billions in US Treasuries to raise dollar liquidity. Primary dealer balance sheets were overwhelmed under the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) constraints, causing the world's deepest government bond market to experience unprecedented illiquidity and widening spreads (He, Nagel & Song 2022). The Federal Reserve responded with an unconstrained backstop: purchasing over $1 trillion in Treasuries in three weeks, establishing the FIMA Repo Facility, and temporarily exempting reserves and Treasuries from dealer SLR calculation.

July 2012: Draghi's "Whatever It Takes" & OMT

During the height of the European sovereign debt crisis, sovereign bond yields for Spain and Italy widened past 7%, reflecting a self-fulfilling "redenomination risk" premium. On July 26, 2012, ECB President Mario Draghi declared that the ECB was ready to do "whatever it takes to preserve the euro." In September 2012, the ECB introduced the Outright Monetary Transactions (OMT) framework, promising unlimited purchases of sovereign debt for member states complying with European Stability Mechanism (ESM) programs. Remarkably, the ECB never had to purchase a single euro of bonds under OMT: the mere existence of an unconstrained credible backstop permanently crushed the bad equilibrium, compressing peripheral spreads by hundreds of basis points.

October 2022: The UK Gilts Liability-Driven Investment (LDI) Crisis

Following the UK government's "mini-budget" on September 23, 2022, 30-year gilt yields spiked by over 130 bps in days. UK defined-benefit pension schemes employing Liability-Driven Investment (LDI) strategies faced massive collateral calls on interest-rate swaps. To meet margin calls, LDIs were forced to dump long-dated gilts, driving yields higher in a textbook forced-seller feedback loop. On September 28, the Bank of England intervened with a temporary emergency purchase facility of up to £65 billion, acting as a market maker of last resort and arresting the self-reinforcing crash.

4. Inline Lab: Simulating Bank Runs & Central Bank Backstops

The lab below simulates depositor withdrawals, fire-sale asset liquidations, and central bank collateralized par lending facilities.

Lab 1 · Bank Run, Fire Sales & Central Bank Backstops

Simulating rapid depositor runs on duration-mismatched banks shows that without a policy backstop, forced liquidation of illiquid assets creates severe fire-sale losses, whereas a central bank collateralized par lending facility eliminates fire sales and halts the run.

"""Lab GM5-L05-1: Bank Runs, Dash for Cash & Policy Backstop Interventions.

Simulates depositor runs on duration-mismatched banks (Diamond & Dybvig 1983 / FRB 2023 SVB Review),
comparing fire-sale insolvency against a central bank emergency liquidity facility (BTFP / Discount Window).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any


def simulate_bank_run_with_backstop(
    total_deposits: float,
    uninsured_deposit_share: float,
    cash_reserves: float,
    securities_par_value: float,
    securities_market_value: float,
    daily_run_withdrawal_rate: float,
    fire_sale_haircut_slope: float,
    central_bank_facility_active: bool = False,
    n_days: int = 5,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Simulate depositor withdrawals, asset liquidations, and lender-of-last-resort support.

    Parameters
    ----------
    total_deposits : float
        Initial bank deposits in millions USD.
    uninsured_deposit_share : float
        Proportion of deposits above deposit insurance limit (e.g. 0.90 for SVB).
    cash_reserves : float
        Liquid cash held at central bank.
    securities_par_value : float
        Face value of held-to-maturity (HTM) / available-for-sale (AFS) bond portfolio.
    securities_market_value : float
        Mark-to-market value of securities portfolio reflecting interest-rate duration losses.
    daily_run_withdrawal_rate : float
        Fraction of uninsured deposits attempting withdrawal per day.
    fire_sale_haircut_slope : float
        Secondary fire-sale discount parameter as liquidation volume increases.
    central_bank_facility_active : bool
        If True, central bank advances cash against collateral valued at par (e.g. Fed BTFP).
    n_days : int
        Days simulated.

    Returns
    -------
    dict[str, float | bool | list[float]]
        Solvency status, cumulative withdrawals, fire-sale losses, and terminal equity.
    """
    cash = cash_reserves
    securities_m2m = securities_market_value
    deposits = total_deposits
    uninsured_deposits = total_deposits * uninsured_deposit_share

    bank_failed = False
    failure_day = -1
    total_fire_sale_loss = 0.0
    cash_history = [cash]
    deposits_history = [deposits]

    for day in range(1, n_days + 1):
        # Withdrawals demanded by panicking uninsured depositors
        withdrawal_demand = uninsured_deposits * daily_run_withdrawal_rate

        if central_bank_facility_active:
            # Policy backstop: bank pledges securities at PAR to receive discount window/BTFP cash
            borrowed_from_cb = min(securities_par_value, withdrawal_demand)
            cash += borrowed_from_cb
            securities_par_value -= borrowed_from_cb

        # Fulfill withdrawals using cash
        if cash >= withdrawal_demand:
            cash -= withdrawal_demand
            deposits -= withdrawal_demand
            uninsured_deposits -= withdrawal_demand
        else:
            # Cash exhausted: must fire-sale securities in open market
            deficit = withdrawal_demand - cash
            deposits -= cash
            uninsured_deposits -= cash
            cash = 0.0

            # Fire sale discount: market value drops as selling pressure mounts
            discount = min(0.35, fire_sale_haircut_slope * (deficit / securities_market_value))
            effective_price_ratio = 1.0 - discount
            securities_needed = deficit / effective_price_ratio

            fire_sale_loss = securities_needed * discount
            total_fire_sale_loss += fire_sale_loss

            if securities_m2m >= securities_needed:
                securities_m2m -= securities_needed
                deposits -= deficit
                uninsured_deposits -= deficit
            else:
                # Bank runs out of liquidatable assets: failure
                bank_failed = True
                failure_day = day
                break

        cash_history.append(cash)
        deposits_history.append(deposits)

    terminal_equity = cash + securities_m2m - deposits if not bank_failed else -total_fire_sale_loss

    return {
        "bank_failed": bank_failed,
        "failure_day": failure_day,
        "terminal_deposits": deposits_history[-1],
        "total_fire_sale_loss": total_fire_sale_loss,
        "terminal_equity": terminal_equity,
    }


def main() -> None:
    # Stylized parameters resembling Silicon Valley Bank (March 2023)
    deposits_0 = 175000.0  # $175B total deposits
    uninsured_share = 0.88  # 88% uninsured deposits
    cash_0 = 15000.0  # $15B cash
    sec_par = 120000.0  # $120B par securities
    sec_m2m = 99000.0  # $99B market value ($21B unrealized duration loss)

    # Without central bank backstop
    run_no_backstop = simulate_bank_run_with_backstop(
        total_deposits=deposits_0,
        uninsured_deposit_share=uninsured_share,
        cash_reserves=cash_0,
        securities_par_value=sec_par,
        securities_market_value=sec_m2m,
        daily_run_withdrawal_rate=0.28,  # 28% run per day ($42B/day)
        fire_sale_haircut_slope=0.25,
        central_bank_facility_active=False,
        n_days=3,
    )

    # With central bank policy backstop (lending at par against eligible securities)
    run_with_backstop = simulate_bank_run_with_backstop(
        total_deposits=deposits_0,
        uninsured_deposit_share=uninsured_share,
        cash_reserves=cash_0,
        securities_par_value=sec_par,
        securities_market_value=sec_m2m,
        daily_run_withdrawal_rate=0.28,
        fire_sale_haircut_slope=0.25,
        central_bank_facility_active=True,
        n_days=3,
    )

    print("Initial Deposits ($M):", round(deposits_0, 1))
    print("Without Backstop - Bank Failed:", run_no_backstop["bank_failed"])
    print("Without Backstop - Failure Day:", run_no_backstop["failure_day"])
    print("Without Backstop - Fire Sale Loss ($M):", round(float(run_no_backstop["total_fire_sale_loss"]), 1))
    print("With Backstop - Bank Failed:", run_with_backstop["bank_failed"])
    print("With Backstop - Fire Sale Loss ($M):", round(float(run_with_backstop["total_fire_sale_loss"]), 1))
    print("With Backstop - Terminal Deposits ($M):", round(float(run_with_backstop["terminal_deposits"]), 1))

5. Where the Canon Breaks: Moral Hazard & The Shadow Banking Boundary

The classical doctrine of central banking treats the lender of last resort as a simple emergency cash window for commercial banks subject to reserve requirements. In modern finance, this paradigm breaks along three critical dimensions:

  • The Regulatory Perimeter Problem: Modern funding runs occur in non-bank financial intermediaries (hedge funds, LDI pension schemes, private credit funds, stablecoins) outside the direct regulatory umbrella. When the central bank backstops non-banks, it socializes market risk without corresponding prudential supervision.
  • Collateral Velocity & Rehypothecation: In secured funding markets, a single Treasury bond may be rehypothecated across multiple financial institutions. A backstop that stabilizes primary dealers does not necessarily resolve collateral chains in shadow repo networks.
  • Fiscal-Monetary Entanglement: Interventions like the Fed's corporate credit facilities or the ECB's sovereign bond purchases take on credit and duration risks that border on fiscal policy, raising democratic legitimacy and institutional independence concerns.

6. Misconceptions

  1. A bank run only occurs when a financial institution is fundamentally insolvent.

    Diamond and Dybvig (1983) prove that perfectly solvent institutions can fail from purely coordinated liquidity panics if short-term depositors rush to withdraw before long-term assets can mature.

  2. Central bank emergency lending facilities always put taxpayer money at immediate risk of loss.

    When lending against high-quality collateral with adequate haircuts, central bank backstops virtually never suffer credit losses. Facilities like the BTFP and 2008 Primary Dealer Credit Facility yielded full repayment plus interest to the central bank.

  3. A policy backstop must execute massive market purchases to be effective.

    As demonstrated by Mario Draghi's Outright Monetary Transactions (OMT) in 2012, a credible and legally unconstrained policy backstop can eliminate a bad run equilibrium purely through market expectations without executing a single market purchase.

7. Problem Set

A regional bank holds $100 billion in deposits (80% uninsured) and $10 billion in cash reserves. In a digital bank run, uninsured depositors withdraw 25% of their funds on Day 1. Calculate the remaining cash reserves and determine whether the bank must liquidate securities.

Solution

Uninsured deposits equal \(0.80 \times \$100\text{B} = \$80\text{B}\). A 25% withdrawal demands \(0.25 \times \$80\text{B} = \$20\text{B}\) in cash. Because initial cash is only $10 billion, cash reserves are fully exhausted ($0 remaining), and the bank faces an immediate $10 billion deficit that mandates emergency securities liquidation or borrowing.

Suppose a bank must liquidate $5 billion of long-duration bonds in an illiquid market where the secondary market price discount is given by \(d = 0.04 \times (\text{Liquidation Volume} / \$1\text{B})\). Calculate the total fire-sale loss incurred by the bank.

Solution

With volume = $5B, the discount rate is \(d = 0.04 \times 5 = 0.20\) (20%). The effective price ratio is \(1 - 0.20 = 0.80\). The face value of securities required to raise $5 billion in cash is \(\$5\text{B} / 0.80 = \$6.25\text{B}\). The total deadweight fire-sale loss realized is \(\$6.25\text{B} - \$5.00\text{B} = \$1.25\text{B}\).

In the Federal Reserve's Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP) established in March 2023, what was the primary structural difference in collateral valuation compared to traditional Discount Window lending?

Solution

The traditional Discount Window lends against collateral at prevailing mark-to-market value subject to haircuts, whereas the BTFP advanced cash loans against eligible collateral valued at par (100% of face value) with zero haircut, directly insulating banks from duration-driven market value losses.

Prove that under the Diamond-Dybvig (1983) payoff structure, full government deposit insurance completely eliminates the run equilibrium without requiring government expenditure along the equilibrium path.

Solution

Let deposit insurance guarantee that every depositor receives \(r_1 = 1\) at \(T=1\) or \(r_2 = R > 1\) at \(T=2\), backed by government taxation authority. If all other depositors run at \(T=1\), a patient depositor still receives \(R > 1\) at \(T=2\) from the insurance fund. Since \(R > 1\), patient depositors have no incentive to withdraw early. With zero patient depositors running, only truly impatient depositors withdraw, the bank remains solvent, and no insurance payouts are ever triggered in equilibrium.

Show that a market maker of last resort (MMLR) offering to purchase collateral at price floor \(P_{\text{floor}}\) truncates the downward spiral in Shleifer-Vishny (2011) fire-sale models.

Solution

In Shleifer-Vishny fire sales, price is determined by the balance-sheet capacity of unconstrained specialists: \(P(Q) = \bar{V} - \kappa Q\). If liquidation volume \(Q\) expands, \(P\) drops indefinitely. When a policy authority establishes an elastic bid at \(P_{\text{floor}}\), the clearing price satisfies \(P^* = \max(P(Q), P_{\text{floor}})\). This caps liquidation losses at \(\bar{V} - P_{\text{floor}}\), preventing margin calls from exceeding intermediary equity and terminating the feedback loop.

Prove that relaxing dealer balance-sheet constraints (e.g., exempting reserves and Treasuries from the Supplementary Leverage Ratio) strictly increases primary dealer capacity to absorb market sales.

Solution

Under the standard SLR, Tier 1 Capital must satisfy \(K \ge \rho (A_{\text{risk}} + A_{\text{Treasuries}} + A_{\text{reserves}})\). The maximum Treasury holding capacity is \(A_{\text{Treasuries}} \le K/\rho - A_{\text{risk}} - A_{\text{reserves}}\). If Treasuries and reserves are exempted, the constraint simplifies to \(K \ge \rho A_{\text{risk}}\), decoupling government bond absorption from regulatory leverage limits and removing the binding balance-sheet bottleneck.

According to the Federal Reserve Board Review of the Supervision and Regulation of Silicon Valley Bank (2023), how many billions of dollars in deposits were withdrawn from SVB on March 9, 2023, and how many billions were scheduled for withdrawal on March 10 before regulators intervened?

Solution

According to the official Fed report authored by Vice Chair Michael Barr, depositors withdrew over $42 billion in deposits on March 9, 2023, and had queued an unprecedented additional $100 billion of withdrawal orders for March 10, representing a total run of over 81% of total bank deposits in approximately 36 hours.

In He, Nagel & Song (2022), what was the estimated magnitude of foreign central bank sales of US Treasuries during the March 2020 dash for cash?

Solution

He, Nagel, and Song document that foreign official institutions sold approximately $300 billion in US Treasuries during March 2020 to raise US dollar liquidity for domestic currency defense and crisis support, overwhelming primary dealer balance sheets.

How much total emergency bond purchases did the Bank of England execute during its October 2022 LDI intervention, and what was the maximum authorized capacity of the facility?

Solution

The Bank of England authorized a temporary purchase facility of up to £65 billion (consisting of £5 billion daily auctions over 13 trading days). However, because the credible announcement restored gilt market stability, the Bank executed only £19.3 billion in actual purchases before safely unwinding the entire portfolio at a profit.

Analyze the economic trade-off between providing emergency central bank liquidity at par (as in the BTFP) versus at fair market value with punitive haircuts (Bagehot's classical rule).

Solution

Lending at par immediately neutralizes duration-driven fire-sale insolvency, arresting panic contagion across the banking system. However, it transfers duration risk to the public balance sheet and distorts market discipline by subsidizing poorly managed duration risk, encouraging banks to hold long-dated unhedged assets under the expectation of a central bank bailout.

Explain why the proliferation of digital mobile banking and social media financial communities (e.g., Venture Capital WhatsApp groups during SVB) altered the structural velocity of bank runs.

Solution

Historical runs required physical queuing at brick-and-mortar branches, imposing mechanical latency on deposit outflows. In contrast, modern mobile banking allows near-instantaneous digital transfers of billions of dollars, while real-time social media accelerates informational cascades, condensing multi-week runs into a matter of hours.

Design an optimal macroprudential liquidity framework to prevent fire-sale cascades in non-bank Liability-Driven Investment (LDI) pension funds without relying on central bank monetization.

Solution

An optimal LDI regulatory framework requires: (1) mandatory dynamic cash collateral buffers calibrated to 300+ bps interest rate shocks; (2) standardized bilateral liquidity facilities with commercial banks backed by high-quality corporate bonds; and (3) explicit stress-testing of synthetic derivative overlay leverage under simultaneous sovereign yield curve steepening.

9. Retrieval Quiz

  1. What is the core economic insight of the Diamond and Dybvig (1983) bank run model?

    Options

    Diamond-Dybvig proves that maturity mismatch creates strategic complementarity, producing an inefficient run equilibrium regardless of initial asset solvency.

  2. What was the primary driver of Silicon Valley Bank's balance-sheet vulnerability prior to its March 2023 collapse?

    Options

    SVB held long-duration Treasuries and Agency MBS with large unrealized duration losses funded by flight-prone uninsured corporate deposits.

  3. How did the Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP) differ from the Federal Reserve's traditional Discount Window?

    Options

    The BTFP advanced one-year loans against eligible securities valued at par, removing the haircut and duration markdown of market collateral valuation.

  4. During the March 2020 US Treasury "dash for cash," why were primary dealers unable to absorb the surge in sovereign bond selling?

    Options

    Primary dealers hit post-crisis regulatory leverage constraints (SLR), which penalized them for holding large balance sheets of Treasuries.

  5. What was the primary economic mechanism that made Mario Draghi's 2012 "whatever it takes" speech and OMT framework so successful?

    Options

    The credible commitment of unlimited purchases removed the panic equilibrium, restoring market stability without requiring bond purchases.

  6. In the October 2022 UK gilts crisis, what type of financial institutions were forced to sell long-dated gilts to meet margin calls?

    Options

    Defined-benefit pension funds running LDI strategies faced sudden collateral calls on derivative hedges, forcing them to dump gilts.

  7. According to Walter Bagehot's classical rule, how should a central bank respond to an internal liquidity drain?

    Options

    Bagehot's classical dictum advises lending freely to solvent banks against sound collateral, but at a rate high enough to deter unnecessary reliance.

  8. What is the main systemic hazard created when central banks repeatedly provide unconditional liquidity backstops during asset market drops?

    Options

    Unconditional backstops create moral hazard (the "Fed Put"), incentivizing institutions to run excessive leverage and duration mismatch.
