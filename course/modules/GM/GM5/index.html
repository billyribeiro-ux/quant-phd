Crises as Mechanisms

Financial crises are not inexplicable acts of nature or erratic bouts of market psychology; they are deterministic, path-dependent mechanical chain reactions triggered when vulnerable balance-sheet structures interact with liquidity constraints and market illiquidity. GM5 dissects the anatomy of global financial dislocations from primary regulatory records and foundational theory. You will master formal causal mechanism mapping, balance-sheet deleveraging and margin spirals, speculative attacks on currency pegs, crowded carry-trade unwinds, bank runs under duration mismatch, and central bank emergency backstops across ten landmark historical episodes.

  • Prerequisites: GM4 Macro Strategy Construction and SR1 Systemic Risk Foundations.
  • Project: Crisis Mechanism Dossier, graded by autograder and rubric (24 hours).
  • Hours: 80 study hours (56 h across 5 lessons, 24 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Construct formal causal crisis mechanism maps separating initial trigger shocks, amplification propagation channels, and terminal stabilization backstops.
  2. Derive the coupled loss and margin spirals of Brunnermeier & Pedersen (2009) and quantify forced-seller liquidation volumes under leverage constraints.
  3. Analyze currency-peg abandonment dynamics, distinguishing between first-generation reserve depletion and second-generation escape clauses.
  4. Model carry-trade crowding, negative return skewness, and endogenous unwind cascades triggered by funding-currency rate shocks and volatility spikes.
  5. Formalize central bank lender-of-last-resort interventions, evaluating price preservation and spiral multiplier attenuation across collateral discount windows and asset purchases.
  6. Synthesize primary archival evidence into production-grade quantitative crisis simulators capable of stress-testing institutional portfolios against systemic runs.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight testing prerequisite GM4 and SR1 concepts, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the capstone project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In a margin call resolution where a fund has $100,000 margin deficit, $20,000 unencumbered cash, and faces a collateral haircut \(h = 0.20\) on an asset trading at \(P = \$50.00\), what is the exact minimum liquidation quantity \(\Delta Q\) required to satisfy the margin constraint?

    Options

    Cash reduces the deficit dollar-for-dollar to $80,000. Because selling \(\Delta Q\) at price \(P\) pays down debt by \(\Delta Q \cdot P\) and reduces required margin by \(h \cdot \Delta Q \cdot P\), the post-sale deficit is eliminated when \(h \cdot \Delta Q \cdot P = \text{Remaining Deficit}\). Dividing by \(h \cdot P = \$10.00\) yields 8,000 units. Rung 1 skill check.

  2. When forced selling of \(\Delta Q\) units causes a linear price impact \(\Delta P = \lambda \Delta Q\) under Kyle (1985), what is the effective average execution price per unit realized by the liquidating fund?

    Options

    With linear temporary price impact along a continuous liquidation trajectory, the price degrades linearly from \(P_0\) to \(P_0 - \lambda \Delta Q\). Integrating the marginal price yields the average execution price \(\bar{P}_{\text{exec}} = P_0 - \frac{1}{2} \lambda \Delta Q\). Rung 2 skill check.

  3. In Brunnermeier & Pedersen (2009), what distinguishes the "loss spiral" from the "margin spiral"?

    Options

    Under constant haircuts, asset price declines erode net worth and force sales (loss spiral). When volatility rises, margin requirements also expand (\(h_t > h_0\)), forcing additional liquidations (margin spiral). SR1 prerequisite.

  4. Under the Diamond & Dybvig (1983) banking model, why does a sequential-service constraint generate a bank run equilibrium?

    Options

    First-come, first-served payment combined with illiquid long-term assets creates strategic complementarity: if patient depositors believe others will withdraw, their optimal response is to withdraw immediately before cash runs out. SR1 prerequisite.

  5. In Shleifer & Vishny (1997), why do professional arbitrageurs fail to stabilize prices when an asset deviates severely from fundamental value?

    Options

    Because investors judge arbitrageurs by short-term performance, interim price divergence leads to capital withdrawals, forcing arbitrageurs to liquidate precisely when the mispricing is widest. SR1 prerequisite.

  6. In Krugman's (1979) canonical first-generation balance-of-payments model, when does the speculative attack against a fixed currency peg occur?

    Options

    No-arbitrage conditions prevent discrete jumps in the exchange rate at the attack moment. Rational speculators attack exactly when the shadow floating rate reaches the peg parity, exhausting remaining reserves in one sudden wave. GM4/E1 prerequisite.

  7. What structural balance-sheet mismatch was the primary catalyst in the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023?

    Options

    SVB held long-duration fixed-rate bonds classified as Held-to-Maturity (HTM) while funded by concentrated, uninsured tech start-up deposits. Rapid Fed tightening caused massive unrealized mark-to-market losses, sparking an uninsured depositor run. SR1 prerequisite.

  8. In liability-driven investment (LDI) strategies utilized by UK pension schemes in September 2022, what triggered the forced selling of long-dated UK Gilts?

    Options

    LDI funds used interest rate swaps to hedge liability duration. When gilt yields spiked following the mini-budget, swap values fell precipitously, triggering billions in collateral margin calls that funds met by dumping gilts, worsening the spike. GM4 prerequisite.

  9. Why do currency carry trades exhibit pronounced negative skewness and "peanuts vs. steamroller" return distributions?

    Options

    As documented in Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen (2008), carry trades offer persistent positive carry in low-vol regimes, but carry unwinds correlate with global risk-off shocks, producing extreme negative crash tails. GM4 prerequisite.

  10. What is the primary operational mechanism of a central bank "lender of last resort" facility such as the Fed's Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP)?

    Options

    By valuing collateral at par, the BTFP eliminated the need for banks to realize duration losses through open-market fire sales, directly short-circuiting the liquidity spiral. SR1 prerequisite.

Curriculum & Lesson Map

GM5 is structured into five doctoral modules spanning 56 lecture and lab hours:

  1. Lesson L01 · The Anatomy of Financial Crises & Mechanism Mapping

    11 study hours. Methodological foundations of causal crisis mechanism graphs, separating balance-sheet triggers, network contagion paths, and policy backstops. Introduces crisis-mechanism-map.

  2. Lesson L02 · Leverage, Margin Cascades & Forced Sellers

    11 study hours. Balance-sheet leverage, mark-to-market margin calls, endogenous haircut spirals, and asset fire sales. Case studies: 1998 LTCM, 2008 Lehman repo freeze, and 2022 UK gilts LDI crisis. Introduces forced-seller-dynamics.

  3. Lesson L03 · Currency Pegs, Speculative Attacks & Asymmetric Defenses

    11 study hours. First-generation reserve depletion versus second-generation escape-clause models. Shadow exchange rates, interest rate defense costs, and asymmetric peg defense limits. Case studies: 1992 ERM Black Wednesday, 1997 Asian financial crisis, and 2015 Swiss franc de-peg. Introduces peg-abandonment.

  4. Lesson L04 · Carry Trades, Crowding & Endogenous Unwinds

    11 study hours. Uncovered interest parity failure, negative skewness, crash risk, crowded position liquidation, and cross-market volatility spillovers. Case studies: 1998 yen unwind, 2007-08 carry collapse, and August 2024 yen-carry shock. Introduces carry-unwind.

  5. Lesson L05 · Funding Shocks, Fire Sales & Policy Backstops

    12 study hours. Wholesale funding runs, dealer balance-sheet capacity constraints, Treasury market illiquidity ("dash for cash"), sovereign doom loops, and central bank emergency lending facilities. Case studies: 2010–12 Eurozone debt crisis, March 2020 Treasury dislocation, and March 2023 SVB run. Introduces policy-backstop.

Capstone Project Ladder

The 24-hour capstone project, Crisis Mechanism Dossier & Stress Simulator, provides an end-to-end quantitative laboratory spanning analytical mechanics, completion exercises, autograded simulation engines, and structural policy stress testing:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked Example

    Walk through the complete reference implementation of the coupled crisis simulator, tracing liquidity spirals, collateral haircuts, and non-linear market impact across deterministic baseline parameters.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion Problem

    Complete the core computational engine by implementing the fixed-point forced liquidation function, endogenous hair-cut sensitivity, and price-impact clearing equations.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent Project

    Construct the full CrisisSimulator pipeline in reference.py, passing all 7 rigorous property and invariant tests across 50 pseudo-random seeds, and author an archival empirical crisis dossier analyzing the August 2024 yen unwind.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch Goal

    Extend the engine to multi-asset cross-contagion networks with endogenous dealer balance-sheet capacity constraints, evaluating regulatory leverage relaxation during sudden liquidity drains.
