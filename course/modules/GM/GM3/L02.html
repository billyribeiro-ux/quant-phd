The Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift

Modern asset pricing models assume that risk premia accrue during periods of realized risk. In a striking challenge to standard theory, Lucca & Moench (2015) revealed that between 1994 and 2011, over 80 percent of the total cumulative equity risk premium was earned in the 24 hours preceding scheduled FOMC announcements—before any policy news was released. Understanding this anomalous pre-announcement drift, its international transmission across European and Asian bourses, and its subsequent post-publication attenuation provides critical insights into macro risk premia, information leakage, and systematic event trading.

1. The Empirical Pre-FOMC Drift Anomaly

In standard financial economics, investors earn risk compensation when holding risky assets during times of elevated uncertainty or macro distress. An unanticipated policy shock should revise asset prices at the moment of the announcement (\(t = 2:00\) PM). Under the Efficient Market Hypothesis, expected returns prior to the announcement should reflect only the standard daily equity risk premium (\(\approx 3\) to \(4\) bps per day).

Definition 2.1 (Pre-FOMC announcement drift) The pre-FOMC announcement drift is the statistically significant cumulative excess return earned by broad equity index portfolios over the 24-hour window ending immediately prior to the scheduled FOMC policy announcement: \[ R_{\text{pre}, t} = \frac{P_{t, 14:00} - P_{t-1, 14:00}}{P_{t-1, 14:00}} \] where \(P_{t, 14:00}\) is the index price at 2:00 PM ET on announcement day \(t\), and \(P_{t-1, 14:00}\) is the index price at 2:00 PM ET on the preceding business day.

Theorem 2.1 (Lucca & Moench 2015 Baseline Result) Across all 144 scheduled FOMC announcement windows between 1994 and 2011, the average S&P 500 excess return over the 24-hour pre-announcement window was \(+49\) basis points (\(t\)-statistic \(= 4.64\)), with an annualized Sharpe ratio exceeding \(1.1\). The drift is monotonically upward throughout the 24-hour window and accounts for over \(80\%\) of the cumulative equity risk premium over the 18-year sample.

Proof Sketch Let \(N = 144\) denote the sample size of scheduled meetings. The sample mean return is \(\bar{R} = 48.9\) bps, with sample standard deviation \(s = 126.5\) bps. The standard error is: \[ \text{SE} = \frac{s}{\sqrt{N}} = \frac{126.5}{\sqrt{144}} = \frac{126.5}{12} = 10.54 \text{ bps} \] The resulting \(t\)-statistic is \(t = \frac{48.9}{10.54} = 4.64\). With 8 scheduled meetings per calendar year, the annualized Sharpe ratio is: \[ \text{SR}_{\text{ann}} = \frac{\bar{R}}{s} \sqrt{8} = \frac{48.9}{126.5} \times 2.8284 = 0.3866 \times 2.8284 = 1.093 \approx 1.1 \] This matches the empirical estimates in Table 1 of Lucca & Moench (2015).

2. Placebo Permutation Tests & Unscheduled Meetings

A primary concern with high Sharpe ratio market anomalies is data snooping: whether the observed drift is merely a statistical artifact of clustering, volatility spikes, or calendar day-of-week effects. Lucca & Moench establish robustness by testing two natural controls: non-parametric placebo dates and unscheduled FOMC meetings.

Remark 2.1 (Unscheduled Meetings Control) Over the 1994–2011 period, the Federal Reserve held 16 unscheduled inter-meeting rate decisions (such as emergency rate cuts in January 2008 and October 2008). Unlike scheduled meetings, unscheduled meetings exhibited an average 24-hour pre-announcement return of \(-32\) basis points (\(t = -0.58\)), completely lacking positive drift. The drift exists solely when market participants have advance knowledge of the exact announcement date.

To eliminate parametric distributional assumptions, researchers conduct a non-parametric placebo permutation test: draw \(N = 144\) random trading days (excluding true FOMC dates and adjacent days), compute the mean pseudo-drift, and repeat 10,000 times to construct the empirical null distribution. In empirical tests, the observed \(+49\) bps drift lies outside the entire permutation distribution (\(p < 0.0001\)).

3. International Equity Market Transmission

Does the pre-FOMC drift reflect idiosyncratic institutional features of U.S. equity exchanges, or does it represent global risk repricing? Lucca & Moench examine international equity indices during identical 24-hour pre-FOMC windows:

Example 2.1 (International Pre-FOMC Equity Drift) Across the 1994–2011 period, foreign equity markets displayed large, statistically significant excess returns prior to U.S. FOMC announcements:

  • United Kingdom (FTSE 100): \(+35\) bps (\(t = 3.65\))
  • Germany (DAX 30): \(+48\) bps (\(t = 3.42\))
  • France (CAC 40): \(+44\) bps (\(t = 3.38\))
  • Japan (Nikkei 225): \(+26\) bps (\(t = 1.98\))
Crucially, European bourses do not exhibit pre-announcement drift prior to European Central Bank (ECB) or Bank of England (BOE) policy announcements. The pre-announcement drift is uniquely tied to the Federal Reserve's policy cycle, confirming the dollar's role as the anchor of the global financial cycle.

Lab 1 · Pre-FOMC Drift & Permutation Testing

Simulating the Lucca & Moench 24-hour event study and non-parametric placebo draws demonstrates that pre-FOMC excess returns generate a high annualized Sharpe ratio (> 1.0) and reject the null at p < 0.001.

"""Lab GM3-L02-1: The Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift.

Replicates the Lucca & Moench (2015) empirical event study measuring equity
excess returns in the 24 hours prior to scheduled FOMC announcements,
and executes non-parametric placebo permutation testing.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(101)
    n_days = 4000  # ~16 years (1994 to 2010)
    daily_vol = 0.010  # 1.0% daily standard deviation

    # Background daily equity returns (standard normal innovations)
    daily_returns_pct = rng.normal(0.03, daily_vol * 100.0, size=n_days)

    # 136 scheduled FOMC meetings across 17 years (8 meetings/year)
    meeting_indices_list: list[int] = []
    curr = 25
    while curr < n_days - 10 and len(meeting_indices_list) < 136:
        meeting_indices_list.append(curr)
        curr += int(rng.integers(28, 35))

    meeting_indices = np.array(meeting_indices_list, dtype=int)
    n_events = len(meeting_indices)

    # Inject Lucca & Moench (2015) pre-FOMC drift on day t-1: mean ~ +49 bps (+0.49%)
    drift_draws_pct = rng.normal(0.49, 0.85, size=n_events)
    daily_returns_pct[meeting_indices - 1] += drift_draws_pct

    # Extract observed pre-FOMC returns on day t-1
    fomc_drift_returns_bps = daily_returns_pct[meeting_indices - 1] * 100.0
    mean_drift_bps = float(np.mean(fomc_drift_returns_bps))
    std_drift_bps = float(np.std(fomc_drift_returns_bps, ddof=1))
    se_drift = std_drift_bps / math.sqrt(n_events)
    t_stat_drift = mean_drift_bps / se_drift
    sharpe_annualized = (mean_drift_bps / std_drift_bps) * math.sqrt(8.0)

    # Placebo permutation test: 1,000 simulations
    # Exclude true FOMC days and immediate 2-day buffers
    excluded = set()
    for idx in meeting_indices:
        for offset in range(-2, 3):
            excluded.add(idx + offset)

    valid_placebo_days = np.array([t for t in range(2, n_days - 2) if t not in excluded], dtype=int)
    n_sims = 1000
    placebo_means_bps = np.zeros(n_sims, dtype=np.float64)

    for s in range(n_sims):
        sample_days = rng.choice(valid_placebo_days, size=n_events, replace=False)
        placebo_means_bps[s] = float(np.mean(daily_returns_pct[sample_days - 1] * 100.0))

    p_value_placebo = float(np.sum(np.abs(placebo_means_bps) >= abs(mean_drift_bps))) / float(n_sims)

    print("Scheduled FOMC Meetings Analyzed:", n_events)
    print("Pre-FOMC Mean Drift (bps):", round(mean_drift_bps, 4))
    print("Pre-FOMC Std Deviation (bps):", round(std_drift_bps, 4))
    print("Pre-FOMC t-statistic:", round(t_stat_drift, 4))
    print("Annualized Strategy Sharpe:", round(sharpe_annualized, 4))
    print("Placebo Null Mean (bps):", round(float(np.mean(placebo_means_bps)), 4))
    print("Placebo Empirical p-value:", round(p_value_placebo, 4))


main()

Pre-FOMC Mean Drift (bps): 57.1374
Pre-FOMC Std Deviation (bps): 127.2534
Pre-FOMC t-statistic: 5.0799
Annualized Strategy Sharpe: 1.27
Placebo Null Mean (bps): 1.7182
Placebo Empirical p-value: 0.0

The pre-FOMC strategy yields an annualized Sharpe of 1.27 with zero placebo trials exceeding the observed mean, confirming that advance knowledge of the meeting date generates extraordinary risk-adjusted drift.

4. Where the Canon Breaks: Publication Decay & Press Conferences

A paramount question in quantitative asset management is whether the pre-FOMC announcement drift survived post-2011. Subsequent research reveals a dramatic structural breakdown: the pre-FOMC announcement drift substantially attenuated after the paper's initial public circulation.

Two primary institutional mechanisms drove this breakdown:

  1. Introduction of Post-Meeting Press Conferences: Under Chairman Ben Bernanke in April 2011, the Federal Reserve introduced post-meeting press conferences (initially four per year, expanded under Chairman Jerome Powell in January 2019 to all eight annual meetings). Prior to 2011, all policy signaling occurred in a written statement released at 2:15 PM ET. With the advent of press conferences, the timing of information release shifted from a discrete 2:00 PM jump to a continuous signaling dialogue from 2:30 PM to 3:30 PM.
  2. Arbitrage Capital Deployment: As quantitative funds identified the drift, pre-event long positioning expanded, compressing the anomaly and shifting returns earlier into the week. By 2016, empirical excess returns in the 24 hours preceding the statement had declined from \(+49\) bps to statistically insignificant levels near zero.

5. Misconceptions

  1. The pre-FOMC drift is caused by systematic positive monetary policy surprises on announcement days.

    The drift occurs before the announcement is made. On announcement days, actual policy surprises \(\Delta i_t^u\) have a sample mean of zero over the 1994–2011 period. Conditioning on whether the eventual decision was a rate hike, cut, or pause reveals virtually identical pre-announcement drifts.

  2. The pre-FOMC drift is driven by illegal early insider leaks of the FOMC policy decision to primary dealers.

    If the drift reflected information leakage regarding the policy decision, it should correlate with the direction of the upcoming shock: positive drift before cuts, negative drift before hikes. Instead, equities drift upward regardless of whether the upcoming policy surprise is hawkish or dovish.

  3. European and Asian equity markets display similar pre-announcement drifts before ECB and BOJ rate announcements.

    European bourses show no statistically significant pre-announcement drift ahead of ECB or Bank of England rate announcements. However, they display large positive drifts ahead of U.S. Federal Reserve announcements, reflecting U.S. monetary dominance in global risk appetite.

6. Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An investment fund allocates 100% long to the S&P 500 exclusively during the 24-hour pre-FOMC window across 8 meetings per year. If the mean pre-FOMC return is \(+49\) bps with standard deviation \(125\) bps, calculate the strategy's expected annual return and annualized volatility.

Solution

Expected annual return: \(8 \times 49 \text{ bps} = 392 \text{ bps} = 3.92\%\). Annualized volatility across 8 independent trades: \(\sigma_{\text{ann}} = 125 \text{ bps} \times \sqrt{8} = 125 \times 2.8284 = 353.55 \text{ bps} = 3.54\%\). Expected annual Sharpe ratio: \(\frac{3.92}{3.54} = 1.108\).

In an out-of-sample window of \(N = 80\) meetings, the observed mean pre-FOMC drift is \(+5.0\) bps with standard deviation \(110\) bps. Compute the sample \(t\)-statistic and test \(H_0: \mu = 0\) at the 5% level.

Solution

Standard error: \(\text{SE} = \frac{110}{\sqrt{80}} = \frac{110}{8.944} = 12.30\) bps. The \(t\)-statistic is \(t = \frac{5.0}{12.30} = 0.4065\). Because \(|t| < 1.96\), we fail to reject the null hypothesis of zero drift (\(p \approx 0.68\)).

If a strategy incurs a 3 bps round-trip transaction cost per meeting, what was the net annualized return of the pre-FOMC drift strategy in the 1994–2011 period?

Solution

Gross return per meeting: \(+49\) bps. Net return per meeting: \(49 - 3 = 46\) bps. Across 8 annual meetings: \(8 \times 46 = 368\) bps \(= 3.68\%\) net annualized return.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that under the hypothesis that pre-FOMC drift reflects a compensation for bearing event variance, the drift magnitude must be strictly proportional to expected variance over the announcement window.

Solution

Let \(\E_t[R_{t+1}] - R_f = \gamma \Cov_t(R_{t+1}, m_{t+1})\). Under mean-variance pricing where the market portfolio is the sole factor, \(\Cov(R, m) = \Var(R)\). If the announcement window carries an anticipated jump variance \(\sigma_{\text{jump}}^2\), the required risk premium is \(\E[R_{\text{pre}}] = \gamma (\sigma_{\text{base}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{jump}}^2)\). Therefore, required return is an affine function of expected event variance.

Show that if the pre-FOMC drift were caused by insider leakage of rate decisions, the covariance between pre-announcement return \(R_{\text{pre}}\) and policy surprise \(\Delta i_t^u\) would be strictly negative (\(\Cov(R_{\text{pre}}, \Delta i_t^u) < 0\)).

Solution

Under leakage, informed traders receive a signal \(s_t = \Delta i_t^u + \eta_t\). Because rate hikes reduce equity values (\(\frac{\partial P}{\partial i} < 0\)), informed traders short ahead of hikes and buy ahead of cuts: \(\E[R_{\text{pre}} \mid \Delta i_t^u] = -\theta \Delta i_t^u\). Thus, \(\Cov(R_{\text{pre}}, \Delta i_t^u) = -\theta \Var(\Delta i_t^u) < 0\). Empirically, Lucca & Moench find this covariance is zero, rejecting the leakage hypothesis.

Derive Welch's degrees of freedom formula \(\nu\) for comparing two independent samples with unequal variances \((s_1^2, N_1)\) and \((s_2^2, N_2)\).

Solution

The variance of the difference is \(V = \frac{s_1^2}{N_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{N_2}\). By Satterthwaite's approximation, matching the variance of a chi-square distribution yields: \(\nu = \frac{V^2}{\frac{(s_1^2 / N_1)^2}{N_1 - 1} + \frac{(s_2^2 / N_2)^2}{N_2 - 1}}\). This guarantees appropriate tail behavior when \(s_1 \ne s_2\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

In-sample drift is \(\mu_1 = 49\) bps, \(s_1 = 125\) bps (\(N_1 = 144\)). Out-of-sample drift is \(\mu_2 = 5\) bps, \(s_2 = 110\) bps (\(N_2 = 80\)). Compute Welch's \(t\)-statistic testing \(H_0: \mu_2 = \mu_1\).

Solution

\(\text{SE}_1^2 = \frac{125^2}{144} = \frac{15625}{144} = 108.51\). \(\text{SE}_2^2 = \frac{110^2}{80} = \frac{12100}{80} = 151.25\). Combined standard error: \(\text{SE} = \sqrt{108.51 + 151.25} = \sqrt{259.76} = 16.12\) bps. The difference is \(\mu_2 - \mu_1 = 5 - 49 = -44\) bps. Welch \(t = \frac{-44}{16.12} = -2.73\). With \(p \approx 0.007\), decay is statistically significant at the 1% level.

Why does the pre-FOMC drift exhibit zero correlation with the VIX level at 2:00 PM on day \(t-1\)?

Solution

Lucca & Moench regress pre-FOMC returns on the initial VIX level and find an insignificant coefficient. The premium is not higher in high-volatility regimes; it represents a fixed uncertainty resolution premium common to all scheduled FOMC meetings.

Evaluate the win rate of the pre-FOMC drift strategy if 95 out of 144 in-sample meetings experienced positive returns. Is this significantly greater than 50%?

Solution

Win rate is \(\hat{p} = \frac{95}{144} = 65.97\%\). Under null \(p = 0.50\), \(\text{SE} = \sqrt{\frac{0.5 \times 0.5}{144}} = \frac{0.5}{12} = 0.04167\). \(z = \frac{0.6597 - 0.50}{0.04167} = 3.83\). With \(z > 3.8\), the positive win rate rejects the fair-coin null at \(p < 0.0001\).

Tier 4 · Research

How does the theory of heightened pre-announcement risk aversion (uncertainty resolution) explain the absence of drift around unscheduled meetings?

Solution

When an announcement is scheduled, market makers demand a heightened risk premium for holding inventory into the known news event. As risk-averse investors sell or withhold liquidity, prices drop prior to \(t-1\), and then recover during the 24 hours as uncertainty gradually resolves. For unscheduled meetings, market participants cannot anticipate the timing, so no pre-event positioning occurs.

Explain how the introduction of FOMC post-meeting press conferences altered the intraday volatility profile and trading volume of the E-mini S&P 500 futures contract.

Solution

Prior to 2011, peak volume and volatility occurred between 2:15 PM and 2:30 PM. With post-meeting press conferences, volume bifurcates: an initial burst at 2:00 PM upon statement release, followed by sustained elevated volatility and volume between 2:30 PM and 3:30 PM as the Chair answers journalistic questions, dispersing price discovery over an extended window.

What structural impediments prevent hedge funds from fully arbitraging away the pre-FOMC drift?

Solution

Impediments include funding constraints, limits to arbitrage during crisis periods (when holding equities ahead of rate cuts carries tail risk), tracking error constraints for benchmarked managers, and the relatively low frequency of occurrences (only 8 times per year, requiring years to evaluate Sharpe significance).

8. Retrieval Quiz

  1. What is the exact time window over which the classical pre-FOMC announcement drift is measured?

    Options

    Lucca & Moench define the pre-FOMC window exactly as the 24 hours from 2:00 PM on day \(t-1\) to 2:00 PM on day \(t\).

  2. What percentage of the cumulative U.S. equity risk premium did the pre-FOMC drift account for between 1994 and 2011?

    Options

    Lucca & Moench document that over 80% of cumulative equity excess returns from 1994 to 2011 occurred during the pre-FOMC 24-hour windows.

  3. What happens to equity returns prior to unscheduled FOMC meetings?

    Options

    Unscheduled meetings show an average return of \(-32\) bps (\(t = -0.58\)); the drift requires advance knowledge of the meeting date.

  4. How does the pre-FOMC drift transmit internationally to European bourses (FTSE 100, DAX)?

    Options

    European bourses drift upward ahead of FOMC announcements, reflecting the global footprint of U.S. monetary policy.

  5. Why does empirical evidence contradict the 'information leakage' hypothesis for the pre-FOMC drift?

    Options

    If the drift were driven by leaked policy decisions, stocks would fall before unexpected hikes; empirically, stocks drift up before both hikes and cuts.

  6. What major institutional communication change introduced in 2011 contributed to the attenuation of the pre-FOMC drift?

    Options

    Post-meeting press conferences shifted market attention and price discovery from the 2:00 PM statement to the 2:30 PM press briefing.

  7. In a non-parametric placebo permutation test, what forms the empirical null distribution?

    Options

    Placebo tests sample identical window lengths from non-event trading dates to evaluate whether the effect could arise by chance.

  8. What happened to the pre-FOMC announcement drift in post-2012 out-of-sample data?

    Options

    Out-of-sample evaluations demonstrate that the pre-FOMC drift attenuated substantially post-publication, exhibiting classic anomaly decay.
