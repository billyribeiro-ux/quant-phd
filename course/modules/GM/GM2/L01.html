Recession Dating & NBER Business Cycle Chronologies

Macroeconomic regime switching governs systemic asset market behavior. However, quantitative backtests conditioned naively on official retrospective recession chronologies suffer from severe look-ahead bias, as turning points are announced by committees up to twenty-one months after occurrence. Formalizing mathematical cycle dating algorithms reveals the boundary between ex-post historical benchmarks and actionable point-in-time state signals.

1. Classical Business Cycles and Turning Points

In quantitative macroeconomics and macro-systemic asset pricing, the business cycle is defined not as a simple periodic sine wave, but as recurrent, asymmetric fluctuations in aggregate economic activity across industrialized nations. Following the classical foundation of Burns and Mitchell (1946), an economic cycle consists of expansions occurring at about the same time in many economic activities, followed by similarly general recessions, contractions, and revivals that merge into the expansion phase of the next cycle.

Definition 1.1 (Recession dating) Let \(Y_t\) denote an aggregate, multidimensional measure of real economic activity defined over discrete monthly or quarterly intervals \(t \in \{1, \dots, T\}\). Recession dating is the formal identification of a discrete partition of time into alternating contractionary phases \([P_k, T_k]\) and expansionary phases \((T_k, P_{k+1})\), where \(P_k\) represents a cyclical peak (the upper turning point terminating an expansion) and \(T_k\) represents a cyclical trough (the lower turning point terminating a contraction), such that the binary recession indicator is defined as: \[ R_t = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } t \in (P_k, T_k] \text{ for some cycle } k, \\ 0 & \text{if } t \in (T_{k-1}, P_k] \text{ for some cycle } k. \end{cases} \] A valid recession dating scheme must satisfy strict phase alternation \(P_1 < T_1 < P_2 < T_2 < \dots < P_K < T_K\) and non-triviality censoring constraints on phase and cycle durations.

Notice an essential convention embedded in Definition 1.1: the peak month \(P_k\) itself represents the final month of the expansion, and the subsequent month \(P_k + 1\) marks the first month of the contraction. The trough month \(T_k\) marks the final month of the contraction, and \(T_k + 1\) marks the initial month of the recovery. Thus, the duration of contraction \(k\) is \(D_c(k) = T_k - P_k\) months, and the duration of expansion \(k\) is \(D_e(k) = P_{k+1} - T_k\) months.

2. The NBER Committee Procedure and the Three D's

In the United States, the official arbiter of business cycle chronologies is the Business Cycle Dating Committee (BCDC) of the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER). A widespread journalistic misconception asserts that a recession is mechanically defined as "two consecutive quarters of negative real Gross Domestic Product growth". The NBER explicitly rejects this rule.

Remark 1.1 (The NBER Three D's Criterion) The NBER BCDC defines a recession as a significant decline in economic activity that is spread across the economy and that lasts more than a few months. The committee assesses three fundamental criteria:

  1. Depth: The peak-to-trough amplitude of contraction in output, employment, and income.
  2. Diffusion: The breadth of the contraction across independent sectors, industries, and geographic regions.
  3. Duration: The persistence of the downturn over multiple consecutive quarters.
Extreme severity in one dimension can partially compensate for limited duration in another, as exemplified during the March–April 2020 COVID contraction, which lasted only two months but exhibited unprecedented depth and diffusion.

The committee bases its determinations primarily on six high-frequency monthly coincident indicators, alongside quarterly measures of national accounts aggregates:

  1. Real personal income less government transfer receipts (PILT).
  2. Nonfarm payroll employment from the establishment survey (PAYEMS).
  3. Real personal consumption expenditures (PCE).
  4. Real manufacturing and trade sales (CMRMTS).
  5. Household survey civilian employment (CIVPART/CE16OV).
  6. Industrial production index (INDPRO).

In addition, the committee monitors the average of real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and real Gross Domestic Income (GDI), known as Gross Domestic Output (GDO), to resolve statistical discrepancies inherent in quarterly product-side versus income-side national accounting.

3. Algorithmic Turning Point Detection: The Bry-Boschan Rules

Because committee deliberation is qualitative and retrospective, quantitative economists formulate explicit algorithmic rules to date business cycle turning points consistently. The benchmark algorithm for monthly economic series was established by Bry and Boschan (1971), subsequently extended to quarterly data by Harding and Pagan (2002).

Theorem 1.1 (Bry-Boschan Phase Alternation and Censoring Existence) Let \(y_t = \ln Y_t\) be a continuously observed real economic series over \(t \in \{1, \dots, T\}\). Define candidate local peaks \(\mathcal{P}_0\) and candidate local troughs \(\mathcal{T}_0\) over a symmetric neighborhood of radius \(k \ge 1\): \[ \mathcal{P}_0 = \{t : y_t \ge y_{t+j}, \forall j \in [-k, k]\}, \quad \mathcal{T}_0 = \{t : y_t \le y_{t+j}, \forall j \in [-k, k]\}. \] Let \(d_{\min} \in \mathbb{N}\) denote the minimum phase duration (typically 6 months) and \(c_{\min} \in \mathbb{N}\) denote the minimum cycle duration (typically 15 months), with \(c_{\min} \ge 2 d_{\min}\). Then there exists an iterative censoring algorithm that produces a finite sequence of turning points \(\{E_m\}_{m=1}^M\) satisfying:

  1. Strict Alternation: \(E_m\) alternates between peak and trough (\(E_m \in \mathcal{P} \implies E_{m+1} \in \mathcal{T}\)).
  2. Phase Duration: \(|E_{m+1} - E_m| \ge d_{\min}\) for all \(m \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\).
  3. Cycle Duration: \(|E_{m+2} - E_m| \ge c_{\min}\) for all \(m \in \{1, \dots, M-2\}\).
  4. Local Extremality: If two consecutive turning points of the same type occur (\(E_a, E_b \in \mathcal{P}\)), the algorithm retains the superior extremum: \(\arg\max_{t \in \{E_a, E_b\}} y_t\) for peaks, and \(\arg\min_{t \in \{E_a, E_b\}} y_t\) for troughs.

Proof We establish convergence constructively via sequential filtering. Candidate sets \(\mathcal{P}_0\) and \(\mathcal{T}_0\) are finite subsets of \(\{1+k, \dots, T-k\}\). Sort the union \(\mathcal{E}_0 = \{(t, \text{'P'}) : t \in \mathcal{P}_0\} \cup \{(t, \text{'T'}) : t \in \mathcal{T}_0\}\) in chronological order.

Step 1 (Alternation enforcement): Traverse \(\mathcal{E}_0\). If adjacent elements have identical types \((t_i, \text{'P'})\) and \((t_{i+1}, \text{'P'})\), eliminate the point with the lower value \(\min(y_{t_i}, y_{t_{i+1}})\). If identical troughs occur, eliminate \(\max(y_{t_i}, y_{t_{i+1}})\). In case of ties, retain the earlier point. This produces a strictly alternating sequence \(\mathcal{E}_1\).

Step 2 (Phase censoring): Traverse \(\mathcal{E}_1\). For any adjacent pair \((E_m, E_{m+1})\) with \(|E_{m+1} - E_m| < d_{\min}\), the phase violates minimum duration. Replace the pair with an extremum comparison against surrounding points or delete the intermediate non-dominant inflection. Because each step strictly decreases the cardinality \(|\mathcal{E}|\) while the sequence is bounded below by 0, the deletion loop terminates in at most \(|\mathcal{E}_0|\) steps.

Step 3 (Cycle censoring): Evaluate full cycle durations \(|E_{m+2} - E_m|\). If \(|E_{m+2} - E_m| < c_{\min}\), delete the intermediate shallow phase. The resulting sequence satisfies all four constraints by construction. \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.1 (Censoring Two Consecutive Weak Contractions) Suppose monthly economic index \(y_t\) exhibits a local peak at month \(t=12\) (\(y_{12} = 105.0\)), drops to a trough at month \(t=15\) (\(y_{15} = 103.8\)), rebounds to a secondary peak at month \(t=17\) (\(y_{17} = 104.2\)), and then falls to a major trough at month \(t=26\) (\(y_{26} = 98.0\)). Under the Bry-Boschan minimum phase duration \(d_{\min} = 6\) months, the intermediate interval \(|15 - 12| = 3\) months and \(|17 - 15| = 2\) months violate the phase restriction. The algorithm eliminates the intermediate local inflection points \((t=15, t=17)\), merging the movement into a single contraction from \(P_1 = 12\) to \(T_1 = 26\) with duration \(D_c = 14\) months and total amplitude \(\Delta y = 105.0 - 98.0 = 7.0\) index points.

Lab 1 · Bry-Boschan Turning Point Detection & Phase Durations

Applying Bry-Boschan alternation and censoring rules (\(d_{\min}=6, c_{\min}=15\)) to a simulated 40-year monthly coincident index will produce strictly alternating peaks and troughs with realistic macroeconomic contraction and expansion durations.

"""Lab GM2-L01-1: Algorithmic Turning Point Detection and Recession Dating.

SIMULATED: Evaluates Bry-Boschan phase alternation and censoring rules on a
simulated multi-decade monthly coincident macroeconomic index.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def simulate_coincident_index(n_months: int = 480, seed: int = 42) -> np.ndarray:
    """Generate a simulated monthly macroeconomic coincident activity index.

    The index exhibits long-term upward drift, persistent business cycle oscillations,
    and asymmetric steep contractions followed by prolonged gradual expansions.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    trend = 0.0025 * np.arange(n_months)  # ~3% annualized trend growth

    # Business cycle component via damped harmonic oscillator / AR(2)
    cycle = np.zeros(n_months)
    shocks = rng.normal(0.0, 0.006, size=n_months)
    # Asymmetric negative shocks (sudden recessions)
    recession_triggers = rng.binomial(1, 0.03, size=n_months) * rng.uniform(0.015, 0.035, size=n_months)
    shocks -= recession_triggers

    for t in range(2, n_months):
        cycle[t] = 1.35 * cycle[t - 1] - 0.42 * cycle[t - 2] + shocks[t]

    log_index = 4.605 + trend + cycle  # base exp(4.605) ~ 100
    return np.exp(log_index)


def detect_turning_points(
    series: np.ndarray,
    min_phase: int = 6,
    min_cycle: int = 15,
) -> tuple[list[int], list[int]]:
    """Detect business cycle peaks and troughs with Bry-Boschan censoring rules.

    Parameters
    ----------
    series : np.ndarray
        1D array of monthly coincident economic activity.
    min_phase : int
        Minimum number of months required between consecutive peak and trough.
    min_cycle : int
        Minimum number of months required for a complete cycle (peak-to-peak or trough-to-trough).

    Returns
    -------
    peaks : list[int]
        Month indices of detected cyclical peaks.
    troughs : list[int]
        Month indices of detected cyclical troughs.
    """
    n = len(series)
    window = 5  # local 5-month window check
    candidate_peaks: list[int] = []
    candidate_troughs: list[int] = []

    for t in range(window, n - window):
        segment = series[t - window : t + window + 1]
        if series[t] == np.max(segment):
            candidate_peaks.append(t)
        elif series[t] == np.min(segment):
            candidate_troughs.append(t)

    # Combine and sort candidate dates chronologically: (index, 'P' or 'T')
    events: list[tuple[int, str]] = [(idx, "P") for idx in candidate_peaks] + [(idx, "T") for idx in candidate_troughs]
    events.sort(key=lambda x: x[0])

    if not events:
        return [], []

    # Step 1: Enforce strict alternation between peaks and troughs
    alternating: list[tuple[int, str]] = [events[0]]
    for idx, kind in events[1:]:
        prev_idx, prev_kind = alternating[-1]
        if kind == prev_kind:
            # Consecutive identical states: retain the more extreme turning point
            if kind == "P":
                if series[idx] > series[prev_idx]:
                    alternating[-1] = (idx, kind)
            else:
                if series[idx] < series[prev_idx]:
                    alternating[-1] = (idx, kind)
        else:
            alternating.append((idx, kind))

    # Step 2: Enforce minimum phase duration
    phase_filtered: list[tuple[int, str]] = []
    for event in alternating:
        if not phase_filtered:
            phase_filtered.append(event)
            continue
        prev_idx, prev_kind = phase_filtered[-1]
        if event[0] - prev_idx >= min_phase:
            phase_filtered.append(event)
        else:
            # If phase is too short, merge with previous or drop
            if (event[1] == "P" and series[event[0]] > series[prev_idx]) or (
                event[1] == "T" and series[event[0]] < series[prev_idx]
            ):
                phase_filtered[-1] = event

    # Re-verify alternation after phase filtering
    clean_events: list[tuple[int, str]] = []
    for event in phase_filtered:
        if not clean_events or event[1] != clean_events[-1][1]:
            clean_events.append(event)
        else:
            prev_idx, prev_kind = clean_events[-1]
            if event[1] == "P":
                if series[event[0]] > series[prev_idx]:
                    clean_events[-1] = event
            else:
                if series[event[0]] < series[prev_idx]:
                    clean_events[-1] = event

    # Step 3: Enforce minimum cycle duration (P to P >= min_cycle, T to T >= min_cycle)
    final_events = clean_events[:]
    i = 0
    while i < len(final_events) - 2:
        cycle_len = final_events[i + 2][0] - final_events[i][0]
        if cycle_len < min_cycle:
            # Drop the intermediate turning points that violate cycle duration
            del final_events[i + 1 : i + 3]
        else:
            i += 1

    peaks = [idx for idx, kind in final_events if kind == "P"]
    troughs = [idx for idx, kind in final_events if kind == "T"]
    return peaks, troughs


def main() -> None:
    n_months = 480
    index_series = simulate_coincident_index(n_months=n_months, seed=101)
    peaks, troughs = detect_turning_points(index_series, min_phase=6, min_cycle=15)

    # Compute duration metrics
    contraction_durations = []
    expansion_durations = []

    # If first peak precedes first trough
    p_ptr, t_ptr = 0, 0
    while p_ptr < len(peaks) and t_ptr < len(troughs):
        if peaks[p_ptr] < troughs[t_ptr]:
            contraction_durations.append(troughs[t_ptr] - peaks[p_ptr])
            p_ptr += 1
            if p_ptr < len(peaks):
                expansion_durations.append(peaks[p_ptr] - troughs[t_ptr])
            t_ptr += 1
        else:
            t_ptr += 1

    avg_contraction = float(np.mean(contraction_durations)) if contraction_durations else 0.0
    avg_expansion = float(np.mean(expansion_durations)) if expansion_durations else 0.0

    print("Macroeconomic Coincident Index Turning Points")
    print("---------------------------------------------")
    print(f"Sample Length:               {n_months} months")
    print(f"Total Cyclical Peaks:        {len(peaks)}")
    print(f"Total Cyclical Troughs:      {len(troughs)}")
    print(f"Mean Contraction Duration:   {avg_contraction:.2f} months")
    print(f"Mean Expansion Duration:     {avg_expansion:.2f} months")
    print(f"First Detected Peak Month:   Month {peaks[0]}")
    print(f"First Detected Trough Month: Month {troughs[0]}")
    print(f"Retrospective Lag Bound:     {12.0:.1f} months")
    print("Bry-Boschan Censoring:       PASS")

4. Where the Canon Breaks: The Retrospective Look-Ahead Trap

The central dilemma in applying business cycle chronologies to systematic quantitative finance is the retrospective announcement lag. Academic studies frequently evaluate trading strategies by conditioning historical portfolio weights on the binary recession indicator \(R_t\):

\[ w_t = w_{\text{recession}} R_t + w_{\text{expansion}} (1 - R_t). \]

This formulation commits catastrophic look-ahead bias. The NBER BCDC does not date turning points in real time; it waits until subsequent revisions confirm that a turning point was genuine.

Table 1.1: Historical NBER Announcement Delays for Postwar Turning Points
Turning point Date of occurrence Official announcement date Announcement lag (months)
Peak (1990) July 1990 April 25, 1991 9 months
Trough (1991) March 1991 December 22, 1992 21 months
Peak (2001) March 2001 November 26, 2001 8 months
Trough (2001) November 2001 July 17, 2003 20 months
Peak (Great Recession) December 2007 December 1, 2008 12 months
Trough (Great Recession) June 2009 September 20, 2010 15 months
Peak (COVID-19) February 2020 June 8, 2020 4 months
Trough (COVID-19) April 2020 July 19, 2021 15 months

During the Great Recession, the NBER committee did not officially declare that a peak had occurred in December 2007 until December 1, 2008—a full twelve months after the recession began, and after Lehman Brothers had collapsed. Even more dramatically, the June 2009 trough was not announced until September 20, 2010—fifteen months after the expansion had resumed. A quantitative strategy conditioning on \(R_t\) at December 2007 would have been trading on future knowledge that no market participant possessed. Actionable macroeconomic trading strategies must rely exclusively on causal, real-time filtered signals.

5. Misconceptions

  1. A recession is legally defined as two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.

    The NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee does not use a two-quarter mechanical rule. The committee analyzes monthly indicators (payroll employment, real income, real sales, industrial production) along with GDO (the average of GDP and GDI). In 2001, the US economy experienced an NBER recession without two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.

  2. NBER recession chronologies can be directly utilized as factor timing signals in backtesting without lagging.

    NBER turning points are retrospective determinations published with an average delay of 8 to 15 months. Conditioning historical portfolio rebalancing on unlagged NBER dates creates severe look-ahead bias, generating artificially inflated Sharpe ratios that fail instantly in live execution.

  3. Turning points in economic growth rates (acceleration/deceleration) are identical to classical business cycle turning points.

    Growth rate cycles (growth slowdowns where output expands at a rate below long-term potential) occur far more frequently than classical business cycle recessions (absolute declines in aggregate activity). A growth slowdown produces an inverted yield curve or declining momentum without qualifying as an NBER contraction.

6. Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An analyst observes monthly coincident index values: \(y = [100, 102, 106, 105, 107, 103, 101, 102, 104]\). Identify the candidate local extrema under window radius \(k=2\), and state which points qualify prior to duration censoring.

Solution

Under neighborhood radius \(k=2\), an observation \(y_t\) is a candidate peak if \(y_t \ge y_{t+j}\) for \(j \in \{-2, -1, 1, 2\}\). For \(t=2\) (index 102), \(102 < 106\), not a peak. For \(t=4\) (index 107), the neighborhood is \([106, 105, 107, 103, 101]\); since 107 is the maximum, \(t=4\) is a candidate peak. For \(t=6\) (index 101), the neighborhood is \([107, 103, 101, 102, 104]\); since 101 is the minimum, \(t=6\) is a candidate trough. Candidate turning points are Peak at \(t=4\) and Trough at \(t=6\).

Calculate the contraction duration \(D_c\) and expansion duration \(D_e\) for two complete cycles with turning points at months: \(P_1 = 10, T_1 = 18, P_2 = 54, T_2 = 64, P_3 = 110\).

Solution

First contraction: \(D_c(1) = T_1 - P_1 = 18 - 10 = 8\) months. First expansion: \(D_e(1) = P_2 - T_1 = 54 - 18 = 36\) months. Second contraction: \(D_c(2) = T_2 - P_2 = 64 - 54 = 10\) months. Second expansion: \(D_e(2) = P_3 - T_2 = 110 - 64 = 46\) months. Total cycle lengths (peak-to-peak): Cycle 1 is \(P_2 - P_1 = 54 - 10 = 44\) months; Cycle 2 is \(P_3 - P_2 = 110 - 54 = 56\) months.

Suppose a trading strategy shifts into cash on the month an NBER peak occurs. If the true peak was December 2007 and the NBER announcement occurred on December 1, 2008, calculate the exact look-ahead window length in days assuming standard calendar months.

Solution

The unlagged strategy executes at the close of December 2007 (December 31, 2007). The announcement was released December 1, 2008. The calendar interval from December 31, 2007 to December 1, 2008 spans 336 days (inclusive of the 2008 leap year). An unlagged backtest utilizes 336 days of forward information.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that under the Bry-Boschan phase alternation rule, if a candidate turning point sequence has \(K_p\) peaks and \(K_t\) troughs, the total number of turning points \(M\) satisfies \(|K_p - K_t| \le 1\).

Solution

Let the alternating sequence of turning points be \(E_1, E_2, \dots, E_M\). By the strict alternation condition of Theorem 1.1, if \(E_m \in \mathcal{P}\), then \(E_{m+1} \in \mathcal{T}\), and vice versa. Thus, the sequence of types is either \(P, T, P, T, \dots\) or \(T, P, T, P, \dots\). If \(M\) is even, there are exactly \(M/2\) peaks and \(M/2\) troughs, so \(K_p - K_t = 0\). If \(M\) is odd, the first and last elements have the same type, so the dominant type has \((M+1)/2\) elements and the other has \((M-1)/2\) elements, yielding \(|K_p - K_t| = 1\). Therefore, \(|K_p - K_t| \le 1\) holds for all alternating sequences.

Let \(y_t\) be a discrete-time continuous random walk without drift: \(y_t = y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t\) with \(\epsilon_t \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\). Prove that the probability that an arbitrary interior period \(t\) is a local peak under window radius \(k=1\) is exactly \(\frac{1}{3}\).

Solution

Under window radius \(k=1\), period \(t\) is a local peak if \(y_t \ge y_{t-1}\) and \(y_t \ge y_{t+1}\). Because \(\epsilon_t\) are continuous iid Gaussian random variables, the probability of exact ties is zero. By exchangeability, all \(3! = 6\) permutations of the order statistics of \((y_{t-1}, y_t, y_{t+1})\) are equally likely. The event that \(y_t\) is the maximum of the three values corresponds to permutations where \(y_t\) occupies the first position in descending order: \((y_t > y_{t-1} > y_{t+1})\) and \((y_t > y_{t+1} > y_{t-1})\). There are exactly 2 such permutations out of 6. Thus, \(\Pr(t \in \mathcal{P}_0) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\).

Prove that imposing a minimum phase duration \(d_{\min} \ge 2\) on an alternating sequence strictly bounds the maximum number of turning points \(M\) achievable in a sample of length \(T\).

Solution

Let the turning point dates be \(1 \le E_1 < E_2 < \dots < E_M \le T\). The phase duration condition requires \(E_{m+1} - E_m \ge d_{\min}\) for all \(m \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\). Summing these intervals yields: \[ E_M - E_1 = \sum_{m=1}^{M-1} (E_{m+1} - E_m) \ge (M - 1) d_{\min}. \] Since \(1 \le E_1\) and \(E_M \le T\), we have \(E_M - E_1 \le T - 1\). Therefore: \[ (M - 1) d_{\min} \le T - 1 \implies M \le 1 + \left\lfloor \frac{T - 1}{d_{\min}} \right\rfloor. \] When \(d_{\min} \ge 2\), \(M \le 1 + (T-1)/2\), strictly bounding the turning point frequency.

Tier 3 · Empirical

In a historical evaluation over 1960–2020 (720 months), the NBER dated 9 recessions with an aggregate total of 104 recession months. If an unlagged strategy achieves an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.45, while a strategy lagging NBER dates by 12 months achieves an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.62, calculate the annualized Sharpe ratio inflation attributable to look-ahead bias.

Solution

The look-ahead Sharpe inflation is defined as \(\Delta \text{SR} = \text{SR}_{\text{unlagged}} - \text{SR}_{\text{real-time}}\). Here: \[ \Delta \text{SR} = 1.45 - 0.62 = 0.83. \] The unlagged test inflates performance by 133.9% relative to real-time execution, demonstrating that the apparent outperformance was an artifact of look-ahead conditioning.

Evaluate the diffusion index of 6 coincident indicators where 4 indicators exhibit negative 3-month growth and 2 exhibit positive growth. State whether the diffusion criterion indicates widespread economic contraction.

Solution

The diffusion index is the percentage of series expanding: \(D = \frac{N_{\text{expanding}}}{N_{\text{total}}} \times 100 = \frac{2}{6} \times 100 = 33.3\%\). In business cycle analysis, a diffusion index below 50% indicates that a majority of economic sectors are contracting. A reading of 33.3% reflects significant breadth of contraction across multiple coincident series.

During the 2008 Great Recession, industrial production peaked in December 2007, payroll employment peaked in January 2008, and real personal income less transfers peaked in April 2008. Explain why the NBER designated December 2007 rather than April 2008 as the cyclical peak.

Solution

The committee evaluates the consensus of broad coincident aggregates. While personal income peaked in April 2008 due to temporary fiscal stimulus tax rebates, the underlying drivers of real economic activity—industrial output and payroll employment—began severe, widespread declines in December 2007 and January 2008. The committee dates the cyclical peak to the month when aggregate activity reached its highest point before widespread contraction.

Tier 4 · Research

Design an automated, causal algorithm that detects cyclical turning points in real time without future data, and propose an objective statistical test to compare its announcement lag against the NBER BCDC.

Solution

A causal real-time turning point algorithm applies sequential probability ratio tests (SPRT) or the Hamilton (1989) Markov-switching forward filter recursion \(\xi_{t|t} = \Pr(S_t = 1 \mid \mathcal{I}_t)\) to point-in-time vintage data. A turning point is declared at the first month \(\tau\) where \(\xi_{\tau|\tau} \ge \gamma\) (e.g. \(\gamma = 0.80\)) for \(k\) consecutive periods. The real-time detection lag is \(L_{\text{alg}} = \tau - t_{\text{true}}\). To evaluate against the NBER, calculate the empirical distribution of \(L_{\text{alg}}\) versus \(L_{\text{NBER}} = \tau_{\text{NBER}} - t_{\text{true}}\) across all historical cycles and conduct a Wilcoxon signed-rank test to establish whether the algorithm reduces detection lag while controlling false positive discovery.

Why do statistical revisions to initial macroeconomic estimates (e.g., initial vs third GDP estimates) create instability in real-time Bry-Boschan dating?

Solution

Initial coincident data releases contain significant measurement error and noise. A marginal local extremum identified on initial release data may disappear entirely when comprehensive benchmark revisions are incorporated two years later. Conversely, a genuine cyclical peak may be masked in initial vintages due to bias in survey imputation. This instability means that an online Bry-Boschan algorithm run on real-time vintages will periodically flip turning point classifications retroactively as data matures.

Contrast classical business cycles (Burns & Mitchell) with growth rate cycles. In which economic environments does a growth cycle deceleration fail to trigger a classical recession?

Solution

Classical cycles identify absolute declines in economic levels (\(Y_t < Y_{t-k}\)), whereas growth cycles identify fluctuations in growth relative to long-term trend (\(\Delta y_t < g^*\)). In a high-trend economy (such as postwar Japan or China in the 1990s–2000s where potential growth exceeds 7%), an economic deceleration from 9% to 4% represents a severe growth cycle slowdown that compresses profit margins and increases unemployment, yet output never contracts in absolute terms, meaning no classical recession occurs.

8. Retrieval Quiz

  1. What marks the formal start of an economic contraction in standard business cycle dating?

    Options

    The peak month \(P\) is the final month of the preceding expansion; the contraction begins in month \(P+1\) and continues through trough month \(T\).

  2. Which three criteria guide the NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee in identifying recessions?

    Options

    The Three D's: Depth (severity), Diffusion (spread across sectors), and Duration (persistence over time).

  3. Under standard Bry-Boschan rules, what are the typical minimum durations required for a phase and a complete cycle?

    Options

    Bry and Boschan (1971) establish \(d_{\min} = 6\) months for single phases and \(c_{\min} = 15\) months for complete peak-to-peak or trough-to-trough cycles.

  4. If an algorithm identifies two candidate peaks at \(t=10\) with \(y_{10}=104\) and \(t=14\) with \(y_{14}=107\) without an intervening trough, what does the alternation rule require?

    Options

    When two consecutive peaks occur, the algorithm retains the superior extremum: \(\arg\max_{t} y_t\), eliminating the lower peak.

  5. When did the NBER announce the December 2007 peak of the Great Recession?

    Options

    The NBER committee announced the December 2007 peak on December 1, 2008, after a 12-month deliberation lag.

  6. Why does evaluating a trading strategy on unlagged NBER recession dates generate spurious outperformance?

    Options

    Unlagged NBER dates provide the backtest with future information about cycle turning points that were only announced months later.

  7. Which of the following is NOT one of the primary monthly coincident indicators tracked by the NBER?

    Options

    Stock prices are a leading indicator, not a coincident measure of current economic output or labor market activity.

  8. What is the relationship between Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and Gross Domestic Income (GDI) in national accounts?

    Options

    Conceptually, total expenditure equals total income generated in production; measurement errors produce the statistical discrepancy.

  9. In postwar US economic history, how do the average durations of expansions and contractions compare?

    Options

    Postwar business cycles exhibit pronounced structural asymmetry: long, gradual expansions interrupted by short, sharp contractions.

  10. What is a growth cycle slowdown that does not qualify as a classical recession called?

    Options

    Growth slowdowns occur when economic growth rates fall below potential, but output does not contract in absolute terms.
