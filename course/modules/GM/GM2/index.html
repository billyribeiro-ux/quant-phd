Regimes & the Business Cycle

Macroeconomic regimes fundamentally govern cross-asset risk premia, return volatilities, and diversification dynamics. GM2 develops the rigorous econometric theory and quantitative architecture required to detect business cycle turning points, forecast recessions using yield-curve term spreads, model the historic sign flip in stock-bond correlations, quantify asset class resilience across inflation regimes, and classify filtered states in real time without look-ahead bias. You will master NBER chronologies, Estrella-Mishkin probit models, Campbell-Sunderam-Viceira bond risk decompositions, Neville et al. inflationary asset performance, and Hamilton Markov-switching filters. The module culminates in building an end-to-end real-time regime classification and dynamic portfolio allocation engine.

  • Prerequisites: GM1 Macro Data & Nowcasting.
  • Project: Real-Time Regime Classification & Regime-Conditional Asset Allocation, graded by autograder and rubric (24 hours).
  • Hours: 60 study hours (36 h across 5 lessons, 24 h capstone project).

Learning objectives

  1. Formalize business cycle turning point chronologies and evaluate the look-ahead risks of retrospective recession dating.
  2. Estimate yield-curve term spread probit models via maximum likelihood to quantify recession probabilities and forecast horizons.
  3. Decompose stock-bond return covariances under Campbell-Shiller identities to explain the structural sign flip between supply and demand regimes.
  4. Measure real asset class returns, drawdown risks, and hedging efficacy across historical inflation regimes.
  5. Implement the Hamilton forward filter recursion to evaluate point-in-time filtered regime probabilities without future information leakage.
  6. Construct a dynamic regime-conditional asset allocator that balances expected returns, covariance regularizations, and turnover frictions.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on prerequisite GM1 and foundational time series concepts, and two skill checks testing what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must achieve the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. How does an online quadrant classifier define the Goldilocks macroeconomic regime in terms of standardized growth \(z_g\) and inflation \(z_\pi\) z-scores?

    Options

    Goldilocks represents benign economic expansion characterized by strong economic activity without overheating inflationary price pressure. Rung 1–2 skill check.

  2. When projecting an unconstrained weight vector \(v \in \R^N\) onto the long-only probability simplex \(\{w \ge 0, \sum w_i = 1\}\), what mathematical algorithm guarantees Euclidean distance minimization?

    Options

    Euclidean simplex projection requires finding an optimal Lagrange multiplier \(\theta\) that shifts all components uniformly prior to positive clipping, satisfying KKT optimality conditions. Naive zero-clipping distorts relative optimality. Rung 1–2 skill check.

  3. Under the Croushore-Stark framework from GM1, why does conducting a macroeconomic backtest using current revised data introduce look-ahead bias?

    Options

    Subsequent macroeconomic revisions embody future survey information unavailable to market participants in real time.

  4. In dynamic factor nowcasting (GM1), how does the Kalman filter address ragged edges caused by asynchronous publication delays?

    Options

    Selection matrix \(M_t\) reduces observation vectors and measurement covariances to observed dimensions, preserving exact Bayesian updating.

  5. Under the Expectations Hypothesis of the term structure, what does an inverted yield curve (\(y^{(10)} < y^{(3m)}\)) imply about market expectations of future short rates?

    Options

    An inversion reflects expectations of aggressive monetary easing in response to expected economic contraction or falling inflation.

  6. In a binary probit recession forecasting model \(\Pr(R_{t+k}=1) = \Phi(\alpha + \beta S_t)\), what is the empirical sign of the term spread coefficient \(\beta\)?

    Options

    Estrella & Hardouvelis (1991) and Estrella & Mishkin (1998) establish that \(\beta\) is strongly negative: yield curve inversion reliably precedes recessions.

  7. According to Campbell, Sunderam & Viceira (2017), why did the stock-bond return correlation flip from positive to negative around 1998?

    Options

    When demand shocks dominate, recessions cause both earnings declines and falling interest rates, driving bond prices up while equities fall (\(\mathrm{Cov}(r_S, r_B) < 0\)).

  8. What empirical result does Neville et al. (2021) document regarding the real return of nominal bonds during historical inflationary surges?

    Options

    Rising yields inflict severe mark-to-market capital losses while fixed nominal coupons lose real purchasing power.

  9. In the Hamilton (1989) Markov-switching model, what is the key difference between filtered probabilities \(\xi_{t|t}\) and smoothed probabilities \(\xi_{t|T}\)?

    Options

    Smoothed probabilities incorporate future data \(\tau > t\), introducing severe look-ahead bias if used in real-time trading strategies.

  10. When optimizing a portfolio over ill-conditioned or small-sample covariance matrices, why is Ledoit-Wolf or ridge shrinkage applied?

    Options

    Sample covariance matrices estimated from sparse regime observations suffer from extreme estimation error; shrinkage regularizes eigenvalues and stabilizes weights.

Lesson map

Lesson Hours Concepts introduced & primary topics
L01 · Recession Dating & NBER Chronologies 7 Introduces recession dating. Formalizes Bry-Boschan phase alternation, minimum cycle censoring rules, and retrospective announcement lags.
L02 · Yield Curve Term Spread & Recession Probits 7 Introduces yield-curve recession signals and recession-probability models. Estrella-Hardouvelis (1991), Estrella-Mishkin (1998) probit estimation, pseudo-\(R^2\), and ROC/AUC metrics.
L03 · Stock–Bond Correlation Regimes & The Sign Flip 7 Introduces stock–bond correlation regimes. Campbell, Sunderam & Viceira (2017), supply vs demand shocks, deflation hedges vs inflation bets, and 60/40 portfolio diversification.
L04 · Inflation Regimes & Asset Class Resilience 8 Introduces inflation regimes and asset returns. Neville et al. (2021), historical inflation surges, commodities, nominal Treasuries, and trend-following momentum resilience.
L05 · Real-Time Regime Classification & Filtered States 7 Introduces real-time regime classification. Hamilton (1989) Markov-switching forward filter recursion, filtered vs smoothed states, and small-sample inference.
