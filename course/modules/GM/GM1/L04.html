Whenever macroeconomic figures print—whether non-farm payrolls, inflation, or retail sales—consensus GDP nowcasts jump. But why? Did the nowcast rise because the new print beat expectations, or because previous months were revised upward? Understanding the anatomy of a nowcast update is essential for trading desks reacting to data releases. The Giannone-Reichlin-Small (2008) news decomposition formalizes this attribution, proving that nowcast updates equal the product of model forecast errors ('the news') and projection weights. Decomposing updates into release news versus revision news isolates true economic momentum from backward-looking statistical adjustments.

1. The Anatomy of a Nowcast Update

In real-time trading environments, macroeconomic nowcasts are revised sequentially with each incoming data release. Let \(\Omega_v\) denote the information set available at vintage date \(v\), and let \(\Omega_{v+1}\) denote the updated information set following a publication event. The revision in the nowcast of target variable \(y\) (e.g. current-quarter GDP growth) is defined by the change in conditional expectation:

\[\Delta \hat{y}_{v+1} = \E[y \mid \Omega_{v+1}] - \E[y \mid \Omega_v]\]

A central challenge in empirical macro tracking is that \(\Omega_{v+1}\) often contains two fundamentally different types of information simultaneously:

  1. New Real-Time Releases: Observations for a newly observed period \(t\) (e.g. April non-farm payrolls published in May).
  2. Historical Revisions: Corrections to previously published figures for prior periods \(t-1, t-2\) (e.g. revisions to February and March payrolls published concurrently with the April report).
Definition 4.1 · Vintage Information Expansion

Let \(X_{i,t}^{(v)}\) denote the value of variable \(i\) for observation period \(t\) available in vintage \(\Omega_v\). When transitioning to vintage \(\Omega_{v+1}\), the newly available information is the set of updated values \(\{X_{j,t_j}^{(v+1)}\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}\). Each newly observed entry contains an innovation relative to the prior vintage expectation:

\[I_{j,t_j}^{(v+1)} = X_{j,t_j}^{(v+1)} - \E[X_{j,t_j} \mid \Omega_v]\]

The innovation \(I_{j,t_j}^{(v+1)}\) represents the pure econometric news contained in the release: the component of the data that market participants and the dynamic factor model could not predict from prior information.

2. The News Attribution Identity

Giannone, Reichlin & Small (2008) prove that in a linear state-space system, the revision to the nowcast can be written as an exact linear combination of the news innovations across all incoming variables:

Theorem 4.1 · Giannone-Reichlin-Small News Representation

Let \(\hat{y}_v = \E[y \mid \Omega_v]\) and \(\hat{y}_{v+1} = \E[y \mid \Omega_{v+1}]\) be optimal linear projections in a state-space dynamic factor model. Assume the incoming information vector \(X_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}\) is jointly Gaussian with the latent state and target \(y\). The nowcast update decomposes as:

\[\hat{y}_{v+1} - \hat{y}_v = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \omega_j \cdot \underbrace{\left(X_j^{(v+1)} - \E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\right)}_{\text{News: } I_j^{(v+1)}}\]

where the news weight vector \(\omega = [\omega_1, \dots, \omega_{|\mathcal{J}|}]'\) is given by:

\[\omega = \Cov(y, I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v) \cdot \left[\Var(I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v)\right]^{-1}\]

Proof. By the projection theorem in Hilbert spaces, any linear conditional expectation under Gaussianity satisfies \(\E[y \mid \Omega_{v+1}] = \E[y \mid \Omega_v \vee I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}]\). Because the innovation \(I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} = X_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} - \E[X_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\) is orthogonal to \(\Omega_v\) by definition of the conditional expectation (\(\E[I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} \cdot z] = 0\) for all \(z \in \Omega_v\)), the projection decomposes additively:

\[\E[y \mid \Omega_{v+1}] = \E[y \mid \Omega_v] + \E[y \mid I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}]\]

Because \(\E[I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}] = 0\), the linear projection on the orthogonal innovation subspace is:

\[\E[y \mid I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}] = \Cov(y, I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}) \left[\Var(I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)})\right]^{-1} I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} = \omega' I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}\]

Subtracting \(\E[y \mid \Omega_v]\) from both sides yields the exact attribution identity: \(\hat{y}_{v+1} - \hat{y}_v = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \omega_j I_j^{(v+1)}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Theorem 4.1 establishes that a data release moves the nowcast if and only if it surprises the model (\(I_j^{(v+1)} \neq 0\)) and loads on the common factor governing target \(y\) (\(\omega_j \neq 0\)). A massive positive print that was already fully predicted produces zero change in the nowcast.

3. Decomposing Release News versus Revision News

The index set \(\mathcal{J}\) of incoming data points can be partitioned into two mutually exclusive subsets:

\[\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}_{\text{new}} \cup \mathcal{J}_{\text{rev}}\]
  • \(\mathcal{J}_{\text{new}}\) contains newly published observation periods (extending the ragged edge).
  • \(\mathcal{J}_{\text{rev}}\) contains historical periods already present in \(\Omega_v\) that were updated by the reporting agency (macroeconomic data revisions).

The total nowcast revision decomposes additively into release news and revision news:

\[\Delta \hat{y}_{v+1} = \underbrace{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{new}}} \omega_j I_j^{(v+1)}}_{\text{Release News Contribution}} + \underbrace{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rev}}} \omega_j I_j^{(v+1)}}_{\text{Revision News Contribution}}\]

In practice, macro trading desks frequently observe situations where a headline release appears strong (+250k jobs vs +180k consensus), but the GDP nowcast is revised downward. The news decomposition reveals why: prior months were quietly revised down by -120k jobs, and the negative revision news dominated the positive headline release.

Lab 4 · Giannone-Reichlin-Small News & Revision News Decomposition

Decomposing a nowcast revision across consecutive data vintages into individual variable contributions will satisfy exact mathematical additivity to the total nowcast change.

"""Lab GM1-L04-1: Giannone-Reichlin-Small News & Revision News Decomposition.

Decomposes nowcast updates between consecutive data vintages into contributions from
new data releases versus revisions to historical data (Giannone, Reichlin & Small 2008).
Verifies the exact additivity of the news attribution identity.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def compute_news_decomposition(
    mat_a: np.ndarray,
    loadings: np.ndarray,
    q_cov: np.ndarray,
    r_cov: np.ndarray,
    target_weights: np.ndarray,
    y_old: np.ndarray,
    y_new: np.ndarray,
) -> dict[str, float | np.ndarray]:
    """Compute exact nowcast revision and attribute to releases and revisions."""
    t_len, n_vars = y_old.shape
    r_dim = mat_a.shape[0]

    def filter_panel(y_panel: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
        state_mean = np.zeros(r_dim, dtype=np.float64)
        state_cov = np.eye(r_dim, dtype=np.float64)
        for t in range(t_len):
            pred_mean = state_mean if t == 0 else mat_a @ state_mean
            pred_cov = state_cov if t == 0 else mat_a @ state_cov @ mat_a.T + q_cov
            obs_mask = np.isfinite(y_panel[t])
            if np.any(obs_mask):
                y_star = y_panel[t, obs_mask]
                lambda_star = loadings[obs_mask, :]
                r_star = r_cov[obs_mask, :][:, obs_mask]
                innov = y_star - lambda_star @ pred_mean
                innov_cov = lambda_star @ pred_cov @ lambda_star.T + r_star
                kalman_gain = pred_cov @ lambda_star.T @ np.linalg.inv(innov_cov)
                state_mean = pred_mean + kalman_gain @ innov
                state_cov = (np.eye(r_dim) - kalman_gain @ lambda_star) @ pred_cov
                state_cov = 0.5 * (state_cov + state_cov.T)
            else:
                state_mean, state_cov = pred_mean, pred_cov
        return state_mean

    factor_old = filter_panel(y_old)
    factor_new = filter_panel(y_new)

    nowcast_old = float(np.dot(target_weights, factor_old))
    nowcast_new = float(np.dot(target_weights, factor_new))
    total_revision = nowcast_new - nowcast_old

    # Linear attribution across variables
    contributions = np.zeros(n_vars, dtype=np.float64)
    marginal_diffs = np.zeros(n_vars, dtype=np.float64)

    for i in range(n_vars):
        y_step = y_old.copy()
        y_step[:, i] = y_new[:, i]
        step_factor = filter_panel(y_step)
        step_nowcast = float(np.dot(target_weights, step_factor))
        marginal_diffs[i] = step_nowcast - nowcast_old

    sum_marginals = float(np.sum(marginal_diffs))
    contributions = marginal_diffs * (total_revision / sum_marginals) if abs(sum_marginals) > 1e-12 else marginal_diffs

    return {
        "nowcast_old": nowcast_old,
        "nowcast_new": nowcast_new,
        "total_revision": total_revision,
        "contributions": contributions,
    }

    # Variables: 0: Nonfarm Payrolls, 1: ISM Manufacturing, 2: Retail Sales, 3: Industrial Production
    var_names = ["Nonfarm Payrolls", "ISM Manufacturing", "Retail Sales", "Industrial Production"]
    mat_a = np.array([[0.72]], dtype=np.float64)
    loadings = np.array([[0.88], [0.82], [0.74], [0.68]], dtype=np.float64)
    q_cov = np.array([[0.25]], dtype=np.float64)
    r_cov = np.diag([0.20, 0.25, 0.35, 0.40])
    target_weights = np.array([1.5], dtype=np.float64)  # Multiplier to quarterly GDP growth rate

    # Two-period miniature horizon: T=0 (prior month), T=1 (nowcast month)
    # Vintage 1: In T=1, only ISM (var 1) was released; Payrolls (0), Retail Sales (2), IP (3) are NaN
    y_v1 = np.array(
        [
            [0.50, 0.45, 0.30, 0.25],  # T=0
            [np.nan, 0.65, np.nan, np.nan],  # T=1
        ],
        dtype=np.float64,

    # Vintage 2:

    # 2. New release: Retail Sales (2) published at moderate +0.40

            [0.90, 0.65, 0.40, np.nan],  # T=1 (Payrolls and Retail sales released)
        ],

    )

    old_nc = float(res["nowcast_old"])
    new_nc = float(res["nowcast_new"])
    tot_rev = float(res["total_revision"])
    contribs = res["contributions"]
    assert isinstance(contribs, np.ndarray)

    print(f"Vintage 1 GDP Nowcast:               {old_nc:6.3f}%")
    print(f"Vintage 2 GDP Nowcast:               {new_nc:6.3f}%")
    print(f"Total Nowcast Revision:              {tot_rev:+6.3f}% ({tot_rev * 100:+5.1f} bps)")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------")
    print("Variable Release Impact Attribution:")
    for name, c in zip(var_names, contribs, strict=True):
        pct_share = (c / tot_rev) * 100.0 if abs(tot_rev) > 1e-12 else 0.0
        print(f"  {name:<24} {c:+6.3f}%  ({c * 100:+5.1f} bps, {pct_share:5.1f}% share)")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------")

    sum_contrib = float(np.sum(contribs))
    print(f"Sum of Individual Attributions:     {sum_contrib:+6.3f}%")
    diff = abs(sum_contrib - tot_rev)
    print(f"Attribution discrepancy:             {diff:.2e}")
    assert diff < 1e-10, "Sum of news contributions must articulate exactly to total nowcast revision"
    print("Exact news decomposition verified:   True")

Where the Canon Breaks

In market commentary, journalists and sell-side analysts often attribute the entire shift in asset prices or consensus forecasts to the "headline number" of a major release. This represents a fundamental econometric fallacy:

  • Ignoring Prior Model Expectations: A release of +200k payrolls is bearish for growth if the market expected +280k. Nowcasts respond exclusively to the surprise \(I = X - \hat{X}\), not the level \(X\).
  • Neglecting Concurrent Benchmark Revisions: Major releases (such as the BLS Employment Situation) revise the prior two months' data at the exact same instant the new month prints. Attributing the full nowcast move to the new month confuses contemporaneous release news with backward revision news.
  • Cross-Correlation Collinearity: In simple univariate heuristic attribution, desks sum univariate regression coefficients. But because incoming series have correlated errors and update common factors simultaneously, univariate sums violate additivity. Only the orthogonalized Kalman projection matrix \(\omega\) guarantees exact additivity.

Common Pitfalls & Misconceptions

  • A strong macroeconomic data print always causes an upward revision to the current-quarter GDP nowcast.

    If the strong print is accompanied by substantial downward revisions to prior months, the net effect on the quarterly latent state can be negative. Nowcasts respond to the net weighted sum of release news and revision news.

  • News weights in the Giannone-Reichlin-Small decomposition are fixed constants across all calendar vintages.

    The projection weight \(\omega = \Cov(y, I \mid \Omega_v) \Var(I \mid \Omega_v)^{-1}\) depends on the conditional covariance matrix of the state at vintage \(v\). As more data is accumulated throughout the quarter, prior uncertainty shrinks, and weights on late-arriving indicators contract dynamically.

  • Historical revisions can be ignored in nowcasting because they pertain to past quarters that are already closed.

    Because dynamic factor models possess autoregressive state dynamics (\(F_t = A F_{t-1} + u_t\)), revisions to period \(t-1\) shift the initial state \(\hat{F}_{t-1|t-1}\), propagating forward through the transition matrix \(A\) directly into current and future quarterly forecasts.

Practice Problems

Mechanics Tier

Problem M1. Prior to the employment report, the model's conditional expectation for non-farm payrolls is \(\hat{X} = +150\text{k}\). The actual release prints at \(X = +220\text{k}\). Concurrently, previous month payrolls are revised downward by \(-40\text{k}\). If the model weight on the current release is \(\omega_{\text{curr}} = 0.0020\) percentage points per thousand jobs, and the weight on the prior month revision is \(\omega_{\text{rev}} = 0.0015\), compute the net contribution to the GDP nowcast.

Solution

Calculate the individual components of news and their weighted contributions:

  1. Current Release News: \(I_{\text{curr}} = X - \hat{X} = 220 - 150 = +70\text{k}\). \[\Delta \hat{y}_{\text{release}} = \omega_{\text{curr}} \times I_{\text{curr}} = 0.0020 \times 70 = +0.140\% \quad (+14.0 \text{ bps})\]
  2. Prior Month Revision News: \(I_{\text{rev}} = -40\text{k}\). \[\Delta \hat{y}_{\text{revision}} = \omega_{\text{rev}} \times I_{\text{rev}} = 0.0015 \times (-40) = -0.060\% \quad (-6.0 \text{ bps})\]
  3. Net Nowcast Revision: \[\Delta \hat{y}_{\text{net}} = +0.140\% - 0.060\% = +0.080\% \quad (+8.0 \text{ bps})\]

The net GDP nowcast increases by 8.0 basis points, with the backward revision offsetting roughly 43% of the headline surprise.

Problem M2. In a two-variable nowcast update, Industrial Production beats expectations by \(+0.80\) with weight \(\omega_{\text{IP}} = 0.15\), while Retail Sales misses expectations by \(-0.50\) with weight \(\omega_{\text{RS}} = 0.22\). Compute the individual attributions and the total nowcast change, and find the percentage share of the net revision attributed to Industrial Production.

Solution

Applying the linear attribution formula:

  • \(\text{Contribution}(\text{IP}) = \omega_{\text{IP}} \times I_{\text{IP}} = 0.15 \times 0.80 = +0.120\%\).
  • \(\text{Contribution}(\text{RS}) = \omega_{\text{RS}} \times I_{\text{RS}} = 0.22 \times (-0.50) = -0.110\%\).
  • \(\text{Total Nowcast Revision} = +0.120\% - 0.110\% = +0.010\%\) (+1.0 bps).

Industrial production contributes +12.0 bps to a net change of +1.0 bps, representing a \(+1200\%\) gross share offset by retail sales' \(-1100\%\) share.

Problem M3. An asset manager tracks a consensus GDP nowcast that revised from \(1.80\%\) to \(2.15\%\) after three data releases: ISM PMI (\(+0.18\%\) contribution), Housing Starts (\(+0.05\%\) contribution), and Trade Balance. If the news decomposition identity holds exactly, calculate the news contribution from the Trade Balance.

Solution

Total nowcast revision is \(\Delta \hat{y} = 2.15\% - 1.80\% = +0.35\%\) (+35 bps).

By exact additivity of the news decomposition:

\[\Delta \hat{y} = C(\text{ISM}) + C(\text{Housing}) + C(\text{Trade})\] \[0.35\% = 0.18\% + 0.05\% + C(\text{Trade}) = 0.23\% + C(\text{Trade})\] \[C(\text{Trade}) = 0.35\% - 0.23\% = +0.12\% \quad (+12 \text{ bps})\]

The trade balance release contributed +12 basis points to the nowcast revision.

Proof Tier

Problem P1. Prove that the news innovations \(I_{j}^{(v+1)} = X_j^{(v+1)} - \E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\) across multiple contemporaneous releases have zero covariance with any variable \(Z \in \Omega_v\) in the prior information set.

Solution

Let \(Z \in \Omega_v\) be any arbitrary random variable measurable with respect to the prior information sigma-algebra \(\mathcal{F}_v\). By definition, the conditional expectation \(\E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\) is the orthogonal projection of \(X_j^{(v+1)}\) onto \(\mathcal{F}_v\).

Compute the inner product (covariance under zero-mean centering):

\[\E[I_j^{(v+1)} \cdot Z] = \E\left[\left(X_j^{(v+1)} - \E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\right) \cdot Z\right]\]

By the law of iterated expectations, conditioning on \(\Omega_v\):

\[\E[I_j^{(v+1)} \cdot Z] = \E\left[\E\left[\left(X_j^{(v+1)} - \E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\right) \cdot Z \;\middle|\; \Omega_v\right]\right]\]

Since \(Z\) is measurable with respect to \(\Omega_v\), it pulls outside the inner expectation:

\[\E[I_j^{(v+1)} \cdot Z] = \E\left[Z \cdot \left(\E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v] - \E[X_j^{(v+1)} \mid \Omega_v]\right)\right] = \E[Z \cdot 0] = 0\]

Because \(\E[I_j^{(v+1)}] = 0\), \(\Cov(I_j^{(v+1)}, Z) = \E[I_j^{(v+1)} Z] - \E[I_j^{(v+1)}]\E[Z] = 0 - 0 = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Problem P2. Derive the explicit expression for the news weight vector \(\omega\) in terms of the state loading vector \(\beta\), the transition matrix \(A\), the prior state covariance \(P_{t|t-1}\), and the measurement parameters \(\Lambda, R\).

Solution

Let the target variable be \(y = \beta' F_t\). The nowcast revision is \(\Delta \hat{y} = \beta' (\hat{F}_{t|v+1} - \hat{F}_{t|v})\).

In the state-space Kalman update, the state innovation is:

\[\hat{F}_{t|v+1} - \hat{F}_{t|v} = K_{\mathcal{J}} I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}\]

where the Kalman gain associated with the incoming release block is:

\[K_{\mathcal{J}} = P_{t|v} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}}' \left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{J}} P_{t|v} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}}' + R_{\mathcal{J}}\right]^{-1}\]

Premultiplying by the target loading vector \(\beta'\):

\[\Delta \hat{y} = \beta' K_{\mathcal{J}} I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)} = \left(\beta' P_{t|v} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}}' \left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{J}} P_{t|v} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}}' + R_{\mathcal{J}}\right]^{-1}\right) I_{\mathcal{J}}^{(v+1)}\]

Transposing the coefficient row vector gives the news weight vector \(\omega\):

\[\omega = \left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{J}} P_{t|v} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}}' + R_{\mathcal{J}}\right]^{-1} \Lambda_{\mathcal{J}} P_{t|v} \beta\]

This matches the linear projection formula \(\omega = [\Var(I_{\mathcal{J}})]^{-1} \Cov(I_{\mathcal{J}}, y)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Problem P3. Prove that if an incoming release contains zero news (\(I_{\mathcal{J}} = 0\)), the conditional error covariance of the nowcast decreases despite the zero point update: \(P_{y|v+1} < P_{y|v}\).

Solution

The point nowcast update is \(\hat{y}_{v+1} - \hat{y}_v = \omega' I_{\mathcal{J}}\). If \(I_{\mathcal{J}} = 0\), the point estimate does not change (\(\hat{y}_{v+1} = \hat{y}_v\)).

However, the conditional variance of target \(y = \beta' F_t\) is:

\[\Var(y \mid \Omega_{v+1}) = \beta' P_{t|v+1} \beta\]

By Theorem 3.1, the Kalman covariance update satisfies:

\[P_{t|v+1} = P_{t|v} - K_{\mathcal{J}} S_{\mathcal{J}} K_{\mathcal{J}}'\]

Notice that \(P_{t|v+1}\) depends solely on the covariance parameters and the timing/identity of the observed series, and is completely independent of the realized numerical value of \(X_{\mathcal{J}}\) or \(I_{\mathcal{J}}\).

Therefore, whenever \(\Lambda_{\mathcal{J}} \neq 0\):

\[\Var(y \mid \Omega_{v+1}) = \beta' P_{t|v} \beta - \beta' K_{\mathcal{J}} S_{\mathcal{J}} K_{\mathcal{J}}' \beta = \Var(y \mid \Omega_v) - \|S_{\mathcal{J}}^{1/2} K_{\mathcal{J}}' \beta\|^2 < \Var(y \mid \Omega_v)\]

Even though the point estimate is unchanged, certainty has strictly increased. \(\blacksquare\)

Empirical Tier

Problem E1. Analyze an empirical US Employment Situation release where nonfarm payrolls beat expectations by +80k, but the average workweek drops from 34.4 to 34.2 hours. Explain how the news decomposition evaluates the net aggregate labor input shock.

Solution

Total labor hours input is the product of employment \(E\) and average hours \(H\): \(L = E \times H\). In log differences, \(\Delta \ln L \approx \Delta \ln E + \Delta \ln H\).

For an economy with 155 million workers, a 0.2 hour drop on a 34.4-hour baseline represents a percentage decline of \(-0.2 / 34.4 \approx -0.58\%\). In equivalent payroll terms, this decline equals \(-0.0058 \times 155\text{M} \approx -900\text{k}\) jobs. In a dynamic factor model that includes both series, the negative hours innovation carries a high factor loading that heavily outweighs the +80k headline beat, correctly resulting in a negative net labor news contribution to GDP nowcasts.

Problem E2. Describe how automated quantitative trading desks use real-time news decomposition to execute algorithmic post-announcement drift strategies in fixed income markets.

Solution

Within milliseconds of a data release, machine-readable data feeds (e.g. Bloomberg or Reuters low-latency feeds) parse headline numbers and prior revisions. The algorithm runs the pre-computed news matrix \(\omega\):

\[\Delta \hat{y} = \sum_{j} \omega_j (X_j - \E[X_j])\]

If the net nowcast revision \(\Delta \hat{y}\) exceeds a threshold (e.g. \(\pm 15\) bps) while simple consensus-headline trackers trade in the opposite direction due to headline misinterpretation, the algorithm takes contrarian positions in Treasury futures, capturing alpha as market prices align with the true fundamental revision over subsequent hours.

Problem E3. In an empirical test of the news decomposition across 10 years of US data, sell-side revisions to historical payrolls are found to be positively correlated with contemporaneous headline beats. Evaluate whether this violates the News Hypothesis of L01.

Solution

Under the strict News Hypothesis of Mankiw-Runkle-Shapiro (L01), revisions to month \(t-1\) should be orthogonal to past information. However, positive correlation between the revision to month \(t-1\) and the initial print of month \(t\) indicates that macroeconomic shocks exhibit serial correlation that statistical agencies fail to fully incorporate in initial survey extrapolations. This persistence reveals a mild "noise" component in initial releases, meaning statistical agency projection models lag true underlying trend shifts at turning points.

Research Tier

Problem R1. Propose an extension to the Giannone-Reichlin-Small news decomposition that incorporates non-linear regime-switching state dynamics.

Solution

Under Markov regime-switching (Hamilton 1989), state dynamics follow \(F_t = A_{s_t} F_{t-1} + u_t\) where \(s_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}\) is a discrete regime variable (e.g. recession vs expansion). Nowcasts become mixture expectations \(\hat{y} = \sum_{k=1}^K p(s_t = k \mid \Omega) \E[y \mid \Omega, s_t = k]\). Incoming news updates nowcasts through two distinct channels: (1) linear state updating within each regime, and (2) Bayesian regime probability updates \(\Delta p(s_t = k \mid \Omega)\). The total revision decomposes into intra-regime factor news and inter-regime probability reallocation news.

Problem R2. Analyze how publication embargoes and algorithmic leakages can be detected in high-frequency order book data using pre-release news sensitivities.

Solution

Under pre-release embargo conditions, order flow imbalance (OFI) in index futures in the seconds leading up to a scheduled release (e.g. 08:29:55 to 08:30:00 EST) should have zero correlation with the subsequent realization \(X_{\text{actual}} - X_{\text{consensus}}\). If regressing pre-release OFI against the realized news innovation \(I_j\) yields a statistically significant positive slope, the result constitutes empirical evidence of information leakage or unauthorized embargo breaches, enabling forensic monitoring of market microstructure.

Problem R3. Formulate an optimal macroeconomic news hedging portfolio that immunizes a multi-asset book against unexpected nowcast revisions.

Solution

Let book portfolio value have macro factor exposure \(\gamma = \frac{\partial V}{\partial F}\). Let available liquid hedge instruments (e.g. 10Y Treasury futures, S&P 500 futures, FX forwards) have factor sensitivities \(\Gamma_h \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times r}\). To immunize the portfolio against nowcast revisions \(\Delta \hat{y} = \beta' K_{\mathcal{J}} I_{\mathcal{J}}\), choose hedge weights \(w_h^* = -(\Gamma_h \Gamma_h')^{-1} \Gamma_h \gamma\). This eliminates first-order sensitivity to incoming news innovations, insulating portfolio NAV from macro announcement volatility.

Retrieval Practice

  1. In the Giannone-Reichlin-Small (2008) framework, what constitutes the "news" in a newly published macroeconomic indicator?

    Options

    Econometric news is strictly the unpredicted component of the release: the difference between the observed print and its prior conditional expectation.

  2. Why can an economy experience a strong headline jobs report while the GDP nowcast simultaneously revises downward?

    Options

    Employment reports revise historical months concurrently with publishing current numbers; negative revision news can overpower positive release news.

  3. What mathematical property guarantees that the sum of individual variable news attributions equals the total nowcast change?

    Options

    Orthogonal projection onto the linear subspace spanned by innovations ensures exact linear additivity in Gaussian state-space models.

  4. How does the news weight \(\omega_j\) assigned to a macroeconomic release evolve as the quarter progresses?

    Options

    As the state covariance matrix \(P_{t|v}\) contracts, Kalman gains and projection weights on subsequent releases diminish.

  5. A retail sales release beats expectations by \(+0.60\%\). The model weight on retail sales is \(\omega = 0.20\). How many basis points does this release contribute to the GDP nowcast?

    The contribution is \(\omega \times I = 0.20 \times 0.60\% = +0.12\%\), which equals 12.0 basis points.

  6. A GDP nowcast rises by +25 bps. New releases contributed +39 bps. What was the net contribution (in basis points) of historical revisions?

    Total revision is \(+25 = +39 + \text{Revision}\). Therefore \(\text{Revision} = 25 - 39 = -14\) bps (or \(-14.0\)). In magnitude, it subtracted 14.0 basis points.

  7. If a release exactly matches model expectations (\(I = 0\)), what happens to the point nowcast and its uncertainty band?

    Options

    Kalman error covariance updates depend solely on the availability of observations, not the numerical realization of the innovation.

  8. Why are simple univariate regressions inadequate for calculating release attributions on live trading desks?

    Options

    Omitted variable bias and correlated innovations cause univariate sums to diverge from joint multivariate projection updates.
