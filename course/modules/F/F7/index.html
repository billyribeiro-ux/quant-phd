Performance Evaluation & Skill

Separating true investment skill from lucky sample paths, unpriced risk factor loadings, mark-to-model return smoothing, and concealed tail risk is the central challenge of modern quantitative allocation. Across five rigorous doctoral lessons, we deconstruct the asymptotic distributions of alpha and the Sharpe ratio, the econometric inversion of appraisal smoothing, and the option-like nonlinear payoff profiles of dynamic hedge fund strategies. The module culminates in authoring an end-to-end quantitative manager due-diligence engine that extracts factor-adjusted alpha under HAC standard errors, corrects Sharpe ratios for serial autocorrelation, uncovers true latent risk via Getmansky–Lo–Makarov maximum likelihood unsmoothing, detects hidden short-volatility exposure, and produces an honest fiduciary manager ranking.

  • Prerequisites: F2 The Classic Factors (multi-factor models, spanning regressions, factor construction) and S3 Time Series (autocorrelation functions, ARMA processes, state-space representations).
  • Unlocks: Advanced Portfolio Studios, Quantitative Macro Strategies, and Chief Investment Officer Asset Allocation.
  • Project: Manager Due-Diligence Tool: authoring due_diligence.py to evaluate multi-manager track records across factor alpha, serial-correlation-adjusted Sharpe ratios, GLM return unsmoothing, and nonlinear option convexity.
  • Data: Deterministic simulated panels reproducing empirical hedge fund return distributions, appraisal smoothing schedules, and option-like tail payoffs.

36 h lessons · 24 h project (40%)

Objectives

By the end of F7 you can:

  1. Formulate the asymptotic sampling distribution of Jensen's alpha, apply White heteroskedasticity-consistent and Newey–West HAC covariance estimators, and implement cross-sectional false discovery rate (FDR) adjustments separating skill from luck (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the Jobson–Korkie and Lo (2002) asymptotic distributions of the Sharpe ratio, proving why naive \(\sqrt{12}\) annualization overstates risk-adjusted performance under positive serial correlation, and evaluate the \(\eta(q)\) correction factor (Lesson 2).
  3. Analyze the structural mechanisms of return smoothing in illiquid asset classes, formalizing the Getmansky–Lo–Makarov moving-average framework, proving the variance reduction ratio \(\xi = \sum \theta_j^2\), and diagnosing zero-return threshold discontinuities (Lesson 3).
  4. Implement constrained maximum likelihood estimation of moving-average smoothing parameters, recover unobserved true economic volatility \(\sigma^*\), and reconstruct unsmoothed fund track records (Lesson 4).
  5. Deconstruct nonlinear option-like exposures in hedge funds and alternative risk premia, estimate Treynor–Mazuy and Fung–Hsieh curvature metrics, and identify short-volatility carry strategies masquerading as alpha (Lesson 5).
  6. Execute quantitative operational and track-record due diligence, adjusting for survivorship, backfill, and selection biases while auditing for operational fraud indicators (Lesson 5).
  7. Author the complete autograded due-diligence engine, passing 50 randomized seeds and defending manager allocations against hostile investment committee cross-examination (Project).

Prerequisites

F7 directly builds on the foundational econometric and empirical asset pricing machinery of the quantitative curriculum: From the Fama–French three-factor model, the Carhart four-factor model, the Fama–French five-factor model, and spanning regressions in F2, F7 extracts systematic factor tilts to isolate genuine manager alpha. From the autocorrelation function, ARMA models, and state-space models in S3, F7 models the serial dependence induced by illiquidity and appraisal pricing. From the Sharpe ratio in S1 and Jensen's alpha in F1, F7 constructs exact finite-sample and asymptotic confidence intervals.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six testing prerequisite concepts from F2 and S3, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In a Carhart (1997) four-factor benchmark regression (F2), what economic risk exposure does the WML (winner-minus-loser) factor loading control for?

    Options

    Carhart (1997) added the momentum factor to demonstrate that common fund persistence was largely explained by systematic momentum exposures rather than persistent stock-picking skill.

  2. Under the classical Fama–French three-factor model (F2), if a fund manager holds high book-to-market equities, how does their single-factor CAPM alpha compare to their three-factor alpha?

    Options

    A single-factor regression omits the value factor HML; if the value premium is positive, an omitted factor with positive loading biases the single-factor intercept upward.

  3. In time-series analysis (S3), what is the theoretical autocovariance function \(\gamma_k\) of an MA(2) process \(X_t = \epsilon_t + \theta_1 \epsilon_{t-1} + \theta_2 \epsilon_{t-2}\) for lag \(k > 2\)?

    Options

    An MA(\(q\)) process has autocovariances that are identically zero for all lags \(k > q\), distinguishing it from autoregressive processes.

  4. Why does the presence of conditional heteroskedasticity and serial correlation in regression residuals invalidate classical OLS standard errors (S3)?

    Options

    Non-spherical errors leave OLS point estimates unbiased but invalidate classical variance formulas, necessitating Newey–West HAC covariance estimation.

  5. What structural property characterizes a stationary autoregressive AR(1) process \(r_t = \phi r_{t-1} + \epsilon_t\) (S3)?

    Options

    Weak stationarity requires the root of the autoregressive polynomial to lie outside the complex unit circle, which for AR(1) is \(|\phi| < 1\).

  6. How does spanning regression analysis (F2) determine whether a candidate factor adds value to an existing benchmark model?

    Options

    If the intercept is zero, the candidate factor's returns are fully spanned by the existing model and it cannot expand the mean-variance efficient frontier.

  7. Skill check 1: In the Lo (2002) time-aggregation correction factor \(\eta(q) = \sqrt{\frac{q}{q + 2\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}(q-k)\rho_k}}\), why does positive serial correlation (\(\rho_k > 0\)) force \(\eta(q) < 1\)?

    Options

    When \(\rho_k > 0\), the cross-product terms \(2(q-k)\rho_k\) are positive, increasing the denominator and driving \(\eta(q) < 1\).

  8. Skill check 2: In the Getmansky, Lo & Makarov (2004) return smoothing model with MA(2) weights \(\theta_0 = 0.50, \theta_1 = 0.30, \theta_2 = 0.20\), what is the theoretical variance reduction ratio \(\xi = \sum_{j=0}^2 \theta_j^2\)?

    \(\xi = 0.50^2 + 0.30^2 + 0.20^2 = 0.25 + 0.09 + 0.04 = 0.3800\).

Lesson Map

  1. Lesson 01 · Classical Performance Attribution & Alpha Inference

    7 study hours

    The Jensen (1968) paradigm, multi-factor benchmarking (Carhart 4-factor, Fama–French 5-factor), White and Newey–West HAC standard errors, cross-sectional bootstrapping, and the False Discovery Rate (FDR).

  2. Lesson 02 · The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios & Serial Correlation

    7 study hours

    Jobson–Korkie asymptotic variance, Lo (2002) serial-correlation corrections, the \(\eta(q)\) annualization haircut, non-normality extensions (Mertens), and robust Sharpe hypothesis testing.

  3. Lesson 03 · Illiquidity, Mark-to-Model, and Return Smoothing

    7 study hours

    Appraisal pricing in private equity and real estate, the Getmansky–Lo–Makarov MA(k) specification, volatility dampening, artificial Sharpe inflation, and zero-return threshold manipulation.

  4. Lesson 04 · Econometric Return Unsmoothing

    7 study hours

    Geltner AR(1) inversion, GLM constrained maximum likelihood estimation, the Herfindahl concentration index \(\xi\), recovering true latent volatility \(\sigma^*\), and modern state-space extensions.

  5. Lesson 05 · Nonlinear Payoffs, Option Exposures & Due Diligence

    8 study hours

    Fung & Hsieh (2001) trend-following straddles, Treynor–Mazuy quadratic curvature, short volatility masquerading as alpha, database biases (survivorship and backfill), and operational due diligence.

Project Ladder

The module culminates in a comprehensive 24-hour quantitative project structured across the four-rung ladder:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance evaluating a 5-manager institutional portfolio across factor alpha, Lo adjusted Sharpe ratios, GLM unsmoothing, and nonlinear option convexity.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Complete the core econometric functions for serial correlation adjustment and GLM constrained likelihood estimation with instant verification.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Author the complete due_diligence.py engine, passing 50 randomized seeds and delivering an institutional fiduciary evaluation memo.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the engine to hierarchical Bayesian false discovery shrinkage across 1,000 managers with regime-switching factor exposures and non-Gaussian priors.
