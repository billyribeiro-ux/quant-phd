    Lab 1 · Volatility-Scaled Momentum Worked Example

    Inverse-volatility scaling dampens momentum exposure during volatile periods, stabilizing monthly return risk and mitigating peak drawdown without sacrificing long-term risk-adjusted returns.

    """Lab F4-project-1: Project Rung 1 - Worked Example on Volatility-Managed Momentum.

Demonstrates the core mechanics of constructing dollar-neutral WML weights,
estimating strictly causal trailing realized volatility, and applying inverse-volatility
scaling with leverage caps.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def main() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(123)
    n_months = 120
    n_stocks = 20

    # Simulate stock returns with positive momentum drift
    rets = rng.normal(0.008, 0.05, size=(n_months, n_stocks))

    # Signal: past 12-month return skipping month t-1
    signals = np.zeros_like(rets)
    for t in range(12, n_months):
        signals[t] = np.sum(rets[t - 12 : t - 1], axis=0)

    # Dollar-neutral weights: top 4 long (+0.25 each), bottom 4 short (-0.25 each)
    weights = np.zeros_like(rets)
    for t in range(12, n_months):
        ranks = np.argsort(np.argsort(signals[t]))
        weights[t, ranks >= (n_stocks - 4)] = 0.25
        weights[t, ranks < 4] = -0.25

    # Unscaled WML returns
    wml_raw = np.sum(weights * rets, axis=1)

    # Causal realized volatility (6-month trailing window)
    vol_est = np.zeros(n_months)
    for t in range(n_months):
        hist = wml_raw[max(0, t - 6) : t]
        vol_est[t] = 0.15 if len(hist) < 3 else max(0.04, float(np.std(hist, ddof=1)) * np.sqrt(12.0))

    # Scaled weights: target vol 0.12, max leverage 2.0
    w_vol = np.clip(0.12 / vol_est, 0.0, 2.0)
    wml_scaled = w_vol * wml_raw

    # Performance
    sh_raw = float(np.mean(wml_raw[12:])) / max(1e-6, float(np.std(wml_raw[12:], ddof=1))) * np.sqrt(12.0)
    sh_scaled = float(np.mean(wml_scaled[12:])) / max(1e-6, float(np.std(wml_scaled[12:], ddof=1))) * np.sqrt(12.0)

    cum_raw = np.cumprod(1.0 + wml_raw[12:])
    cum_scaled = np.cumprod(1.0 + wml_scaled[12:])
    dd_raw = float(np.max((np.maximum.accumulate(cum_raw) - cum_raw) / np.maximum.accumulate(cum_raw)))
    dd_scaled = float(np.max((np.maximum.accumulate(cum_scaled) - cum_scaled) / np.maximum.accumulate(cum_scaled)))

    print("=== PROJECT RUNG 1: VOLATILITY-SCALED MOMENTUM WORKED EXAMPLE ===")
    print(f"Raw WML:        Annualized Sharpe={sh_raw:.2f}, Max Drawdown={dd_raw:.2%}")
    print(f"Vol-Scaled WML: Annualized Sharpe={sh_scaled:.2f}, Max Drawdown={dd_scaled:.2%}")
    print(f"Mean Exposure Scalar: {float(np.mean(w_vol[12:])):.2f}x (Leverage Cap: 2.0x)")


main()

Project Specification

Context
Momentum strategies offer attractive long-term risk premia but are plagued by catastrophic crashes during market rebounds following bear market panics (Daniel & Moskowitz 2016). Quantitative hedge funds need robust risk-management overlays that eliminate crash risk without forfeiting out-of-sample alpha or succumbing to transaction cost drag (Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz 2018, Cederburg et al. 2020).
Inputs
A 30-year monthly dataset generated deterministically by make_instance(seed) in instance.py, containing stock_returns (shape \(360 \times 50\)), market_returns (length 360), risk_free_rate (\(2\%\) annualized), and cost_bps (15 bps per unit turned over).
Required outputs
A fully typed, robust Python module crash_overlay.py providing:
  • compute_momentum_signals: Cross-sectional momentum return signals with lookback and skip-month.
  • construct_wml_weights: Dollar-neutral long/short portfolio weights.
  • estimate_realized_volatility: Strictly causal rolling standard deviation estimator.
  • identify_panic_states: Bear market panic state detection (\(\sum r_{\text{mkt}} < 0\)).
  • compute_dynamic_overlay_weights: Target volatility and panic-state dynamic exposure scalars.
  • backtest_strategy: Backtest engine with linear transaction cost deduction.
  • evaluate_performance: Annualized mean, volatility, Sharpe ratio, skewness, and max drawdown.
  • run_overlay_experiment: Comprehensive experiment pipeline across strategies.
Constraints
Strict causality: volatility estimation at month \(t\) must strictly exclude month \(t\) returns. Dollar neutrality: weights must sum to exactly zero, with long positions summing to \(+1.0\) and short positions summing to \(-1.0\). Leverage capping: exposure scalars cannot exceed max_leverage. Transaction costs: must deduct turnover multiplied by basis points from gross returns. All code must run without errors under Python 3.14 and pass mypy --strict.
Grading
This project is evaluated by both autograder and rubric (dual assessment). The autograder validates mathematical invariants, edge cases, and crash mitigation across 50 randomized seeds (0–49). The rubric evaluates your quantitative methodology, economic defense, and executive verdict memo.

Exemplar 1 · Level 4 (Distinguished)

Executive Verdict Memo: Institutional Evaluation of Dynamic Momentum & Crash-Risk Overlays

We evaluate whether the dynamic momentum strategy formulated by Daniel & Moskowitz (2016) survives out of sample, net of transaction costs, and against a simple volatility-scaled benchmark across 30 years of empirical market cycles. Unmanaged cross-sectional momentum generates attractive returns during calm bull markets but suffers catastrophic left-tail crashes during violent market rebounds following bear market panic states. During these turning points, distressed past losers act like out-of-the-money call options on firm assets, causing their market beta to surge from \(\approx 1.15\) to \(>3.20\), while winner betas remain anchored near \(0.85\). This produces an extreme negative up-market beta (\(\beta_{WML} \approx -2.40\)), driving catastrophic momentum losses exceeding \(-70\%\) during rebounds. While simple inverse-volatility scaling (Harvey et al. 2018) stabilizes risk across steady regimes, it remains vulnerable when market volatility has not yet peaked at the inflection point. The Daniel & Moskowitz crash overlay—scaling exposure by forecast variance and aggressively reducing exposure to \(20\%\) when trailing 2-year market returns are negative—eliminates these crash losses. Net of institutional transaction costs (\(15\text{ bps}\) per unit turned over per Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz 2018), dynamic momentum preserves capital during crisis rebounds, cuts maximum drawdown by more than half, converts negative skewness into positive skewness, and delivers a robust out-of-sample Sharpe ratio exceeding \(0.50\). Addressing the Cederburg et al. (2020) critique, we demonstrate that while unconditional volatility scaling can suffer from parameter sensitivity and turnover drag, conditioning on the macro panic state is economically grounded in structural optionality and provides genuine out-of-sample crash protection. We recommend implementing the dynamic crash overlay for institutional momentum allocations.
