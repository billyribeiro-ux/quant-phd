Data & Methodology

Empirical asset pricing rests on foundational data structures, point-in-time matching discipline, survivorship resolution, and portfolio sorting methodologies. This module establishes the rigorous data engineering and non-parametric econometric toolkits required to test asset pricing anomalies and construct investable risk factors. Across six intensive lessons, we dissect the database structures of CRSP and Compustat alongside bitemporal link tables (Lesson 1); analyze survivorship bias and historical database inclusion rules (Lesson 2); quantify delisting bias and implement Shumway's missing-return corrections (Lesson 3); formulate univariate and bivariate portfolio sorting methodologies (Lesson 4); examine the microcap problem and evaluate the necessity of NYSE breakpoints (Lesson 5); and contrast value-weighted versus equal-weighted aggregation schemes under monthly rebalancing (Lesson 6). The module culminates in Milestone A7 of Atlas: authoring an autograded cross-sectional sorting and asset pricing engine.

  • Prerequisites: S2 Linear Models & Classical Inference, AP2 Portfolio Theory & Classical Asset Pricing, QR1 Financial Data Architecture, and S5 GMM & Cross-Sectional Econometrics.
  • Unlocks: F2 The Classic Factors, and Part F research studios.
  • Project: Atlas A7 Cross-Sectional Engine: a high-performance, fully typed Python module (atlas.cross_section) implementing Shumway delisting corrections, NYSE breakpoint portfolio sorts, two-pass Fama–MacBeth with Shanken standard errors, and the Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (GRS) exact $F$-test.
  • Data: Deterministic simulated panels reproducing empirical exchange allocations, microcap skewness, distress hazards, and factor structures.

42 h lessons · 18 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of F1 you can:

  1. Deconstruct the database architecture of CRSP and Compustat, execute bitemporal identifier matching via CCM link tables, and enforce point-in-time June portfolio formation discipline (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the mathematical mechanism of survivorship bias and quantify the phantom alpha created by conditioning samples on historical survival (Lesson 2).
  3. Identify performance delisting events in CRSP and apply Shumway's empirical delisting return corrections across major exchanges (Lesson 3).
  4. Construct non-parametric univariate quantiles and bivariate $2 \times 3$ independent and conditional portfolio sorts to isolate factor premia (Lesson 4).
  5. Analyze the microcap problem in equity anomalies and prove why sorting breakpoints must be derived from NYSE securities (Lesson 5).
  6. Compare value-weighted and equal-weighted portfolio performance, calculate monthly rebalancing turnover, and bound microstructure bid-ask bounce drag (Lesson 6).
  7. Engineer the complete Atlas A7 cross-sectional engine, executing portfolio sorts, Fama–MacBeth regressions with Shanken corrections, and the GRS test across 50 seeded test instances (Project).

Prerequisites

F1 builds directly upon foundational concepts from quantitative finance, econometrics, and data engineering: From OLS estimator and errors-in-variables in S2, F1 derives cross-sectional regression mechanics and attenuation bias. From CAPM, beta, Jensen's alpha, and linear factor models in AP2, F1 formalizes empirical risk factor tests. From look-ahead bias, data vintage, and bitemporal data model in QR1, F1 implements point-in-time fundamental linking. From Fama-MacBeth regression, Shanken correction, and the GRS test in S5, F1 embeds multivariate pricing error tests into the core factor engine.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on prerequisite concepts from S2, AP2, QR1, and S5, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Fama-French (1993) portfolio formation convention (QR1, AP2), why is accounting data for a fiscal year ending in December of calendar year \(t-1\) not used to form portfolios until the end of June of calendar year \(t\)?

    Options

    Fama and French established a 6-month lag (forming at the end of June) because historical SEC 10-K filing deadlines permitted up to 90 days after fiscal year-end, ensuring all annual data was fully available to public markets.

  2. In linear regression (S2), how does Classical Errors-in-Variables (measurement error in an explanatory regressor \(X\)) affect the estimated OLS slope \(\hat{\beta}\)?

    Options

    Uncorrelated measurement error introduces attenuation bias toward zero, which is the foundational econometric problem that motivates portfolio sorting and Shanken corrections.

  3. In two-pass cross-sectional asset pricing regressions (S5), what error does the Shanken (1992) correction adjust for?

    Options

    The Shanken correction accounts for the errors-in-variables problem arising because first-pass factor loadings (betas) are estimated rather than known population constants.

  4. What is the exact null hypothesis tested by the Gibbons, Ross, and Shanken (GRS 1989) test statistic (S5)?

    Options

    The GRS test evaluates the joint efficiency of benchmark factors by testing whether all \(N\) intercept terms (\(\alpha\)) equal zero simultaneously.

  5. A two-pass factor model has factor Sharpe ratio \(\operatorname{SR}_f = 0.30\). If the factor portfolio has sample mean \(\bar{f} = 0.006\) and variance \(\sigma_f^2 = 0.0004\), what is the Shanken multiplier \(c = 1 + \bar{f}^2 / \sigma_f^2\)?

    \(c = 1 + \frac{0.006^2}{0.0004} = 1 + \frac{0.000036}{0.0004} = 1 + 0.09 = 1.090\) (or \(1 + \operatorname{SR}^2 = 1 + 0.09 = 1.09\)).

  6. Under modern financial data architecture (QR1), how does a bitemporal data model store corporate restatements?

    Options

    A bitemporal store retains historical data states as known at any prior point in history without overwriting, eliminating retrospective restatement lookahead bias.

  7. Skill check (Rung 1 · Project): An active NYSE stock earns raw monthly return \(R_{\text{CRSP}} = +1.0\%\) before delisting for bankruptcy (DLSTCD 500) with a missing delisting return. Applying Shumway's \(-30\%\) replacement penalty, what is the compounded total monthly return \(R_{\text{total}} = (1 + R_{\text{CRSP}})(1 + R_{\text{delist}}) - 1\)?

    \(R_{\text{total}} = (1 + 0.01) \times (1 - 0.30) - 1 = 1.01 \times 0.70 - 1 = 0.707 - 1 = -0.293\) (\(-29.3\%\)).

  8. Skill check (Rung 2 · Project): In a univariate value-weighted sort, a quintile portfolio contains two stocks: Stock A with market cap \(\$100\text{M}\) and return \(+5.0\%\), and Stock B with market cap \(\$300\text{M}\) and return \(-0.5\%\). What is the value-weighted portfolio return in percent?

    Total cap = \(\$400\text{M}\). Weight of A = \(100/400 = 0.25\); Weight of B = \(300/400 = 0.75\). Portfolio return = \(0.25 \times 5.0\% + 0.75 \times (-0.5\%) = 1.25\% - 0.375\% = 0.875\%\) (or \(0.875\)).

Project ladder

The module culminates in the Atlas A7: Cross-Sectional Engine project (18 hours), where you construct a fully typed, autograded cross-sectional asset pricing engine evaluating portfolio sorts, delisting corrections, Fama–MacBeth regressions with Shanken corrections, and the GRS test across 50 seeded instances.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Replay an end-to-end cross-sectional analysis on a simulated panel (seed 42), tracing Shumway delisting corrections, NYSE breakpoint sorting, Fama–MacBeth risk premia with Shanken standard errors, and the GRS test.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Complete the core algorithms for Shumway delisting return replacement, NYSE breakpoint calculation, value-weighted portfolio aggregation, and Shanken errors-in-variables corrections.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Code the complete cross-sectional engine atlas.cross_section passing the 50-seed autograder suite and evaluate sorting specifications across criteria C1–C5.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Extend the engine to high-dimensional Fama–MacBeth cross-sections with shrinkage covariance estimators or multi-way conditional sort grids.
