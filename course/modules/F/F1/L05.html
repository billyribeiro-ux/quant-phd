NYSE Breakpoints & The Microcap Problem

In the US equity cross-section, exchange listing standards create an extreme disparity in company size. The New York Stock Exchange hosts established large-cap enterprises, while NASDAQ and AMEX list thousands of highly speculative microcaps. If sorting breakpoints are calculated across all listed firms, tiny NASDAQ microcaps dominate the bottom quantile bins, creating an illusion of robust anomaly returns that disappear once real-world trading costs are applied. Establishing breakpoints exclusively on NYSE securities and applying those thresholds across all exchanges is the foundational convention pioneered by Fama and French to insulate factor models from the microcap problem and ensure institutional investability.

1The Cross-Sectional Exchange Distribution Disparity

A primary structural reality of the US public equity market is that the distribution of firm capitalization is profoundly non-uniform across trading venues. The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE, exchange code 1) enforces stringent initial listing standards regarding pre-tax earnings, publicly held shares, and aggregate market capitalization.

In contrast, the American Stock Exchange (AMEX, exchange code 2) and the NASDAQ Stock Market (NASDAQ, exchange code 3) historically admitted early-stage, unprofitable, and speculative growth firms with small market capitalizations. Consequently, NASDAQ contains over 50% to 65% of all publicly listed corporate entities in the United States, but represents less than 20% to 25% of total market capitalization.

Definition 5.1 · The Microcap Problem
The microcap problem refers to the empirical vulnerability wherein cross-sectional anomalies and factor return spreads are driven predominantly or entirely by stocks below the 20th percentile of NYSE market capitalization. Microcaps represent roughly 60% of all public equity names but account for only ~3% of total market capitalization, suffering from extreme bid-ask spreads, severe market impact, and prohibitive short-borrow fees.

As documented extensively in the empirical asset pricing literature (Novy-Marx & Velikov 2015; Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020), many anomalies documented in academic journals cannot be traded profitably by institutional asset managers because their paper alpha is concentrated in illiquid microcaps.

2NYSE Breakpoints: Definition and Allocation Mechanics

To prevent the sheer count of NASDAQ microcaps from distorting cross-sectional sorting thresholds, Kenneth French and Eugene Fama established the foundational convention of calculating sorting breakpoints using NYSE-listed securities only (French Data Library; Fama & French 1993).

Definition 5.2 · NYSE Breakpoints
NYSE breakpoints are cross-sectional quantile threshold values \(c_1 < c_2 < \dots < c_{K-1}\) computed exclusively on the sub-sample of eligible common stocks listed on the New York Stock Exchange (HEXCD = 1): \[\begin{aligned} c_k &= \operatorname{Quantile}\left(\{X_{i,t} : i \in \text{NYSE}\}, \frac{k}{K}\right), \quad k = 1, \dots, K-1. \end{aligned}\] Once established, these exact thresholds are applied to partition all eligible securities from NYSE, AMEX, and NASDAQ into the \(K\) portfolios.

Theorem 5.1 · Microcap Breakpoint Distortion Theorem
Let the market be partitioned into NYSE securities (\(N_1\) firms with characteristic distribution \(F_1(x)\)) and NASDAQ securities (\(N_2\) firms with distribution \(F_2(x)\)), where \(N_2 > N_1\). If characteristic \(X\) represents market equity and \(F_2\) is first-order stochastically dominated by \(F_1\) (\(F_2(x) \ge F_1(x)\) for all \(x\)), then all-stock quantile breakpoints \(c_k^{\text{all}}\) are strictly lower than NYSE breakpoints \(c_k^{\text{NYSE}}\): \[\begin{aligned} c_k^{\text{all}} &< c_k^{\text{NYSE}}, \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, K-1\}. \end{aligned}\]

Proof
The aggregate market CDF is a convex mixture: \[\begin{aligned} F_{\text{all}}(x) &= \frac{N_1}{N_1 + N_2} F_1(x) + \frac{N_2}{N_1 + N_2} F_2(x). \end{aligned}\] By stochastic dominance, \(F_2(x) \ge F_1(x)\) with strict inequality over the interior of the support. Therefore: \[\begin{aligned} F_{\text{all}}(x) &> F_1(x), \quad \forall x \in (x_{\min}, x_{\max}). \end{aligned}\] Let \(p \in (0, 1)\). By definition, \(F_{\text{all}}(c^{\text{all}}) = p = F_1(c^{\text{NYSE}})\). Evaluating \(F_{\text{all}}\) at \(c^{\text{NYSE}}\) yields: \[\begin{aligned} F_{\text{all}}(c^{\text{NYSE}}) &> F_1(c^{\text{NYSE}}) = p = F_{\text{all}}(c^{\text{all}}). \end{aligned}\] Because the cumulative distribution function \(F_{\text{all}}\) is strictly monotonically increasing, \(c^{\text{all}} < c^{\text{NYSE}}\), establishing the theorem.

Theorem 5.1 proves why all-stock breakpoints fail: by depressing the bottom breakpoint threshold \(c_1\), all-stock sorts push the bottom portfolio deep into the microcap swamp, creating portfolios where over 80% to 90% of names are illiquid NASDAQ penny stocks.

Lab 1 · NYSE Breakpoints & Microcap Problem Simulator

Simulating an empirical exchange cross-section will confirm that all-stock sorting compresses the size breakpoint to $35M, concentrating NASDAQ stocks in Q1, whereas NYSE breakpoints establish a robust $1,470M threshold that allocates investable capital across bins.

"""Lab F1-L05-1: NYSE Breakpoints & The Microcap Problem.

Demonstrates that calculating breakpoints on all exchange-listed stocks
causes NASDAQ microcaps to dominate bottom bins, while NYSE breakpoints
preserve cross-sectional stability and prevent microcap contamination.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def simulate_nyse_breakpoints(seed: int = 88) -> dict[str, float | int]:
    """Compare all-stock breakpoints versus NYSE breakpoints across exchanges."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)

    # 1. Simulate cross-section: 100 NYSE firms (large) and 200 NASDAQ firms (skewed microcaps)
    n_nyse = 100
    n_nasdaq = 200
    n_total = n_nyse + n_nasdaq

    nyse_caps = rng.lognormal(mean=8.0, sigma=1.2, size=n_nyse)  # Large cap median ~$3000M
    nasdaq_caps = rng.lognormal(mean=4.2, sigma=1.1, size=n_nasdaq)  # Micro/small cap median ~$65M

    all_caps = np.concatenate([nyse_caps, nasdaq_caps])
    ex_codes = np.concatenate([np.ones(n_nyse, dtype=np.int32), np.full(n_nasdaq, 3, dtype=np.int32)])

    total_market_cap = float(np.sum(all_caps))
    microcap_threshold = float(np.percentile(nyse_caps, 20.0))  # 20th percentile of NYSE ME

    # Aggregate share of microcaps
    microcap_mask = all_caps <= microcap_threshold
    microcap_firm_share = float(np.mean(microcap_mask) * 100.0)
    microcap_cap_share = float((np.sum(all_caps[microcap_mask]) / total_market_cap) * 100.0)

    # 2. Quintile sorting on size using all-stock breakpoints vs NYSE breakpoints
    cuts_all = np.percentile(all_caps, [20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0])
    cuts_nyse = np.percentile(nyse_caps, [20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0])

    q_all = np.digitize(all_caps, cuts_all)
    q_nyse = np.digitize(all_caps, cuts_nyse)

    # Count NASDAQ stocks in the bottom quintile (Q1) under both schemes
    nasdaq_in_q1_all = int(np.sum((q_all == 0) & (ex_codes == 3)))
    nasdaq_in_q1_nyse = int(np.sum((q_nyse == 0) & (ex_codes == 3)))

    total_in_q1_all = int(np.sum(q_all == 0))
    total_in_q1_nyse = int(np.sum(q_nyse == 0))

    pct_nasdaq_q1_all = float(nasdaq_in_q1_all / total_in_q1_all * 100.0)
    pct_nasdaq_q1_nyse = float(nasdaq_in_q1_nyse / total_in_q1_nyse * 100.0)

    return {
        "n_total": n_total,
        "microcap_firm_share_pct": round(microcap_firm_share, 1),
        "microcap_cap_share_pct": round(microcap_cap_share, 2),
        "cut1_all_m": round(float(cuts_all[0]), 1),
        "cut1_nyse_m": round(float(cuts_nyse[0]), 1),
        "pct_nasdaq_q1_all": round(pct_nasdaq_q1_all, 1),
        "pct_nasdaq_q1_nyse": round(pct_nasdaq_q1_nyse, 1),
        "total_in_q1_nyse": total_in_q1_nyse,
    }


def main() -> None:
    print("Lab F1-L05-1 | NYSE Breakpoints & Microcap Problem Simulator")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------")
    res = simulate_nyse_breakpoints(seed=88)
    print(f"Total listed universe:                  {res['n_total']} firms")
    print(f"Microcaps (<20th pct NYSE) share of firms: {res['microcap_firm_share_pct']:.1f}%")
    print(f"Microcaps share of total market cap:    {res['microcap_cap_share_pct']:.2f}%")
    print(f"Size Q1 breakpoint (All Stocks):        ${res['cut1_all_m']:.1f}M")
    print(f"Size Q1 breakpoint (NYSE Only):         ${res['cut1_nyse_m']:.1f}M")
    print(f"NASDAQ concentration in Q1 (All Cuts):  {res['pct_nasdaq_q1_all']:.1f}%")
    print(f"NASDAQ concentration in Q1 (NYSE Cuts): {res['pct_nasdaq_q1_nyse']:.1f}%")
    print(f"Total firms allocated to Q1 (NYSE):     {res['total_in_q1_nyse']} firms")


main()

3Anomaly Attenuation Net of Trading Costs

The practical consequence of the microcap problem is that published anomaly returns attenuate dramatically once real-world execution frictions are applied. As proved by Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015), trading costs can be decomposed into:

  • Effective Half-Spread: The cost of crossing the bid-ask spread on market orders. For microcaps, effective half-spreads often exceed 1.5% to 3.0% (300 to 600 bps round-trip), compared to less than 0.05% (10 bps) for large-cap NYSE equities.
  • Price Impact: Slicing large institutional parent orders through illiquid order books moves the market against the trader.
  • Short-Borrow Fees: The short leg of anomaly strategies requires borrowing shares. Microcaps are frequently on "hard-to-borrow" lists with annualized borrow fees exceeding 10% to 25%, completely extinguishing paper short alpha.

4Where the Canon Breaks: International Breakpoints and Delisting Disparities

While NYSE breakpoints provide an ideal anchor in the United States, the methodology cannot be applied directly to international markets (Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020). In European, Japanese, and emerging markets, there is often no single exchange that mirrors the NYSE large-cap concentration.

In global multi-country factor models (e.g. Fama & French 2012, 2017), researchers compute country-specific market equity percentiles or group small European exchanges into regional clusters to prevent cross-border regulatory listing disparities from distorting breakpoints.

5Misconceptions

  1. "Using NYSE breakpoints means that non-NYSE stocks are excluded from the test portfolios."

    NYSE breakpoints are used exclusively to determine the threshold cutoffs \(c_k\). Once the cutoffs are computed on NYSE stocks, all eligible stocks from NYSE, AMEX, and NASDAQ are sorted into the resulting portfolios according to those cutoffs.

  2. "Equal-weighted factor portfolios are more democratic and therefore better represent the market."

    Equal-weighting allocates equal capital to a $20M illiquid microcap and a $2T mega-cap. Because microcaps represent 60% of names, equal-weighted portfolios are effectively 60% invested in microcaps, generating returns that cannot be harvested by real-world capital.

  3. "If an anomaly is statistically significant with a t-statistic over 4.0 in equal-weighted sorts, it must be an investable trading strategy."

    Anomalies with extreme t-statistics in equal-weighted sorts frequently collapse to zero or become negative net of transaction costs once value-weighted and purged of microcaps, as shown by Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015).

6Problem Set

Mechanics

An empirical sample has 100 NYSE stocks with median market capitalization of \(\$2,500\text{M}\) and 300 NASDAQ stocks with median market capitalization of \(\$80\text{M}\). What is the median size breakpoint under: (a) NYSE breakpoints; (b) all-stock breakpoints?

Solution

(a) Under NYSE breakpoints, the median is computed on the 100 NYSE stocks alone: \(\text{Breakpoint} = \$2,500\text{M}\). (b) Under all-stock breakpoints, the median is the 200th stock of the 400 sorted stocks. Because there are 300 NASDAQ stocks with low valuations, the 200th stock is a NASDAQ firm, resulting in a median breakpoint near \(\$80\text{M}\) to \(\$150\text{M}\), vastly lower than NYSE.

A microcap stock has market capitalization \(\$25\text{M}\), quoted bid price \(\$4.80\), and ask price \(\$5.20\). What is the quoted percentage half-spread, and what is the round-trip transaction cost percentage?

Solution

Midpoint: \(P_{\text{mid}} = \frac{4.80 + 5.20}{2} = \$5.00\). Quoted half-spread: \(\frac{5.20 - 5.00}{5.00} = \frac{0.20}{5.00} = 4.0\%\) (400 bps). Round-trip spread cost: \(\frac{5.20 - 4.80}{5.00} = \frac{0.40}{5.00} = 8.0\%\) (800 bps).

The 20th percentile of NYSE market capitalization is \(\$650\text{M}\). If a universe has 3,000 listed firms of which 1,800 have market capitalization below \(\$650\text{M}\), what percentage of firms are classified as microcaps?

Solution

Under the standard Fama-French size taxonomy, microcaps are defined as all firms with market equity below the 20th NYSE percentile (\(\text{ME} < \text{ME}_{\text{NYSE}, 20} = \$650\text{M}\)). Given that 1,800 out of 3,000 firms fall below this cutoff, the percentage of microcaps in the universe is \(\frac{1,800}{3,000} \times 100\% = 60.0\%\). This illustrates that microcaps constitute an outright numerical majority of listed firms despite representing a tiny fraction of total market wealth.

Proof

Prove that under value-weighting, the weight allocated to microcaps asymptotically approaches zero as aggregate market capitalization expands.

Solution

Let total market capitalization be \(M_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i \in \text{Large}} ME_i + \sum_{j \in \text{Micro}} ME_j\). The total market equity of microcaps is bounded by \(N_{\text{micro}} \times c_{0.20}^{\text{NYSE}}\). As the large-cap sector compounds, \(\sum_{i \in \text{Large}} ME_i \to \infty\), while microcap capitalization remains bounded by exchange graduation rules. Therefore, \(w_{\text{micro}} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \text{Micro}} ME_j}{M_{\text{total}}} \le \frac{N_{\text{micro}} c_{0.20}^{\text{NYSE}}}{M_{\text{total}}} \to 0\).

Prove that in an equal-weighted portfolio of \(N\) assets where \(N_{\text{micro}} = 0.60 N\), the microcap sector receives exactly 60% of total portfolio capital regardless of market capitalization.

Solution

Under equal weighting, every asset receives weight \(w_i = \frac{1}{N}\). The aggregate weight assigned to the microcap partition is \(W_{\text{micro}} = \sum_{j \in \text{Micro}} \frac{1}{N} = N_{\text{micro}} \times \frac{1}{N} = \frac{0.60 N}{N} = 0.60\) (60.0%), regardless of whether microcaps represent 3% or 0.01% of market wealth.

Prove that any anomaly whose return spread is strictly positive in equal-weighted sorts but non-positive in value-weighted sorts is driven by microcaps.

Solution

Decompose the portfolio return into large-cap and microcap components: \(R_{EW} = 0.40 R_{\text{large}} + 0.60 R_{\text{micro}}\) and \(R_{VW} \approx 0.97 R_{\text{large}} + 0.03 R_{\text{micro}}\). For the spread: \(\Delta R_{VW} \le 0 \implies R_{\text{large}}^{\text{spread}} \le 0\). For \(\Delta R_{EW} > 0\), we must have \(0.40 R_{\text{large}}^{\text{spread}} + 0.60 R_{\text{micro}}^{\text{spread}} > 0\). Since \(R_{\text{large}}^{\text{spread}} \le 0\), it strictly requires \(R_{\text{micro}}^{\text{spread}} > -\frac{0.40}{0.60} R_{\text{large}}^{\text{spread}} \ge 0\). Hence, the anomaly spread exists exclusively within the microcap segment.

Empirical

In Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015), what percentage of published cross-sectional anomalies fail to generate significant alpha after accounting for effective transaction costs?

Solution

Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015, Table 3) demonstrated that over two-thirds of published anomalies do not survive transaction costs, with the failure rate exceeding 80% among anomalies that trade frequently or rely on equal-weighted microcap sorts.

Why do microcaps exhibit significant short-sale borrowing constraints compared to large-cap equities?

Solution

Microcaps are primarily held by retail investors who do not participate in institutional securities lending programs. Institutional custody lendable supply is scarce, resulting in "special" status with high borrowing fees and high recall risk.

How does the SEC's tick size pilot program (2016-2018), which widened quoting increments to $0.05 for small-caps, illustrate microcap market quality frictions?

Solution

Widening the tick size increased effective bid-ask spreads and round-trip trading costs for small and microcap firms, reducing trading volume and increasing market impact, further degrading the net-of-cost performance of high-turnover anomaly strategies.

Research

Design an institutional investability screen that preserves cross-sectional factor validity while eliminating microcap distortion.

Solution

An institutional screen filters out all stocks with price below $5.00 (eliminating penny-stock tick distortions) and market capitalization below the 20th percentile of NYSE equity. The remaining universe is sorted using NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted, ensuring all factor portfolios can absorb at least $100M in assets without exceeding 5% of daily volume.

Evaluate the econometric implications of running Fama-MacBeth regressions weighted by market capitalization (WLS) versus OLS.

Solution

Standard OLS Fama-MacBeth treats every stock equally, allowing microcaps to dominate slope estimates. WLS Fama-MacBeth weights each observation by its square-root market equity, downweighting microcaps and aligning cross-sectional regression slopes with investable value-weighted portfolio returns.

How does the emergence of commission-free retail trading platforms impact modern microcap liquidity and price discovery?

Solution

Commission-free brokerages routing via payment for order flow (PFOF) increased retail trading in microcaps. While bid-ask spreads narrowed slightly during periods of retail exuberance, order flow became heavily directional and sentiment-driven, increasing idiosyncratic volatility and short-squeeze risks.

8Retrieval Quiz

  1. Why do Fama and French calculate portfolio sorting breakpoints using NYSE stocks only?

    Options

    NYSE breakpoints prevent the large number of NASDAQ microcaps from skewing the distribution, ensuring that quantile buckets are anchored to economically substantial firms.

  2. What defines the microcap segment in empirical asset pricing?

    Options

    The standard definition establishes microcaps as securities below the 20th percentile of NYSE market equity.

  3. Approximately what percentage of aggregate US equity market capitalization do microcaps represent, despite accounting for ~60% of all public firms?

    Microcaps represent approximately 3.0% of total market capitalization (Fama & French 2008).

  4. What happens to non-NYSE stocks when NYSE breakpoints are used in portfolio sorting?

    Options

    NYSE breakpoints establish the cutoff values; all eligible stocks from all exchanges are then sorted according to those cutoffs.

  5. According to Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015), what primary factor causes equal-weighted anomaly spreads to collapse?

    Options

    Equal-weighted anomaly strategies trade heavily in illiquid microcaps where round-trip transaction costs wipe out paper profits.

  6. In an equal-weighted portfolio containing 1,000 stocks where 600 stocks are microcaps, what percentage of portfolio capital is allocated to microcaps?

    Capital share = \(\frac{600}{1000} \times 100\% = 60.0\%\).

  7. Why is shorting microcaps in real-world portfolios often practically impossible?

    Options

    Microcaps have scarce institutional custody lendable supply, making them hard to borrow with borrow fees often exceeding 10% to 25% p.a.

  8. What does Theorem 5.1 prove about all-stock quantile breakpoints compared to NYSE breakpoints?

    Options

    Because the NASDAQ size distribution is dominated by smaller firms, the mixture distribution shifts all-stock cutoffs strictly downward.
