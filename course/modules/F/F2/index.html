The Classic Factors

Decades of empirical asset pricing research have demonstrated that the single-factor Capital Asset Pricing Model fails to explain the cross-section of expected equity returns. In its place stands a rich canon of systematic risk and behavioral factors: size, value, momentum, operating profitability, investment, betting against beta, and quality. Across six rigorous doctoral lessons, we dissect the economic theories, portfolio formation mechanics, spanning regressions, and finite-sample econometrics governing factor models. The module culminates in a comprehensive empirical horse race comparing CAPM, the Fama–French 3-Factor, Carhart 4-Factor, Fama–French 5-Factor, and Hou–Xue–Zhang \(q\)-factor models using the Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (GRS) exact test, the Barillas–Shanken maximum squared Sharpe ratio metric, and out-of-sample predictive cross-validation.

  • Prerequisites: F1 Data & Methodology (CRSP/Compustat merging, portfolio sorts, NYSE breakpoints, weighting schemes).
  • Unlocks: Part F Advanced Factor Studios and Quantitative Macro Factor Research.
  • Project: Factor-Model Horse Race: authoring horse_race.py to execute time-series regressions, compute exact finite-sample GRS \(F\)-statistics, evaluate Barillas–Shanken maximum squared Sharpe ratios, and measure out-of-sample RMSPE across 25 size-value test portfolios.
  • Data: Deterministic simulated panels reproducing empirical cross-sectional distributions, factor correlations, panic momentum crashes, and firm fundamentals.

66 h lessons · 24 h project (27%)

  2. Formulate cross-sectional momentum, explain the necessity of skipping month \(t-1\), and model the option-like leverage expansion driving momentum crashes during panic rebounds (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the dividend discount valuation identity linking discount rates to the profitability factor and investment factor, and contrast Novy-Marx gross profitability with Fama–French operating profitability (Lesson 3).
  4. Analyze the borrowing-constrained flat security market line, construct the betting-against-beta factor, examine the idiosyncratic-volatility puzzle, and formulate multi-dimensional quality-minus-junk scores (Lesson 4).
  5. Specify the Fama–French three-factor model, Carhart four-factor model, Fama–French five-factor model, and q-factor model, executing spanning regressions to identify factor redundancy (Lesson 5).
  6. Prove the Barillas–Shanken theorem on comparing factor models by maximum squared Sharpe ratio, relate the GRS statistic to Sharpe ratio expansion, and navigate multiple testing hurdles (Lesson 6).
  7. Author the complete autograded factor-model horse race suite, passing 50 randomized test seeds and defending benchmark choices against adversarial committee review (Project).

Prerequisites

F2 directly integrates econometric foundations, asset pricing theory, and cross-sectional data engineering: From CRSP & Compustat structure, portfolio sorts, NYSE breakpoints, the microcap problem, and weighting schemes in F1, F2 constructs investable long-short anomaly portfolios. From CAPM, beta, Jensen's alpha, linear factor models, and arbitrage pricing theory in AP2, F2 derives equilibrium multi-factor representations. From the OLS estimator and errors-in-variables in S2, F2 executes time-series regressions and establishes finite-sample residual properties. From the GRS test, Fama–MacBeth regressions, and the Shanken correction in S5, F2 implements exact multivariate pricing error diagnostics.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six testing prerequisite concepts from F1, AP2, S2, and S5, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In cross-sectional asset pricing (F1), why must sorting breakpoints for size and book-to-market be derived exclusively from NYSE securities rather than the combined NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ universe?

    Options

    NYSE breakpoints prevent microcaps (which represent the vast majority of stock count but less than 3% of market equity) from dominating the sort breakpoints and driving factor returns.

  2. Under the Sharpe–Lintner CAPM (AP2), what does a statistically significant positive Jensen's alpha (\(\alpha_i > 0\)) imply?

    Options

    A non-zero alpha indicates that the benchmark factor fails to span the asset's expected return, violating the mean-variance efficiency of the benchmark.

  3. In time-series linear regressions (S2), why does the unbiased estimator of the residual covariance matrix \(\hat{\Sigma}_\epsilon\) divide by \(T - K - 1\) rather than \(T\)?

    Options

    Estimating \(K\) slope coefficients plus one constant intercept consumes \(K+1\) degrees of freedom, requiring division by \(T - K - 1\) to guarantee unbiased finite-sample covariance estimation.

  4. What null hypothesis does the Gibbons, Ross & Shanken (1989) GRS test evaluate across \(N\) test assets (S5)?

    Options

    The GRS test is an exact finite-sample \(F\)-test of the joint hypothesis that all \(N\) alphas in a multivariate factor regression are simultaneously equal to zero.

  5. In the Fama–French (1993) portfolio formation convention (F1), what lag is enforced between corporate fiscal year-end and portfolio formation?

    Options

    The June formation rule ensures all corporate annual 10-K statements have been published, eliminating lookahead bias.

  6. According to Classical Errors-in-Variables theory (S2), how does measurement error in factor betas affect two-pass cross-sectional risk premia estimates?

    Options

    Measurement noise in first-pass betas biases second-pass slope estimates toward zero, which motivates the Shanken (1992) correction.

  7. Skill check 1: In the Gibbons–Ross–Shanken (1989) test statistic \(F = \frac{T - N - K}{N} \frac{1}{1 + Sh^2(f)} \hat{\alpha}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_\epsilon^{-1} \hat{\alpha}\), why does the factor quadratic term enter as \(\frac{1}{1 + Sh^2(f)}\)?

    Options

    The term \((1 + \bar{f}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_f^{-1} \bar{f})^{-1} = (1 + Sh^2(f))^{-1}\) accounts for the finite-sample variability of the sample factor mean vector.

  8. Skill check 2: A factor model has monthly maximum squared Sharpe ratio \(Sh^2(f) = 0.0400\). What is its annualized maximum squared Sharpe ratio?

    Annualized squared Sharpe ratio is \(Sh^2_{ann} = Sh^2_{mth} \times 12 = 0.0400 \times 12 = 0.4800\).

Lesson Map

  1. Lesson 01 · Size & Value: Economic Rationale & Portfolio Construction

    11 study hours

    Banz (1981) size effect, Berk (1995) equilibrium discount rate mirror, Fama–French (1993) \(2 \times 3\) SMB and HML sorts, and the distress risk vs. overreaction debate.

    Introduces: size effect, value premium (HML).

  2. Lesson 02 · Momentum & Reversal: Horizons, Crashes & Behavioral Dynamics

    11 study hours

    Short-term reversal vs. intermediate cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993), 12-2 formation skipping month \(t-1\), Carhart (1997) WML, and Daniel & Moskowitz (2016) crash mechanics.

    Introduces: cross-sectional momentum.

  3. Lesson 03 · Investment & Profitability: Production-Based Asset Pricing

    11 study hours

    The valuation equation discount rate link, Novy-Marx (2013) gross profitability, Fama–French (2015) operating profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA), and negative factor correlations.

    Introduces: profitability factor, investment factor.

  4. Lesson 04 · Low-Risk Anomalies, IVOL & Quality: Leverage & Preferences

    11 study hours

    Black (1972) borrowing constraints and flat SML, Frazzini & Pedersen (2014) Betting Against Beta (BAB), Ang et al. (2006) idiosyncratic volatility puzzle, and Asness et al. (2019) Quality Minus Junk (QMJ).

    Introduces: low-risk anomaly and betting against beta, idiosyncratic-volatility puzzle, quality minus junk.

  5. Lesson 05 · Multi-Factor Models & Spanning Regressions

    11 study hours

    Evolution of multi-factor models: FF3, Carhart 4, FF5, and Hou–Xue–Zhang \(q\)-factor; econometric theory of spanning regressions and the empirical redundancy of HML.

    Introduces: Fama–French three-factor model, Carhart four-factor model, Fama–French five-factor model, q-factor model, spanning regression.

  6. Lesson 06 · Model Comparison, Horse Races & The Tangency Frontier

    11 study hours

    Barillas & Shanken (2017) test-asset invariance theorem, maximum squared Sharpe ratio metric, GRS test geometry, Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) multiple testing hurdles, and factor zoo discipline.

    Introduces: comparing factor models by maximum squared Sharpe ratio.

Project ladder

The F2 capstone project is the Factor-Model Horse Race (24 project hours). You will implement the complete benchmark comparison suite evaluating CAPM, FF3, Carhart 4, FF5, and \(q\)-factor models across 25 size-value portfolios under both in-sample exact GRS testing and out-of-sample predictive cross-validation.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Run the reference comparison harness on simulated 30-year factor returns, tracing model estimation, residual covariance matrix inversion, GRS \(F\)-statistic scaling, and Barillas–Shanken Sharpe metrics.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Fill in the missing algebraic steps in starter.py: implement exact GRS finite-sample scaling and the Barillas–Shanken maximum squared Sharpe ratio metric.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent implementation: Author the complete horse_race.py module implementing OLS factor time-series regressions, GRS test statistics with \(p\)-values, Barillas–Shanken tangency Sharpe metrics, and out-of-sample expanding-window RMSPE evaluation across 50 autograder seeds.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Formulate an augmented 6-factor model uniting FF5 with momentum or QMJ, and derive the exact finite-sample Bayesian posterior probability of model dominance under diffuse priors.
