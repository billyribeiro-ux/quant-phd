Multi-Factor Models & Spanning Regressions

Modern quantitative asset pricing has evolved through four canonical benchmark architectures: the Fama–French 3-Factor, Carhart 4-Factor, Fama–French 5-Factor, and Hou–Xue–Zhang \(q\)-factor models. Disentangling which factors reflect fundamental sources of risk and which are redundant statistical duplicates requires the rigorous econometric machinery of spanning regressions. Mastering spanning regressions reveals why the classic value premium is absorbed by profitability and investment in the 5-factor model, proves that momentum remains unspanned by corporate fundamentals, and establishes the formal frontier of traded factors.

1Evolution of Canonical Multi-Factor Models

Empirical asset pricing transitioned from the single-factor CAPM to multi-factor linear pricing specifications designed to price cross-sections of equity portfolios.

Definition 5.1 · Fama–French Three-Factor Model
The Fama–French three-factor model (Fama & French 1993) models the excess return of asset \(i\) as: \[\begin{aligned} R_{i,t} - R_{f,t} &= \alpha_i + \beta_{i,M} (R_{M,t} - R_{f,t}) + s_i SMB_t + h_i HML_t + \epsilon_{i,t}, \end{aligned}\] where \(R_{M,t} - R_{f,t}\) is the market excess return, \(SMB_t\) (Small Minus Big) captures the size effect, and \(HML_t\) (High Minus Low) captures the book-to-market value premium.

Definition 5.2 · Carhart Four-Factor Model
The Carhart four-factor model (Carhart 1997) augments the Fama–French three-factor model with the cross-sectional momentum factor \(WML_t\) (PR1YR): \[\begin{aligned} R_{i,t} - R_{f,t} &= \alpha_i + \beta_{i,M} (R_{M,t} - R_{f,t}) + s_i SMB_t + h_i HML_t + w_i WML_t + \epsilon_{i,t}. \end{aligned}\]

Definition 5.3 · Fama–French Five-Factor Model
The Fama–French five-factor model (Fama & French 2015) incorporates operating profitability and capital investment: \[\begin{aligned} R_{i,t} - R_{f,t} &= \alpha_i + \beta_{i,M} (R_{M,t} - R_{f,t}) + s_i SMB_t + h_i HML_t + r_i RMW_t + c_i CMA_t + \epsilon_{i,t}, \end{aligned}\] where \(RMW_t\) (Robust Minus Weak) is the operating profitability factor and \(CMA_t\) (Conservative Minus Aggressive) is the investment factor.

Definition 5.4 · q-Factor Model
The q-factor model (Hou, Xue & Zhang 2015) derives linear factor pricing from corporate production and \(q\)-theory: \[\begin{aligned} R_{i,t} - R_{f,t} &= \alpha_i + \beta_{i,M} (R_{M,t} - R_{f,t}) + \beta_{i,ME} R_{ME,t} + \beta_{i,I/A} R_{I/A,t} + \beta_{i,ROE} R_{ROE,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}, \end{aligned}\] where \(R_{ME}\) is the size factor (market equity), \(R_{I/A}\) is the investment-to-assets factor, and \(R_{ROE}\) is the quarterly return on equity profitability factor.

2Econometric Theory of Spanning Regressions

When multiple factor models compete, researchers must evaluate whether adding an additional factor provides new pricing information or merely repackages existing factors. The formal statistical method is the spanning regression.

Definition 5.5 · Spanning Regression
A spanning regression is an ordinary least squares time-series regression of a candidate factor \(f_k\) on a benchmark set of \(K-1\) existing factors \(f_{-k}\): \[\begin{aligned} f_{k,t} &= \alpha_k + \beta_k^\top f_{-k,t} + \epsilon_{k,t}, \quad t = 1, \dots, T. \end{aligned}\] Factor \(f_k\) is defined as redundant (or spanned) with respect to \(f_{-k}\) if and only if the spanning intercept is zero: \[\begin{aligned} H_0: \alpha_k = 0. \end{aligned}\]

Theorem 5.1 · Factor Redundancy and the Tangency Frontier
Let \(f = (f_{-k}^\top, f_k)^\top\) denote the augmented vector of \(K\) traded factors, with mean \(\mu_f\) and covariance matrix \(\Sigma_f\). Let \(Sh^2(f) = \mu_f^\top \Sigma_f^{-1} \mu_f\) denote the maximum squared Sharpe ratio achievable by any portfolio of \(f\). Then: \[\begin{aligned} Sh^2(f) &= Sh^2(f_{-k}) + \frac{\alpha_k^2}{\Var(\epsilon_k)}. \end{aligned}\] Consequently:

  1. Factor \(f_k\) strictly expands the factor mean-variance efficient frontier if and only if \(\alpha_k \neq 0\).
  2. If \(\alpha_k = 0\), factor \(f_k\) is completely redundant: \(Sh^2(f) = Sh^2(f_{-k})\), and any pricing model using \(f\) can be matched by \(f_{-k}\).

Proof
By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, we can re-orthogonalize factor \(f_k\) with respect to \(f_{-k}\). Define the orthogonalized factor \(\tilde{f}_k = f_k - \beta_k^\top f_{-k} = \alpha_k + \epsilon_k\). By OLS construction, \(\Cov(f_{-k}, \tilde{f}_k) = 0\). The transformed factor vector \(\tilde{f} = (f_{-k}^\top, \tilde{f}_k)^\top\) has block-diagonal covariance matrix: \[\begin{aligned} \Sigma_{\tilde{f}} &= \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma_{f_{-k}} & 0 \\ 0 & \Var(\epsilon_k) \end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned}\] The expected value is \(\mu_{\tilde{f}} = (\mu_{f_{-k}}^\top, \alpha_k)^\top\). Because a linear invertible transformation of assets preserves the maximum squared Sharpe ratio: \[\begin{aligned} Sh^2(f) &= Sh^2(\tilde{f}) = \mu_{\tilde{f}}^\top \Sigma_{\tilde{f}}^{-1} \mu_{\tilde{f}} \\ &= \mu_{f_{-k}}^\top \Sigma_{f_{-k}}^{-1} \mu_{f_{-k}} + \alpha_k (\Var(\epsilon_k))^{-1} \alpha_k \\ &= Sh^2(f_{-k}) + \frac{\alpha_k^2}{\Var(\epsilon_k)}. \end{aligned}\] Thus \(Sh^2(f) > Sh^2(f_{-k})\) if and only if \(\alpha_k \neq 0\).

3The Redundancy of HML and the Independence of Momentum

Applying spanning regressions to canonical US equity factors produces two fundamental empirical discoveries:

1. The Empirical Redundancy of HML in FF5

In their 2015 paper, Fama & French (2015) regressed \(HML\) on \(MKT, SMB, RMW,\) and \(CMA\):

\[\begin{aligned} HML_t &= \alpha_{HML} + \beta_M MKT_t + \beta_S SMB_t + \beta_R RMW_t + \beta_C CMA_t + \epsilon_t. \end{aligned}\]

They found that the intercept \(\alpha_{HML}\) was approximately zero (\(-0.06\%\) per month, \(t = -0.60\)), with an \(R^2\) exceeding \(0.55\). \(HML\) loads heavily positive on \(CMA\) (\(\beta_C \approx 0.9\)) and negative on \(RMW\) (\(\beta_R \approx -0.3\)). Value stocks are predominantly conservative-investment, low-profitability operations; once CMA and RMW are included in the model, HML provides no incremental pricing power.

2. The Unspanned Status of Momentum

In contrast, regressing the Carhart momentum factor \(WML\) on the Fama–French five factors yields an annualized alpha exceeding \(7\%\) (\(t > 3.5\)), proving that momentum is completely unspanned by fundamental accounting factors. The Carhart 4-factor model and Fama–French 5-factor model are mutually non-nested.

Lab 1 · Multi-Factor Model Spanning Regressions

Simulating 30 years of monthly multi-factor returns will verify that HML is statistically spanned by CMA and RMW in FF5 (|t| < 2.0), while momentum (WML) retains a statistically significant positive spanning alpha (t > 3.0).

"""Lab F2-L05-1: Multi-Factor Model Spanning Regressions and Factor Redundancy.

Executes spanning regressions across the Fama-French 3-Factor, Carhart 4-Factor,
Fama-French 5-Factor, and q-factor models. Tests the Fama-French (2015) finding
of HML redundancy in FF5 and tests whether momentum (WML) is spanned by FF5.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def run_spanning_regressions(seed: int = 42) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Simulate canonical multi-factor return series and run spanning tests."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n_months = 360  # 30 years

    # True factor return dynamics
    mkt = rng.normal(0.065 / 12, 0.155 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)
    smb = rng.normal(0.025 / 12, 0.100 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)
    rmw = rng.normal(0.038 / 12, 0.080 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)
    cma = rng.normal(0.032 / 12, 0.070 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)

    # In Fama-French (2015), HML is spanned by CMA and RMW:
    # HML_t = 0.08 * Mkt + 0.05 * SMB - 0.30 * RMW + 0.90 * CMA + e_t
    e_hml = rng.normal(0, 0.04 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)
    hml = (0.015 / 12) + 0.08 * mkt + 0.05 * smb - 0.30 * rmw + 0.90 * cma + e_hml

    # WML has strong independent alpha of 7.5% annualized relative to fundamentals
    wml_raw = rng.normal(0.075 / 12, 0.120 / np.sqrt(12), size=n_months)
    wml = wml_raw + (0.075 / 12 - float(np.mean(wml_raw)))  # preserve empirical mean

    # OLS Spanning Regression helper: Y on X (with intercept)
    def ols_spanning(y: np.ndarray, x_mat: np.ndarray) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
        t_obs = len(y)
        k_vars = x_mat.shape[1]
        x_design = np.column_stack([np.ones(t_obs), x_mat])
        beta = np.linalg.lstsq(x_design, y, rcond=None)[0]
        alpha = beta[0]
        resid = y - x_design @ beta
        s2 = np.sum(resid**2) / (t_obs - k_vars - 1)
        cov = s2 * np.linalg.inv(x_design.T @ x_design)
        se_alpha = np.sqrt(cov[0, 0])
        t_alpha = alpha / se_alpha
        r2 = 1.0 - np.sum(resid**2) / np.sum((y - np.mean(y)) ** 2)
        return float(alpha * 12 * 100), float(t_alpha), float(r2)

    # 1. Spanning HML on FF4 (MKT, SMB, RMW, CMA)
    x_ff4 = np.column_stack([mkt, smb, rmw, cma])
    alpha_hml, t_hml, r2_hml = ols_spanning(hml, x_ff4)

    # 2. Spanning WML on FF5 (MKT, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA)
    x_ff5 = np.column_stack([mkt, smb, hml, rmw, cma])
    alpha_wml, t_wml, r2_wml = ols_spanning(wml, x_ff5)

    return {
        "n_months": float(n_months),
        "alpha_hml_ann": round(alpha_hml, 2),
        "t_alpha_hml": round(t_hml, 2),
        "r2_hml": round(r2_hml, 4),
        "alpha_wml_ann": round(alpha_wml, 2),
        "t_alpha_wml": round(t_wml, 2),
        "r2_wml": round(r2_wml, 4),
    }


def main() -> None:
    print("Lab F2-L05-1 | Multi-Factor Spanning Regressions (Fama-French 2015)")
    print("------------------------------------------------------------------")
    res = run_spanning_regressions(seed=42)
    print(f"Sample length: {int(res['n_months'])} months (30 years)")
    print("Spanning HML on [MKT, SMB, RMW, CMA]:")
    print(f"  Annualized Alpha: {res['alpha_hml_ann']:+.2f}% (t = {res['t_alpha_hml']:.2f}, R2 = {res['r2_hml']:.4f})")
    print("  Verdict: HML is spanned and redundant in FF5 (|t| < 2.0)")
    print("Spanning Momentum (WML) on FF5 [MKT, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA]:")
    print(f"  Annualized Alpha: {res['alpha_wml_ann']:+.2f}% (t = {res['t_alpha_wml']:.2f}, R2 = {res['r2_wml']:.4f})")
    print("  Verdict: WML is NOT spanned by FF5 (t > 3.0; Carhart 4 is non-nested)")


main()

Spanning regressions demonstrate that HML is empirically spanned by RMW and CMA in the 5-factor model (|t| < 2.0), while Carhart momentum (WML) retains a large, statistically significant alpha (t > 3.0), establishing that momentum is unspanned by corporate fundamentals.

Where the Canon Breaks

While HML appears redundant in 1963–2013 US equity data, factor spanning is highly sensitive to sample horizons and geographic scope. First, in European, Japanese, and Emerging Market equities, spanning regressions reject HML redundancy: HML retains statistically significant positive alphas exceeding \(2.5\%\) annualized relative to RMW and CMA (Fama & French 2015). Second, during the post-2020 inflation surge and interest rate tightening cycle (2021–2024), traditional energy, materials, and value equities outperformed while high-profitability growth equities stalled, generating substantial positive unspanned HML alpha. Declaring a factor permanently redundant from a single country-sample risks sample-period overfitting.

Misconceptions

  1. A factor with a low standalone Sharpe ratio is economically worthless and should be excluded from asset pricing models.

    From Theorem 5.1, factor expansion depends on spanning alpha, not raw Sharpe ratio. A factor with a low or negative average return can substantially expand the tangency Sharpe ratio if it provides a valuable hedge against existing factors (negative covariance), creating a large positive spanning alpha.

  2. Because HML has zero alpha in FF5 spanning regressions, book-to-market value is a statistical illusion.

    HML's spanning redundancy means its return variation is spanned by the combination of investment (CMA) and profitability (RMW), confirming the valuation identity (\(M/B\) as a function of profitability and investment). Value exists fundamentally, but the 5-factor model captures it through its underlying production drivers.

  3. The Carhart four-factor model is nested within the Fama–French five-factor model.

    Carhart 4 includes momentum (WML) but excludes profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA). FF5 includes RMW and CMA but excludes WML. Neither model nests the other; they represent distinct factor dimensions.

Practice Problems

Tier 1 · Mechanics

In a spanning regression of factor \(f_k\) on benchmark factors \(f_{-k}\), the estimated intercept is \(\hat{\alpha} = 0.20\%\) per month with a standard error of \(0.08\%\). What is the \(t\)-statistic on factor alpha, and is factor \(f_k\) redundant at the 5% significance level?

Solution

The \(t\)-statistic is \(t = \frac{\hat{\alpha}}{\text{SE}(\hat{\alpha})} = \frac{0.20\%}{0.08\%} = 2.50\). Because \(|t| = 2.50 > 1.96\), we reject the null hypothesis \(H_0: \alpha = 0\) at the 5% level. Factor \(f_k\) is not redundant and expands the factor efficient frontier.

Suppose a 4-factor model has an annualized maximum squared Sharpe ratio of \(Sh^2(f_{-k}) = 0.40\). A fifth factor \(f_k\) is added, producing an annualized spanning alpha of \(\alpha_k = 3.0\%\) and residual annual volatility of \(\sigma_\epsilon = 10.0\%\). Compute the new maximum squared Sharpe ratio \(Sh^2(f)\).

Solution

From Theorem 5.1: \(Sh^2(f) = Sh^2(f_{-k}) + \frac{\alpha_k^2}{\sigma_\epsilon^2} = 0.40 + \frac{(0.03)^2}{(0.10)^2} = 0.40 + \frac{0.0009}{0.0100} = 0.40 + 0.09 = 0.49\). The maximum Sharpe ratio expands from \(\sqrt{0.40} \approx 0.632\) to \(\sqrt{0.49} = 0.700\).

State the five factors in the Fama–French (2015) model and their economic definitions.

Solution

The five factors are: (1) \(MKT\): market excess return over the risk-free rate; (2) \(SMB\): Small Minus Big market equity (size); (3) \(HML\): High Minus Low book-to-market equity (value); (4) \(RMW\): Robust Minus Weak operating profitability; and (5) \(CMA\): Conservative Minus Aggressive capital investment (asset growth).

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that in any spanning regression \(f_k = \alpha_k + \beta^\top f_{-k} + \epsilon\), the regression \(R^2\) can be expressed in terms of factor variances and covariance: \(R^2 = 1 - \frac{\Var(\epsilon)}{\Var(f_k)}\).

Solution

By OLS orthogonality, \(\Cov(\beta^\top f_{-k}, \epsilon) = 0\). Taking the sample variance of both sides: \(\Var(f_k) = \Var(\beta^\top f_{-k}) + \Var(\epsilon)\). Dividing both sides by \(\Var(f_k)\): \(1 = \frac{\Var(\beta^\top f_{-k})}{\Var(f_k)} + \frac{\Var(\epsilon)}{\Var(f_k)}\). By definition, \(R^2 \equiv \frac{\Var(\beta^\top f_{-k})}{\Var(f_k)} = 1 - \frac{\Var(\epsilon)}{\Var(f_k)}\).

Prove that if factor \(f_k\) is uncorrelated with all benchmark factors (\(\Cov(f_k, f_{-k}) = 0\)), its spanning alpha equals its unconditional mean \(\E[f_k]\) and its spanning slope coefficients \(\beta_k\) are identically zero.

Solution

The OLS slope vector is \(\beta_k = \Sigma_{f_{-k}}^{-1} \Cov(f_{-k}, f_k)\). When \(\Cov(f_{-k}, f_k) = 0\), \(\beta_k = \Sigma_{f_{-k}}^{-1} \mathbf{0} = \mathbf{0}\). The spanning intercept is \(\alpha_k = \E[f_k] - \beta_k^\top \E[f_{-k}] = \E[f_k] - 0 = \E[f_k]\). Thus, an uncorrelated factor is spanned if and only if its unconditional mean return is zero.

Prove that testing whether candidate factor \(f_k\) is redundant is econometrically identical to testing whether the tangency portfolio weights on \(f_k\) are zero.

Solution

The tangency portfolio weights on factors \(f = (f_{-k}, f_k)\) are \(w^* \propto \Sigma_f^{-1} \mu_f\). In the orthogonalized representation \(\tilde{f} = (f_{-k}, \tilde{f}_k)\), \(\Sigma_{\tilde{f}}\) is block diagonal. The weight on the orthogonalized component is \(\tilde{w}_k \propto \frac{\alpha_k}{\Var(\epsilon_k)}\). Because \(f_k = \tilde{f}_k + \beta_k^\top f_{-k}\), the weight on \(f_k\) in the original factor space is zero if and only if \(\tilde{w}_k = 0\), which occurs if and only if \(\alpha_k = 0\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Why does Fama & French's (2015) finding of HML redundancy fail in European and emerging equity markets?

Solution

Outside the US, corporate accounting reporting and corporate governance vary widely, weakening the clean mapping from accounting profitability and physical asset growth to discount rates. In international markets, high book-to-market stocks contain country-specific structural distress and banking leverage that are not spanned by physical capital investment and operating margins, leaving HML with statistically significant positive alpha.

In Hou, Xue & Zhang (2015), how does the quarterly ROE factor compare to the annual Fama–French RMW factor in capturing earnings surprises?

Solution

Hou, Xue & Zhang rebalance their ROE factor monthly using the most recently announced quarterly earnings (Compustat quarterly), capturing immediate earnings momentum and post-earnings-announcement drift. In contrast, Fama & French rebalance RMW once a year in June using trailing fiscal year accounting data, lagging quarterly operational shifts by up to 18 months.

Explain why regressing momentum (WML) on FF5 yields an insignificant beta on HML even though univariate value and momentum are negatively correlated.

Solution

In a multivariate regression on FF5, the other factors (RMW and CMA) absorb the underlying growth/investment components of value. Losers have low profitability and high investment; winners have high profitability and low investment. Once RMW, CMA, and Market are controlled for, the residual covariance between WML and HML shrinks to near zero.

Tier 4 · Research

Formulate the Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) multiple testing critique of newly proposed factors in empirical finance.

Solution

Harvey, Liu & Zhu document that hundreds of anomalous factors ('the factor zoo') have been published in top finance journals using the classical \(t > 2.0\) significance hurdle. Because researchers test thousands of candidate signals across samples (data mining), the true false discovery rate is massive. To maintain a false positive rate of 5% across hundreds of tests, the threshold \(t\)-statistic must be raised to at least \(t > 3.0\) (or Bonferroni / false discovery rate adjustments applied).

How does Barillas & Shanken (2017) demonstrate that factor model comparisons should be conducted using only traded factors rather than ad-hoc test assets?

Solution

Barillas & Shanken prove that when factor models consist of traded portfolios, comparing models on test assets (such as 25 Size-BM portfolios) introduces arbitrary test-asset dependence. The model that best prices test assets is mathematically identical to the model whose factors span the competing model's factors and generate the highest maximum squared Sharpe ratio.

Propose a unified 6-factor model that integrates the best dimensions of Fama–French 5, Carhart 4, and q-factor architectures.

Solution

A unified 6-factor model includes: (1) Market excess return; (2) Size (SMB); (3) High-frequency quarterly ROE (from HXZ); (4) Conservative investment (CMA or I/A); (5) Value (HML, preserved for international robustness); and (6) Momentum (WML or residual momentum). Spanning regressions show that every factor in this 6-factor model retains statistically significant alpha, delivering the highest Sharpe ratio across global markets.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What does a spanning regression test in empirical factor analysis?

    Options

    A spanning regression regresses a candidate factor on other factors; if the alpha is zero, the factor is redundant and does not expand the tangency Sharpe ratio.

  2. What did Fama & French (2015) find when regressing HML on MKT, SMB, RMW, and CMA in their 1963–2013 US sample?

    Options

    In FF5, HML's return is fully explained by its positive loading on CMA and negative loading on RMW, driving its spanning alpha to zero.

  3. What are the four factors in the Hou, Xue & Zhang (2015) q-factor model?

    Options

    The q-factor model consists of Market, Size (ME), Investment (I/A), and Profitability (ROE), motivated by neoclassical production theory.

  4. If a benchmark factor model has \(Sh^2(f_{-k}) = 0.40\) and a candidate factor delivers spanning alpha \(\alpha = 0.03\) with residual volatility \(\sigma_\epsilon = 0.10\), what is the augmented maximum squared Sharpe ratio \(Sh^2(f)\)?

    \(Sh^2(f) = 0.40 + \frac{0.03^2}{0.10^2} = 0.40 + \frac{0.0009}{0.0100} = 0.40 + 0.09 = 0.49\).

  5. Is the cross-sectional momentum factor (WML) spanned by the Fama–French five-factor model?

    Options

    Spanning regressions confirm that momentum is unspanned by FF5 fundamental factors; Carhart 4 and FF5 are mutually non-nested.

  6. Why does Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) argue that new factor discoveries require a t-statistic hurdle of t > 3.0?

    Options

    Because researchers have mined data testing hundreds of potential factors, the classical t > 2.0 hurdle produces false discoveries. Multiple testing corrections demand t > 3.0.

  7. In a spanning regression, estimated alpha is \(\hat{\alpha} = 0.25\%\) per month with standard error \(0.10\%\). What is the \(t\)-statistic on alpha?

    \(t = \frac{0.25}{0.10} = 2.50\).

  8. Under what condition does adding a candidate factor \(f_k\) strictly expand the factor tangency portfolio Sharpe ratio?

    Options

    Theorem 5.1 proves that the maximum squared Sharpe ratio expands by \(\alpha_k^2 / \Var(\epsilon_k)\), which is positive if and only if \(\alpha_k \neq 0\).
