The Pooled-Regression Critique

The econometric foundation of time-series momentum in Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) rested on pooled panel regressions of scaled future returns on past signs across 58 contracts, producing staggering \(t\)-statistics above 10. However, Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) uncovered a critical flaw: pooled OLS without fixed effects confounds cross-sectional dispersion in unconditional asset means with genuine serial autocorrelation. Once fixed effects are applied or regressions are estimated asset-by-asset, statistical significance evaporates. This lesson unpacks the econometric decomposition and stages a structured debate on whether TSMOM remains a genuine source of alpha.

1. The Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen (2012) Pooled Specification

In establishing time-series momentum, Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) estimated a pooled panel OLS regression across all \(N = 58\) futures contracts and all sample periods \(t\):

\[ \frac{r_{t+1}^i}{\sigma_t^i} = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{sign}\left(r_{t-k:t}^i\right) + \epsilon_{t+1}^i \]

where \(r_{t+1}^i\) is the excess return of instrument \(i\) at month \(t+1\), \(\sigma_t^i\) is the conditional trailing volatility estimate, and \(r_{t-k:t}^i\) is the cumulative return over lookback formation horizon \(k = 12\) months.

Under this pooled specification, Moskowitz et al. (2012) obtained \(\hat{\beta} = +0.015\) with a pooled \(t\)-statistic of \(10.51\) (with standard errors clustered by time). They interpreted this astronomical \(t\)-statistic as decisive proof that past 12-month returns universally forecast future returns across global futures markets.

A critical feature of the MOP specification is that it pooled observations across all instruments with a single common intercept \(\alpha\), omitting instrument-specific fixed effects or asset-level demeaning.

2. The Huang–Li–Wang–Zhou (2020) Decomposition

In a landmark econometric critique, Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) demonstrated that pooled panel regressions without fixed effects confound within-asset time-series autocorrelation with between-asset cross-sectional dispersion in unconditional expected returns.

Definition 5.1 · The TSMOM Pooled-Regression Critique

The pooled-regression critique of time-series momentum denotes the econometric finding by Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) that pooled panel OLS regressions of scaled future returns on past signs produce spuriously inflated slope estimates and massive statistical significance even when asset returns are strictly independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) over time with zero serial autocorrelation, whenever assets possess heterogeneous unconditional expected returns (\(\Var_i(\mu_i) > 0\)).

Theorem 5.1 · Between-Asset Mean Bias in Pooled Regressions

Suppose an economy consists of \(N\) assets whose monthly returns follow independent processes with asset-specific unconditional means \(\E[r_t^i] = \mu_i\) and identical variances \(\Var(r_t^i) = \sigma^2\). Assume returns are strictly serially independent: \(\Cov(r_t^i, r_{t+s}^i) = 0\) for all \(s \neq 0\) (true time-series momentum is identically zero). If unconditional means vary across assets with cross-sectional variance \(\sigma_\mu^2 = \Var_i(\mu_i) > 0\), the probability limit of the pooled OLS slope estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}}\) satisfies:

\[ \text{plim}_{N, T \to \infty} \hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}} = \frac{\Cov_i\left(\mu_i, \E[\text{sign}(r_{t-k:t}^i)]\right)}{\Var_{i,t}\left(\text{sign}(r_{t-k:t}^i)\right)} > 0 \]

In particular, \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}}\) is strictly positive, and standard OLS standard errors (shrinking at rate \(1/\sqrt{NT}\)) reject the null hypothesis of zero predictability with probability approaching 1.

Proof of Theorem 5.1

Let \(y_{t+1}^i = r_{t+1}^i / \sigma\) and \(x_t^i = \text{sign}(r_{t-k:t}^i)\). The pooled OLS slope is \(\hat{\beta} = \frac{\widehat{\Cov}(x_t^i, y_{t+1}^i)}{\widehat{\Var}(x_t^i)}\). By the law of total covariance, decomposing across asset entities \(i\) and time \(t\):

\[ \Cov(x_t^i, y_{t+1}^i) = \E_i\left[\Cov_t(x_t^i, y_{t+1}^i \mid i)\right] + \Cov_i\left(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i], \E_t[y_{t+1}^i \mid i]\right) \]

By the assumption of serial independence, \(\Cov_t(x_t^i, y_{t+1}^i \mid i) = 0\) for every individual asset \(i\). The within-asset term vanishes identically: \(\E_i[0] = 0\).

Now evaluate the between-asset covariance term. For asset \(i\), the unconditional expected future return is \(\E_t[y_{t+1}^i \mid i] = \mu_i / \sigma\). The expected past sign is \(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i] = \Prob(r_{t-k:t}^i > 0) - \Prob(r_{t-k:t}^i < 0) = 2 \Phi\left(\frac{k \mu_i}{\sigma \sqrt{k}}\right) - 1 = 2 \Phi\left(\frac{\mu_i \sqrt{k}}{\sigma}\right) - 1\). This is a strictly monotonically increasing function of \(\mu_i\). When \(\mu_i > 0\), \(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i] > 0\); when \(\mu_i < 0\), \(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i] < 0\). Consequently, the cross-sectional covariance between \(\E_t[x_t^i]\) and \(\mu_i\) is strictly positive whenever \(\Var_i(\mu_i) > 0\):

\[ \Cov_i\left(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i], \E_t[y_{t+1}^i \mid i]\right) = \frac{1}{\sigma}\Cov_i\left(2\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_i \sqrt{k}}{\sigma}\right) - 1, \mu_i\right) > 0 \]

Since \(\Var(x_t^i) > 0\), the ratio is strictly positive. Therefore, pooled OLS yields a positive slope even though no asset has any serial correlation.

Example 5.1 · Spurious Predictability in Two-Asset Economy

Consider two independent assets. Asset A (e.g. S&P 500) has annual mean return \(+10\%\) and volatility \(16\%\); Asset B (e.g. cash proxy) has mean return \(-2\%\) and volatility \(16\%\). Both assets have zero serial autocorrelation (i.i.d.). Because Asset A has a positive drift, its past 12-month return is positive \(75\%\) of the time, and its future monthly return is positive \(60\%\) of the time. Asset B's past 12-month return is negative \(60\%\) of the time, and its future return is negative \(55\%\) of the time. Pooling observations into one regression, the econometrician observes that whenever the past sign is positive (mostly Asset A), the future return is positive; whenever the past sign is negative (mostly Asset B), the future return is negative. Pooled OLS outputs \(\hat{\beta} > 0\) with \(t = 5.2\), declaring 'time-series momentum' when the true data generating process is entirely static drift.

3. Within Estimators and Asset-by-Asset Tests

To eliminate the between-asset unconditional mean bias, econometricians employ the standard panel fixed-effects (within) estimator, demeaning the dependent variable and regressor for each asset:

\[ \left(\frac{r_{t+1}^i}{\sigma_t^i} - \bar{y}_i\right) = \beta_{\text{within}} \left(\text{sign}\left(r_{t-k:t}^i\right) - \bar{x}_i\right) + u_{t+1}^i \]

where \(\bar{y}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \frac{r_{t+1}^i}{\sigma_t^i}\) and \(\bar{x}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \text{sign}\left(r_{t-k:t}^i\right)\).

Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) re-estimated the Moskowitz et al. (2012) regressions using the within estimator. Across equity indices, bonds, and currencies, the estimated slope coefficient \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{within}}\) collapsed, and statistical significance fell below standard critical thresholds. Furthermore, when regressions were estimated asset-by-asset, only 4 out of 58 contracts (less than \(7\%\)) exhibited a statistically significant \(t\)-statistic at the 5% level—virtually indistinguishable from the expected 5% false discovery rate under the null hypothesis of no predictability.

Lab 1 · TSMOM Pooled Regression vs Huang et al. (2020) Critique

Simulating i.i.d. returns with heterogeneous unconditional means will produce a spuriously large positive pooled OLS t-statistic, while the within-asset fixed effects estimator will recover an insignificant slope.

"""Lab F3-L05-1: TSMOM Pooled Regression vs Within-Asset Econometric Decomposition.

Implements the Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) pooled regression and the Huang,
Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) critique. Simulates i.i.d. asset returns with cross-sectional
dispersion in unconditional means, demonstrating that pooled OLS produces a
spurious positive t-statistic, while the fixed-effects within estimator recovers zero.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def evaluate_tsmom_pooled_critique(seed: int = 42) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Demonstrate the between-asset bias in pooled TSMOM regressions."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n_assets = 50
    n_months = 360  # 30 years
    lookback = 12

    # 1. Simulate assets with heterogeneous unconditional means but STRICTLY ZERO serial correlation (iid)
    # Means vary across assets: N(0.04/12, 0.05/12)
    asset_means = rng.normal(0.04 / 12.0, 0.05 / 12.0, size=n_assets)
    asset_vols = rng.uniform(0.10 / np.sqrt(12.0), 0.25 / np.sqrt(12.0), size=n_assets)

    returns = np.zeros((n_months, n_assets))
    for i in range(n_assets):
        returns[:, i] = rng.normal(asset_means[i], asset_vols[i], size=n_months)

    # 2. Build pooled panel: dependent y = r_{t+1}^i / sigma_t^i, regressor x = sign(past 12m)
    y_list: list[float] = []
    x_list: list[float] = []
    asset_id_list: list[int] = []

    for t in range(lookback, n_months - 1):
        for i in range(n_assets):
            past_ret = np.sum(returns[t - lookback : t, i])
            # Historical volatility
            hist_vol = float(np.std(returns[max(0, t - 24) : t, i], ddof=1))
            if hist_vol <= 0:
                hist_vol = 0.01

            y = float(returns[t + 1, i] / hist_vol)
            x = float(np.sign(past_ret))

            y_list.append(y)
            x_list.append(x)
            asset_id_list.append(i)

    y_arr = np.array(y_list)
    x_arr = np.array(x_list)
    asset_ids = np.array(asset_id_list)
    n_obs = len(y_arr)

    # Model A: Naive Pooled OLS (Moskowitz et al. 2012 specification)
    x_pooled = np.column_stack([np.ones(n_obs), x_arr])
    beta_pooled, _, _, _ = np.linalg.lstsq(x_pooled, y_arr, rcond=None)
    residuals_pooled = y_arr - x_pooled @ beta_pooled
    se_pooled = np.sqrt(np.sum(residuals_pooled**2) / (n_obs - 2) * np.linalg.inv(x_pooled.T @ x_pooled)[1, 1])
    t_pooled = float(beta_pooled[1] / se_pooled)

    # Model B: Asset-demeaned Fixed Effects / Within Estimator (Huang et al. 2020)
    # Demean y and x within each asset
    y_within = np.zeros_like(y_arr)
    x_within = np.zeros_like(x_arr)
    for i in range(n_assets):
        mask = asset_ids == i
        y_within[mask] = y_arr[mask] - np.mean(y_arr[mask])
        x_within[mask] = x_arr[mask] - np.mean(x_arr[mask])

    # OLS on demeaned data without intercept
    beta_within = float(np.sum(x_within * y_within) / np.sum(x_within**2))
    resid_within = y_within - beta_within * x_within
    se_within = float(np.sqrt(np.sum(resid_within**2) / (n_obs - n_assets - 1) / np.sum(x_within**2)))
    t_within = float(beta_within / se_within)

    # Model C: Asset-by-asset individual regressions (% with positive and significant t-stat)
    sig_count = 0
    for i in range(n_assets):
        mask = asset_ids == i
        y_sub = y_arr[mask]
        x_sub = x_arr[mask]
        x_mat = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(x_sub), x_sub])
        b_sub = np.linalg.lstsq(x_mat, y_sub, rcond=None)[0]
        res_sub = y_sub - x_mat @ b_sub
        se_sub = np.sqrt(np.sum(res_sub**2) / (len(y_sub) - 2) * np.linalg.inv(x_mat.T @ x_mat)[1, 1])
        t_sub = b_sub[1] / se_sub
        if t_sub > 1.96:
            sig_count += 1
    pct_sig_individual = float(sig_count / n_assets * 100.0)

    return {
        "beta_pooled": round(float(beta_pooled[1]), 4),
        "t_pooled": round(t_pooled, 2),
        "beta_within": round(beta_within, 4),
        "t_within": round(t_within, 2),
        "pct_sig_individual": round(pct_sig_individual, 1),
    }


def main() -> None:
    print("Lab F3-L05-1 | TSMOM Pooled Regression vs Huang et al. (2020) Critique")
    print("---------------------------------------------------------------------")
    res = evaluate_tsmom_pooled_critique(seed=42)
    print(f"Pooled OLS Slope (beta):       {res['beta_pooled']:+.4f} (t = {res['t_pooled']:.2f})")
    print(f"Within-Asset FE Slope (beta):  {res['beta_within']:+.4f} (t = {res['t_within']:.2f})")
    print(f"Assets with Sig Individual t:  {res['pct_sig_individual']:.1f}% (nominal 5% test)")
    print("-" * 69)
    tp, tw = res["t_pooled"], res["t_within"]
    print(
        f"Under true i.i.d. returns, pooled OLS is biased (t = {tp:.2f}), "
        f"while within FE is insignificant (t = {tw:.2f})."
    )


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The debate surrounding time-series momentum exposes deep econometric and market realities:

  • Econometric Significance vs. Trading Profits: An econometrician testing for serial autocorrelation in returns removes unconditional asset means (\(\mu_i\)). However, an actual investment fund trading a TSMOM strategy earns the total return \(w_t^i r_{t+1}^i\), which benefits from both unconditional asset drifts and any active trend-timing alpha. Removing \(\mu_i\) answers the question of pure timing, but understates investable dollar performance.
  • Data Snooping in Formation Windows: Testing dozens of lookback windows (1 to 12 months) across multiple futures contracts creates family-wise false discovery inflation (multiple testing). Without pre-registration or Bonferroni/Holm corrections, isolated statistical significance can be an artifact of data mining.
  • Post-2009 Performance Decay: As documented by Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023), global factor returns experienced widespread post-publication decay after 2008, raising active debate over whether TSMOM continues to deliver genuine alpha.

Debate Brief: Is Time-Series Momentum Real After 2009?

1. Resolution

Resolved: Time-series momentum across global futures markets was largely an artifact of historical unconditional drifts and pooled-regression econometric bias, and does not constitute a viable, persistent source of out-of-sample risk-adjusted alpha in post-2009 financial markets.

2. Strongest Case for the Critique (Critique Paradigm)

The critique stance, spearheaded by Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020), argues that TSMOM is primarily an econometric illusion:

  • The Unconditional Mean Confounder: Pooled OLS regressions with \(t > 10\) simply capture the fact that some assets have positive average risk premia (e.g. equities and bonds) while others have negative drifts. Once fixed effects remove unconditional means, serial predictability collapses (Huang et al. 2020).
  • Failure of Asset-by-Asset Predictability: If time-series momentum were a universal physical law of asset pricing, individual contracts would display robust predictability. In reality, less than \(7\%\) of individual futures pass nominal \(5\%\) tests, matching random chance.
  • Post-2009 Performance Stagnation: Between 2009 and 2021, the benchmark Société Générale Trend Index delivered near-zero real returns, lagging cash. Once central banks suppressed yields and macroeconomic trends, trend following collapsed.

3. Strongest Case for Trend Following (Replicability Paradigm)

The trend-following defense, supported by Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) and Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (2017), maintains that trend following is a robust macroeconomic reality:

  • Portfolio Trading Profits vs. Regression Slopes: Investors trade portfolios, not demeaned residuals. A strategy that is long high-drift assets and cuts exposure during prolonged drawdowns earns superior risk-adjusted Sharpe ratios, regardless of whether the gain is classified as 'static drift' or 'dynamic timing'.
  • A Century of Out-of-Sample Resilience: The 137-year history in Hurst et al. (2017) spans 10 distinct decades dating back to 1880, including periods where bonds and commodities had negative long-term drifts. TSMOM was profitable in every single decade.
  • The 2022 Inflation Shock Vindication: During the 2022 global bear market (where traditional 60/40 portfolios plunged \(-16\%\) to \(-20\%\)), trend-following CTAs gained \(+20\%\) to \(+30\%\) by shorting bonds and equities and going long commodities, reaffirming the indispensable role of crisis alpha.

4. Graded Evidence Comparison

Misconceptions

  1. A pooled OLS regression across 58 assets with \(t > 10\) provides conclusive proof that each asset's past return predicts its future return.

    Evidence: Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) prove that cross-sectional variation in unconditional means produces \(t > 10\) in pooled regressions even when every asset is strictly i.i.d. with zero predictability.

  2. If the within-asset regression slope is statistically insignificant, the physical trading strategy must have made zero trading profit.

    Evidence: Trading profits incorporate the unconditional drift of the assets; while the within estimator isolates pure timing alpha, the investment portfolio earns the positive static risk premium as well.

  3. Time-series momentum permanently stopped working after the 2008 global financial crisis.

    Evidence: While trend following stagnated during 2010–2021 central bank QE, it delivered massive positive returns (+20% to +30%) during the 2022 global inflation shock, proving its enduring crisis protection.

Problem Set

Mechanics

In a 2-asset panel of \(T = 100\) months, Asset 1 has mean return \(\mu_1 = +0.02\) and Asset 2 has mean return \(\mu_2 = -0.02\). If an econometrician fits pooled OLS without entity demeaning, will the estimated slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}}\) be positive, zero, or negative?

Solution

The estimated slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}}\) will be strictly positive. Because Asset 1 has a positive drift (\(\mu_1 = +0.02\)), its past sign is positive more frequently, and its future return is higher on average. Because Asset 2 has a negative drift (\(\mu_2 = -0.02\)), its past sign is negative more frequently, and its future return is lower. The cross-sectional covariance \(\Cov_i(\E[x^i], \E[y^i]) > 0\) creates a positive pooled regression slope even under pure i.i.d. returns.

Suppose an asset has monthly demeaned return series \(y_t - \bar{y} = [0.02, -0.01, 0.03, -0.04]\) and demeaned sign regressor \(x_t - \bar{x} = [0.5, -0.5, 0.5, -0.5]\). Calculate the within-estimator slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{within}}\).

Solution

The within estimator is given by the OLS formula on demeaned variables without intercept:

\[ \hat{\beta}_{\text{within}} = \frac{\sum (x_t - \bar{x})(y_t - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_t - \bar{x})^2} \] \[ \sum (x_t - \bar{x})(y_t - \bar{y}) = 0.5(0.02) + (-0.5)(-0.01) + 0.5(0.03) + (-0.5)(-0.04) = 0.010 + 0.005 + 0.015 + 0.020 = 0.050 \] \[ \sum (x_t - \bar{x})^2 = (0.5)^2 + (-0.5)^2 + (0.5)^2 + (-0.5)^2 = 0.25 \times 4 = 1.00 \] \[ \hat{\beta}_{\text{within}} = \frac{0.050}{1.00} = 0.050 \]

The within slope is \(+0.050\).

Out of 58 independent asset tests conducted at a nominal significance level of \(\alpha = 0.05\), what is the expected number of false positive rejections under the null hypothesis of zero predictability?

Solution

Under the null hypothesis that none of the 58 assets possess genuine predictability, each test has a probability \(\alpha = 0.05\) of rejecting the null by chance. The expected number of false rejections is:

\[ \E[N_{\text{false}}] = N \times \alpha = 58 \times 0.05 = 2.90 \]

Finding approximately 3 or 4 significant contracts out of 58 is completely consistent with random chance.

Proof

Prove that the fixed-effects within estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{within}}\) is algebraically identical to running OLS with dummy variables for each entity (LSDV).

Solution

Consider the panel model \(y_{it} = \alpha_i + \beta x_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\), where \(\alpha_i\) are entity-specific intercepts. In matrix notation, let \(D\) be the \(NT \times N\) matrix of entity dummy variables and \(X\) be the vector of regressors. By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) theorem, the OLS estimator for \(\beta\) in \(y = D\alpha + X\beta + \epsilon\) is obtained by regressing the residual of \(y\) projected off \(D\) on the residual of \(X\) projected off \(D\). The projection matrix off \(D\) is \(M_D = I - D(D^\top D)^{-1}D^\top\). For any entity \(i\), multiplying by \(M_D\) replaces each observation with its entity-demeaned value: \((M_D y)_{it} = y_{it} - \bar{y}_i\) and \((M_D X)_{it} = x_{it} - \bar{x}_i\). Therefore, the LSDV estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{LSDV}} = (X^\top M_D X)^{-1} X^\top M_D y\) is algebraically identical to the within estimator, proving the equivalence.

Prove that if asset returns have identical unconditional means (\(\mu_i = \mu\) for all \(i\)), the between-asset covariance term in Theorem 5.1 is zero.

Solution

In Theorem 5.1, the between-asset covariance is \(\Cov_i\left(\E_t[x_t^i \mid i], \E_t[y_{t+1}^i \mid i]\right)\). If all assets have identical unconditional means \(\mu_i = \mu\), then \(\E_t[y_{t+1}^i \mid i] = \mu / \sigma\) is constant across all assets \(i\). The covariance of any random variable with a constant is identically zero: \(\Cov_i(Z_i, c) = \E[Z_i \cdot c] - \E[Z_i]\cdot c = c \E[Z_i] - c \E[Z_i] = 0\). Thus, when unconditional expected returns are homogeneous across instruments, pooled OLS is unbiased and does not generate false predictability.

Prove that when an asset has a positive unconditional drift \(\mu > 0\), the probability of a positive cumulative return over lookback \(k\), \(\Prob(\sum_{s=1}^k r_{t-s} > 0)\), is strictly greater than \(0.5\) for Gaussian i.i.d. returns.

Solution

Let \(r_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) be i.i.d. Gaussian returns. The cumulative return over lookback \(k\) is \(S_k = \sum_{s=1}^k r_{t-s}\). By the linearity of Gaussian distributions, \(S_k \sim \mathcal{N}(k\mu, k\sigma^2)\). Standardizing \(S_k\):

\[ \Prob(S_k > 0) = \Prob\left(\frac{S_k - k\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{k}} > \frac{0 - k\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{k}}\right) = \Prob\left(Z > -\frac{\mu \sqrt{k}}{\sigma}\right) = \Phi\left(\frac{\mu \sqrt{k}}{\sigma}\right) \]

where \(\Phi(\cdot)\) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Since \(\mu > 0\), \(k \ge 1\), and \(\sigma > 0\), the argument \(\frac{\mu \sqrt{k}}{\sigma} > 0\). Because \(\Phi(0) = 0.5\) and \(\Phi(\cdot)\) is strictly monotonically increasing, \(\Phi\left(\frac{\mu \sqrt{k}}{\sigma}\right) > 0.5\), proving that assets with positive drift have past signs that are positive more than half the time.

Empirical

In Huang et al. (2020), when the 58 futures contracts were grouped into the four asset classes (Equities, Bonds, FX, Commodities), which asset class exhibited the largest drop in statistical significance when moving from pooled OLS to the within estimator?

Solution

Equity indices and sovereign bonds exhibited the most dramatic collapse. In equities and government bonds, historical excess returns have strong, persistent positive unconditional risk premia (equity risk premium and term premium). Naive pooled OLS heavily leveraged these positive drifts, yielding pooled \(t\)-statistics exceeding \(6.0\). When within-asset demeaning removed the equity and term premia, the \(t\)-statistics plunged to near zero (under \(1.2\)), proving that historical 'momentum' in equities and bonds was largely unconditional drift.

Explain why commodities exhibited the highest persistence of within-asset predictability in Huang et al. (2020) compared to equities and currencies.

Solution

Commodities are driven by physical supply-demand imbalances, storage constraints, and inelastic short-run production (e.g. multi-year mine development or agricultural weather cycles). When a structural supply deficit occurs, inventory drawdowns sustain upward price trends for 12 to 24 months before new supply arrives. Consequently, commodities exhibit genuine time-series autocorrelation and roll yield momentum that survives within-asset demeaning.

Analyze the performance of systematic trend-following CTAs during the 2022 macroeconomic tightening cycle. How did their returns refute the claim that TSMOM was permanently dead?

Solution

In 2022, global central banks aggressively raised policy rates to combat four-decade-high inflation. The S&P 500 plunged \(-18\%\) and global aggregate bonds crashed \(-16\%\), delivering the worst year for 60/40 portfolios since 1937. In stark contrast, diversified trend-following CTAs (such as the SG Trend Index) gained \(+20\%\) to \(+25\%\). Trend models held massive short positions in US Treasuries and European bonds, long positions in energy (crude oil, natural gas), and short positions in equities, proving that when persistent macro trends return, TSMOM delivers massive crisis alpha.

Research

How can a Bayesian hierarchical model reconcile the pooled estimator (which assumes identical slopes) with individual asset regressions (which suffer from high estimation variance)?

Solution

A Bayesian hierarchical model posits that individual asset slopes \(\beta_i\) are drawn from a common prior distribution: \(\beta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\beta, \tau_\beta^2)\). When estimating \(\beta_i\), the posterior estimate shrinks the noisy individual asset estimate \(\hat{\beta}_i\) toward the global cross-asset mean \(\mu_\beta\): \(\beta_i^{\text{Bayes}} = w_i \hat{\beta}_i + (1 - w_i) \mu_\beta\), where shrinkage weight \(w_i = \tau_\beta^2 / (\tau_\beta^2 + \sigma_i^2)\). This avoids the all-or-nothing dichotomy of pooled OLS (zero cross-sectional variance) versus unpooled regressions (high noise), delivering optimal mean squared error out-of-sample.

Why might an institutional investor prefer allocating to a multi-asset trend portfolio even if the econometric null of \(\beta_{\text{within}} = 0\) cannot be rejected at the 5% significance level?

Solution

An institutional allocator optimizes the portfolio's utility and drawdown profile, not statistical \(p\)-values. Even if \(\beta_{\text{within}}\) is modestly positive without clearing the \(t = 1.96\) hurdle, the strategy provides two critical structural benefits: (1) Convex tail hedging: it acts as a synthetic long straddle that pays off during deep liquidity crises; (2) Uncorrelated drift: harvesting the unconditional risk premia of commodities, bonds, and currencies in a risk-managed, inverse-volatility-weighted structure diversifies equity-heavy institutional portfolios.

Propose an empirical specification to test whether the Huang et al. (2020) critique applies equally to cross-sectional momentum (CSMOM).

Solution

In CSMOM, portfolios are strictly dollar-neutral (\(\sum w_{it} = 0\)). However, if an industry or sector has a persistently higher unconditional mean, CSMOM may persistently overweight that industry, confounding sector drift with momentum. To test this, one can estimate a panel regression of cross-sectionally demeaned returns \(\tilde{r}_{t+1}^i = r_{t+1}^i - \bar{r}_{t+1}\) on cross-sectionally demeaned past ranks \(\tilde{x}_t^i\), comparing pooled OLS with a model including asset fixed effects. If the slope shrinks substantially upon adding entity fixed effects, CSMOM also suffers from unconditional mean confounding.

Comprehension Check

  1. What econometric specification did Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) use to report a t-statistic above 10 for time-series momentum?

    Options

    Moskowitz et al. (2012) pooled scaled future returns on past signs across all 58 contracts with a single common intercept \(\alpha\).

  2. According to Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020), what primary source of bias inflates the pooled OLS slope estimator?

    Options

    Assets with positive average drift have positive past returns and positive future returns, creating a positive cross-asset covariance that pooled OLS mistakes for time-series momentum.

  3. What econometric estimator eliminates the between-asset unconditional mean bias identified by Huang et al. (2020)?

    Options

    Subtracting the asset-specific mean from both dependent and independent variables purges the between-asset drift, isolating pure within-asset serial correlation.

  4. In Huang et al. (2020), approximately what percentage of the 58 individual futures contracts exhibited a statistically significant positive t-statistic at the 5% level in asset-by-asset tests?

    Only 4 out of 58 contracts (~7%) exhibited statistically significant individual t-statistics, roughly matching the 5% false discovery rate expected under random chance.

  5. Why can an investor earn positive trading profits from a TSMOM strategy even if the within-asset regression slope is statistically zero?

    Options

    While econometricians isolate timing alpha by subtracting means, investors capture both timing and unconditional drifts in their actual dollar returns.

  6. What historical crisis episode in 2022 provided strong empirical vindication for the crisis alpha of trend-following strategies?

    Options

    In 2022, trend following produced large positive returns during a rare simultaneous collapse of global stocks and bonds, demonstrating robust crisis alpha.

  7. What phenomenon documented by Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023) describes the general reduction in anomaly returns following publication?

    Options

    Post-publication decay occurs when publication attracts investor capital that trades away the anomaly's historical return premium.

  8. If all assets in an economy have identical unconditional means (\(\mu_i = \mu\)), what is the between-asset covariance bias in the pooled OLS slope?

    When unconditional means are identical across assets, cross-sectional covariance is zero (\(\Cov_i(\E[x], \mu) = 0\)), eliminating the between-asset bias.
