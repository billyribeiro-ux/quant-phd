Factors Across Asset Classes

Systematic asset pricing anomalies do not stop at the boundaries of equity exchanges. Canonical risk factors—value, momentum, carry, and trend—pervade government bond yields, foreign exchange forwards, commodity curves, and equity index futures. Across five doctoral lessons and an autograded capstone project, this module unifies cross-asset empirical finance: analyzing the Asness–Moskowitz–Pedersen value-momentum negative correlation, formalizing Koijen–Moskowitz–Pedersen–Vrugt general carry across curves, deconstructing Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen time-series momentum and Fung–Hsieh straddle convexity over a century of data, and dissecting the Huang–Li–Wang–Zhou pooled-regression econometric critique.

  • Prerequisites: F2 The Classic Factors (cross-sectional momentum, value premium, spanning regressions).
  • Unlocks: Advanced Macro Quant Factor Portfolios and Part F Studio Capstones.
  • Project: Multi-Asset Factor Book: authoring factor_book.py to construct carry, momentum, and value across Rates, FX, and Commodities, evaluate crash skewness, and synthesize a risk-parity factor allocation.
  • Data: Embedded multi-asset panels and deterministic synthetic histories covering Rates, FX, and Commodities.

45 h lessons · 25 h project (36%)

  3. Contrast time-series momentum against cross-sectional momentum, formalize trailing volatility scaling, and model crisis alpha dynamics during macroeconomic drawdowns (Lesson 3).
  4. Synthesize long-history evidence on trend following spanning 1880 to 2016, proving Fung–Hsieh lookback straddle smile convexity and estimating institutional execution drag (Lesson 4).
  5. Deconstruct the pooled-regression critique of time-series momentum, isolating between-asset unconditional mean dispersion from within-asset serial predictability (Lesson 5).
  6. Defend or challenge post-2009 quantitative easing performance decay in trend following within a structured formal debate brief (Lesson 5).
  7. Author an autograded multi-asset factor book in factor_book.py, passing 50 randomized seeds and demonstrating risk-parity Sharpe expansion (Project).

F3 builds directly on the theoretical and empirical foundations of doctoral asset pricing: From cross-sectional momentum, value premium, spanning regressions, and betting against beta in F2, F3 generalizes factor structures across asset classes. From CAPM, tangency portfolio, and linear factor models in AP2, F3 evaluates cross-market efficiency and factor integration. From OLS estimator, heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors, and two-way clustering in S2, F3 models panel regression asymptotics and cross-sectional dependence. From autocorrelation function, unit roots, and Dickey–Fuller tests in S3, F3 tests time-series predictability and persistence.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six testing prerequisite concepts from F2, AP2, S2, and S3, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In cross-sectional equity factor models (F2), why does Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) momentum skip the most recent month \(t-1\) when ranking performance over \(t-12\) to \(t-2\)?

    Options

    Skipping month \(t-1\) eliminates microstructure noise (bid-ask spread bounce) and short-term liquidity reversals that would otherwise offset intermediate momentum gains.

  2. Under the Fama–French (2015) 5-factor model (F2), what empirical result occurs when the Value factor (HML) is regressed on the other four factors?

    Options

    Fama and French (2015) show that HML alpha drops to near zero in spanning regressions including CMA and RMW, rendering HML redundant in US equity sample spans.

  3. In linear factor models (AP2), what does a spanning regression \(\alpha_f = 0\) establish about factor \(f\)?

    Options

    A zero alpha in a spanning regression proves that the test factor lies within the mean-variance frontier spanned by the benchmark factors, adding zero marginal Sharpe ratio.

  4. In panel regression econometrics (S2), why are standard OLS standard errors downward-biased when residuals exhibit cross-sectional and serial correlation?

    Options

    Positive error covariances across entities and over time expand the sampling variance of parameter estimates, necessitating double-clustering (Petersen 2009) to avoid false statistical significance.

  5. In time-series econometrics (S3), what is the key difference between a stationary process and a unit-root process?

    Options

    Unit-root processes exhibit infinite memory where shocks permanently shift the level, leading to spurious regression results if regressions are run on un-differenced non-stationary data.

  6. In Daniel & Moskowitz (2016) momentum crash research (F2), during what market regimes do momentum crashes occur?

    Options

    Momentum crashes happen when a distressed market rebounds sharply: the short side of momentum consists of high-beta distressed options that skyrocket, causing catastrophic short-side losses.

  7. Skill check 1: In constructing a cross-sectional dollar-neutral factor from raw signals \(s_t \in \R^N\), why must portfolio weights be computed as \(w_t = (s_t - \bar{s}_t) / \sum_{i=1}^N |s_{t,i} - \bar{s}_t|\)?

    Options

    Demeaning enforces \(\sum_{i=1}^N w_{t,i} = 0\), establishing zero net dollar exposure, and dividing by \(\sum |s_{t,i} - \bar{s}_t|\) sets gross exposure \(\sum |w_{t,i}| = 1\).

  8. Skill check 2: Value and momentum factors each have an annualized Sharpe ratio of \(0.50\) and a correlation of \(\rho = -0.50\). Under equal volatility scaling, what is the annualized Sharpe ratio of their 50/50 combination?

    Using \(\SR_{\text{combo}} = \SR / \sqrt{(1+\rho)/2} = 0.50 / \sqrt{(1 - 0.50)/2} = 0.50 / \sqrt{0.25} = 0.50 / 0.50 = 1.00\) (wait, when \(\SR = 0.50\) and factor is 2.0x, combined Sharpe is \(1.00\); the expansion multiplier is \(2.00\)).

Lesson map

Five lessons of 9 study hours each (45 hours total):

  1. Lesson 1 · Value and Momentum Everywhere

    Cross-asset evidence from Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013). Negative correlation, Sharpe doubling theorem, global factor pricing, and liquidity risk.

    9 hours · Introduces value-and-momentum-everywhere

  2. Lesson 2 · Carry Across Asset Classes

    General carry formulation from Koijen et al. (2018). Forward premium puzzle in FX, roll-down in bonds, basis in commodities, and crash risk skewness.

    9 hours · Introduces carry-factor

  3. Lesson 3 · Time-Series Momentum

    Absolute trend following from Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012). Volatility scaling, crisis alpha during market drawdowns, and comparison to cross-sectional momentum.

    9 hours · Introduces time-series-momentum

  4. Lesson 4 · Long-History Evidence on Trend Following

    A century of evidence from Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (2017). 1880–2016 persistence, Fung–Hsieh lookback straddle smile convexity, and execution friction drag.

    9 hours · Introduces trend-following-long-history

  5. Lesson 5 · The Pooled-Regression Critique

    The Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020) critique. Between-asset unconditional mean bias, asset-by-asset tests, post-2009 performance decay, and formal debate.

    9 hours · Introduces tsmom-pooled-regression-critique · Structured debate
