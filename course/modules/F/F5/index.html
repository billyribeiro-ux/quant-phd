The Factor Zoo & Replication

Over the past half-century, empirical asset pricing researchers have published hundreds of firm-level characteristics claiming to predict the cross-section of expected equity returns—a proliferation famously dubbed the "factor zoo." This doctoral module investigates the econometric, statistical, and institutional mechanisms driving discovery, publication, and replication in empirical finance. We rigorously examine multiple testing adjustments, publication bias, methodological divergence between competing replication paradigms, hierarchical Bayesian shrinkage, and machine-learning double-selection tests. The module culminates in authoring a full Replication-Crisis Adjudication Engine that audits candidate factor claims across portfolio weighting schemes, universe screens, control models, and high-dimensional factor libraries.

  • Prerequisites: F2 The Classic Factors, S5 GMM & Cross-Sectional Econometrics, and S7 Research Integrity & Multiple Testing.
  • Unlocks: Advanced Empirical Asset Pricing Studios and Macro-Finance Factor Research.
  • Project: Replication-Crisis Adjudication Engine: authoring adjudication.py to execute multiple testing hurdles, replication matrices, empirical Bayes shrinkage, and Feng-Giglio-Xiu double-selection tests across 10 candidate equity anomalies.
  • Data: Deterministic simulated panels reproducing empirical cross-sectional distributions, microcap distortions, factor correlations, and publication selection filters.

48 h lessons · 32 h project (40%)

Objectives

By the end of F5 you can:

  1. Formalize the factor zoo, analyze why classical \(t \ge 1.96\) significance testing produces catastrophic false discovery rates under sequential discovery, and compute multiple-testing haircuts including Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) raised hurdles (Lesson 1).
  2. Quantify publication bias, decompose post-publication anomaly alpha decay into statistical in-sample p-hacking versus institutional arbitrage capital inflow, and implement Egger funnel asymmetry tests (Lesson 2).
  3. Define and compute the anomaly replication rate, adjudicating the empirical clash between Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) and Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023) across benchmark models and significance thresholds (Lesson 3).
  4. Deconstruct the methodological choices that drive replication outcomes, proving how microcap universe screens, NYSE breakpoints, and equal-versus-value weighting systematically alter factor returns (Lesson 4).
  5. Derive and calibrate hierarchical Bayesian shrinkage of factor alphas, estimating hyperparameter priors via empirical Bayes marginal maximum likelihood to mitigate the winner's curse in backtested asset pricing (Lesson 5).
  6. Formulate the double-selection test for new factors (Feng, Giglio & Xiu 2020), purging omitted-variable bias and establishing valid asymptotic inference when proposing candidate factors against high-dimensional control libraries (Lesson 6).
  7. Author the complete autograded Replication-Crisis Adjudication Engine, passing 50 randomized test seeds and defending benchmark choices against adversarial committee review (Project).

Prerequisites

F5 directly integrates foundational concepts across factor pricing, cross-sectional econometrics, and multiple-testing statistics: From Fama–French three-factor model, Fama–French five-factor model, q-factor model, spanning regression, and maximum squared Sharpe ratio comparison in F2, F5 constructs baseline benchmark control models and evaluates spanning redundancy. From Fama–MacBeth regressions, Shanken correction, and the GRS test in S5, F5 formulates multivariate test asset pricing errors and cross-sectional risk premia. From data-snooping bias, family-wise error rate, Bonferroni correction, Holm step-down, false discovery rate, Benjamini–Hochberg, empirical Bayes shrinkage, and t-statistic hurdles in S7, F5 scales multiple-testing corrections across hundreds of cross-sectional factor candidates.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six testing prerequisite concepts from F2, S5, and S7, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In the Fama–French (2015) five-factor model (F2), which classic factor becomes redundant in spanning regressions once operating profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA) are included?

    Options

    Fama and French (2015) show that the traditional HML factor produces an intercept statistically indistinguishable from zero when regressed on the market, SMB, RMW, and CMA factors.

  2. Under the Hou, Xue & Zhang (2015) \(q\)-factor model (F2), what economic framework justifies investment and return on equity as common cross-sectional factors?

    Options

    The \(q\)-factor model derives factor premia from the first-order condition of firm value-maximizing investment decisions under convex capital adjustment costs.

  3. In cross-sectional two-pass regressions (S5), what econometric distortion does the Shanken (1992) correction adjust for?

    Options

    The Shanken correction inflates second-pass asymptotic covariance matrices to account for the estimation uncertainty embedded in the first-pass beta estimates.

  4. What does the Gibbons, Ross & Shanken (1989) GRS test evaluate across \(N\) test assets (S5)?

    Options

    The GRS test evaluates the joint pricing error null \(H_0: \alpha_1 = \dots = \alpha_N = 0\) using exact finite-sample \(F\)-distribution scaling.

  5. In multiple hypothesis testing (S7), how does False Discovery Rate (FDR) control differ from Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) control?

    Options

    FWER controls \(\Prob(\text{False Positives} \ge 1)\), while Benjamini–Hochberg FDR controls \(\E[\text{False Discoveries} / \max(\text{Total Discoveries}, 1)]\).

  6. In empirical Bayes shrinkage (S7), what determines the shrinkage weight \(w_i\) applied to individual parameter estimates?

    Options

    Empirical Bayes shrinks noisy sample means toward the grand mean according to the signal-to-total-variance ratio, pulling noisy estimates with high standard errors most aggressively toward the prior.

  7. In cross-sectional factor adjudication (Skill Check 1), what causes an anomaly that is highly statistically significant in equal-weighted (EW) sorts (\(t > 5.0\)) to fail replication under value-weighting (VW) (\(t < 1.5\))?

    Options

    Equal weighting weights each firm equally, allowing illiquid microcaps (with severe bid-ask bounce and limits to arbitrage) to dominate the spread. Value-weighting places >80% weight on mega-caps where the anomaly disappears.

  8. In the Feng, Giglio & Xiu (2020) double-selection factor test (Skill Check 2), why does single-selection LASSO fail to produce valid inference for a candidate factor?

    Options

    Double selection performs selection on both the candidate factor (Step 1) and test asset pricing errors (Step 2), guaranteeing that any control factor capable of causing omitted-variable bias is retained.

Lesson Hours Concept Introduced Core Content
L01 · The Factor Zoo & Multiple Testing Haircuts 8 h factor-zoo The Cochrane (2011) factor zoo explosion, failure of classical \(t \ge 1.96\) significance testing, Harvey-Liu-Zhu raised hurdles (\(t > 3.0\)), Bonferroni, Holm, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustments.
L02 · Publication Bias & Anomaly Alpha Decay 8 h publication-bias Editorial gatekeeping conditioning on statistical significance, McLean & Pontiff (2016) 58% post-publication alpha decay, statistical p-hacking versus arbitrage capital, Egger funnel asymmetry tests.

L04 · Methodological Divergence in Empirical Finance 8 h replication-methodology-choices Anatomy of operational sorting choices, the microcap problem, NYSE breakpoints versus all-stock quintiles, rebalancing lags, delisting return corrections (Shumway 1997), Chen & Zimmermann (2022) open asset pricing.
L05 · Hierarchical Bayesian Shrinkage of Factor Alphas 8 h bayesian-replication-shrinkage Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen (2023) hierarchical Bayesian alpha model, signal-to-noise shrinkage weights, empirical Bayes marginal maximum likelihood hyperparameter estimation, mitigating the winner's curse.
L06 · Double-Selection Machine Learning Factor Tests 8 h double-selection-factor-test Testing candidate factors against high-dimensional libraries, pitfalls of single-selection LASSO, Belloni-Chernozhukov-Hansen / Feng-Giglio-Xiu (2020) double-selection framework, asymptotic normality of risk premia.

The Four-Rung Project Ladder

The unit culminates in authoring a professional Replication-Crisis Adjudication Engine (project specification), spanning 32 project study hours.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A complete, runnable implementation of the replication adjudication pipeline, calculating multiple-testing hurdles, replication matrices, empirical Bayes alpha shrinkage, and double-selection factor tests on 10 candidate anomalies.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    The identical adjudication task with key mathematical calculations removed: implementing Holm step-down ordering, precision-weighted prior mean estimation, and orthogonal projection residuals.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The full, graded deliverable: authoring adjudication.py in grader/, passing 50 randomized test seeds, validating mathematical invariants, and submitting a rigorous 4-page research adjudication report.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch extensions

    High-dimensional international cross-market replication across 40 global equity exchanges, transaction-cost bid-ask penalty modeling, and deep latent-factor IPCA controls.
