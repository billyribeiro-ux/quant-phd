Distressed Debt & Restructuring

Corporate default prediction, empirical recovery rate distributions, the Absolute Priority Rule and its strategic violations, out-of-court debt exchange offers versus Chapter 11 judicial reorganization, and fulcrum security valuation across the capital structure recovery waterfall.

Prerequisites: CR1, CF5
Unlocks: STUDIO-CR (Credit Analyst Desk)
Project: Capital Structure Recovery Waterfall & Fulcrum Valuation Engine

30 h lessons across 5 lessons @ 6 h · 20 h project (40.0% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CR4 you can:

  1. Estimate multivariate default prediction scores using Altman's (1968) Linear Discriminant Analysis ($Z$, $Z'$, $Z''$) and evaluate classification trade-offs against structural distance-to-default models.
  2. Model empirical recovery rates across debt seniority tranches and quantify the negative correlation between macroeconomic default frequencies and asset recovery values.
  3. Derive the equilibrium carve-out payment in strategic Chapter 11 bargaining and explain why consensual plans frequently violate the strict Absolute Priority Rule under Section 1121 exclusivity.
  4. Analyze the coordination failure in out-of-court debt exchange offers under Section 316(b) of the Trust Indenture Act and demonstrate how exit consents eliminate creditor holdout incentives (Gilson, John & Lang 1990).
  5. Construct a multi-tranche capital structure recovery waterfall to identify the fulcrum security across enterprise value scenarios and allocate Plan of Reorganization consideration (cash, take-back debt, reorganized equity).
  6. Value distressed secondary debt purchases, calculating scenario-weighted expected recoveries, multiples on invested capital (MOIC), and annualized internal rates of return (IRR).

Prerequisites

CR4 assumes CR1 (Structural & Reduced-Form Credit Models), CF5 (M&A, LBOs & Restructuring), and their prerequisite closure. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From CR1: Merton structural credit model, distance to default, reduced-form credit model, and default intensity (hazard rate).
  • From CF5: Chapter 11 bankruptcy, sources and uses of funds, leveraged buyout mechanics, and multiple on invested capital (MOIC).
  • From earlier foundations: trade-off theory (CF2), costs of financial distress (CF2), and maximum likelihood estimation (S1).

Placement diagnostic

Pass this diagnostic (score \(\ge 80\%\) and answer both skill-testing items correctly) to place out of rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder.

  1. A distressed borrower has $\$30\text{M}$ of senior secured first lien debt, $\$50\text{M}$ of second lien notes, $\$60\text{M}$ of senior unsecured bonds, and common equity. If the court establishes a reorganized enterprise value of $\$65\text{M}$, which tranche is the fulcrum security under strict Absolute Priority?

    Options
    Under strict absolute priority, first lien receives $\min(65, 30) = 30\text{M}$ ($100\%$ recovery). The remaining $\$35\text{M}$ is allocated to second lien, satisfying $\$35\text{M}$ of its $\$50\text{M}$ claim ($70\%$ recovery). Unsecured notes and equity receive $\$0$. The fulcrum security is the partially covered tranche that receives the residual equity of the reorganized debtor.

  2. In a Plan of Reorganization with $\$200\text{M}$ enterprise value, the plan distributes $\$30\text{M}$ of balance-sheet cash, issues $\$70\text{M}$ of new take-back debt, and distributes reorganized common equity. If strict absolute priority allocates $\$80\text{M}$ total recovery to the fulcrum class, and the fulcrum class receives $\$20\text{M}$ of take-back debt and $\$0$ cash, what percentage of reorganized common equity does the fulcrum class own?

    Options
    Total equity value equals enterprise value minus cash distributed minus new debt issued: \(200 - 30 - 70 = 100\text{M}\). The fulcrum class is allocated \(\$80\text{M}\) total value. Having received \(\$20\text{M}\) in new debt, its remaining \(\$60\text{M}\) recovery is distributed as reorganized stock, yielding \(60 / 100 = 60.0\%\) ownership.

  3. In the Merton (1974) structural credit model (CR1), what does the Merton distance to default (DD) measure?

    Options
    In Merton (1974), distance to default is \(DD = (\ln(V_0/D) + (\mu - 0.5\sigma_V^2)T) / (\sigma_V\sqrt{T})\), quantifying the standardized distance between expected logarithmic asset value and the default barrier.

  4. Under Chapter 11 bankruptcy mechanics (CF5), what constitutes "debtor-in-possession" (DIP) financing?

    Options
    Under Section 364, DIP lenders provide critical liquidity during reorganization, protected by super-priority administrative status (§ 364(c)) or priming liens on existing collateral (§ 364(d)).

  5. What voting threshold is required under Bankruptcy Code § 1126(c) for an impaired class of creditors to accept a proposed Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization?

    Options
    Section 1126(c) establishes the dual statutory standard: class acceptance requires at least two-thirds in allowed dollar amount and a majority in number of creditors who actually cast ballots.

Lesson map

Five lessons of 6 study hours each (30 hours total):

  1. L01 · Default Prediction & The Altman Z-Score Model 6 h · Introduces default-prediction · Edward Altman (1968)

    Multivariate linear discriminant analysis (LDA), financial ratios, classification cutoffs, non-manufacturing and private revisions ($Z'$, $Z''$), and empirical benchmarking against structural Merton distance-to-default models.

  2. L02 · Empirical Recovery Rates & Seniority Structure 6 h · Introduces recovery-rate

    Empirical distribution of post-default recoveries across the capital structure (first lien, second lien, senior unsecured, subordinated debt), bimodal recovery characteristics, and procyclical loss given default driven by credit-cycle distress.

  3. L03 · The Absolute Priority Rule & Historical Violations 6 h · Introduces absolute-priority-rule, apr-violation

    Statutory absolute priority under Bankruptcy Code § 1129(b), holdout power during debtor exclusivity (§ 1121), administrative estate burn, and strategic bargaining leading to consensual equity carve-outs and APR deviations.

  4. L04 · Chapter 11 Reorganization vs. Out-of-Court Workouts 6 h · Introduces out-of-court-restructuring · Stuart Gilson, Kose John & Larry Lang (1990)

    The workout choice spectrum, Section 316(b) of the Trust Indenture Act, public bondholder coordination failure and free-riding, exit consents, and the institutional determinants of restructuring success.

  5. L05 · Distressed Debt Investing & Fulcrum Security Analysis 6 h · Introduces distressed-investing, recovery-waterfall

    Distressed debt strategies (liquidating, active blocking, loan-to-own), capital structure recovery waterfall mechanics, identifying the fulcrum tranche across enterprise value scenarios, and evaluating investor MOIC and IRR.
