Credit Derivatives & Correlation

Contractual mechanics of single-name credit default swaps, bootstrapping piecewise-constant hazard curves, the arbitrage economics of the CDS–bond basis, multi-name latent variable copula models, CDO tranche capital structure pricing, and the mathematical and institutional breakdown of correlation models during the 2008 credit crisis.

Prerequisites: CR1 (Structural & Reduced-Form Credit Models)
Unlocks: CR3, STUDIO-CR
Project: CDS bootstrapper and tranche pricer (autograder)

50 h lessons across 6 lessons (L01–L04: 8 h, L05–L06: 9 h) · 20 h project (28.6% of the 70 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CR2 you can:

  1. Price single-name credit default swaps by decomposing contractual cash flows into default protection and premium annuity legs under standard ISDA post-Big Bang conventions.
  2. Bootstrap piecewise-constant default intensities (hazard rate curves) from observed market CDS spreads and evaluate curve sensitivity to recovery rate assumptions.
  3. Formulate capital structure arbitrage strategies exploiting the CDS–bond basis, accounting for repo financing haircuts, counterparty credit risk, and short-sale frictions.
  4. Construct portfolio credit loss distributions under the one-factor Gaussian copula using the Large Homogeneous Portfolio (LHP) analytical approximation and numerical quadrature.
  5. Value synthetic CDO tranches across the capital structure (equity, mezzanine, senior, super-senior) and verify portfolio loss articulation identities.
  6. Deconstruct the failure of correlation models in the 2008 financial crisis by demonstrating the mathematical consequences of zero tail dependence in Gaussian copulas versus fat-tailed Student-t copulas.

Prerequisites

CR2 assumes completion of CR1 and its prerequisite closure (CF2, SC3). It uses the following concepts without re-introducing them:

  • From CR1: default intensity (hazard rate), reduced-form credit model, Merton structural credit model, and credit-spread puzzle.
  • From SC3: Girsanov's theorem, equivalent martingale measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), and risk-neutral pricing.
  • From SC1 / SC2: geometric Brownian motion, Itô's lemma, and first-passage hitting times.

Placement diagnostic

Pass this diagnostic (score \(\ge 80\%\) and answer both skill-testing items correctly) to place out of rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder.

  1. In single-name CDS hazard curve bootstrapping, what is the exact formula for the continuous-time survival probability \(Q(T)\) under a piecewise-constant hazard rate \(\lambda(t) = \lambda_k\) for \(t \in (T_{k-1}, T_k]\)?

    Options
    Cumulative survival probability is \(Q(T) = \exp(-\int_0^T \lambda(s) ds)\). When \(\lambda(s)\) is piecewise constant, the integral decomposes into the sum of rectangular areas \(\lambda_j \Delta T_j\) over each sub-interval up to \(T\).

  2. Under the one-factor Gaussian copula (Li 2000), why does increasing the systematic asset correlation parameter \(\rho\) increase the expected loss and break-even spread of a senior CDO tranche \([15\%, 30\%]\) while decreasing the break-even spread of the equity tranche \([0\%, 3\%]\)?

    Options
    By the tower property, unconditional expected loss \(\E[L] = (1 - R) p\) is invariant to correlation. Increasing \(\rho\) widens the dispersion of the loss distribution: in the left tail few names default (equity tranche absorbs zero loss), but in the deep right tail many names default simultaneously, blowing through mezzanine tranches and hitting senior tranches.

  3. Under the Duffie–Singleton (1999) reduced-form intensity model, what is the default-adjusted discount rate for valuing a defaultable claim with recovery of market value (RMV) \(R\)?

    Options
    Under RMV, the fractional loss upon default is \((1 - R)\). The fractional default loss intensity is \(\lambda_t (1 - R)\), so default risk is accounted for by adding \(\lambda_t (1 - R)\) directly to the riskless discount rate.

  4. What is the sign of the CDS–bond basis when a corporate bond's Z-spread over Libor/benchmarks exceeds the corresponding CDS spread on the same obligor?

    Options
    The CDS–bond basis is defined as \(S_{\text{CDS}} - S_{\text{bond}}\). When the cash spread exceeds the CDS spread, the basis is negative. The trade buys the cash bond (earning the higher spread) and buys CDS protection (paying the lower spread), capturing positive gross carry.

Project ladder

The unit project is CDS bootstrapper and tranche pricer (20 hours, graded by autograder). Work through the 4-rung ladder:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: A fully solved quantitative credit pipeline demonstrating single-name hazard curve bootstrapping, mark-to-market CDS valuation, basis trade carry economics, and Gaussian vs Student-t copula tranche valuation.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: An interactive problem with missing analytical steps: compute survival probabilities, mark-to-market upfront fee, net repo carry, and super-senior tranche expected losses. Graded instantly.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: The graded deliverable: implement a deterministic, robust Python credit derivatives engine passing the 16-test acceptance suite across 50 seeded market scenarios.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch extension: Implement random factor loadings and stochastic correlation regimes, analyzing bespoke tranche sensitivity to asset correlation spikes during liquidity crises.
