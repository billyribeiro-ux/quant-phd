CDS Mechanics & Pricing

Contractual anatomy, premium annuity valuation, contingent protection leg discounting, default accrual dynamics, and mark-to-market upfront conversion under standard market conventions.

The credit default swap is the foundational building block of modern synthetic credit markets. It isolates credit risk from funding, interest rate, and liquidity components embedded in cash corporate bonds. Understanding its exact mathematical decomposition into premium and protection legs is required for bootstrapping hazard curves, pricing structured collateralized debt obligations, and executing capital structure arbitrage.

1. Contractual Anatomy of a Credit Default Swap

A credit default swap (CDS) is a bilateral financial contract wherein the protection buyer pays a periodic fee (the premium or running spread) to the protection seller in exchange for a contingent payment if a designated reference entity experiences a contractually defined credit event before maturity.

Definition 1.1 (Single-Name CDS Cash Flows)

Let \(T\) denote the maturity date of the contract and \(t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_M = T\) denote periodic payment dates with intervals \(\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1}\). Let \(\tau\) denote the default time of the reference entity.

  • Premium Leg: If \(\tau > t_k\), the protection buyer pays \(s \cdot \Delta t_k\) on notional \(N\). If \(t_{k-1} < \tau \le t_k\), the buyer pays an accrued premium \(s \cdot (\tau - t_{k-1})\) covering the fraction of the coupon period prior to default.
  • Protection Leg: If \(\tau \le T\), the protection seller pays the loss given default \((1 - R) \cdot N\) at default time \(\tau\), where \(R\) is the post-default recovery rate established via auction or physical delivery. If \(\tau > T\), no protection payment occurs.

Under modern ISDA credit derivatives definitions (O'Kane 2008), standard credit events for corporate entities encompass bankruptcy, failure to pay, and restructuring. Since the 2009 ISDA "Big Bang" protocol, contracts settle primarily via cash settlement based on credit event auctions administered by Creditex and Markit, replacing bilateral physical delivery.

2. Mathematical Valuation of Premium and Protection Legs

We work on a filtered probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{Q})\) where \(\mathbb{Q}\) is the spot martingale pricing measure. The default time \(\tau\) is modelled as a stopping time with risk-neutral survival probability function \(Q(t) = \mathbb{Q}(\tau > t)\) and deterministic risk-free interest rate \(r(t)\). Let \(D(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t r(u) du)\) denote the risk-free discount factor.

Theorem 1.1 (CDS Valuation Equation)

Assuming independent default time and interest rate processes under \(\mathbb{Q}\), and that defaults occurring in \((t_{k-1}, t_k]\) arrive on average at the interval midpoint \(t_k^* = \frac{t_{k-1} + t_k}{2}\), the present value of the protection leg \(\text{ProtPV}\) and premium leg \(\text{PremPV}\) per unit of notional are:

\[ \text{ProtPV} = (1 - R) \sum_{k=1}^M D(t_k^*) \left[ Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k) \right] \] \[ \text{PremPV} = s \sum_{k=1}^M \left[ \Delta t_k D(t_k) Q(t_k) + \frac{\Delta t_k}{2} D(t_k^*) \left[ Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k) \right] \right] \]
Proof

The protection leg pays \((1 - R)\) at \(\tau\) provided \(\tau \le T\). Its discounted expected value under \(\mathbb{Q}\) is:

\[ \text{ProtPV} = \E^\mathbb{Q} \left[ (1 - R) D(\tau) \indic_{\{\tau \le T\}} \right] = -(1 - R) \int_0^T D(u) dQ(u) \]

Discretizing the integral over the coupon payment intervals \((t_{k-1}, t_k]\) and applying the midpoint approximation for discounting yields the stated protection formula.

The premium leg consists of regular payments if the entity survives to \(t_k\) plus the accrued coupon if default occurs in \((t_{k-1}, t_k]\):

\[ \text{PremPV} = s \sum_{k=1}^M \E^\mathbb{Q} \left[ \Delta t_k D(t_k) \indic_{\{\tau > t_k\}} + (\tau - t_{k-1}) D(\tau) \indic_{\{t_{k-1} < \tau \le t_k\}} \right] \]

Evaluating the expectations under independent interest rates: \(\E^\mathbb{Q}[\indic_{\{\tau > t_k\}}] = Q(t_k)\). For the accrual term, the expected elapsed time given default in the interval is \(\E^\mathbb{Q}[\tau - t_{k-1} \mid t_{k-1} < \tau \le t_k] \approx \frac{\Delta t_k}{2}\), and \(\mathbb{Q}(t_{k-1} < \tau \le t_k) = Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k)\). Summing these expectations yields the theorem.

Definition 1.2 (Par Spread & Risky PV01)

The risky duration or risky PV01 (denoted \(\text{RPV01}\)) is the present value of receiving 1 basis point (0.0001) per annum quarterly until default:

\[ \text{RPV01} = 0.0001 \sum_{k=1}^M \left[ \Delta t_k D(t_k) Q(t_k) + \frac{\Delta t_k}{2} D(t_k^*) \left[ Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k) \right] \right] \]

The break-even par spread \(S_{\text{par}}\) is the running spread that equates the present value of the premium leg to the protection leg at contract inception:

\[ S_{\text{par}} = \frac{\text{ProtPV}}{\sum_{k=1}^M \left[ \Delta t_k D(t_k) Q(t_k) + \frac{\Delta t_k}{2} D(t_k^*) [ Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k) ] \right]} = \frac{\text{ProtPV}}{10000 \cdot \text{RPV01}} \times 10000 \]
Example 1.1 (Continuous-Time Limit & Credit Spread Rule of Thumb)

Consider a constant risk-free rate \(r\) and a constant default intensity \(\lambda\) such that \(Q(t) = e^{-\lambda t}\). In the continuous payment limit (\(\Delta t_k \to 0\)), the legs become:

\[ \text{ProtPV} = (1 - R) \lambda \int_0^T e^{-(r + \lambda) u} du = (1 - R) \lambda \frac{1 - e^{-(r + \lambda) T}}{r + \lambda} \] \[ \text{PremPV} = s \int_0^T e^{-(r + \lambda) u} du = s \frac{1 - e^{-(r + \lambda) T}}{r + \lambda} \]

Equating \(\text{ProtPV} = \text{PremPV}\) yields the exact relationship:

\[ S_{\text{par}} = \lambda (1 - R) \]

For an investment-grade firm with default intensity \(\lambda = 180\text{ bp}\) and recovery \(R = 40\%\), the continuous rule of thumb gives \(S_{\text{par}} \approx 180 \times 0.60 = 108.0\text{ bp}\). Quarterly payment discrete compounding adjusts this par spread upward by approximately \(0.4\text{ bp}\) to \(108.4\text{ bp}\).

3. Post-Big Bang Conventions & Mark-to-Market Valuation

Prior to April 2009, CDS contracts traded at bespoke running spreads equal to the prevailing par spread, with zero upfront exchange at trade inception. Following the ISDA Big Bang protocol (Hull 2021), North American and European corporate CDS contracts trade with fixed standard coupons \(c_{\text{fixed}} \in \{100\text{ bp}, 500\text{ bp}\}\).

Definition 1.3 (Upfront Fee & Unwind Value)

When a CDS contract is transacted with fixed running coupon \(c_{\text{fixed}}\), the protection buyer pays (or receives) an upfront fee at inception to compensate for the deviation between the running coupon and the par spread:

\[ \text{Upfront} = \text{ProtPV} - \text{PremPV}(c_{\text{fixed}}) = (S_{\text{par}} - c_{\text{fixed}}) \cdot \text{RPV01} \cdot 10000 \]

If \(S_{\text{par}} > c_{\text{fixed}}\), the contract trades at a premium; the buyer pays an upfront fee to the seller. If \(S_{\text{par}} < c_{\text{fixed}}\), the seller pays the buyer upfront.

Lab 1 · Mark-to-Market CDS Valuation and Risky Duration

We hypothesize that for a 5-year CDS with default intensity \(\lambda = 180\text{ bp}\), recovery \(R = 40\%\), and risk-free rate \(r = 3\%\), the exact quarterly par spread is \(108.40\text{ bp}\). Trading at a fixed running coupon of \(100\text{ bp}\) generates an upfront fee of \(+0.37\%\) of notional, while the continuous formula gives \(108.00\text{ bp}\). 

"""Lab CR2-L01-1: Single-name CDS valuation, running spread, upfront fee, and risky PV01.

Demonstrates mark-to-market valuation of a credit default swap under constant hazard rate,
calculating premium leg PV, default protection leg PV, upfront fee, and risky PV01.
Standard library only; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    """Standard normal cumulative distribution function."""
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def main() -> None:
    # CDS contract parameters
    tenor = 5.0
    coupon_freq = 4
    dt = 1.0 / coupon_freq
    num_periods = round(tenor * coupon_freq)

    # Market observables
    hazard_rate = 0.0180  # 180 bps default intensity
    recovery = 0.40  # 40% senior unsecured recovery
    r = 0.03  # 3% continuously compounded risk-free rate
    quoted_running_spread_bps = 100.0  # standard 100 bp post-Big Bang coupon

    times = [(k + 1) * dt for k in range(num_periods)]

    # Compute premium leg (with default accrual) and protection leg
    prem_pv01 = 0.0
    prot_pv = 0.0

    print("Quarterly Cash Flow Schedule (Tenor = 5Y, lambda = 180 bp, R = 40%):")
    print("  Quarter | Time (yr) | Discount D(t) | Survival Q(t) | Default Prob | Risky PV01 (bp)")
    print("----------+-----------+---------------+---------------+--------------+----------------")

    for k, tk in enumerate(times):
        t_prev = times[k - 1] if k > 0 else 0.0
        df_k = math.exp(-r * tk)
        df_mid = math.exp(-r * 0.5 * (t_prev + tk))
        q_prev = math.exp(-hazard_rate * t_prev)
        q_k = math.exp(-hazard_rate * tk)
        dp_k = q_prev - q_k

        # 1 bp premium leg component
        step_pv01 = 0.0001 * (dt * df_k * q_k + 0.5 * dt * df_mid * dp_k)
        prem_pv01 += step_pv01
        prot_pv += (1.0 - recovery) * df_mid * dp_k

        if (k + 1) in [1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20]:
            print(
                f"    {k + 1:2d}    |   {tk:4.2f}    |    {df_k:6.4f}     |    "
                f"{q_k:6.4f}     |    {dp_k:6.4f}    |     {step_pv01 * 10000:6.3f}"
            )

    running_c = quoted_running_spread_bps / 10000.0
    premium_leg_pv = (running_c / 0.0001) * prem_pv01
    upfront_fee = prot_pv - premium_leg_pv
    par_spread_bps = (prot_pv / (prem_pv01 * 10000.0)) * 10000.0

    # Theoretical continuous approximation: par_spread ~ lambda * (1 - R)
    approx_par_bps = hazard_rate * (1.0 - recovery) * 10000.0

    print("\nValuation Summary (Notional = 10000000):")
    print(f"  Risky PV01 (Risky Duration): {prem_pv01 * 10000:6.4f} years")
    print(f"  Protection Leg PV:           {prot_pv * 10_000_000:10.2f}")
    print(f"  Premium Leg PV (100 bp):     {premium_leg_pv * 10_000_000:10.2f}")
    print(f"  Upfront Fee (Buyer pays):    {upfront_fee * 10_000_000:10.2f} ({upfront_fee * 100:5.2f}%)")
    print(f"  Exact Par CDS Spread:        {par_spread_bps:6.2f} bp")
    err_bps = par_spread_bps - approx_par_bps
    print(f"  Continuous Approximation:    {approx_par_bps:6.2f} bp (error = {err_bps:+.2f} bp)")

    # Assertions on financial identities
    assert prem_pv01 > 0.0
    assert prot_pv > 0.0
    assert abs(prot_pv - (par_spread_bps / 10000.0 / 0.0001) * prem_pv01) < 1e-10


main()

4. Where the Canon Breaks

The standard CDS valuation canon breaks down when counterparty credit risk cannot be decoupled from reference entity default risk (Duffie & Singleton 1999). If the protection seller defaults concurrently with the reference entity—termed wrong-way risk—the protection payment fails to materialize in the exact state where it was purchased to provide insurance. This vulnerability triggered the failure of AIG in September 2008, as AIG had sold uncollateralized CDS protection on senior subprime CDO tranches.

Furthermore, physical settlement rules create a cheapest-to-deliver option: when multiple debt obligations of the reference entity qualify for delivery, the protection buyer delivers the bond trading at the deepest market discount, depressing realized recovery below the expected auction recovery rate.

Misconceptions

  • A credit default swap is equivalent to an insurance policy and subject to state insurance regulations.

    CDS contracts are bilateral OTC derivatives, not insurance policies. Unlike insurance, the protection buyer is not required to hold an insurable interest (an underlying cash bond position). "Naked CDS" allows market participants to establish synthetic short positions or hedge macro credit exposures without owning the underlying reference debt.

  • The CDS par spread equals the reference entity's physical probability of default.

    The par spread reflects the risk-neutral expected default loss \(\lambda (1 - R)\) under measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), augmented by systematic credit risk premia, jump-to-default risk premia, and liquidity premia. The physical probability of default \(\Prob(\tau \le t)\) under historical measure \(\Prob\) is substantially lower than the risk-neutral default probability implied by market CDS quotes.

  • When a credit event occurs, the CDS contract immediately terminates with zero further premium payment.

    Under standard ISDA contract rules, the protection buyer is legally obligated to pay accrued premium from the preceding coupon date up to the exact credit event determination date. Neglecting this default accrual systematically understates the premium leg value.

Problem set

A 3-year CDS on reference entity Omega Corp trades at a par spread of 150 bp. Coupons are paid quarterly with recovery \(R = 40\%\) and flat risk-free rate \(r = 4\%\). Calculate the continuous-time default intensity approximation \(\lambda\) and compare it with the exact 1-year survival probability \(Q(1)\).

Worked solution

Under the continuous approximation, \(S_{\text{par}} \approx \lambda (1 - R)\). Setting \(0.0150 = \lambda (1 - 0.40)\) yields \(\lambda = \frac{0.0150}{0.60} = 0.0250\) (250 bp intensity). The 1-year survival probability is \(Q(1) = \exp(-\lambda \cdot 1) = \exp(-0.0250) \approx 0.97531\). Under exact quarterly discrete accrual, \(\lambda\) will be slightly lower at approximately 248.8 bp to account for positive discounting within the year.

An investor buys $25,000,000 notional of 5-year CDS on Titan Inc. with a fixed running coupon of 100 bp. The current break-even par spread is 165 bp, and the contract's risky PV01 is 4.3500 years. Calculate the upfront fee payable at trade inception in dollars.

Worked solution

The upfront fee per dollar of notional is \(\text{Upfront} = (S_{\text{par}} - c_{\text{fixed}}) \cdot \text{RPV01} = (0.0165 - 0.0100) \times 4.3500 = 0.0065 \times 4.3500 = 0.028275\) (2.8275% of notional). Multiplying by the contract notional gives \(\text{Upfront} = \$25,000,000 \times 0.028275 = \$706,875\). Because \(S_{\text{par}} > c_{\text{fixed}}\), the protection buyer pays this upfront fee to the protection seller.

Suppose a reference entity defaults exactly 45 days into a 90-day quarterly coupon period on a $10,000,000 CDS with a 500 bp running coupon. Calculate the accrued premium payment owed by the protection buyer on the default settlement date.

Worked solution

The running coupon rate is \(s = 0.0500\). The fraction of the coupon period elapsed is \(\frac{45}{360} = 0.125\) years (or \(\frac{45}{90} \times 0.25 = 0.125\)). Accrued premium is \(N \cdot s \cdot \Delta t_{\text{accrued}} = \$10,000,000 \times 0.0500 \times 0.125 = \$62,500\). This payment is netted against the protection payout \((1 - R) \cdot N\) in auction settlement.

Prove that in the limit as maturity \(T \to 0\), the break-even par CDS spread \(S_{\text{par}}(T)\) approaches \(\lambda_0 (1 - R)\), where \(\lambda_0\) is the instantaneous spot hazard rate.

Worked solution

As \(T \to 0\), \(\text{ProtPV}(T) = (1 - R) \int_0^T D(u) \lambda(u) e^{-\int_0^u \lambda(s)ds} du = (1 - R) \lambda_0 T + o(T)\), since \(D(0) = 1\) and \(Q(0) = 1\). Similarly, \(\text{PremPV}(T) = s \int_0^T D(u) e^{-\int_0^u \lambda(s)ds} du = s T + o(T)\). Equating \(\text{ProtPV} = \text{PremPV}\) yields \(s T = (1 - R) \lambda_0 T + o(T)\). Dividing by \(T\) and taking the limit \(T \to 0\) gives \(\lim_{T \to 0} S_{\text{par}}(T) = \lambda_0 (1 - R)\).

Prove that the risky PV01 of a defaultable claim is strictly less than the riskless annuity of identical maturity and payment frequency whenever \(\lambda(t) > 0\).

Worked solution

The riskless annuity is \(\text{Annuity}_{\text{rf}} = \sum_{k=1}^M \Delta t_k D(t_k)\). The risky PV01 is \(\text{RPV01} = \sum_{k=1}^M \Delta t_k D(t_k) Q(t_k) + \sum_{k=1}^M \frac{\Delta t_k}{2} D(t_k^*) [Q(t_{k-1}) - Q(t_k)]\). Since \(\lambda(t) > 0\), survival probability \(Q(t) < 1\) for all \(t > 0\). Writing \(\text{RPV01} < \sum_{k=1}^M \Delta t_k D(t_k) Q(t_{k-1})\) and noting that \(D(t) Q(t)\) is strictly decreasing guarantees that each discounted cash flow is strictly bounded above by its riskless counterpart.

Show that an increase in the assumed recovery rate \(R\) strictly increases the bootstrapped hazard rate \(\lambda\) for a fixed observed market par spread \(S_{\text{par}}\).

Worked solution

The protection leg payoff upon default is \((1 - R)\). For a fixed observed par spread \(S_{\text{par}}\), the no-arbitrage identity equates \(\text{ProtPV}(\lambda, R) = S_{\text{par}} \cdot \text{PremPV}(\lambda)\). Differentiating implicitly with respect to \(R\): \(\frac{\partial \text{ProtPV}}{\partial R} = -\int_0^T D(u) dQ(u) < 0\). To maintain equality when \(R\) increases, \(\text{ProtPV}\) must increase through an increase in default probability, meaning \(\frac{\partial \lambda}{\partial R} > 0\). Intuitively, if recovery is higher, each default delivers a smaller loss, so default must occur with higher intensity to match the market spread.

Empirical studies (Collin-Dufresne et al. 2001) show that changes in market CDS spreads are driven heavily by an aggregate systemic factor rather than firm-specific accounting news. Formulate a regression specification to test whether daily CDS spread changes are dominated by market-wide credit factors versus firm-specific equity returns.

Worked solution

Estimate the panel regression \(\Delta S_{i,t} = \alpha_i + \beta_1 \Delta S_{\text{CDX},t} + \beta_2 r_{i,t} + \beta_3 \Delta \sigma_{i,t} + \beta_4 \Delta y_{10,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}\), where \(\Delta S_{\text{CDX},t}\) is the change in the investment-grade CDX index, \(r_{i,t}\) is firm \(i\)'s stock return, and \(\Delta \sigma_{i,t}\) is implied equity volatility. In empirical corporate credit samples, \(\Delta S_{\text{CDX},t}\) accounts for over 60% of the explained variance (\(R^2\)), confirming the dominant role of aggregate credit risk premia.

Examine how the 2009 Big Bang protocol standardized quarterly coupon payment dates to the 20th of March, June, September, and December. Why did this date convention enhance secondary market liquidity for single-name contracts?

Worked solution

Prior to April 2009, contracts matured on arbitrary rolling dates. Standardizing maturities to IMM dates (March 20, June 20, September 20, December 20) and fixing running coupons at 100 bp or 500 bp concentrated trading into fungible contracts. This permitted multi-party portfolio compression, central clearing via clearinghouses (ICE Clear Credit), and eliminated the need to negotiate bespoke running spreads on every trade unwind.

In the credit event auction for Lehman Brothers in October 2008, the final clearing price established a recovery rate of 8.625%. Calculate the net protection payout percentage on Lehman CDS contracts and discuss its implications for CDS protection sellers.

Worked solution

With recovery \(R = 8.625\%\), the loss given default was \(1 - 0.08625 = 91.375\%\). For every $10,000,000 of CDS notional, protection sellers were required to transfer $9,137,500 in cash to protection buyers. This auction demonstrated that auction-based cash settlement could function orderly during massive systemic bankruptcies without physical delivery gridlock.

Evaluate the impact of mandatory central clearing for single-name CDS. How does the replacement of bilateral counterparty exposures with a central counterparty (CCP) alter wrong-way risk and systemic stability?

Worked solution

Central clearing multilateralizes netting, significantly reducing gross counterparty exposure. The CCP enforces daily variation margin in cash and mutualizes residual default risk via initial margin and a default waterfall fund. However, as shown by Duffie & Singleton (1999), clearing concentrates systemic risk at the CCP itself; failure of the CCP's risk models during extreme correlated defaults could trigger catastrophic liquidity shortages across member banks.

Design a structural framework linking CDS upfront fees to firm balance sheet leverage. How does Merton's (1974) structural model provide an economic anchor for the risk-neutral hazard rate bootstrapped from CDS quotes?

Worked solution

In Merton's model, the risk-neutral default probability is \(\mathbb{Q}(\tau \le T) = \Phi(-d_2)\), where \(d_2 = \frac{\ln(V/D) + (r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_V^2)T}{\sigma_V \sqrt{T}}\). The implied hazard rate is \(\lambda(T) = -\frac{1}{T} \ln(1 - \Phi(-d_2))\). By equating the Merton-implied default probability with the CDS survival curve, one can invert observable equity prices and asset volatilities to test whether CDS markets price credit risk efficiently relative to underlying corporate assets.

Analyze the economic determinants of sovereign CDS contracts. Why do sovereign CDS spreads often exceed the yield spread of local currency sovereign debt over benchmark rates?

Worked solution

Sovereign CDS contracts trade almost exclusively in hard currency (USD or EUR). Local currency sovereign bonds carry inflation and currency debasement risk rather than explicit credit default risk (since the sovereign can print domestic currency). In contrast, sovereign CDS contracts insure against restructuring or default on foreign-currency external debt, reflecting true balance-sheet solvency and foreign reserve availability.

Retrieval quiz

  1. What are the standard fixed coupons used in North American and European corporate CDS trading post-Big Bang?

    Options
    The 2009 Big Bang protocol standardized trading around 100 bp (investment grade) and 500 bp (high yield) running coupons, settling price differences via upfront cash fees.

  2. How does the accrual-on-default payment affect the premium leg valuation of a CDS?

    Options
    The accrual term compensates the protection seller for credit protection provided between the previous coupon date and the default event, adding positive present value to the premium leg.

  3. If an entity's 5-year par CDS spread is 250 bp and a contract is written with a fixed running coupon of 100 bp, what is the upfront payment direction?

    Options
    Because the market par spread (250 bp) exceeds the contractual running coupon (100 bp), the ongoing coupon underpays for protection; the buyer must pay an upfront fee to make the net present value zero.

  4. What is the relationship between the risky PV01 (risky duration) and the risk-free annuity of the same maturity?

    Options
    Risky PV01 weights future payments by both risk-free discount factors \(D(t)\) and survival probabilities \(Q(t) < 1\), strictly reducing the present value below the riskless annuity.

  5. Under the continuous-time intensity model, if a firm's hazard rate is \(\lambda = 0.0200\) and recovery is \(R = 0.40\), what is the continuous par spread?

    Options
    The continuous par spread formula is \(S_{\text{par}} = \lambda(1 - R) = 0.0200 \times (1 - 0.40) = 0.0120\), or 120 basis points.

  6. How did the 2009 Big Bang protocol resolve the delivery disputes that historically plagued physical settlement?

    Options
    ISDA auction settlement determines a single transparent market clearing recovery price for all market participants, eliminating short squeezes on physical deliverable bonds.

  7. What constitutes "wrong-way risk" in a credit default swap contract?

    Options
    Wrong-way risk occurs when credit risk of the reference entity is positively correlated with the credit risk of the counterparty, leaving the buyer unhedged when protection is most needed.

  8. Which dates are the standard quarterly maturity dates for CDS contracts under ISDA rules?

    Options
    ISDA contracts mature on the 20th of March, June, September, and December, aligning credit default swap maturities worldwide.
