The Gilchrist–Zakrajšek Credit Spread

Macroeconomists historically relied on index-level bond yield spreads, such as the Moody's Baa–Aaa spread, to assess credit conditions. However, commercial bond indices suffer from severe composition biases, maturity drift, and embedded call option distortions. The Gilchrist–Zakrajšek (2012) framework constructs bottom-up micro credit spreads matched to synthetic Treasury benchmarks, creating an uncorrupted barometer of macro-financial risk.

1Failures of Conventional Aggregate Credit Indices

For decades, empirical studies of credit markets utilized aggregate yield indices published by rating agencies or investment banks. Gilchrist and Zakrajšek (2012) demonstrated that these indices introduce substantial measurement noise due to three structural flaws:

  • Maturity Drift: A 10-year corporate bond index comprises issues whose remaining maturities range between 7 and 10 years, shifting systematically over time as issuers refinance or ratings transition.
  • Duration Mismatch: Subtracting a constant-maturity Treasury (CMT) yield from a corporate bond yield ignores the coupon effect: higher-coupon corporate debt has shorter duration than par Treasuries, biasing spread changes when the yield curve slopes upward or shifts.
  • Embedded Option Distortions: Many corporate bonds contain call provisions allowing early redemption. In standard indices, call premia are conflated with credit risk.

2Synthetic Treasury Curve Matching

To eliminate maturity and duration mismatch, the GZ methodology prices a hypothetical, synthetic risk-free security for every individual corporate coupon bond traded in secondary markets.

Definition 2.1 (Gilchrist–Zakrajšek Credit Spread)

Let bond \(k\) of firm \(i\) at time \(t\) have contractual cash flows \(\{C_{k, m}\}_{m=1}^{M}\) on future dates \(\{t + \tau_m\}_{m=1}^{M}\). The gz-credit-spread (Gilchrist–Zakrajšek credit spread) is the difference between the actual secondary market corporate bond yield to maturity \(y_{it}^k\) and the yield of a synthetic Treasury security \(y_{it}^{k, f}\):

\[ s_{it}^k = y_{it}^k - y_{it}^{k, f} \]

where the synthetic Treasury price \(P_{it}^{k, f}\) is obtained by discounting each cash flow along the continuous Svensson (1994) risk-free zero-coupon yield curve \(y_t^f(\tau)\):

\[ P_{it}^{k, f} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} C_{k, m} \exp\left(-y_t^f(\tau_m) \tau_m\right) \]

and \(y_{it}^{k, f}\) is the unique internal rate of return solving \(\sum_{m=1}^{M} C_{k, m} \exp\left(-y_{it}^{k, f} \tau_m\right) = P_{it}^{k, f}\).

Theorem 2.1 (Svensson Parametric Zero Curve)

The instantaneous forward rate \(f_t(\tau)\) and continuously compounded zero-coupon yield \(y_t^f(\tau)\) for maturity \(\tau > 0\) under the Gürkaynak, Sack, and Wright (2007) implementation of the Svensson (1994) family are:

\[ y_t^f(\tau) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}}{\tau/\tau_1}\right] + \beta_2 \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}}{\tau/\tau_1} - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}\right] + \beta_3 \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_2}}{\tau/\tau_2} - e^{-\tau/\tau_2}\right] \]

where \(\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3\) govern the level, slope, first curvature (hump), and second curvature (hump), and \(\tau_1, \tau_2 > 0\) determine decay velocity and peak locations.

Proof

The Svensson instantaneous forward rate is defined as:

\[ f_t(u) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 e^{-u/\tau_1} + \beta_2 \frac{u}{\tau_1} e^{-u/\tau_1} + \beta_3 \frac{u}{\tau_2} e^{-u/\tau_2} \]

By no-arbitrage in continuous time, the zero-coupon yield is the average integrated forward rate: \(y_t^f(\tau) = \frac{1}{\tau} \int_0^\tau f_t(u) du\). Integrating each term individually:

  1. \(\frac{1}{\tau} \int_0^\tau \beta_0 du = \beta_0\).
  2. \(\frac{\beta_1}{\tau} \int_0^\tau e^{-u/\tau_1} du = \beta_1 \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}}{\tau/\tau_1}\right]\).
  3. Using integration by parts on the third term, \(\int_0^\tau u e^{-u/\tau_1} du = \tau_1^2 [1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}(1 + \tau/\tau_1)]\), so dividing by \(\tau \tau_1\) yields \(\beta_2 \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}}{\tau/\tau_1} - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}\right]\).
  4. Applying the identical integration by parts to the fourth term with parameter \(\tau_2\) yields the second curvature component.

Summing the four terms establishes the closed-form zero-coupon yield function.

3Cross-Sectional Micro Spread Pricing Model

Having computed micro credit spreads \(s_{it}^k\) for thousands of individual corporate bonds across non-financial corporations, Gilchrist & Zakrajšek specify a log-linear panel regression to control for firm-level distance to default and bond indenture provisions:

\[ \ln s_{ijt}^k = \beta' x_{it}^k + \gamma \text{EDF}_{it} + \alpha_j + \theta_t + \epsilon_{it}^k \]

where \(\text{EDF}_{it}\) is the firm's Merton distance-to-default or expected default frequency (Bharath and Shumway 2008), \(x_{it}^k\) includes bond-level duration, coupon rate, issue age, call options, and amount outstanding, \(\alpha_j\) is a 2-digit industry fixed effect, and \(\theta_t\) is a period fixed effect.

Example 2.1 (Synthetic Treasury Pricing)

A corporate bond matures in 2 years with annual coupon \(6.0\%\) and par \(\$100\). The Svensson curve gives zero yields \(y^f(1) = 3.0\%\) and \(y^f(2) = 4.0\%\). The synthetic Treasury price is \(P^f = 6.0 e^{-0.03 \times 1} + 106.0 e^{-0.04 \times 2} = 6.0 \times 0.970446 + 106.0 \times 0.923116 = 5.823 + 97.850 = \$103.673\). Solving \(\$103.673 = 6.0 e^{-y^f} + 106.0 e^{-2 y^f}\) numerically gives synthetic yield \(y^f = 3.971\%\). If the corporate bond trades at yield \(y = 6.121\%\), the micro GZ spread is \(s = 6.121\% - 3.971\% = 2.150\%\) (215 basis points).

Lab 1 · Synthetic Treasury curve matching

Verifies that discounting corporate cash flows along a parametric Svensson zero curve extracts exact micro credit spreads free of duration mismatch across maturities.

"""Lab CR3-L02-1: Synthetic Treasury Matching and Gilchrist-Zakrajšek Credit Spread.

SIMULATED: constructs synthetic risk-free benchmark bond matching corporate cash flows
under a Svensson zero-coupon Treasury curve, demonstrating elimination of maturity
and coupon bias present in naive maturity-matched corporate credit spreads.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from typing import NamedTuple


class BondItem(NamedTuple):
    id: str
    face: float
    coupon: float
    mat: float
    mkt_price: float


def svensson_zero_yield(t: float, beta: list[float], tau: list[float]) -> float:
    """Evaluate Svensson (1994) zero-coupon Treasury yield at maturity t in years.

    beta: [beta0, beta1, beta2, beta3]
    tau: [tau1, tau2]
    """
    if t <= 1e-6:
        return beta[0] + beta[1]

    t_tau1 = t / tau[0]
    t_tau2 = t / tau[1]
    e1 = math.exp(-t_tau1)
    e2 = math.exp(-t_tau2)

    term1 = beta[0]
    term2 = beta[1] * ((1.0 - e1) / t_tau1)
    term3 = beta[2] * ((1.0 - e1) / t_tau1 - e1)
    term4 = beta[3] * ((1.0 - e2) / t_tau2 - e2)

    return term1 + term2 + term3 + term4


def bond_price_from_yield(face: float, coupon_rate: float, maturity: float, ytm: float, freq: int = 2) -> float:
    """Calculate dirty price of fixed-coupon bond given continuous/semi-annual yield."""
    n_periods = round(maturity * freq)
    coupon = (face * coupon_rate) / freq
    price = 0.0
    for i in range(1, n_periods + 1):
        t = i / freq
        df = 1.0 / ((1.0 + ytm / freq) ** (freq * t))
        cf = coupon + (face if i == n_periods else 0.0)
        price += cf * df
    return price


def bond_yield_to_maturity(face: float, coupon_rate: float, maturity: float, price: float, freq: int = 2) -> float:
    """Solve for internal rate of return / yield to maturity using Newton-Raphson."""
    y = coupon_rate
    for _ in range(30):
        p = bond_price_from_yield(face, coupon_rate, maturity, y, freq)
        dp = 0.0
        n_periods = round(maturity * freq)
        coupon = (face * coupon_rate) / freq
        for i in range(1, n_periods + 1):
            t = i / freq
            df = 1.0 / ((1.0 + y / freq) ** (freq * t + 1))
            cf = coupon + (face if i == n_periods else 0.0)
            dp -= t * cf * df
        diff = p - price
        if abs(diff) < 1e-9:
            break
        y -= diff / dp
    return y


def price_synthetic_treasury(
    face: float,
    coupon_rate: float,
    maturity: float,
    beta: list[float],
    tau: list[float],
    freq: int = 2,
) -> float:
    """Price synthetic Treasury security by discounting each cash flow at exact zero yield."""
    n_periods = round(maturity * freq)
    coupon = (face * coupon_rate) / freq
    price = 0.0
    for i in range(1, n_periods + 1):
        t = i / freq
        z_rate = svensson_zero_yield(t, beta, tau)
        # Continuous or semi-annual discounting
        df = math.exp(-z_rate * t)
        cf = coupon + (face if i == n_periods else 0.0)
        price += cf * df
    return price


def main() -> None:
    # Calibrated US Treasury parameters: upward-sloping curve
    beta_ust = [0.045, -0.020, 0.015, -0.010]
    tau_ust = [1.5, 6.0]

    # Sample of three corporate bonds with different coupons and maturities
    bonds = [
        BondItem("Bond A (High Coupon)", 100.0, 0.075, 5.0, 108.50),
        BondItem("Bond B (Low Coupon)", 100.0, 0.030, 5.0, 94.20),
        BondItem("Bond C (Long Mat)", 100.0, 0.055, 10.0, 98.70),
    ]

    print("Gilchrist-Zakrajsek (2012) Micro Credit Spread Construction:")
    print("  Bond ID              | Corp YTM (%) | Synth T-YTM (%) | GZ Spread (bp) | Naive TS Spread (bp)")
    print("-----------------------+--------------+-----------------+----------------+---------------------")

    for b in bonds:
        corp_ytm = bond_yield_to_maturity(b.face, b.coupon, b.mat, b.mkt_price)
        synth_price = price_synthetic_treasury(b.face, b.coupon, b.mat, beta_ust, tau_ust)
        synth_ytm = bond_yield_to_maturity(b.face, b.coupon, b.mat, synth_price)

        # GZ micro credit spread
        gz_spread_bp = (corp_ytm - synth_ytm) * 10000.0

        # Naive benchmark: single maturity zero rate
        naive_zero = svensson_zero_yield(b.mat, beta_ust, tau_ust)
        naive_spread_bp = (corp_ytm - naive_zero) * 10000.0

        c_pct = corp_ytm * 100
        s_pct = synth_ytm * 100
        print(
            f"  {b.id:20s} |    {c_pct:5.2f}%   |     {s_pct:5.2f}%     |    "
            f"{gz_spread_bp:5.1f}       |       {naive_spread_bp:5.1f}"
        )

        assert gz_spread_bp > 0.0


main()

4Where the Canon Breaks: The Option-Adjusted Spread Residual

While the GZ methodology substantially outperforms index-level spreads, it faces an analytical challenge when pricing bonds with complex embedded options (call provisions, make-whole calls, and sinking funds). Standard option-adjusted spread (OAS) models assume a specific interest rate diffusion process. In times of severe market turbulence, interest rate volatility jumps unpredictably, causing model-dependent call valuations to contaminate the estimated micro spread.

Misconceptions

  • Subtracting the 10-year Treasury yield from a 10-year corporate bond yield yields an accurate credit spread.

    A 10-year coupon-paying corporate bond has a Macauley and modified duration substantially below 10 years (typically 7 to 8 years) due to intermediate coupon cash flows. If the yield curve is upward sloping, the true risk-free rate matching the bond's cash flows is lower than the 10-year Treasury yield, causing the naive spread to significantly underestimate the true credit risk spread.

  • The Gilchrist–Zakrajšek spread is an equity market sentiment indicator.

    The GZ spread is derived exclusively from secondary market corporate bond transaction prices. While it correlates with equity volatility, it reflects pricing conditions and balance-sheet capacity in the fixed income and institutional debt markets.

  • The cross-sectional log-linear bond regression is designed to predict individual bond defaults.

    The regression does not predict default timing for specific bonds. Its econometric purpose is to isolate the portion of observed yield spreads attributable to firm-level credit risk (\(\text{EDF}\)) and contractual features, allowing researchers to isolate the systemic non-default residual risk premium.

Problem set

Mechanics

P1 · Synthetic Treasury Discounting

A corporate bond with annual coupons of \(\$8.00\) and principal \(\$100\) matures in \(T = 2\) years. If the Svensson zero yields are \(y^f(1) = 4.0\%\) and \(y^f(2) = 5.0\%\), compute the synthetic Treasury price \(P^f\) using continuous discounting.

Solution

The cash flows are \(\$8.00\) at \(t = 1\) and \(\$108.00\) at \(t = 2\). The synthetic price is \(P^f = 8.0 \times e^{-0.04 \times 1} + 108.0 \times e^{-0.05 \times 2} = 8.0 \times 0.960789 + 108.0 \times 0.904837 = 7.6863 + 97.7224 = \$105.4087\).

P2 · Micro Credit Spread Calculation

Suppose the corporate bond from Problem P1 trades at a yield to maturity of \(y = 7.25\%\). If the synthetic Treasury yield to maturity is \(y^f = 4.96\%\), calculate the micro credit spread in basis points.

Solution

The micro GZ credit spread is the direct arithmetic difference between the corporate bond yield and the synthetic benchmark yield: \(s = y - y^f = 0.0725 - 0.0496 = 0.0229\), which equals exactly \(229\) basis points.

P3 · Aggregate GZ Spread Average

A credit desk monitors a cross-section of 4 corporate bonds with micro spreads \(s_1 = 140\text{ bp}\), \(s_2 = 210\text{ bp}\), \(s_3 = 280\text{ bp}\), and \(s_4 = 330\text{ bp}\). Calculate the equal-weighted aggregate GZ credit spread.

Solution

The equal-weighted aggregate GZ spread is the arithmetic mean of the cross-section: \(S^{\text{GZ}} = \frac{1}{4}(140 + 210 + 280 + 330) = \frac{960}{4} = 240\) basis points.

Proof

P4 · Proof of Synthetic Price Monotonicity

Prove that for any positive cash flows \(C_m > 0\), the synthetic Treasury price \(P^f(y) = \sum_{m=1}^M C_m e^{-y \tau_m}\) is strictly decreasing and strictly convex in the synthetic yield \(y\) for \(y > 0\).

Solution

Differentiating with respect to \(y\) yields \(\frac{dP^f}{dy} = -\sum_{m=1}^M \tau_m C_m e^{-y \tau_m}\). Because \(\tau_m > 0\), \(C_m > 0\), and \(e^{-y \tau_m} > 0\), every summand is strictly positive, ensuring \(\frac{dP^f}{dy} < 0\) strictly (strictly decreasing). Differentiating a second time yields \(\frac{d^2 P^f}{dy^2} = \sum_{m=1}^M \tau_m^2 C_m e^{-y \tau_m} > 0\), confirming strict convexity. By the intermediate value theorem, there exists a unique positive yield \(y^f\) matching any positive price \(P^f\).

P5 · Asymptotic Svensson Yield Behavior

Prove that under the Svensson (1994) parametric zero-coupon formula, the short-rate limit as \(\tau \to 0\) is \(\beta_0 + \beta_1\) and the long-term infinite maturity limit as \(\tau \to \infty\) is \(\beta_0\).

Solution

Consider the zero-coupon yield function \(y_t^f(\tau)\). As \(\tau \to 0\), applying L'Hôpital's rule to the slope term gives \(\lim_{\tau \to 0} \frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_1}}{\tau/\tau_1} = 1\). The curvature terms evaluate to \(\lim_{\tau \to 0} \left[\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_i}}{\tau/\tau_i} - e^{-\tau/\tau_i}\right] = 1 - 1 = 0\). Thus, \(\lim_{\tau \to 0} y_t^f(\tau) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times 1 + 0 + 0 = \beta_0 + \beta_1\). Conversely, as \(\tau \to \infty\), \(\tau/\tau_i \to \infty\), so \(\frac{1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_i}}{\tau/\tau_i} \to 0\) and \(e^{-\tau/\tau_i} \to 0\). All decaying terms vanish, leaving \(\lim_{\tau \to \infty} y_t^f(\tau) = \beta_0\).

P6 · Unbiasedness of Micro Spread Aggregation

Let individual micro spreads be \(s_{it} = S_t^{\text{GZ}} + \eta_{it}\), where \(\E[\eta_{it} \mid S_t^{\text{GZ}}] = 0\) and \(\text{Cov}(\eta_{it}, \eta_{jt}) = 0\) for \(i \ne j\). Prove that the sample mean \(\bar{s}_t = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_t} s_{it}\) is an unbiased and consistent estimator of the latent aggregate credit spread as \(N_t \to \infty\).

Solution

Taking expectations, \(\E[\bar{s}_t] = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \E[S_t^{\text{GZ}} + \eta_{it}] = S_t^{\text{GZ}} + \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \E[\eta_{it}] = S_t^{\text{GZ}}\), proving unbiasedness. The variance of the sample estimator is \(\text{Var}(\bar{s}_t) = \frac{1}{N_t^2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \text{Var}(\eta_{it}) = \frac{\sigma_\eta^2}{N_t}\). By Chebyshev's inequality, \(\lim_{N_t \to \infty} \text{Var}(\bar{s}_t) = 0\), establishing convergence in probability \(\bar{s}_t \xrightarrow{p} S_t^{\text{GZ}}\) (consistency).

Empirical

P7 · Quantifying Duration Bias in Naive Spreads

A 10-year corporate bond has coupon \(8\%\) and duration \(7.2\) years. The 10-year CMT yield is \(4.50\%\), while the 7.2-year zero rate is \(4.00\%\). If the bond yield is \(6.20\%\), calculate the naive spread and the duration-matched spread, and identify the magnitude of the bias.

Solution

The naive spread subtracting the 10-year CMT yield is \(s_{\text{naive}} = 6.20\% - 4.50\% = 1.70\%\) (\(170\) bp). The duration-matched spread is \(s_{\text{matched}} = 6.20\% - 4.00\% = 2.20\%\) (\(220\) bp). The naive spread underestimates the true credit spread by \(220 - 170 = 50\) basis points due to upward yield curve slope.

P8 · Panel Regression of Micro Spreads on Distance-to-Default

In a cross-sectional regression \(\ln s_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EDF}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{Dur}_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\), the estimated coefficient on Merton EDF is \(\hat{\beta}_1 = 0.085\) with standard error \(0.007\). Interpret the t-statistic and the economic effect of a 5-percentage-point increase in default probability.

Solution

The t-statistic is \(t = 0.085 / 0.007 \approx 12.14\), indicating statistical significance at \(p < 0.001\). A 5-percentage-point increase in EDF corresponds to \(\Delta \ln s = 0.085 \times 5 = 0.425\), representing an approximate \(42.5\%\) relative increase in the firm's credit spread (\(\exp(0.425) - 1 \approx +53.0\%\)), confirming that default risk is a powerful driver of corporate yields.

P9 · Aggregate GZ Spread vs Moody's Baa Spread

During a market expansion, the Moody's Baa spread is \(180\text{ bp}\) while the GZ spread is \(130\text{ bp}\). Explain why the Moody's index is substantially wider during tranquil periods.

Solution

Moody's Baa index consists of long-maturity bonds (averaging over 20 years to maturity), while the GZ sample includes the full distribution of intermediate maturities (average maturity 6 to 8 years). Because credit spreads typically slope upward with maturity for investment-grade issuers, the 20-year Moody's index mechanically carries a larger term premium and liquidity premium than the maturity-matched GZ cross-section.

Research

P10 · Eliminating Call Option Distortions

Explain how Gilchrist and Zakrajšek handle callable bonds in their sample without discarding half of the corporate bond universe.

Solution

Rather than dropping all callable bonds (which would severely reduce sample size and over-sample short-dated debt), GZ explicitly include callable indicator variables and estimated option-adjusted duration metrics in the cross-sectional regression vector \(x_{it}^k\). By controlling for the call feature and its interaction with the yield curve slope, the regression strips out the embedded option premium, preventing it from leaking into the residual risk premium.

P11 · Svensson vs Nelson–Siegel in Bond Pricing

Critically assess why the 6-parameter Svensson (1994) curve is preferred over the 4-parameter Nelson–Siegel (1987) curve when pricing synthetic Treasury benchmarks.

Solution

The standard Nelson–Siegel model permits only a single hump or trough in the term structure. In real Treasury markets, simultaneous short-rate expectations and long-term supply/pension demand effects frequently create complex double-hump or S-shaped yield curves. The additional curvature term \(\beta_3\) with decay \(\tau_2\) in the Svensson specification allows the model to fit both intermediate (3–7 year) and long-term (10–30 year) inflection points without introducing pricing anomalies.

P12 · Cross-Sectional Aggregation Weighting Schemes

Compare the properties of equal-weighted versus market-value-weighted aggregation of micro credit spreads in macroeconomic forecasting.

Solution

Market-value weighting weights large corporate issuers heavily, reflecting the aggregate dollar cost of corporate debt. However, equal weighting assigns equal importance to each distinct corporate balance sheet, providing a better reflection of pervasive credit constraints across the broader corporate sector. Gilchrist and Zakrajšek find that equal-weighted aggregation delivers slightly superior leading indicator properties for real GDP and employment, as distress among mid-sized firms often leads aggregate downturns.

Retrieval quiz

  1. How does the GZ methodology eliminate duration and maturity mismatch when computing corporate credit spreads?

    Options
    GZ discount every specific promised cash flow along the Svensson zero curve to price a hypothetical risk-free bond with the exact same payment schedule.

  2. In the Svensson (1994) yield curve model, what does the parameter \(\beta_0\) represent?

    Options
    As maturity \(\tau \to \infty\), all exponential decay terms approach zero, leaving \(\beta_0\) as the asymptotic long rate.

  3. Why does subtracting the 10-year Treasury yield from an 8% coupon 10-year corporate bond yield underestimate the true credit spread when the yield curve is upward sloping?

    Options
    With an upward-sloping curve, the effective risk-free discount rate for intermediate cash flows is lower than 10-year yield; subtracting the 10-year yield subtracts too high a rate, artificially compressing the spread.

  4. What role does the Expected Default Frequency (\(\text{EDF}\)) play in the GZ cross-sectional panel regression?

    Options
    Merton EDF acts as a firm-level control variable for expected default probability, enabling the decomposition of credit spreads into default and non-default components.

  5. Which empirical advantage does the equal-weighted GZ spread have over market-cap weighted credit indices in predicting recessions?

    Options
    Equal weighting treats each corporate balance sheet equally, reflecting credit conditions across mid-market firms that frequently experience credit contraction before mega-cap conglomerates.

  6. What additional parameter does the Svensson (1994) curve include relative to the Nelson–Siegel (1987) curve?

    Options
    Svensson adds a second hump term with parameters \(\beta_3\) and \(\tau_2\), enabling the model to fit flexible yield curve shapes.

  7. Why are callable corporate bonds explicitly retained in the GZ sample rather than dropped?

    Options
    Because a substantial portion of US corporate debt contains call features, dropping them would create massive selection bias; GZ retain them and control for call features econometrically.

  8. A corporate bond yield is \(7.25\%\) and its synthetic Treasury benchmark yield is \(4.96\%\). What is the micro credit spread in basis points?

    The credit spread is \(s = 7.25\% - 4.96\% = 2.29\%\) or exactly 229 basis points.

  9. Four bonds have micro spreads of 140, 210, 280, and 330 basis points. What is their equal-weighted aggregate credit spread in basis points?

    The arithmetic mean is \((140 + 210 + 280 + 330) / 4 = 960 / 4 = 240\) basis points.
