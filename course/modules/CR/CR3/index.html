Credit Cycles & Macro-Credit

Macroeconomic credit fluctuations, the financial accelerator mechanism, micro-level bond pricing and synthetic Treasury curve matching, decomposition into expected default risk and the excess bond premium, credit-market sentiment reversals, and structural underwriting dynamics in leveraged loans and private credit.

Prerequisites: CR1, S3
Unlocks: CR4
Project: Credit-cycle indicator & recession forecasting (autograder and rubric)

30 h lessons across 5 lessons @ 6 h · 20 h project (40.0% of the 50 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CR3 you can:

  1. Formulate the macro-financial accelerator mechanism and compute balance-sheet feedback amplification under endogenous collateral constraints.
  2. Construct the micro-level Gilchrist–Zakrajšek (2012) credit spread by pricing synthetic risk-free benchmark bonds from parametric Svensson yield curves.
  3. Decompose corporate credit spreads into expected default risk and the non-default excess bond premium (EBP) using cross-sectional distance-to-default controls.
  4. Quantify credit-market sentiment using high-yield issuance shares and estimate multi-period predictive spread reversals and real activity contractions (López-Salido et al. 2017).
  5. Model recovery waterfalls and loss-given-default for covenant-lite leveraged loans and private-credit tranches subject to EBITDA add-back distortions.
  6. Evaluate out-of-sample macro recession probability forecasts comparing credit-cycle indicators against the Estrella–Mishkin term spread.

Prerequisites

CR3 assumes structural and reduced-form credit foundations from CR1, time-series econometrics from S3, and their prerequisite closure. It uses these concepts directly:

  • From CR1: Merton structural model, distance to default, and the credit-spread puzzle.
  • From S3: stationarity, vector autoregression, and state-space systems.
  • From mathematical foundations: Ordinary least squares estimation, logit and probit link functions, and multivariate numerical optimization.

Placement diagnostic

Pass this diagnostic (score \(\ge 80\%\) and answer both skill-testing items correctly) to place out of rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder.

  1. In the Gilchrist & Zakrajšek (2012) methodology, how is the micro-level credit spread \(s_{it}\) of an individual corporate coupon bond defined?

    Options
    Gilchrist and Zakrajšek construct a synthetic risk-free benchmark bond for every corporate issue by discounting each promised coupon and principal payment along the continuous Svensson Treasury zero-coupon curve, completely eliminating maturity and duration mismatch.

  2. What distinguishes the Excess Bond Premium (\(\text{EBP}_t\)) from the predicted default-risk component (\(\hat{S}_t\)) of the aggregate GZ spread?

    Options
    The GZ decomposition separates the spread into the portion explained by firm-level distance-to-default and contract terms (\(\hat{S}_t\)), and the residual risk premium (\(\text{EBP}_t\)), which captures intermediary risk aversion and financial sector capitalization.

  3. According to López-Salido, Stein, & Zakrajšek (2017), what pattern emerges when credit-market sentiment is unusually elevated (e.g., elevated high-yield issuance share and compressed spreads)?

    Options
    López-Salido et al. document predictable boom-bust dynamics: exuberant sentiment forecasts subsequent credit spread widening, which in turn acts as an adverse contractionary shock on real output.

  4. Why do covenant-lite (“cov-lite”) leveraged loans exhibit lower historical recovery rates during insolvency than traditional maintenance-covenant loans?

    Options
    Maintenance covenants trigger early technical default while the borrower still has viable asset value; without them, borrowers burn through enterprise value before reaching a liquidity crisis, depressing final recoveries.

  5. In a cross-sectional GZ decomposition, an aggregate corporate bond spread is 250 basis points. The micro-level regression fitted value \(\hat{S}_t\) based on firm distance-to-default and bond duration is 90 basis points. What is the value of the Excess Bond Premium \(\text{EBP}_t\) in basis points?

    By definition of the additive decomposition, \(\text{EBP}_t = S_t^{\text{GZ}} - \hat{S}_t = 250 - 90 = 160\) basis points.

Lesson map

The 30 lesson study hours are split across 5 lessons of 6 hours each:

  1. Lesson 1 · The Credit Cycle & Financial Accelerator

    Macroeconomic credit fluctuations, endogenous collateral constraints, external finance premia, and balance-sheet feedback loops.

  2. Lesson 2 · The Gilchrist–Zakrajšek Credit Spread

    Micro-level secondary bond pricing, parametric Svensson synthetic Treasury curve matching, and cross-sectional spread aggregation.

  3. Lesson 3 · The Excess Bond Premium as an Economic Leading Indicator

    Decomposing spreads into expected default risk and intermediary risk tolerance, and macro forecasting horse races.

  4. Lesson 4 · Credit-Market Sentiment and Predictable Reversals

    Extrapolative sentiment dynamics, high-yield issuance shares, two-stage predictive regressions, and macroeconomic boom-bust cycles.

  5. Lesson 5 · The Leveraged-Loan and Private-Credit Cycle

    Collateralized Loan Obligations (CLOs), covenant-lite underwriting erosion, EBITDA add-backs, and post-crisis recovery dynamics.
