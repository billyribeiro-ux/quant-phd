Structural & Reduced-Form Credit Models

Continuous-time corporate debt pricing, endogenous capital structure, default intensity jump processes, empirical distance-to-default forecasting, and corporate credit spread decomposition.

Prerequisites: CF2, SC3
Unlocks: CR2, CR3, STUDIO-CR
Project: Atlas A14: credit module (autograder)

66 h lessons across 6 lessons @ 11 h · 24 h project (26.7% of the 90 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of CR1 you can:

  1. Compute equity and risky debt values using the Merton (1974) option pricing analogy and analyze credit spread term structures across solvency and distress regimes.
  2. Derive the Black–Cox (1976) first-passage hitting time distribution and evaluate credit spreads under continuous safety covenant monitoring.
  3. Calculate the endogenous default boundary and optimal capital structure in the Leland (1994) model by applying the smooth pasting optimality condition.
  4. Value defaultable zero-coupon bonds under reduced-form intensity models (Jarrow–Turnbull 1995, Duffie–Singleton 1999) using Recovery of Market Value and Recovery of Face Value.
  5. Invert the simultaneous Merton/KMV nonlinear two-equation system to estimate unobservable firm asset values and evaluate default prediction power against Bharath–Shumway (2008) naive distance to default.
  6. Decompose empirical corporate bond yield spreads into expected default loss, credit risk premium, and non-credit residual components to quantify the credit-spread puzzle (Collin-Dufresne et al. 2001, Huang & Huang 2012).

Prerequisites

CR1 assumes CF2 (Cost of Capital & Capital Structure), SC3 (Change of Measure), and their prerequisite closure. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From CF2: Modigliani–Miller propositions, interest tax shield, costs of financial distress, trade-off theory, and WACC.
  • From SC3: Girsanov's theorem, market price of risk, martingale representation theorem, and change of numeraire.
  • From earlier foundations: geometric Brownian motion (SC2), Itô's lemma (SC2), first-passage time (SC1), maximum likelihood estimation (S1), and principal component analysis (M3).

Placement diagnostic

Pass this diagnostic (score \(\ge 80\%\) and answer both skill-testing items correctly) to place out of rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder.

  1. In the Merton (1974) model, a firm has asset value \(V_0 = \$100\), single zero-coupon debt maturing in \(T = 1\) year with face value \(D = \$80\), risk-free rate \(r = 5\%\), and asset volatility \(\sigma_V = 20\%\). What is the exact balance-sheet articulation identity relating the market value of equity \(E_0\) and risky debt \(B_0\)?

    Options
    By no-arbitrage and definition of claims, equity is a call option \(E_T = \max(V_T - D, 0)\) and debt payoff is \(B_T = \min(V_T, D) = V_T - \max(V_T - D, 0)\). Summing payoffs yields \(E_T + B_T = V_T\) in every state, guaranteeing \(E_0 + B_0 = V_0\).

  2. Under the Duffie & Singleton (1999) Recovery of Market Value (RMV) convention with constant risk-free rate \(r\), constant default intensity \(\lambda\), and constant recovery rate \(R\), what is the credit spread \(s\) on a defaultable zero-coupon bond?

    Options
    Under RMV, the default-adjusted discount rate is \(R_t = r + \lambda (1 - R)\). The zero-coupon bond price is \(P(0, T) = \exp(-(r + \lambda (1 - R)) T)\), giving bond yield \(y = r + \lambda (1 - R)\) and credit spread \(s = y - r = \lambda (1 - R)\).

  3. According to Girsanov's theorem (SC3), how does a change of measure from the physical measure \(\Prob\) to the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) affect the drift and volatility of a continuous diffusion process?

    Options
    Girsanov's theorem preserves equivalent probability null sets and quadratic variation, meaning instantaneous volatility \(\sigma\) is unaffected, while the Brownian drift transforms from \(\mu\) to \(r\) by subtracting \(\lambda \sigma\).

  4. In trade-off theory (CF2), what determines the optimal capital structure of a levered corporation?

    Options
    Trade-off theory balances the tax benefits of deductible interest payments against deadweight bankruptcy and agency costs of financial distress.

  5. A corporate bond has an annual default intensity \(\lambda = 0.0200\) (200 basis points) and an expected recovery rate of market value \(R = 0.40\) (40%). Under Duffie-Singleton RMV, what is the credit spread in basis points?

    The credit spread is \(s = \lambda (1 - R) = 0.0200 \times (1 - 0.40) = 0.0120\), which equals exactly 120 basis points.

Lesson map

The 66 lesson study hours are split across 6 rigorous lessons of 11 hours each:

  1. Lesson 1 · The Merton (1974) Structural Model

    Firm assets as geometric Brownian motion, equity as a European call option, risky zero-coupon debt, credit spread term structures across solvency regimes, and the zero short-spread puzzle.

  2. Lesson 2 · First-Passage Time Credit Models: Black–Cox (1976)

    Safety covenants, early reorganization barriers, hitting time distributions of Brownian motion with drift, closed-form recovery discounting, and positive short-term spreads.

  3. Lesson 3 · Endogenous Default & Optimal Capital Structure: Leland (1994)

    Perpetual debt, corporate tax shields, bankruptcy costs, smooth pasting optimality conditions, endogenous default boundaries, and optimal debt capacity.

  4. Lesson 4 · Reduced-Form Credit Models & Intensity Pricing

    Default intensities and hazard rates, Cox processes, Jarrow–Turnbull and Duffie–Singleton pricing, Recovery of Market Value versus Recovery of Face Value, and affine credit spread term structures.

  5. Lesson 5 · Distance to Default & Empirical Implementation

    KMV simultaneous non-linear inversion for unobservable asset values, distance-to-default metrics, and the Bharath & Shumway (2008) naive default forecasting benchmark.

  6. Lesson 6 · The Credit-Spread Puzzle & Spread Decomposition

    Empirical breakdown of corporate yield spreads, Collin-Dufresne et al. (2001) systematic aggregate factor, Huang & Huang (2012) calibration, and non-credit residual attribution.
