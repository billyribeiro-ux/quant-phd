While liability-driven investment hedges long-term economic solvency, funding overlays through leveraged derivative contracts exposes institutions to acute liquidity risk. When sovereign bond yields experience sudden upward spikes, derivative overlays suffer massive variation margin calls. If liquid cash buffers are exhausted, funds are forced to liquidate bonds into illiquid markets, driving yields higher and provoking cascading margin demands. This lesson models the dynamics of this collateral spiral and evaluates central-bank liquidity backstops.

1Leverage, Margin Calls, and Liquidity Risk in LDI

As established in L05, liability-driven investment matches liability DV01 by entering into interest-rate swap overlays. However, bilateral and centrally cleared derivative contracts require continuous variation margining to eliminate counterparty credit risk. When long-term rates surge, receive-fixed swap contracts suffer marked-to-market losses that must be collateralized in cash within hours.

Definition 1.1 (LDI collateral spiral)

The LDI collateral spiral is an endogenous systemic feedback loop wherein an initial rise in sovereign bond yields triggers derivative variation margin calls on leveraged liability-driven investment funds, depleting their liquid cash buffers and compelling forced liquidations of underlying sovereign bonds, which depresses bond prices, drives yields higher, and generates self-reinforcing secondary margin calls.

Assumption 1.2 (the discrete collateral spiral model)

The collateral spiral operates through a sequence of discrete iterative steps \(r \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}\) parameterized as follows:

A1 (overlay position). The fund maintains an interest-rate swap overlay with notional \(N > 0\) cents and modified duration \(D_h \ge 0\) in hundredths of a year (so \(D_h / 100\) is duration in years). All monetary amounts are non-negative integer cents.

A2 (cumulative margin due). For a cumulative yield move of \(Y\) basis points, the cumulative margin due is:

\[ \mathrm{Due}(Y) = \mathrm{round\_half\_up}\left( \frac{N \cdot D_h \cdot Y}{10^6} \right) \]

where \(\mathrm{round\_half\_up}(x) = \lfloor x + 1/2 \rfloor\) in exact rational arithmetic. At step \(r\), the incremental margin call is \(M_r = \mathrm{Due}(Y_r) - \mathrm{Due}(Y_{r-1})\), with \(Y_{-1} = 0\) and \(Y_0 = s_0\) (the initial exogenous yield shock in basis points).

A3 (liquid buffer payment and forced sales). The fund holds an initial liquid cash buffer \(B_0 = B \ge 0\) cents. At step \(r\), the fund pays \(P_r = \min(M_r, B_r)\) from remaining buffer \(B_r\), updating buffer to \(B_{r+1} = B_r - P_r\). The unpaid margin call triggers an immediate forced sale of bonds: \(S_r = M_r - P_r\).

A4 (central-bank backstop). A central bank provides a liquidity backstop with total capacity \(G \ge 0\) cents, activated when cumulative yields reach a trigger threshold \(\bar{y}\) (\(Y_r \ge \bar{y}\)). When active, the backstop absorbs forced sales up to remaining capacity \(G_r\): \(A_r = \min(S_r, G_r)\), updating capacity to \(G_{r+1} = G_r - A_r\). Absorbed sales generate zero market price impact.

A5 (endogenous price impact and termination). Unabsorbed forced sales \(U_r = S_r - A_r\) generate market price impact of \(I_r = \mathrm{round\_half\_up}(\lambda U_r / 10^9)\) basis points, where \(\lambda \ge 0\) is the price-impact coefficient in basis points per \(10^9\) cents sold. Cumulative yields update to \(Y_{r+1} = Y_r + I_r\). The spiral terminates at the first step where \(S_r = 0\) or \(I_r = 0\), or after 50 steps.

2The Forced-Selling Threshold and Spiral Dynamics

Before any dynamic feedback occurs, an initial yield shock \(s_0\) must be sufficiently large to breach the fund's liquid buffer. If the initial margin call can be paid entirely from cash, no forced asset sales occur (\(S_0 = 0\)), price impact is zero (\(I_0 = 0\)), and the spiral halts immediately at step 0. We derive the exact threshold shock that triggers forced liquidations.

Proposition 2.1 (closed-form forced-selling threshold)

Under Assumption 1.2 with \(N D_h > 0\), the smallest non-negative integer yield shock \(s^*\) that exhausts the cash buffer and triggers forced asset sales (\(\mathrm{Due}(s^*) > B\)) equals:

\[ s^* = \left\lceil \frac{(2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6}{2 N D_h} \right\rceil \]

When \(N D_h = 0\), margin calls are identically zero for all shocks, and no forced-selling threshold exists.

Proof

By Assumption 1.2, forced selling occurs at step 0 if and only if \(\mathrm{Due}(s) > B\), which for integer cents means \(\mathrm{Due}(s) \ge B + 1\). The cumulative due function is \(\mathrm{Due}(s) = \lfloor x + 1/2 \rfloor\) where \(x = \frac{N D_h s}{10^6}\). For any real \(x\) and integer \(B\), \(\lfloor x + 1/2 \rfloor \ge B + 1\) is equivalent to:

\[ x + \frac{1}{2} \ge B + 1 \iff x \ge B + \frac{1}{2} \iff \frac{N D_h s}{10^6} \ge \frac{2 B + 1}{2} \]

Multiplying by \(2 \times 10^6\) yields:

\[ 2 N D_h s \ge (2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6 \iff s \ge \frac{(2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6}{2 N D_h} \]

Because the yield shock \(s\) is an integer number of basis points, the smallest integer satisfying this inequality is the ceiling \(\lceil \frac{(2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6}{2 N D_h} \rceil\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.2 (threshold calculation and boundary verification)

Let an LDI fund hold an overlay with notional \(N = 100\,000\,000\) cents (1,000,000 dollars) and duration \(D_h = 500\) (5.00 years), backed by a cash buffer of \(B = 1\,000\,000\) cents (10,000 dollars).

We compute the threshold numerator and denominator:

\[ (2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6 = (2\,000\,001) \times 10^6 = 2\,000\,001\,000\,000 \] \[ 2 N D_h = 2 \times 100\,000\,000 \times 500 = 100\,000\,000\,000 = 10^{11} \]

The quotient is:

\[ \frac{2\,000\,001\,000\,000}{100\,000\,000\,000} = 20.00001 \implies s^* = \lceil 20.00001 \rceil = 21\text{ bps} \]

Checking both sides of the boundary:

  • At \(s = 20\) bps: \(\mathrm{Due}(20) = \lfloor \frac{10^8 \times 500 \times 20}{10^6} + \frac{1}{2} \rfloor = \lfloor 1\,000\,000.5 \rfloor = 1\,000\,000\) cents. Since \(\mathrm{Due}(20) \le B\), buffer cash covers the call in full (\(P_0 = 10^6\), \(S_0 = 0\)). No forced sales occur.
  • At \(s = 21\) bps: \(\mathrm{Due}(21) = \lfloor \frac{10^8 \times 500 \times 21}{10^6} + \frac{1}{2} \rfloor = \lfloor 1\,050\,000.5 \rfloor = 1\,050\,000\) cents. Since \(\mathrm{Due}(21) > B\), the fund exhausts its buffer and is forced to liquidate \(S_0 = 1\,050\,000 - 1\,000\,000 = 50\,000\) cents ($500) of bonds.

Lab 1 · The LDI collateral spiral and central-bank backstop

Verifies that the closed-form threshold shock s* separates stable buffer absorption from forced liquidations, and demonstrates that a central-bank liquidity backstop absorbs forced sales, eliminating price impact and truncating the collateral spiral.

"""BK3 Lab L06: The LDI Collateral Spiral and Central-Bank Backstop.

SIMULATED. Illustrative LDI fund parameters, not market data.
Simulates a leveraged LDI fund with interest-rate swap overlay facing a sharp rise in sovereign bond yields.
Calculates the forced-selling threshold s* in closed form and checks it against discrete evaluation.
Traces the multi-step collateral spiral:
  - Yield shock triggers variation margin call: Due(Y) = round_half_up(N * D_h * Y / 10^6)
  - Fund exhausts cash buffer; unpaid calls trigger forced asset sales: S_r = M_r - P_r
  - Unabsorbed sales create endogenous price impact: I_r = round_half_up(lambda * S_unabsorbed / 10^9) bps
  - Higher yields provoke subsequent margin calls in a cascading loop.
Compares the spiral trajectory with and without a central-bank liquidity backstop.
All amounts are exact in integer cents.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FundConfig:
    overlay_notional_cents: int
    overlay_duration_cy: int
    buffer_cents: int
    impact_bps_per_gcent: int
    backstop_trigger_bps: int
    backstop_capacity_cents: int


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class StepRecord:
    step: int
    cumulative_bps: int
    margin_call_cents: int
    buffer_paid_cents: int
    forced_sale_cents: int
    absorbed_cents: int
    impact_bps: int


def _round_half_up(val: Fraction) -> int:
    return math.floor(val + Fraction(1, 2))


def margin_call(fund: FundConfig, cumulative_bps: int) -> int:
    exact = Fraction(fund.overlay_notional_cents * fund.overlay_duration_cy * cumulative_bps, 1_000_000)
    return _round_half_up(exact)


def threshold_closed_form(fund: FundConfig) -> int:
    num = (2 * fund.buffer_cents + 1) * 1_000_000
    den = 2 * fund.overlay_notional_cents * fund.overlay_duration_cy
    return -(-num // den)


def simulate_spiral(fund: FundConfig, initial_shock_bps: int, use_backstop: bool) -> list[StepRecord]:
    steps: list[StepRecord] = []
    rem_buffer = fund.buffer_cents
    rem_capacity = fund.backstop_capacity_cents if use_backstop else 0
    cum_bps = initial_shock_bps
    prev_due = 0

    for step_idx in range(50):
        cum_due = margin_call(fund, cum_bps)
        step_call = cum_due - prev_due
        prev_due = cum_due

        paid = min(step_call, rem_buffer)
        rem_buffer -= paid
        forced_sale = step_call - paid

        absorbed = 0
        if use_backstop and cum_bps >= fund.backstop_trigger_bps:
            absorbed = min(forced_sale, rem_capacity)
            rem_capacity -= absorbed

        unabsorbed = forced_sale - absorbed
        impact = _round_half_up(Fraction(fund.impact_bps_per_gcent * unabsorbed, 1_000_000_000))

        steps.append(
            StepRecord(
                step=step_idx,
                cumulative_bps=cum_bps,
                margin_call_cents=step_call,
                buffer_paid_cents=paid,
                forced_sale_cents=forced_sale,
                absorbed_cents=absorbed,
                impact_bps=impact,
            )
        )

        if forced_sale == 0 or impact == 0:
            break
        cum_bps += impact

    return steps


def main() -> None:
    fund = FundConfig(
        overlay_notional_cents=500_000_000,  # $5,000,000 notional
        overlay_duration_cy=1500,  # 15.00 years
        buffer_cents=10_000_000,  # $100,000 cash buffer
        impact_bps_per_gcent=1500,  # 1500 bps per $10M sold
        backstop_trigger_bps=50,  # Backstop triggers at +50 bps
        backstop_capacity_cents=15_000_000,  # $150,000 intervention capacity
    )

    s_star = threshold_closed_form(fund)
    print("=== LDI Collateral Spiral: Forced-Selling Threshold ===")
    print(f"Overlay Notional: ${fund.overlay_notional_cents / 100:,.2f}")
    print(f"Overlay Duration: {fund.overlay_duration_cy / 100:.2f} years")
    print(f"Liquid Buffer:    ${fund.buffer_cents / 100:,.2f}")
    print(f"Threshold Shock:  s* = {s_star} bps")
    call_below = margin_call(fund, s_star - 1)
    call_at = margin_call(fund, s_star)
    print(f"Margin call at {s_star - 1} bps: ${call_below / 100:,.2f} <= Buffer (${fund.buffer_cents / 100:,.2f})")
    print(f"Margin call at {s_star} bps:     ${call_at / 100:,.2f} > Buffer (${fund.buffer_cents / 100:,.2f})\n")

    initial_shock = 60  # Initial yield shock exceeds threshold
    steps_unhedged = simulate_spiral(fund, initial_shock, use_backstop=False)
    steps_backstopped = simulate_spiral(fund, initial_shock, use_backstop=True)

    print(f"=== Simulation 1: Spiral WITHOUT Backstop (Initial Shock = {initial_shock} bps) ===")
    hdr1 = (
        f"{'Step':<5} | {'Yield (bps)':<12} | {'Margin Call':<14} | "
        f"{'Buffer Paid':<14} | {'Forced Sale':<14} | {'Price Impact'}"
    )
    print(hdr1)
    print("-" * len(hdr1))
    for s in steps_unhedged:
        print(
            f"{s.step:<5} | {s.cumulative_bps:<12} | ${s.margin_call_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | "
            f"${s.buffer_paid_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | ${s.forced_sale_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | +{s.impact_bps} bps"
        )

    print(f"\n=== Simulation 2: Spiral WITH Backstop (Trigger = {fund.backstop_trigger_bps} bps) ===")
    hdr2 = (
        f"{'Step':<5} | {'Yield (bps)':<12} | {'Margin Call':<14} | "
        f"{'Buffer Paid':<14} | {'Forced Sale':<14} | {'Absorbed':<12} | {'Price Impact'}"
    )
    print(hdr2)
    print("-" * len(hdr2))
    for s in steps_backstopped:
        print(
            f"{s.step:<5} | {s.cumulative_bps:<12} | ${s.margin_call_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | "
            f"${s.buffer_paid_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | ${s.forced_sale_cents / 100:<13,.2f} | "
            f"${s.absorbed_cents / 100:<11,.2f} | +{s.impact_bps} bps"
        )

    tot_forced_1 = sum(s.forced_sale_cents for s in steps_unhedged)
    tot_forced_2 = sum(s.forced_sale_cents for s in steps_backstopped)
    final_yield_1 = steps_unhedged[-1].cumulative_bps + steps_unhedged[-1].impact_bps
    final_yield_2 = steps_backstopped[-1].cumulative_bps + steps_backstopped[-1].impact_bps

    print("\n=== Spiral Comparison Summary ===")
    print(
        f"Without backstop: {len(steps_unhedged)} steps | "
        f"Forced Sales: ${tot_forced_1 / 100:,.2f} | Final Shock: +{final_yield_1} bps"
    )
    print(
        f"With backstop:    {len(steps_backstopped)} steps | "
        f"Forced Sales: ${tot_forced_2 / 100:,.2f} | Final Shock: +{final_yield_2} bps"
    )
    print(f"Backstop halted the spiral earlier: {len(steps_backstopped) < len(steps_unhedged)}")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The discrete collateral spiral model isolates the core feedback loop between derivative margin calls and fire sales. In actual market crises, several compounding factors amplify systemic fragility:

  • Procyclical haircuts: In stress episodes, clearing brokers and repo counterparties increase collateral haircuts, reducing the cash borrowing capacity of unencumbered bonds and accelerating buffer depletion.
  • Cross-fund herd selling: When multiple LDI funds hold identical sovereign bond benchmarks and similar overlay durations, simultaneous liquidations exhaust dealer balance-sheet capacity, causing market depth to evaporate non-linearly.
  • Operational settlement delays: Moving cash from sponsor corporate treasuries into LDI collateral accounts typically requires several days of governance approvals, while margin calls must be met within hours.
  • Moral hazard of policy backstops: When central banks intervene to purchase sovereign debt and halt fire sales, they create expectations of future bailouts, encouraging pension funds to maintain higher leverage and leaner liquidity buffers.

Misconceptions

  • A collateral spiral occurs because the pension fund has become economically insolvent.

    A collateral spiral is an acute liquidity crisis, not an economic insolvency. When yields rise, the present value of pension liabilities declines faster than physical assets, often improving the long-term funding ratio. However, derivative overlays demand immediate cash collateral that cannot be delivered in time.

  • Forced bond selling always halts naturally as soon as the initial interest-rate shock stops.

    In an illiquid market, forced bond selling depresses prices, creating endogenous secondary yield increases. These yield increases provoke fresh margin calls, sustaining the spiral even without further macroeconomic shocks.

  • Central bank liquidity backstops permanently subsidize pension fund losses.

    A central-bank backstop acts as a temporary circuit breaker by purchasing bonds at distressed prices to absorb fire-sale volume. Once market functioning stabilizes and pension funds replenish cash buffers, the central bank unwinds its bond holdings, often at a profit.

Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An LDI fund holds an overlay with notional \(N = 200\,000\,000\) cents (2 million dollars), duration \(D_h = 1000\) (10.00 years), and cash buffer \(B = 4\,000\,000\) cents (40,000 dollars). Compute the forced-selling threshold \(s^*\) in basis points.

Solution

The numerator is \((2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6 = (8\,000\,001) \times 10^6 = 8\,000\,001\,000\,000\). The denominator is \(2 N D_h = 2 \times 200\,000\,000 \times 1000 = 400\,000\,000\,000\). The quotient is \(8\,000\,001\,000\,000 / 400\,000\,000\,000 = 20.0000025\). Taking the ceiling gives \(s^* = 21\) basis points.

For the fund in Problem M1, compute the margin call due, buffer paid, and forced sale under an initial shock of \(s_0 = 30\) basis points.

Solution

Margin call due: \(\mathrm{Due}(30) = \lfloor 200\,000\,000 \times 1000 \times 30 / 10^6 + 1/2 \rfloor = \lfloor 6\,000\,000.5 \rfloor = 6\,000\,000\) cents ($60,000). Buffer paid: \(P_0 = \min(6\,000\,000, 4\,000\,000) = 4\,000\,000\) cents ($40,000). Forced sale: \(S_0 = 6\,000\,000 - 4\,000\,000 = 2\,000\,000\) cents ($20,000).

If the forced sale in Problem M2 produces market price impact with \(\lambda = 1500\) bps per \(10^9\) cents sold, compute the resulting yield increase \(I_0\) in basis points.

Solution

Price impact is \(I_0 = \lfloor 1500 \times 2\,000\,000 / 10^9 + 1/2 \rfloor = \lfloor 3\,000\,000\,000 / 10^9 + 1/2 \rfloor = \lfloor 3.5 \rfloor = 3\) basis points. The yield increases by 3 bps, raising cumulative yield move to \(30 + 3 = 33\) bps.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that if the price impact coefficient \(\lambda = 0\) (perfectly elastic market depth), any collateral spiral terminates strictly at step 0.

Solution

Under Assumption 1.2, at step 0 the fund incurs forced sale \(S_0 \ge 0\). If \(\lambda = 0\), the price impact is \(I_0 = \lfloor 0 \cdot S_0 / 10^9 + 1/2 \rfloor = 0\) basis points. By Assumption 1.2, the spiral terminates whenever \(I_r = 0\). Therefore, the process halts at \(r=0\) with no further yield moves or margin calls, proving strict termination.

Prove that the threshold shock \(s^*\) is strictly increasing in the cash buffer \(B\) and strictly decreasing in overlay notional \(N\).

Solution

The continuous argument inside the ceiling function is \(f(B, N) = \frac{(2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6}{2 N D_h}\). Differentiating with respect to \(B\): \(\frac{\partial f}{\partial B} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^6}{2 N D_h} > 0\), which is strictly positive. Differentiating with respect to \(N\): \(\frac{\partial f}{\partial N} = -\frac{(2 B + 1) \cdot 10^6}{2 N^2 D_h} < 0\), which is strictly negative. Applying the monotone ceiling function preserves weak monotonicity and ensures strictly higher discrete jump points.

Prove that total buffer cash paid across all steps of the spiral cannot exceed the initial cash buffer \(B_0\).

Solution

At each step \(r\), buffer paid is \(P_r = \min(M_r, B_r) \ge 0\), and buffer updates as \(B_{r+1} = B_r - P_r\). Telescoping the sum across \(K\) steps: \(\sum_{r=0}^K P_r = \sum_{r=0}^K (B_r - B_{r+1}) = B_0 - B_{K+1}\). Because \(B_{r+1} \ge 0\) by construction, \(B_0 - B_{K+1} \le B_0\), proving total buffer paid is bounded above by \(B_0\).

Tier 3 · Empirical

Using the parameters from Lab 1, explain why a 60 bps shock triggers an unhedged spiral of forced sales while a 10 bps shock produces zero forced sales.

Solution

The closed-form threshold for the fund is \(s^* = 14\) basis points. A 10 bps shock lies strictly below \(s^*\), generating a margin call of $75,000 that is paid in full by the $100,000 cash buffer with $25,000 buffer remaining, producing zero forced sales and halting immediately. A 60 bps shock exceeds \(s^*\), generating a $450,000 call that exhausts the buffer and triggers $350,000 in forced liquidations.

Describe the operational friction that prevents defined-benefit pension sponsors from injecting cash into LDI collateral accounts during an intraday margin call.

Solution

Corporate pension sponsors hold liquidity in corporate treasury operating accounts or bank credit facilities. Initiating a capital contribution requires corporate board or investment committee authorization, trustee approvals, and custodian bank wire processing, which typically requires two to five business days, whereas clearing brokers demand variation margin within hours.

Explain how pooled LDI funds can experience run dynamics similar to open-end mutual funds during a collateral spiral.

Solution

In a pooled LDI vehicle, multiple pension schemes invest in common leveraged collateral pools. When margin calls arrive, schemes that recapitalize slowly force the pooled manager to sell bonds pro rata. Well-capitalized schemes that recognize the risk redeem early to avoid bearing market impact discounts, triggering an investor run on the pooled fund.

Tier 4 · Research

Analyze how central clearing mandates for interest-rate derivatives interact with pension fund liquidity management during sovereign bond market stress.

Solution

Central counterparty (CCP) clearing rules mandate strict intraday cash variation margining and eliminate bilateral non-cash collateral flexibility. While CCPs eliminate bilateral counterparty default contagion, they concentrate and accelerate cash liquidity demands, transforming counterparty credit risk into systemic funding liquidity risk during rapid yield moves.

Evaluate the macroprudential dilemma faced by a central bank conducting quantitative tightening while simultaneously providing a temporary emergency liquidity backstop to pension funds.

Solution

A central bank tightening monetary policy to combat inflation seeks to shrink its balance sheet and raise yields. However, if rising yields trigger an LDI collateral spiral that threatens market functioning, the central bank must temporarily buy long-term bonds to prevent market collapse, creating conflicting communications and temporary monetary expansion.

Discuss regulatory proposals requiring LDI funds to hold minimum liquidity buffers calibrated to multi-hundred basis point stress scenarios.

Solution

Mandating large liquidity buffers (e.g. 300 to 400 bps of yield resilience) prevents fire-sale spirals by ensuring margin calls are paid from cash or repo facilities. However, higher cash buffers create cash drag, reducing portfolio returns and requiring corporate sponsors to contribute additional equity capital to close historic deficits.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What fundamental mechanism defines the LDI collateral spiral?

    The spiral is driven by the feedback loop connecting margin calls, liquid buffer exhaustion, forced asset sales, and price impact.

  2. What is the closed-form expression for the forced-selling threshold shock \(s^*\) in basis points?

    Proposition 2.1 derives the exact minimum integer shock that forces margin calls to strictly exceed the available cash buffer.

  3. Under what condition does an initial yield shock \(s_0\) produce zero forced asset sales?

    When the shock is below the threshold, the cash buffer absorbs the call in full (\(P_0 = M_0\)), so forced sales are zero (\(S_0 = 0\)).

  4. Why is an LDI collateral spiral primarily classified as a liquidity crisis rather than an insolvency crisis?

    Higher discount rates reduce the present value of liabilities faster than assets, but margin calls require immediate cash.

  5. How does a central-bank liquidity backstop halt an LDI collateral spiral?

    By absorbing fire-sale volume, the backstop prevents market prices from dropping, eliminating the feedback loop of further margin calls.

  6. If a fund doubles its liquid cash buffer \(B\), how does the forced-selling threshold \(s^*\) respond?

    The numerator of \(s^*\) is proportional to \(2B + 1\); doubling \(B\) approximately doubles the critical shock threshold.

  7. What operational bottleneck typically prevents corporate sponsors from curing LDI margin calls intraday?

    Corporate governance and multi-institution wire transfers take days to execute, conflicting with intraday clearing deadlines.

  8. What market phenomenon occurs when multiple leveraged LDI funds simultaneously liquidate sovereign bonds?

    Simultaneous block liquidations overwhelm dealer market-making capacity, amplifying price impact and yield spikes.
