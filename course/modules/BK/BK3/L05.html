Because defined-benefit pension liabilities have durations spanning 15 to 25 years, unhedged plans suffer massive funding ratio swings whenever benchmark bond yields move. Traditional asset management invested heavily in equities to generate growth, leaving the liability duration mismatch unhedged. Liability-driven investment (LDI) matches the interest-rate sensitivity of assets to liabilities using derivative overlays, separating asset-liability matching from capital growth.

1The Mechanics of Liability-Driven Investment

Under conventional portfolio management, pension trustees allocated assets across equities and bonds based on expected returns and volatility. However, if interest rates decline, liabilities expand dramatically, overwhelming equity gains. Liability-driven investment restructures the balance sheet to immunize the pension funding ratio against interest-rate shocks.

Definition 1.1 (liability-driven investment)

Liability-driven investment (LDI) is an institutional investment framework wherein an asset owner constructs and manages an investment portfolio whose primary benchmark is the sensitivity of its contractual liabilities to interest-rate, inflation, and longevity risks, rather than an unhedged broad-market asset index.

In fixed income LDI, immunization requires equating the dollar duration (DV01) of the asset portfolio to the dollar duration of the liabilities:

\[ \mathrm{DV01}_A = \mathrm{DV01}_L \quad \iff \quad A \cdot D_A = L \cdot D_L \]

where \(D_A\) and \(D_L\) represent the respective modified durations in years.

Assumption 1.2 (leveraged LDI derivative overlay model)

Because pension schemes often hold return-seeking growth assets (equities, real estate, private credit) with near-zero interest-rate duration, achieving \(\mathrm{DV01}_A = \mathrm{DV01}_L\) using only physical sovereign bonds requires dedicating 100% of plan capital to low-yielding bonds. LDI overcomes this constraint by using a derivative overlay:

A1 (overlay structure). The fund holds physical assets \(A\) with duration \(D_A\), and enters into an interest-rate swap overlay receiving fixed and paying floating, with notional \(N \ge 0\) and modified duration \(D_{\text{ov}} \ge 0\).

A2 (parallel curve shift). Shocks to the discount curve occur as a uniform parallel shift \(\Delta y = s / 10\,000\), where \(s\) is the shift in basis points.

2First-Order Immunization and the Second-Order Residual

By augmenting physical assets with an interest-rate swap overlay, the total effective asset DV01 becomes the sum of the physical asset DV01 and the overlay DV01. We now derive the required overlay notional and evaluate the higher-order residual error.

Proposition 2.1 (closed-form overlay sizing for DV01 matching)

Under Assumption 1.2, to achieve exact first-order immunization \(\mathrm{DV01}_{\text{total}} = \mathrm{DV01}_L\), the required overlay notional \(N^*\) equals:

\[ N^* = \frac{\mathrm{DV01}_L - \mathrm{DV01}_A}{D_{\text{ov}} / 10\,000} = \frac{L D_L - A D_A}{D_{\text{ov}}} \]
Proof

The dollar duration per basis point of liabilities is \(\mathrm{DV01}_L = L D_L / 10\,000\). The dollar duration of physical assets is \(\mathrm{DV01}_A = A D_A / 10\,000\). An interest-rate swap with notional \(N\) and modified duration \(D_{\text{ov}}\) changes in market value by \(\Delta V_{\text{ov}} \approx -N D_{\text{ov}} \Delta y\). Per basis point (\(\Delta y = 1/10\,000\)), the swap contributes \(\mathrm{DV01}_{\text{ov}} = N D_{\text{ov}} / 10\,000\). Equating total asset DV01 to liability DV01:

\[ \mathrm{DV01}_A + \mathrm{DV01}_{\text{ov}} = \mathrm{DV01}_L \iff \frac{A D_A}{10\,000} + \frac{N D_{\text{ov}}}{10\,000} = \frac{L D_L}{10\,000} \]

Multiplying by \(10\,000\) and solving for \(N\) yields \(N^* = \frac{L D_L - A D_A}{D_{\text{ov}}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.2 (the convexity residual)

Under Assumption 1.2 with first-order DV01 matching (\(N = N^*\)), the second-order Taylor expansion of net surplus \(S(\Delta y) = (A + \Delta V_{\text{ov}}) - L\) under a yield shift \(\Delta y\) satisfies:

\[ \Delta S(\Delta y) = \frac{1}{2} \left( C_A A + C_{\text{ov}} N^* - C_L L \right) (\Delta y)^2 + \mathcal{O}((\Delta y)^3) \]

where \(C_A\), \(C_{\text{ov}}\), and \(C_L\) denote the modified convexities of the assets, overlay, and liabilities. If net dollar convexity is negative (\(C_A A + C_{\text{ov}} N^* < C_L L\)), net surplus deteriorates symmetrically for both upward and downward yield shocks.

Proof

By Taylor's theorem from M2, the price change of any bond or liability portfolio up to second order is:

\[ \Delta P \approx -P D \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} P C (\Delta y)^2 \]

Summing the changes across physical assets and the overlay, and subtracting the liability change:

\[ \Delta S = (\Delta A + \Delta V_{\text{ov}}) - \Delta L \approx -(\mathrm{DV01}_{\text{total}} - \mathrm{DV01}_L) \cdot 10\,000 \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} (A C_A + N^* C_{\text{ov}} - L C_L) (\Delta y)^2 \]

Because \(N = N^*\), the first-order term vanishes identically: \(\mathrm{DV01}_{\text{total}} - \mathrm{DV01}_L = 0\). The remaining leading-order term is \(\frac{1}{2} (A C_A + N^* C_{\text{ov}} - L C_L) (\Delta y)^2\). Since \((\Delta y)^2 > 0\) for any non-zero shock, if \((A C_A + N^* C_{\text{ov}} - L C_L) < 0\), \(\Delta S < 0\) regardless of whether \(\Delta y > 0\) or \(\Delta y < 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.3 (numerical LDI hedge and convexity residual)

Consider a pension fund with \(L = 100\) million dollars (\(D_L = 18.0\) years, \(C_L = 420\)) and physical assets \(A = 80\) million dollars (\(D_A = 7.0\) years, \(C_A = 75\)).

  • \(\mathrm{DV01}_L = 100\,000\,000 \times 18.0 / 10\,000 = 180\,000\) dollars per bp.
  • \(\mathrm{DV01}_A = 80\,000\,000 \times 7.0 / 10\,000 = 56\,000\) dollars per bp.
  • \(\mathrm{DV01}_{\text{gap}} = 180\,000 - 56\,000 = 124\,000\) dollars per bp.

Using a 20-year swap overlay (\(D_{\text{ov}} = 20.0\) years, \(C_{\text{ov}} = 500\)), the required notional is:

\[ N^* = \frac{124\,000}{20.0 / 10\,000} = \frac{124\,000}{0.0020} = 62\,000\,000\text{ dollars} \]

Now evaluate dollar convexities:

  • Liability dollar convexity: \(100\text{M} \times 420 = 42\,000\text{M}\).
  • Physical asset dollar convexity: \(80\text{M} \times 75 = 6\,000\text{M}\).
  • Overlay swap dollar convexity: \(62\text{M} \times 500 = 31\,000\text{M}\).
  • Total asset dollar convexity: \(6\,000\text{M} + 31\,000\text{M} = 37\,000\text{M}\).

The net dollar convexity is \(37\,000\text{M} - 42\,000\text{M} = -5\,000\) million dollars. Under a \(\pm 100\) bps shock (\(\Delta y = \pm 0.01\)), the first-order hedge error is zero, but the second-order convexity drag causes a net loss of:

\[ \Delta S \approx \frac{1}{2} \times (-5\,000\,000\,000) \times (0.01)^2 = -250\,000\text{ dollars} \]

The fund loses $250,000 in net surplus whether yields rise by 100 bps or fall by 100 bps.

Lab 1 · Liability-Driven Investment: DV01 matching and the convexity residual

Verifies that the closed-form overlay notional achieves exact first-order DV01 matching, and demonstrates that a negative net dollar convexity produces a strictly negative surplus impact under both positive and negative yield shocks.

"""BK3 Lab L05: Liability-Driven Investment: DV01 matching and the convexity residual.

SIMULATED. Illustrative LDI portfolio parameters, not market data.
Models a defined-benefit pension scheme matching the interest-rate sensitivity (DV01) of its
liabilities using physical bonds and an interest-rate swap derivative overlay.
Solves for the required overlay notional N in closed form:
  N = (DV01_L - DV01_A) / (D_overlay / 10000)
Evaluates the first-order hedge across parallel yield shocks from -200 bps to +200 bps.
Computes the second-order convexity residual:
  Residual = Delta(Assets + Overlay - Liab) - First_Order_Mismatch
and demonstrates that when liability convexity exceeds asset plus overlay convexity, the net surplus
deteriorates symmetrically for both upward and downward yield shifts.
All calculations are exact using fractions.Fraction.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    # Pension scheme parameters:
    # Liabilities: L = $100,000,000, Modified Duration D_L = 18.0 years, Convexity C_L = 420
    # Physical Assets: A = $80,000,000, Modified Duration D_A = 7.0 years, Convexity C_A = 75
    # Overlay (Receive-Fixed Swap): Modified Duration D_ov = 20.0 years, Convexity C_ov = 500
    l_val = Fraction(100_000_000, 1)
    d_l = Fraction(18, 1)
    c_l = Fraction(420, 1)

    a_val = Fraction(80_000_000, 1)
    d_a = Fraction(7, 1)
    c_a = Fraction(75, 1)

    d_ov = Fraction(20, 1)
    c_ov = Fraction(500, 1)

    # Dollar duration per basis point (DV01)
    dv01_l = l_val * d_l / Fraction(10_000, 1)
    dv01_a = a_val * d_a / Fraction(10_000, 1)
    dv01_gap = dv01_l - dv01_a

    # Required overlay notional for exact first-order DV01 matching
    # N * (D_ov / 10000) = DV01_gap
    overlay_notional = dv01_gap * Fraction(10_000, 1) / d_ov

    print("=== Liability-Driven Investment: DV01 Hedge Sizing ===")
    print(f"Liability:      Value = ${float(l_val):,.0f} | Duration = {float(d_l):.1f}y | DV01 = ${float(dv01_l):,.2f}")
    print(f"Physical Asset: Value = ${float(a_val):,.0f} | Duration = {float(d_a):.1f}y | DV01 = ${float(dv01_a):,.2f}")
    print(f"Duration Gap:   DV01 Gap = ${float(dv01_gap):,.2f} per basis point")
    print(f"Overlay Swap:   Duration = {float(d_ov):.1f}y | Required Notional = ${float(overlay_notional):,.2f}")

    total_dv01_assets = dv01_a + (overlay_notional * d_ov / Fraction(10_000, 1))
    print(
        f"Post-Hedge Total Asset DV01: ${float(total_dv01_assets):,.2f} (Exact match: {total_dv01_assets == dv01_l})\n"
    )

    # Convexity analysis
    dollar_c_l = l_val * c_l
    dollar_c_a = a_val * c_a
    dollar_c_ov = overlay_notional * c_ov
    total_dollar_c_assets = dollar_c_a + dollar_c_ov
    net_dollar_convexity = total_dollar_c_assets - dollar_c_l

    print("=== Dollar Convexity Breakdown ===")
    print(f"Liability Dollar Convexity:     ${float(dollar_c_l):,.0f}")
    print(f"Physical Asset Dollar Conv:     ${float(dollar_c_a):,.0f}")
    print(f"Overlay Swap Dollar Conv:       ${float(dollar_c_ov):,.0f}")
    print(f"Total Hedged Asset Dollar Conv: ${float(total_dollar_c_assets):,.0f}")
    print(f"Net Dollar Convexity (A - L):   ${float(net_dollar_convexity):,.0f}\n")

    # Shock testing
    test_shocks_bps = [-200, -100, -50, 0, 50, 100, 200]
    print(f"{'Shock (bps)':<12} | {'First-Order Delta':<20} | {'Convexity Term':<18} | {'Total Net Impact':<18}")
    print("-" * 76)

    for shock in test_shocks_bps:
        dy = Fraction(shock, 10_000)
        first_order = -(total_dv01_assets - dv01_l) * Fraction(shock, 1)
        conv_term = Fraction(1, 2) * net_dollar_convexity * (dy**2)
        total_impact = first_order + conv_term
        print(
            f"{shock:+12d} | ${float(first_order):+18,.2f} | "
            f"${float(conv_term):+16,.2f} | ${float(total_impact):+16,.2f}"
        )

    print(
        "\nConvexity residual causes symmetric loss in net funding for rate shocks:",
        net_dollar_convexity < 0,
    )


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

LDI duration matching appears analytically complete under ideal textbook assumptions, but breaks down in real trading environments:

  • Collateral and cash drag: Swaps require bilateral variation margin. As yields spike, the overlay loses value, demanding immediate cash collateral even while the underlying pension liabilities fall in present value.
  • Curve reshaping: Swaps matched to a 20-year benchmark do not protect against curve steepening or twists between 5-year and 30-year tenors.
  • Basis risk: Swaps trade against interbank benchmark rates (SONIA, SOFR), while pension liabilities are discounted against sovereign gilt or Treasury curves. The swap spread introduces unhedged basis volatility.
  • Leverage limits: Highly leveraged LDI funds (e.g. 3x to 5x leverage) hold thin cash buffers, exposing them to liquidity insolvency during rapid market yield moves.

Misconceptions

  • A duration-matched LDI portfolio completely eliminates all financial risk for the pension scheme.

    DV01 matching is a first-order local hedge valid only for infinitesimal parallel yield shifts. It leaves the fund exposed to second-order convexity residuals, non-parallel curve twists, swap spread basis risk, and severe liquidity margin demands.

  • Because swaps have zero initial value, enterring into an LDI swap overlay creates no leverage on the pension fund.

    While the upfront market value of a newly struck par swap is zero, the swap creates large economic leverage. A $100M notional overlay exposes the scheme to the same dollar interest-rate swings as $100M of physical long-term bonds, without requiring upfront cash funding.

  • A pension fund with negative net convexity benefits when bond yields experience extreme volatility.

    Negative net convexity implies that liability value increases faster than assets when yields fall, and assets decrease faster than liabilities when yields rise. Increased yield volatility guarantees a structural erosion in net surplus.

Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

A pension scheme has liabilities of \(L = 250\) million dollars with duration \(D_L = 20.0\) years, and physical assets of \(A = 200\) million dollars with duration \(D_A = 8.0\) years. Compute the liability DV01, the asset DV01, and the required notional of a 25-year duration swap overlay to achieve first-order immunization.

Solution

\(\mathrm{DV01}_L = 250\,000\,000 \times 20.0 / 10\,000 = 500\,000\) dollars per bp. \(\mathrm{DV01}_A = 200\,000\,000 \times 8.0 / 10\,000 = 160\,000\) dollars per bp. The DV01 gap is \(500\,000 - 160\,000 = 340\,000\) dollars per bp. The required swap notional is \(N^* = 340\,000 / (25.0 / 10\,000) = 340\,000 / 0.0025 = 136\,000\,000\) dollars.

For the scheme in Problem M1, if benchmark yields rise by 50 basis points, compute the change in the market value of the swap overlay.

Solution

The swap has notional \(N = 136\) million and duration \(D_{\text{ov}} = 25.0\) years. The value change is \(\Delta V_{\text{ov}} \approx -N D_{\text{ov}} \Delta y = -136\,000\,000 \times 25.0 \times (50 / 10\,000) = -136\,000\,000 \times 0.125 = -17\,000\,000\) dollars. The overlay experiences a 17 million dollar decline.

In Problem M2, compute the change in liability present value under the same 50 basis point yield increase, and verify that the combined net change is zero to first order.

Solution

Liability change: \(\Delta L \approx -L D_L \Delta y = -250\,000\,000 \times 20.0 \times 0.0050 = -25\,000\,000\) dollars. Physical asset change: \(\Delta A \approx -200\,000\,000 \times 8.0 \times 0.0050 = -8\,000\,000\) dollars. Total asset change: \(\Delta A + \Delta V_{\text{ov}} = -8\text{M} - 17\text{M} = -25\text{M}\). Net change: \((\Delta A + \Delta V_{\text{ov}}) - \Delta L = -25\text{M} - (-25\text{M}) = 0\), confirming exact first-order hedging.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that if an LDI manager immunizes the dollar duration of assets to liabilities (\(\mathrm{DV01}_A = \mathrm{DV01}_L\)) but leaves asset convexity strictly less than liability convexity, the portfolio surplus \(A - L\) is a strictly concave function of the yield \(y\) around the hedge point.

Solution

Let \(S(y) = A(y) - L(y)\). Differentiating twice with respect to \(y\): \(S''(y) = A''(y) - L''(y)\). By definition of convexity, \(A''(y) = A C_A\) and \(L''(y) = L C_L\). If asset dollar convexity is strictly less than liability dollar convexity (\(A C_A < L C_L\)), we have \(S''(y) < 0\). A function with a strictly negative second derivative is strictly concave, meaning any departure from the initial yield reduces surplus.

Prove that an LDI overlay sizing strategy that hedges liabilities with zero-coupon sovereign bonds achieves non-negative net convexity for all positive yields.

Solution

For a zero-coupon bond maturing at \(t\), convexity is \(C = t(t+1)v^2\). For liabilities comprising non-negative cash flows \(c_t \ge 0\), the aggregate convexity is a positive linear combination of zero-coupon convexities. If the physical portfolio replicates or spans the liability cash-flow timing, total asset dollar convexity matches or exceeds liability dollar convexity, ensuring \(C_A A - C_L L \ge 0\).

Prove that adding an interest-rate swap overlay does not alter the physical capital invested in the fund's return-seeking assets.

Solution

An interest-rate swap is an unfunded derivative contract with zero initial market value at inception: \(V_{\text{swap}}(0) = 0\). Therefore, committing to notional \(N\) requires no initial cash outlay, leaving 100% of physical asset capital \(A\) fully invested in growth assets (equities, real estate, private markets), proving capital conservation.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Explain why UK defined-benefit pension funds rapidly increased their adoption of leveraged LDI strategies between 2000 and 2020.

Solution

Regulatory reforms (e.g. FRS 17 and the Pensions Act 2004) forced UK corporate sponsors to mark pension deficits directly onto corporate balance sheets. Facing substantial funding ratio volatility, sponsors adopted LDI to hedge rate exposure while using leverage overlays to maintain equity allocations aimed at closing historic deficits.

Analyze how a persistent decline in long-term gilt yields between 2008 and 2021 affected the collateral requirements of leveraged LDI funds holding receive-fixed swap overlays.

Solution

As gilt yields fell steadily, receive-fixed swaps gained massive positive market value. Counterparties posted substantial variation margin cash to LDI funds. This extended period of receiving collateral lulled pension trustees into a false sense of security regarding liquidity buffers, leaving them unprepared for rapid upward rate reversals.

Using the results from Lab 1, evaluate the magnitude of the cash collateral needed if benchmark yields increase by 200 basis points over a one-month period.

Solution

In Lab 1, the overlay notional is $62M with duration 20.0 years, giving an overlay DV01 of $124,000 per bp. Under a +200 bps yield spike, the cumulative loss on the swap overlay is approximately \(124\,000 \times 200 = 24\,800\,000\) dollars. The fund must deliver nearly $25M in cash variation margin, representing 31% of its entire physical asset base.

Tier 4 · Research

Discuss the liquidity transformation friction inherent in pooled LDI funds compared to segregated LDI mandates.

Solution

In a segregated mandate, the pension scheme directly owns collateral and determines asset liquidation schedules. In a pooled LDI fund, multiple schemes share a single collateral pool. If some schemes fail to meet capital calls promptly, the fund manager must liquidate assets across the pooled vehicle, imposing liquidity run externalities and forced deleveraging on solvent members.

Evaluate whether liability-driven investment creates structural upward pressure on long-duration sovereign bond prices.

Solution

As pension funds systematically buy long-dated sovereign debt and receive fixed in long-term swaps to hedge liabilities, they generate persistent price-inelastic demand for long duration. This structural demand depresses long-term yields, inverting yield curves and creating duration shortages in sovereign debt markets.

Analyze how the convexity residual interacts with dynamic hedging algorithms during large market yield dislocations.

Solution

Because the convexity residual generates asymmetric losses when rates move significantly, an LDI manager attempting to maintain exact DV01 matching must dynamically adjust overlay notionals. As yields rise, bond durations contract faster than liability durations, forcing the manager to purchase additional swap duration precisely when market liquidity is impaired.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What is the primary objective of liability-driven investment (LDI)?

    LDI aligns asset duration with liability duration to prevent interest-rate shocks from destabilizing the pension funding ratio.

  2. Why do pension schemes frequently use derivative overlays (such as interest-rate swaps) in LDI strategies?

    Unfunded swap overlays add substantial duration, freeing physical cash to remain invested in growth-seeking assets.

  3. What is the formula for the required swap notional \(N^*\) to achieve first-order DV01 matching?

    Proposition 2.1 equates total asset dollar duration (\(A D_A + N D_{\text{ov}}\)) to liability dollar duration (\(L D_L\)).

  4. What does the second-order Taylor expansion reveal when net dollar convexity is negative?

    When net convexity is negative, the leading error term is proportional to \(-(\Delta y)^2\), which is negative for any non-zero shock.

  5. If a receive-fixed interest-rate swap overlay is in place and benchmark yields spike sharply upward, what happens to the swap?

    Receiving fixed loses value when market rates rise above the contract fixed rate, generating bilateral variation margin calls.

  6. What is basis risk in an LDI interest-rate swap overlay?

    Swaps reference floating interbank indices (e.g. SONIA/SOFR), whereas liabilities discount against gilts or Treasuries; spread moves create basis risk.

  7. What does DV01 measure in fixed income and LDI portfolio management?

    DV01 (dollar value of an 01) measures dollar sensitivity per single basis point change in the yield curve.

  8. Why can a leveraged LDI fund become illiquid even when its long-term funding ratio improves?

    Rising yields reduce liability present value (economic gain) but inflict cash margin calls on the overlay, creating a cash shortfall.
