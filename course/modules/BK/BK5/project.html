Lab 1 · Worked example: household wealth plan and Monte Carlo simulation

Stated before running. A 25-year career with $100,000 initial salary growing at 1.5% and discounted at 3.5% yields human capital of $1,930,184.21. For a $1,000,000 portfolio split 60% equity and 40% bonds across $400k Taxable, $400k TDA, and $200k Roth, optimal asset location places all $400,000 of bonds into TDA, $400,000 equity in Taxable, and $200,000 equity in Roth. Across 200 moving-block bootstrap paths (block length 5, horizon 30 years), withdrawal rates of 3.50% and 4.00% yield 0.0% ruin probability, while 4.50% yields 0.5% ruin and 5.00% yields 1.0% ruin.

"""Rung 1 worked example: household wealth plan and Monte Carlo withdrawal analysis, step by step.

Standard library only. All inputs are SIMULATED illustrative values.
Demonstrates human capital valuation, tax-optimal asset location, and moving-block bootstrap decumulation.
"""

import random


def main() -> None:
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Step 1: Human Capital Valuation
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    working_years = 25
    base_salary_cents = 10_000_000  # $100,000.00
    salary_growth = 0.015  # 1.5%
    discount_bps = 350  # 3.50%
    r = discount_bps / 10_000.0

    earnings_cents: list[int] = []
    curr = float(base_salary_cents)
    for _ in range(working_years):
        earnings_cents.append(round(curr))
        curr *= 1.0 + salary_growth

    human_capital_cents = round(sum(e / ((1.0 + r) ** t) for t, e in enumerate(earnings_cents, start=1)))
    print(f"Human capital PV: ${human_capital_cents / 100:,.2f} ({human_capital_cents} cents)")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Step 2: Optimal Asset Location
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    total_wealth_cents = 100_000_000  # $1,000,000
    bond_target_cents = 40_000_000  # 40%

    taxable_cap_cents = 40_000_000  # $400k
    tda_cap_cents = 40_000_000  # $400k
    roth_cap_cents = 20_000_000  # $200k

    # Rule: Bonds to TDA first
    tda_bond = min(bond_target_cents, tda_cap_cents)
    rem_bond = bond_target_cents - tda_bond
    taxable_bond = min(rem_bond, taxable_cap_cents)
    roth_bond = rem_bond - taxable_bond

    tda_eq = tda_cap_cents - tda_bond
    taxable_eq = taxable_cap_cents - taxable_bond
    roth_eq = roth_cap_cents - roth_bond

    print(f"Location - Taxable: Equity ${taxable_eq / 100:,.0f} | Bond ${taxable_bond / 100:,.0f}")
    print(f"Location - TDA:     Equity ${tda_eq / 100:,.0f} | Bond ${tda_bond / 100:,.0f}")
    print(f"Location - Roth:    Equity ${roth_eq / 100:,.0f} | Bond ${roth_bond / 100:,.0f}")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Step 3: Moving-Block Bootstrap and Withdrawal Sustainability
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    rng_hist = random.Random(101)
    hist_returns: list[float] = []
    prev_r = 0.05
    for _ in range(50):
        shock = rng_hist.gauss(0.0, 0.11)
        ret = 0.05 + 0.15 * (prev_r - 0.05) + shock
        hist_returns.append(round(ret, 4))
        prev_r = ret

    block_length = 5
    horizon = 30
    n_sims = 200
    rng_boot = random.Random(42)
    num_blocks = len(hist_returns) - block_length + 1

    paths: list[list[float]] = []
    for _ in range(n_sims):
        p: list[float] = []
        while len(p) < horizon:
            idx = rng_boot.randint(0, num_blocks - 1)
            p.extend(hist_returns[idx : idx + block_length])
        paths.append(p[:horizon])

    rates_bps = [350, 400, 450, 500]
    for w_bps in rates_bps:
        w_dollar = (total_wealth_cents * w_bps) // 10_000
        ruin_count = 0
        terminal_wealths: list[float] = []
        for path in paths:
            w = float(total_wealth_cents)
            ruined = False
            for ret in path:
                w -= w_dollar
                if w <= 0.0:
                    ruined = True
                    break
                w *= 1.0 + ret
                if w <= 0.0:
                    ruined = True
                    break
            if ruined:
                ruin_count += 1
                terminal_wealths.append(0.0)
            else:
                terminal_wealths.append(w)

        ruin_prob = ruin_count / n_sims
        sorted_tw = sorted(terminal_wealths)
        med_tw = sorted_tw[n_sims // 2]
        print(
            f"Withdrawal {w_bps / 100:4.2f}% (${w_dollar / 100:6,.0f}/yr) | "
            f"Ruin prob: {ruin_prob * 100:4.1f}% | Median terminal: ${med_tw / 100:10,.0f}"
        )

Lab 3 · Rung 3 check: reference instance verification

Stated before running. Evaluates the reference instance parameters for seed 0. Human capital present value is $1,901,130.31. Asset location allocates $400,000 equity to Taxable, $400,000 bonds to TDA, and $200,000 equity to Roth. Across 200 MBB return paths, the safe withdrawal rate under a 5.0% ruin tolerance is 6.00% (600 bps), with a median terminal wealth of $2,697,264.

"""Rung 3 check: the reference instance, its asset location table, and safe withdrawal rate.

Standard library only. All inputs are SIMULATED illustrative values.
Self-contained implementation verifying end-to-end execution of the retirement planning engine.
"""

import random


def human_capital_value(earnings_cents: list[int], discount_rate_bps: int) -> int:
    """Calculate present value of earnings in integer cents."""
    r = discount_rate_bps / 10_000.0
    return round(sum(e / ((1.0 + r) ** t) for t, e in enumerate(earnings_cents, start=1)))


def optimal_asset_location(
    equity_target_cents: int,
    bond_target_cents: int,
    taxable_capacity_cents: int,
    tda_capacity_cents: int,
    roth_capacity_cents: int,
) -> dict[str, dict[str, int]]:
    """Allocate equity and bond targets across accounts to maximize tax efficiency."""
    tda_bond = min(bond_target_cents, tda_capacity_cents)
    rem_bond = bond_target_cents - tda_bond
    taxable_bond = min(rem_bond, taxable_capacity_cents)
    roth_bond = rem_bond - taxable_bond

    tda_equity = tda_capacity_cents - tda_bond
    taxable_equity = taxable_capacity_cents - taxable_bond
    roth_equity = roth_capacity_cents - roth_bond

    return {
        "taxable": {"equity": taxable_equity, "bond": taxable_bond},
        "tda": {"equity": tda_equity, "bond": tda_bond},
        "roth": {"equity": roth_equity, "bond": roth_bond},
    }


def moving_block_bootstrap(
    returns: list[float],
    block_length: int,
    horizon: int,
    n_paths: int,
    seed: int,
) -> list[list[float]]:
    """Generate synthetic return paths via moving-block bootstrap."""
    num_blocks = len(returns) - block_length + 1
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    paths: list[list[float]] = []
    for _ in range(n_paths):
        path: list[float] = []
        while len(path) < horizon:
            start_idx = rng.randint(0, num_blocks - 1)
            path.extend(returns[start_idx : start_idx + block_length])
        paths.append(path[:horizon])
    return paths


def simulate_withdrawals(
    initial_wealth_cents: int,
    initial_withdrawal_bps: int,
    return_paths: list[list[float]],
) -> tuple[float, int]:
    """Simulate decumulation and return (ruin_probability, median_terminal_wealth_cents)."""
    withdrawal_cents = (initial_wealth_cents * initial_withdrawal_bps) // 10_000
    n_paths = len(return_paths)
    ruin_count = 0
    terminal_wealths: list[int] = []

    for path in return_paths:
        w = float(initial_wealth_cents)
        ruined = False
        for r in path:
            w -= withdrawal_cents
            if w <= 0.0:
                ruined = True
                w = 0.0
                break
            w *= 1.0 + r
            if w <= 0.0:
                ruined = True
                w = 0.0
                break
        if ruined:
            ruin_count += 1
            terminal_wealths.append(0)
        else:
            terminal_wealths.append(round(w))

    ruin_prob = round(ruin_count / n_paths, 4)
    sorted_tw = sorted(terminal_wealths)
    med_tw = (
        sorted_tw[n_paths // 2] if n_paths % 2 == 1 else (sorted_tw[n_paths // 2 - 1] + sorted_tw[n_paths // 2]) // 2
    )
    return ruin_prob, med_tw


def safe_withdrawal_rate(
    initial_wealth_cents: int,
    return_paths: list[list[float]],
    max_ruin_prob: float = 0.05,
    step_bps: int = 10,
) -> int:
    """Find maximum withdrawal rate in bps whose ruin probability <= max_ruin_prob."""
    best_rate = 0
    current_rate = 100
    while current_rate <= 1000:
        ruin_prob, _ = simulate_withdrawals(initial_wealth_cents, current_rate, return_paths)
        if ruin_prob <= max_ruin_prob:
            best_rate = current_rate
        else:
            break
        current_rate += step_bps
    return best_rate


def main() -> None:
    # Deterministic reference scenario parameters
    earnings_cents = [10_000_000 + i * 150_000 for i in range(25)]
    discount_rate_bps = 350
    hc = human_capital_value(earnings_cents, discount_rate_bps)
    print(f"Human capital: ${hc / 100:,.2f} ({hc} cents)")

    total_wealth_cents = 100_000_000
    eq_target = 60_000_000
    bond_target = 40_000_000
    tax_cap = 40_000_000
    tda_cap = 40_000_000
    roth_cap = 20_000_000

    loc = optimal_asset_location(eq_target, bond_target, tax_cap, tda_cap, roth_cap)
    print("Asset location:")
    for acct in ("taxable", "tda", "roth"):
        eq = loc[acct]["equity"]
        bd = loc[acct]["bond"]
        print(f"  {acct.upper():7s} | Equity: ${eq / 100:10,.0f} | Bond: ${bd / 100:10,.0f}")

    rng_hist = random.Random(101)
    hist_returns: list[float] = []
    prev_r = 0.05
    for _ in range(50):
        shock = rng_hist.gauss(0.0, 0.11)
        ret = 0.05 + 0.15 * (prev_r - 0.05) + shock
        hist_returns.append(round(ret, 4))
        prev_r = ret

    paths = moving_block_bootstrap(hist_returns, block_length=5, horizon=30, n_paths=200, seed=42)
    swr_bps = safe_withdrawal_rate(total_wealth_cents, paths, max_ruin_prob=0.05, step_bps=10)
    ruin_prob, med_tw = simulate_withdrawals(total_wealth_cents, swr_bps, paths)

    print(f"Safe withdrawal rate (max ruin 5%): {swr_bps / 100:.2f}% ({swr_bps} bps)")
    print(f"Ruin probability at SWR: {ruin_prob * 100:.2f}%")
    print(f"Median terminal wealth at SWR: ${med_tw / 100:,.0f}")

Exemplar A (Distinguished · Level 4)

MEMORANDUM

TO: Dr. and Mr. Hastings
FROM: Quantitative Wealth Management Group
DATE: November 10, 2026
SUBJECT: Comprehensive Lifetime Wealth Architecture and Sustainable Retirement Decumulation Plan

1. Executive Summary
We have conducted an exhaustive quantitative analysis of your household financial position as you approach retirement at age 65. Our framework integrates your total economic balance sheet—explicitly accounting for the transition of your remaining human capital into financial wealth—and optimizes your $2,000,000 investment portfolio across taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts. Utilizing a moving-block bootstrap simulation that captures historical market dependencies, we have sized your maximum safe annual spending at $80,000 per year (a 4.00% initial withdrawal rate, adjusted annually for inflation) with a 95% survival probability over a 30-year horizon.

2. The Economic Household Balance Sheet and Human Capital
A traditional balance sheet records only your tangible assets. Our economic balance sheet incorporates your final two years of projected executive earnings ($320,000), discounted at a bond-like rate of 3.50% to yield a current human capital value of $309,240. Because your employment income is stable and uncorrelated with stock market swings, your human capital has functioned throughout your career as an implicit, non-tradable municipal bond. As you retire and this bond-like asset amortizes to zero, your liquid financial portfolio must naturally reduce its equity allocation from 80% down to your retirement target of 60% equities and 40% fixed income ($1,200,000 equities and $800,000 bonds).

3. Tax-Aware Asset Location Optimization
Your portfolio is distributed across three tax buckets: $800,000 in your Traditional 401(k)/TDA, $800,000 in a Taxable brokerage account, and $400,000 in a Roth IRA. Rather than naively duplicating a 60/40 mix in every account, we deploy an optimal asset location strategy:

  • Traditional TDA ($800,000): We allocate all $800,000 of your fixed-income bonds here. In a taxable account, corporate bond interest is taxed annually at your top 32% ordinary income rate. Shielding bonds inside your TDA eliminates annual tax drag completely.
  • Roth IRA ($400,000): We allocate $400,000 of high-growth global equities here. Because Roth distributions are permanently tax-free, housing your highest-expected-return assets in Roth maximizes the absolute dollar value of your tax-free compounding shield.
  • Taxable Brokerage ($800,000): We allocate the remaining $800,000 of equities here. Equities in a taxable account benefit from preferential qualified dividend tax rates (15%–20%), complete tax deferral of capital gains until sale, tax-loss harvesting optionality, and a potential step-up in cost basis under Section 1014 for your heirs.
Over your 30-year retirement horizon, this location strategy captures an estimated $285,000 in incremental after-tax wealth—pure 'tax alpha' achieved without taking a single dollar of additional market risk.

4. Monte Carlo Decumulation: Moving-Block Bootstrap vs. Traditional Assumptions
Standard retail retirement calculators make the dangerous assumption that market returns are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) each year. In reality, economic history proves that financial markets exhibit multi-year persistence, volatility clustering, and extended valuation contractions (such as 1929–1932, 1973–1974, and 2000–2002). To capture these realistic risks, our model uses a moving-block bootstrap algorithm with 5-year contiguous blocks sampled across historical market data. By preserving consecutive negative years in our 1,000 simulated retirement lifetimes, our stress testing reflects real macroeconomic downturns rather than smoothed mathematical abstractions.

5. Sequence-of-Returns Risk and Spending Guardrails
The greatest hazard facing your retirement is not a market crash in Year 25; it is a severe bear market in Years 1 through 5. During your working years, the order of returns did not matter: multiplying returns together yields the exact same final number regardless of whether good or bad years come first. But in retirement, when you are withdrawing $80,000 each year to live, a 20% market drop forces you to sell twice as many shares to generate that cash. Those liquidated shares are permanently gone; when the market eventually rebounds, your remaining balance is too small to recover.

To insulate you against this sequence-of-returns risk, we establish two vital safeguards:

  1. The Three-Year Liquidity Buffer: We maintain $240,000 (three years of living expenses) in liquid, short-term Treasury bills within your fixed-income sleeve. If a severe equity bear market strikes during your early retirement, we fund your living expenses entirely from this cash buffer, allowing your equity holdings to recover without forced liquidations.
  2. Dynamic Spending Guardrails: While your baseline plan supports $80,000/year, we implement a Guyton-Klinger guardrail. If your total portfolio drops by more than 20% due to an extraordinary market crash, we will temporarily trim discretionary travel spending by 10% ($8,000) until the market recovers. This modest temporary belt-tightening virtually eliminates portfolio exhaustion risk.

Dear Client,
You have $2,000,000 in your accounts. You are retiring soon, so you have almost no human capital left. We recommend a 60/40 portfolio because that is the standard balanced portfolio for people your age.

You have a Taxable account, a 401(k), and a Roth. We recommend putting 60% stocks and 40% bonds in all three accounts so that each account is properly diversified. Roth is good because it is tax free, but 401(k) gives you tax deductions.

We ran a Monte Carlo simulation using historical stock returns. The average return of the stock market is about 9% and bonds return 4%, so your portfolio should average 7%. With inflation at 3%, you have a 4% real return.

Under the 4% rule created by Bengen in 1994, you can safely withdraw 4% of your money, which is $80,000 every year adjusted for inflation. You should not worry about market drops because over 30 years the market always goes up in the end. Average returns will protect your portfolio.

If you have any questions about your investments, please feel free to call our office. We look forward to managing your wealth.
