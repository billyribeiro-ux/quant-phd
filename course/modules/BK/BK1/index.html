Banking & Asset-Liability Management

Commercial banks intermediate between savers and borrowers by issuing liquid, short-term liabilities to fund illiquid, long-term credit assets. This fundamental maturity transformation generates net interest margin but exposes the banking book to structural interest rate and liquidity risks. In this module, you will analyze why delegated monitoring justifies the existence of banks, formalize the mechanics of repricing gaps, evaluate Economic Value of Equity (EVE) and Net Interest Income (NII) sensitivities under yield curve shocks, quantify the interest rate hedge provided by the deposit franchise and empirical deposit betas, and examine the regulatory architecture of Basel III capital requirements, the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), and the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR). The module concludes with the design and stress-testing of an enterprise Asset-Liability Management (ALM) engine.

  • Prerequisites: FC1 The Financial System, Markets & Instruments, FI1 Fixed Income Instruments & Markets, and AC1 Financial Accounting & Statement Analysis
  • Project: Asset-Liability Management (ALM) Engine with executive memo (Graded by both autograder and rubric)
  • Data: U.S. Treasury constant-maturity yields and Treasury bill rates from Federal Reserve H.15 (ust_daily.csv) for empirical rate shocks; balance sheets are SIMULATED.

64 h lessons · 26 h project (28.9% of the 90 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of BK1 you can:

  1. Derive the delegated monitoring cost savings of financial intermediation over direct bilateral lending and prove the credit risk diversification boundary as loan portfolio size grows (Lesson 1).
  2. Model the structural refinancing gap arising from maturity transformation and evaluate cash flow shortfalls under liability reset shocks (Lesson 2).
  3. Deconstruct Net Interest Margin (NIM) into earning asset yields and liability costs, and calculate NIM compression under asymmetric interest rate adjustments (Lesson 3).
  4. Construct multi-bucket repricing gap schedules, prove the algebraic identity linking periodic gaps to total rate-sensitive positions, and estimate 1-year earnings sensitivity (Lesson 4).
  5. Calculate Economic Value of Equity (EVE) under monthly compounding conventions and quantify EVE impairment across parallel shifts and yield curve twists (Lesson 5).
  6. Formalize the deposit franchise as an interest rate hedge, derive the linearity of NII sensitivity with respect to deposit beta, and critique franchise stability under run conditions (Lesson 6).
  7. Compute Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) and Basel III regulatory capital ratios (CET1, Tier 1, Total Capital) and solve for exact credit loss solvency thresholds (Lesson 7).
  8. Evaluate the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) under 30-day severe cash run-off scenarios and assess structural 1-year funding balance using the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) (Lesson 8).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions testing prerequisite knowledge across FC1, FI1, AC1, and quantitative foundations, alongside two skill checks testing ALM modeling competencies taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80% (7 of 8 items correct). To place out of rungs 1 and 2, you must satisfy the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the balance sheet equation from AC1, how is shareholders' equity defined?

    Options

    Assets equal Liabilities plus Equity; therefore, Equity equals Total Assets minus Total Liabilities (AC1, balance sheet).

  2. What is the present value of $100 payable in 1 year at an annual discount rate of 5.00% under annual compounding?

    \(PV = 100 / (1 + 0.05) = 95.238\) (M1, present value).

  3. A bond portfolio has a modified duration of 4.5 years. According to first-order duration approximation, what is the percentage price change (\(\Delta P / P\)) for a +100 bp (+0.01) rise in yield?

    \(\Delta P / P \approx -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y = -4.5 \times (+0.01) = -0.045 = -4.5\%\) (FI1, duration).

  4. In FC1, what primary mechanism enables commercial banks to create money in the modern financial system?

    Options

    Commercial banks create deposit money endogenously whenever they originate loans, crediting borrower deposit liabilities against new loan assets (FC1, bank money creation).

  5. Which evidence grade is assigned to a mathematical theorem proven deductively from explicit assumptions?

    Options

    Grade T represents mathematical theorems and algebraic identities derived without empirical error (P0.1, evidence grade).

  6. If a yield curve has 1-year spot rate 3.00% and 2-year spot rate 4.00%, what is the forward rate \(f(1, 2)\) for the second year under annual compounding?

    Options

    \((1 + 0.04)^2 / (1 + 0.03) - 1 = 1.0816 / 1.03 - 1 \approx 0.0501\) or 5.01% (FI1, forward rate).

  7. Skill check (rung 1). A bank holds $10,000 (1,000,000 cents) of floating loans that reprice at month 6, and $5,000 (500,000 cents) of non-maturity deposits with a deposit beta of 0.50. Under a +100 bp (+0.01) parallel rate shock over a 12-month horizon, what is the net change in Net Interest Income in cents?

    Asset interest income increases by \(1,000,000 \times 0.01 \times \frac{12 - 6}{12} = +5,000\) cents. Deposit interest expense increases by \(500,000 \times 0.50 \times 0.01 = +2,500\) cents. Net NII change is \(5,000 - 2,500 = +2,500\) cents (project rung 1).

  8. Skill check (rung 2). A bank has $100,000 of assets repricing in 3 months (weight 9/12) and $60,000 of liabilities repricing in 6 months (weight 6/12), funded by $80,000 of deposits with beta 0.50. Under a +100 bp rate rise, what is the net interest income change in dollars?

    Asset income: \(100,000 \times 0.01 \times (9/12) = \$750\). Liability expense: \(60,000 \times 0.01 \times (6/12) = \$300\). Deposit expense: \(80,000 \times 0.50 \times 0.01 = \$400\). Net NII change: \(750 - 300 - 400 = \$50\) (or 5,000 cents) (project rung 2).

Lessons

Eight lessons of eight study hours each, summing to 64 lesson hours. Each lesson develops core theoretical principles, includes a fully reproducible Python lab twin, clarifies common industry misconceptions, provides a 12-problem worked problem set across mechanics, proof, empirical, and research tiers, summarizes key results with formal evidence grades, and concludes with a retrieval quiz.

L01 · 8 h Why Banks Exist: Delegated Monitoring Diamond's (1984) delegated monitoring framework, duplication of monitoring costs in direct credit markets, and portfolio diversification boundaries. L02 · 8 h Maturity Transformation and Refinancing Risk The mechanics of borrowing short to lend long, structural liquidity mismatch, rollover rollover risk, and multi-period refinancing shortfall arithmetic. L03 · 8 h Net Interest Margin and Earning Asset Dynamics Deconstructing Net Interest Margin (NIM), yield-cost spreads, earning asset turnover, and margin compression under asymmetric rate resets. L04 · 8 h Repricing Gap Analysis Time bucket scheduling, Rate-Sensitive Assets (RSA) vs Liabilities (RSL), periodic gaps, the summation identity, and cumulative 1-year gap forecasting. L05 · 8 h Economic Value of Equity and NII Sensitivity Balance sheet present valuation, duration gap mismatch, Taylor expansion bounds, parallel rate shocks, and empirical yield curve twists from Federal Reserve H.15 data. L06 · 8 h The Deposit Franchise and Deposit Beta Market power in non-maturity deposits, empirical deposit betas (Drechsler et al. 2017, 2021), deposit franchise hedging, and run vulnerability during tightening (Jiang et al. 2024). L07 · 8 h Basel III Capital Requirements and Solvency Capital tiers (CET1, Tier 1, Total Capital), Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) density, regulatory capital ratios, credit write-down absorption, and distance-to-insolvency. L08 · 8 h Liquidity Regulation: LCR and NSFR Basel III liquidity standards, 30-day acute stress survival under the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), and 1-year structural funding equilibrium under the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR).

Project Ladder: ALM Engine

The unit culminate in building an enterprise Asset-Liability Management (ALM) engine (alm.py) and authoring an executive ALCO risk memo (memo.md). The 4-rung ladder structures your progression:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Step-by-step walkthrough of a 5-position SIMULATED banking book, calculating repricing gaps, 12-month NII sensitivity under +100 bp, and EVE impacts under parallel and twist shocks in exact cents.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Implement the core NII tenor weighting formula \(\frac{12 - m}{12}\) and deposit pass-through expense in an interactive completion lab graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Implement alm.py to published API specifications, passing strict autograder invariants across 50 seeded balance sheets, and draft the ALCO executive memo evaluated against an observable 5-criterion rubric.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch extensions

    Extend the engine to value non-maturity core deposits dynamically as interest rate swaptions, incorporating interest rate path simulation and non-linear deposit run-off models.
