To forecast how interest rate movements alter annual net interest income, bank treasury departments organize every position on the balance sheet into time buckets according to when interest rates reset. This scheduling methodology is repricing gap analysis. By comparing rate-sensitive assets against rate-sensitive liabilities across discrete maturity windows, the repricing gap identifies whether a bank faces asset-sensitive expansion or liability-sensitive margin squeeze over specified planning horizons. Understanding the mathematical articulation between periodic bucket gaps and cumulative 1-year earnings sensitivity is an essential competency for quantitative bank risk managers.

1Time Bucket Scheduling and Rate Sensitivity

Every asset and liability on a bank's balance sheet either reprices at contractual reset dates (for floating-rate instruments), matures at fixed dates (for fixed-rate loans and bonds), or does not reprice at all (for physical premises, cash in vault, and pure common equity).

Definition 1.1 (repricing gap and rate-sensitive positions)

An asset is a Rate-Sensitive Asset (\(\mathrm{RSA}\)) over horizon \(H\) if its contractual interest rate resets or its principal matures and must be reinvested within horizon \(H\). A liability is a Rate-Sensitive Liability (\(\mathrm{RSL}\)) over horizon \(H\) if its interest rate resets or its principal matures and must be refinanced within horizon \(H\).

The repricing gap (\(\mathrm{Gap}_H\)) over horizon \(H\) is the dollar difference:

\[ \mathrm{Gap}_H = \mathrm{RSA}_H - \mathrm{RSL}_H \]

Positions that do not reprice or mature within horizon \(H\) are non-rate-sensitive.

Definition 1.2 (standard regulatory time buckets)

Bank risk management partitions the time horizon into five mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive repricing buckets based on whole months \(m\) to contractual reset:

  • Bucket 0 (0 to 3 months): \(0 < m \le 3\)
  • Bucket 1 (3 to 6 months): \(3 < m \le 6\)
  • Bucket 2 (6 to 12 months): \(6 < m \le 12\)
  • Bucket 3 (1 to 5 years): \(12 < m \le 60\)
  • Bucket 4 (over 5 years): \(m > 60\)

Positions with \(m = \text{None}\) (e.g. non-earning cash, premises, common equity) do not reprice and are excluded from all repricing buckets.

Proposition 1.3 (repricing gap summation identity)

For each bucket \(b \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}\), let periodic gap be \(G_b = \mathrm{RSA}_b - \mathrm{RSL}_b\). Then the sum of all periodic gaps identically equals the total rate-sensitive assets minus total rate-sensitive liabilities across the banking book:

\[ \sum_{b=0}^4 G_b = \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSA}_b - \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSL}_b = \mathrm{RSA}_{\text{total}} - \mathrm{RSL}_{\text{total}} \]
Proof

By Definition 1.2, the buckets form a partition of the set of all rate-sensitive tenors \(m \in (0, \infty)\). Every rate-sensitive asset position \(i\) has a unique tenor \(m_i > 0\) that maps to exactly one bucket \(b \in \{0, \dots, 4\}\). Therefore, \(\mathrm{RSA}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i} A_i = \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSA}_b\). Similarly, \(\mathrm{RSL}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{j} L_j = \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSL}_b\). Summing the periodic gaps gives \(\sum_{b=0}^4 G_b = \sum_{b=0}^4 (\mathrm{RSA}_b - \mathrm{RSL}_b) = \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSA}_b - \sum_{b=0}^4 \mathrm{RSL}_b = \mathrm{RSA}_{\text{total}} - \mathrm{RSL}_{\text{total}}\). Non-rate-sensitive positions carry \(m = \text{None}\), are assigned to no bucket, and contribute zero to both sides. \(\blacksquare\)

2Cumulative Gap and Earnings Sensitivity

The cumulative gap through bucket \(k\) is \(\mathrm{CG}_k = \sum_{b=0}^k G_b\). Of central importance to bank asset-liability committees (ALCO) is the 1-year cumulative gap \(\mathrm{CG}_2 = G_0 + G_1 + G_2\), which measures net balance sheet rate exposure over a standard 12-month budget cycle.

Theorem 1.4 (12-month NII sensitivity under parallel shock)

Consider a 12-month horizon (\(H = 12\)). Let each rate-sensitive position \(i\) reprice at integer month \(m_i \in \{1, \dots, 12\}\). Under a parallel interest rate shift \(\Delta r\), the exact 12-month change in Net Interest Income is:

\[ \Delta \mathrm{NII}_{12} = \sum_{i \in \text{Assets}, 1 \le m_i \le 12} A_i \cdot \Delta r \cdot \left(\frac{12 - m_i}{12}\right) - \sum_{j \in \text{Liab}, 1 \le m_j \le 12} L_j \cdot \Delta r \cdot \left(\frac{12 - m_j}{12}\right) \]

If all positions within bucket \(b\) reprice at effective mean month \(\bar{m}_b\), the first-order approximation relates directly to the 1-year cumulative gap:

\[ \Delta \mathrm{NII}_{12} \approx \mathrm{CG}_2 \cdot \Delta r \cdot \bar{w} \]

where \(\bar{w} \in (0, 1)\) is the average time-weighting factor across the 12-month horizon.

Proof

When an asset of principal \(A_i\) reprices at month \(m_i \le 12\), it earns the new rate \(r_0 + \Delta r\) for the remaining \(12 - m_i\) months of the year. The additional interest income earned is \(\Delta \mathrm{II}_i = A_i \cdot \Delta r \cdot \frac{12 - m_i}{12}\). Positions with \(m_i > 12\) do not reprice within 12 months, earning \(\Delta \mathrm{II}_i = 0\). Non-rate-sensitive assets (\(m = \text{None}\)) likewise earn \(\Delta \mathrm{II} = 0\). Summing over all assets yields \(\Delta \mathrm{II} = \sum_{i, m_i \le 12} A_i \Delta r \frac{12 - m_i}{12}\). Applying identical logic to liabilities, interest expense changes by \(\Delta \mathrm{IE} = \sum_{j, m_j \le 12} L_j \Delta r \frac{12 - m_j}{12}\). Subtracting \(\Delta \mathrm{IE}\) from \(\Delta \mathrm{II}\) gives the exact formula. Factoring out \(\Delta r\) and approximating the weighted sums by the unweighted total \(\mathrm{CG}_2 = \sum_{b=0}^2 (\mathrm{RSA}_b - \mathrm{RSL}_b)\) scaled by average weight \(\bar{w} \approx 0.50\) gives the standard first-order relation. \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.5 (gap schedule and NII estimation)

A bank balance sheet has Bucket 0 (0-3m) RSA = \$500k, RSL = \$500k (\(G_0 = 0\)); Bucket 1 (3-6m) RSA = \$400k, RSL = \$450k (\(G_1 = -\$50\text{k}\)); Bucket 2 (6-12m) RSA = \$350k, RSL = \$300k (\(G_2 = +\$50\text{k}\)). The 1-year cumulative gap is \(\mathrm{CG}_2 = 0 + (-50\text{k}) + (+50\text{k}) = \$0\). Because rate-sensitive assets and liabilities repricing within 12 months are equal in total volume, the bank has zero net gap over the 1-year horizon, neutralizing first-order annual NII sensitivity to parallel rate shifts.

Lab 4 · Repricing Gap Schedule and Invariant Verification

Verifying that for a multi-position banking book, the sum of periodic bucket gaps identically matches total RSA minus total RSL, and boundary items are allocated correctly according to the strict interval definitions.

"""Lab BK1-L04-1: Repricing gap analysis and time bucket scheduling.

Repricing gap analysis groups rate-sensitive assets (RSA) and rate-sensitive liabilities (RSL)
into maturity/repricing time buckets:
    Bucket 0: 0 < m <= 3 months
    Bucket 1: 3 < m <= 6 months
    Bucket 2: 6 < m <= 12 months
    Bucket 3: 12 < m <= 60 months (1 to 5 years)
    Bucket 4: m > 60 months (> 5 years)

For each bucket b, the periodic repricing gap is G_b = RSA_b - RSL_b.
The cumulative gap through bucket k is CG_k = sum_{b=0}^k G_b.
The 1-year cumulative gap CG_2 is the primary indicator of short-term interest rate risk.

This lab simulates:
1. A multi-asset, multi-liability banking book (SIMULATED, in cents).
2. Calculation of periodic and cumulative gaps.
3. Verification of the gap summation identity and boundary conditions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Position:
    name: str
    cents: int
    months: int | None
    side: str  # "asset" or "liability"


# SIMULATED bank positions in cents
POSITIONS: list[Position] = [
    # Assets
    Position("Cash & Reserves", 15_000_000, None, "asset"),  # Non-rate-sensitive
    Position("Fed Funds Sold", 20_000_000, 1, "asset"),  # Bucket 0
    Position("3-Month T-Bills", 30_000_000, 3, "asset"),  # Bucket 0 (boundary)
    Position("Floating CRE Loans", 40_000_000, 5, "asset"),  # Bucket 1
    Position("1-Year Fixed Auto Loans", 35_000_000, 12, "asset"),  # Bucket 2 (boundary)
    Position("3-Year Fixed Mortgages", 80_000_000, 36, "asset"),  # Bucket 3
    Position("10-Year Fixed Treasury", 50_000_000, 120, "asset"),  # Bucket 4
    # Liabilities
    Position("Non-interest Checking", 25_000_000, None, "liability"),  # Non-rate-sensitive
    Position("Wholesale Repo Funding", 50_000_000, 2, "liability"),  # Bucket 0
    Position("6-Month Term CDs", 45_000_000, 6, "liability"),  # Bucket 1 (boundary)
    Position("9-Month Brokered Deposits", 30_000_000, 9, "liability"),  # Bucket 2
    Position("3-Year Senior Bank Notes", 60_000_000, 36, "liability"),  # Bucket 3
    Position("10-Year Subordinated Debt", 40_000_000, 120, "liability"),  # Bucket 4
]


def bucket_index(months: int) -> int:
    """Map months to bucket index 0..4."""
    if months <= 0:
        raise ValueError("months must be positive")
    if months <= 3:
        return 0
    elif months <= 6:
        return 1
    elif months <= 12:
        return 2
    elif months <= 60:
        return 3
    else:
        return 4


def compute_gap_schedule(positions: list[Position]) -> list[dict[str, int]]:
    """Compute RSA, RSL, and Periodic Gap for each bucket 0..4."""
    rsa = [0] * 5
    rsl = [0] * 5

    for p in positions:
        if p.months is None:
            continue
        b = bucket_index(p.months)
        if p.side == "asset":
            rsa[b] += p.cents
        else:
            rsl[b] += p.cents

    schedule: list[dict[str, int]] = []
    cumulative = 0
    for b in range(5):
        gap = rsa[b] - rsl[b]
        cumulative += gap
        schedule.append(
            {
                "bucket": b,
                "rsa_cents": rsa[b],
                "rsl_cents": rsl[b],
                "gap_cents": gap,
                "cum_gap_cents": cumulative,
            }
        )
    return schedule


def main() -> None:
    print("A. Repricing Gap Schedule by Bucket (SIMULATED, in cents)")
    labels = ["0-3m", "3-6m", "6-12m", "1-5y", ">5y"]
    schedule = compute_gap_schedule(POSITIONS)

    for row in schedule:
        b = row["bucket"]
        print(
            f"Bucket {b} ({labels[b]:5s}) | RSA: {row['rsa_cents']:10d} | "
            f"RSL: {row['rsl_cents']:10d} | Periodic Gap: {row['gap_cents']:+11d} | "
            f"Cumulative Gap: {row['cum_gap_cents']:+11d} cents"
        )

    print()
    print("B. Key Gap Totals and 1-Year Repricing Risk")
    one_year_cum_gap = schedule[2]["cum_gap_cents"]
    print(f"1-Year Cumulative Repricing Gap (CG_2): {one_year_cum_gap:+11d} cents")
    # For a +100 bp rate rise, first-order estimated NII impact: CG_2 * 0.01
    approx_nii_100bp = one_year_cum_gap // 100
    print(f"First-order estimated NII change for +100 bp shock: {approx_nii_100bp:+10d} cents")

    print()
    print("C. Algebraic Invariant Checks")
    total_rsa = sum(p.cents for p in POSITIONS if p.months is not None and p.side == "asset")
    total_rsl = sum(p.cents for p in POSITIONS if p.months is not None and p.side == "liability")
    sum_periodic_gaps = sum(row["gap_cents"] for row in schedule)
    final_cum_gap = schedule[4]["cum_gap_cents"]

    print(f"Sum of periodic gaps equals total RSA minus total RSL: {sum_periodic_gaps == total_rsa - total_rsl}")
    print(f"Final cumulative gap equals sum of periodic gaps: {final_cum_gap == sum_periodic_gaps}")
    # Boundary tests
    print(f"3-month position placed in bucket 0 (0-3m): {bucket_index(3) == 0}")
    print(f"12-month position placed in bucket 2 (6-12m): {bucket_index(12) == 2}")

Where the Canon Breaks

While repricing gap analysis is universally utilized by bank regulators and ALCO executives, its assumptions fail in fundamental market environments:

  • Non-parallel rate shocks: Gap analysis assumes all tenors shift by an identical parallel \(\Delta r\). If the yield curve inverts or twists (e.g., 3-month rates rise 400 bp while 10-year rates rise 150 bp), a single gap figure cannot capture the net interest impact.
  • Basis risk: Assets and liabilities repricing within the same bucket may be tied to different rate benchmarks (e.g. SOFR loans funded by Prime-indexed commercial deposits). Even if \(\mathrm{RSA} = \mathrm{RSL}\), widening basis spreads alter net interest income.
  • Ignoring cash flow timing within buckets: All positions within a 3-month or 1-year bucket are lumped together. A liability repricing on day 1 incurs nearly 365 days of higher expense, whereas one repricing on day 364 incurs only 1 day of higher expense.
  • Core deposit non-contractual maturities: Non-maturity checking and savings deposits have an effective contractual maturity of 0 (immediate withdrawal), but behavioral maturities of 3 to 10 years due to customer inertia. Treating them as Bucket 0 distorts bank risk profiles.

3Misconceptions

  1. “A bank with a zero 1-year cumulative gap (\(\mathrm{CG}_2 = 0\)) has zero interest rate risk.”

    Zero 1-year cumulative gap immunizes only 1-year accrual Net Interest Income against parallel shocks. It does not immunize the Economic Value of Equity (EVE). A bank with $100M of 1-year floating assets and $100M of 1-year liabilities has \(\mathrm{CG}_2 = 0\), but if it also holds $500M of 30-year fixed mortgages funded by 3-year term debt (both in Buckets 3 and 4), EVE will suffer devastating losses when long-term discount rates rise.

  2. “Positions with no stated maturity should be assigned to the longest bucket (> 5 years).”

    Non-rate-sensitive positions such as cash, premises, and equity have no contractual repricing dates (\(m = \text{None}\)) and must be excluded from repricing gap schedules entirely. Including them in long-term buckets falsely inflates asset or liability duration.

  3. “Bucket gaps must be computed on market values rather than book values.”

    Repricing gap analysis is an accrual accounting cash flow tool designed to measure Net Interest Income. It operates strictly on contractual principal cash flows (book value or par). Market value sensitivity is captured by Economic Value of Equity (EVE) and duration gap analysis, not by repricing gap schedules.

4Problem Set

Mechanics

M1 · Single bucket gap and NII impact

A bank has \$120,000,000 in Rate-Sensitive Assets and \$150,000,000 in Rate-Sensitive Liabilities repricing in Bucket 0. Compute the periodic repricing gap \(G_0\) in dollars. If interest rates rise by +150 bp, estimate the first-order NII change assuming an average time weight of \(\bar{w} = 0.85\).

Solution

The gap is \(G_0 = \mathrm{RSA}_0 - \mathrm{RSL}_0 = \$120,000,000 - \$150,000,000 = -\$30,000,000\). The estimated NII change is \(\Delta \mathrm{NII} \approx G_0 \times \Delta r \times \bar{w} = -\$30,000,000 \times 0.0150 \times 0.85 = -\$382,500\).

M2 · Cumulative gap computation

Given periodic gaps \(G_0 = +\$10\text{M}\), \(G_1 = -\$25\text{M}\), and \(G_2 = +\$30\text{M}\), compute cumulative gaps \(\mathrm{CG}_0\), \(\mathrm{CG}_1\), and \(\mathrm{CG}_2\) in millions of dollars.

Solution

\(\mathrm{CG}_0 = G_0 = +\$10\text{M}\). \(\mathrm{CG}_1 = \mathrm{CG}_0 + G_1 = \$10\text{M} - \$25\text{M} = -\$15\text{M}\). \(\mathrm{CG}_2 = \mathrm{CG}_1 + G_2 = -\$15\text{M} + \$30\text{M} = +\$15\text{M}\). Over the 1-year horizon, the bank is asset-sensitive by \$15M.

M3 · Exact time-weighted NII change

A bank holds one asset of \$10,000,000 repricing at month 3 and one liability of \$8,000,000 repricing at month 9. Under a +200 bp (+0.02) parallel shock, compute the exact 12-month change in Net Interest Income in dollars.

Solution

Asset income gain: \(\$10,000,000 \times 0.02 \times \frac{12 - 3}{12} = \$200,000 \times \frac{9}{12} = \$150,000\). Liability expense increase: \(\$8,000,000 \times 0.02 \times \frac{12 - 9}{12} = \$160,000 \times \frac{3}{12} = \$40,000\). Net NII change is \(\$150,000 - \$40,000 = +\$110,000\).

Proof

P1 · Cumulative gap recursion

Prove by induction that for any sequence of periodic gaps \(G_b\), the cumulative gap satisfies the recursive relation \(\mathrm{CG}_k = \mathrm{CG}_{k-1} + G_k\) for all \(k \ge 1\), with base case \(\mathrm{CG}_0 = G_0\).

Solution

By definition, \(\mathrm{CG}_k = \sum_{b=0}^k G_b\). For \(k = 0\), \(\mathrm{CG}_0 = \sum_{b=0}^0 G_b = G_0\). For \(k \ge 1\), \(\mathrm{CG}_k = \sum_{b=0}^k G_b = \left(\sum_{b=0}^{k-1} G_b\right) + G_k = \mathrm{CG}_{k-1} + G_k\). This establishes the exact linear recurrence relation used in ALM scheduling algorithms.

P2 · Preservation of summation under refinement

Prove that subdividing a repricing bucket \((T_1, T_2]\) into two sub-buckets \((T_1, T^*]\) and \((T^*, T_2]\) preserves the total cumulative gap across the full horizon.

Solution

Let original bucket have \(\mathrm{RSA} = \sum_{i \in (T_1, T_2]} A_i\) and \(\mathrm{RSL} = \sum_{j \in (T_1, T_2]} L_j\), so \(G = \mathrm{RSA} - \mathrm{RSL}\). Partition \((T_1, T_2]\) into disjoint sets \(S_1 = (T_1, T^*]\) and \(S_2 = (T^*, T_2]\). Then \(\mathrm{RSA} = \mathrm{RSA}_1 + \mathrm{RSA}_2\) and \(\mathrm{RSL} = \mathrm{RSL}_1 + \mathrm{RSL}_2\). The sum of sub-bucket gaps is \(G_1 + G_2 = (\mathrm{RSA}_1 - \mathrm{RSL}_1) + (\mathrm{RSA}_2 - \mathrm{RSL}_2) = (\mathrm{RSA}_1 + \mathrm{RSA}_2) - (\mathrm{RSL}_1 + \mathrm{RSL}_2) = \mathrm{RSA} - \mathrm{RSL} = G\). Hence refinement preserves total gap sums identically.

P3 · Monotonicity of NII sensitivity in asset gap

Prove that for any fixed positive parallel shock \(\Delta r > 0\) and fixed liability schedule, increasing Rate-Sensitive Assets in any month \(m \le 12\) strictly increases 12-month Net Interest Income sensitivity.

Solution

From Theorem 1.4, \(\frac{\partial (\Delta \mathrm{NII})}{\partial A_i} = \Delta r \cdot \frac{12 - m_i}{12}\). For any month \(m_i < 12\), \(\frac{12 - m_i}{12} > 0\). Since by hypothesis \(\Delta r > 0\), the partial derivative satisfies \(\frac{\partial (\Delta \mathrm{NII})}{\partial A_i} > 0\). Thus, holding liabilities constant, adding rate-sensitive assets at any tenor prior to month 12 strictly increases net interest income sensitivity.

Research

R1 · Dynamic balance sheet simulation

Why do modern bank ALM systems combine static repricing gap schedules with dynamic multi-scenario Monte Carlo simulations?

Solution

Static repricing gaps assume no future asset growth, constant liability balances, and contractual maturity rollovers. Dynamic ALM models project future business strategy: new loan origination volumes, deposit pricing competition, credit line draws under liquidity stress, and borrower prepayment behavior conditional on stochastic yield curve paths.

R2 · Behavioral life of non-maturity deposits

How do commercial banks estimate the behavioral decay rate of non-maturity checking accounts, and why does this alter the repricing gap?

Solution

Banks apply statistical run-off models and survival analysis (e.g. log-logistic survival distributions) to historical customer account balances. Core deposits exhibit low attrition and high price insensitivity, allowing banks to model a significant core deposit fraction as possessing a 5-to-10-year behavioral maturity, shifting balances from Bucket 0 to Bucket 3.

R3 · Regulatory interest rate risk standards (IRRBB)

How does the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision's standard on Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB) use repricing gaps to establish supervisory outlier test thresholds?

Solution

Under the BCBS IRRBB framework, supervisors mandate that banks report standardized cash flow schedules across 19 maturity buckets. Banks are subjected to 6 standardized interest rate shock scenarios (+/-200 bp parallel, steepener, flattener, short rates up, short rates down). A bank whose projected 12-month \(\Delta \mathrm{NII}\) or \(\Delta \mathrm{EVE}\) exceeds 15% of Tier 1 Capital is designated a supervisory outlier requiring immediate capital remediation.

    \(10\text{M} \times 0.02 \times \frac{9}{12} - 8\text{M} \times 0.02 \times \frac{3}{12} = \$150,000 - \$40,000 = \$110,000\) (Problem M3).
