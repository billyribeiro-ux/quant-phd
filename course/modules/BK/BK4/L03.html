The CCP Default Waterfall and Margin Cover

  • Uses: central counterparty and multilateral netting (L02); the algebra of integer sums and floors
  • Introduces: CCP default waterfall; margin cover level
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Cworking paper, practitioner research or single sample
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

In this model, when a member of a central counterparty defaults, its loss is paid from a fixed sequence of resources, and the sequence decides who bears the loss first. This lesson defines that sequence as the CCP default waterfall and proves that its layers pay the loss exactly, with any part no layer covers named. It then defines the margin cover level, the share of scenario losses that the defaulter's margin pays in full, and proves that the smallest margin reaching a target cover is the k-th smallest scenario loss, for a k that the target fixes. Lab 1 computes both cases in integer cents and checks each sizing answer by brute force.

1The waterfall for one default

Three resources pay a loss in this lesson, and the default funds of the surviving members pay what remains after them. The initial margin of a member is the collateral it posts to the CCP against its positions. The default fund of a member is its contribution to a pool held for defaults. The CCP capital is the clearing house's own equity committed to losses. The defaulter is the member that defaults, and a surviving member is any other member. Each loss is one input: the lesson takes the loss \(L\) as given and does not model how it arises from closing out the defaulter's positions or from netting. Section 3 names the close-out part of that limit.

Assumptions A1 and A2 (the layer order and the inputs)
  • A1 (layer order). A loss is paid from the layers of Definition 1.1, in that order. The order is a rule of this model. The lesson does not claim that any clearing house uses it.
  • A2 (inputs). One member \(\delta\) defaults. The loss \(L\), the initial margin \(\mathrm{IM}_i\) and the default fund \(\mathrm{DF}_i\) of each member \(i\), and the CCP capital \(K\) are non-negative integers in cents.
Definition 1.1 (CCP default waterfall)

Under A1 and A2, let \(\mathcal{V}\) be the set of surviving members, and let \(D = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} \mathrm{DF}_i\) be their total default fund. The CCP default waterfall pays the loss \(L\) through three layers, in this order:

  1. the defaulter's initial margin pays \(a_1 = \min(L, \mathrm{IM}_\delta)\);
  2. the defaulter's default fund pays \(a_2 = \min(L - a_1, \mathrm{DF}_\delta)\);
  3. the CCP capital pays \(a_3 = \min(L - a_1 - a_2, K)\).

The residual is \(Z = L - a_1 - a_2 - a_3\). If \(Z \ge D\), each surviving member \(i\) pays \(\mathrm{DF}_i\) and the uncovered loss is \(u = Z - D\). If \(Z < D\), each surviving member pays \(x_i = \lfloor Z\,\mathrm{DF}_i / D \rfloor\), and the leftover \(\ell = Z - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} x_i\) goes one cent each to the first \(\ell\) surviving members, in ascending member id, that have \(\mathrm{DF}_i > 0\). Then \(u = 0\). A member with \(\mathrm{DF}_i = 0\) pays nothing.

Proposition 1.2 (conservation)

Assuming A1 and A2, for every loss \(L \ge 0\), the layers and the survivor payments add to the loss: \[ a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} x_i + u = L, \] where \(x_i\) and \(u\) are the survivor payments and the uncovered loss of Definition 1.1, and \(x_i\) is taken to be \(\mathrm{DF}_i\) in the case \(Z \ge D\).

Proof

Each minimum is at most its own remaining argument. So \(a_1 \le L\), \(a_2 \le L - a_1\) and \(a_3 \le L - a_1 - a_2\), and \(Z \ge 0\). Case \(Z \ge D\): the survivors pay \(D\) in total, so the sum is \(a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + D + (Z - D) = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + Z = L\). Case \(Z < D\): the survivors pay \(\sum_i x_i + \ell\), and \(\sum_i x_i + \ell = Z\) by the definition of \(\ell\), with \(u = 0\). So the sum is \(a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + Z = L\). The second case needs \(\ell\) to be a non-negative integer that the surviving members with a positive default fund can absorb, which Proposition 1.3 establishes. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 1.3 (the survivor rule is well defined)

Assuming A1 and A2, suppose \(0 \le Z < D\). Let \(n^{+}\) be the number of surviving members with \(\mathrm{DF}_i > 0\). Then \(D > 0\), \(0 \le \ell \le n^{+} - 1\), and every survivor's payment satisfies \(x_i \le \mathrm{DF}_i\), and \(x_i + 1 \le \mathrm{DF}_i\) for every survivor with \(\mathrm{DF}_i > 0\) that receives a leftover cent.

Proof

Since \(Z \ge 0\) and \(Z < D\), we have \(D > 0\). Write \(p_i = Z\,\mathrm{DF}_i / D\), so \(\sum_i p_i = Z\) and \(x_i = \lfloor p_i \rfloor\). Then \(\ell = \sum_i (p_i - \lfloor p_i \rfloor)\), a sum of fractional parts. Each fractional part is non-negative, so \(\ell \ge 0\). A survivor with \(\mathrm{DF}_i = 0\) has \(p_i = 0\) and fractional part 0. Each other fractional part is strictly less than 1, so \(\ell < n^{+}\), and \(\ell\) is an integer, so \(\ell \le n^{+} - 1\). Hence the first \(\ell\) survivors with a positive default fund exist. Next, \(Z < D\) gives \(p_i < \mathrm{DF}_i\). Since \(\mathrm{DF}_i\) is an integer and \(x_i \le p_i\), we get \(x_i \le \mathrm{DF}_i - 1\) whenever \(\mathrm{DF}_i > 0\), so \(x_i + 1 \le \mathrm{DF}_i\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.4 (each layer waits for the one before it)

Under Definition 1.1, a layer pays only after the layer before it is used in full. Precisely: \(a_2 > 0\) implies \(a_1 = \mathrm{IM}_\delta\); \(a_3 > 0\) implies \(a_2 = \mathrm{DF}_\delta\); and \(Z > 0\) implies \(a_3 = K\). Proof: \(a_2 > 0\) gives \(L - a_1 > 0\), so \(a_1 < L\), and \(a_1 = \min(L, \mathrm{IM}_\delta) < L\) forces \(a_1 = \mathrm{IM}_\delta\). The other two follow the same way, using \(L - a_1 - a_2 > 0\) and \(L - a_1 - a_2 - a_3 > 0\).

Example 1.5 (the fixture of Lab 1, Part A1)

Let the defaulter be member 3, with \(\mathrm{IM}_3 = 100\), \(\mathrm{DF}_3 = 50\) and \(K = 30\). The survivors are members 1 and 2, with \(\mathrm{DF}_1 = 40\) and \(\mathrm{DF}_2 = 60\), so \(D = 100\). For \(L = 200\): \(a_1 = 100\), \(a_2 = \min(100, 50) = 50\), \(a_3 = \min(50, 30) = 30\), and \(Z = 200 - 180 = 20 < 100\). At \(L = 181\), \(Z = 1\), the shares are \(\lfloor 0.4 \rfloor = 0\) and \(\lfloor 0.6 \rfloor = 0\), and the leftover cent goes to member 1. 2Margin cover and the sizing rule

The next definition takes the defaulter's initial margin as a single number \(M\) and asks how many scenario losses it pays in full. By Remark 2.2, that is the case where the margin alone pays the loss in the waterfall of Section 1.

Definition 2.1 (margin cover level)

Let a scenario set consist of \(S\) losses \(L_1, \dots, L_S\), each a non-negative integer in cents, with \(S \ge 1\). For an integer margin \(M\) in cents, let \(c(M) = \#\{s : L_s \le M\}\) be the number of scenarios whose loss the margin covers in full. The margin cover level in basis points is \(\mathrm{cover}(M) = \lfloor 10000\,c(M) / S \rfloor\). Its basis is the count of scenarios in the set, floored to a whole basis point. It is not a dollar-weighted share and not an expected value.

Remark 2.2 (the margin alone pays the loss)

Let a loss \(L\) and the defaulter's initial margin \(\mathrm{IM}_\delta = M\) enter Definition 1.1. Then \(a_1 = \min(L, M)\) equals \(L\) if and only if \(L \le M\). So a loss is covered in full by the margin alone exactly when it is counted in \(c(M)\) of Definition 2.1. A loss equal to the margin is counted, because \(L = M\) satisfies \(L \le M\).

Assumptions A3 and A4 (the target and the scenario set)
  • A3 (target). The target cover \(t\) is an integer in basis points with \(1 \le t \le 10000\).
  • A4 (scenario set). The scenario set is non-empty, with \(S \ge 1\) losses that are non-negative integers in cents.
Proposition 2.3 (the sized margin is an order statistic)

Assuming A3 and A4, sort the losses as \(L_{(1)} \le L_{(2)} \le \dots \le L_{(S)}\), with repetitions kept, and let \(k = \lceil t\,S / 10000 \rceil\). Then:

  1. \(1 \le k \le S\);
  2. for every integer margin \(M\), \(\mathrm{cover}(M) \ge t\) if and only if \(M \ge L_{(k)}\);
  3. the smallest integer margin with cover at least \(t\) is \(L_{(k)}\).
Proof

Part 1. Since \(t \ge 1\), \(tS / 10000 > 0\), so \(k \ge 1\). Since \(t \le 10000\), \(tS / 10000 \le S\), and \(S\) is an integer, so \(k \le S\).

Part 2. For an integer \(t\) and a real number \(y\), \(\lfloor y \rfloor \ge t\) if and only if \(y \ge t\). Applying this with \(y = 10000\,c(M) / S\): \(\mathrm{cover}(M) \ge t\) if and only if \(10000\,c(M) \ge tS\), that is \(c(M) \ge tS / 10000\). Since \(c(M)\) is an integer, \(c(M) \ge tS / 10000\) holds if and only if \(c(M) \ge k\). It remains to show that \(c(M) \ge k\) if and only if \(M \ge L_{(k)}\). If \(M \ge L_{(k)}\), then \(L_{(1)}, \dots, L_{(k)}\) are all at most \(M\), so \(c(M) \ge k\). If \(M < L_{(k)}\), then \(L_{(j)} > M\) for every \(j \ge k\), so at most the \(k - 1\) losses \(L_{(1)}, \dots, L_{(k-1)}\) are at most \(M\), and \(c(M) \le k - 1\).

Part 3. By Part 2, the integer margin \(L_{(k)}\) has cover at least \(t\), and every integer margin with cover at least \(t\) is at least \(L_{(k)}\). \(L_{(k)}\) is a non-negative integer, so it is the smallest. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 2.4 (the sized margin rises with the target)

Assuming A3 and A4, let \(t \le t'\) be two targets, with sized margins \(M(t)\) and \(M(t')\) given by Proposition 2.3. Then \(M(t) \le M(t')\).

Proof

Since \(t \le t'\), \(tS/10000 \le t'S/10000\). The ceiling function does not decrease, so \(k \le k'\). The sorted losses do not decrease with the index, so \(L_{(k)} \le L_{(k')}\). Proposition 2.3 gives \(M(t) = L_{(k)}\) and \(M(t') = L_{(k')}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.5 (two targets with 200 scenarios)

Let \(S = 200\). For \(t = 9900\), \(tS / 10000 = 198\) exactly, so \(k = 198\). For \(t = 9975\), \(tS / 10000 = 199.5\), so \(k = 200\), and the sized margin is the largest loss. Lab 1, Part B draws 200 seeded scenario losses and prints both sized margins, 2961 cents and 2992 cents, and the brute-force minimum agrees with each.

Remark 2.6 (what the cover does not count)

The cover of Definition 2.1 counts scenarios that the margin pays in full. It does not count scenarios that the default fund or the CCP capital pays. A scenario with loss above the margin is uncovered in this sense even when the waterfall pays all of it.

Lab 1 · The waterfall and the sizing rule on seeded SIMULATED inputs

The lab draws SIMULATED inputs from seed 2026, in integer cents. Part A1 runs the fixture of Example 1.5, and Part A2 runs the remainder case of Example 1.6. """BK4 lab L03: the CCP default waterfall and margin cover, in integer cents.

Standard library only; no network. Every input is generated from a fixed seed, so the inputs are SIMULATED and
every run prints the same numbers. All money is an int in cents, and every cover is an integer count of basis
points. Part A runs the layer order of Definition 1.1 on a fixture with hand-checkable amounts, on the remainder
rule with a three-survivor fixture, and on three seeded scenarios. Part B sizes the defaulter's margin for two cover
targets from seeded scenario losses with the order statistic of Proposition 2.3, then checks each sized margin by a
brute-force search over the integer margins 0, 1, 2, and so on.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 2026
MEMBERS = (1, 2, 3, 4)
DEFAULTER = 3
TARGETS_BPS = (9900, 9975)
SCENARIOS = 200
MAX_LOSS_CENTS = 3000


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Waterfall:
    """The payments for one loss, in cents, by layer.

    margin_cents, default_fund_cents and ccp_capital_cents are the three layers a1, a2 and a3 of Definition 1.1.
    survivor_cents maps each surviving member id to its payment from its default fund. uncovered_cents is the
    part of the loss that no layer pays.
    """

    margin_cents: int
    default_fund_cents: int
    ccp_capital_cents: int
    survivor_cents: dict[int, int]
    uncovered_cents: int

    def total_cents(self) -> int:
        """All payments for the loss, in cents. Proposition 1.2 says this equals the loss."""
        return (
            self.margin_cents
            + self.default_fund_cents
            + self.ccp_capital_cents
            + sum(self.survivor_cents.values())
            + self.uncovered_cents
        )


def survivor_payments(
    residual_cents: int, df_cents: dict[int, int], survivors: list[int]
) -> tuple[dict[int, int], int]:
    """Split the residual loss over the surviving default funds, in cents.

    If the residual is at least the total of the survivors' default funds, each survivor pays its whole default
    fund and the excess is uncovered. Otherwise each survivor pays its floor share of the residual in proportion
    to its default fund, and the leftover cents go one each to the first survivors, in ascending id order, that
    have a positive default fund. Returns the payments and the uncovered loss.
    """
    total = sum(df_cents[i] for i in survivors)
    if residual_cents >= total:
        return {i: df_cents[i] for i in survivors}, residual_cents - total
    pay = {i: residual_cents * df_cents[i] // total for i in survivors}
    leftover = residual_cents - sum(pay.values())
    for i in survivors:
        if leftover == 0:
            break
        if df_cents[i] > 0:
            pay[i] += 1
            leftover -= 1
    if leftover != 0:
        raise RuntimeError("the leftover cents were not all assigned")
    return pay, 0


def waterfall(
    loss_cents: int,
    defaulter: int,
    im_cents: dict[int, int],
    df_cents: dict[int, int],
    capital_cents: int,
) -> Waterfall:
    """Pay one loss in the layer order of Definition 1.1.

    The layers are the defaulter's initial margin, then the defaulter's default fund, then the CCP capital, then
    the surviving default funds. Each layer pays the smaller of the loss left and its own size. Inputs are
    non-negative integers in cents.
    """
    if loss_cents < 0:
        raise ValueError("loss cannot be negative")
    margin = min(loss_cents, im_cents[defaulter])
    fund = min(loss_cents - margin, df_cents[defaulter])
    capital = min(loss_cents - margin - fund, capital_cents)
    residual = loss_cents - margin - fund - capital
    survivors = sorted(i for i in df_cents if i != defaulter)
    pay, uncovered = survivor_payments(residual, df_cents, survivors)
    return Waterfall(margin, fund, capital, pay, uncovered)


def cover_count(losses_cents: list[int], margin_cents: int) -> int:
    """Number of scenarios whose loss the margin covers in full, that is the losses with loss <= margin."""
    return sum(1 for loss in losses_cents if loss <= margin_cents)


def cover_bps(losses_cents: list[int], margin_cents: int) -> int:
    """Margin cover in basis points, floored: 10000 times the covered count, divided by the scenario count."""
    return 10000 * cover_count(losses_cents, margin_cents) // len(losses_cents)


def sized_margin(losses_cents: list[int], target_bps: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """The smallest margin with cover at least target_bps, by the order statistic of Proposition 2.3.

    Returns the margin in cents and the index k = ceil(target_bps * S / 10000), computed in integers as
    -((-target_bps * S) // 10000), where S is the scenario count. The margin is the k-th smallest loss.
    """
    k = -((-target_bps * len(losses_cents)) // 10000)
    return sorted(losses_cents)[k - 1], k


def brute_force_margin(losses_cents: list[int], target_bps: int) -> int:
    """The smallest integer margin with cover at least target_bps, found by testing 0, 1, 2 in turn.

    The search uses no sorting and no formula. The largest loss covers every scenario, so the search always ends
    at or before the largest loss.
    """
    for margin in range(max(losses_cents) + 1):
        if cover_bps(losses_cents, margin) >= target_bps:
            return margin
    raise RuntimeError("no integer margin reaches the target")


def show(label: str, loss: int, w: Waterfall) -> None:
    """Print the layers of one loss and whether they sum to it."""
    survivors = ", ".join(f"member {i} {v}" for i, v in sorted(w.survivor_cents.items()))
    print(
        f"{label}: loss {loss}; margin {w.margin_cents}, default fund {w.default_fund_cents}, "
        f"CCP capital {w.ccp_capital_cents}; survivors {survivors}; uncovered {w.uncovered_cents}; "
        f"layers sum to the loss: {w.total_cents() == loss}"
    )


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    im = {i: rng.randint(1000, 5000) for i in MEMBERS}
    df = {i: rng.randint(100, 900) for i in MEMBERS}
    capital = rng.randint(500, 2000)
    im_line = ", ".join(f"member {i} {im[i]}" for i in MEMBERS)
    df_line = ", ".join(f"member {i} {df[i]}" for i in MEMBERS)
    print(f"SIMULATED inputs from seed {SEED}, in cents. Initial margins: {im_line}.")
    print(f"Default funds: {df_line}. CCP capital: {capital}. Defaulter: member {DEFAULTER}.")

    fixture_im = {1: 0, 2: 0, 3: 100}
    fixture_df = {1: 40, 2: 60, 3: 50}
    print("Part A1, fixture: defaulter 3 with margin 100, default fund 50, CCP capital 30; survivors 1 and 2 with")
    print("default funds 40 and 60. Each row is one loss.")
    fixture_ok = True
    for loss in (80, 120, 170, 180, 181, 200, 205, 300):
        w = waterfall(loss, 3, fixture_im, fixture_df, 30)
        fixture_ok = fixture_ok and w.total_cents() == loss
        show(f"loss {loss}", loss, w)
    print(f"every fixture row sums to its loss: {fixture_ok}")

    print("Part A2, remainder rule: defaulter 4 with no margin and no default fund; survivors 1, 2 and 3 with")
    print("default fund 1 each; CCP capital 0. Each row is one loss.")
    for loss in (1, 2):
        show(f"loss {loss}", loss, waterfall(loss, 4, {4: 0, 1: 0, 2: 0, 3: 0}, {1: 1, 2: 1, 3: 1, 4: 0}, 0))

    defaulter = DEFAULTER
    survivors_df = sum(df[i] for i in MEMBERS if i != defaulter)
    scenarios = (
        ("Part A3, within the margin", im[defaulter] // 2),
        ("Part A3, reaching the default fund", im[defaulter] + df[defaulter] // 2),
        ("Part A3, reaching the surviving members", im[defaulter] + df[defaulter] + capital + survivors_df // 2),
    )
    print("Part A3, seeded scenarios for defaulter 3:")
    for label, loss in scenarios:
        show(label, loss, waterfall(loss, defaulter, im, df, capital))

    losses = [rng.randint(0, MAX_LOSS_CENTS) for _ in range(SCENARIOS)]
    print(f"Part B, sizing: {SCENARIOS} seeded scenario losses from 0 to {MAX_LOSS_CENTS} cents (SIMULATED).")
    for target in TARGETS_BPS:
        margin, k = sized_margin(losses, target)
        brute = brute_force_margin(losses, target)
        print(f"target {target} bps: k = {k} of {SCENARIOS}; sized margin {margin} cents")
        print(
            f"cover at the sized margin {cover_bps(losses, margin)} bps; "
            f"cover one cent below {cover_bps(losses, margin - 1)} bps"
        )
        print(f"brute-force minimal margin {brute} cents; agrees with the sized margin: {brute == margin}")

3Where the canon breaks

  • The layer order is a model rule. A1 fixes the order of Definition 1.1. This lesson reads no clearing house's rulebook, and the registry holds no source on CCP waterfalls. So the order is a stated assumption, and no statement here says which order any real CCP uses.
  • The scenario set is an input. The cover of Definition 2.1 is a count over the \(S\) losses supplied to the model. It is not a probability, not an estimate from data, and not a forecast. At 9900 basis points with \(S = 200\), Lab 1 prints a cover of exactly 9900 basis points at the sized margin, which means 2 of the 200 scenarios lie above the margin.
  • No close-out period is modelled. The loss \(L\) enters as one number. The model does not show how it changes while the defaulter's positions are closed out, so Proposition 1.2 is an identity about the waterfall and says nothing about the path of the loss.
  • One default at a time. The model pays one defaulter's loss from one set of layers. It does not model a second default, or the same layers reused after the first.
  • Variation margin is outside the model. A variation margin is a payment that moves with changes in a position's value between margin calls. The model has initial margin only, so every statement here about the margin concerns initial margin.
  • A low target leaves a tail. At 9900 basis points and \(S = 200\), the two largest scenarios are above the sized margin. For those two, the margin pays its full amount, and the rest goes to the layers after it in Definition 1.1: the default fund, the CCP capital, then the surviving members and any uncovered loss. While each of these two losses stays at or above the sized margin, changing it does not change the sized margin, because the 198 smallest losses stay the same.

4Misconceptions

  1. “A loss equal to the margin is not covered.”

    Definition 2.1 counts a scenario when \(L_s \le M\), and Remark 2.2 shows that \(a_1 = \min(L, M) = L\) when \(L = M\). The margin pays that loss in full, so the scenario is counted.

  2. “A target of 9900 basis points means losses average 99 percent of the margin.”

    The cover is a count share of the scenarios, floored, as Definition 2.1 states. It says nothing about the size of the losses. Example 2.5 turns the target into a count: with \(S = 200\), 9900 basis points requires \(k = 198\) of the 200 scenarios. The cover depends only on how many losses sit at or below the margin.

  3. “The default fund pays alongside the margin, so both are used on every loss.”

    Remark 1.4 shows that \(a_2 > 0\) requires \(a_1 = \mathrm{IM}_\delta\). A loss inside the margin uses the margin alone, and Lab 1, Part A3 prints a scenario in which the default fund and the CCP capital pay nothing.

  4. “Surviving members pay in proportion to their initial margins.”

    Definition 1.1 shares the residual by default fund, \(\mathrm{DF}_i\). The survivors' initial margins do not appear in the waterfall at all, so they cannot change a survivor's payment.

5Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1.

Mechanics

M1 · The waterfall at a loss of 200

Use Example 1.5. The defaulter has \(\mathrm{IM}_3 = 100\), \(\mathrm{DF}_3 = 50\), and \(K = 30\). The survivors have \(\mathrm{DF}_1 = 40\) and \(\mathrm{DF}_2 = 60\). Find each layer, the residual, and each survivor's payment for a loss of 200 cents.

Solution

\(a_1 = \min(200, 100) = 100\). \(a_2 = \min(100, 50) = 50\). \(a_3 = \min(50, 30) = 30\). The residual is \(Z = 200 - 180 = 20\). Since \(D = 100 > 20\), member 1 pays \(\lfloor 20 \cdot 40 / 100 \rfloor = 8\) cents and member 2 pays \(\lfloor 20 \cdot 60 / 100 \rfloor = 12\) cents. The leftover is \(20 - 20 = 0\), and the uncovered loss is 0. The total is \(100 + 50 + 30 + 8 + 12 = 200\).

M2 · The cover of one margin, and the margin for one target

Four scenarios have losses 1000, 2000, 3000 and 5000 cents. Find \(\mathrm{cover}(4000)\) in basis points, and the smallest integer margin with cover at least 7500 basis points.

Solution

Three losses are at most 4000, so \(c(4000) = 3\) and \(\mathrm{cover}(4000) = \lfloor 10000 \cdot 3 / 4 \rfloor = 7500\). For the target \(t = 7500\), \(k = \lceil 7500 \cdot 4 / 10000 \rceil = \lceil 3 \rceil = 3\), so the margin is \(L_{(3)} = 3000\) cents. Check: \(\mathrm{cover}(3000) = \lfloor 10000 \cdot 3 / 4 \rfloor = 7500\), and \(\mathrm{cover}(2999) = \lfloor 10000 \cdot 2 / 4 \rfloor = 5000\).

M3 · Three targets on five losses

The scenario losses are 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 cents. Find the sized margin for the targets 8000, 8001 and 10000 basis points.

Solution

For \(t = 8000\), \(tS/10000 = 8000 \cdot 5 / 10000 = 4\), so \(k = 4\) and the margin is \(L_{(4)} = 40\). For \(t = 8001\), \(tS/10000 = 4.0005\), so \(k = 5\) and the margin is \(L_{(5)} = 50\). For \(t = 10000\), \(k = 5\) and the margin is 50. The three answers are 40, 50 and 50 cents.

Proof

P1 · A layer needs the one before it

Under Definition 1.1, prove that \(a_3 > 0\) implies \(a_2 = \mathrm{DF}_\delta\) and \(a_1 = \mathrm{IM}_\delta\).

Solution

\(a_3 = \min(L - a_1 - a_2, K) > 0\) gives \(L - a_1 - a_2 > 0\), so \(a_2 < L - a_1\). Then \(a_2 = \min(L - a_1, \mathrm{DF}_\delta)\) is less than \(L - a_1\), which forces the minimum to be \(\mathrm{DF}_\delta\): \(a_2 = \mathrm{DF}_\delta\). Next, \(a_2 \ge 0\) and \(a_2 < L - a_1\) give \(L - a_1 > 0\), so \(a_1 < L\). The minimum \(a_1 = \min(L, \mathrm{IM}_\delta)\) is then \(\mathrm{IM}_\delta\), because a minimum below \(L\) must be its second argument.

P2 · The index k is in range

Let \(t\) be an integer with \(1 \le t \le 10000\) and let \(S \ge 1\) be an integer. Show that \(\lceil tS / 10000 \rceil\) is an integer from 1 to \(S\).

Solution

Because \(t \ge 1\) and \(S \ge 1\), \(tS/10000 > 0\), so its ceiling is at least 1. Because \(t \le 10000\), \(tS/10000 \le S\). The ceiling of a number \(x \le S\) is at most \(S\), since \(S\) is an integer and \(S \ge x\). So the ceiling is an integer with \(1 \le \lceil tS/10000 \rceil \le S\).

P3 · The brute-force search always stops

Under A4, show that \(c(M)\) does not decrease as \(M\) increases, and that \(\mathrm{cover}(M) = 10000\) for every \(M \ge \max_s L_s\). Use this to explain why the search of Lab 1, Part B tests only margins up to the largest loss.

Solution

If \(M \le M'\), then every scenario with \(L_s \le M\) also has \(L_s \le M'\), so \(c(M) \le c(M')\). If \(M \ge \max_s L_s\), then every scenario is counted, so \(c(M) = S\) and \(\mathrm{cover}(M) = \lfloor 10000 \cdot S / S \rfloor = 10000\). So a margin at the largest loss meets every target in A3, and the search over 0, 1, 2, and so on, ends at or before the largest loss.

Empirical

E1 · The scenario that reaches the default fund

Lab 1, Part A3 prints a scenario with loss 3160 cents for defaulter 3, whose margin is 3058 cents. Which layers pay, and by how much?

Solution

The printed layers are a margin of 3058 cents, a default fund of 102 cents, and CCP capital of 0 cents, with no survivor payment and no uncovered loss. Check: \(3058 + 102 = 3160\), and the margin is \(\mathrm{IM}_3 = 3058\) in full, as Remark 1.4 requires before the default fund pays. The default fund pays \(3160 - 3058 = 102\).

E2 · The surviving-members scenario

Lab 1, Part A3 prints a scenario with loss 5850 cents, layers of 3058, 205 and 1730 cents, and survivor payments of 312, 381 and 164 cents for members 1, 2 and 4. Show that the payments add to the residual, and state why they are exactly half of each survivor's default fund.

Solution

The residual is \(Z = 5850 - 3058 - 205 - 1730 = 857\), and \(312 + 381 + 164 = 857\), so the payments add to the residual. The survivors' default funds are 624, 762 and 328 cents, with total \(D = 1714\). Since \(Z = 857 = D/2\), each share is \(\lfloor \mathrm{DF}_i / 2 \rfloor\). The three default funds 624, 762 and 328 are even, so each share is exactly \(\mathrm{DF}_i / 2\): \(624/2 = 312\), \(762/2 = 381\) and \(328/2 = 164\). No leftover arises, consistent with the printed payments.

E3 · What the 9900 cover counts

Lab 1, Part B prints a cover of 9900 basis points at the sized margin for target 9900, with 200 scenarios. How many scenarios lie above that margin, and how do you know?

Solution

Cover is \(\lfloor 10000 \cdot c / 200 \rfloor = \lfloor 50c \rfloor = 9900\), which holds only for \(c = 198\), because \(50 \cdot 198 = 9900\) and \(50 \cdot 199 = 9950\). So 198 scenarios are at or below the margin, and \(200 - 198 = 2\) lie above it. The lab does not print the losses, so this count comes from the printed cover alone.

Research

R1 · Real margins give the same answer

Suppose the margin may be any non-negative real number of cents, not only an integer. Show that the smallest margin with cover at least \(t\) is still \(L_{(k)}\), with \(k\) as in Proposition 2.3.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 2.3, Part 2, uses only that \(M\) is a real number. For real \(M\), \(c(M) \ge k\) if and only if \(M \ge L_{(k)}\), and the cover condition is \(c(M) \ge tS/10000\), which is \(c(M) \ge k\). So the set of margins with cover at least \(t\) is the interval \([L_{(k)}, \infty)\). Its smallest element is \(L_{(k)}\), which is attained. Definition 2.1 gives c(M) and the cover by the same formula for real M, so moving from integer to real margins leaves the answer unchanged.

R2 · One large loss with 100 scenarios

Take \(S = 100\) and compare the targets 9900 and 10000 basis points. Find \(k\) for each. Show that the margin for 9900 leaves at most one scenario above it, and state when it leaves exactly one.

Solution

For \(t = 9900\), \(tS/10000 = 99\) exactly, so \(k = 99\) and the margin is \(L_{(99)}\). For \(t = 10000\), \(k = 100\) and the margin is \(L_{(100)}\), the largest loss. Since \(c(L_{(99)}) \ge 99\), at most \(100 - 99 = 1\) scenario lies above \(L_{(99)}\). The count is exactly 1 when \(c(L_{(99)}) = 99\), which holds if and only if \(L_{(99)} < L_{(100)}\). If the two largest losses are equal, no scenario lies above the margin for 9900.

R3 · When a 9975 target covers every scenario

For which scenario counts \(S\) does the target 9975 basis points require every scenario to be covered, so that \(k = S\)? Prove your answer.

Solution

\(k = S\) holds if and only if \(\lceil 0.9975 S \rceil = S\), that is \(0.9975 S > S - 1\). This gives \(0.0025 S < 1\), so \(S < 400\). So for \(1 \le S \le 399\), \(k = S\) and the margin is the largest loss. For \(S \ge 400\), \(k \le S - 1\), so one scenario may lie above the margin. At \(S = 400\), \(0.9975 \cdot 400 = 399\) and \(k = 399\), which is consistent with the boundary.

7Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. In Definition 1.1, which layer pays after the defaulter's initial margin is used in full?

    Options

    Step 2 of Definition 1.1 pays \(a_2 = \min(L - a_1, \mathrm{DF}_\delta)\), the defaulter's default fund. Step 3 comes after it.

  2. A loss of 200 cents, the fixture of Example 1.5. Member 1 has a default fund of 40 cents and member 2 has 60 cents. How many cents does member 1 pay?

    The residual is \(200 - 100 - 50 - 30 = 20\). Member 1 pays \(\lfloor 20 \cdot 40 / 100 \rfloor = 8\) cents.

  3. Same loss of 200 cents and the same fixture. How many cents do the two survivors pay in total?

    The payments are 8 and 12 cents, which total 20 cents, the residual.

  4. Under Definition 2.1, is a scenario whose loss equals the margin counted in the margin cover?

    Options

    Definition 2.1 counts \(L_s \le M\), and Remark 2.2 shows that the margin pays a loss equal to it in full.

  5. Four scenarios have losses 1000, 2000, 3000 and 5000 cents. What is the margin cover, in basis points floored, for a margin of 4000 cents?

    Three losses are at most 4000 cents, so \(\lfloor 10000 \cdot 3 / 4 \rfloor = 7500\).

  6. Four scenarios, and a target of 7500 basis points. What is the sorted position \(k\) used by Proposition 2.3?

    \(k = \lceil 7500 \cdot 4 / 10000 \rceil = \lceil 3 \rceil = 3\).

  7. Five losses are 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 cents. Which is the sized margin for a target of 8001 basis points?

    Options

    \(8001 \cdot 5 / 10000 = 4.0005\), so \(k = 5\) and the margin is the fifth smallest loss, 50 cents. Target 8000 gives \(k = 4\) and 40 cents.

  8. Three survivors each have a default fund of 1 cent, and the residual is 2 cents. How many cents does the first survivor receive?

    Each survivor's floor share is \(\lfloor 2 \cdot 1 / 3 \rfloor = 0\). The leftover is 2 cents, given one each to members 1 and 2, so member 1 receives 1 cent.

  9. With 200 scenarios, a target of 9900 basis points is met at the sized margin of Lab 1. What does that establish?

    Options

    The cover is a count share, so \(\lfloor 50c \rfloor = 9900\) gives \(c = 198\). The cover says nothing about the average loss, and the model assumes no distribution.

  10. With 400 scenarios and a target of 9975 basis points, what is \(k\)?

    \(9975 \cdot 400 / 10000 = 399\) exactly, so \(k = 399\). By Research problem R3, this is the first count where one scenario may lie above the margin.
