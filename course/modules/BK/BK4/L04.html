Stablecoins and Tokenization

  • Uses: settlement cycle, bank money creation
  • Introduces: stablecoin, tokenization
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Cworking paper, practitioner research or single sample
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A stablecoin (Definition 1.1) is a token whose issuer promises to redeem it at a reference value. In the model of Section 2, whether that promise can be kept depends on the reserves held against the claims outstanding, both defined in Section 1. This lesson defines the reserve-to-claims ratio (Definition 1.2), defines coverage (Definition 2.1), derives its value after a redemption share (Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.2) under Assumptions 1 to 3, and shows that coverage is below 1 exactly when the ratio is below 1 (Proposition 2.4). It then defines tokenization (Section 3), which records a claim on a shared record, and says nothing about settlement speed or cost. The derivation values reserves at face value, a stated limit.

1Stablecoins and the reserve-to-claims ratio

The claims and the reserves at face value are integer cents of a reference unit, the unit of account in which a stablecoin's reference value is stated. The measured reserves of Remark 4.1 need not be whole cents. A claim is an amount that an issuer owes a holder, and the claims outstanding are the total of those amounts at a date. A reserve is an asset that the issuer holds against the claims outstanding. A redemption is a holder's exchange of a claim for its reference value, paid by the issuer.

Definition 1.1 (stablecoin)

A stablecoin is a token recorded on a ledger whose issuer undertakes to redeem each unit at a stated amount of a reference unit. The definition describes the undertaking. It does not assert that the undertaking is met, that the reserves have a particular composition, or that the token trades at the reference value. Sections 2 and 4 treat those questions under explicit assumptions.

Definition 1.2 (reserve-to-claims ratio)

Let \(\mathrm{Res}_0\) be the reserves and \(C_0>0\) the claims outstanding at the start date, both in integer cents at face value. The reserve-to-claims ratio is \[ \rho=\frac{\mathrm{Res}_0}{C_0}. \] The ratio has no unit. Its numerator and denominator are both face values at the same date. Its value in basis points is \(\lfloor 10000\rho\rfloor\), the floor of \(10000\rho\), as in Definition 2.1. The ratio compares face values only. Section 4 treats a constant haircut to face value (Assumption 4).

Remark 1.3 (the ratio in Lab 1)

Lab 1 (at the end of Section 2) takes claims of 2,000,000 cents and two reserve ratios, \(19/20\) and \(21/20\). These give reserves of 1,900,000 cents and 2,100,000 cents. The ratio \(19/20\) is below 1 and \(21/20\) is above 1, and the two cases show the two directions of the sign rule (Proposition 2.4).

2Redemptions and coverage

The derivations in this section rest on Assumptions 1 to 3. Assumption 4 is stated here, and its uses are listed after it. All four are model assumptions, not statements about any issuer.

  1. Assumption 1 (par redemption). Each redeemed claim is paid its full face value in cents out of reserves, with reserves used at the value that Assumption 4 gives them, which is face value when Assumption 4 is not assumed.
  2. Assumption 2 (no new issuance). The claims outstanding change only through redemptions.
  3. Assumption 3 (no other flows). The reserves change only through the payments in Assumption 1. There are no fees, no interest and no other inflow or outflow.
  4. Assumption 4 (valuation convention). The reserves are measured at \((1-h)\) of their face value, for a haircut \(0\le h<1\). This assumption is used in Assumption 1 (the valuation clause), Definition 1.2 (a pointer), Remark 4.1, Problem R1, Misconception 1, the key-results table, quiz items 8 and 9 and the Lab 1 haircut check.
Definition 2.1 (cumulative redemption share and coverage)

After a start date, let \(D\) be the total face value in cents of the claims redeemed since the start date. The cumulative redemption share is \(x=D/C_0\), a fraction of the start-date claims. The coverage at a date is \(\theta=\mathrm{Res}/C\), where \(\mathrm{Res}\) and \(C\) are the reserves and the claims outstanding at that date, both in cents at face value. The coverage is a ratio with no unit. Its value in basis points is \(\lfloor 10000\theta\rfloor\), the floor of \(10000\theta\), computed exactly. A share \(x\) is admissible for a ratio \(r\) when \(0\le x<1\) and \(x\le r\).

Proposition 2.2 (coverage after a redemption share)

Hypotheses: (1) Assuming Assumptions 1 to 3; (2) \(C_0>0\) and \(\rho=\mathrm{Res}_0/C_0\); (3) the cumulative share satisfies \(0\le x<1\) and \(x\le\rho\). Conclusion: after the redemption of \(xC_0\) cents of claims, the reserves are \(C_0(\rho-x)\ge 0\), the claims outstanding are \(C_0(1-x)>0\), and the coverage is \(\theta=(\rho-x)/(1-x)\).

Proof

By the definition of \(x\), the redeemed claims have face value \(D=xC_0\). By Assumption 1 each redeemed claim is paid from reserves at face value, so the payments total \(xC_0\). By Assumption 3 this is the only change in the reserves, so the reserves after the redemptions are \(\mathrm{Res}_0-xC_0\). Substituting \(\mathrm{Res}_0=\rho C_0\) gives \(C_0\rho-xC_0=C_0(\rho-x)\). This is nonnegative because \(x\le\rho\) by hypothesis (3).

By Assumption 2 the claims change only through redemptions, so the claims outstanding after the redemptions are \(C_0-xC_0=C_0(1-x)\). This is positive because \(C_0>0\) and \(x<1\).

The coverage is the reserves divided by the claims. The factor \(C_0\) cancels, so \(\theta=\dfrac{C_0(\rho-x)}{C_0(1-x)}=\dfrac{\rho-x}{1-x}\). \(\square\)

Remark 2.3 (why the share is bounded by \(\rho\))

Hypothesis (3) requires \(x\le\rho\). If \(x\) exceeded \(\rho\), the payments under Assumption 1 would exceed the reserves, so the reserves would have to be negative and Assumption 1 could not hold at that share. The formula would then return a negative number that describes no state of the issuer. Lab 1 does not compute such shares. It counts them and prints the count.

Proposition 2.4 (the sign rule)

Hypotheses: (1) Assuming Assumptions 1 to 3; (2) the hypotheses of Proposition 2.2 hold for a share \(x\). Conclusion: \(\theta-1=(\rho-1)/(1-x)\). Hence \(\theta<1\) if and only if \(\rho<1\), \(\theta=1\) if and only if \(\rho=1\), and \(\theta>1\) if and only if \(\rho>1\).

Proof

Subtracting one from \(\theta=(\rho-x)/(1-x)\) over the common denominator gives \(\theta-1=\dfrac{(\rho-x)-(1-x)}{1-x}=\dfrac{\rho-1}{1-x}\). Since \(1-x>0\), the number \(\theta-1\) has the sign of \(\rho-1\), and it is zero exactly when \(\rho-1\) is zero. \(\square\)

Proposition 2.5 (direction of change along a path)

Hypotheses: (1) Assuming Assumptions 1 to 3; (2) \(0\le x<y<1\), with \(x\le\rho\) and \(y\le\rho\); (3) \(\theta(x)\) and \(\theta(y)\) are the coverages of Proposition 2.2 at the shares \(x\) and \(y\). Conclusion: \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)=\dfrac{(y-x)(\rho-1)}{(1-x)(1-y)}\). Hence the coverage falls as the share rises when \(\rho<1\), rises as the share rises when \(\rho>1\), and is 1 at every such share when \(\rho=1\).

Proof

Write both coverages over the common denominator \((1-x)(1-y)\). The numerator of \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)\) is \((\rho-y)(1-x)-(\rho-x)(1-y)\). Expanding the first product gives \(\rho-\rho x-y+xy\), and expanding the second gives \(\rho-\rho y-x+xy\). Their difference is \(-\rho x-y+\rho y+x=(y-x)(\rho-1)\). Both denominators are positive because \(x<1\) and \(y<1\), so the identity holds. Since \(y-x>0\), the sign of \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)\) is the sign of \(\rho-1\). When \(\rho=1\), Proposition 2.4 gives \(\theta=1\) at each share. \(\square\)

Proposition 2.6 (coverage against the starting ratio)

Hypotheses: (1) Assuming Assumptions 1 to 3; (2) the hypotheses of Proposition 2.2 hold for a share \(x\). Conclusion: \(\theta-\rho=\dfrac{x(\rho-1)}{1-x}\). If \(\rho\le 1\), then \(\theta\le\rho\), with equality if and only if \(x=0\) or \(\rho=1\). If \(\rho\ge 1\), then \(\theta\ge\rho\).

Proof

Over the common denominator \(1-x\), \(\theta-\rho=\dfrac{(\rho-x)-\rho(1-x)}{1-x}=\dfrac{\rho x-x}{1-x}=\dfrac{x(\rho-1)}{1-x}\). Since \(1-x>0\) and \(x\ge 0\), the sign of \(\theta-\rho\) is the sign of \(\rho-1\) whenever \(x>0\). The expression is zero exactly when \(x=0\) or \(\rho=1\). \(\square\)

Proposition 2.7 (the full-cover threshold survives flooring)

Hypotheses: (1) \(y\) is a real number; (2) \(\lfloor y\rfloor\) is the greatest integer not greater than \(y\). Conclusion: \(\lfloor y\rfloor\ge 10000\) if and only if \(y\ge 10000\). Consequently, if \(y=10000\theta\) for a coverage \(\theta\), the basis-point coverage is at least 10000 if and only if \(\theta\ge 1\).

Proof

If \(y\ge 10000\), then the integer 10000 is not greater than \(y\), so the greatest such integer \(\lfloor y\rfloor\) is at least 10000. If \(\lfloor y\rfloor\ge 10000\), then \(y\ge\lfloor y\rfloor\ge 10000\). The consequence follows because \(y\ge 10000\) is the same statement as \(\theta\ge 1\) when \(y=10000\theta\). \(\square\)

Remark 2.8 (worked values)

Take \(\rho=19/20\) and \(x=1/2\). Then \(x\le\rho\), and Proposition 2.2 gives \(\theta=(19/20-1/2)/(1/2)=9/10\), which is 9000 basis points. For \(\rho=21/20\) the same share gives \(\theta=11/10\), or 11000 basis points. For \(\rho=19/20\) and \(x=1/7\), the coverage is \(\theta=113/120\), and \(\lfloor 10000\theta\rfloor=9416\). Lab 1 prints the same three values.

Lab 1 · Reserve coverage under a seeded SIMULATED redemption path

SIMULATED. The claims, the reserve ratios and the redemption path are generated inputs with seed 7. They describe no issuer. Before running, the expected result is this. For \(\rho=19/20\), every computed share has coverage below 1, and the share 99/100 is omitted because it exceeds \(\rho\). For \(\rho=21/20\), no computed coverage is below 1, and no share is omitted. In both tables the identity \(\theta=(\rho-x)/(1-x)\) holds, and the difference identity of Proposition 2.5 holds for every pair of computed shares. The run uses exact fractions and integer cents, and it prints the haircut check of Remark 4.1 with the threshold \(1/(1-h)\) for \(h=1/20\).

"""BK4 L04 lab 1: reserve coverage of a stablecoin under a seeded SIMULATED redemption path, in exact arithmetic.

SIMULATED. The claims, the reserve ratios and the redemption path are illustrative inputs. They describe no issuer and
no market. The path is drawn from random.Random with a fixed seed, so every run prints the same lines.

The model is the one in Section 2 of the lesson. Claims outstanding are C_0 integer cents. Reserves at the start are
rho times C_0, where rho is the reserve-to-claims ratio. A cumulative redemption share x satisfies 0 <= x < 1 and
x <= rho. Under the lesson's assumptions (redemptions paid at par from reserves, no new issuance, no fees), the reserves
left are C_0 (rho - x) and the claims left are C_0 (1 - x), so the coverage is (rho - x) / (1 - x).

The cents columns are integers. The coverage is an exact Fraction. Its value in basis points is the floor of 10000 times
the coverage, taken exactly. A share with x greater than rho is not computed, because the reserves would be negative and
no par payment exists; the lab counts those shares and prints the count.

The lab checks three statements on the computed rows: the identity coverage = (rho - x) / (1 - x), the sign rule
(coverage is below 1 exactly when rho is below 1), and the difference identity for two shares x < y. It prints each
check's result; it does not assert them.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 7
PATH_LENGTH = 6
CLAIMS_CENTS = 2_000_000  # C_0: 20,000.00 of the reference unit, in cents
RHOS = (Fraction(95, 100), Fraction(105, 100))  # reserve-to-claims ratios below and above 1, exact
BPS = 10_000
HAIRCUT = Fraction(1, 20)  # h, the haircut in the valuation convention of Section 4 of the lesson


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Row:
    """The state after one cumulative redemption share on a reserve path. Amounts are integer cents."""

    share: Fraction
    reserves_left_cents: int
    claims_left_cents: int
    coverage: Fraction
    coverage_bps: int


def cents(value: Fraction) -> int:
    """Return the amount in cents as an int. Raise ValueError if the exact amount is not a whole number of cents."""
    if value.denominator != 1:
        raise ValueError(f"amount {value} is not a whole number of cents")
    return value.numerator


def coverage_of(rho: Fraction, share: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """The coverage (rho - x) / (1 - x) after a cumulative redemption share x, for 0 <= x < 1 and x <= rho."""
    if not 0 <= share < 1 or share > rho:
        raise ValueError("the share must satisfy 0 <= x < 1 and x <= rho")
    return (rho - share) / (1 - share)


def seeded_path(seed: int, length: int) -> list[Fraction]:
    """Return the `length` distinct sampled shares k/100 with k in 1 to 99, in increasing order, then append 99/100.

    The returned list has length + 1 shares. The appended 99/100 exceeds 19/20, so the omission rule is exercised for
    that ratio, and it does not exceed 21/20. When the sample contains 99, the appended 99/100 repeats that share.
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    hundredths = sorted(rng.sample(range(1, 100), length))
    return [Fraction(k, 100) for k in hundredths] + [Fraction(99, 100)]


def state_after(rho: Fraction, share: Fraction) -> Row:
    """The reserves and claims in integer cents after redeeming `share` of C_0, and the coverage they give.

    Reserves start at rho times C_0 and fall by the redeemed claims, which are paid at par. Claims fall by the same
    amount. Both amounts are whole cents for the share grid of this lab.
    """
    reserves_start = cents(rho * CLAIMS_CENTS)
    redeemed = cents(share * CLAIMS_CENTS)
    reserves_left = reserves_start - redeemed
    claims_left = CLAIMS_CENTS - redeemed
    coverage = Fraction(reserves_left, claims_left)
    return Row(share, reserves_left, claims_left, coverage, math.floor(BPS * coverage))


def print_path_table(rho: Fraction, path: list[Fraction]) -> list[Row]:
    """Print one row per share on the path that the model can compute, and return those rows."""
    reserves_start = cents(rho * CLAIMS_CENTS)
    print(f"rho {rho} reserves_0 {reserves_start} claims_0 {CLAIMS_CENTS} rho_below_1 {rho < 1}")
    rows: list[Row] = []
    omitted = 0
    for share in path:
        if share > rho:
            omitted += 1
            print(f"  x {share} omitted: x exceeds rho, so the reserves would be negative")
            continue
        row = state_after(rho, share)
        rows.append(row)
        print(
            f"  x {share} reserves_left {row.reserves_left_cents} claims_left {row.claims_left_cents} "
            f"coverage {row.coverage} bps {row.coverage_bps} below_1 {row.coverage < 1}"
        )
    below = sum(1 for row in rows if row.coverage < 1)
    identity = all(row.coverage == coverage_of(rho, row.share) for row in rows)
    sign_rule = all((row.coverage < 1) == (rho < 1) for row in rows)
    print(f"  computed rows {len(rows)} omitted rows {omitted} coverage below 1 in {below} computed rows")
    print(f"  identity (rho - x) / (1 - x) equals reserves over claims in all computed rows: {identity}")
    print(f"  coverage below 1 exactly when rho is below 1, in all computed rows: {sign_rule}")
    return rows


def check_differences(rho: Fraction, rows: list[Row]) -> None:
    """Check the difference identity and the direction of change for every pair of computed shares x < y."""
    pairs = [(a, b) for i, a in enumerate(rows) for b in rows[i + 1 :]]
    identity = all(
        b.coverage - a.coverage == (b.share - a.share) * (rho - 1) / ((1 - a.share) * (1 - b.share)) for a, b in pairs
    )
    direction = all((b.coverage - a.coverage) * (rho - 1) > 0 for a, b in pairs)
    print(f"  pairs of computed shares {len(pairs)}")
    print(f"  difference identity (y - x)(rho - 1) / ((1 - x)(1 - y)) holds in all pairs: {identity}")
    print(
        "  coverage falls as the share rises when rho is below 1, and rises when rho is above 1, "
        f"in all pairs: {direction}"
    )


def haircut_check() -> None:
    """Print the full-cover threshold 1/(1 - h) and the coverage at share 1/2 for a ratio just below it and at it.

    The convention values the reserves at (1 - h) of face value, so the effective ratio is rho times (1 - h). The
    coverage at share 1/2 is the same formula with the effective ratio in place of rho.
    """
    threshold = Fraction(1) / (1 - HAIRCUT)
    print(f"haircut h {HAIRCUT} full-cover threshold 1/(1 - h) {threshold}")
    for rho in (Fraction(21, 20), threshold):
        effective = rho * (1 - HAIRCUT)
        value = coverage_of(effective, Fraction(1, 2))
        print(f"rho {rho} effective {effective} x 1/2 coverage {value} bps {math.floor(BPS * value)}")


def main() -> None:
    """Run the seeded path for two reserve ratios, the difference checks, the worked values and the haircut check."""
    path = seeded_path(SEED, PATH_LENGTH)
    print(f"SIMULATED: seed {SEED}, claims C_0 {CLAIMS_CENTS} cents, cumulative shares x in the lesson's model")
    print("path " + " ".join(str(share) for share in path))
    for rho in RHOS:
        rows = print_path_table(rho, path)
        check_differences(rho, rows)
    print("worked values, exact")
    for rho, share in (
        (Fraction(19, 20), Fraction(1, 2)),
        (Fraction(21, 20), Fraction(1, 2)),
        (Fraction(19, 20), Fraction(1, 7)),
    ):
        value = coverage_of(rho, share)
        print(f"rho {rho} x {share} coverage {value} bps {math.floor(BPS * value)}")
    print("haircut valuation convention, exact")
    haircut_check()


main()

3Tokenization

Definition 3.1 (tokenization)

Tokenization is the recording of a claim on an asset as a unit that transfers on a shared record. The definition fixes the technical meaning only. Whether a token gives its holder a legal claim on the asset depends on the law and on the terms of issue, and this lesson states neither. The definition makes no statement about the speed, cost or risk of settlement.

Remark 3.2 (the settlement cycle and bank money)

A transfer on a shared record changes which holder owns a unit. The settlement cycle of Lesson 1 is the period between a trade and its settlement. This lesson does not model whether a shared record changes that period. Bank money differs. FC1 defines bank money creation, in the model of its Assumption 2.0, as “the increase in total deposits that results when the bank makes a loan and the proceeds are deposited back at the same bank” (FC1, Section 2, Definition 2.1), and in that model deposits are the bank’s liabilities (FC1, Section 2). A stablecoin reserve is an asset that the issuer holds against claims, and Definition 1.2 measures only its face value. The two are different balance-sheet objects, and this lesson states nothing about the legal status of either.

4Where the canon breaks

Remark 4.1 (a haircut under the valuation convention)

Assuming Assumptions 1 to 3 and Assumption 4, the reserves are measured at \((1-h)\) of face value, and redemptions are still paid at par out of that measured amount. The measured reserve ratio is then \(\rho(1-h)\), and the proof of Proposition 2.2 applies with \(\rho\) replaced by \(\rho(1-h)\). The measured coverage is \(\theta_h=(\rho(1-h)-x)/(1-x)\) for each share \(0\le x<1\) with \(x\le\rho(1-h)\). By Proposition 2.4 applied to \(\rho(1-h)\), the measured coverage is at least 1 exactly when \(\rho(1-h)\ge 1\), that is, when \(\rho\ge 1/(1-h)\). The threshold \(1/(1-h)\) is therefore the smallest face-value ratio that keeps the measured coverage at least 1 at every share admissible for \(\rho(1-h)\). For \(\rho=1\) and \(h>0\), the measured coverage is below 1 for each share admissible for \(\rho(1-h)\), because \(\rho(1-h)<1\). Lab 1 prints the case \(h=1/20\), whose threshold is \(20/19\).

  • The valuation convention is not a sale. Remark 4.1 measures the reserves at \((1-h)\) of face value. It does not model the sale of reserve assets that funds a redemption. A sale at a discount changes the reserves by an amount that Assumption 1 does not describe, and that case is outside Lab 1 and this lesson.
  • New issuance, fees and interest are excluded. Assumption 2 excludes new issuance, and Assumption 3 excludes fees and interest on reserves. A model with either needs a different derivation.
  • Timing is absent. Proposition 2.2 gives the coverage at a share, not the path that reaches it. The lab's path is a list of shares in non-decreasing order, and the printed seed-7 path is strictly increasing. The order of events between the shares is not modelled.
  • No observed behaviour is claimed. The lesson makes no statement about redemptions, runs or reserve holdings of any issuer. No source for such a statement is cited on this page, so none is made.
  • Lab 1 is SIMULATED. Its claims, ratios and path are generated from seed 7. Its checks are exact algebra on those inputs.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A fully reserved stablecoin keeps its coverage at 1 whatever the reserve assets are worth.”

    The belief holds under Assumptions 1 to 3, where Proposition 2.4 gives \(\theta=1\) at every share admissible for \(\rho\) when \(\rho=1\). It fails once the reserves are measured at \((1-h)\) of face value with \(h>0\). The measured ratio is then \(1-h<1\), and Remark 4.1 gives a measured coverage below 1 at every share admissible for \(1-h\).

  2. “After half the claims are redeemed, the coverage halves.”

    Halving would give \(\rho/2\). Proposition 2.2 gives \((\rho-x)/(1-x)\) instead. For \(\rho=19/20\) and \(x=1/2\) the coverage is \(9/10\), which is 9000 basis points (Remark 2.8, printed by Lab 1), not \(19/40\).

  3. “Paying redemptions at par leaves the coverage ratio unchanged.”

    The coverage stays at \(\rho\) only when \(x=0\) or \(\rho=1\) (Proposition 2.6). When \(\rho<1\) it falls as the share rises, and when \(\rho>1\) it rises (Proposition 2.5). Both directions appear in the two tables that Lab 1 prints.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Coverage after one redemption

At the start, reserves are 3 units and claims are 4 units. A holder redeems one quarter of the start-date claims at par. Find \(\rho\), check the hypothesis \(x\le\rho\), and find the coverage afterwards.

Solution

The ratio is \(\rho=3/4\). The share is \(x=1/4\), which satisfies \(0\le x<1\) and \(x\le 3/4\), so Proposition 2.2 applies. The redeemed claims are 1 unit, so the reserves become \(3-1=2\) units and the claims become \(4-1=3\) units. The coverage is \(2/3\). The formula gives \((3/4-1/4)/(1-1/4)=(1/2)/(3/4)=2/3\), the same value.

M2 · A larger share with a ratio below 1

Take \(\rho=3/5\) and \(x=2/5\). Compute \(\theta\), and state whether Proposition 2.4 says that \(\theta\) is below 1.

Solution

The share satisfies \(x\le\rho\), since \(2/5\le 3/5\). The formula gives \(\theta=(3/5-2/5)/(1-2/5)=(1/5)/(3/5)=1/3\). Since \(\rho=3/5<1\), Proposition 2.4 says \(\theta<1\), and \(1/3<1\) confirms it.

M3 · The ratio in basis points

The start-date reserves are 1,900,000 cents and the claims are 2,000,000 cents. Give \(\rho\) and the value of \(10000\rho\) in basis points, floored.

Solution

The ratio is \(\rho=1900000/2000000=19/20\). In basis points, \(10000\cdot 19/20=9500\) exactly, so the floor is 9500. Lab 1 prints reserves of 1900000 cents for \(\rho=19/20\) on claims of 2000000 cents.

Proof

P1 · The difference identity

Prove the difference identity in Proposition 2.5 for \(0\le x<y<1\), with no assumption on \(\rho\) beyond it being a real number.

Solution

Over the common denominator \((1-x)(1-y)\), the numerator of \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)\) is \((\rho-y)(1-x)-(\rho-x)(1-y)\). The first product is \(\rho-\rho x-y+xy\), and the second is \(\rho-\rho y-x+xy\). Subtracting the second from the first leaves \(-\rho x-y+\rho y+x\), which regroups as \(\rho(y-x)-(y-x)=(y-x)(\rho-1)\). Both denominators are positive, so \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)=(y-x)(\rho-1)/((1-x)(1-y))\). No assumption on \(\rho\) was used beyond its being real.

P2 · The coverage cannot exceed the starting ratio below 1

Let \(\rho\le 1\) and \(0\le x<1\) with \(x\le\rho\). Prove \(\theta\le\rho\), and find the shares for which equality holds.

Solution

By Proposition 2.6, \(\theta-\rho=x(\rho-1)/(1-x)\). Since \(1-x>0\) and \(x\ge 0\), and \(\rho-1\le 0\), the product \(x(\rho-1)\) is at most zero, so \(\theta\le\rho\). Equality holds exactly when \(x(\rho-1)=0\), that is, when \(x=0\) or \(\rho=1\). For \(\rho<1\), equality holds only at \(x=0\).

P3 · The threshold after flooring

Prove Proposition 2.7, and state what it says about a coverage that is just below 1.

Solution

For a real \(y\), if \(y\ge 10000\) then 10000 is an integer not greater than \(y\), so the greatest such integer \(\lfloor y\rfloor\) is at least 10000. Conversely, if \(\lfloor y\rfloor\ge 10000\) then \(y\ge\lfloor y\rfloor\ge 10000\). With \(y=10000\theta\), the statement \(y\ge 10000\) is \(\theta\ge 1\). So a coverage strictly below 1 has a basis-point figure strictly below 10000, and flooring cannot lift a coverage below 1 to the full-cover figure.

Research

R1 · The face-value ratio that keeps the measured coverage at 1

Under Assumptions 1 to 3 and Assumption 4, with a haircut \(0\le h<1\), find the smallest face-value ratio \(\rho^{*}\) such that the measured coverage is at least 1 at every share admissible for \(\rho(1-h)\). Give the shares admissible for \(\rho^{*}(1-h)\), and show that no smaller ratio works.

Solution

Under Assumption 4 the measured ratio is \(\rho(1-h)\), so the measured coverage is \((\rho(1-h)-x)/(1-x)\). By Proposition 2.4 applied to \(\rho(1-h)\), the coverage is at least 1 exactly when \(\rho(1-h)\ge 1\). Hence \(\rho^{*}=1/(1-h)\). For this ratio, every share with \(0\le x<1\) is admissible for \(\rho^{*}(1-h)\), because \(x<1\le\rho^{*}(1-h)\). For any ratio \(0\le\rho<\rho^{*}\), the share \(x=0\) is admissible for \(\rho(1-h)\), and the measured coverage there is \(\rho(1-h)<1\). So no smaller ratio keeps the measured coverage at least 1 at every share admissible for its measured ratio. For \(h=1/20\), the threshold is \(20/19\), which Lab 1 prints.

R2 · The share at which a target coverage is reached

Let \(\rho<1\) and \(0\le c\le\rho\). Find the cumulative share \(x^{*}\) at which the coverage equals \(c\), and show that \(x^{*}\) satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition 2.2.

Solution

Set \((\rho-x)/(1-x)=c\). Clearing the denominator gives \(\rho-x=c-cx\), so \(\rho-c=x(1-c)\). Since \(c\le\rho<1\), the factor \(1-c\) is positive, and \(x^{*}=(\rho-c)/(1-c)\). Then \(x^{*}\ge 0\) because \(\rho\ge c\), and \(1-x^{*}=(1-\rho)/(1-c)>0\), so \(x^{*}<1\). Also \(\rho-x^{*}=c(1-\rho)/(1-c)\ge 0\), so \(x^{*}\le\rho\). Substituting back gives \(\theta=c(1-\rho)/(1-c)\cdot(1-c)/(1-\rho)=c\). For \(\rho=19/20\) and \(c=9/10\), this gives \(x^{*}=1/2\), the worked share of Remark 2.8.

R3 · Redemptions in whole cents

Let \(m\) be a whole number of cents with \(0\le m\le\mathrm{Res}_0\) and \(m<C_0\). After \(m\) cents of claims are paid at par from reserves, state the coverage in cents, and show that it equals \((\rho-x)/(1-x)\) with \(x=m/C_0\). State the condition under which \(x\le\rho\) holds.

Solution

The reserves after the payment are \(\mathrm{Res}_0-m\) cents, and the claims outstanding are \(C_0-m\) cents, which is positive because \(m<C_0\). The coverage is \((\mathrm{Res}_0-m)/(C_0-m)\). Put \(\mathrm{Res}_0=\rho C_0\) and \(m=xC_0\), so that \(x=m/C_0\). The numerator is \(C_0(\rho-x)\) and the denominator is \(C_0(1-x)\). Dividing and cancelling \(C_0\) gives \((\rho-x)/(1-x)\). The condition \(x\le\rho\) is \(m\le\mathrm{Res}_0\), which is the requirement that the reserves cover the payment.

8Retrieval quiz

  1. Under Assumptions 1 to 3, with \(0\le x<1\) and \(x\le\rho\), what is the coverage after a cumulative redemption share \(x\)?

    Options

    Proposition 2.2 divides the reserves \(C_0(\rho-x)\) by the claims \(C_0(1-x)\). The factor \(C_0\) cancels, which leaves \((\rho-x)/(1-x)\).

  2. With \(\rho=19/20\) and \(x=1/2\), what is the coverage?

    \((19/20-1/2)/(1/2)=(9/20)\cdot 2=9/10=0.9\). Remark 2.8 gives the same value, which is 9000 basis points.

  3. If \(\rho=1\) and the share \(x\) is admissible for \(\rho\), what is the coverage under Assumptions 1 to 3?

    Options

    Proposition 2.4 gives \(\theta-1=(\rho-1)/(1-x)\). With \(\rho=1\) this is zero for every share \(x\) admissible for \(\rho\), so the coverage is exactly 1.

  4. With \(\rho=21/20\) and \(x=1/2\), the coverage is \(11/10\). What is its value in basis points, floored?

    The coverage is \(11/10\), and \(10000\cdot 11/10=11000\). The answer is 11000, not 9000. The value 9000 is the basis-point figure for \(\rho=19/20\), which is a different case.

  5. For \(\rho<1\), how does the coverage move as the share \(x\) rises, with \(x\) admissible for \(\rho\)?

    Options

    Proposition 2.5 gives \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)=(y-x)(\rho-1)/((1-x)(1-y))\). For \(\rho<1\) and \(x<y\), the right side is negative, so the coverage falls.

  6. With \(\rho=19/20\) and \(x=1/7\), the coverage is \(113/120\). What is \(\lfloor 10000\theta\rfloor\)?

    \(10000\cdot 113/120=9416\frac{2}{3}\), and the floor is 9416. Lab 1 prints the same value in its worked values.

  7. Under Assumption 1, what is the situation when a share \(x\) exceeds \(\rho\)?

    Options

    Remark 2.3 explains that \(x\le\rho\) is needed for the reserves to stay nonnegative. Lab 1 omits such shares and prints a count of them.

  8. With \(\rho=1\), reserves measured at \((1-h)\) of face value for a haircut \(h>0\), and a share \(x\) admissible for \(\rho(1-h)\), what is the measured coverage?

    Options

    Remark 4.1 replaces \(\rho\) with \(\rho(1-h)=1-h<1\). Proposition 2.4 applied to \(1-h\) gives a measured coverage below 1.

  9. Under the valuation convention with \(h=1/20\), what is the smallest face-value ratio \(\rho^{*}\) with measured coverage at least 1? Give the decimal rounded to ten decimal places.

    \(\rho^{*}=1/(1-h)=20/19\), which is 1.0526315789 to ten decimal places. Lab 1 prints the threshold as 20/19, and Problem R1 derives it.

  10. Which statement is the definition of tokenization in this lesson?

    Options

    Definition 3.1 states the technical meaning. It makes no statement about speed, cost or risk, and Remark 3.2 states that the lesson does not model whether a shared record changes the settlement period.
