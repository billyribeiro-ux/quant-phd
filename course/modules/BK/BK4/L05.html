Operational Risk in Market Infrastructure

  • Uses: settlement cycle (L01, Definition 1.2)
  • Introduces: operational risk
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Cworking paper, practitioner research or single sample
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

This lesson defines operational risk as the risk of a loss from failed or inadequate processes, people, systems or external events. It models one loss from that list, the cost of settlement failures (Definitions 1.2, 2.1) for three trades. Proposition 2.1 shows that the mean does not depend on how failures are linked. Proposition 2.4 gives the same mean when all three trades fail together. Proposition 2.5 shows that, for a failure probability strictly between 0 and 1 and a positive cost per day, the variance when all three fail together is at least the variance under independence (Assumption 1.3, A3 and A4). Lab 1 checks Proposition 2.2 and prints the quantities of Propositions 2.4 to 2.6.

1Operational risk and settlement failures

A trade settles on the scheduled date that the settlement cycle of L01 gives it. Definition 1.2 gives the meaning of a settlement failure, a departure from that date, and of the delay of a failed trade. The lesson models no source of loss other than failed settlements.

Definition 1.1 (operational risk)

In this unit, operational risk is the risk of a loss from failed or inadequate processes, people or systems, or from external events. The term has this meaning and no other here. The definition gives no measure of the risk. Section 2 gives one cost, the cost of a settlement failure, and no other.

Definition 1.2 (settlement failure and delay)

A trade fails to settle on its scheduled settlement date when the payment or delivery that completes it has not moved by the end of that date. Its delay is the number \(\ell\) of business days from the scheduled date to the day on which it settles, a whole number with \(\ell \ge 0\). The symbol \(\ell\) is not the settlement cycle \(d\) of L01, Definition 1.2. In this lesson \(\ell\) names a delay only.

Assumption 1.3 (the model of three trades)

There are three trades, numbered \(i=1,2,3\). The failure events are defined on a finite probability space \(\Omega\), so every expectation below is a finite sum. A joint law of the failure events is their joint probability distribution on \(\Omega\). Let \(F_i\) be the event that trade \(i\) fails, and let \(\indic\{F_i\}\) be its indicator, equal to 1 on \(F_i\) and to 0 off \(F_i\). Four assumptions are used. Each result states which of them it needs.

A1 (marginal failure probability). \(\Prob(F_i)=p\) for each \(i\), where \(p\) is a fixed number with \(0\lt p\lt 1\).

A2 (fixed delay and linear cost). If trade \(i\) fails, it settles \(\ell_i\) business days late, where \(\ell_i\ge 0\) is a fixed whole number. Its cost is \(\kappa\ell_i\) cents, where \(\kappa\ge 0\) is a fixed whole number of cents per business day. No recovery action shortens a delay, and no other cost arises.

A3 (independent failures). The events \(F_1, F_2, F_3\) are independent.

A4 (comonotone failures). \(F_1=F_2=F_3\). The three trades fail together or not at all. By A1 the common event has probability \(p\).

2The expected cost of settlement failures

Definition 2.1 (cost of the three trades)

Under A2 the cost of the three trades is \(C=\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\,\indic\{F_i\}\), in cents. Its expectation is \(\E[C]\), in cents, and its variance is \(\Var(C)\), in square cents.

Proposition 2.1 (the mean needs only the marginal probabilities)

Hypotheses: (1) the finite probability space of Assumption 1.3; (2) A1 and A2. Conclusion: \(\E[C]=p\,\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\) cents. No assumption about the joint law of \(F_1, F_2, F_3\) is used.

Proof

On a finite space, \(\E[\indic\{F_i\}]=\sum_{\omega\in F_i}\Prob(\{\omega\})=\Prob(F_i)\), which is \(p\) by A1. The constants \(\kappa\ell_i\) are fixed by A2, and the expectation of a finite sum is the sum of the expectations. Therefore \(\E[C]=\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\,\E[\indic\{F_i\}]=p\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.2 (enumeration equals the closed form of Proposition 2.1 under independence)

Hypotheses: the finite space of Assumption 1.3, and A1, A2 and A3. For a fail pattern \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\{0,1\}^3\) write \(P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}p^{x_i}(1-p)^{1-x_i}\) and \(C(x)=\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i x_i\). Conclusion: \(\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^3}P(x)\,C(x)=p\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\).

Proof

By A3, the probability that \(\indic\{F_i\}=x_i\) for \(i=1,2,3\) is the product of the three single-trade probabilities. By A1 each of these is \(p\) or \(1-p\), so that probability is \(P(x)\). The sum is therefore the expectation of \(C\) written over the eight patterns. Expanding \(C(x)\), \[ \sum_{x}P(x)\,C(x)=\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\sum_{x}P(x)\,x_i . \] For fixed \(i\), the inner sum runs over the patterns with \(x_i=1\). The factor for trade \(i\) is \(p\), and each other factor sums to \(p+(1-p)=1\) over \(x_j\in\{0,1\}\). So \(\sum_x P(x)x_i=p\), and the whole sum is \(p\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (what Lab 1 checks)

Lab 1, Part B, lists the eight patterns with the probabilities of Proposition 2.2. The lab checks that these probabilities sum to one, and that the enumerated mean equals the closed form for the printed parameters. Those checks concern one parameter set. Proposition 2.2 is the statement for every \(p\), \(\kappa\) and \(\ell\) that satisfy the assumptions.

Proposition 2.4 (dependent failures leave the mean unchanged)

Hypotheses: the finite space of Assumption 1.3, and A1, A2 and A4. Conclusion: \(\E[C]=p\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\), the same value as in Propositions 2.1 and 2.2.

Proof

Under A4 each \(\Prob(F_i)\) equals \(p\), which is A1. The proof of Proposition 2.1 used only the finite space, A1 and A2, and not the joint law of the events. It therefore applies to A4 unchanged. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.5 (the comonotone variance is never smaller)

Hypotheses: the finite space of Assumption 1.3; A1 and A2; A3 for \(\Var_3(C)\) and A4 for \(\Var_4(C)\); \(0\lt p\lt 1\); \(\kappa>0\). Here \(\Var_3(C)\) and \(\Var_4(C)\) are the variance of \(C\) under A3 and under A4. Conclusion: \(\Var_4(C)-\Var_3(C)=2\kappa^2p(1-p)\sum_{i\lt j}\ell_i\ell_j\ge 0\). The difference is zero exactly when at most one \(\ell_i\) is positive.

Proof

Write \(a_i=\kappa\ell_i\). Since \(\indic\{F_i\}^2=\indic\{F_i\}\), A1 gives \(\Var(\indic\{F_i\})=p-p^2=p(1-p)\). Under A3, for \(i\ne j\), \(\E[\indic\{F_i\}\indic\{F_j\}]=\Prob(F_i\cap F_j)=p^2=\E[\indic\{F_i\}]\E[\indic\{F_j\}]\), so the covariance of the two indicators is zero. Therefore \(\Var_3(C)=\sum_i a_i^2\,p(1-p)\). Under A4, \(C=\kappa\bigl(\sum_i\ell_i\bigr)\indic\{F_1\}\), so \(\Var_4(C)=\kappa^2\bigl(\sum_i\ell_i\bigr)^2p(1-p)\). Subtracting, \[ \Var_4(C)-\Var_3(C)=\kappa^2p(1-p)\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_i\ell_i\Bigr)^2-\sum_i\ell_i^2\Bigr]=2\kappa^2p(1-p)\sum_{i\lt j}\ell_i\ell_j . \] Each product \(\ell_i\ell_j\) is nonnegative, and \(\kappa^2p(1-p)>0\). The sum is zero exactly when every product is zero, which holds exactly when at most one \(\ell_i\) is positive. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.6 (the probability of no cost)

Hypotheses: the finite space of Assumption 1.3; A1 and A2; A3 for the first value and A4 for the second; \(0\lt p\lt 1\); \(\ell_i\ge 1\) for \(i=1,2,3\); \(\kappa\ge 1\). Conclusion: \(\Prob(C=0)=(1-p)^3\) under A3, and \(\Prob(C=0)=1-p\) under A4. The first value is strictly smaller than the second.

Proof

Each failed trade adds \(\kappa\ell_i\ge 1\) cents to \(C\), so \(C=0\) exactly when no trade fails. Under A3, the probability that no trade fails is \(P(0,0,0)=(1-p)^3\) by the product formula in Proposition 2.2. Under A4, the probability that no trade fails is \(\Prob(F_1^{c})=1-p\). Since \(0\lt 1-p\lt 1\), we have \((1-p)^2\lt 1\), and so \((1-p)^3=(1-p)(1-p)^2\lt 1-p\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.7 (three times p is not the probability of a failure)

Take \(p=1/8\). Under A3 the probability that at least one trade fails is \(1-(7/8)^3=169/512\). Under A4 it is \(p=64/512\). Three times \(p\) is \(3/8=192/512\), which is neither value. For general \(0\lt p\lt 1\), expanding \((1-p)^3=1-3p+3p^2-p^3\) gives \(1-(1-p)^3=3p-3p^2+p^3\), so \(3p-\bigl(1-(1-p)^3\bigr)=p^2(3-p)\gt 0\), and under A4 the probability is \(p\lt 3p\). Lab 1, Part B, prints \(169/512\) and \(3/8\).

Example 2.8 (the parameters of Lab 1)

Take \(p=1/8\), \(\kappa=2500\) cents per business day and \(\ell=(1,2,4)\) business days. Proposition 2.1 gives \(\E[C]=\tfrac18\cdot 2500\cdot 7=\tfrac{17500}{8}=\tfrac{4375}{2}=2187.5\) cents. The exact value holds a half cent. Under the rule used in Lab 1, the expected cost rounded to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half cent going up, is 2188 cents, which is 21.88 dollars. The rounding is applied once, to the exact value.

3Checking the model by enumeration

Lab 1 checks Proposition 2.2 on one parameter set, and it prints the quantities that Propositions 2.4, 2.5 and 2.6 compare. Its parameters are SIMULATED and illustrative. They are not estimates for any trade, any market or any failure rate.

Lab 1 · Enumeration and closed form for three trades (SIMULATED)

Predicted before running, for \(p=1/8\), \(\kappa=2500\) cents per business day and delays \((1,2,4)\) business days, all SIMULATED: the enumerated expected cost equals the closed form \(p\kappa\sum_i\ell_i=4375/2\) cents (Proposition 2.2), which rounds half up to 2188 cents. The eight pattern probabilities sum to one. With equal delays of 2 business days the enumeration gives 1875 cents. The probability that no trade fails is 343/512 under A3 and 7/8 under A4, the two means are equal (Proposition 2.4), and the variance under A4 is larger (Proposition 2.5).

"""BK4 L05 lab 1: the expected cost of settlement failures for three trades, by enumeration and in closed form.

SIMULATED. The failure probability, the cost per day and the delays are illustrative parameters. They describe no real
trade, no real market and no real failure rate. Every probability is an exact Fraction. Every cost is an integer number
of cents, except an expected cost, which is exact and may be a fraction of a cent. The only rounding in the program is
one step: an exact expected cost is rounded to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half cent going up.

The model. Trade i fails with the same probability p for each trade. A failed trade i is delayed by DELAYS_DAYS[i]
business days, and each business day of delay costs COST_PER_DAY_CENTS cents. The cost C of the three trades is the
sum of the costs of the failed trades. Two joint laws for the three failure events are compared. Both give each trade
the marginal probability p:

* independent failures: a fail pattern with k failed trades has probability p^k (1 - p)^(3 - k);
* comonotone failures: all three trades fail together with probability p, and none fails with probability 1 - p.

Part A prints the parameters. Part B lists the eight fail patterns under independent failures, with their
probabilities and costs, and compares the enumerated expected cost with the closed form p * kappa * (sum of the
delays). Part C takes equal delays ell and compares the enumeration with 3 * p * kappa * ell. Part D gives the
probability of no cost, the mean and the variance of the cost under both joint laws. Standard library only; no
randomness and no network, so every run prints the same lines.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import math
from fractions import Fraction

P_FAIL = Fraction(1, 8)
COST_PER_DAY_CENTS = 2500
DELAYS_DAYS = (1, 2, 4)
EQUAL_DELAY_DAYS = 2

Pattern = tuple[bool, ...]
Outcomes = list[tuple[Fraction, int]]


def all_patterns(count: int) -> list[Pattern]:
    """Every fail pattern of count trades, as tuples of booleans (True means the trade fails)."""
    return list(itertools.product((False, True), repeat=count))


def pattern_probability(pattern: Pattern, p: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Probability of one fail pattern under independent failures: the product of p or 1 - p over the trades."""
    prob = Fraction(1)
    for failed in pattern:
        prob *= p if failed else 1 - p
    return prob


def pattern_cost_cents(pattern: Pattern, delays: tuple[int, ...], cost_per_day: int) -> int:
    """Cost in cents of one fail pattern: cost_per_day times the delay of each failed trade, summed."""
    return sum(cost_per_day * delay for failed, delay in zip(pattern, delays, strict=True) if failed)


def independent_outcomes(p: Fraction, delays: tuple[int, ...], cost_per_day: int) -> Outcomes:
    """The (probability, cost in cents) pair of every fail pattern under independent failures."""
    return [
        (pattern_probability(pat, p), pattern_cost_cents(pat, delays, cost_per_day))
        for pat in all_patterns(len(delays))
    ]


def comonotone_outcomes(p: Fraction, delays: tuple[int, ...], cost_per_day: int) -> Outcomes:
    """The two outcomes under comonotone failures: no failure with probability 1 - p, all failing with probability p."""
    return [(1 - p, 0), (p, cost_per_day * sum(delays))]


def mean(outcomes: Outcomes) -> Fraction:
    """Exact expected cost in cents of a finite distribution given as (probability, cost) pairs."""
    return sum((prob * cost for prob, cost in outcomes), Fraction(0))


def variance(outcomes: Outcomes) -> Fraction:
    """Exact variance in square cents of a finite distribution given as (probability, cost) pairs."""
    second = sum((prob * cost * cost for prob, cost in outcomes), Fraction(0))
    return second - mean(outcomes) ** 2


def closed_form_mean(p: Fraction, delays: tuple[int, ...], cost_per_day: int) -> Fraction:
    """The closed form p * kappa * (sum of the delays), in cents. It uses only the marginal probability p."""
    return p * cost_per_day * sum(delays)


def round_half_up(amount: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of a nonnegative exact amount, with an exact half cent going up."""
    if amount < 0:
        raise ValueError("round_half_up needs a nonnegative amount")
    return math.floor(amount + Fraction(1, 2))


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to D. Probabilities are exact fractions; costs are cents; variances are square cents."""
    print("A. Parameters (SIMULATED, illustrative)")
    print(f"failure probability per trade p = {P_FAIL}")
    print(f"cost per business day of delay kappa = {COST_PER_DAY_CENTS} cents")
    print(f"delays ell_1, ell_2, ell_3 = {DELAYS_DAYS} business days")

    print("B. Independent failures: each fail pattern, with its probability and cost")
    total = Fraction(0)
    for pat in all_patterns(len(DELAYS_DAYS)):
        prob = pattern_probability(pat, P_FAIL)
        total += prob
        label = " ".join("fail" if failed else "ok" for failed in pat)
        cost = pattern_cost_cents(pat, DELAYS_DAYS, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS)
        print(f"pattern ({label}): probability {prob}, cost {cost} cents")
    enumerated = mean(independent_outcomes(P_FAIL, DELAYS_DAYS, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS))
    closed = closed_form_mean(P_FAIL, DELAYS_DAYS, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS)
    print(f"probabilities sum to one: {total == 1}")
    print(f"expected cost by enumeration: {enumerated} cents")
    print(f"closed form p * kappa * sum of delays: {closed} cents")
    print(f"enumeration equals closed form: {enumerated == closed}")
    print(f"closed form rounded half up to the cent: {round_half_up(closed)} cents")
    no_failure = pattern_probability((False,) * len(DELAYS_DAYS), P_FAIL)
    print(f"probability that no trade fails: {no_failure}")
    print(f"probability that at least one trade fails: {1 - no_failure}")
    print(f"three times p, for comparison: {3 * P_FAIL}")
    print(f"probability that at least one trade fails is below three times p: {1 - no_failure < 3 * P_FAIL}")

    print(f"C. Equal delays of {EQUAL_DELAY_DAYS} business days for each trade")
    equal_delays = (EQUAL_DELAY_DAYS,) * len(DELAYS_DAYS)
    enumerated_equal = mean(independent_outcomes(P_FAIL, equal_delays, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS))
    three_p_kappa_ell = 3 * P_FAIL * COST_PER_DAY_CENTS * EQUAL_DELAY_DAYS
    print(f"expected cost by enumeration: {enumerated_equal} cents")
    print(f"closed form 3 * p * kappa * ell: {three_p_kappa_ell} cents")
    print(f"enumeration equals 3 * p * kappa * ell: {enumerated_equal == three_p_kappa_ell}")

    print("D. Same marginal probability, two joint laws: no-cost probability, mean and variance")
    comonotone = comonotone_outcomes(P_FAIL, DELAYS_DAYS, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS)
    independent = independent_outcomes(P_FAIL, DELAYS_DAYS, COST_PER_DAY_CENTS)
    for name, outcomes in (("comonotone", comonotone), ("independent", independent)):
        zero = sum((prob for prob, cost in outcomes if cost == 0), Fraction(0))
        print(
            f"{name}: probability of no cost {zero}, mean {mean(outcomes)} cents, "
            f"variance {variance(outcomes)} square cents"
        )
    print(f"the two means are equal: {mean(comonotone) == mean(independent)}")
    print(f"the comonotone variance is larger: {variance(comonotone) > variance(independent)}")


main()

4Where the model stops

Each result of Section 2 holds under the assumptions it names. The model is a simplification of settlement failures, and four places need the analyst's attention:

  • The failure probability is a parameter. A1 fixes \(p\). The lesson states no failure rate for any market, and it cites no source that gives one. Any value of \(p\) for a real trade needs its own source, stated by the analyst.
  • The cost is linear and fixed. A2 assumes a fixed cost per business day and no other cost. Fees, penalties, caps and costs that do not grow with the delay are outside the model.
  • Delays are fixed, and only two joint laws are compared. A2 fixes each delay, and the model does not represent variation in delays. A3 and A4 are two joint laws with the same marginal probability. Proposition 2.1 holds for any joint law with that marginal, but the lesson does not say where real failures lie between A3 and A4.
  • Operational risk is wider than settlement failures. Definition 1.1 names processes, people, systems and external events. The model covers the cost of failed settlements only. It models no recovery action and no loss from the other sources.

The Basel III framework is named in the registry at index level, as Basel III: international regulatory framework for banks. The lesson does not state what that framework says about operational risk, because its operational-risk section was not read for this lesson.

5Misconceptions

  1. “Dependent failures cost more on average than independent failures.”

    Proposition 2.1 gives the same mean under any joint law with the marginal probability \(p\), because the mean is linear. Proposition 2.4 confirms this under A4. Replacing A3 by A4 leaves the mean unchanged and, for \(\kappa\gt 0\) and \(0\lt p\lt 1\), changes the variance unless at most one delay is positive (Proposition 2.5). Lab 1, Part D, prints the same mean, 4375/2 cents, under both laws.

  2. “With three trades that each fail with probability \(p\), the chance that at least one fails is \(3p\).”

    Under A3 the chance is \(1-(1-p)^3\). For \(p=1/8\) that is 169/512, while \(3p\) is 192/512 (Example 2.7). Under A4 the chance is \(p\). For \(0\lt p\lt 1\), the figure \(3p\) is not the probability of a failure under A3 or under A4 (Example 2.7).

  3. “The expected cost depends only on the failure probability.”

    Proposition 2.1 gives \(\E[C]=p\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\), which depends on the cost per day \(\kappa\) and on every delay \(\ell_i\) as well. Lab 1, Part B, uses delays \((1,2,4)\) and gives 4375/2 cents. Part C uses equal delays of 2 business days, \((2,2,2)\), with the same \(p\), and gives 1875 cents.

6Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Mechanics problems use the definitions and the closed form. Proof problems use the assumptions named in each problem. Empirical problems take their data only from the printed output of Lab 1, which is SIMULATED. Research problems extend the model.

Mechanics

M1 · Three trades, one delay each

Three trades each fail with probability 1/4 under A1. The cost is \(\kappa=1000\) cents per business day, and the delays are 2, 3 and 1 business days. Under A1 and A2, and for either joint law, find \(\E[C]\) in cents.

Solution

The mean needs only the marginal probabilities, so Proposition 2.1 applies under either joint law. The sum of the delays is \(2+3+1=6\), so \(\E[C]=\tfrac14\cdot 1000\cdot 6=1500\) cents. The value is a whole number of cents, so no rounding is needed.

M2 · No failure among three trades

Three trades fail independently, each with probability 1/8. Find the probability that no trade fails, and the probability that at least one trade fails.

Solution

By the product formula for the pattern \((0,0,0)\) in Proposition 2.2, the probability that no trade fails is \((7/8)^3=343/512\). The probability that at least one trade fails is the complement, \(1-343/512=169/512\). Lab 1, Part B, prints both values for the printed parameters, and the first is the probability of the all-ok pattern.

M3 · Equal delays

Three trades with \(p=1/8\) and \(\kappa=2500\) cents per business day each settle 2 business days late if they fail. Find \(\E[C]\) in cents under A1 and A2, by the closed form \(3p\kappa\ell\).

Solution

With \(\ell=2\), the closed form gives \(3\cdot\tfrac18\cdot 2500\cdot 2=\tfrac{15000}{8}=1875\) cents. This is a whole number of cents, and it equals the enumerated value that Lab 1, Part C, prints.

Proof

P1 · The expectation of an indicator

Let \(A\) be an event on a finite probability space. Prove that \(\E[\indic\{A\}]=\Prob(A)\), and use it to prove Proposition 2.1. State every hypothesis the argument uses.

Solution

On a finite space, \(\E[\indic\{A\}]=\sum_{\omega}\indic\{A\}(\omega)\Prob(\{\omega\})=\sum_{\omega\in A}\Prob(\{\omega\})=\Prob(A)\). For Proposition 2.1, \(C=\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\indic\{F_i\}\) by Definition 2.1, so linearity of the finite sum gives \(\E[C]=\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\Prob(F_i)\). A1 sets \(\Prob(F_i)=p\). The hypotheses used are the finite space, A1 for the probabilities, and A2 for the constants \(\kappa\ell_i\). Nothing about the joint law is used.

P2 · The variance of a sum of indicators under independence

Let \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) be real constants. Under A1 and A3, prove that \(\Var(\sum_i a_i\indic\{F_i\})=p(1-p)\sum_i a_i^2\). State which step uses independence.

Solution

Since \(\indic\{F_i\}^2=\indic\{F_i\}\), \(\Var(\indic\{F_i\})=p-p^2=p(1-p)\) by A1. Expanding the variance of the sum gives \(\sum_i a_i^2\Var(\indic\{F_i\})+2\sum_{i\lt j}a_ia_j\Cov(\indic\{F_i\},\indic\{F_j\})\). Independence, A3, gives \(\Cov=0\) for \(i\ne j\), because \(\E[\indic\{F_i\}\indic\{F_j\}]=p^2\) equals the product of the means. Only the covariance step uses A3.

P3 · The no-cost probability under the two laws

Under A1 and A2, take \(p\) with \(0\lt p\lt 1\), \(\ell_i\ge 1\) and \(\kappa\ge 1\). Show that \(\Prob(C=0)\) is \((1-p)^3\) under A3 and \(1-p\) under A4, and that \((1-p)^3\lt 1-p\).

Solution

Every failed trade adds at least 1 cent, since \(\kappa\ell_i\ge 1\). So \(C=0\) exactly when no trade fails. Under A3 that probability is \((1-p)^3\), the pattern probability \(P(0,0,0)\) of Proposition 2.2. Under A4 it is \(1-p\), the complement of \(F_1\). Because \(0\lt 1-p\lt 1\), \((1-p)^2\lt 1\), and so \((1-p)^3\lt 1-p\). This is Proposition 2.6.

Research

R1 · Equal delays and n trades

Let there be \(n\) trades, each with marginal failure probability \(p\) where \(0\lt p\lt 1\), each delayed by the same \(\ell\ge 1\) business days, and cost \(\kappa\ge 1\) cents per day. Find \(\E[C]\) under A3 and A4, and the ratio of the variance under A4 to the variance under A3.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 2.1 does not use the number of trades, so \(\E[C]=np\kappa\ell\) under A3, and Proposition 2.4 gives the same value under A4. The proof of Proposition 2.5, which also does not use the number of trades, with \(a_i=\kappa\ell\), gives \(\Var_3(C)=n\,p(1-p)\kappa^2\ell^2\). Under A4, \(C=n\kappa\ell\,\indic\{F_1\}\), so \(\Var_4(C)=n^2\,p(1-p)\kappa^2\ell^2\). The ratio is \(\Var_4/\Var_3=n\). For \(n=3\) the ratio is 3. Lab 1 does not print this ratio, and the value comes from the formulas above.

R2 · The range of the no-failure probability

Three trades each have marginal failure probability \(p=1/8\). Under any joint law with these marginals, show that \(\Prob(\text{no trade fails})\) lies in the interval \([5/8,\,7/8]\), and show that both endpoints are attained.

Solution

Let \(U=F_1\cup F_2\cup F_3\). Then \(\Prob(\text{no trade fails})=1-\Prob(U)\). Since \(F_1\subseteq U\), \(\Prob(U)\ge p=1/8\), so the no-failure probability is at most \(7/8\). For the other bound, \(\Prob(A\cup B)\le\Prob(A)+\Prob(B)\) for any events, because \(\Prob(A\cup B)=\Prob(A)+\Prob(B)-\Prob(A\cap B)\). Applying this twice gives \(\Prob(U)\le 3p=3/8\), so the no-failure probability is at least \(5/8\). The value \(7/8\) is attained under A4. The value \(5/8\) is attained when the three failure events are disjoint, each of probability \(1/8\), on the space \(\Omega=\{\omega_0,\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3\}\) with \(\Prob(\{\omega_0\})=5/8\), \(\Prob(\{\omega_i\})=1/8\), and \(F_i=\{\omega_i\}\).

R3 · Monotonicity in the inputs

On the domain \(0\le p\le 1\), \(\kappa\ge 0\) and \(\ell_i\ge 0\), show that \(\E[C]=p\kappa\sum_i\ell_i\) is nondecreasing in \(p\), in \(\kappa\), and in each \(\ell_i\).

Solution

By Proposition 2.1, \(\E[C]\) is the product of \(p\), \(\kappa\), and \(\sum_i\ell_i\). The proof of Proposition 2.1 uses the marginal probabilities \(\Prob(F_i)=p\) and the finite space, so the formula also holds at \(p=0\) and \(p=1\), for any joint law with those marginals. That proof uses A2 only as the fixed constants \(\kappa\ell_i\), so whole-number values are not needed. Each factor is nonnegative on the domain. Increasing one factor, while the others stay nonnegative and fixed, cannot decrease a product of nonnegative numbers. So \(\E[C]\) is nondecreasing in \(p\), in \(\kappa\), and in each \(\ell_i\), where \(\ell_i\) enters through the sum.
