Fund-Economics Model

Build a complete computational model of the active asset management industry under the rational equilibrium framework of Berk and Green (2004). You will simulate Bayesian belief updating from noisy returns, endogenous capacity limits from decreasing returns to scale, and rational flow dynamics driving expected net investor alpha to zero while managers extract economic rents through management fees.

The project ladder

Four rungs scaffold your progress. If you achieved 80 percent on the placement diagnostic and answered both skill checks, you may skip rungs 1 and 2 and proceed directly to rung 3.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Fully solved single-period instance in Lab 1 below: initial capacity sizing, Bayesian precision update from noisy portfolio returns, end-of-period asset accounting, and net investor flow determination.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Interactive completion lab in Lab 2 below: complete the precision-weighted Bayesian update, equilibrium capacity truncation, and flow determination lines to verify instant checks.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    The core deliverable: implement the multi-period dynamic simulator fund_model.py, verified locally and in-browser by test_project.py across 50 seeded instances, and draft an analytical economic memo.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Optional extension: multi-fund competitive industry simulation with investor search cost heterogeneity (Sirri–Tufano) and endogenous fee competition across asset classes.

Lab 1 · Worked example: Single-period Berk–Green mechanics

Demonstrates the step-by-step arithmetic of prior capacity determination, noisy Bayesian updating, new equilibrium capacity sizing, and external investor flow calculation.

"""Lab BK2-project-1: Project Rung 1 - Worked example of the Berk-Green model.

Calculates single-period fund capacity, Bayesian belief updating with observation
noise, end-of-period asset value, net investor flows, and rent extraction.
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def worked_example() -> dict[str, float]:
    # Initial parameters
    mu_0 = 0.030  # 3.0% prior expected skill
    sigma_0_sq = 0.0004  # prior variance (sigma_0 = 2.0%)
    sigma_eps_sq = 0.0100  # noise variance (sigma_eps = 10.0%)
    fee_rate = 0.012  # 1.2% annual management fee (120 bps)
    lam = 2.0e-5  # scale cost parameter per million AUM
    q_0 = 100.0  # $100M initial AUM

    # 1. Capacity before observation
    q_star_0 = max(0.0, (mu_0 - fee_rate) / lam)

    # 2. Realization: manager skill = 3.5%, noise shock = +4.0%
    true_skill = 0.035
    shock = 0.040
    gross_alpha = true_skill - lam * q_0
    gross_ret = gross_alpha + shock
    net_ret = gross_ret - fee_rate
    fees_collected = fee_rate * q_0
    investor_profit = q_0 * net_ret

    # 3. Bayesian update from observed gross return
    signal = gross_ret + lam * q_0
    tau_prior = 1.0 / sigma_0_sq
    tau_noise = 1.0 / sigma_eps_sq
    tau_post = tau_prior + tau_noise
    sigma_post_sq = 1.0 / tau_post
    mu_1 = (tau_prior * mu_0 + tau_noise * signal) / tau_post

    # 4. Next period target capacity and net flow
    q_star_1 = max(0.0, (mu_1 - fee_rate) / lam)
    assets_end = max(0.0, q_0 * (1.0 + net_ret))
    net_flow = q_star_1 - assets_end

    return {
        "initial_capacity": q_star_0,
        "gross_return_pct": gross_ret * 100.0,
        "net_return_pct": net_ret * 100.0,
        "observed_signal_pct": signal * 100.0,
        "posterior_mean_pct": mu_1 * 100.0,
        "posterior_variance": sigma_post_sq,
        "new_capacity": q_star_1,
        "net_flow": net_flow,
        "fees_collected": fees_collected,
        "investor_profit": investor_profit,
    }


def main() -> None:
    res = worked_example()
    print("Project Rung 1: Berk-Green Single-Period Worked Example")
    print("------------------------------------------------------")
    print(f"Initial Prior Capacity AUM:  ${res['initial_capacity']:,.1f}M")
    print(f"Realized Gross Return:       {res['gross_return_pct']:.2f}%")
    print(f"Realized Net Return:         {res['net_return_pct']:.2f}%")
    print(f"Extracted Skill Signal:      {res['observed_signal_pct']:.2f}%")
    print(f"Updated Posterior Skill:     {res['posterior_mean_pct']:.3f}%")
    print(f"New Equilibrium Capacity:    ${res['new_capacity']:,.1f}M")
    print(f"Net Investor Flow:           ${res['net_flow']:,.2f}M")
    print(f"Manager Fees Collected:      ${res['fees_collected']:,.3f}M")
    print(f"Investor Dollar Profit:      ${res['investor_profit']:,.3f}M")


main()

Specification (Rung 3)

Context
In competitive capital markets with rational investors and decreasing returns to scale in active asset management, capital flows to skilled managers until expected net excess return to investors is zero: \(\mathbb{E}_t[R_{n, t+1}] = 0\). You are an institutional quantitative research analyst tasked with modeling a fund's multi-period lifecycle under the Berk & Green (2004) framework. Your simulator must track managerial skill learning, capacity expansion and contraction, net investor flows, and the economic division of gross value creation between fund managers and investors.
Inputs
A fund instance dictionary provided by grader/instance.py:make_instance(seed) containing:
  • prior_mean (\(\mu_0\)): Prior expected manager skill (annualized excess return).
  • prior_variance (\(\sigma_0^2\)): Prior variance over manager skill.
  • noise_variance (\(\sigma_\epsilon^2\)): Idiosyncratic annual portfolio return variance.
  • fee_rate (\(f\)): Annual proportional management fee rate.
  • cost_parameter (\(\lambda\)): Decreasing returns to scale parameter (\(\lambda > 0\) per million AUM).
  • initial_aum (\(q_0\)): Launch fund assets in millions.
  • true_skill (\(a\)): True unobserved manager skill.
  • num_periods (\(T\)): Number of annual simulation horizons.
  • noise_shocks: List of \(T\) Gaussian return noise shocks \(\epsilon_t\).
  • test_signals: List of test signals for unit verification.
Required outputs
The module fund_model.py implementing:
  • FundParameters: Immutable frozen dataclass holding fund configuration parameters.
  • update_belief(prior_mean, prior_variance, signal, noise_variance) -> tuple[float, float]: Gaussian conjugate Bayesian update returning (post_mean, post_variance).
  • capacity_aum(expected_skill, fee_rate, cost_parameter) -> float: Equilibrium capacity \(q^* = \max\left(0, \frac{\mu - f}{\lambda}\right)\).
  • gross_alpha(skill, aum, cost_parameter) -> float: Gross alpha \(\alpha_g = \text{skill} - \lambda \cdot \text{aum}\).
  • net_alpha(skill, aum, cost_parameter, fee_rate) -> float: Net investor alpha \(\alpha_n = \alpha_g - f\).
  • extract_signal(gross_return, aum, cost_parameter) -> float: Scale-invariant signal \(s = R_g + \lambda \cdot \text{aum}\).
  • manager_rent_share(total_fees, investor_profit) -> float: Ratio of manager fees to total net economic surplus.
  • simulate_fund_lifecycle(params, true_skill, noise_shocks) -> dict[str, Any]: Full multi-period lifecycle dictionary tracking series of AUM, returns, signals, beliefs, capacities, flows, and cumulative rent shares.
In addition to the code deliverable, an analytical economic memo (1,000–1,500 words) answering where investor alpha goes and comparing empirical fund evidence with model predictions.
Constraints
  • Pure Python 3.14 standard library. No external linear algebra packages required.
  • Non-negativity: Capacity AUM and fund assets must never be negative (\(q \ge 0\)).
  • Zero-skill boundary: If expected skill falls below management fee (\(\mu \le f\)), capacity must evaluate to exactly 0.0.
  • Precision articulation: Precision must sum linearly (\(\tau_{\text{post}} = \tau_{\text{prior}} + \tau_{\text{noise}}\)).
  • Flow identity: Net flow in each active period must strictly satisfy \(F_t = q_{t+1}^* - \max(0, q_t (1 + R_{n, t}))\).
Grading
Grading is based on both an autograder and a rubric. The autograder runs grader/test_project.py across 50 seeded instances (seeds 0–49), validating precision additivity, capacity monotonicity, flow conservation, rent extraction bounds, and zero-net-alpha equilibrium convergence. The economic memo is evaluated by rubric across five criteria (C1 through C5), each with four observable performance levels.

Lab 3 · Verification runner: Multi-period fund simulation

Runs a multi-period Berk-Green lifecycle simulation over five annual periods, displaying AUM evolution, net returns, manager fees, investor profits, and cumulative rent extraction.

"""Lab BK2-project-3: Project Rung 3 - Full fund-economics lifecycle model verification.

Validates the full multi-period Berk-Green fund lifecycle simulation,
checking flow accounting, fee extraction, and zero-net-alpha equilibrium.
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def run_verification() -> None:
    prior_mean = 0.030
    prior_variance = 0.0004
    noise_variance = 0.0100
    fee_rate = 0.015
    cost_param = 2.0e-5
    init_aum = 100.0
    true_skill = 0.035
    shocks = [0.015, -0.020, 0.030, -0.010, 0.005]

    aum = init_aum
    mu = prior_mean
    var = prior_variance

    total_fees = 0.0
    total_inv_profit = 0.0

    print("Project Rung 3: Lifecycle Simulation Verification")
    print("-------------------------------------------------")
    print(
        f"{'Period':<7} {'AUM ($M)':<11} {'Net Return':<12} {'Fees ($M)':<11} {'InvProfit ($M)':<15} {'Post Skill':<10}"
    )
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------------------")

    for t, shock in enumerate(shocks, start=1):
        alpha_g = true_skill - cost_param * aum
        r_gross = alpha_g + shock
        r_net = r_gross - fee_rate
        fees = fee_rate * aum
        inv_prof = aum * r_net

        total_fees += fees
        total_inv_profit += inv_prof

        # Bayesian update
        sig = r_gross + cost_param * aum
        tau_prior = 1.0 / var
        tau_noise = 1.0 / noise_variance
        tau_post = tau_prior + tau_noise
        var = 1.0 / tau_post
        mu = (tau_prior * mu + tau_noise * sig) / tau_post

        target_cap = max(0.0, (mu - fee_rate) / cost_param)

        print(
            f"{t:<7} "
            f"${aum:>8.1f}M  "
            f"{r_net * 100.0:>8.2f}%     "
            f"${fees:>7.3f}M   "
            f"${inv_prof:>10.3f}M    "
            f"{mu * 100.0:>7.3f}%"
        )

        aum = target_cap

    rent_share = total_fees / (total_fees + total_inv_profit) if (total_fees + total_inv_profit) > 0 else 1.0
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------------------")
    print(f"Cumulative Manager Fees:    ${total_fees:,.3f}M")
    print(f"Cumulative Investor Profit: ${total_inv_profit:,.3f}M")
    print(f"Manager Rent Share:         {rent_share * 100.0:.2f}%")


def main() -> None:
    run_verification()

Executive Summary & Theoretical Resolution

Empirical studies consistently document that active mutual funds deliver average net alphas indistinguishable from zero after fees and expenses. Conventional critiques interpret this finding as evidence that active managers possess no investment skill. Following Berk and Green (2004), we demonstrate that in a competitive rational market where managers have decreasing returns to scale (\(\lambda > 0\)), zero average net alpha is an equilibrium outcome rather than proof of absent skill. Managerial talent exists and generates positive gross economic surplus, but competitive investor capital flows bid away all net investor alpha, allowing fund sponsors to capture 100% of expected economic rents through management fees.

Equilibrium Capacity & Scale Mechanics

Let manager ability be \(a\) and fund size be \(q_t\). The fund's gross excess return is \(R_{g, t} = a - \lambda q_t + \epsilon_t\), where \(\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\epsilon^2)\). With annual proportional management fee \(f\), net investor return is \(R_{n, t} = a - \lambda q_t - f + \epsilon_t\). In competitive capital markets with zero search costs, capital flows until expected net excess return is zero: \(\mathbb{E}_t[R_{n, t+1}] = \mu_t - \lambda q_{t+1}^* - f = 0\). This establishes equilibrium capacity \(q^* = \max\left(0, \frac{\mu_t - f}{\lambda}\right)\). At capacity \(q^*\), expected gross alpha is \(\alpha_g(q^*) = f\), proving that the fund generates gross outperformance exactly equal to its fee burden.

Conjugate Bayesian Learning & Flow Dynamics

Investors do not observe talent \(a\) directly. Beginning with Gaussian prior \(a \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2)\) with precision \(\tau_0 = 1/\sigma_0^2\), investors observe gross return \(R_{g, t}\) and extract signal \(s_t = R_{g, t} + \lambda q_t = a + \epsilon_t\). Because noise variance is \(\sigma_\epsilon^2\), noise precision is \(\tau_\epsilon = 1/\sigma_\epsilon^2\). Conjugate updating yields posterior precision \(\tau_t = \tau_{t-1} + \tau_\epsilon\) and posterior mean \(\mu_t = \frac{\tau_{t-1}\mu_{t-1} + \tau_\epsilon s_t}{\tau_t}\). Flows adjust to enforce \(q_{t+1}^*\): \(F_t = q_{t+1}^* - q_t(1 + R_{n, t})\). Consequently, young funds with high track record uncertainty experience extreme flow volatility per unit of performance shock, whereas mature funds experience dampened percentage flow responses as \(\tau_t \to \infty\).

Economic Rent Extraction Decomposition

Total economic value created by managerial talent across \(T\) periods is \(V = \sum_{t=1}^T q_t(a - \lambda q_t)\). Manager fee revenue is \(\sum_{t=1}^T f \cdot q_t\), while investor excess profits equal \(\sum_{t=1}^T q_t R_{n, t}\). In rational expectation, \(\mathbb{E}[q_t R_{n, t}] = 0\). Therefore, the expected manager rent share is identically 100%: \(\frac{\text{Fees}}{\text{Fees} + \text{Investor Profit}} = 1.0\). Our numerical simulation across 50 seeds verifies that while realized investor profit fluctuates around zero due to idiosyncratic noise \(\epsilon_t\), cumulative manager fees grow deterministically with capacity.

Institutional Frictions & Empirical Violations

While Berk and Green (2004) resolves the active management paradox in frictionless markets, real-world data reveals significant deviations: (1) Convex flow-performance sensitivity (Sirri & Tufano 1998; Chevalier & Ellison 1997) shows that underperforming funds do not experience outflows sufficient to liquidate them to \(q^* = 0\), creating persistent negative net alpha funds; (2) Retail distribution fees (12b-1) and investor search costs insulate incumbent funds; (3) Tax overhang in taxable accounts penalizes redemptions, trapping capital in sub-scale active funds.

Rung 4 · Stretch

For students seeking advanced portfolio projects, extend the single-fund Berk–Green framework into a multi-fund competitive industry simulation:

  • Heterogeneous Search Costs: Model an investor population with Pareto-distributed search costs (following Sirri & Tufano 1998), showing how search frictions allow underperforming funds to survive with persistent negative net alpha.
  • Endogenous Fee Competition: Allow fund managers to choose annual fee rate \(f\) endogenously to maximize steady-state fee revenue \(f \cdot q^*(f) = f \cdot \frac{a - f}{\lambda}\), proving that the revenue-maximizing fee is \(f^* = a / 2\).
  • Capacity Cross-Externalities: Simulate multiple active managers competing in the same market sector where aggregate scale \(\sum q_j\) reduces industry-wide alpha.
