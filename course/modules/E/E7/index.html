Monetary Economics & Central Banking

Central banks govern the world's most critical financial prices: short-term interest rates, reserve remuneration, and sovereign term premia. This module builds the doctoral-level theory and empirical methodology of modern central banking. We rigorously analyze the time inconsistency of optimal policy and the inflationary bias under discretion, study operating frameworks transitioning from corridor systems with scarce reserves to floor systems with abundant reserves, evaluate the mechanics of quantitative easing and forward guidance at the zero lower bound, master high-frequency identification of monetary policy shocks using futures tick data, estimate dynamic transmission with Jordà local projections, compare narrative shock identification against market surprises, and explore the determination of the equilibrium natural rate of interest (\(r^*\)), the fiscal theory of the price level, and the 2021–23 post-pandemic inflation surge.

  • Prerequisites: New Keynesian model, dynamic IS curve, Taylor principle, and Taylor rule from E6/E5; vector autoregressions and Kalman filtering from S3.
  • Project: Monetary-shock identification using high-frequency futures surprises and Jordà local projections, compared to the Romer–Romer narrative series. Graded by both autograder and qualitative research note rubric.
  • Data: Empirical event studies, futures price jump formulas, and SIMULATED macroeconomic panels calibrated to historical US policy episodes.

100 h lessons · 30 h project (23.1% of 130 h)

Objectives

By the end of E7 you will be able to:

  1. Formulate the Kydland–Prescott (1977) social loss function and derive the exact discretionary inflation bias under rational private expectations (Lesson 1).
  2. Construct infinitely repeated game trigger strategies and solve for the critical discount factor sustaining zero inflation credibility in the Barro–Gordon (1983) model (Lesson 2).
  3. Dissect central bank balance sheet items, autonomous liquidity factors, and estimate the nonlinear reserve demand curve across scarce and abundant regimes (Lesson 3).
  4. Analyze interbank market clearing in corridor frameworks versus floor frameworks and model the Bech–Klee (2011) GSE arbitrage discount below IORB (Lesson 4).
  5. Model the zero lower bound constraint on nominal policy rates and evaluate Odyssean forward guidance commitments along the term structure (Lesson 5).
  6. Decompose quantitative easing announcement yields into portfolio-balance duration extraction, signalling, and safe asset scarcity channels (Lesson 6).
  7. Extract high-frequency monetary surprises from Fed funds futures and Eurodollar contracts in 30-minute FOMC event windows (Lesson 7).
  8. Estimate dynamic macroeconomic impulse responses to monetary shocks using Jordà (2005) local projections with Newey–West standard errors (Lessons 8 and 9).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions assessing theoretical, institutional, and econometric readiness for advanced central banking, including two skill checks testing the implementation skills required in project rungs 1 and 2. Mastery threshold is 80% (8 correct out of 9).

  1. In the baseline Kydland-Prescott (1977) model, what is the equilibrium output under discretionary monetary policy?

    Options

    Because private agents form rational expectations, \(\pi^e = \pi\) in equilibrium, leaving surprise inflation at zero and output at the natural rate \(y_n\).

  2. If \(\alpha = 1.0\), \(\gamma = 2.0\), \(k = 1.05\), and \(y_n = 100\), what is the discretionary inflation bias \(\pi_d = (\alpha/\gamma)(k-1)y_n\)?

    \(\pi_d = (1.0 / 2.0) \times (1.05 - 1.0) \times 100 = 0.5 \times 5.0 = 2.5\).

  3. Why does the interbank rate in a floor system typically clear below the Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB) rate in the US?

    Options

    GSEs have accounts at the Federal Reserve but are barred from earning IORB; they lend cash below IORB to commercial banks who capture the arbitrage spread.

  4. Which transmission channel of QE operates by removing duration risk from private balance sheets to lower term premia?

    Options

    Under imperfect asset substitutability (Vayanos & Vila), central bank duration extraction lowers term premia across all long-term fixed income instruments.

  5. In high-frequency event studies of FOMC announcements, what narrow window is standard in Kuttner (2001) and Gürkaynak et al. (2005)?

    Options

    A tight 30-minute window isolates policy surprises from confounding macroeconomic data releases and news events.

  6. Skill check (rung 1): In a 30-day month, an FOMC announcement occurs on day 10. What is the Kuttner scaling factor \(M / (M - d)\)?

    \(30 / (30 - 10) = 30 / 20 = 1.5\).

  7. Skill check (rung 2): Why do Jordà (2005) local projections require Newey–West standard errors for horizons \(h \ge 1\)?

    Options

    Multi-step forward forecasts share common shock terms over the overlapping \(h\)-period window, creating an MA(\(h\)) residual structure.

  8. What does the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (FTPL) state determines the initial price level?

    Options

    Under FTPL, government debt is an equity claim on future fiscal surpluses: \(B_0 / P_0 = \sum_{t=0}^\infty \beta^t s_t\).

  9. If trend productivity growth is \(g = 1.2\%\) and persistent non-growth factors are \(z = 0.3\%\), what is the Holston–Laubach–Williams natural rate \(r^* = g + z\)?

    \(r^* = 1.2\% + 0.3\% = 1.5\%\).

The project ladder

The module culminates in the Monetary-Shock Identification project (30 hours), structured into a four-rung doctoral ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Step-by-step analytical derivation of futures jump scaling and a complete local projection walkthrough.

  2. Completion problem

    Implement the high-frequency surprise calculation and single-horizon local projection regression, validated against instant test feedback.

  3. Independent project

    Author a production Python module monetary_shocks.py computing policy surprises, estimating local projections across horizons, and comparing against narrative shocks, evaluated by automated suite and research note rubric. Project specifications.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the model to estimate proxy-SVAR impulse responses with external instruments or decompose surprises into pure policy vs. central bank information shocks.
