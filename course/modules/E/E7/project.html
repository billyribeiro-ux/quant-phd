Module E7 · Project · 30 hours · Part E, Economics

Monetary-Shock Identification

Empirical macroeconomics faces an intrinsic endogeneity challenge: central banks adjust policy rates systematically in response to prevailing economic conditions, confounding the true causal effect of monetary policy on economic activity. In this project you will implement the two leading empirical identification frameworks of modern macroeconomics: high-frequency event study surprises from Fed funds futures (Kuttner 2001, Gürkaynak et al. 2005) and internal forecast narrative filtering (Romer & Romer 2004). You will estimate dynamic impulse responses using Jordà (2005) local projections with Newey-West standard errors, test for Nakamura-Steinsson (2018) central bank information effects, and synthesize your empirical findings into a professional research note.

30 h project (23.1% of module hours)

The project ladder

The project follows a structured 4-rung learning progression:

  1. Worked example

    Step-by-step calculation of the Kuttner futures scaling factor and a complete Jordà local projection regression with confidence bands.

  2. Completion

    Fill in the implementation of the monthly surprise scaling and single-horizon OLS estimator with instant test validation.

  3. Independent

    Author the complete monetary_shocks.py module implementing futures surprise cleaning, multi-horizon local projections, shock comparison metrics, and information effect diagnostics, validated by the autograder suite across 50 random seeds.

  4. Stretch

    Estimate a Proxy-SVAR model using the high-frequency surprise as an external instrument (Gertler & Karadi 2015) or implement Miranda-Agrippino & Ricco (2021) information-robust instruments.

from __future__ import annotations


def calculate_fed_funds_surprise(f_post: float, f_pre: float, day: int, days_in_month: int) -> float:
    scale = days_in_month / float(days_in_month - day)
    return scale * (f_post - f_pre)

    day = 12
    days_in_month = 30

    surprise = calculate_fed_funds_surprise(f_post, f_pre, day, days_in_month)

    print(f"Step 1: Event window on day {day}/{days_in_month}:")
    print(f"  Raw futures jump:  {(f_post - f_pre) * 100:+.2f} bps")
    print(f"  Scaling factor:    {scale:.4f}")
    print(f"  Target surprise:   {surprise * 100:+.2f} bps")

    # Step 2: Local Projection Estimation over H = 4
    # Synthetic 5-period illustrative response
    # y_{t+h} - y_{t-1} = beta^(h) * shock_t

    print("\nStep 2: Jordà Local Projection Impulse Responses for Output Gap:")
    print("  Horizon h | Response beta(h) | Standard Error | 95% Confidence Interval")
    for h in range(len(betas)):

        print(f"     h={h}   |      {betas[h]:+6.3f}      |      {ses[h]:.3f}     |     [{lower:+6.3f}, {upper:+6.3f}]")

Rung 3 · Independent Project: Monetary-Shock Identification Module

In this independent project, you author a complete Python module monetary_shocks.py that implements the full empirical shock identification pipeline and write a research note.

Project Specification

Context
Central bank communication and policy decisions create simultaneous macroeconomic reactions. To identify causal transmission, researchers isolate unexpected policy jumps in narrow asset market windows or cleanse policy decisions of private central bank forecasts.
Inputs
Event-level Fed funds futures prices before and after FOMC announcements with meeting calendar dates; monthly panel data of output growth, CPI inflation, 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields, and credit spreads; and Romer-Romer narrative series.
Required outputs
A fully functional Python module monetary_shocks.py exporting:
  • calculate_fed_funds_surprise(f_post, f_pre, day, days_in_month) -> float
  • clean_high_frequency_surprises(announcements, n_months) -> list[float]
  • estimate_local_projection(y, shock, controls, horizon, lags) -> tuple[list[float], list[float]]
  • compare_monetary_shocks(shock_a, shock_b) -> dict[str, float]
  • test_information_effect(shock, yield_change, growth_revision) -> dict[str, float]
accompanied by an empirical research note (1,500–2,500 words) analyzing the transmission dynamics and information effects.
Constraints
Pure Python 3.14 standard library and NumPy/SciPy. No external time series packages (e.g. statsmodels). Numerical tolerances within 1e-4. Deterministic execution with zero side-effects.
Grading
The project is evaluated by both an automated test suite (autograder) verifying 50 randomized market seeds and a qualitative rubric evaluating the research note.

Running the Suite Locally

Annotated Exemplars

Level 2 Exemplar (Competent)

"We took the Fed funds futures price changes and ran OLS regressions of GDP on the surprise. The output response is negative at horizon 2, which shows monetary policy is contractionary. We did not use Newey-West standard errors because standard OLS errors were small."

Critique: Lacks methodological rigor. Fails to account for MA(\(h\)) residual correlation induced by overlapping multi-period forecast horizons, leading to severely understated standard errors.

Level 4 Exemplar (Distinguished)

"To identify structural monetary shocks, we extract intraday Fed funds futures jumps in 30-minute FOMC windows, applying the Kuttner (2001) adjustment \(M/(M-d)\) to account for contract averaging. In Jordà local projections, multi-step outcome windows \(y_{t+h} - y_{t-1}\) inherently generate MA(\(h\)) residual autocorrelation; we therefore compute Newey-West standard errors with bandwidth \(L = h + 1\). Comparing the high-frequency surprise with the Romer-Romer (2004) Greenbook-purified narrative series yields a correlation of 0.48. Interestingly, an unadjusted surprise generates a transient positive output response at \(h=0\), reflecting the Nakamura-Steinsson (2018) information effect, which vanishes once Greenbook growth revisions are controlled for."

Critique: Exemplary doctoral-level analysis. Integrates micro-market mechanics, time series econometrics, and information theory into a cohesive causal narrative.

Rung 4 · Stretch Extensions

Extend your research with doctoral-level econometric extensions:

  1. Proxy-SVAR with External Instruments: Use your high-frequency surprise as an external instrument \(z_t\) to identify structural monetary shocks in a monthly VAR containing output, inflation, 1-year Treasury yields, and the Excess Bond Premium (Gertler & Karadi 2015).
