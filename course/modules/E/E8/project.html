CIP-Basis Monitor and Mechanism Memo

Prior to 2008, Covered Interest Parity held as an iron law of international finance. Today, persistent deviations make synthetic dollar borrowing via FX swaps substantially more expensive than cash borrowing. In this project, you construct an automated cross-currency basis monitor in Python, test it against a 50-seed autograder suite, and write a calibrated mechanism memo explaining why post-crisis leverage regulations prevent global dealers from arbitraging the gap.

  4. Stretch

Conventions and definitions

The terms below govern all calculations and code implementations across this project.

Covered Interest Parity (CIP)
The theoretical condition that the return on a risk-free cash deposit in domestic currency equals the return on a foreign-currency deposit covered against foreign exchange risk through a forward contract.
Synthetic dollar borrowing rate
For spot rate \(S_t\) and forward rate \(F_t\) quoted as US dollars per unit of foreign currency, cash foreign rate \(y_{\mathrm{f}, t}\), and accrual fractions \(\tau_{\mathrm{USD}}\) and \(\tau_{\mathrm{f}}\), the annualized synthetic dollar borrowing rate is: \[ \begin{aligned} y^{\mathrm{implied}}_{\mathrm{USD}, t} = \frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{USD}}} \left[ \frac{S_t}{F_t} (1 + y_{\mathrm{f}, t}\,\tau_{\mathrm{f}}) - 1 \right] \end{aligned} \]
Cross-currency basis
The difference between the direct cash dollar interest rate and the synthetic dollar borrowing rate, expressed in basis points (1 bp = 0.0001 = 0.01%): \[ \begin{aligned} x_t = 10000 \cdot (y_{\mathrm{USD}, t} - y^{\mathrm{implied}}_{\mathrm{USD}, t}) \end{aligned} \] Under this market convention, a negative basis (\(x_t < 0\)) indicates that borrowing synthetic dollars via FX swaps costs more than direct cash borrowing.
Day-count conventions
All US dollar money market instruments use ACT/360: \(\tau_{\mathrm{USD}} = \text{tenor\_days} / 360.0\). For foreign currencies, Euro (EUR) uses ACT/360, whereas Japanese Yen (JPY) and British Pound (GBP) use ACT/365: \(\tau_{\mathrm{f}} = \text{tenor\_days} / 365.0\).
Quarter-end reporting dates
A quarter end is defined as the last weekday (Monday through Friday) of March (month 3), June (month 6), September (month 9), and December (month 12). If the final calendar day falls on Saturday, the quarter-end reporting date is Friday (final day minus 1); if on Sunday, Friday (final day minus 2).
Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR)
A non-risk-weighted capital requirement under Basel III requiring global systemically important banks (G-SIBs) to maintain Tier 1 capital of at least 5–6% against total unweighted balance-sheet exposures, including gross repo and FX swap legs.

Lab 1 · Worked example: Synthetic dollar loan and CIP basis

Given cash rates and spot/forward quotes for JPY/USD, synthetic dollar borrowing through an FX swap yields an implied USD rate that diverges from cash USD SOFR. The resulting cross-currency basis reflects the shadow cost of intermediary balance sheets. Expected basis is approximately -38.2 bp.

"""Project Lab 1 (Rung 1): Fully worked example of cross-currency basis calculation.

SIMULATED single-day quote row. Standard library only.
Computes the implied dollar borrowing rate, cross-currency basis in basis points,
and evaluates regulatory quarter-end status step-by-step.
"""

from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Market input quote row (EUR/USD 3-month contract on 2024-03-29)
    date_iso = "2024-03-29"
    spot = 1.0850  # USD per EUR
    forward = 1.0815  # USD per EUR
    usd_rate = 0.0535  # 5.35% annual cash USD rate (SOFR)
    foreign_rate = 0.0385  # 3.85% annual foreign interbank rate (EURIBOR)
    days_usd = 90
    days_foreign = 90
    basis_daycount = 360

    # 2. Accrual fractions (Actual/360 convention on both legs)
    tau_usd = days_usd / basis_daycount  # 0.25
    tau_foreign = days_foreign / basis_daycount  # 0.25

    # 3. Step 1: Foreign leg gross accumulation factor
    foreign_accrual = 1.0 + foreign_rate * tau_foreign  # 1 + 0.0385 * 0.25 = 1.009625

    # 4. Step 2: FX swap conversion factor (Spot / Forward)
    fx_conversion = spot / forward  # 1.0850 / 1.0815 = 1.0032362...

    # 5. Step 3: Implied gross dollar factor
    gross_implied_usd = fx_conversion * foreign_accrual  # 1.0128926...

    # 6. Step 4: Implied annual simple dollar rate
    implied_usd = (gross_implied_usd - 1.0) / tau_usd  # 0.0515707... (5.157%)

    # 7. Step 5: Cross-currency basis in basis points
    # Convention: basis = (usd_rate - implied_usd) * 10,000
    basis_bp = (usd_rate - implied_usd) * 10000.0  # +19.29 bp

    # 8. Step 6: Quarter-end identification
    # March 31 2024 was Sunday -> last business weekday was Friday March 29, 2024
    _year, month, day = (int(x) for x in date_iso.split("-"))
    # In March (month 3), last weekday is 29
    is_qe = month == 3 and day == 29

    print("=== Rung 1: Worked Example Cross-Currency Basis ===")
    print(f"Date:                       {date_iso} (Quarter-End: {is_qe})")
    print(f"Spot Rate (S):              {spot:.4f} USD per EUR")
    print(f"Forward Rate (F):           {forward:.4f} USD per EUR")
    print(f"Cash USD Rate (i_usd):      {usd_rate * 100:.2f}%")
    print(f"Cash EUR Rate (i_foreign):  {foreign_rate * 100:.2f}%")
    print(f"Foreign Accrual Factor:     {foreign_accrual:.6f}")
    print(f"FX Conversion (S / F):      {fx_conversion:.6f}")
    print(f"Implied USD Borrowing Rate: {implied_usd * 100:.3f}%")
    print(f"Cross-Currency Basis:       {basis_bp:+.2f} bp")

    # Exact Fraction check for identity verification
    tau_frac = Fraction(90, 360)
    implied_frac = (Fraction(1, 1) / tau_frac) * (
        (Fraction(10850, 10000) / Fraction(10815, 10000)) * (Fraction(1, 1) + Fraction(385, 10000) * tau_frac)
        - Fraction(1, 1)
    )
    basis_frac_bp = (Fraction(535, 10000) - implied_frac) * Fraction(10000, 1)
    print(f"\nExact Fraction Basis:       {float(basis_frac_bp):.4f} bp")

    assert is_qe is True, "2024-03-29 must be flagged as quarter end"
    assert abs(basis_bp - float(basis_frac_bp)) < 1e-6, "Float and Fraction must match"

Lab 3 · Rung 3 independent check: Panel monitor summary

Across a 60-business-day synthetic panel covering a quarter-end date, the CIP monitor tracks daily basis deviations. Quarter-end dates exhibit statistically significant basis widening relative to non-quarter-end trading sessions.

"""Project Lab 3 (Rung 3 check): CIP-Basis Monitor Summary Table.

SIMULATED 60-day panel. Standard library only.
Computes summary statistics of the cross-currency basis comparing quarter-end vs. non-quarter-end dates.
"""

import datetime
import math
import random

SEED = 42


def is_quarter_end(date_iso: str) -> bool:
    """Return True if date_iso is the last weekday of a calendar quarter."""
    dt = datetime.date.fromisoformat(date_iso)
    if dt.month not in (3, 6, 9, 12):
        return False
    max_day = 31 if dt.month in (3, 12) else 30
    last_cal_day = datetime.date(dt.year, dt.month, max_day)
    if last_cal_day.weekday() == 5:
        target_day = max_day - 1
    elif last_cal_day.weekday() == 6:
        target_day = max_day - 2
    else:
        target_day = max_day
    return dt.day == target_day


def generate_panel(seed: int = SEED) -> list[dict[str, object]]:
    """Generate 60 business days of synthetic market quotes."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    current_date = datetime.date(2024, 2, 1)
    rows: list[dict[str, object]] = []

    spot = 1.0950
    usd_rate = 0.0525
    foreign_rate = 0.0350
    tau = 90.0 / 360.0

    while len(rows) < 60:
        if current_date.weekday() < 5:
            date_str = current_date.isoformat()
            is_qe = is_quarter_end(date_str)

            # Random drift
            spot *= 1.0 + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.002)
            usd_rate = max(0.01, usd_rate + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.0003))
            foreign_rate = max(0.01, foreign_rate + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.0003))

            # Basis in basis points: -25 bp normal, -65 bp quarter end
            target_basis = (-65.0 + rng.gauss(0.0, 3.0)) if is_qe else (-25.0 + rng.gauss(0.0, 2.5))

            # Forward rate implied by basis
            implied_usd = usd_rate - (target_basis / 10000.0)
            forward = spot * (1.0 + foreign_rate * tau) / (1.0 + implied_usd * tau)

            rows.append(
                {
                    "date": date_str,
                    "spot": spot,
                    "forward": forward,
                    "usd_rate": usd_rate,
                    "foreign_rate": foreign_rate,
                    "tau_usd": tau,
                    "tau_foreign": tau,
                    "basis_bp": target_basis,
                    "is_quarter_end": is_qe,
                }
            )
        current_date += datetime.timedelta(days=1)

    return rows


def main() -> None:
    records = generate_panel(SEED)

    qe_bases: list[float] = [float(str(r["basis_bp"])) for r in records if r["is_quarter_end"]]
    normal_bases: list[float] = [float(str(r["basis_bp"])) for r in records if not r["is_quarter_end"]]

    mean_qe_basis = sum(qe_bases) / len(qe_bases) if qe_bases else 0.0
    mean_normal_basis = sum(normal_bases) / len(normal_bases) if normal_bases else 0.0

    std_qe = (
        math.sqrt(sum((x - mean_qe_basis) ** 2 for x in qe_bases) / (len(qe_bases) - 1)) if len(qe_bases) > 1 else 0.0
    )
    std_normal = (
        math.sqrt(sum((x - mean_normal_basis) ** 2 for x in normal_bases) / (len(normal_bases) - 1))
        if len(normal_bases) > 1
        else 0.0
    )

    print("=== Rung 3 Check: CIP-Basis Monitor Output Summary ===")
    print(f"Total Trading Days Processed: {len(records)}")
    print(f"Normal Trading Days (N={len(normal_bases)}):")
    print(f"  Mean Cross-Currency Basis: {mean_normal_basis:+.1f} bp")
    print(f"  Standard Deviation:        {std_normal:.1f} bp")
    print(f"Quarter-End Days (N={len(qe_bases)}):")
    print(f"  Mean Cross-Currency Basis: {mean_qe_basis:+.1f} bp")
    print(f"  Standard Deviation:        {std_qe:.1f} bp")
    print(f"\nQuarter-End Spread Discrepancy: {mean_qe_basis - mean_normal_basis:+.1f} bp")

    assert len(records) == 60, "Expected 60 records"
    assert len(qe_bases) >= 1, "Must contain at least 1 quarter end"

Specification

The project deliverable consists of two integrated components: a verified Python implementation cip_monitor.py and a 500–1000 word mechanism memo analyzing cross-currency basis dynamics.

Context
In international money markets, global banks and non-US institutional investors use foreign exchange swaps to convert local-currency liquidity into US dollar funding. Prior to the 2007–2008 financial crisis, Covered Interest Parity (CIP) held as an empirical identity up to transaction costs. Following the implementation of post-crisis prudential regulation (including the Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio and liquidity coverage metrics), CIP broke down systematically. The cross-currency basis—the difference between cash dollar funding and synthetic dollar funding via FX swaps—became persistently negative for major funding currencies such as EUR and JPY, widening dramatically during funding stress and on regulatory quarter-end reporting dates.
Inputs
A daily market quotes panel represented as a list of dictionaries with keys: date (ISO 8601 string YYYY-MM-DD), spot (USD per foreign currency), forward (USD per foreign currency), usd_rate (cash dollar interest rate as a decimal float, ACT/360), foreign_rate (cash foreign interest rate as a decimal float), and tenor_days (integer maturity, typically 90 days). For JPY and GBP, foreign day count is ACT/365; for EUR, ACT/360.
Required outputs
  1. A Python module cip_monitor.py exposing four functions: implied_usd_rate(spot, forward, foreign_rate, tenor_days, usd_basis, foreign_basis) returning the synthetic dollar rate; cross_currency_basis_bp(spot, forward, usd_rate, foreign_rate, tenor_days, usd_basis, foreign_basis) returning the basis in basis points; is_quarter_end(date) returning a boolean for the last business weekday of the quarter; and monitor_table(panel, usd_basis, foreign_basis) returning a list of enriched observation records.   2. A mechanism memo (500–1000 words) titled The Cross-Currency Basis and the Shadow Price of Balance Sheet explaining the economic drivers of the basis, quarter-end window dressing, currency-specific asymmetries, and central bank swap line facilities.

Annotated exemplars

Exemplar A · Exemplary Level 4 Memo

1. The Covered Interest Parity Condition and Basis Formulation

Covered Interest Parity (CIP) dictates that an investor holding cash US dollars must earn the same riskless return whether lending directly in US money markets at rate \(y_{\mathrm{USD}, t}\) or swapping dollars into foreign currency, investing at \(y_{\mathrm{f}, t}\), and covering foreign exchange risk with a forward contract. The exact annualized synthetic dollar borrowing rate is given by \(y^{\mathrm{implied}}_{\mathrm{USD}, t} = \frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{USD}}} [ \frac{S_t}{F_t} (1 + y_{\mathrm{f}, t}\,\tau_{\mathrm{f}}) - 1 ]\), where \(\tau_{\mathrm{USD}} = \text{tenor}/360\) under money-market ACT/360 conventions. For Japanese Yen and British Pound, \(\tau_{\mathrm{f}} = \text{tenor}/365\), whereas the Euro uses ACT/360. The cross-currency basis is defined as \(x_t = 10000 \cdot (y_{\mathrm{USD}, t} - y^{\mathrm{implied}}_{\mathrm{USD}, t})\). A negative basis (\(x_t < 0\)) demonstrates that borrowing synthetic dollars costs strictly more than direct cash borrowing, creating an apparent arbitrage opportunity.

  • Meets C1 level 4: states exact non-linear compounding formula, explicit day-count conventions across currencies, and exact basis point mapping.
2. Calendar Timing and Leverage Ratio Frictions

Empirical monitoring reveals sharp spikes in the basis on regulatory quarter-end reporting dates. Under Basel III Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) rules, European and Japanese banks face capital requirements assessed on spot balance-sheet snapshots on the final business day of March, June, September, and December. When month-end falls on a weekend, calendar roll conventions push the reporting date backwards to the preceding Friday (e.g., June 28 for June 30). Because FX swaps require banks to expand gross balance-sheet assets and liabilities, global dealers sharply curtail FX swap intermediation on reporting dates to minimize regulatory capital charges. In contrast, US G-SIBs face daily-average leverage requirements, creating regulatory arbitrage and severe liquidity dry-ups at European and Japanese reporting dates.

  • Meets C2 level 4: specifies weekend backward roll logic, connects directly to Basel III SLR snapshots, and contrasts European point-in-time vs US daily-average reporting.
3. Empirical Diagnostics and Monitor Findings

Across our 60-day monitoring panel, the mean cross-currency basis is -42.8 bp with a standard deviation of 18.4 bp. Normal non-quarter-end days trade at an average basis of -36.5 bp. On quarter-end reporting dates, however, the basis widens dramatically to -78.4 bp—a widening of 41.9 bp over the non-quarter-end baseline. Stressed funding dates exhibit even more severe dislocations, reaching -112.5 bp. These empirical diagnostics confirm that the basis is not white noise, but a structured premium reflecting balance-sheet scarcity.

  • Meets C3 level 4: presents complete distributional diagnostics (mean, standard deviation, quarter-end spread vs baseline) traced to the monitoring engine.
4. Dealer Balance-Sheet Mechanics and Shadow Prices

Why do arbitrageurs fail to eliminate a 40–80 bp risk-free spread? An arbitrageur seeking to exploit a negative basis must borrow cash USD, sell spot FX, buy foreign bonds, and buy USD forward. This trade is self-financing and riskless, but expands the intermediary's gross balance sheet. Let \(\lambda_t\) denote the shadow price of the dealer's leverage constraint. The optimal arbitrage condition requires \(x_t + \lambda_t = 0\), implying that the basis directly measures the marginal cost of balance-sheet capacity (\(-x_t = \lambda_t\)). Because non-US institutions have structural dollar funding deficits, their inelastic demand for synthetic dollars drives the basis into deeply negative territory.

  • Meets C4 level 4: formulates the intermediary balance-sheet constraint, derives the shadow price equality \(-x_t = \lambda_t\), and links structural dollar demand to persistent basis deviations.
5. Central Bank Liquidity Swap Lines and Policy Limits

To prevent systemic dollar funding squeezes, the Federal Reserve maintains bilateral central bank liquidity swap lines with five major central banks (ECB, BoJ, BoE, SNB, BoC). Under these facilities, foreign central banks borrow USD against foreign-currency collateral and lend them to domestic banks at OIS + 25 bp. This standing facility establishes an effective economic ceiling on the CIP basis. However, swap lines do not completely eliminate basis deviations due to operational haircuts, eligible collateral requirements, and bank reluctance to borrow due to perceived stigma. Thus, while central bank backstops prevent catastrophic illiquidity, regulatory capital frictions sustain non-zero basis spreads in normal conditions.

  • Meets C5 level 4: analyzes swap line mechanics, pricing spread, collateral rules, and explains why swap lines act as a backstop ceiling rather than restoring exact CIP.

Exemplar B · Developing Level 2 Memo

Market Overview and Basis Calculation

Covered Interest Parity says that interest rates between two currencies should be equal when converted with forward contracts. The basis is calculated as the difference between the dollar interest rate and the foreign rate adjusted for the forward premium: \(x \approx r_{\mathrm{USD}} - r_{\mathrm{foreign}} - (F - S)/S\). When this number is negative, it means synthetic dollar borrowing is expensive.

  • Meets C1 level 2: gives an intuitive linear approximation and signs the basis, but lacks exact non-linear compounding and day-count conventions.
Quarter-End Observations

In our data, we notice that on quarter-end days the basis gets wider. This happens at the end of March, June, September, and December. When a quarter ends on a Saturday or Sunday, the market looks at the Friday before. This seems to be related to bank reporting periods.

  • Meets C2 level 2: notes quarter-end widening and mentions the Friday roll, but does not provide regulatory details on Basel III SLR snapshot differences.
Data Summary

The basis is usually around -40 basis points. On some days it reaches -70 or -100 basis points. The numbers show that CIP does not hold in practice.

  • Meets C3 level 2: quotes a few rough numbers, but fails to present structured summary statistics (mean, standard deviation, quarter-end vs baseline).
Why Arbitrage Fails

Normally banks would arbitrage this difference by borrowing where it is cheap and investing where it is expensive. However, banks have counterparty credit risk and capital limits, so they cannot take infinite trades.

  • Meets C4 level 2: mentions capital limits and credit risk in general terms, but does not formulate the balance-sheet constraint or shadow price mechanism.
Central Bank Support

The Federal Reserve provides swap lines to foreign central banks to provide dollars when markets are stressed. This helps calm the markets down.

  • Meets C5 level 2: acknowledges central bank swap lines, but does not explain pricing, operational details, or limits of the intervention.
