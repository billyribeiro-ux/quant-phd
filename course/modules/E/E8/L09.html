Safe Assets and Convenience Yields

US Treasury securities occupy a unique standing in global financial architecture. They serve not merely as financial claims yielding interest, but as the premier global safe asset providing non-pecuniary convenience services: pristine collateral for repo markets, zero regulatory risk weights, and instant liquidity in market turmoil. Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) and Jiang, Krishnamurthy & Lustig (2021) show that this convenience yield lowers the borrowing cost of the US government and generates an exorbitant privilege that directly shapes the international value of the US dollar.

1Theoretical Definition of Safe Assets and Convenience Yields

Definition 1.1 (safe asset)

A safe asset is a debt instrument that preserves its nominal and real purchasing power across states of the world, exhibiting minimal default risk, negligible asymmetric information (information-insensitivity), and superior liquidity during periods of systemic financial distress.

Definition 1.2 (convenience yield on safe assets)

The convenience yield on safe assets is the non-pecuniary value that investors attach to holding a safe asset for its utility in relaxing collateral, regulatory, or liquidity constraints. Let \(P_t^{\mathrm{safe}}\) be the market price of a sovereign Treasury bond with cash flows \(C_{t+k}\), and let \(P_t^{\mathrm{benchmark}}\) be the price of an identically cash-flow-matched private debt claim stripped of default credit risk. The safe asset trades at a price premium:

\[ P_t^{\mathrm{safe}} > P_t^{\mathrm{benchmark}} \]

In yield space, the convenience yield \(\mathrm{CY}_t\) is the resulting yield discount:

\[ \mathrm{CY}_t = y_t^{\mathrm{benchmark}} - y_t^{\mathrm{safe}} - \mathrm{CreditPremium}_t > 0 \]
Assumption 1.3 (utility from safe asset holdings)

Assume (A1) representative investors maximize expected utility with safe asset holdings providing direct liquidity services: \(U(C_t, \frac{B_t}{P_t})\), where \(u_B > 0\) and \(u_{BB} < 0\); and (A2) private debt benchmarks have identical maturity and coupon structure but do not enter the liquidity utility function.

Theorem 1.4 (Euler equation and convenience yield wedge)

Under Assumption 1.3, the first-order Euler condition for holding sovereign safe debt \(B_t\) versus private debt \(D_t\) generates a strictly positive convenience yield wedge:

\[ y_t^D - y_t^B = \frac{u_B(C_t, B_t/P_t)}{u_C(C_t, B_t/P_t)} > 0 \]

Furthermore, because \(u_{BB} < 0\), the marginal convenience yield is strictly downward-sloping in the aggregate supply of sovereign safe debt: \(\frac{\partial \mathrm{CY}}{\partial B} < 0\).

Proof

The investor maximizes \(\E_0 \sum_{t=0}^\infty \beta^t \left[ u(C_t) + v(B_t/P_t) \right]\) subject to the budget constraint \(C_t + \frac{B_t}{(1 + y_t^B) P_t} + \frac{D_t}{(1 + y_t^D) P_t} \le W_t\). The first-order condition with respect to private debt \(D_t\) yields the standard pricing relation: \(\frac{u'(C_t)}{(1 + y_t^D) P_t} = \beta \E_t\left[\frac{u'(C_{t+1})}{P_{t+1}}\right]\). The first-order condition with respect to sovereign safe debt \(B_t\) incorporates marginal convenience utility \(v'(B_t/P_t)\):

\[ \frac{u'(C_t)}{(1 + y_t^B) P_t} = \beta \E_t\left[\frac{u'(C_{t+1})}{P_{t+1}}\right] + \frac{v'(B_t/P_t)}{P_t} \]

Dividing by \(u'(C_t)/P_t\) and substituting the private debt Euler relation yields:

\[ \frac{1}{1 + y_t^B} - \frac{1}{1 + y_t^D} = \frac{v'(B_t/P_t)}{u'(C_t)} \]

In linear approximation, \(\frac{1}{1 + y_t^B} - \frac{1}{1 + y_t^D} \approx y_t^D - y_t^B = \mathrm{CY}_t\). Since \(v' > 0\) and \(u' > 0\), the wedge \(\mathrm{CY}_t\) is strictly positive. Differentiating with respect to real debt supply \(b = B/P\) gives \(\frac{\partial \mathrm{CY}}{\partial b} = \frac{v''(b)}{u'(C)} < 0\), because \(v''(b) < 0\) by diminishing marginal liquidity utility. \(\blacksquare\)

2Empirical Evidence on Treasury Convenience Yields

Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) estimate the convenience yield on US Treasuries from 1926 to 2008 by comparing Treasury yields with AAA corporate bonds, commercial paper, and Agency debt. Key empirical regularities include:

  • Magnitude: The average convenience yield on long-term US Treasuries is approximately 73 basis points, and exceeds 100 basis points for short-term Treasury bills.
  • Downward-Sloping Demand: A 10 percentage point increase in the US debt-to-GDP ratio reduces the Treasury convenience yield by approximately 12 to 17 basis points.
  • Foreign Safe Asset Demand: Jiang, Krishnamurthy & Lustig (2021) show that global demand for dollar safe assets appreciates the US dollar and widens the convenience yield spread during flight-to-safety episodes.
  • Limits in Stress Episodes: During extreme panics such as March 2020, dealer balance-sheet constraints can temporarily disrupt convenience yields, causing even Treasuries to trade at an "inconvenience yield" relative to cash (He, Nagel & Song 2022).

Lab 1 · Convenience Yield Measurement and Debt Supply Sensitivity

Measures the Treasury convenience yield across 3-month, 5-year, and 10-year maturities as the credit-stripped yield spread over private/agency debt, and tests downward-sloping supply elasticity for a +/-10% debt-to-GDP shock.

"""Lab 9: Safe Asset Convenience Yield Measurement and Supply Sensitivity.

SIMULATED numbers. Standard library only.
Measures the Treasury convenience yield as the credit-stripped yield spread
between private/agency benchmarks and US Treasuries (Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen 2012).
Evaluates convenience yield expansion under high safe-asset demand.
"""

from fractions import Fraction


class ConvenienceYieldBenchmark:
    def __init__(
        self,
        tenor: str,
        treasury_yield: Fraction,  # Annual yield (percent per year)
        private_benchmark_yield: Fraction,  # e.g. AAA corporate / Agency yield
        credit_default_spread: Fraction,  # CDS or credit risk adjustment
    ) -> None:
        self.tenor = tenor
        self.treasury_yield = treasury_yield
        self.private_yield = private_benchmark_yield
        self.credit_spread = credit_default_spread

    def compute_convenience_yield_bp(self) -> Fraction:
        """Compute the non-pecuniary convenience yield in basis points.

        Convenience Yield = (Private Benchmark - Treasury Yield - Credit Spread) * 100 bp/percent
        """
        raw_spread = self.private_yield - self.treasury_yield
        net_convenience_pct = raw_spread - self.credit_spread
        # 1 percentage point = 100 basis points
        return net_convenience_pct * Fraction(100, 1)


def simulate_supply_shock(
    base_convenience_bp: Fraction,
    debt_to_gdp_change: float,  # Change in public debt supply as % of GDP
) -> float:
    """Semi-elasticity of convenience yield with respect to Treasury supply.

    Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) estimate that higher Treasury supply
    depresses the marginal convenience yield (downward-sloping demand for safe assets).
    """
    # Slope approx -1.2 bp per 1 percentage point increase in Debt/GDP
    slope = -1.2
    return float(base_convenience_bp) + slope * debt_to_gdp_change


def main() -> None:
    # 3 tenors: 3-month (cash/bill), 5-year, 10-year
    # All yields in percent per year (e.g. 4.25% = 425/100)
    benchmarks = [
        ConvenienceYieldBenchmark(
            tenor="3-Month",
            treasury_yield=Fraction(420, 100),  # 4.20%
            private_benchmark_yield=Fraction(465, 100),  # 4.65% (A1/P1 commercial paper)
            credit_default_spread=Fraction(5, 100),  # 0.05% (5 bp credit risk)
        ),
        ConvenienceYieldBenchmark(
            tenor="5-Year",
            treasury_yield=Fraction(380, 100),  # 3.80%
            private_benchmark_yield=Fraction(445, 100),  # 4.45% (AAA corporate)
            credit_default_spread=Fraction(25, 100),  # 0.25% (25 bp credit risk)
        ),
        ConvenienceYieldBenchmark(
            tenor="10-Year",
            treasury_yield=Fraction(400, 100),  # 4.00%
            private_benchmark_yield=Fraction(485, 100),  # 4.85% (AAA corporate)
            credit_default_spread=Fraction(35, 100),  # 0.35% (35 bp credit risk)
        ),
    ]

    print("=== Safe Asset Convenience Yield Measurement ===")
    print(f"{'Tenor':<8} | {'Treasury':<10} | {'Benchmark':<10} | {'Credit Spread':<14} | {'Convenience Yield':<18}")
    print("-" * 70)

    convenience_bps: list[Fraction] = []
    for b in benchmarks:
        cy_bp = b.compute_convenience_yield_bp()
        convenience_bps.append(cy_bp)
        ty = float(b.treasury_yield)
        py = float(b.private_yield)
        cs = float(b.credit_spread) * 100
        print(f"{b.tenor:<8} | {ty:.2f}%     | {py:.2f}%     | {cs:.0f} bp        | {float(cy_bp):.1f} bp")

    # Supply elasticity analysis on 10-year
    base_10y_cy = convenience_bps[2]
    print("\n=== Supply & Demand Sensitivity (10-Year Treasury) ===")
    print(f"Baseline Convenience Yield: {float(base_10y_cy):.1f} bp")

    # Scenario A: Treasury supply expands by +10% of GDP
    cy_supply_expand = simulate_supply_shock(base_10y_cy, debt_to_gdp_change=+10.0)
    print(f"Treasury Supply Expands (+10% Debt/GDP): Convenience Yield narrows to {cy_supply_expand:.1f} bp")

    # Scenario B: Flight-to-safety safe asset shortage (-10% supply shock / high foreign demand)
    cy_safety_scramble = simulate_supply_shock(base_10y_cy, debt_to_gdp_change=-10.0)
    print(f"Safe Asset Scramble (-10% effective supply): Convenience Yield widens to {cy_safety_scramble:.1f} bp")

    assert all(cy > 0 for cy in convenience_bps), "All convenience yields must be positive"
    assert cy_safety_scramble > float(base_10y_cy) > cy_supply_expand, (
        "Convenience yield must be downward-sloping in supply"
    )


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The safe asset paradigm assumes sovereign Treasuries are perpetually liquid and risk-free. In real market stress:

  • The March 2020 Treasury Market Seizure: In March 2020, massive foreign central bank selling and hedge fund basis-trade liquidations overwhelmed primary dealer balance sheets, causing Treasury yields to spike and bid-ask spreads to blow out (He, Nagel & Song 2022). The Federal Reserve was forced to intervene with trillions in emergency purchases.
  • Fiscal Capacity Limits: If sovereign debt supply expands beyond an economy's fiscal backing capacity, safe assets can suddenly lose their safety premium and transition rapidly into risky debt (the safe asset trap).

Misconceptions

  1. US Treasury yields are lower than corporate bond yields solely due to the absence of credit default risk.

    Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) prove that even after stripping out CDS credit default premia completely, Treasuries trade at a persistent 40 to 75 bp premium over fully state-guaranteed agency bonds, confirming non-pecuniary convenience utility.

  2. A convenience yield is identical to the convenience yield of a physical commodity.

    Commodity convenience yields reflect the physical benefit of having inventory on hand to avoid production stockouts. Safe-asset convenience yields reflect regulatory zero-risk-weight privileges, collateral rehypothecation capacity, and systemic information-insensitivity in repo markets.

  3. The supply of safe assets can be expanded indefinitely without lowering the convenience yield.

    Empirical evidence confirms that the demand curve for safe assets is strictly downward-sloping. Expanding the supply of Treasury debt reduces the marginal liquidity value of safe assets, driving convenience yields toward zero.

Problem Set

Mechanics Tier

A 5-year Treasury note yields 3.85%. An identically matched 5-year AAA corporate bond yields 4.50%. If the market-implied credit default risk spread for the corporate bond is 0.25%, what is the Treasury convenience yield in basis points?

Solution

Convenience yield is \(\mathrm{CY} = 4.50\% - 3.85\% - 0.25\% = 0.40\% = 40\) basis points.

Using Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen's semi-elasticity estimate of \(-1.4\) basis points per 1 percentage point of Debt/GDP, calculate the change in convenience yield if the US debt-to-GDP ratio increases by 15 percentage points.

Solution

Change in convenience yield is \(\Delta \mathrm{CY} = -1.4 \times 15 = -21.0\) basis points (a narrowing of 21 bp).

A government issues 5 trillion dollars in safe debt. If the convenience yield on this debt is 60 basis points, what is the annual interest expense savings for the government in billions of dollars?

Solution

Annual savings is \(5{,}000 \text{ billion} \times 0.0060 = 30\) billion dollars per year.

Proof Tier

Prove that an increase in foreign demand for safe dollar assets causes the real exchange rate of the US dollar to appreciate under the Jiang, Krishnamurthy & Lustig (2021) framework.

Solution

Let foreign investors derive convenience utility \(\gamma_t^\ast\) from holding US safe debt. The modified uncovered interest parity condition is \(i_t - i_t^\ast - \mathrm{CY}_t^\ast = \E_t[\Delta s_{t+1}]\). An exogenous surge in foreign safe asset demand raises \(\mathrm{CY}_t^\ast\), widening the required spread. Because expected future exchange rates are tied to long-run fundamentals, the current spot exchange rate \(s_t\) must immediately appreciate (drop in USD per foreign currency) to generate the required expected future depreciation.

Prove that if two sovereign debt securities have identical cash flows and zero default risk, any persistent yield differential between them violates the law of one price unless one security provides differential collateral convenience.

Solution

Let bonds \(A\) and \(B\) pay identical cash flow \(C\) at maturity \(T\). In a frictionless market with law of one price, absence of arbitrage requires \(P_A = P_B = M_T C\), where \(M_T\) is the unique stochastic discount factor. If \(P_A > P_B\), an investor can short \(A\) and buy \(B\), locking in riskless cash profit \(P_A - P_B > 0\) at \(t=0\) and using \(B\)'s cash flow to extinguish the short at \(T\). Therefore, \(P_A > P_B\) can persist in equilibrium only if shorting \(A\) forfeits non-pecuniary convenience services (such as collateral eligibility in repo contracts) that cannot be replicated with \(B\).

Show that the convenience yield functions as an implicit liquidity subsidy in the government's intertemporal budget constraint.

Solution

The real government budget constraint is \(B_t = (1 + r_t - \mathrm{CY}_t) B_{t-1} + G_t - T_t\). Iterating forward with no-Ponzi condition yields \(B_0 = \sum_{t=1}^\infty \left[ \prod_{s=1}^t (1 + r_s - \mathrm{CY}_s)^{-1} \right] (T_t - G_t)\). Because \(\mathrm{CY}_t > 0\), the effective discount rate is reduced from \(r_s\) to \(r_s - \mathrm{CY}_s\), expanding the present value of fiscal debt capacity that can be sustained by future primary surpluses.

Empirical Tier

How does Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) use the spread between Agency debt (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac) and US Treasuries to isolate convenience utility from default risk?

Solution

Following government conservatorship in 2008, US Agency bonds carried the full faith and credit backing of the US government, eliminating default risk differences relative to Treasuries. Yet Treasuries continued to trade at a 15 to 40 bp lower yield than Agencies, directly isolating the non-pecuniary liquidity and regulatory convenience premium of Treasuries.

Explain how the "exorbitant privilege" of the United States relates directly to the Treasury convenience yield.

Solution

Because foreign central banks and investors eagerly hold US Treasuries for safety and reserves, the US borrows at depressed interest rates (earning the convenience yield discount). Meanwhile, US outward foreign investment is concentrated in higher-yielding foreign equities and direct investment, generating a persistent positive net investment income return for the US.

Describe the "Treasury inconvenience yield" documented by He, Nagel & Song (2022) during the March 2020 COVID-19 financial shock.

Solution

In March 2020, massive forced selling of long-term Treasuries by foreign central banks and relative-value hedge funds overwhelmed primary dealer balance sheets. Dealers faced strict leverage constraints and could not absorb the volume, causing long-term Treasury yields to rise above overnight index swap rates, briefly turning the convenience yield negative (an inconvenience yield).

Research Tier

Evaluate the "safe asset shortage" hypothesis proposed by Caballero, Farhi & Gourinchas (2008) and its relationship to global secular stagnation.

Solution

Emerging market economies grow rapidly and generate immense demand for safe store-of-value assets, but lack the domestic institutional quality to produce safe debt. The global demand for safe assets outpaces the supply of advanced-economy sovereign debt, pushing global equilibrium real interest rates downward toward the zero lower bound.

How does the issuance of central bank digital currencies (CBDC) or tokenized cash impact commercial bank deposits versus Treasury convenience yields?

Solution

A direct central bank digital currency offers a zero-credit-risk digital claim accessible to non-banks. If CBDCs are interest-bearing, they compete directly with Treasury bills and commercial bank deposits for liquidity services, potentially compressing short-term Treasury convenience yields and disintermediating commercial bank funding.

Formulate the optimal sovereign debt maturity structure problem when the convenience yield is downward-sloping and steeper at short maturities.

Solution

Short-term Treasury bills command a significantly higher convenience yield than long-term bonds because money-market funds and non-banks require immediate cash-equivalent collateral. A debt management office trade-off balances issuance of cheap short-term bills (maximizing convenience savings) against refinancing and rollover risk during debt rollover crises.

    Options

    The convenience yield is the non-pecuniary value investors attach to safety, liquidity, and collateral acceptability.

  2. According to Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012), what is the shape of the aggregate demand curve for US Treasury debt?

    Options

    Increasing Treasury supply satisfies liquidity demand, depressing the marginal convenience yield.

  3. If a 10-year Treasury yields 4.15%, an identically matched AAA corporate bond yields 4.90%, and credit default risk is 0.30%, what is the convenience yield in basis points?

    \(\mathrm{CY} = 4.90\% - 4.15\% - 0.30\% = 0.45\% = 45\) basis points.

  4. How does global flight-to-safety demand for US Treasuries affect the US dollar exchange rate according to Jiang et al. (2021)?

    Options

    Foreign demand for dollar safe assets creates upward pressure on the dollar, appreciating the currency during global crises.

  5. If the semi-elasticity of the convenience yield with respect to Debt/GDP is \(-1.2\) bp/pct, by how many basis points does the convenience yield narrow if Debt/GDP expands by 10 percentage points?

    \(\Delta \mathrm{CY} = 1.2 \times 10 = 12\) basis points narrowing.

  6. What extraordinary event occurred in the US Treasury market in March 2020 as documented by He, Nagel & Song (2022)?

    Options

    Massive selling overwhelmed primary dealer balance sheets, causing temporary severe Treasury market liquidity dislocations.

  7. Why do short-term Treasury bills command a higher convenience yield than long-term Treasury bonds?
