Floating exchange rates exhibit massive short-run volatility that far exceeds the volatility of underlying economic fundamentals such as money supplies and GDP. The seminal model of Dornbusch (1976) solved this mystery by combining rational expectations and uncovered interest parity with sticky goods prices. When an unexpected monetary expansion occurs, fast-moving financial asset markets must clear immediately while sluggish goods prices adjust slowly. To re-equilibrate the domestic money market, the spot exchange rate must jump beyond its long-run equilibrium before gradually appreciating back to purchasing power parity.

1The Dynamic Overshooting Model Specification

Definition 1.1 (Dornbusch overshooting model)

Dornbusch overshooting describes the phenomenon where the spot exchange rate undergoes an initial discontinuous jump that exceeds its long-run steady-state change in response to a permanent monetary shock. In discrete time, the model consists of three core building blocks:

  • Money Market Equilibrium: \[ m_t - p_t = \phi \bar{y} - \lambda i_t \] where \(m_t = \ln M_t\), \(p_t = \ln P_t\), \(\bar{y}\) is potential output normalized to 0, and \(\lambda > 0\) is the interest semi-elasticity of money demand.
  • Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP): \[ i_t - i^\ast = \E_t[s_{t+1}] - s_t \] where \(s_t = \ln S_t\) is the log nominal exchange rate (USD per unit foreign currency), and \(i^\ast\) is the foreign interest rate.
  • Sticky Goods Price Adjustment: \[ p_{t+1} - p_t = \theta (y_t^d - \bar{y}) = \theta \gamma (s_t - p_t) \] where \(\theta \in (0, 1)\) is the speed of price adjustment, and aggregate demand is \(y_t^d = \gamma (s_t - p_t)\) driven by the real exchange rate. Normalizing \(\gamma = 1\), the inflation equation is \(p_{t+1} - p_t = \theta (s_t - p_t)\).
Assumption 1.2 (long-run monetary neutrality and perfect foresight)

Assume (A1) long-run money neutrality: a permanent shift in money supply \(\Delta m\) eventually leads to a proportional 1-for-1 increase in goods prices and the exchange rate: \(\bar{p} = \bar{s} = \Delta m\); (A2) along the deterministic adjustment trajectory, market participants possess perfect foresight: \(\E_t[s_{t+1}] = s_{t+1}\); and (A3) goods prices are strictly predetermined at \(t=0\) (\(p_0 = 0\)).

2Saddle-Path Dynamics and the Overshooting Jump

Theorem 2.1 (the overshooting jump theorem)

Under Assumption 1.2, following a permanent unanticipated monetary expansion \(\Delta m > 0\), the spot exchange rate at \(t=0\) jumps to:

\[ s_0 = \bar{s} + \kappa \Delta m = (1 + \kappa) \Delta m > \bar{s} \]

where \(\kappa = \frac{-\theta + \sqrt{\theta^2 + 4\theta/\lambda}}{2\theta} > 0\). The spot exchange rate strictly overshoots its long-run equilibrium \(\bar{s}\). In subsequent periods, the exchange rate monotonically appreciates toward \(\bar{s}\) as domestic goods prices rise monotonically toward \(\bar{p}\).

Proof

In the long-run steady state, \(i = i^\ast\) and \(\bar{p} = \bar{s} = \Delta m\). In the short run (\(t=0\)), price rigidity dictates \(p_0 = 0\). From the money market equation, the domestic interest rate at \(t=0\) drops to:

\[ i_0 - i^\ast = -\frac{1}{\lambda} (\Delta m - p_0) = -\frac{1}{\lambda} \Delta m < 0 \]

Under UIP and perfect foresight, \(s_1 - s_0 = i_0 - i^\ast = -\frac{1}{\lambda} \Delta m < 0\). This requires the domestic currency to be expected to appreciate between period 0 and period 1 (\(s_1 < s_0\)).

Along the stable saddle-path trajectory toward steady state \((\bar{s}, \bar{p})\), the spot rate is related to the price level by the linear manifold \(s_t - \bar{s} = -\kappa (p_t - \bar{p})\). At \(t=0\), substituting \(p_0 = 0\) and \(\bar{p} = \bar{s} = \Delta m\) yields:

\[ s_0 - \Delta m = -\kappa (0 - \Delta m) = \kappa \Delta m \implies s_0 = (1 + \kappa) \Delta m \]

To determine \(\kappa\), substitute the saddle manifold into the price adjustment equation: \(p_{t+1} - \bar{p} = (p_t - \bar{p}) + \theta [(s_t - \bar{s}) - (p_t - \bar{p})] = [1 - \theta(1 + \kappa)](p_t - \bar{p}) = \mu (p_t - \bar{p})\), where \(\mu = 1 - \theta(1 + \kappa)\) is the stable eigenvalue. From UIP: \(s_{t+1} - s_t = -\kappa(\mu - 1)(p_t - \bar{p}) = \kappa \theta(1 + \kappa)(p_t - \bar{p})\). Equating this to \(-\frac{1}{\lambda}(\Delta m - p_t) = \frac{1}{\lambda}(p_t - \bar{p})\) yields the characteristic quadratic equation: \(\theta \kappa^2 + \theta \kappa - \frac{1}{\lambda} = 0\). The unique positive root is \(\kappa = \frac{-\theta + \sqrt{\theta^2 + 4\theta/\lambda}}{2\theta} > 0\). Because \(\kappa > 0\), we have \(s_0 > \bar{s}\), proving that the initial exchange rate jumps strictly above its long-run equilibrium. Monotonicity follows because \(0 < \mu < 1\) under stability. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.2 (the economic intuition for overshooting)

The intuition rests entirely on the asymmetry between asset market and goods market speeds of adjustment. When money supply increases, real balances \(M/P\) swell because prices are sticky. To induce investors to hold the increased money supply, domestic interest rates must fall below world rates. But by UIP, a lower domestic interest rate is sustainable only if investors expect the domestic currency to appreciate in the future. Because the currency must depreciate in the long run to \(\bar{s}\), the only way it can be expected to appreciate is if it first depreciates by MORE than \(\bar{s}\) at the moment of the shock. Thus, exchange rate overshooting is a necessary consequence of financial market clearing when goods markets are sticky.

Lab 1 · Dornbusch Overshooting Dynamic Saddle Path Simulation

Simulates the discrete-time Dornbusch model for a 10% permanent monetary expansion with lambda = 4.0 and theta = 0.25, confirming that the initial spot rate jumps to 0.1618 (overshooting 0.1000 by 61.8%) and tracks monotonic convergence back to 0.1000.

"""Lab 6: Dornbusch Exchange Rate Overshooting Simulation.

Deterministic simulation. Standard library only.
Solves the discrete-time Dornbusch (1976) model with sticky prices and UIP.
Demonstrates that a monetary expansion induces an immediate spot exchange rate jump
that overshoots its long-run equilibrium before appreciating back to purchasing power parity.
"""

import math


class DornbuschModel:
    def __init__(
        self,
        lambda_interest: float,  # Interest semi-elasticity of money demand
        theta_price: float,  # Speed of price adjustment to real exchange rate
    ) -> None:
        self.lam = lambda_interest
        self.theta = theta_price

        # Derive discrete-time stable eigenvalue mu
        # Dynamics in p: p_{t+1} - p_t = theta * (s_t - p_t)
        # UIP: s_{t+1} - s_t = -(1 / lam) * (m - p_t)
        # On saddle path: s_t - s_bar = -kappa * (p_t - p_bar)
        # Substituting: p_{t+1} - p_bar = (1 - theta*(1 + kappa)) * (p_t - p_bar) = mu * (p_t - p_bar)
        # And from UIP: s_{t+1} - s_bar = -kappa * mu * (p_t - p_bar) = s_t - s_bar - (1/lam)*(p_bar - p_t)
        # => -kappa * mu = -kappa + 1/lam => kappa * (1 - mu) = 1/lam
        # Since 1 - mu = theta * (1 + kappa), we get:
        # kappa * theta * (1 + kappa) = 1/lam => theta * kappa^2 + theta * kappa - 1/lam = 0
        # Positive root for kappa:
        a = self.theta
        b = self.theta
        c = -1.0 / self.lam
        self.kappa = (-b + math.sqrt(b**2 - 4.0 * a * c)) / (2.0 * a)
        self.mu = 1.0 - self.theta * (1.0 + self.kappa)

    def simulate(
        self,
        delta_m: float,
        periods: int = 25,
    ) -> list[dict[str, float]]:
        """Simulate dynamic trajectory following a permanent monetary shock delta_m."""
        # Long-run steady state: p_bar = s_bar = delta_m, i_bar = i*
        p_bar = delta_m
        s_bar = delta_m

        # At t=0, price is sticky at p_0 = 0.0
        trajectory: list[dict[str, float]] = []
        p_t = 0.0

        for t in range(periods):
            # Saddle path saddle equation: s_t - s_bar = -kappa * (p_t - p_bar)
            s_t = s_bar - self.kappa * (p_t - p_bar)
            # Interest rate differential from money market: i_t - i* = -(1/lam)*(delta_m - p_t)
            i_diff = -(1.0 / self.lam) * (delta_m - p_t)

            trajectory.append(
                {
                    "period": t,
                    "spot": s_t,
                    "price": p_t,
                    "interest_diff": i_diff,
                    "real_fx": s_t - p_t,
                }
            )

            # Next period sticky price adjustment
            p_next = p_t + self.theta * (s_t - p_t)
            p_t = p_next

        return trajectory


def main() -> None:
    # Model parameters: lambda = 4.0, theta = 0.25
    model = DornbuschModel(lambda_interest=4.0, theta_price=0.25)
    delta_m = 0.10  # 10% permanent monetary expansion

    traj = model.simulate(delta_m=delta_m, periods=20)

    s_0 = traj[0]["spot"]
    s_bar = delta_m
    overshoot_pct = (s_0 - s_bar) / s_bar * 100.0

    print("=== Dornbusch (1976) Overshooting Dynamics ===")
    print(f"Monetary Expansion (Delta m): {delta_m * 100:.1f}%")
    print(f"Long-Run Neutral Exchange Rate (s_bar): {s_bar:.4f}")
    print(f"Initial Jump Spot Rate (s_0):           {s_0:.4f}")
    print(f"Overshooting Premium:                   {(s_0 - s_bar):.4f} ({overshoot_pct:+.1f}%)")
    print(f"Initial Interest Rate Wedge (i_0 - i*): {traj[0]['interest_diff'] * 100:.2f}%\n")

    print("Period |  Spot (s_t) |  Price (p_t) | Interest Spread | Real FX (s - p)")
    print("-----------------------------------------------------------------------")
    for row in traj[:8]:
        p = int(row["period"])
        s_val = row["spot"]
        p_val = row["price"]
        i_spread = row["interest_diff"] * 100
        r_fx = row["real_fx"]
        print(f"  {p:2d}   |   {s_val:.4f}    |    {p_val:.4f}    |     {i_spread:+.2f}%     |    {r_fx:.4f}")
    print(" ...")
    last = traj[-1]
    p_end = int(last["period"])
    s_end = last["spot"]
    p_end_val = last["price"]
    i_end = last["interest_diff"] * 100
    r_end = last["real_fx"]
    print(f"  {p_end:2d}   |   {s_end:.4f}    |    {p_end_val:.4f}    |     {i_end:+.2f}%     |    {r_end:.4f}")

    # Verifications
    assert s_0 > s_bar, f"Initial spot {s_0} must overshoot long-run equilibrium {s_bar}"
    assert traj[0]["interest_diff"] < 0, "Initial domestic interest rate must drop below world rate"
    assert abs(last["spot"] - s_bar) < 0.005, f"Spot must converge to s_bar, got {last['spot']}"
    assert abs(last["price"] - s_bar) < 0.005, f"Price must converge to s_bar, got {last['price']}"

Where the Canon Breaks

While the Dornbusch model provides the definitive explanation for exchange rate volatility, its empirical performance exhibits notable limitations:

  • Exchange Rate Disconnect Puzzle: Meese & Rogoff (1983) demonstrated that structural macroeconomic models—including the Dornbusch monetary model—fail to outperform a naive random walk forecast at short to medium horizons (1 to 12 months).
  • Half-Life of PPP Deviations: In the Dornbusch model, real exchange rate deviations dissipate relatively quickly as domestic prices adjust. In empirical data, real exchange rate deviations have an estimated half-life of 3 to 5 years, far longer than plausible goods-price stickiness alone can justify.

Misconceptions

  1. Exchange rate overshooting requires irrational speculation or herd behavior in currency markets.

    Dornbusch overshooting is derived under strict rational expectations and frictionless financial arbitrage. The overshooting jump is the optimal, equilibrium response of rational asset markets to sticky goods prices.

  2. A permanent monetary expansion immediately causes instantaneous domestic inflation.

    Goods prices are constrained by nominal rigidities (menu costs, multi-period wage contracts) and do not jump. Only asset prices (exchange rates, bond yields) jump instantaneously upon the arrival of news.

  3. The nominal exchange rate remains permanently at its overshot peak.

    The overshooting peak is strictly transient. As goods prices gradually adjust over time, real money balances contract, domestic interest rates rise back to world rates, and the exchange rate appreciates back to its long-run purchasing power parity level.

Problem Set

Mechanics Tier

In a Dornbusch model with \(\lambda = 2.0\) and \(\theta = 0.50\), compute the overshooting parameter \(\kappa = \frac{-\theta + \sqrt{\theta^2 + 4\theta/\lambda}}{2\theta}\) and the initial spot rate jump \(s_0\) for an 8% monetary expansion (\(\Delta m = 0.08\)).

Solution

We compute: \(\theta^2 + 4\theta/\lambda = 0.5^2 + 4(0.5)/2.0 = 0.25 + 1.0 = 1.25\). Then \(\sqrt{1.25} \approx 1.11803\). So \(\kappa = (-0.5 + 1.11803) / (2 \times 0.5) = 0.61803\). The initial spot rate jump is \(s_0 = (1 + \kappa) \Delta m = (1 + 0.61803) \times 0.08 = 1.61803 \times 0.08 = 0.1294\) (a 12.94% initial depreciation, overshooting 8.0% by 4.94%).

If the long-run neutral exchange rate is \(\bar{s} = 0.15\) and the initial spot jump is \(s_0 = 0.22\), what is the overshooting premium in percent of the long-run change?

Solution

The overshooting magnitude is \(s_0 - \bar{s} = 0.22 - 0.15 = 0.07\). Expressed as a percentage of the long-run equilibrium change: \((0.07 / 0.15) \times 100\% = 46.67\%\).

If \(\lambda = 5.0\) and prices are predetermined at \(p_0 = 0\), what is the initial drop in the domestic interest rate \(i_0 - i^\ast\) following a 5% monetary expansion (\(\Delta m = 0.05\))?

Solution

From the money market equation: \(i_0 - i^\ast = -(1/\lambda) \Delta m = -(1/5.0) \times 0.05 = -0.010\) (-1.00%, or -100 basis points).

Proof Tier

Prove that as price adjustment becomes instantaneous (\(\theta \to \infty\)), the overshooting parameter \(\kappa\) approaches 0, and the exchange rate jumps directly to \(\bar{s}\) without overshooting.

Solution

We analyze \(\kappa = \frac{-\theta + \sqrt{\theta^2 + 4\theta/\lambda}}{2\theta}\). Factoring \(\theta\) gives \(\kappa = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4/(\lambda \theta)}}{2}\). As \(\theta \to \infty\), \(4/(\lambda \theta) \to 0\). By Taylor expansion, \(\sqrt{1 + x} \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}x\). Substituting yields \(\kappa \approx \frac{-1 + (1 + 2/(\lambda \theta))}{2} = \frac{1}{\lambda \theta} \to 0\). Therefore, \(s_0 = (1 + \kappa)\Delta m \to \Delta m = \bar{s}\). Instantaneous price flexibility eliminates exchange rate overshooting completely.

Prove that the real exchange rate \(q_t = s_t - p_t\) converges monotonically to zero along the stable saddle path.

Solution

Along the saddle path, \(s_t - \bar{s} = -\kappa(p_t - \bar{p})\). Since \(\bar{s} = \bar{p}\), we have \(q_t = s_t - p_t = (s_t - \bar{s}) - (p_t - \bar{p}) = -(1 + \kappa)(p_t - \bar{p})\). In the previous theorem proof, prices evolve according to \(p_t - \bar{p} = \mu^t (p_0 - \bar{p})\), where \(\mu = 1 - \theta(1 + \kappa) \in (0, 1)\). Substituting gives \(q_t = -(1 + \kappa)\mu^t (p_0 - \bar{p}) = (1 + \kappa)\mu^t \Delta m\). Because \(0 < \mu < 1\), \(q_t > 0\) decays monotonically to 0 as \(t \to \infty\).

Prove that a higher interest semi-elasticity of money demand \(\lambda\) dampens the magnitude of overshooting \(\kappa\).

Solution

From \(\kappa = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4/(\lambda \theta)}}{2}\), differentiate with respect to \(\lambda\): \(\frac{\partial \kappa}{\partial \lambda} = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{1 + 4/(\lambda \theta)}} \cdot \left( -\frac{4}{\lambda^2 \theta} \right) = -\frac{1}{\lambda^2 \theta \sqrt{1 + 4/(\lambda \theta)}} < 0\). A higher \(\lambda\) means real money balances can be absorbed with a smaller drop in interest rates, requiring a smaller expected appreciation under UIP, and therefore a smaller initial overshooting jump.

Empirical Tier

Explain the Meese and Rogoff (1983) empirical puzzle and its implications for structural exchange rate models.

Solution

Meese & Rogoff (1983) evaluated out-of-sample forecasting performance across major exchange rate models, including the Dornbusch sticky-price model and flexible-price monetary models. They showed that at horizons up to 12 months, no economic model could outperform a naive driftless random walk in root mean squared forecast error, establishing the famous "exchange rate disconnect puzzle."

Why does empirical evidence find that exchange rates jump following unexpected central bank interest rate announcements?

Solution

High-frequency event-study regressions show that unexpected policy rate hikes trigger instantaneous currency appreciations within minutes of announcement. Because financial markets are forward-looking and adjust instantaneously while product prices remain rigid, the high-frequency response exhibits the sharp initial asset-market jump predicted by Dornbusch.

How does the concept of the "half-life of PPP deviations" relate to the speed of price adjustment \(\theta\) in the Dornbusch model?

Solution

The half-life \(t_{1/2}\) is the time required for real exchange rate deviations to decay by 50%: \(\mu^{t_{1/2}} = 0.5 \implies t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 0.5}{\ln \mu}\). In the model, with \(\theta = 0.25\) per quarter, the half-life is approximately 1 to 2 years. In empirical data, the estimated half-life is 3 to 5 years, suggesting that real frictions beyond nominal price stickiness prolong currency misalignments.

Research Tier

How does the continuous-time formulation of the Dornbusch model using linear differential equations compare with the discrete-time formulation?

Solution

In continuous time, the system is \(\dot{p} = \theta(s - p)\) and \(\dot{s} = i - i^\ast = -\frac{1}{\lambda}(m - p)\). In matrix form, \(\begin{pmatrix} \dot{p} \\ \dot{s} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\theta & \theta \\ 1/\lambda & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} p - \bar{p} \\ s - \bar{s} \end{pmatrix}\). The determinant of the Jacobian is \(-\theta/\lambda < 0\), proving the existence of one positive (unstable) and one negative (stable) eigenvalue. The saddle-path property ensures a unique jump of the non-predetermined variable \(s(0)\) that places the system onto the stable manifold.

Evaluate the effect of anticipated future monetary expansions on current exchange rate dynamics in the Dornbusch framework.

Solution

If a monetary expansion is announced at \(t=0\) to occur at future date \(T > 0\), the exchange rate jumps immediately at \(t=0\), even though money supply has not yet changed. Between \(t=0\) and \(t=T\), the exchange rate depreciates continuously while domestic interest rates rise slightly. When the shock realizes at \(t=T\), the exchange rate does not jump further, demonstrating that forward-looking asset markets price future policy shifts immediately.

How does imperfect exchange rate passthrough (due to local currency pricing) modify the Dornbusch overshooting magnitude?

Solution

Under local currency pricing (LCP), foreign firms preset import prices in domestic currency, severely dampening the expenditure-switching effect of exchange rate movements (\(\gamma \approx 0\)). As a consequence, aggregate demand responds sluggishly to currency depreciation, requiring even larger exchange rate fluctuations and amplifying the overshooting jump to clear asset markets.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What structural friction causes the spot exchange rate to overshoot its long-run equilibrium in the Dornbusch model?

    Options

    Asset markets must clear immediately via exchange rate jumps because goods prices adjust slowly over time.

  2. Immediately following an unanticipated permanent monetary expansion, what happens to the domestic nominal interest rate?

    Options

    Because prices are sticky, real money supply expands, lowering domestic interest rates below foreign rates to clear the money market.

  3. Following a 10% permanent monetary expansion (\(\Delta m = 0.10\)), what is the long-run steady-state change in the nominal exchange rate \(\bar{s}\)?

    By long-run monetary neutrality, \(\bar{s} = \Delta m = 0.10\).

  4. Why must the spot exchange rate be expected to appreciate along the convergence path following a monetary expansion?

    Options

    By UIP, \(i - i^\ast = \E[\Delta s]\). Since \(i < i^\ast\), expected depreciation must be negative (expected appreciation).

  5. If \(\kappa = 0.50\) and \(\Delta m = 0.10\), what is the initial spot exchange rate jump \(s_0\)?

    \(s_0 = (1 + \kappa)\Delta m = 1.50 \times 0.10 = 0.15\).

  6. What empirical result was demonstrated by Meese & Rogoff (1983)?

    Options

    Meese and Rogoff showed that a random walk outperforms structural models out-of-sample at 1 to 12 month horizons.

  7. As the speed of price adjustment \(\theta\) approaches infinity (perfect price flexibility), what happens to exchange rate overshooting?

    Options

    With fully flexible prices, prices jump immediately to clear the money market, keeping interest rates equal to world rates and eliminating overshooting.

  8. In the long-run steady state of the Dornbusch model, what is the value of the real exchange rate misalignment \(s - p\)?

    In the long run, purchasing power parity holds, so \(s - p = 0.0\).
