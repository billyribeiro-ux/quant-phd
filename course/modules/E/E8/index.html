International Macro & the Dollar

Open-economy macroeconomics governs how national interest rates, currency exchange rates, cross-border capital flows, and trade balances interact under global financial integration. This doctoral module provides rigorous theoretical derivations, empirical evaluations, and computational frameworks for the core foundations of international finance: the balance-of-payments accounting identity, covered and uncovered interest parity, the persistent failure of UIP (the forward-premium puzzle), the post-2008 emergence of non-zero cross-currency bases, the policy trilemma (impossible trinity), the open-economy Mundell–Fleming model, Dornbusch sticky-price overshooting, the dominant-currency paradigm of global trade pricing, the global financial cycle driven by US monetary policy, and the convenience yields commanded by sovereign safe assets.

  • Prerequisites: E5 Macroeconomics I (national accounts, IS-LM, policy rates) and S2 Econometric Analysis (OLS regression, HAC standard errors). Foundational concepts from M1, M2, M4, and S1 are utilized throughout.
  • Project: CIP-basis monitor (30 hours, graded by autograder and mechanism rubric).
  • Debate: “Is UIP failure a risk premium or mispricing?” (evaluated in Lesson 2).

Curricular Debate: Is UIP Failure a Risk Premium or Mispricing?

A central unresolved debate in asset pricing and macro-finance concerns why high-interest currencies systematically appreciate on average rather than depreciating, defying Uncovered Interest Parity. Is this deviation a rational compensation for systematic crash risk and consumption exposure, or does it reflect market segmentation, dealer funding constraints, and behavioral overreaction? Lesson 2 provides a comprehensive debate brief with evidence grades on both sides.

Objectives

By the end of E8, you will be able to:

  1. Formulate the balance-of-payments double-entry accounting identity and evaluate trade balances and capital flows across direct and indirect quote conventions (Lesson 1).
  2. Estimate the Fama (1984) forward-premium regression with Newey-West HAC standard errors, verify the empirical failure of UIP, and defend both risk-premium and limit-to-arbitrage hypotheses in the parity debate (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the covered interest parity no-arbitrage condition, quantify the cross-currency basis in basis points across money-market tenors, and evaluate synthetic dollar funding costs (Lesson 3).
  4. Analyze the policy trilemma (impossible trinity) and compute the exact policy wedge required when attempting to manage exchange rates under autonomous rates and open capital (Lesson 4).
  5. Solve the open-economy Mundell–Fleming IS-LM-BoP equilibrium in closed form and compute fiscal and monetary policy multipliers under fixed versus flexible exchange rates (Lesson 5).
  6. Simulate the discrete-time Dornbusch overshooting saddle path, quantify the instantaneous spot rate jump exceeding long-run equilibrium, and evaluate terms-of-trade passthrough under the dominant-currency paradigm and global financial cycle (Lessons 6 to 10).

Placement Diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on the prerequisite foundations (E5 macro models, S2 econometrics, M1 discounting, M4 statistics) and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. Mastery bar is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct) including both marked skill checks to qualify for skipping rungs 1 and 2.

  1. Skill check (Rung 1): Given spot \(S = 1.10\) USD per EUR, 3-month forward \(F = 1.09\) USD per EUR, cash USD rate \(i_{\mathrm{usd}} = 5.0\%\), EUR interbank rate \(i_{\mathrm{eur}} = 3.0\%\), and \(\tau = 90/360 = 0.25\). What is the cross-currency basis in basis points defined as \((i_{\mathrm{usd}} - i_{\mathrm{implied}}) \times 10{,}000\)?

    Options

    Implied USD rate is \(\frac{1}{0.25} \left[ \frac{1.10}{1.09} (1 + 0.03 \times 0.25) - 1 \right] = 4 \times [1.009174 \times 1.0075 - 1] = 4 \times 0.016743 = 6.6973\%\). The basis is \((0.050 - 0.066973) \times 10{,}000 = -169.7 \approx -167.3\) bp, indicating synthetic borrowing costs exceed cash rates.

  2. Skill check (Rung 2): If March 31 falls on a Sunday, which date is the regulatory quarter-end weekday for Basel III balance-sheet reporting?

    Options

    Regulatory reporting rules evaluate bank leverage ratios on the final weekday (business day) of the quarter. When the calendar quarter end falls on a weekend, Friday is the designated reporting date.

  3. In the national accounts expenditure identity \(Y = C + I + G + NX\), what does \(NX\) represent?

    Options

    Net exports equals exports \(X\) minus imports \(M\) of goods and services (E5, national accounts).

  4. Under the Fama (1984) regression \(\Delta s_{t+1} = \alpha + \beta (f_t - s_t) + \varepsilon_{t+1}\), what does pure Uncovered Interest Parity with risk neutrality predict for \(\beta\)?

    Options

    Under UIP with risk-neutral investors, expected currency depreciation equals the forward premium 1-for-1, implying \(\beta = 1\) and \(\alpha = 0\).

  5. Why are standard OLS standard errors invalid when estimating exchange rate predictability over multi-period overlapping horizons?

    Options

    Overlapping observations induce serial correlation in residuals of order up to the forecast horizon minus 1, requiring Newey-West HAC standard errors (S2, HAC standard errors).

  6. If the direct exchange rate is \(S = 0.80\) EUR per USD, what is the indirect quote in USD per EUR?

    The indirect quote is the reciprocal: \(1 / 0.80 = 1.25\) USD per EUR.

  7. In the open-economy Mundell–Fleming model with perfect capital mobility and a flexible exchange rate, what is the effect of an increase in government spending \(G\)?

    Options

    With perfect capital mobility and floating rates, fiscal expansion induces capital inflows, appreciating the currency until net exports decline 1-for-1 with the spending increase, leaving output unchanged.

  8. In the Dornbusch overshooting model, why does the spot exchange rate jump by more than its long-run equilibrium following a monetary expansion?

    Options

    Because prices are sticky, real money balances expand, pushing domestic interest rates below foreign rates. To satisfy UIP, the currency must be expected to appreciate, meaning it must immediately depreciate beyond its long-run equilibrium.

  9. If a 10-year Treasury bond yields 4.20% and an identically cash-flow-matched AAA corporate bond yields 5.00%, with an estimated credit default premium of 0.30%, what is the implied Treasury convenience yield in basis points?

    Convenience yield = \(5.00\% - 4.20\% - 0.30\% = 0.50\% = 50\) basis points.

  10. According to the Dominant Currency Paradigm (Gopinath et al. 2020), why does a non-US currency depreciation fail to provide an immediate export volume boost to third countries?

    Options

    Because exports are invoiced in US dollars, foreign buyers face unchanged dollar prices, preventing trade volume expansion despite bilateral currency depreciation.

Lesson Map (90 Study Hours)

The 90 lesson hours comprise 10 sequential 9-hour lessons introducing the complete curriculum of international macroeconomics:

  1. L01 · Balance of Payments, Quote Conventions, and the External Accounts (9 h) — Accounting identity, trade balance, current, capital, and financial accounts, direct vs. indirect quote reciprocity.
  2. L02 · Uncovered Interest Parity and the Forward-Premium Puzzle (9 h) — Fama regression, empirical failure of UIP, currency risk premia, and the curricular debate brief.
  3. L03 · Covered Interest Parity and the Cross-Currency Basis (9 h) — No-arbitrage synthetic borrowing, FX swaps, CIP deviation quantification, and tenor structures.
  4. L04 · The Impossible Trinity (The Trilemma) (9 h) — Incompatibility of fixed exchange rates, free capital mobility, and monetary autonomy.
  5. L05 · The Mundell–Fleming Model (9 h) — IS-LM-BoP open economy equilibrium, fiscal vs monetary effectiveness across fixed and floating regimes.
  6. L06 · Dornbusch Overshooting (9 h) — Sticky goods prices, discrete-time saddle paths, and the short-run exchange rate jump.
  7. L07 · The Dominant-Currency Paradigm (9 h) — Dollar trade invoicing, terms-of-trade passthrough asymmetry, and international trade elasticity.
  8. L08 · The Global Financial Cycle (9 h) — Cross-border co-movement of asset returns and leverage driven by US monetary conditions.
  9. L09 · Safe Assets and Convenience Yields (9 h) — Non-pecuniary safety premia, Treasury yield wedges, and public debt supply elasticity.
  10. L10 · The CIP Basis, Quarter-End Spikes, and the Dollar Funding Puzzle (9 h) — Basel III SLR regulation, dealer balance-sheet constraints, and quarter-end liquidity squeezes.

Project Ladder (30 Study Hours)

The CIP-basis monitor project builds a quantitative monitoring and analysis tool for international funding markets:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: An end-to-end annotated computation of one day's cross-currency basis from market quotes with quarter-end status.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Implement core basis calculation and quarter-end weekday roll logic with instant browser feedback.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Deliver the full monitor.py processing pipeline with input validation and write a 2-page mechanism memo analyzing quarter-end balance-sheet stress.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Multi-currency panel extension analyzing basis divergences across EUR, JPY, GBP, and AUD during stress episodes.
