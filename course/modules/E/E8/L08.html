Modern global capital markets are bound together by powerful synchronous waves of cross-border credit expansion, risk asset inflation, and banking leverage. Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020) establish that a single global factor—the Global Financial Cycle—accounts for a dominant share of variation in international risky asset prices and capital flows across all major continents. Crucially, this cycle is driven primarily by US monetary policy decisions. When the Federal Reserve tightens, global dollar liquidity contracts, bank leverage shrinks worldwide, and risk premia spike across emerging and developed markets alike.

1Factor Representation of the Global Financial Cycle

Definition 1.1 (global financial cycle)

The global financial cycle (GFC) is the empirical phenomenon whereby international asset prices, cross-border gross capital flows, and global banking leverage exhibit strong, synchronized co-movement driven by a single common latent global factor. In dynamic factor notation, let \(x_{i,t}\) denote the standardized asset return, capital flow, or credit growth for market \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) at time \(t \in \{1, \dots, T\}\):

\[ x_{i,t} = \lambda_i f_t + e_{i,t} \]

where \(f_t\) is the unobserved scalar global financial cycle factor, \(\lambda_i\) is the factor loading of country or market \(i\), and \(e_{i,t}\) is an idiosyncratic, cross-sectionally uncorrelated residual component.

Assumption 1.2 (orthogonality and factor normalization)

Assume (A1) \(\E[f_t] = 0\) and \(\Var(f_t) = 1\); (A2) \(\E[e_{i,t}] = 0\) with \(\Cov(f_t, e_{i,t}) = 0\) for all \(i\); (A3) idiosyncratic errors are cross-sectionally orthogonal: \(\Cov(e_{i,t}, e_{j,t}) = 0\) for \(i \neq j\); and (A4) the panel dimension \(N\) and time dimension \(T\) are sufficiently large for consistent factor extraction.

Theorem 1.3 (variance decomposition of the global factor)

Under Assumption 1.2, the total variance of the \(N\)-dimensional system decomposes into common and idiosyncratic components:

\[ \mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma) = \sum_{i=1}^N \Var(x_{i,t}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^N \Var(e_{i,t}) \]

The population variance share explained by the global financial factor \(f_t\) is:

\[ \mathcal{V}_f = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i^2} \]

where \(\sigma_i^2 = \Var(e_{i,t})\). As the loadings become large relative to idiosyncratic noise, \(\mathcal{V}_f \to 1\).

Proof

The \(N \times N\) covariance matrix of \(\mathbf{x}_t\) is \(\Sigma = \E[\mathbf{x}_t \mathbf{x}_t^\top]\). Substituting \(\mathbf{x}_t = \boldsymbol{\lambda} f_t + \mathbf{e}_t\) yields \(\Sigma = \E[(\boldsymbol{\lambda} f_t + \mathbf{e}_t)(\boldsymbol{\lambda} f_t + \mathbf{e}_t)^\top] = \boldsymbol{\lambda} \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \E[f_t^2] + \boldsymbol{\lambda} \E[f_t \mathbf{e}_t^\top] + \E[\mathbf{e}_t f_t] \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top + \E[\mathbf{e}_t \mathbf{e}_t^\top]\). Under Assumption 1.2, \(\E[f_t^2] = 1\), \(\E[f_t \mathbf{e}_t^\top] = \mathbf{0}\), and \(\E[\mathbf{e}_t \mathbf{e}_t^\top] = \Psi = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_N^2)\). Therefore, \(\Sigma = \boldsymbol{\lambda} \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top + \Psi\). Taking the matrix trace yields \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma) = \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\lambda} \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top) + \mathrm{Tr}(\Psi) = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda} + \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i^2\). The fraction of variance attributable to the common factor is the trace of the common component divided by the total trace, yielding \(\mathcal{V}_f\). \(\blacksquare\)

2Transmission of US Monetary Policy Spillovers

Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020) show that the global financial cycle is causally driven by US monetary policy shocks identified using high-frequency federal funds futures surprises. The transmission operates through international banking and risk-taking channels:

  • Global Bank Leverage Channel: Global banks fund cross-border lending heavily in US dollars. When the Federal Reserve raises interest rates, funding costs rise, asset volatility increases (VIX spikes), and Value-at-Risk (VaR) constraints bind. Banks procyclically contract balance sheets globally.
  • Cross-Currency Basis Widening: As dollar liquidity tightens globally, synthetic dollar borrowing spreads widen, driving the cross-currency basis more negative and elevating credit spreads worldwide (Du et al. 2018).

Lab 1 · Global Financial Cycle Common Factor Decomposition

Simulates a 5-economy panel of international asset returns driven by a latent global financial cycle factor, extracts the dominant eigenvector via power iteration, and computes the variance share explained by the first principal component, demonstrating that a single factor explains >70% of total variation.

"""Lab 8: Global Financial Cycle (GFC) Common Factor Decomposition.

SIMULATED panel. Standard library only.
Models cross-border capital flows and asset returns across 5 economies (Miranda-Agrippino & Rey 2020).
Extracts the global factor using power iteration and computes the variance share of the first principal component.
"""

import math
import random

SEED = 101
N_COUNTRIES = 5
T_PERIODS = 150


def simulate_gfc_panel(seed: int = SEED) -> list[list[float]]:
    """Simulate asset returns across 5 economies driven by a common global financial cycle factor."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)

    # Factor loadings (sensitivities to global financial conditions)
    loadings = [0.85, 0.92, 0.78, 0.65, 0.80]

    # Latent global factor AR(1) process (driven by US monetary conditions & global risk aversion)
    f = 0.0
    panel: list[list[float]] = [[] for _ in range(N_COUNTRIES)]

    for _ in range(T_PERIODS):
        # Global shock
        u_f = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        f = 0.6 * f + u_f

        for i in range(N_COUNTRIES):
            # Idiosyncratic national noise
            e_i = rng.gauss(0.0, 0.5)
            x_it = loadings[i] * f + e_i
            panel[i].append(x_it)

    return panel


def power_iteration(cov_matrix: list[list[float]], num_simulations: int = 50) -> tuple[float, list[float]]:
    """Extract top eigenvalue and eigenvector from covariance matrix using power iteration."""
    n = len(cov_matrix)
    # Start with unit vector
    b_k = [1.0 / math.sqrt(n)] * n

    for _ in range(num_simulations):
        # Matrix-vector multiply
        b_k1 = [sum(cov_matrix[i][j] * b_k[j] for j in range(n)) for i in range(n)]
        # Norm
        norm = math.sqrt(sum(x**2 for x in b_k1))
        b_k = [x / norm for x in b_k1]

    # Rayleigh quotient for eigenvalue
    ab_k = [sum(cov_matrix[i][j] * b_k[j] for j in range(n)) for i in range(n)]
    eigenval = sum(b_k[i] * ab_k[i] for i in range(n))
    return eigenval, b_k


def analyze_global_factor(panel: list[list[float]]) -> tuple[float, float, float, list[float]]:
    """Compute sample covariance matrix, trace, top eigenvalue, and variance share."""
    n = len(panel)
    t = len(panel[0])

    # De-mean each series
    means = [sum(panel[i]) / t for i in range(n)]
    demeaned = [[panel[i][time_idx] - means[i] for time_idx in range(t)] for i in range(n)]

    # Compute covariance matrix
    cov = [[0.0] * n for _ in range(n)]
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            cov[i][j] = sum(demeaned[i][k] * demeaned[j][k] for k in range(t)) / (t - 1)

    # Trace of covariance matrix = total variance
    total_variance = sum(cov[i][i] for i in range(n))

    # Top eigenvalue
    top_eigenval, top_vector = power_iteration(cov)
    variance_share = top_eigenval / total_variance

    return top_eigenval, total_variance, variance_share, top_vector


def main() -> None:
    panel = simulate_gfc_panel(SEED)
    top_eval, tot_var, var_share, top_vec = analyze_global_factor(panel)

    print("=== Global Financial Cycle Factor Analysis (SIMULATED) ===")
    print(f"Sample: {N_COUNTRIES} economies, {T_PERIODS} monthly periods")
    print(f"Total System Variance (Trace of Cov): {tot_var:.4f}")
    print(f"Top Eigenvalue (First Principal Component): {top_eval:.4f}")
    print(f"Variance Explained by Global Factor:        {var_share * 100:.2f}%\n")

    print("Global Factor Loadings (Normalized Eigenvector):")
    for idx, weight in enumerate(top_vec):
        print(f"  Country {idx + 1}: {weight:+.4f}")

    print("\nInterpretation (Miranda-Agrippino & Rey 2020):")
    print(f"A single common global factor explains {var_share * 100:.1f}% of cross-border financial variation.")
    print("National capital flows and risky asset returns are heavily synchronized across borders.")

    assert var_share > 0.65, f"Global factor variance share {var_share} should exceed 65%"
    assert all(w > 0 for w in top_vec) or all(w < 0 for w in top_vec), "Loadings must have uniform sign"

Where the Canon Breaks

The existence of a powerful Global Financial Cycle challenges traditional macroeconomic models that assume flexible exchange rates insulate domestic economies:

  • The Dilemma Hypothesis: Under strong GFC spillovers, independent monetary policy is circumscribed even for countries with floating exchange rates. Capital flows and domestic credit conditions fluctuate with the Federal Reserve's policy stance rather than local macroeconomic needs.
  • Asymmetric Hegemony: The transmission is strictly asymmetric: US monetary policy spills over aggressively into foreign asset prices and banking leverage, whereas foreign monetary policy has negligible reciprocal impact on US financial conditions.

Misconceptions

  1. A country with zero trade exposure to the United States is insulated from US monetary policy shocks.

    Transmission occurs through the global financial cycle (international dollar debt, global bank leverage, risk appetites, and sovereign credit spreads), transmitting US monetary shocks to economies with minimal direct bilateral goods trade with the US.

  2. The Global Financial Cycle means all national stock markets experience identical returns.

    The GFC explains a dominant share (typically 20% to 40% of individual equity returns and >60% of international banking flows), but idiosyncratic national shocks, local industry compositions, and domestic political developments still generate meaningful cross-sectional return dispersion.

  3. The global financial cycle is driven solely by commodity price swings.

    Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020) prove that the cycle persists strongly across service-oriented economies, non-commodity assets, and sovereign credit spreads, and is causally linked to US monetary stance and global risk premia rather than physical commodity supply shocks.

Problem Set

Mechanics Tier

In a 4-asset panel, the factor loadings on the common GFC factor are \(\lambda = [0.8, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6]\), and each asset has an idiosyncratic variance of \(\sigma_i^2 = 0.25\). Compute the total variance of the system and the variance share explained by the global factor.

Solution

Sum of squared loadings: \(\sum \lambda_i^2 = 0.8^2 + 0.9^2 + 0.7^2 + 0.6^2 = 0.64 + 0.81 + 0.49 + 0.36 = 2.30\). Total idiosyncratic variance: \(4 \times 0.25 = 1.00\). Total variance: \(2.30 + 1.00 = 3.30\). Variance share: \(\mathcal{V}_f = 2.30 / 3.30 = 0.6970\) (69.70%).

If the top eigenvalue of a \(5 \times 5\) sample correlation matrix of international asset returns is \(\lambda_1 = 3.20\), what percentage of total system variation does the first principal component explain?

Solution

For a correlation matrix, the trace equals the dimension: \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma) = 5.0\). The percentage explained by the first principal component is \((\lambda_1 / 5.0) \times 100\% = (3.20 / 5.0) \times 100\% = 64.0\%\).

Suppose a US monetary policy surprise of +25 basis points causes the global factor \(f_t\) to contract by 0.5 standard deviations. If Country A's loading is \(\lambda_A = 0.80\), what is the predicted impact on Country A's asset return?

Solution

The asset return changes by \(\Delta x_{A} = \lambda_A \Delta f = 0.80 \times (-0.5) = -0.40\) standard deviations.

Proof Tier

Prove that the sample covariance between any two distinct assets \(i \neq j\) in the one-factor GFC model equals the product of their factor loadings.

Solution

From \(x_{i,t} = \lambda_i f_t + e_{i,t}\) and \(x_{j,t} = \lambda_j f_t + e_{j,t}\), the covariance is \(\Cov(x_{i,t}, x_{j,t}) = \E[(\lambda_i f_t + e_{i,t})(\lambda_j f_t + e_{j,t})]\). Expanding yields \(\lambda_i \lambda_j \E[f_t^2] + \lambda_i \E[f_t e_{j,t}] + \lambda_j \E[e_{i,t} f_t] + \E[e_{i,t} e_{j,t}]\). By Assumption 1.2, \(\E[f_t^2] = 1\), \(\E[f_t e_j] = \E[f_t e_i] = 0\), and idiosyncratic errors are uncorrelated (\(\E[e_i e_j] = 0\)). Thus \(\Cov(x_{i,t}, x_{j,t}) = \lambda_i \lambda_j\).

Prove that as the number of cross-sectional assets \(N \to \infty\), the sample average of cross-sectional asset returns converges in probability to a constant multiple of the global factor \(f_t\).

Solution

The cross-sectional average is \(\bar{x}_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_{i,t} = \left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i\right) f_t + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N e_{i,t}\). Let \(\bar{\lambda} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i\). For the noise term, \(\Var\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N e_{i,t}\right) = \frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i^2 \le \frac{\sigma_{\max}^2}{N} \to 0\) as \(N \to \infty\). By Chebyshev's inequality, \(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N e_{i,t} \xrightarrow{p} 0\), so \(\bar{x}_t \xrightarrow{p} \bar{\lambda} f_t\), recovering the latent global factor up to scale.

Show that the power iteration algorithm \(b_{k+1} = \Sigma b_k / \|\Sigma b_k\|\) converges to the dominant eigenvector of \(\Sigma\).

Solution

Let \(\Sigma\) have eigenvalues \(\mu_1 > \mu_2 \ge \dots \ge \mu_N \ge 0\) with orthonormal eigenvectors \(v_1, \dots, v_N\). Any initial vector can be written \(b_0 = c_1 v_1 + \sum_{j=2}^N c_j v_j\). Applying \(\Sigma^k\) yields \(\Sigma^k b_0 = c_1 \mu_1^k v_1 + \sum_{j=2}^N c_j \mu_j^k v_j = \mu_1^k \left( c_1 v_1 + \sum_{j=2}^N c_j (\mu_j / \mu_1)^k v_j \right)\). Because \(\mu_1 > \mu_j\), the ratios \((\mu_j / \mu_1)^k \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty\). Normalizing at each step ensures \(b_k \to v_1\), isolating the dominant principal component.

Empirical Tier

How does the Chicago Board Options Exchange Volatility Index (VIX) relate empirically to the Global Financial Cycle?

Solution

Rey (2013) and Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020) show that the VIX is strongly negatively correlated with the global factor (\(\rho \approx -0.7\)). A spike in the VIX reflects global risk aversion, immediately precipitating global bank balance-sheet deleveraging, capital flight from emerging markets, and sharp drops in international risky asset prices.

Why are gross cross-border capital flows (inflows plus outflows) far more volatile than net capital flows during GFC cycles?

Solution

Gross flows reflect multi-trillion dollar international banking asset and liability adjustments. During GFC downturns, global banks simultaneously retrench domestic lending to foreigners (outflow collapse) while foreign residents repatriate offshore capital (inflow collapse). These massive gross movements partially offset in net flow numbers, masking the true magnitude of global financial stress.

Describe the empirical evidence on the impact of US Federal Reserve quantitative easing (QE) on emerging market capital flows.

Solution

Federal Reserve QE lowered long-term US bond yields and compressed global risk premia, boosting the GFC factor. Global investors engaged in search-for-yield, channeling hundreds of billions of dollars into emerging market sovereign and corporate bond markets, until the 2013 "Taper Tantrum" reversed the flows violently.

Research Tier

How does Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020) isolate exogenous US monetary policy shocks using high-frequency financial market surprises?

Solution

They measure the price change of Federal Funds futures and Eurodollar futures within a tight 30-minute window around Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) announcements. Because macroeconomic fundamentals do not change within a 30-minute window, the price revision isolates the unexpected monetary policy surprise. They purge these surprises of central bank information effects using Greenbook forecasts, isolating clean exogenous policy shocks.

Formulate the global bank Value-at-Risk (VaR) constraint and show how dollar appreciation forces global deleveraging.

Solution

Let a global bank hold loans to foreign firms with dollar equity \(E\). The VaR constraint is \(\alpha \sigma \cdot \mathrm{Assets} \le E \implies \mathrm{Assets} / E \le \frac{1}{\alpha \sigma}\). When the US dollar appreciates, foreign borrowers face higher debt servicing burdens in local currency, increasing expected default probabilities and loan portfolio volatility \(\sigma\). As \(\sigma\) increases, the bank's maximum permitted leverage ratio falls, forcing active liquidation of cross-border loans and shrinking global credit supply.

Evaluate policy instruments that small open economies can deploy to insulate themselves from the Global Financial Cycle.

Solution

Policymakers deploy a multi-layered macro-financial framework: (1) flexible exchange rates to absorb external terms-of-trade shocks; (2) countercyclical macroprudential limits on bank foreign borrowing; (3) foreign exchange reserve accumulation during GFC expansions to fund emergency intervention during retrenchments; and (4) targeted capital flow management measures during extreme speculative surges.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What empirical regularities define the Global Financial Cycle?

    Options

    The GFC captures synchronized co-movement in international asset markets and credit flows.

  2. According to Miranda-Agrippino & Rey (2020), which central bank's policy shocks serve as the primary driver of the Global Financial Cycle?

    Options

    US monetary policy decisions drive global dollar liquidity, international bank leverage, and global risk appetite.

  3. If the sum of squared factor loadings is 3.0 and the sum of idiosyncratic variances is 1.0, what percentage of total variance is explained by the global factor?

    \(\mathcal{V}_f = 3.0 / (3.0 + 1.0) = 3.0 / 4.0 = 0.75 = 75\%\).

  4. How does global risk aversion (as proxied by the VIX) correlate with the Global Financial Cycle factor?

    Options

    When global volatility rises, risk appetite collapses and global credit and capital flows contract.

  5. What is the "Dilemma" hypothesis proposed by Rey (2013)?

    Options

    The dilemma states that floating exchange rates cannot protect an economy from the global financial cycle without capital controls.

  6. In a 5-variable correlation system, the first principal component has an eigenvalue of 3.0. What percentage of total variance does it explain?

    \((3.0 / 5.0) \times 100\% = 60\%\).

  7. Why does a Federal Reserve rate hike cause global bank leverage to contract?

    Options

    Higher dollar funding rates and heightened asset volatility tighten bank regulatory and internal risk limits.

  8. What algorithm extracts the dominant eigenvector and eigenvalue from a sample covariance matrix?

    Options

    Repeated matrix multiplication and normalization in power iteration isolates the leading eigenvector.
