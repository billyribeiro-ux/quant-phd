Conventions and definitions

The terms below are defined here, and the labs use these definitions. A quarter is a calendar quarter. In a data file a quarter is written by its first day, for example 2026-07-01. Rates and growth rates are in percent per year unless a term says otherwise.

Annualised GDP growth
For real GDP \(Y_t\) in quarter \(t\), the growth rate is \(g_t = 100\,[(Y_t/Y_{t-1})^4 - 1]\), the quarter-on-quarter ratio compounded to a year. It is not a simple annual change.
Inflation
For a price index \(P_t\), year-on-year inflation is \(\pi^{\mathrm{yoy}}_t = 100\,(P_t/P_{t-4} - 1)\), the change over the four quarters ending at \(t\). This page writes it with the superscript so that \(\pi_t\) keeps the one-period meaning of Lessons 1 and 6. The base year of the index does not matter for this ratio.
Window and trend
The window is the 20 quarters ending at the diagnosis quarter \(t\). The trend is the least-squares line in \(\ln Y\) fitted over the window. Its value at the window's last quarter, \(t\), is \(\ln \tilde Y_t\), so \(\tilde Y_t\) is the trend level at \(t\).
Output gap
The output gap is the log gap \(y^{\mathrm{log}}_t = 100\,(\ln Y_t - \ln \tilde Y_t)\), as 100 times a difference of logs. A positive gap means output is above trend. It is not the percent-of-trend gap \(100\,(Y_t - \tilde Y_t)/\tilde Y_t\), which Lesson 7 writes as \(y_t\), computed with this page's trend. At each date the two have the same sign, because \(\ln x\) and \(x - 1\) have the same sign for \(x > 0\). At 2026 Q3 both print \(-0.18\) to two decimals: the log gap is -0.18384 and the percent-of-trend gap is -0.18367, both unrounded.
Cycle flag
"above trend and growing" when \(g_t > 0\) and \(y^{\mathrm{log}}_t > 0\); "below trend and not growing" when \(g_t \le 0\) and \(y^{\mathrm{log}}_t \le 0\); "mixed" otherwise.
Term spread
In percentage points, the 10-year constant-maturity yield minus the 3-month bill rate. The quarter's term spread is the mean of the daily spreads on the days when both yields are published.
Stated parameters
The inflation target is \(\bar\pi = 2.0\). The natural real interest rate is \(r^{n} = 2.0\). The natural rate of unemployment is \(u^{n} = 4.0\). The Taylor coefficients are \(\phi_\pi = 0.5\) and \(\phi_y = 0.5\). These are assumptions that this dashboard states. They are not estimates, and a diagnosis may use other values if it states them.
Taylor-rule value and policy gap
The Taylor-rule value is \(i^{\ast}_t = r^{n} + \pi^{\mathrm{yoy}}_t + \phi_\pi(\pi^{\mathrm{yoy}}_t - \bar\pi) + \phi_y y^{\mathrm{log}}_t\), in percent per year. The policy gap is the policy rate \(i_t\) minus \(i^{\ast}_t\), in percentage points.
Rounding
Computations use unrounded values. Each printed rate, gap and spread is rounded to two decimals from its unrounded value; day counts and quarter labels are printed as they are. A figure quoted from a printed output is the printed figure, and a recomputation from printed figures can differ from the unrounded result in the last printed digit.

Lab 1 · Worked example: one quarter, step by step

On the SIMULATED path the latest quarter is 2026 Q3, and the window runs from 2021 Q4 to 2026 Q3. """Rung 1 worked example: GDP growth, an inflation rate from a price index, and one cycle flag, on a SIMULATED path.

Standard library only. Every series here is SIMULATED: a seeded generator (seed 2026) draws 24 quarters, 2020 Q4 to
2026 Q3. The first four quarters are lags for the year-on-year inflation rate. The last twenty quarters are the
five-year window. All rates are in percent per year.

* GDP growth, annualised: 100 * ((Y_t / Y_(t-1)) ** 4 - 1), from the quarter-on-quarter change, compounded to a year.
* Inflation, year on year: 100 * (P_t / P_(t-4) - 1), from the price index P.
* Output gap, the log gap y_log of the conventions: 100 * (ln Y_t minus the least-squares trend of ln Y over the
  window).
* Cycle flag: "above trend and growing" when growth and the gap are both positive, "below trend and not growing" when
  both are zero or negative, and "mixed" otherwise.

Every printed rate and gap is rounded to two decimals from its unrounded float; the flag uses the unrounded values.
"""

import math
import random

SEED = 2026
QUARTERS = 24
WINDOW = 20


def quarter_label(k: int) -> str:
    """The quarter k of the path, where k = 0 is 2020 Q4, written as year and quarter, for example 2026 Q3."""
    month_index = 9 + 3 * k  # months after January 2020; 9 is October 2020
    year = 2020 + month_index // 12
    quarter = (month_index % 12) // 3 + 1
    return f"{year} Q{quarter}"


def simulated_path(seed: int = SEED) -> dict[str, list[float]]:
    """A SIMULATED quarterly path: GDP level in billions, price index (2020 Q4 = 100), unemployment and policy rate."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    gdp = [20000.0]
    for _ in range(QUARTERS - 1):
        gdp.append(gdp[-1] * (1 + 0.004 + 0.012 * (rng.random() - 0.5)))
    price = [100.0]
    for _ in range(QUARTERS - 1):
        price.append(price[-1] * (1 + 0.005 + 0.004 * (rng.random() - 0.5)))
    unemployment = [4.2]
    for _ in range(QUARTERS - 1):
        step = unemployment[-1] + 0.4 * (rng.random() - 0.5)
        unemployment.append(min(7.0, max(3.5, step)))
    policy = [1.0]
    for _ in range(QUARTERS - 1):
        policy.append(max(0.0, policy[-1] + 0.5 * (rng.random() - 0.5)))
    return {"gdp": gdp, "price": price, "unemployment": unemployment, "policy": policy}


def growth_annualised(gdp: list[float], t: int) -> float:
    """GDP growth in percent per year: the quarter-on-quarter ratio raised to the fourth power, minus one."""
    return 100.0 * ((gdp[t] / gdp[t - 1]) ** 4 - 1.0)


def inflation_yoy(price: list[float], t: int) -> float:
    """Inflation in percent per year: the change in the price index over the four quarters ending at t."""
    return 100.0 * (price[t] / price[t - 4] - 1.0)


def trend_fit(gdp: list[float], t: int) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """The least-squares intercept and slope of ln Y over the window of WINDOW quarters that ends at t.

    The time index inside the window runs 0 to WINDOW - 1, so the intercept is the trend at the window's first quarter.
    """
    first = t - WINDOW + 1
    xs = [float(k) for k in range(WINDOW)]
    ys = [math.log(gdp[first + k]) for k in range(WINDOW)]
    xbar = sum(xs) / WINDOW
    ybar = sum(ys) / WINDOW
    slope = sum((x - xbar) * (y - ybar) for x, y in zip(xs, ys, strict=True)) / sum((x - xbar) ** 2 for x in xs)
    return ybar - slope * xbar, slope


def output_gap(gdp: list[float], t: int) -> float:
    """The output gap in percent: 100 times ln Y_t minus the fitted trend at t, which is WINDOW - 1 in window time."""
    intercept, slope = trend_fit(gdp, t)
    return 100.0 * (math.log(gdp[t]) - (intercept + slope * (WINDOW - 1)))


def cycle_flag(growth: float, gap: float) -> str:
    """The cycle flag of the rule in the module docstring."""
    if growth > 0 and gap > 0:
        return "above trend and growing"
    if growth <= 0 and gap <= 0:
        return "below trend and not growing"
    return "mixed"


def main() -> None:
    path = simulated_path()
    gdp, price = path["gdp"], path["price"]
    latest = QUARTERS - 1
    growth = growth_annualised(gdp, latest)
    inflation = inflation_yoy(price, latest)
    gap = output_gap(gdp, latest)
    print("SIMULATED path, seed", SEED, "| latest quarter:", quarter_label(latest))
    print("GDP growth, annualised (%):", f"{growth:.2f}")
    print("inflation, year on year (%):", f"{inflation:.2f}")
    print("output gap (%):", f"{gap:.2f}")
    print("cycle flag:", cycle_flag(growth, gap))
    print("window:", quarter_label(latest - WINDOW + 1), "to", quarter_label(latest), "| quarters:", WINDOW)


main()

GDP growth, annualised (%): 0.21
inflation, year on year (%): 1.90
output gap (%): -0.18
cycle flag: mixed
window: 2021 Q4 to 2026 Q3 | quarters: 20

Each function implements one definition from the conventions. The growth rate is annualised from the change of one quarter, so the printed 0.21 is 100 times the fourth power of the quarter's ratio minus one, and it is not a change of 0.21 in one quarter. The inflation rate uses the price index four quarters back, so the lags are needed and are not printed. The flag is "mixed" because the growth rate is positive and the output gap is negative: the two signals disagree, and the flag reports that instead of picking one. The printed numbers describe the SIMULATED path only.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A macro analyst must give a one-page diagnosis of where the economy stands in its cycle. The diagnosis must rest on five series, each with a named source and a stated transform, and on the rules that turn those series into signals. Every number in the diagnosis must cite the series, the date and the transform that produced it.
Inputs
Five series, each described in the data section: real GDP, a price index, the unemployment rate and the policy rate, which you supply as four CSV files in the schemas of that section, and the term spread, computed from the embedded snapshot data/ust_daily.csv. The page embeds none of your four files. The project uses no series beyond the five listed in the data section.
Required outputs
Two named files in a folder of your own, and one produced by the form in the self-assessment section. diagnosis.md is the one-page diagnosis under the headings of the brief. dashboard, with the file extension of the tool you choose (a notebook, a spreadsheet or a static page), shows the five series with their units and the signals of Lab 3 for the default state. self_assessment.md is the summary the form produces.
Constraints
Every number in the diagnosis is recomputed from unrounded values, and the rounding rule is stated where the number first appears. Every number cites its series, its date and its transform. The stated parameters of the conventions are named wherever they are used, and they are called assumptions. The diagnosis makes no claim about a series beyond its source's coverage, and it states the coverage it relies on. The diagnosis is one page, which this project takes to mean no more than 600 words in the body. Standard library, or any tool you choose; the rubric does not depend on the tool.
Grading mode
Rubric only. The dashboard and the diagnosis are scored against the five criteria of the rubric, each with four observable levels. You score your own work with the self-assessment form, then score the two exemplars with their annotations hidden, and the form shows where your scores differ from the official ones. No adversarial review is used for this project.

    return 100.0 * (price[t] / price[t - 4] - 1.0)


def output_gap(gdp: list[float], t: int) -> float:
    """The output gap in percent: 100 times ln Y_t minus the least-squares trend in ln Y over the window ending at t."""
    first = t - WINDOW + 1
    xs = [float(k) for k in range(WINDOW)]
    ys = [math.log(gdp[first + k]) for k in range(WINDOW)]
    xbar = sum(xs) / WINDOW
    ybar = sum(ys) / WINDOW
    slope = sum((x - xbar) * (y - ybar) for x, y in zip(xs, ys, strict=True)) / sum((x - xbar) ** 2 for x in xs)
    intercept = ybar - slope * xbar
    return 100.0 * (math.log(gdp[t]) - (intercept + slope * (WINDOW - 1)))


def taylor_value(inflation: float, gap: float) -> float:
    """The policy rate the Taylor-rule value prescribes, in percent per year, at the dashboard's stated parameters."""
    return NATURAL_REAL_RATE + inflation + PHI_PI * (inflation - TARGET) + PHI_Y * gap


def cycle_flag(growth: float, gap: float) -> str:
    """The cycle flag of rung 1: above trend and growing, below trend and not growing, or mixed."""
    if growth > 0 and gap > 0:
        return "above trend and growing"
    if growth <= 0 and gap <= 0:
        return "below trend and not growing"
    return "mixed"


def sign(value: float | Fraction) -> str:
    """The sign of a number as a word: positive, negative or zero."""
    return "positive" if value > 0 else "negative" if value < 0 else "zero"


def read_quarter_spreads(file: Path) -> dict[str, tuple[Fraction, int]]:
    """Each quarter's average term spread (exact) and the number of days in it, from the H.15 snapshot.

    The file's header must be date,tbill_3m,cmt_10y. A value that is not a decimal number raises ValueError. A day on
    which either yield is blank is skipped, so each quarter averages only the days on which both are published.
    """
    totals: dict[str, Fraction] = {}
    days: dict[str, int] = {}
    with file.open(encoding="utf-8") as handle:
        header = next(handle).strip().split(",")
        if header != ["date", "tbill_3m", "cmt_10y"]:
            raise ValueError(f"unexpected header {header}")
        for line in handle:
            date, tbill, cmt = line.strip().split(",")
            if tbill == "" or cmt == "":
                continue
            label = f"{int(date[:4])} Q{(int(date[5:7]) - 1) // 3 + 1}"
            totals[label] = totals.get(label, Fraction(0)) + (Fraction(cmt) - Fraction(tbill))
            days[label] = days.get(label, 0) + 1
    return {label: (totals[label] / days[label], days[label]) for label in totals}

The brief

Write diagnosis.md for a reader who has the dashboard in front of them and does not have this page. Use these six headings, in this order, and no other headings.

  1. Summary: the cycle position at the diagnosis quarter, in at most 80 words, with the flag.
  2. Data: each of the five series, its source, its transform, its date range and its coverage limit.
  3. Computation: the growth, inflation and output-gap formulas, each with the rounding rule and the function or line of your code that computes it.
  4. Diagnosis: each rule of the conventions, applied to the stated values, with the sign of each signal and the cycle flag.
  5. Policy reading: the Taylor value at the stated parameters, the policy gap and its sign, and one sensitivity of the gap to a stated coefficient.
  6. Limits: the assumptions, the coverage of each series, and the basis of the spread, each with the direction in which it would move the diagnosis.

A claim graded B or C on the program's evidence scale must name its counter-evidence. B is published evidence that is not robustly replicated or is contested; C is a working paper or a single-sample result. A diagnosis that makes no such claim says so in its Limits section.

Rubric

Each level contains the one below it. A criterion scores the highest level whose every element the memo shows. A memo that meets level 3 or above on every criterion meets the bar.

Exemplar A

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 3. Basis, computations and rules are complete, and the policy sensitivity is derived with its basis. C5 falls short of level 4 once: the memo gives a direction for the spread's basis and for the target, but names the trend's end-point as a limit with no direction for its effect.

Summary

On the SIMULATED path at 2026 Q3, GDP growth is positive at 0.21 percent per year and the output gap is negative at -0.18 percent, so the cycle flag is "mixed". Inflation is below the 2.0 percent target, unemployment is above the 4.0 percent natural rate, and the policy rate is 2.22 points below its Taylor value. The term spread, 0.96 points, is real; the other four series are SIMULATED.

  • Meets C3 level 3: states the flag and the sign of each signal at the stated values.
Data

Real GDP, the price index, the unemployment rate and the policy rate are the SIMULATED path of Lab 3, seed 2026, over 2020 Q4 to 2026 Q3. The window is its last 20 quarters, 2021 Q4 to 2026 Q3. The term spread is real: the mean of the daily cmt_10y minus tbill_3m in the embedded ust_daily.csv over the 64 days of 2026 Q3 that Lab 3 counts, from a snapshot covering 2000-01-03 to 2026-09-30, retrieved 2026-10-20. The bill rate is on a discount basis, so the spread is the published difference and is not converted.

  • Meets C1 level 4: names the source, the transform and the date range of each series, the spread's basis, and the coverage of the path and of the snapshot.
Computation

Growth is 100 times the quarter-on-quarter ratio raised to the fourth power, minus one. Inflation is the year-on-year change in the price index. The output gap is 100 times the natural logarithm of output at 2026 Q3, minus the trend fitted over the 20 quarters ending there, read at the window's last quarter. Lab 3 computes growth_annualised, inflation_yoy, output_gap and taylor_value from unrounded values. The spread is the exact quarter mean from read_quarter_spreads. The unemployment and policy rates are the series of simulated_path, and the policy gap is the line policy[t] - taylor in main. Rounding applies only to the printed output, to two decimals.

  • Meets C2 level 4: names the function that computes each printed number from unrounded values, and says that rounding applies only to the printed output.
Diagnosis

The rules at 2026 Q3 give these signs: growth positive; the output gap negative; inflation minus the 2.0 target negative; unemployment minus the 4.0 natural rate positive; the policy gap negative; and the term spread positive. The flag is "mixed", because growth is positive and the gap is negative. Growth and the gap disagree, so the diagnosis reports a mixed state.

  • Meets C3 level 4: names growth, which disagrees with the gap, as the signal that contradicts the verdict, and says how the diagnosis treats it.
Policy reading

At the stated parameters the Taylor value at 2026 Q3 is 3.75 percent per year, as Lab 3 prints. From the unrounded inputs, inflation between 1.895 and 1.905 and gap between -0.185 and -0.175, the value lies between 3.75 and 3.77, so 3.75 is at the low end. The policy rate is 1.53, so the policy gap is negative at -2.22 points. Raising the output coefficient from 0.5 to 1.0 changes the Taylor value by 0.5 times the gap. The gap is negative, so the value falls by about 0.09 points and the policy gap rises to about -2.13. The size rests on the unrounded gap, between -0.185 and -0.175, so half of it lies between -0.0925 and -0.0875.

  • Meets C4 level 3: computes the Taylor value from the stated inputs and states the sign of the policy gap.
  • Meets C4 level 4: states the change from one stated coefficient, with its sign, its size and the basis of the size.
Limits

The target 2.0, the natural real rate 2.0, the natural unemployment rate 4.0 and the coefficients 0.5 are stated assumptions, not estimates. A higher target lowers the Taylor value, because the inflation gap falls as the target rises and its coefficient is positive. The four macro series are SIMULATED, so the verdict describes the simulated path only. The gap uses a trend fitted to the same 20 quarters, so the trend's end-point is a limit: new quarters can change the gap, and this memo does not say which way. The bill rate is on a discount basis and the 10-year is a constant-maturity yield, so the spread mixes two bases; a yield-based spread would be smaller than the published one. The memo makes no claim graded B or C.

  • Meets C5 level 3: names the stated assumptions, the coverage of the series and the mixed bases, and states that no claim is graded B or C.
  • Misses C5 level 4: names the trend's end-point as a limit, but gives no direction for its effect.
