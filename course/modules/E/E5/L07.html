Central-Bank Policy and the Taylor Rule

  • Uses: price indices and measured inflation (Lesson 1), business cycle (Lesson 4)
  • Introduces: central-bank policy rate, Taylor rule
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

This lesson defines one policy rule. It sets a nominal policy rate from current inflation and the output gap, with a natural real rate, an inflation target and two coefficients. The rule is affine in inflation, so its responses can be computed exactly (Problem P1). Under the stated assumptions, a one-point rise in inflation raises the nominal rate by one point plus the inflation coefficient, and it raises the real rate by the coefficient alone (Part B of Lab 1). The rule is a definition in this lesson: its coefficients are inputs, and no estimate of them is given. Lesson L08 holds the policy rate fixed when it computes the fiscal multiplier.

1The policy rate, the gaps and the rule

Rates are in percent per year, so 2 means 2% a year, and no compounding convention is needed: the rule is an affine relation between numbers of percentage points. Inflation is the simple form of Lesson 1 (Definition 2.2 there), which is a fraction per period, converted to percent per period with a period of one year: \(\pi_t = 100\,(P_t/P_{t-1} - 1)\). Lesson 4 (Definition 1.1) defines the output gap as output minus trend output, in units of output. This lesson uses the same gap in percent of trend output: \(y_t = 100\,(Y_t - Y^{\mathrm{trend}}_t)/Y^{\mathrm{trend}}_t\). At one date the change is a change of units: each gap keeps its sign, and the ordering of gaps at that date is unchanged, provided trend output is positive. Across dates with different trends the ordering can change. A gap of 2 at a trend of 10 is 20 percent, and a gap of 3 at a trend of 100 is 3 percent.

Definition 1.1 (central-bank policy rate)

The central-bank policy rate \(i_t\) is the nominal interest rate, in percent per year, that the rule below sets at date \(t\). In this lesson it is a number the rule returns. The page does not describe any institution's procedure for choosing it.

Definition 1.2 (inflation target and inflation gap)

The inflation target \(\bar\pi\) is a constant, in percent per year, that the rule takes as given. The inflation gap is \(\pi_t - \bar\pi\), in percentage points.

Definition 1.3 (natural real rate)

The natural real rate \(r^{n}\) is a constant, in percent per year, that the rule takes as given. Its superscript \(n\) marks it as the natural real rate. It is not the natural rate of unemployment of Lesson 6. The symbol \(R^{\ast}\) is not used for it, because that symbol denotes another object in this program. The real policy rate is \(i_t - \pi_t\).

Definition 1.4 (Taylor rule)

The Taylor rule is the policy-rate reaction function \[ i_t = r^{n} + \pi_t + \phi_{\pi}(\pi_t - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y}\, y_t, \] where \(\phi_{\pi}\) and \(\phi_{y}\) are coefficients, dimensionless numbers that the rule takes as given. Equivalently, the real policy rate \(i_t - \pi_t\) equals \(r^{n} + \phi_{\pi}(\pi_t - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y}\, y_t\). This lesson uses the rule as a definition. It gives no estimate of \(\phi_{\pi}\), \(\phi_{y}\) or \(r^{n}\).

Assumptions (for Propositions 2.1 and 2.2)
  1. A1. \(r^{n}\) and \(\bar\pi\) are constants, in percent per year.
  2. A2. \(\phi_{\pi} \ge 0\) and \(\phi_{y} \ge 0\) are constants. Sign restrictions of this kind are a choice of the model, and this page imposes them to state the sign results below.
  3. A3. The rule holds at each date with the same-date values \(\pi_t\) and \(y_t\). There are no lags and no shocks.

2The rule is linear in inflation, and its real response has the sign of the coefficient

Proposition 2.1 (the one-point inflation response)

Under A1 to A3, fix the output gap \(y\). For any inflation rate \(\pi\) and any change \(h\) in inflation, the nominal rate changes by \((1 + \phi_{\pi})h\). In particular, a one-point rise in inflation, \(h = 1\), raises the nominal rate by \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\) points.

Proof

Write \(i(\pi) = r^{n} + \pi + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y} y\), the rule at fixed \(y\). For \(\pi\) and \(\pi + h\), \[ i(\pi + h) - i(\pi) = h + \phi_{\pi}\bigl((\pi + h - \bar\pi) - (\pi - \bar\pi)\bigr) + \phi_{y}y - \phi_{y}y = h + \phi_{\pi} h = (1 + \phi_{\pi})h. \] The terms \(r^{n}\), \(\bar\pi\) and \(\phi_{y}y\) cancel because they do not depend on \(\pi\), and A1 and A2 make them constants at fixed \(y\). With \(h = 1\) the change is \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\).

Proposition 2.2 (the real response and its sign)

Under A1 to A3, fix the output gap \(y\). For any two inflation rates \(\pi_1 < \pi_2\), the real policy rate satisfies \((i_2 - \pi_2) - (i_1 - \pi_1) = \phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \pi_1)\). Hence the real policy rate is strictly increasing in inflation if and only if \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\).

Proof

By Definition 1.4, the real policy rate is \(r^{n} + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y} y\). At the two inflation rates, \(r^{n}\) and \(\phi_{y} y\) are the same, and \(\bar\pi\) is a constant inside the difference, so the difference of the two real rates is \(\phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \bar\pi) - \phi_{\pi}(\pi_1 - \bar\pi) = \phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \pi_1)\). Since \(\pi_2 - \pi_1 > 0\), this difference is strictly positive if and only if \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\). A2 gives \(\phi_{\pi} \ge 0\), so the only other case is \(\phi_{\pi} = 0\), where the difference is zero and the real rate is constant.

Remark 2.3 (what the rule does not say)

The rule uses current inflation \(\pi_t\), so it needs no expected inflation, and this page uses no expectations term. The page does not say whether the rule makes the economy's equilibrium unique or stable; that question belongs to module E6 (Macroeconomics II), which is outside this unit. The page does not say how any central bank sets its rate. Part A of Lab 1 prints a value of \(-1/2\) percent for one grid point. The rule as defined returns a negative nominal rate there, and the page imposes no lower bound.

Lab 1 · The rule evaluated exactly, and the response to a one-point inflation gap

The parameters are illustrative inputs chosen for the arithmetic, not estimates. The natural real rate is 1, the inflation target is 2, \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\) and \(\phi_{y} = 1/2\), all in the units of Definition 1.4. Part A prints the nominal rate on a grid of inflation rates and output gaps. Part B prints the nominal and real response to a one-point inflation gap for four values of \(\phi_{\pi}\). Propositions 2.1 and 2.2 predict that the nominal response equals \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\), that the real response equals \(\phi_{\pi}\), and that the real rate rises with inflation exactly when \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\). The code uses exact fractions and no random draws, so its output is fixed.

"""E5 L07 lab 1: the Taylor-type policy rule evaluated exactly, and its response to an inflation gap.

Standard library only (fractions). No randomness and no network, so every run prints the same lines.

Every parameter is an illustrative input chosen for the arithmetic, not an estimate: the natural real rate
r^n = 1 percent per year, the inflation target 2 percent per year, and the coefficients phi_pi = 3/2 and
phi_y = 1/2. No published value is used. All rates are in percent per year, and the output gap is in percent of
trend output (Lesson 4). Every number is an exact Fraction, so nothing is rounded.

Part A: the nominal policy rate i = r^n + pi + phi_pi (pi - target) + phi_y y on a grid of inflation rates and
output gaps.
Part B: the nominal and the real response to a one-point inflation gap at output gap zero, for four values of phi_pi.
Part C: the rule is affine in inflation, so its second differences are zero, and the real rate rises with inflation
exactly when phi_pi is positive.
Part D: one worked point, and the neutral point where inflation is at target and the output gap is zero.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
from fractions import Fraction

NATURAL_REAL_RATE = Fraction(1)
TARGET = Fraction(2)
PHI_PI = Fraction(3, 2)
PHI_Y = Fraction(1, 2)
INFLATION_GRID = (Fraction(1), Fraction(2), Fraction(3), Fraction(4), Fraction(5))
GAP_GRID = (Fraction(-2), Fraction(0), Fraction(2))
PHI_PI_GRID = (Fraction(0), Fraction(1, 2), Fraction(1), Fraction(3, 2))


def policy_rate(inflation: Fraction, output_gap: Fraction, phi_pi: Fraction, phi_y: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Nominal policy rate in percent per year: r^n + pi + phi_pi (pi - target) + phi_y y, all arguments in percent."""
    return NATURAL_REAL_RATE + inflation + phi_pi * (inflation - TARGET) + phi_y * output_gap


def real_policy_rate(inflation: Fraction, output_gap: Fraction, phi_pi: Fraction, phi_y: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Real policy rate in percent per year, the nominal rate minus inflation, which the rule sets to r^n + ..."""
    return policy_rate(inflation, output_gap, phi_pi, phi_y) - inflation


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to D in a fixed order. Every line is an exact fraction or a fixed string."""
    print(f"parameters: natural_real_rate {NATURAL_REAL_RATE} target {TARGET} phi_pi {PHI_PI} phi_y {PHI_Y}")

    print("Part A: nominal policy rate i in percent per year, by inflation pi and output gap y")
    for y in GAP_GRID:
        cells = " ".join(f"pi_{p}={policy_rate(p, y, PHI_PI, PHI_Y)}" for p in INFLATION_GRID)
        print(f"output_gap {y}: {cells}")

    print("Part B: response to a one-point inflation gap at output gap 0")
    for phi in PHI_PI_GRID:
        nominal = policy_rate(TARGET + 1, Fraction(0), phi, PHI_Y) - policy_rate(TARGET, Fraction(0), phi, PHI_Y)
        real = real_policy_rate(TARGET + 1, Fraction(0), phi, PHI_Y) - real_policy_rate(TARGET, Fraction(0), phi, PHI_Y)
        print(
            f"phi_pi {phi} nominal_response {nominal} equals_one_plus_phi {nominal == 1 + phi} "
            f"real_response {real} equals_phi {real == phi}"
        )

    print("Part C: second differences in inflation at output gap 0, and the sign of the real response")
    values = [policy_rate(p, Fraction(0), PHI_PI, PHI_Y) for p in INFLATION_GRID]
    first = [b - a for a, b in itertools.pairwise(values)]
    second = [b - a for a, b in itertools.pairwise(first)]
    print("first_differences " + " ".join(str(d) for d in first))
    print("second_differences " + " ".join(str(d) for d in second))
    print(f"affine_in_inflation {all(d == 0 for d in second)}")
    for phi in PHI_PI_GRID:
        reals = [real_policy_rate(p, Fraction(0), phi, PHI_Y) for p in INFLATION_GRID]
        rising = all(b > a for a, b in itertools.pairwise(reals))
        print(f"phi_pi {phi} real_rate_rises_with_inflation {rising} matches_phi_positive {rising == (phi > 0)}")

    print("Part D: a worked point and the neutral point")
    worked = policy_rate(Fraction(3), Fraction(-1), PHI_PI, PHI_Y)
    print(f"worked pi 3 y -1: i {worked}")
    neutral = policy_rate(TARGET, Fraction(0), PHI_PI, PHI_Y)
    neutral_real = real_policy_rate(TARGET, Fraction(0), PHI_PI, PHI_Y)
    matches = neutral_real == NATURAL_REAL_RATE
    print(f"neutral pi 2 y 0: i {neutral} real {neutral_real} equals_natural_real_rate {matches}")

Every check line prints True, and every value is an exact fraction, so nothing is rounded. Part A at output gap 0 reads 1/2, 3, 11/2, 8 and 21/2 for inflation of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 percent: the policy rate rises by 5/2 at each one-point step, which is \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\). Part B shows the same 5/2 at \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\), and the real response equals \(\phi_{\pi}\). Part C shows that the second differences are zero, so the rule is affine in inflation (Problem P1), and it prints the real rate rising with inflation for each positive \(\phi_{\pi}\) in the grid; the one line that prints False, for \(\phi_{\pi} = 0\), is the expected case, because the real rate is then constant. The lab shows the values of this rule at these inputs and nothing about any economy.

3Where the canon breaks

  • A fixed rule with constant coefficients. The rule holds \(\phi_{\pi}\) and \(\phi_{y}\) fixed at every date. Parts B and C of Lab 1 show the response at the four coefficients listed in Part B, with the other inputs fixed. A rule whose coefficients change, or a rule chosen to optimise a loss, is outside this lesson.
  • The output gap is measured, not read off a table. Definition 1.4 takes \(y_t\) as given. Lesson L04 defines the gap as a deviation from trend, and this page does not say how the trend is computed. A different trend gives a different \(y_t\) and a different rule value.
  • No lags. Assumption A3 sets the rule at the same date as the inflation and gap it reads. A policy rate that responds with a delay has a different response path, and this lesson does not compute one.
  • Affine form. The second differences in Part C are zero because the rule is affine in inflation (Problem P1). A rule that is not affine in inflation has second differences that need not be zero, and Proposition 2.1 does not apply to it.

4Misconceptions

  1. “A one-point rise in inflation raises the policy rate by one point.”

    Under the rule, the nominal rate rises by \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\) points (Proposition 2.1). Part B of Lab 1 prints 5/2 for \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\), not 1. The extra \(\phi_{\pi}\) points come from the inflation gap term, which responds to the same one-point rise.

  2. “A rule with \(\phi_{\pi} = 0\) still raises the real rate when inflation rises.”

    With \(\phi_{\pi} = 0\), the real policy rate equals \(r^{n} + \phi_{y} y\) at every inflation rate (Definition 1.4), so it does not change with \(\pi\). Part C of Lab 1 prints real_rate_rises_with_inflation False for \(\phi_{\pi} = 0\), and prints True for each of the three positive coefficients in its grid.

  3. “At the inflation target, the policy rate is zero.”

    At \(\pi = \bar\pi\) and \(y = 0\), the rule gives \(i = r^{n} + \bar\pi\), which is 3 with the lab's inputs (Part D of Lab 1). The real rate at that point is \(r^{n}\), which is 1. The policy rate is the sum of the natural real rate and the target, not zero.

5Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · The rule at a point

Let \(r^{n} = 2\), \(\bar\pi = 2\), \(\phi_{\pi} = 1/2\) and \(\phi_{y} = 1/4\), all in percent or as stated in Definition 1.4. Find \(i\) when \(\pi = 4\) and \(y = -4\).

Solution

The rule gives \(i = r^{n} + \pi + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y} y = 2 + 4 + \tfrac12(4 - 2) + \tfrac14(-4) = 2 + 4 + 1 - 1 = 6\). The policy rate is 6 percent a year.

M2 · The one-point response with a small coefficient

With \(\phi_{\pi} = 1/4\), by how many points does the nominal rate rise when inflation rises by one point at a fixed output gap? By how many does the real rate rise?

Solution

Proposition 2.1 gives the nominal rise as \(1 + \phi_{\pi} = 1 + 1/4 = 5/4\), which is 1.25 points. Proposition 2.2 gives the real rise as \(\phi_{\pi} = 1/4\), which is 0.25 points. The real rise plus the one-point rise in inflation equals the nominal rise, because the real rate is the nominal rate minus inflation.

M3 · The output gap that gives a rate of 4

With \(r^{n} = 1\), \(\bar\pi = 2\), \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\) and \(\phi_{y} = 1/2\), find the output gap \(y\) at which the rule gives \(i = 4\) when \(\pi = 3\).

Solution

At \(\pi = 3\) the rule gives \(i = 1 + 3 + \tfrac32(3 - 2) + \tfrac12 y = 5.5 + 0.5\,y\). Setting this equal to 4 gives \(0.5\,y = -1.5\), so \(y = -3\). The output gap is minus 3 percent of trend.

Proof

P1 · The rule is affine in inflation

At a fixed output gap \(y\), write \(i(\pi)\) as \(\alpha + \beta \pi\) and identify \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

Solution

Expand \(i(\pi) = r^{n} + \pi + \phi_{\pi}\pi - \phi_{\pi}\bar\pi + \phi_{y} y\). Collect the terms in \(\pi\): \(\beta = 1 + \phi_{\pi}\). The remaining terms, \(\alpha = r^{n} - \phi_{\pi}\bar\pi + \phi_{y} y\), do not depend on \(\pi\). So \(i(\pi) = \alpha + \beta\pi\), and the slope \(\beta\) is the one-point response of Proposition 2.1.

P2 · The real rate at target and zero gap

Show that at \(\pi = \bar\pi\) and \(y = 0\), the real policy rate equals \(r^{n}\) for every \(\phi_{\pi}\).

Solution

By Definition 1.4 the real rate is \(r^{n} + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y} y\). At \(\pi = \bar\pi\) the middle term is \(\phi_{\pi} \cdot 0 = 0\), and at \(y = 0\) the last term is \(\phi_{y} \cdot 0 = 0\). The real rate is therefore \(r^{n}\), whatever the coefficients are. This is the neutral point of Part D of Lab 1.

P3 · The real rate with a negative coefficient

Suppose \(\phi_{\pi} < 0\) were allowed. State the sign of the real response to a rise in inflation, and say which assumption of this lesson the case would break.

Solution

Proposition 2.2 gives the real response as \(\phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \pi_1)\) for \(\pi_2 > \pi_1\). With \(\phi_{\pi} < 0\) this is negative, so the real rate falls as inflation rises. The case breaks A2, which requires \(\phi_{\pi} \ge 0\). The difference equals \(\phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \pi_1)\) whatever the sign of \(\phi_{\pi}\), so the identity and the if-and-only-if of Proposition 2.2 hold without A2. A2 is listed among the hypotheses of Proposition 2.2 but is not needed for its statement.

Empirical

E1 · A reading from Part A

Read Part A of Lab 1. What nominal rate does the rule give at inflation 5 and output gap 2?

Solution

Part A prints 23/2 in the row for output gap 2 and the column for inflation 5. Check by the rule: \(1 + 5 + \tfrac32(5 - 2) + \tfrac12 \cdot 2 = 6 + 4.5 + 1 = 11.5\), which is 23/2. The value is 11.5 percent a year.

E2 · The effect of a two-point output gap

Read Part A of Lab 1 for inflation 3. How much does the rate change when the output gap moves from 0 to minus 2, and what does Definition 1.4 predict?

Solution

Part A prints 11/2 at output gap 0 and 9/2 at output gap minus 2, so the rate falls by 1 point. Definition 1.4 predicts a change of \(\phi_{y} \times (-2) = \tfrac12 \times (-2) = -1\). The printed change equals the predicted one.

E3 · Step sizes in Part C

Read Part C of Lab 1. What are the first differences of the nominal rate across the five inflation grid points at output gap 0, and what do they show?

Solution

Part C prints 5/2 for each of the four first differences. Each one-point step in inflation raises the rate by 5/2, which is \(1 + \phi_{\pi}\) with \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\). The equal steps show the affine form of Proposition 2.1, and the second differences of 0 confirm it.

Research

R1 · Joint change in inflation and output

Suppose inflation rises by \(h\) points and the output gap rises by \(k\) points at the same time. Find the change in the real policy rate, and state which terms of Definition 1.4 contribute.

Solution

The real rate is \(r^{n} + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) + \phi_{y} y\). Its change is \(\phi_{\pi} h + \phi_{y} k\), by the same cancellation as in Proposition 2.2. The inflation term contributes \(\phi_{\pi} h\), and the output-gap term contributes \(\phi_{y} k\). The natural real rate and the target do not contribute, because they are constants by A1.

R2 · The inflation gap for a given real rate

With \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\) and \(y = 0\), find the inflation gap \(\pi - \bar\pi\) at which the real policy rate equals \(r^{n} + 1\).

Solution

The real rate is \(r^{n} + \phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi)\) at \(y = 0\). Setting it equal to \(r^{n} + 1\) gives \(\phi_{\pi}(\pi - \bar\pi) = 1\). Since \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\), the inflation gap is \(1/\phi_{\pi}\). With \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\) this is \(2/3\) of a point, or 0.6667 points to four places.

R3 · The lower bound the rule does not impose

Find the output gap at which the rule with the lab's inputs gives a negative nominal rate when inflation is 1, and compare it with the grid in Part A.

Solution

At \(\pi = 1\) the rule gives \(i = 1 + 1 + \tfrac32(1 - 2) + \tfrac12 y = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 y\). The rate is negative when \(y < -1\). At \(y = -2\) it is \(-1/2\), which Part A prints. The grid includes that point, and the page imposes no lower bound on \(i\). A model that needs a lower bound on the nominal rate is outside this unit.

  4. With \(r^{n} = 1\) and \(\bar\pi = 2\), what nominal rate does the rule give at inflation equal to the target and output gap 0?

    \(r^{n} + \bar\pi = 1 + 2 = 3\) (Problem P2 and Part D of Lab 1).

  5. Which variable does the rule in Definition 1.4 use for inflation?

    Options

    The rule reads \(\pi_t\), the same-date value (Assumption A3). It contains no expectations term (Remark 2.3).

  6. With \(r^{n} = 2\), \(\bar\pi = 2\), \(\phi_{\pi} = 1/2\) and \(\phi_{y} = 1/4\), what nominal rate does the rule give at inflation 4 and output gap \(-4\)?

    \(2 + 4 + \tfrac12(2) + \tfrac14(-4) = 6\) (Problem M1).

  7. In Part A of Lab 1, at inflation 1 and output gap \(-2\), the rule gives a negative nominal rate. What does the page say about that value?

    Options

    Part A prints \(-1/2\) at that point, and Remark 2.3 and Problem R3 state that the page imposes no lower bound.

  8. What does Proposition 2.2 say about the real policy rate when \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\)?

    Options

    The difference between two real rates is \(\phi_{\pi}(\pi_2 - \pi_1)\), which is positive when \(\phi_{\pi} > 0\) and \(\pi_2 > \pi_1\) (Proposition 2.2).

  9. With \(\phi_{\pi} = 3/2\) and \(y = 0\), what inflation gap, in points to four decimal places, makes the real policy rate exceed \(r^{n}\) by 1 point?
