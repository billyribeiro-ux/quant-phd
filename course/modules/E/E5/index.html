Module E5 · Tier U · 110 hours · Part E, Economics

Macroeconomics I: Aggregates, Cycles & Policy

Macroeconomic claims are claims about measured totals, output and the price level, and about the models that connect them. This module states the accounting identities that define output and the price index, separates the part of output growth that inputs explain from the residual, and states the static models that a policy discussion uses: the Solow growth model, the IS-LM and AD-AS models, the Phillips curve with its natural rate, the Taylor rule and the Keynesian-cross multiplier. Every model is stated with its assumptions, and the results are proved on the page or checked by a lab. The implicit-function theorem and the monotone convergence theorem are used as standard results and are not proved here. The module is closed-economy and static, except for the Solow recursion, and it does not cover the New Keynesian model, equilibrium determinacy or open-economy macroeconomics. You leave able to derive a growth decomposition, solve a macro model for its equilibrium, trace a policy shock through it, and state exactly what the model cannot say.

  • Prerequisites: E1 Microeconomics and Market Structure (comparative statics and competitive equilibrium), M1 Precalculus and Mathematical Reasoning, and M2 Multivariable Calculus. P0.1 How to Study This Program supplies the evidence grades and the project ladder. E1, M1 and P0.1 are referred to by their concepts, and no page of E1 is built in this program yet.
  • Project: Macro dashboard v0, graded by a rubric with exemplars
  • Data: no embedded national-accounts, price, unemployment or policy-rate data. The labs use SIMULATED or illustrative numbers. The project uses your own CSV files for four series and an embedded Federal Reserve H.15 snapshot for the term spread. See Data status.

Objectives

By the end of E5 you can:

  1. State the expenditure identity and the income identity of the national accounts, and prove that saving equals investment in a closed economy (Lesson 1).
  2. Compute Laspeyres and Paasche price indices as exact fractions, simple and log measured inflation, and the deflator identity, and show that the sign of the gap between the two indices depends on the basket (Lesson 1).
  3. Derive the exact log-growth decomposition of a Cobb-Douglas economy, compute the residual for a chosen capital share, and show how the residual changes with that share (Lesson 2).
  4. Solve the Solow steady state, establish its convergence for the stated assumptions, and derive its comparative statics by the implicit-function theorem (Lesson 3).
  5. Solve the IS and LM relations and the AD-AS equilibrium for linear specifications, trace a demand shock through them, and state the expectations-augmented Phillips curve and the natural rate as models (Lessons 4 to 6).
  6. Evaluate the Taylor rule at stated coefficients and the Keynesian-cross multiplier \(1/(1-c)\) for a stated marginal propensity to consume, and name the limits of each model (Lessons 7 and 8).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on the prerequisites (one on P0.1, two on E1, two on M1 and two on M2) and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent, and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. P0.1 is not yet released, so a result below the bar on its item is provisional until P0.1 ships.

  1. Which evidence grade applies to a result that is proved on the page?

    Options

    Grade T is the theorem grade (P0.1, evidence grade).

  2. In comparative statics, what is compared?

    Options

    Comparative statics compares equilibria across parameter values (E1, comparative statics).

  3. What is \(\ln(e^{2})\)?

    The logarithm is the inverse of the exponential, so \(\ln(e^{2}) = 2\) (M1, exponential and logarithm).

  4. What is the derivative of \(\ln x\) for \(x > 0\)?

    Options

    The derivative of the natural logarithm is \(1/x\) on \(x > 0\) (M2, derivative).

  5. Let \(y\) be defined implicitly by \(x y = 1\) with \(y > 0\). What is \(dy/dx\) at \(x = 2\)?

    Differentiating \(x y = 1\) gives \(y + x\, dy/dx = 0\), so \(dy/dx = -y/x = -1/x^{2} = -0.25\) at \(x = 2\) (M2, implicit-function theorem).

  6. What holds in a competitive market equilibrium?

    Options

    Market clearing at the equilibrium price is the defining condition (E1, competitive market equilibrium).

  7. An output series rises from 100 to 110. Give its log growth, \(\ln(110/100)\), to four decimal places.

    \(\ln 1.1 = 0.0953\) to four decimal places (M1, exponential and logarithm; Lesson 2 uses this convention).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): base prices are \((1, 2)\) and base quantities are \((1, 1)\). Period-1 prices are \((2, 2)\). What is the Laspeyres price index, as a decimal?

    The numerator is \(2 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4\) and the denominator is \(1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 = 3\), so the index is \(4/3 = 1.333333\). This is the calculation the worked example in rung 1 shows.

  9. Skill check (rung 2): for \(Y = A K^{\alpha} L^{1-\alpha}\), which step turns the growth of a product into a sum?

    Options

    Theorem 1.4 of Lesson 2 takes logarithms and subtracts the same identity for the previous period. This is the missing step of the completion problem in rung 2.

Lessons

Eight lessons of ten study hours each, which sum to the 80 lesson hours. Each lesson page shares one structure: graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 10 hNational Accounts, Price Indices and Measured InflationThe expenditure and income identities, the Laspeyres and Paasche indices, simple and log inflation, and the deflator identity. L02 · 10 hGrowth Accounting and the Solow ResidualThe Cobb-Douglas production function, the exact log-growth decomposition, and why the residual depends on the chosen capital share. L03 · 10 hThe Solow Growth ModelThe capital accumulation equation, its steady state, convergence under stated assumptions, and comparative statics by the implicit-function theorem. L04 · 10 hBusiness Cycles and IS–LMThe business cycle described without statistics, and the IS and LM relations solved exactly for a linear specification. L05 · 10 hAggregate Demand and Aggregate SupplyThe aggregate-demand curve from IS-LM, short-run and long-run aggregate supply, and a demand shock traced through the equilibrium. L06 · 10 hThe Phillips Curve and the Natural RateThe expectations-augmented Phillips curve as a model, and the natural rate as the unemployment rate at which inflation does not change. L07 · 10 hCentral-Bank Policy and the Taylor RuleThe policy-rate reaction function, the Taylor rule at stated coefficients, and the policy-rate response to an inflation gap. L08 · 10 hFiscal Policy and the MultiplierThe Keynesian-cross multiplier \(1/(1-c)\), derived as the sum of successive spending increments, and the limits of the closed-economy model.

The project ladder

The module ends in the Macro dashboard v0. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    One fully solved and annotated computation on a SIMULATED five-year path: a GDP growth rate, an inflation rate from a price index, and one cycle flag, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same task with the term-spread line and the output-gap line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Assemble the dashboard from five series and write a one-page cycle diagnosis, diagnosis.md, that cites the values it uses. Graded by the rubric and its exemplars. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a second economy with the same rules, or a comparison of a revised and an unrevised value of one series, suitable for your portfolio.

Data status

No embedded national-accounts, price, unemployment or policy-rate data is used in E5. The curriculum names embedded national-accounts data for the growth-accounting lab. No redistributable source for national accounts is embedded in this program yet, so that lab runs on a generated economy labeled SIMULATED. The numbers in the Lesson 1 to 3 labs are illustrative or SIMULATED, and each of those labs says which.

The project takes four of its five series from your own CSV files, checked in your browser: real GDP, a price index, the unemployment rate and the policy rate. The file never leaves your browser. The fifth series is the term spread, computed from the embedded snapshot ust_daily of the Federal Reserve's H.15 release as the 10-year constant-maturity yield minus the 3-month bill rate, both in percent per year. The 10-year yield is a constant-maturity series, and the 3-month bill rate is on a discount basis as published. The project states that basis before it uses the spread.
