In L04 the informed party moves first, by sending a signal. Here the uninformed party moves first, by offering a menu, and each type chooses the item that suits it. A seller who offers a menu of quantity and payment pairs gives the buyer a reason to reveal its type, but the buyer keeps a utility that depends on its type. This lesson states the two constraints that the menu must satisfy, derives the best menu for a two-type monopoly in exact arithmetic, and shows the cost of the asymmetry in cents.

1A menu of contracts and the two constraints

Money is in integer cents, with 1 CU = 100 cents. A buyer has a type \(\theta \in \{L, H\}\). A type \(\theta\) buyer values one unit of the good at \(\theta\) cents, so the valuation of \(q\) units is \(\theta q\). The base case uses \(\theta_L = 3{,}000\) and \(\theta_H = 6{,}000\) cents per unit, with probabilities \(p_L = 3/4\) and \(p_H = 1/4\). Quantities \(q\) are in units. The seller cannot observe a buyer's type. It knows the two valuations and the two probabilities.

Assumption 1 (quasi-linear utility)

A type \(\theta\) buyer who takes the contract \((q, m)\), with \(q\) units and payment \(m\) in cents, has utility \(U_\theta(q, m) = \theta q - m\).

Assumption 2 (the seller's cost)

The seller's cost of \(q\) units is \(C(q) = q^2/2\) cents. The cost is convex, and \(C(0) = 0\).

Assumption 3 (two types)

There are two types, with \(0 < \theta_L < \theta_H\), and probabilities \(p_L + p_H = 1\) with \(0 < p_H < 1\).

Assumption 4 (outside option)

A buyer who takes no contract has utility 0. The participation constraint in Definition 1.2 is this condition.

Assumption 5 (the seller commits to a menu)

The seller offers a menu of two contracts, one for each type to choose, and maximizes its expected profit \(p_L (m_L - C(q_L)) + p_H (m_H - C(q_H))\) over the menu.

Assumption 6 (nonnegative quantities)

Every quantity in the menu is nonnegative: \(q_L \ge 0\) and \(q_H \ge 0\), in units.

Definition 1.1 (screening)

Screening is a contract design problem in which an uninformed party offers a menu, and each type of the informed party chooses the item that suits it. The choice reveals the type. In this lesson the menu has two items, one for each type, and the seller is the uninformed party.

Definition 1.2 (the two constraints)

Write \(U_\theta\) for the utility of type \(\theta\) from the item it chooses, with \(U_L = \theta_L q_L - m_L\) and \(U_H = \theta_H q_H - m_H\). The incentive constraint of type \(\theta\) requires \(U_\theta\) to be at least its utility from the other item. The participation constraint of type \(\theta\) requires \(U_\theta \ge 0\), which is Assumption 4. The two incentive constraints are:

(IC-L) \(U_L \ge \theta_L q_H - m_H\), and (IC-H) \(U_H \ge \theta_H q_L - m_L\).

The rent of type \(\theta\) is \(U_\theta\), its utility from the item it chooses. The first-best quantity of type \(\theta\) is the quantity that maximizes \(\theta q - C(q)\). The derivative of \(\theta q - q^2/2\) is \(\theta - q\), so the first-best quantity is \(q = \theta\): the valuation equals the marginal cost \(C'(q) = q\).

Lemma 1.3 (monotone quantities and the rent)

Under Assumptions 1 and 3, any menu that satisfies (IC-L) and (IC-H) has \(q_H \ge q_L\), and \(U_H \ge U_L + (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\).

Proof

Add the two incentive constraints. The left side is \(U_L + U_H\), and the right side is \(\theta_L q_H - m_H + \theta_H q_L - m_L\). Substituting \(U_L = \theta_L q_L - m_L\) and \(U_H = \theta_H q_H - m_H\) on the left gives \(\theta_H q_H + \theta_L q_L - m_H - m_L \ge \theta_H q_L + \theta_L q_H - m_L - m_H\). Cancelling the transfers and rearranging gives \((\theta_H - \theta_L)(q_H - q_L) \ge 0\). Since \(\theta_H > \theta_L\), this means \(q_H \ge q_L\). For the rent, (IC-H) gives \(U_H \ge \theta_H q_L - m_L = U_L + (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\). \(\square\)

Proposition 1.4 (the best two-item menu)

Let Assumptions 1 to 6 hold, and let \(\theta_L - \frac{p_H}{p_L}(\theta_H - \theta_L) \ge 0\). Then, among the menus that satisfy the two incentive constraints and both participation constraints, the seller's expected profit is largest at the menu with \(q_L^\ast = \theta_L - \frac{p_H}{p_L}(\theta_H - \theta_L)\) and \(q_H^\ast = \theta_H\), with the transfers \(m_L = \theta_L q_L^\ast\) and \(m_H = \theta_H q_H^\ast - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast\). At this menu the participation constraint of the low type and the incentive constraint of the high type both hold with equality.

Proof

Step 1, the low type's participation binds. Take a menu that satisfies the constraints and has \(U_L > 0\). Raise both transfers by \(U_L\). The differences \(m_H - m_L\) do not change, so (IC-L) and (IC-H) still hold. The new \(U_L\) is 0, and the new \(U_H\) is \(U_H - U_L \ge (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L \ge 0\) by Lemma 1.3 and Assumption 6, so participation holds for both types. The seller's profit rises by \(U_L\), since both transfers rise by that amount. So the best menu has \(U_L = 0\).

Step 2, the high type's incentive constraint binds. Take a menu with \(U_L = 0\) in which (IC-H) is slack, so \(U_H > (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\). Raise \(m_H\) by \(\delta = U_H - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L > 0\). The new \(U_H\) equals \((\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\), so (IC-H) binds, and the new \(U_H\) is still at least 0 because \(q_L \ge 0\) (Assumption 6). For (IC-L), note that \(\theta_L q_H - m_H = U_H - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_H\). After the change this is \((\theta_H - \theta_L)(q_L - q_H)\), which is at most 0 by Lemma 1.3, and \(U_L = 0\) is at least that. So (IC-L) holds. The seller's profit rises by \(p_H \delta\). The best menu has (IC-H) binding.

Step 3, the reduced problem. On the set where \(U_L = 0\) and (IC-H) binds, the seller's profit is \(\Pi(q_L, q_H) = p_L(\theta_L q_L - C(q_L)) + p_H(\theta_H q_H - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L - C(q_H))\). This function is concave, because its second derivatives are \(-p_L\) and \(-p_H\) and it has no cross term. The first-order conditions are \(\partial \Pi / \partial q_H = p_H(\theta_H - q_H) = 0\), which gives \(q_H = \theta_H\), and \(\partial \Pi / \partial q_L = p_L(\theta_L - q_L) - p_H(\theta_H - \theta_L) = 0\), which gives \(q_L = \theta_L - \frac{p_H}{p_L}(\theta_H - \theta_L)\). The point is feasible. Its quantities are nonnegative, since \(q_H^\ast = \theta_H\) and \(q_L^\ast \ge 0\) by the hypothesis, and \(q_H^\ast \ge q_L^\ast\) because \(q_L^\ast \le \theta_L < \theta_H\). The high type's participation holds because \(U_H = (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast \ge 0\). (IC-L) holds because \(\theta_L q_H^\ast - m_H^\ast = (\theta_H - \theta_L)(q_L^\ast - q_H^\ast) \le 0 = U_L\). A concave function's stationary point is its global maximum, so no menu does better. \(\square\)

Remark 1.5 (what the base case gives)

With \(\theta_L = 3{,}000\), \(\theta_H = 6{,}000\), \(p_H = 1/4\) and \(p_L = 3/4\), Proposition 1.4 gives \(q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 - \frac{1/4}{3/4} \cdot 3{,}000 = 2{,}000\) units and \(q_H^\ast = 6{,}000\) units. The transfers are \(m_L = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents and \(m_H = 30{,}000{,}000\) cents. The high type's rent is \(U_H = (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents, and the low type's rent is 0. The high type gets its first-best quantity, 6,000 units, and the low type gets less than its first-best quantity of 3,000 units. Lab 1 checks each of these numbers.

Lab 1 · The best menu in integer cents

The inputs are illustrative, not data: \(\theta_L = 3{,}000\) and \(\theta_H = 6{,}000\) cents per unit, \(p_L = 3/4\), \(p_H = 1/4\), and the cost \(q^2/2\) cents. Part A prints the menu of Remark 1.5 and the slack in each of the four constraints. Part B searches the low-type quantity on the integer grid 0 to 6,000, with the high-type quantity fixed at 6,000. Part C searches the high-type quantity on the grid 0 to 12,000 in steps of 1,000, with the low-type quantity fixed at 2,000. Part D prints the first-best profit and the gap. Proposition 1.4 predicts \(q_L = 2{,}000\), \(q_H = 6{,}000\), a profit of 6,000,000 cents, a first-best profit of 7,875,000 cents and a gap of 1,875,000 cents.

"""E2 L05 lab 1: a two-type menu of quantity-transfer contracts, with incentive and participation constraints.

Inputs are illustrative, not data. A seller offers a menu of two contracts (q_L, m_L) and (q_H, m_H) to a buyer who
is a low type with value 3,000 cents per unit with probability 3/4, or a high type with value 6,000 cents per unit
with probability 1/4. The buyer's utility is theta * q - m, and the seller's cost of q units is q^2 / 2 cents
(Assumption 2 on the page). Quantities are in units and money is in integer cents.

Part A checks the four constraints at the menu that the page derives: the low type's participation constraint and
the high type's incentive constraint bind, and the other two hold. Part B enumerates the low type's quantity on
the integer grid 0..6000 with the high type's quantity fixed at its first-best value, and finds the profit
maximizer. Part C enumerates the high type's quantity on the grid 0..12000 in steps of 1000 with the low type's
quantity fixed at the menu value. Part D prints the first-best profit and the gap to the menu profit.

Profit is computed as exact Fractions and rounded once, half up, to whole cents for printing.
No random draws are used, so every run prints the same lines.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

THETA_LOW = 3_000  # cents per unit, low type
THETA_HIGH = 6_000  # cents per unit, high type
P_LOW = Fraction(3, 4)
P_HIGH = Fraction(1, 4)


def cost(q: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Seller's cost in cents of q units: q^2 / 2 cents."""
    return q * q / 2


def transfers(q_low: Fraction, q_high: Fraction) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction]:
    """Transfers that bind the low type's participation and the high type's incentive constraint."""
    t_low = THETA_LOW * q_low
    t_high = THETA_HIGH * q_high - (THETA_HIGH - THETA_LOW) * q_low
    return t_low, t_high


def profit(q_low: Fraction, q_high: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Expected seller profit in cents for the binding menu (q_low, q_high)."""
    t_low, t_high = transfers(q_low, q_high)
    return P_LOW * (t_low - cost(q_low)) + P_HIGH * (t_high - cost(q_high))


def cents(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """Round a non-negative exact amount half up to whole cents."""
    return math.floor(x + Fraction(1, 2))


def main() -> None:
    q_low = Fraction(2000)
    q_high = Fraction(6000)
    t_low, t_high = transfers(q_low, q_high)
    u_low = THETA_LOW * q_low - t_low
    u_high = THETA_HIGH * q_high - t_high

    print("Part A: the menu and its four constraints (cents)")
    print(f"low contract: q_L = {q_low}, m_L = {t_low}")
    print(f"high contract: q_H = {q_high}, m_H = {t_high}")
    print(f"low type utility, participation: {u_low} >= 0 holds: {u_low >= 0}")
    print(f"high type utility: {u_high}")
    print(f"high type deviation to the low contract: {THETA_HIGH * q_low - t_low}")
    print(f"high type incentive slack: {u_high - (THETA_HIGH * q_low - t_low)}")
    print(f"low type deviation to the high contract: {THETA_LOW * q_high - t_high}")
    print(f"low type incentive slack: {u_low - (THETA_LOW * q_high - t_high)}")
    print(f"information rent of the high type, (theta_H - theta_L) q_L: {(THETA_HIGH - THETA_LOW) * q_low}")

    print("Part B: low-type quantity on the grid 0..6000, high-type quantity fixed at 6000")
    best_q = max(range(0, 6001), key=lambda q: (profit(Fraction(q), Fraction(6000)), -q))
    print(f"profit maximizing low-type quantity on the grid: {best_q}")
    print(f"profit at that quantity, rounded to cents: {cents(profit(Fraction(best_q), Fraction(6000)))}")

    print("Part C: high-type quantity on the grid 0..12000 in steps of 1000, low-type quantity fixed at 2000")
    grid_high = range(0, 12001, 1000)
    best_high = max(grid_high, key=lambda q: (profit(Fraction(2000), Fraction(q)), -q))
    print(f"profit maximizing high-type quantity on the grid: {best_high}")

    print("Part D: first best against the menu (cents)")
    first_low, first_high = Fraction(THETA_LOW), Fraction(THETA_HIGH)
    first_best = P_LOW * (THETA_LOW * first_low - cost(first_low)) + P_HIGH * (
        THETA_HIGH * first_high - cost(first_high)
    )
    menu_profit = profit(q_low, q_high)
    print(f"first-best quantities: q_L = {first_low}, q_H = {first_high}")
    print(f"first-best expected profit: {cents(first_best)}")
    print(f"menu expected profit: {cents(menu_profit)}")
    print(f"gap, first best minus menu: {cents(first_best - menu_profit)}")


main()

Part A shows that the low type's participation constraint and the high type's incentive constraint bind (slack 0), and that the low type's incentive constraint has slack 12,000,000 cents. Parts B and C show that the best grid points are 2,000 and 6,000, the values Proposition 1.4 gives. 2Distortion, rent and what the menu does not do

Remark 2.1 (the distortion and the rent)

The first best sets each type's quantity where its valuation equals the marginal cost \(C'(q) = q\). That gives \(q_L = 3{,}000\) and \(q_H = 6{,}000\), and full extraction gives the seller \(\theta q - C(q)\) from each type. The first-best expected profit is \(0.75 \times 4{,}500{,}000 + 0.25 \times 18{,}000{,}000 = 7{,}875{,}000\) cents. The menu of Remark 1.5 gives 6,000,000 cents, so the gap is 1,875,000 cents. Of the gap, 375,000 cents comes from the low type: cutting its quantity from 3,000 to 2,000 units loses \(4{,}500{,}000 - 4{,}000{,}000 = 500{,}000\) cents of surplus, weighted by \(p_L = 3/4\). The other 1,500,000 cents is \(p_H\) times the high type's rent of 6,000,000 cents, which the menu leaves to the high type. The cut is chosen where \(p_L(\theta_L - q_L) = p_H(\theta_H - \theta_L)\), which is \(750 = 750\) cents per unit at the optimum (Step 3 of Proposition 1.4). Each unit removed from \(q_L\) lowers the rent by \(\theta_H - \theta_L = 3{,}000\) cents (Lemma 1.3). The high type's quantity is not distorted (Proposition 1.4).

4Where the canon breaks

The model is a monopoly with two types, a quadratic cost and a seller that commits to its menu. Rothschild and Stiglitz (1976), "Equilibrium in Competitive Insurance Markets", QJE 90(4), is cited by its title, year and venue only, for competitive insurance markets under imperfect information; this lesson derives no result of that paper and does not derive the competitive case. With a continuum of types the two-type menu of Proposition 1.4 is not the answer, and this lesson does not derive the continuum case. Assumption 5 fixes the seller's commitment to its menu. The lesson does not analyze a seller that cannot commit.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A menu makes the seller learn each buyer's type.”

    The seller never observes the type. The buyer's choice from the menu reveals it, and the seller uses that choice. In the base case, the low type takes the \((2{,}000, 6{,}000{,}000)\) item and the high type takes the \((6{,}000, 30{,}000{,}000)\) item (Lab 1, Part A).

  2. “The seller can take all the surplus from both types.”

    The low type's participation binds, so its rent is 0. The high type keeps the rent \((\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents, because its incentive constraint binds at the menu for the low type's quantity. A menu that gives the high type zero rent while the low type takes 2,000 units violates (IC-H), by Lemma 1.3.

  3. “The low type gets the first-best quantity, and the seller accepts a lower price for it.”

    The low type's quantity is 2,000 units, which is below the first-best 3,000 units (Remark 1.5). The cut is what lowers the high type's rent, by 3,000 cents per unit (Remark 2.1). Lab 1, Part D prints the 1,875,000 cent gap that results.

6Problem set

Mechanics

L05 · Exclusion at equal probabilities

Take the base-case values but with \(p_H = 1/2\). Find \(q_L^\ast\) from Proposition 1.4, and the seller's expected profit at the menu \((q_L, q_H) = (0, 6{,}000)\) with transfers \(m_L = 0\) and \(m_H = 36{,}000{,}000\) cents.

Solution

With \(p_H / p_L = 1\), \(q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 - 3{,}000 = 0\). The low type is excluded. The high type's transfer is \(m_H = 36{,}000{,}000\) cents, which equals \(\theta_H q_H\). The seller's profit is \(\frac{1}{2}(36{,}000{,}000 - 18{,}000{,}000) = 9{,}000{,}000\) cents, because the low type contributes 0 and the high type's cost is \(6{,}000^2 / 2 = 18{,}000{,}000\) cents.

L05 · Rent for a smaller low-type quantity

In the base case, take the menu with \(q_L = 1{,}000\) and \(q_H = 6{,}000\), with \(m_L = \theta_L q_L\) and \(m_H = \theta_H q_H - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\). Compute \(m_L\), \(m_H\), \(U_H\), and check (IC-L).

Solution

\(m_L = 3{,}000 \times 1{,}000 = 3{,}000{,}000\) cents. \(m_H = 36{,}000{,}000 - 3{,}000{,}000 = 33{,}000{,}000\) cents. \(U_H = 36{,}000{,}000 - 33{,}000{,}000 = 3{,}000{,}000\) cents, which equals \((\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L = 3{,}000{,}000\). For (IC-L): \(U_L = 0\), and \(\theta_L q_H - m_H = 18{,}000{,}000 - 33{,}000{,}000 = -15{,}000{,}000\), so \(0 \ge -15{,}000{,}000\) holds.

L05 · Profit of a menu

In the base case, compute the seller's expected profit at the menu of Problem L05-m2, in cents.

Solution

The low type contributes \(m_L - C(1{,}000) = 3{,}000{,}000 - 500{,}000 = 2{,}500{,}000\) cents. The high type contributes \(m_H - C(6{,}000) = 33{,}000{,}000 - 18{,}000{,}000 = 15{,}000{,}000\) cents. The expected profit is \(0.75 \times 2{,}500{,}000 + 0.25 \times 15{,}000{,}000 = 1{,}875{,}000 + 3{,}750{,}000 = 5{,}625{,}000\) cents, which is below the 6,000,000 cents of Remark 1.5.

L05 · Monotone quantities

Prove that any menu satisfying (IC-L) and (IC-H) has \(q_H \ge q_L\), using only Assumption 1, \(\theta_H > \theta_L\), and the two incentive constraints of Definition 1.2.

Solution

Lemma 1.3 gives the proof. Adding the two incentive constraints gives \((\theta_H - \theta_L)(q_H - q_L) \ge 0\) after the transfers cancel. Since \(\theta_H - \theta_L > 0\), the quantity difference is nonnegative.

L05 · The low type's participation binds

Show that a menu with \(U_L > 0\) can be replaced by one with \(U_L = 0\) and higher profit, without violating any constraint.

Solution

Raise both transfers by \(U_L\). The differences \(m_H - m_L\) are unchanged, so both incentive constraints still hold. The new \(U_L\) is 0. The new \(U_H = U_H^{\text{old}} - U_L \ge (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L \ge 0\) by Lemma 1.3 and Assumption 6. The seller's profit rises by \(U_L\), because both transfers rise by it.

L05 · Concavity of the reduced profit

Show that the reduced profit \(\Pi(q_L, q_H)\) of Proposition 1.4 is concave, and that its stationary point is the maximum.

Solution

Write \(\Pi\) as a sum of a linear term in \(q_L\) and \(q_H\) and the terms \(-p_L q_L^2/2\) and \(-p_H q_H^2/2\). The Hessian is \(\diag(-p_L, -p_H)\), which is negative definite, so \(\Pi\) is strictly concave. A stationary point of a strictly concave function on \(\R^2\) is its unique global maximum.

Empirical

L05 · A lab variant with a different probability

Predict the low-type quantity in Lab 1 if \(p_H = 1/3\) and \(p_L = 2/3\), with the other inputs unchanged. Then change the two probabilities in the twin and run it. No data are used.

Solution

\(p_H / p_L = 1/2\), so \(q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 - 1{,}500 = 1{,}500\). Part B prints the best low-type quantity on the grid, 1,500, and the profit at that quantity with \(q_H = 6{,}000\), which is 6,750,000 cents. Part D's menu is fixed at \(q_L = 2{,}000\) in the twin, so it prints a menu profit of 6,666,667 cents (the exact value 6,666,666.67 rounded half up) and a gap of 2,333,333 cents. Those are the profit and gap at 2,000 units, not at the best menu. At \(q_L = 1{,}500\) the profit is \(\tfrac23 (4{,}500{,}000 - 1{,}125{,}000) + \tfrac13 (31{,}500{,}000 - 18{,}000{,}000) = 6{,}750{,}000\) cents, and the gap to the first best of \(\tfrac23 \cdot 4{,}500{,}000 + \tfrac13 \cdot 18{,}000{,}000 = 9{,}000{,}000\) cents is 2,250,000 cents. A different printed value means either the prediction or the code needs checking.

L05 · The rent as the probability changes

Using Proposition 1.4, compute the high type's rent \(U_H\) for \(p_H = 1/4\) and for \(p_H = 1/3\), with \(\theta_L = 3{,}000\) and \(\theta_H = 6{,}000\).

Solution

For \(p_H = 1/4\), \(q_L^\ast = 2{,}000\), so \(U_H = 3{,}000 \times 2{,}000 = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents. For \(p_H = 1/3\), \(q_L^\ast = 1{,}500\), so \(U_H = 3{,}000 \times 1{,}500 = 4{,}500{,}000\) cents. Since \(p_H / p_L = p_H / (1 - p_H)\) increases with \(p_H\), the formula gives a smaller \(q_L^\ast\) as \(p_H\) rises, and the rent \((\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast\) falls with it. The two values above show this.

L05 · Profit against the first best

Using the base case, compute the first-best expected profit and the menu profit in cents, and the percent loss against the first best, stating its base.

Solution

The first best is 7,875,000 cents and the menu profit is 6,000,000 cents. The loss is 1,875,000 cents. As a percent of the first-best profit, the loss is \(1{,}875{,}000 / 7{,}875{,}000 \approx 23.81\%\), with the base being the first-best profit. As a percent of the menu profit, the first best is \(7{,}875{,}000 / 6{,}000{,}000 - 1 = 31.25\%\) higher.

Research

L05 · Can the high type's rent be removed?

Using Lemma 1.3, show that the high type keeps a positive rent at any menu that satisfies both participation constraints and in which the low type takes a positive quantity, and state what that implies for a seller that wants to remove the rent.

Solution

Lemma 1.3 gives \(U_H \ge U_L + (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L \ge (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L\) when \(U_L \ge 0\), which holds at any menu that satisfies participation. If \(q_L > 0\), the bound is strictly positive, so \(U_H > 0\). Removing the rent would require \(q_L \le 0\), which under Assumption 6 means \(q_L = 0\), and that excludes the low type. The seller therefore trades off the rent against the low type's quantity, and Proposition 1.4 gives the resolution for this model.

L05 · Competition between sellers

State what would change if two sellers compete for the buyer rather than one seller choosing a menu. Say which part of this lesson does not transfer.

Solution

With two sellers, the menu of each must be chosen against the other seller's menu, so the single-seller problem changes. Proposition 1.4 assumes a single seller that commits to its menu (Assumption 5), so it does not transfer. The lesson derives nothing about the competitive case, and the registry source cited in section 4 is cited by title only.

L05 · A continuum of types

State the question a continuum of types raises that the two-type model answers by a finite comparison.

Solution

With a continuum of types there is one incentive constraint for each pair of types, so the finite comparison of two constraints in Lemma 1.3 does not apply directly. The two-type model answers the question by one comparison of two constraints. This lesson does not state the continuum result.

8Retrieval quiz

  1. In the base case, what is the best low-type quantity?

    Options

    \(q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 - \frac{1/4}{3/4} \cdot 3{,}000 = 2{,}000\), and Lab 1, Part B prints 2,000.

  2. In cents, what is the high type's information rent \(U_H\) at the base-case menu?

    \(U_H = (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 \times 2{,}000 = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents (Remark 1.5).

  3. With \(p_H = 1/2\) in the base-case valuations, what is the best low-type quantity?

    Options

    Problem L05-m1: \(p_H / p_L = 1\) gives \(q_L^\ast = 3{,}000 - 3{,}000 = 0\).

  4. In cents, what is the first-best expected profit in the base case?

    \(0.75 \times (9{,}000{,}000 - 4{,}500{,}000) + 0.25 \times (36{,}000{,}000 - 18{,}000{,}000) = 7{,}875{,}000\) cents, which Lab 1, Part D prints.

  5. At the optimal menu, which constraint binds for the high type?

    Options

    Proposition 1.4, Step 2: the high type's incentive constraint holds with equality, and its participation constraint holds with slack.

  6. In cents, what is the high-type transfer \(m_H\) at the base-case menu?

    \(m_H = \theta_H q_H^\ast - (\theta_H - \theta_L) q_L^\ast = 36{,}000{,}000 - 6{,}000{,}000 = 30{,}000{,}000\) cents (Remark 1.5).

    Options

    Step 1 of Proposition 1.4 shows that the best menu has \(U_L = 0\). The transfer \(m_L = \theta_L q_L\) makes the low type's utility zero.

  8. Which statement about the distortion at the base-case optimum is correct?

    Options

    The first best is \(q_L = 3{,}000\) and \(q_H = 6{,}000\). The optimum gives \(q_L = 2{,}000\) and \(q_H = 6{,}000\) (Remark 2.1).
