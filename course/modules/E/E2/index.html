Game Theory & Information Economics

After this module you can state the equilibrium of a strategic situation, prove or refute it, and compute the revenue, efficiency and incentive consequences of a rule for allocating goods under private information.

  • Prerequisites: E1 Microeconomics and M4 Probability and Statistics, with M2 and M1 through them
  • Unlocks: E4 and CF3 (curriculum prerequisites)
  • Project: Treasury auction design, autograder and rubric

Objectives

  1. Compute every pure-strategy Nash equilibrium of a finite two-player game by checking each player's best responses against every alternative action.
  2. Prove that a profile of strictly dominant actions is the only pure equilibrium of a game, and that a game with no pure equilibrium exists.
  3. Derive the symmetric first-price bid \((n-1)/n \cdot v\) and verify that it is a Bayesian–Nash equilibrium of the auction with independent uniform values.
  4. Compute the market-consistent prices of a two-quality market with unobserved quality, and the gains lost to withheld trade.
  5. Check the incentive constraints and participation constraints of a menu of contracts, and of a direct mechanism, by enumerating every misreport.
  6. Compare the revenue, the payments and the allocative efficiency of uniform-price and discriminatory auctions on fixed bid profiles, in integer cents.

Prerequisites

E1 supplies the Cournot and Bertrand models, economic surplus and Pareto efficiency. M4 supplies expectation, conditional expectation and independence. Placement diagnostic

A placement pass means meeting the mastery bar of 0.8 and answering every item marked as a skill item. Items marked as skill items test what the worked example and the completion problem teach, and the other items test the prerequisites.

  1. In a uniform-price auction that sells two identical units, which bid sets the price each winner pays?

    Options

    With \(k = 2\) units, the uniform price is the \((k+1)\)-th highest bid, which is the third-highest bid. The project's worked example applies this rule.

  2. In a discriminatory auction that sells two identical units, what does a winner pay?

    Options

    Under the discriminatory format each winner pays its own bid. The completion problem removes this payment line.

  3. For \(\pi_1 = q_1(a - c - q_1 - q_2)\), which equation is the first-order condition for \(q_1\)?

    Options

    The partial derivative of \(q_1(a - c - q_1 - q_2)\) with respect to \(q_1\) is \(a - c - 2q_1 - q_2\).

  4. A bidder's value is uniform on \([0, 1]\). What is the conditional expectation of the value given that it exceeds 0.5?

    Given \(V > 0.5\), the value is uniform on \([0.5, 1]\), whose mean is \(0.75\).

  5. Which statement is true of a Nash equilibrium of a finite game?

    Options

    A Nash equilibrium is defined by the best-response condition of L01, Definition 1.3. It need not maximize the total payoff.

The project ladder

  1. Worked example

    One solved two-unit, four-bidder auction under both formats, in integer cents, with each step explained.

  2. Completion problem

    The same calculation with the clearing-rank and payment lines removed, graded instantly.

  3. Independent project

    The autograded auction engine and the design memo, graded by autograder and rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open extension with no ceiling: asymmetric bidders, a third format, or a stated hypothesis about how bid shading changes with the number of bidders.
