The Grossman–Stiglitz Paradox

In this model a trader who pays for information gains nothing when the price already reveals the signal. This lesson derives the equilibrium price of a CARA-normal market and the gain from information as a cost, \(G(\lambda)\) (Definition 4.3; Lemma 4.5 for \(G(0)\)). With no noise in the supply of the risky asset, the price is a function of the signal, the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)), and no informed fraction \(\lambda\) (Assumption A3) is an equilibrium at a cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\) (Proposition 5.1(b)). With noise, the price stays partly informative, and equilibria exist outside \((G(1), G(0))\). Inside that interval, existence uses Assumption A5. Every other result is proved from A1 to A3.

1The model

A risky asset pays \(v\) in cash, and a riskless asset has gross return \(1\). The model has three assumptions, A1 to A3. Each is numbered, and each result below states the assumptions it uses. Section 5 adds Assumption A5, which Proposition 5.2(c) uses for existence and which justifies the bisection of Section 5.1.

Assumption A1 (preferences)

Each trader has constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) utility (Definition 6.2 of Lesson L06), \(U(w) = -\exp(-a w)\) with \(a > 0\), over terminal wealth \(w\). Each trader starts with wealth \(W_0\) and no shares of the risky asset. A trader who buys \(x\) shares at price \(p\) and holds the rest in the riskless asset ends with \[ W = W_0 + x\,(v - p) \quad\text{or}\quad W = W_0 - c + x\,(v - p), \] where the second form applies to an informed trader, who pays the cost \(c \ge 0\) in wealth. Since \(U'(w) = a\exp(-aw)\) and \(U''(w) = -a^2\exp(-aw)\), the Arrow–Pratt coefficient of absolute risk aversion \(-U''/U'\) equals \(a\) at every \(w\).

Assumption A2 (normal information and noise)

The random variables \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\) are independent, with \(v \sim \Normal(\bar v, \sigma_v^2)\), \(\varepsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\). Here \(\sigma_v^2 > 0\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 \ge 0\). The signal of Lesson L06 is \(s = v + \varepsilon\). The variable \(u\) is the noise-trader supply: the net number of shares of the risky asset that noise traders (Lesson L06) bring to the market. Noise traders are not modelled as optimisers. Their supply \(u\) is an exogenous normal shock.

Assumption A3 (price taking and market clearing)

A continuum of traders takes the price \(p\) as given. A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) of them is informed: each observes the signal \(s\) and pays \(c\). The rest are uninformed: each observes only \(p\). Let \(x_I\) and \(x_U\) be the demands of an informed and an uninformed trader. Market clearing requires \[ \lambda\, x_I + (1 - \lambda)\, x_U = u \qquad\text{in every state.} \]

Assumption A3 is price taking in the sense of the competitive equilibrium concept linked in the header: no single trader's demand moves the price. The symbols used below are local to this lesson. Write \(\tau_v = 1/\sigma_v^2\) and \(\tau_\varepsilon = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) for precisions. The measure of price informativeness is the squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p)^2\), defined in Definition 7.2 of Lesson L07.

Example 1.1 (the parameters of Lab 1)

Set \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_v^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\), and \(\bar v = 0\). These values satisfy A1 to A3. The prior mean \(\bar v\) does not enter any gain or any informativeness measure, because each of them depends on the variances only. Lemma 4.5 (Section 4) gives \(G(0)\) at these values, and Lab 1 prints \(G(1)\).

2Demands and the value of information

Lemma 2.1 (CARA demand)

Assumptions: A1. Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be an information set such that, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(m, V)\) with \(V > 0\), and the price \(p\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable. Let \(W_0'\) be a constant. The conditional expected utility \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}]\) is maximised over \(x\) at \[ x^\ast = \frac{m - p}{a V}, \] and its maximal value is \(-\exp(-a W_0')\,\exp\bigl(-(m - p)^2 / (2V)\bigr)\).

Proof

Given \(\mathcal{I}\), the variable \(-a x (v - p)\) is normal with mean \(-a x (m - p)\) and variance \(a^2 x^2 V\). The moment generating function of a normal variable gives \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-a x (v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}\bigr] = \exp\bigl(-a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\bigr). \] Hence \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}] = -\exp(-a W_0')\exp(f(x))\), where \(f(x) = -a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\). The prefactor is negative, so maximising the expected utility means minimising \(f\). The function \(f\) is a quadratic in \(x\) with leading coefficient \(a^2 V / 2 > 0\). Its derivative \(f'(x) = -a(m - p) + a^2 x V\) vanishes only at \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV)\), so \(x^\ast\) is the unique minimiser. Substituting gives \(f(x^\ast) = -a x^\ast (m - p) + a^2 (x^\ast)^2 V / 2 = -(m-p)^2/V + (m-p)^2/(2V) = -(m - p)^2/(2V)\).

Lemma 2.2 (a normal moment)

Assumptions: none beyond the statement. Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma^2 \ge 0\), and let \(V > 0\). Then \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-Z^2/(2V))\bigr] = \bigl(1 + \sigma^2/V\bigr)^{-1/2}. \]

Proof

If \(\sigma^2 = 0\), then \(Z = 0\) almost surely and both sides equal \(1\). If \(\sigma^2 > 0\), write the density of \(Z\) and combine the exponents: \[ \int_{\R} (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{x^2}{2V}\Bigr)\,dx = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \int_{\R} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2}\bigl(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1}\bigr)\Bigr)\,dx. \] The Gaussian integral equals \(\sqrt{2\pi/(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})}\). Multiplying gives \(\bigl(\sigma^2(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})\bigr)^{-1/2} = (1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2}\).

Fact 2.3 (normal conditioning)

Assumptions: \((x, y)\) is jointly normal and \(\Var(y) > 0\). Then \(\E[x \mid y] = \E x + \beta_{xy}\,(y - \E y)\) with \(\beta_{xy} = \Cov(x, y)/\Var(y)\), and \(\Var(x \mid y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\).

Proof

Let \(r = x - \E x - \beta_{xy}(y - \E y)\). Then \(\Cov(r, y) = \Cov(x, y) - \beta_{xy}\Var(y) = 0\), so \(r\) and \(y\) are uncorrelated. Since \((r, y)\) is jointly normal, they are independent. Therefore \(\E[r \mid y] = \E r = 0\) and \(\Var(r \mid y) = \Var(r)\). Writing \(x = \E x + \beta_{xy}(y - \E y) + r\) gives the conditional mean, and \(\Var(r) = \Var(x) - \beta_{xy}^2\Var(y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\) gives the conditional variance.

Corollary 2.4 (conditional laws of the two groups)

Assumptions: A1 to A3. The posterior of \(v\) (Lesson L06) given an information set is its conditional law given that set. Write \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).

  1. \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s (s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = V_I\).
  2. Let \(\kappa' \in \R\) and let \(\eta = \varepsilon - \kappa' u\), so that \(y = s - \kappa' u = v + \eta\). Let \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa'^2 \sigma_u^2\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\eta^2\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\E[v \mid y] = \bar v + b_y (y - \bar v)\) with \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\), and \(\Var(v \mid y) = V_U\).
Proof

(a) Here \(\Cov(v, s) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\), so Fact 2.3 gives \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + (\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2))(s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).

(b) The pair \((v, y)\) is jointly normal, because \(y\) is a linear combination of \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\). Here \(\Cov(v, y) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(y) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\) by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\) to get \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\Var(v \mid y) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\eta^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).

The uninformed trader observes \(p\), not \(y\). Section 3 shows that \(p\) is an invertible affine function of \(y\) in equilibrium, so \(\sigma(p) = \sigma(y)\) and Corollary 2.4(b) applies to \(p\) as well.

Lemma 2.5 (what the informed trader knows)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) for constants \(\alpha\), \(\beta_p\) and \(\varpi\). Let \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(s, p)\). Then, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\).

Proof

Let \(m(s) = \E[v \mid s]\) and \(r_v = v - m(s)\). By Fact 2.3 applied to \((v, s)\), \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\). It is also independent of \(u\), because \(u\) is independent of \((v, s)\). Since \(u\) is independent of the pair \((r_v, s)\) and \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\), the three variables are mutually independent, so \(r_v\) is independent of \((s, u)\). The variable \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so \(\sigma(s, p) \subseteq \sigma(s, u)\), and \(r_v\) is independent of \(\mathcal{I}\). Since \(s\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable, \(v = m(s) + r_v\) has conditional law \(\Normal(m(s), \Var(r_v))\) given \(\mathcal{I}\), and \(\Var(r_v) = V_I\) by Corollary 2.4(a).

3The equilibrium price

Proposition 3.1 (the linear equilibrium price)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2)\), \(A = \lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A)\).

  1. Suppose \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). If \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) with \(\beta_p \ne 0\) clears the market in every state, then \(\varpi/\beta_p = \kappa\), \(\alpha = (1 - \beta)\bar v\) and \(\beta_p = \beta\).
  2. The price \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\) clears the market in every state.
Proof

Let \(\kappa' = \varpi/\beta_p\), and let \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) be defined from \(\kappa'\) as \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\) are defined from \(\kappa\). By Corollary 2.4(b) with \(\kappa'\), the uninformed trader's posterior mean of \(v\) is \(\bar v + b_y'(y - \bar v)\), where \(y = (p - \alpha)/\beta_p = s - \kappa' u\), and their posterior variance is \(V_U' = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta')\). By Corollary 2.4(a) the informed posterior mean is \(\bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\). Multiply the clearing condition of A3 by \(a\), and use \(x_I = (\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)(\E[v \mid s] - p)/a\) and \(x_U = (\tau_v + \tau_\eta')(\E[v \mid y] - p)/a\). Then use \((\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) b_s = \tau_\varepsilon\), \((\tau_v + \tau_\eta') b_y' = \tau_\eta'\), and \(\lambda(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) + (1 - \lambda)(\tau_v + \tau_\eta') = \tau_v + A'\): \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa' u = a u, \] where the term in \(\kappa' u\) comes from \(y - \bar v = (s - \bar v) - \kappa' u\). Rearranged, \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) = \bigl(a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa'\bigr) u. \qquad(\ast) \] Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\). Both sides are affine in \((s, u)\). The pair \((s, u)\) has a density that is positive on \(\R^2\), because \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So an affine function that vanishes almost surely is identically zero, and the coefficients match:
constant term: \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha) = A' \bar v\), so \(\alpha = \tau_v \bar v/(\tau_v + A') = (1 - \beta')\bar v\);
coefficient of \(s\): \(A' = (\tau_v + A')\beta_p\), so \(\beta_p = \beta'\);
coefficient of \(u\): \((\tau_v + A')\varpi = a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta'\kappa'\). Since \(\varpi = \kappa'\beta_p = \kappa'\beta'\) and \((\tau_v + A')\beta' = A'\), the left side is \(\kappa' A'\). So \(\kappa'(A' - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta') = a\). The bracket is \(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon\), so \(\kappa' = a/(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = \kappa\).
With \(\kappa' = \kappa\), the quantities \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) equal \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\). This proves (a). Each step of the argument is an equivalence, so running it backwards from \(\kappa' = \kappa\) gives (b).

Corollary 3.2 (price informativeness)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\) of Proposition 3.1. Then \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\tau_\eta}{\tau_v + \tau_\eta} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2 \sigma_u^2}. \]

Proof

The price is \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\). Therefore \(\Cov(v, p^\ast) = \beta\sigma_v^2\), and, because \(s\) and \(u\) are independent, \[ \Var(p^\ast) = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2 = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \] where \(\sigma_\eta^2 = 1/\tau_\eta = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\). Hence \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\beta^2\sigma_v^4}{\sigma_v^2 \cdot \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2}. \] The last expression equals \(\tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) after multiplying by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\), as in Corollary 2.4(b). The factor \(\beta\) cancels because \(\beta > 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\).

Lemma 3.3 (informativeness in the informed fraction)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).

  1. \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\).
  2. If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\lambda \mapsto \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\).
  3. If \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), then \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).
Proof

(a) \(\tau_\eta > 0\) and \(\tau_v > 0\), so \(0 < \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) < 1\). (b) As \(\lambda\) rises, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) falls strictly. If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls strictly, so \(\tau_\eta\) rises strictly. The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), since its derivative is \(\tau_v/(\tau_v + t)^2 > 0\). (c) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = \tau_\varepsilon\) for every \(\lambda\), and \(\tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\).

4The gain from information

Proposition 4.1 (the demand gaps and their variances)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\). Define the demand gaps \(D_I = \E[v \mid s] - p^\ast\) and \(D_U = \E[v \mid p^\ast] - p^\ast\). Then \(\E D_I = \E D_U = 0\), and \[ \Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2, \qquad \Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \] where \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).

Proof

Using \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\), \[ D_I = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v) - (1 - \beta)\bar v - \beta s + \kappa\beta u = (b_s - \beta)(s - \bar v) + \kappa\beta u. \] Its mean is zero. The variables \(s\) and \(u\) are independent, so \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2\Var(s) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2\), with \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\).
For the uninformed, let \(y = (p^\ast - (1 - \beta)\bar v)/\beta\). Then \(y - \bar v = (p^\ast - \bar v)/\beta\), and \(y - \bar v = (v - \bar v) + \eta\), so \(\Var(y - \bar v) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(\E[v \mid p^\ast] = \bar v + b_y(y - \bar v)\). Therefore \(D_U = b_y(y - \bar v) - \beta(y - \bar v) = (b_y - \beta)(y - \bar v)\), which has mean zero and variance \((b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\).

Proposition 4.2 (expected utilities of the two groups)

Assumptions: A1 to A3 and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(EU_I\) be the expected utility of an informed trader who chooses demand optimally given \((s, p^\ast)\), net of the cost \(c\). Let \(EU_U\) be the expected utility of an uninformed trader who chooses demand optimally given \(p^\ast\). Then \[ EU_I = -\exp\bigl(-a(W_0 - c)\bigr)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I\bigr)^{-1/2}, \qquad EU_U = -\exp(-aW_0)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U\bigr)^{-1/2}, \] where \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).

Proof

For the informed trader, Lemma 2.5 gives the conditional law of \(v\) given \((s, p^\ast)\) as \(\Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\). Lemma 2.1 with \(m = \E[v \mid s]\), \(V = V_I\) and \(W_0' = W_0 - c\) gives the optimal conditional utility \(-\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-(\E[v \mid s] - p^\ast)^2/(2V_I)) = -\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-D_I^2/(2V_I))\). Taking the expectation over \((s, u)\) and applying Lemma 2.2 to \(D_I \sim \Normal(0, \Var(D_I))\) gives the first formula. The uninformed case is the same with \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(p^\ast)\). Here \(\sigma(p^\ast) = \sigma(y)\), so Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(v \mid p^\ast \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid y], V_U)\), and Lemma 2.2 applies to \(D_U\).

Definition 4.3 (gain from information)

For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), the gain from information is \[ G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\!\left(\frac{1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I}{1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U}\right), \] and the net benefit of information at cost \(c\) is \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\). The gain \(G(\lambda)\) is the largest cost at which an informed trader is at least as well off as an uninformed one. Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0)\) for the case with no informed trader.

Proposition 4.4 (the indifference condition)

Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and \(c \ge 0\). Then \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(\lambda)\). Equality holds exactly when \(c = G(\lambda)\).

Proof

Write \(X = (1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)^{-1/2}\) and \(Y = (1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)^{-1/2}\). By Proposition 4.2, \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) reads \(-\exp(-aW_0)\exp(ac)X \ge -\exp(-aW_0)Y\). Dividing by \(-\exp(-aW_0) < 0\) reverses the inequality: \(\exp(ac)X \le Y\). Taking logarithms, \(ac + \ln X \le \ln Y\). Here \(\ln Y - \ln X = \tfrac12\ln\bigl((1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)/(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)\bigr) = aG(\lambda)\). So the condition is \(ac \le aG(\lambda)\), that is \(c \le G(\lambda)\), because \(a > 0\). Equality is the same computation with equalities.

Lemma 4.5 (no informed trader)

Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\lambda = 0\). Then \(p^\ast = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), and the gain from becoming informed is \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\Bigl(1 + \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}\Bigr). \] This value does not depend on \(\sigma_u^2\).

Proof

With \(\lambda = 0\), the uninformed trader's posterior of \(v\) is the prior, because \(p\) is a function of \(u\), which is independent of \(v\). Lemma 2.1 gives demand \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2)\). Market clearing \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2) = u\) gives \(p = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\).
A deviating informed trader's posterior mean is \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\), so \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\) and \(\Var(D_I) = b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed gap is \(D_U = \bar v - p = a\sigma_v^2 u\), so \(\Var(D_U) = a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\), and \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\).
Let \(X_0 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) and \(q = \sigma_v^2/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). Then \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = X_0\). Using \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(1/V_I = (\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), we get \(b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/V_I = \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2) = q\). Also \(a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2/V_I = X_0(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = X_0(1 + q)\). The numerator is therefore \(1 + q + X_0(1 + q) = (1 + q)(1 + X_0)\). Hence \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\frac{(1 + q)(1 + X_0)}{1 + X_0} = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1 + q). \]

The formula of Definition 4.3 also gives \(G(1)\), the advantage of an informed trader over an uninformed deviator at the price that prevails when every trader is informed. The three cases of Definition 4.6 concern the disjoint sets \(\{0\}\), \(\{1\}\) and \((0, 1)\), so they cannot overlap.

Definition 4.6 (equilibrium informed fraction)

A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\) when no trader wants to switch:

  1. \(\lambda = 0\) and \(G(0) \le c\), so informing is not worth its cost to a deviator;
  2. \(\lambda = 1\) and \(G(1) \ge c\), so staying informed is worth its cost to each informed trader;
  3. \(0 < \lambda < 1\) and \(G(\lambda) = c\), so informed and uninformed traders are equally well off.

Case (iii) is the indifference condition of Proposition 4.4 with equality. Case (ii) follows from Proposition 4.4 at \(\lambda = 1\), which lies in \((0, 1]\): a trader informed at \(\lambda = 1\) is weakly better off than an uninformed trader when \(c \le G(1)\). Case (i) is not an instance of Proposition 4.4 as stated, because \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) is undefined at \(\lambda = 0\). It follows from Lemma 4.5. Its proof gives the demand gaps at \(\lambda = 0\): the deviator's gap \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\), with variance \(\Var(D_I)\) and posterior variance \(V_I\), and the uninformed gap \(D_U = a\sigma_v^2 u\), with \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\). Lemma 2.5 holds with \(\beta_p = 0\), because its proof uses only that \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so the proof of Proposition 4.2 gives the expected utilities at \(\lambda = 0\). The algebra of Proposition 4.4 then gives \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(0)\), so a deviator at \(\lambda = 0\) is weakly worse off when \(c \ge G(0)\).

5The paradox, and solving for the informed fraction

Definition (the Grossman–Stiglitz paradox)

In a model of this kind, the Grossman–Stiglitz paradox is the conflict between two facts. A price that reveals the signal gives the uninformed the value of the information, so no one pays for it. A price that reveals nothing leaves the informed a gain that can cover a positive cost. Proposition 5.1 states the conflict for the noiseless case, and Proposition 5.2 states its resolution with noise.

Proposition 5.1 (the noiseless case)

Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\). Then the following hold.

  1. For every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), the price is the affine function \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\) of the signal, and \(G(\lambda) = 0\).
  2. If \(0 < c < G(0)\), then no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
  3. If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
Proof

(a) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 0\), so \(\tau_\eta = \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(A = \tau_\varepsilon\). Then \(\beta = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\), and \(\kappa\beta u = 0\), so \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\). Also \(b_y = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\) by the formula of Corollary 2.4(b), so \(b_y - \beta = 0\). Then \(\Var(D_U) = 0\), and \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - b_s)^2(\cdot) + 0 = 0\). Hence \(G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1) = 0\). Corollary 3.2 gives the informativeness.
(b) If \(0 < c < G(0)\): case (i) fails, because \(G(0) \le c\) is false. Case (ii) fails, because \(G(1) = 0 < c\). Case (iii) fails for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 \ne c\).
(c) If \(c \ge G(0) > 0\), case (i) holds at \(\lambda = 0\). Case (ii) fails at \(\lambda = 1\), because \(G(1) = 0 < c\), and case (iii) fails for \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\).

Part (a) of Proposition 5.1 is the paradox in this model. When the supply of the risky asset carries no noise, the price \(p^\ast\) is a function of \(s\), so an uninformed trader who observes the price has the signal as well. The information then has no value, so no informed fraction is an equilibrium at a cost between zero and \(G(0)\). Part (b) states this as a theorem: for every cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), no fraction works. Part (c) states that at or above \(G(0)\) the only equilibrium is \(\lambda = 0\), with a price that carries no information.

Proposition 5.2 (the noisy case)

Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\).

  1. \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} G(\lambda) = G(0)\).
  2. If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\). If \(c \le G(1)\), then \(\lambda = 1\) is one.
  3. Assume A5 below. If \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), there is an equilibrium informed fraction \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) with \(G(\lambda^\ast) = c\). On the interval \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\) and \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\) (Lemma 3.3).
Assumption A5 (sign change, the bisection's invariant)

For every bracket \([\ell, h]\) with \(0 \le \ell < h \le 1\), \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) implies that \(N\) has a zero in \([\ell, h]\). This is assumed here. The page does not prove it from earlier results, and Lab 1 uses it only through the bisection rule below.

Proof

(a) Write \(\lambda \downarrow 0\). Then \(\kappa \to \infty\), and \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 \to \infty\) because \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) \to 0\), and \(\tau_\eta \kappa \to 0\). The identity \(\kappa\lambda\tau_\varepsilon = a\) gives \(\kappa A = a + (1 - \lambda)\kappa\tau_\eta \to a\). Therefore \(\kappa\beta = \kappa A/(\tau_v + A) \to a/\tau_v = a\sigma_v^2\), and \(\beta \to 0\). Hence \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + (\kappa\beta)^2\sigma_u^2 \to b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\).
For the uninformed term, \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\), and \(\tau_\eta - A = \lambda(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)\). So \[ (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta) = \frac{\tau_v^2(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)^2}{(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)^2(\tau_v + A)^2}\bigl(\lambda^2\sigma_v^2 + \lambda^2/\tau_\eta\bigr). \] Now \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \lambda^2\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2 \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\). Also \(\tau_\eta \to 0\) and \(A \to 0\), so the displayed fraction tends to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4 = \tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^2\). The limit of \(\Var(D_U)\) is therefore \(\tau_\varepsilon^2 a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2/\tau_v^2 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), using \(\tau_\varepsilon\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\). Since \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) \to \sigma_v^2\), \(\Var(D_U)/V_U \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\). Substituting these limits into Definition 4.3 gives the value of Lemma 4.5, which is \(G(0)\).
(b) If \(c \ge G(0)\), case (i) of Definition 4.6 holds. If \(c \le G(1)\), case (ii) holds.
(c) The formulas of Section 3 are continuous on \((0, 1]\), so \(G\) is continuous there, and \(N\) is continuous on \((0, 1]\) and extends continuously to \(0\) by (a). If \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), then \(N(0) = G(0) - c > 0 > G(1) - c = N(1)\), and A5 gives a zero \(\lambda^\ast\) of \(N\) in \([0, 1]\). The zero is not \(0\) or \(1\), because \(N(0) \ne 0\) and \(N(1) \ne 0\). So \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) satisfies case (iii). The informativeness statement is Lemma 3.3.

Proposition 5.2 resolves the paradox within this model. With noise, the price reveals the signal only up to the error \(\kappa u\), whose variance \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls as \(\lambda\) rises. The uninformed therefore learn from the price, but imperfectly, and the informed keep an advantage. The price stays partly informative, and the cost restricts the equilibrium informed fraction. Costs strictly between \(G(1)\) and \(G(0)\) give interior equilibria under A5. Costs at or above \(G(0)\) give \(\lambda = 0\), and costs at or below \(G(1)\) give \(\lambda = 1\). The interior equilibrium is not claimed to be unique. Lab 1 checks monotonicity only on its grid.

5.1 Solving for the informed fraction

The lab solves \(N(\lambda) = 0\) on the interval \((0, 1)\) by bisection. The boundary rule is applied first. If \(c \ge G(0)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 0\). If \(c \le G(1)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 1\). Otherwise the bracket \([0, 1]\) has \(N(0) > 0 > N(1)\), and the bisection keeps the invariant \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) on the bracket \([\ell, h]\). Each step evaluates \(N\) at the midpoint. It replaces \(\ell\) if \(N\) is positive there and \(h\) otherwise, and it stops when \(h - \ell \le 10^{-12}\). By A5 the bracket contains a zero. The gap printed for each cost is \(N(\lambda^\ast)\) at the reported point, so a small gap is evidence that the reported point is an equilibrium to the printed precision.

"""E4 L08 lab 1: the informed fraction in a CARA-normal model with costly information, by bisection.

The parameters are illustrative constants, not data: CARA coefficient a = 2, prior variance of the payoff
sigma_v^2 = 1, signal-noise variance sigma_eps^2 = 1, noise-trader supply variance sigma_u^2 = 1, prior mean 0.
Parts B to D evaluate exact population formulas in double precision. Part A uses seeded draws and is SIMULATED.
The informed fraction lam lies in [0, 1]. The price is p = (1 - beta) * prior mean + beta * s - kappa * beta * u,
with kappa = a * sigma_eps^2 / lam, where s = v + eps is the signal and u is the noise-trader supply.
The gain from information is the cost at which an informed and an uninformed trader are equally well off.
Part C solves gain(lam) = cost by bisection on (0, 1) to a tolerance of 1e-12 on lam. The boundary rule is:
lam = 0 when gain(0) <= cost, lam = 1 when gain(1) >= cost, and the bisection root otherwise.
The gap printed for each cost is gain(lam) - cost at the reported lam (the net benefit of information).
Standard library only; no network access.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import itertools
import math
import random

CARA = 2.0
VAR_V = 1.0
VAR_EPS = 1.0
VAR_U = 1.0
PRIOR_MEAN = 0.0
COSTS: tuple[float, ...] = (0.12, 0.13, 0.14, 0.15, 0.16, 0.17, 0.18)
TOL = 1e-12
SEED = 20261103
DRAWS = 20000
GRID = 999


def equilibrium(lam: float, var_u: float = VAR_U) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Price coefficients and the gain from information at informed fraction lam in (0, 1].

    var_u is the variance of the noise-trader supply. The returned dictionary holds beta (the weight of the signal in
    the price), kappa (the noise coefficient divided by beta), corr2 (the squared correlation between payoff and price)
    and gain (the cost at which an informed and an uninformed trader are equally well off).
    """
    if not 0.0 < lam <= 1.0:
        raise ValueError("lam must lie in (0, 1]")
    tau_v = 1.0 / VAR_V
    tau_e = 1.0 / VAR_EPS
    kappa = CARA * VAR_EPS / lam
    tau_eta = 1.0 / (VAR_EPS + kappa * kappa * var_u)
    a_w = lam * tau_e + (1.0 - lam) * tau_eta
    beta = a_w / (tau_v + a_w)
    b_s = tau_e / (tau_v + tau_e)
    b_y = tau_eta / (tau_v + tau_eta)
    post_s = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_e)
    post_u = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_eta)
    noise_term = (kappa * beta) ** 2 * var_u
    var_informed = (b_s - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + VAR_EPS) + noise_term
    var_uninformed = (b_y - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + 1.0 / tau_eta)
    gain = math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + var_uninformed / post_u)) / (2.0 * CARA)
    return {
        "beta": beta,
        "kappa": kappa,
        "corr2": b_y,
        "gain": gain,
    }


def gain_at_zero(var_u: float = VAR_U) -> float:
    """Gain from becoming informed when no trader is informed; the price is prior mean minus CARA VAR_V u."""
    total = VAR_V + VAR_EPS
    b_s = VAR_V / total
    post_s = 1.0 / (1.0 / VAR_V + 1.0 / VAR_EPS)
    noise = CARA * CARA * VAR_V * VAR_V * var_u
    var_informed = b_s * b_s * total + noise
    return math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + noise / VAR_V)) / (2.0 * CARA)


def informativeness(lam: float) -> float:
    """Squared correlation between payoff and price at lam; zero when no trader is informed."""
    if lam == 0.0:
        return 0.0
    return equilibrium(lam)["corr2"]


def part_a() -> None:
    """Seeded draws at lam = 0.5: clearing residual below 1e-12, and the sample slope within 0.02 of the formula."""
    lam = 0.5
    eq = equilibrium(lam)
    beta, kappa = eq["beta"], eq["kappa"]
    tau_v = 1.0 / VAR_V
    tau_e = 1.0 / VAR_EPS
    tau_eta = 1.0 / (VAR_EPS + kappa * kappa * VAR_U)
    b_s = tau_e / (tau_v + tau_e)
    b_y = tau_eta / (tau_v + tau_eta)
    post_s = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_e)
    post_u = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_eta)
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    sd_v = math.sqrt(VAR_V)
    sd_e = math.sqrt(VAR_EPS)
    sd_u = math.sqrt(VAR_U)
    worst = 0.0
    prices: list[float] = []
    payoffs: list[float] = []
    for _ in range(DRAWS):
        v = PRIOR_MEAN + rng.gauss(0.0, sd_v)
        eps = rng.gauss(0.0, sd_e)
        u = rng.gauss(0.0, sd_u)
        s = v + eps
        p = (1.0 - beta) * PRIOR_MEAN + beta * s - kappa * beta * u
        demand_i = (PRIOR_MEAN + b_s * (s - PRIOR_MEAN) - p) / (CARA * post_s)
        mean_u = PRIOR_MEAN + (b_y / beta) * (p - PRIOR_MEAN)
        demand_u = (mean_u - p) / (CARA * post_u)
        worst = max(worst, abs(lam * demand_i + (1.0 - lam) * demand_u - u))
        prices.append(p)
        payoffs.append(v)
    n = float(DRAWS)
    mean_p = sum(prices) / n
    mean_v = sum(payoffs) / n
    cov = sum((v - mean_v) * (p - mean_p) for v, p in zip(payoffs, prices, strict=True)) / n
    var_p = sum((p - mean_p) ** 2 for p in prices) / n
    slope = cov / var_p
    formula = b_y / beta
    print(f"part A (SIMULATED draws): lam = {lam}, seed = {SEED}, draws = {DRAWS}")
    print(f"part A: largest market-clearing residual = {worst:.3e} (tolerance 1e-12)")
    print(f"part A: slope of payoff on price, sample = {slope:.4f}, formula b_y / beta = {formula:.4f}")
    print(f"part A: absolute difference = {abs(slope - formula):.4f} (at most 0.02)")
    if worst > 1e-12:
        raise AssertionError("market clearing fails beyond the stated tolerance")
    if abs(slope - formula) > 0.02:
        raise AssertionError("sample slope differs from the formula beyond the stated tolerance")


def part_b() -> None:
    """Closed-form gains at the boundaries (double precision) and monotonicity of gain and informativeness on a grid."""
    g0 = gain_at_zero()
    g1 = equilibrium(1.0)["gain"]
    gmid = equilibrium(0.5)["gain"]
    print(f"part B: gain(0) = {g0:.6f}  gain(0.5) = {gmid:.6f}  gain(1) = {g1:.6f}")
    near = equilibrium(1e-6)["gain"] - g0
    print(f"part B: gain(1e-6) - gain(0) = {near:.2e} (continuity at zero with noise supply)")
    lams = [k / (GRID + 1) for k in range(1, GRID + 1)]
    gains = [equilibrium(x)["gain"] for x in lams]
    corrs = [equilibrium(x)["corr2"] for x in lams]
    dec_gain = all(b < a for a, b in itertools.pairwise(gains))
    inc_corr = all(b > a for a, b in itertools.pairwise(corrs))
    print(f"part B: gain strictly decreasing on {GRID} interior grid points: {dec_gain}")
    print(f"part B: Corr(v,p)^2 strictly increasing on the same grid: {inc_corr}")
    if not (dec_gain and inc_corr):
        raise AssertionError("monotonicity fails on the grid")


def solve_fraction(cost: float) -> tuple[float, float, str]:
    """Return the informed fraction, the net benefit gain(lam) - cost there, and the rule that applied."""
    g0 = gain_at_zero()
    if cost >= g0:
        return 0.0, g0 - cost, "zero"
    g1 = equilibrium(1.0)["gain"]
    if cost <= g1:
        return 1.0, g1 - cost, "one"
    lo, hi = 0.0, 1.0
    while hi - lo > TOL:
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        if equilibrium(mid)["gain"] - cost > 0.0:
            lo = mid
        else:
            hi = mid
    lam = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
    return lam, equilibrium(lam)["gain"] - cost, "bisection"


def part_c() -> None:
    """The informed fraction, the gap and the informativeness across the cost grid."""
    previous = 2.0
    for cost in COSTS:
        lam, gap, rule = solve_fraction(cost)
        corr = informativeness(lam)
        label = {"zero": "boundary: cost >= gain(0)", "one": "boundary: cost <= gain(1)", "bisection": "root"}[rule]
        print(f"part C: cost = {cost:.2f}  lam* = {lam:.9f}  gap = {gap:.2e}  Corr(v,p)^2 = {corr:.6f}  ({label})")
        if lam > previous:
            raise AssertionError("the informed fraction rises with cost on the grid")
        previous = lam

    zero_is_equilibrium = g0 <= cost
    one_is_equilibrium = g1 >= cost
    print(f"part D: sigma_u^2 = 0: largest |gain| on the (0, 1) grid = {worst:.1e}")
    print(f"part D: sigma_u^2 = 0: gain(0) = {g0:.6f}, gain(1) = {g1:.6f}, cost = {cost:.2f}")

    print(f"part D: sigma_u^2 = 0: lam = 1 is an equilibrium: {one_is_equilibrium}")
    if interior_roots or zero_is_equilibrium or one_is_equilibrium:
        raise AssertionError("an equilibrium exists in the noiseless case at this cost")

6Where the canon breaks

  • The closed forms need CARA utility and normal variables. Lemma 2.1 uses the normal moment generating function, and Lemma 2.2 uses the normal density. The formulas of Sections 2 to 4 are derived for CARA utility and normal variables. The page makes no claim for other utilities or distributions.
  • Noise traders are exogenous. The shock \(u\) is a normal supply that does not respond to prices or information. Proposition 5.1(b) gives no equilibrium with informed traders for \(0 < c < G(0)\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), and Proposition 5.2 gives one under A5 when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). That contrast holds for this specification, in which \(u\) is exogenous. Models in which noise traders optimise are outside this lesson.
  • Price taking is an assumption. Assumption A3 says no trader moves the price, and Proposition 3.1 uses that assumption. With a finite number of traders the derivation does not apply, and the page does not derive the equilibrium of that game.
  • The interior root is assumed to exist and is not shown to be unique. Assumption A5 gives a zero of \(N\) for each bracket with a sign change. Lab 1 checks that \(G\) falls on its grid of 999 points, and nothing on the page proves that \(G\) falls on \((0, 1)\) for every parameter choice.
  • The cost is in wealth. The cost \(c\) reduces terminal wealth by the same amount for every trader and every state. A cost in utility units, or a cost that depends on the signal, is outside the model of Proposition 4.4, which takes the cost as a reduction of terminal wealth.

7Misconceptions

  1. “Noise traders only hurt the informed, so noise makes information less valuable.”

    Proposition 5.1 shows the opposite at the margin. Without noise, \(G(\lambda) = 0\) for every \(\lambda > 0\), because the price reveals the signal to the uninformed. With noise (Lab 1, part B), \(G(1) = \ln(5/3)/4 > 0\). Noise is what makes the price less informative, and so it is what gives an informed trader something to gain.

  2. “Information is never worth buying in this model, so the informed fraction is zero.”

    At cost \(c\) below \(G(0) = \ln(2)/4\), Lab 1 gives a positive informed fraction for every cost on its grid from \(0.12\) to \(0.17\), and the value at cost \(0.15\) is \(\lambda^\ast \approx 0.658\). Information is worth buying when its cost is below the gain, and in Lab 1 the bisection root falls as the cost rises.

  3. “In equilibrium the price reveals the payoff, so the informed have no advantage.”

    The squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly below one (Lemma 3.3(a)). Lab 1 prints values from \(0.013\) to \(0.167\) at the costs where some trader is informed. Partial informativeness alone does not give the informed an advantage. Without noise the price is also partly informative, with \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), and the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)). The advantage comes from the noise in the price.

  4. “A higher cost should induce more traders to become informed.”

    The net benefit \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) falls with \(c\). Lab 1 part C shows the informed fraction falling from \(1\) at cost \(0.12\) to \(0\) at cost \(0.18\), with interior values in between. The fraction falls as the cost rises on the grid, and the grid does not show this for other costs.

Mechanics

Mech 1 · The noise coefficient at one fraction

With \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\) and \(\lambda = 1/2\), find \(\kappa\), and find \(\tau_\eta\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\).

Solution

From Proposition 3.1, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2 \cdot 1/(1/2) = 4\). Then \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 16\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + 16) = 1/17\). The variance of the noise \(\eta\) in the signal that the uninformed extract is \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 17\).

Mech 2 · Demand of one trader

An informed trader has posterior mean \(m = 1\), posterior variance \(V = 2\), and faces price \(p = 1/2\). Her risk aversion is \(a = 3\). Find her optimal demand and the maximal conditional expected utility when her initial wealth is \(W_0' = W_0\).

Solution

By Lemma 2.1, \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV) = (1 - 1/2)/(3 \cdot 2) = 1/12\). The maximal value is \(-\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-(1/2)^2/(2 \cdot 2)) = -\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-1/16)\). The exponent is \(-(m-p)^2/(2V) = -(1/4)/4 = -1/16\).

Mech 3 · A normal moment

Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, 4)\) and \(V = 1\). Compute \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)]\).

Solution

Lemma 2.2 gives \((1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2} = (1 + 4)^{-1/2} = 1/\sqrt{5} \approx 0.447214\). As a check, Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(t \mapsto e^{-t}\) gives \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)] \ge \exp(-\E[Z^2]/2) = \exp(-2) \approx 0.135\), and \(0.447 \ge 0.135\) is consistent with that bound.

Proof

Proof 1 · The coefficient of the signal

Starting from the identity \((\ast)\) in the proof of Proposition 3.1, show directly that the coefficient of \(s\) on the right side of \((\ast)\) forces \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\).

Solution

Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) into \((\ast)\). The left side is \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha - \beta_p s + \varpi u) + A'(s - \bar v)\). The coefficient of \(s\) on the left is \(-(\tau_v + A')\beta_p + A'\), and the right side has no \(s\) term. Setting this coefficient to zero gives \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\), which is the coefficient \(\beta\) of Proposition 3.1 when \(A' = A\).

Proof 2 · Strict monotonicity of the informativeness

Show that for \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\tau_\eta(\lambda)\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\), and deduce the monotonicity of \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\).

Solution

Let \(0 < \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le 1\). Then \(\kappa(\lambda_1) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda_1 > \kappa(\lambda_2)\), and both are positive. Since \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\kappa(\lambda_1)^2\sigma_u^2 > \kappa(\lambda_2)^2\sigma_u^2\), so \(\sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_1) > \sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_2)\), and \(\tau_\eta(\lambda_1) < \tau_\eta(\lambda_2)\). The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), so \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\).

Proof 3 · The derivative of the gain with respect to the cost

Show that \(N(\lambda)\) is strictly decreasing in \(c\) at each fixed \(\lambda\), and that the set of costs at which \(\lambda\) is an equilibrium interior fraction is a single point.

Solution

For fixed \(\lambda\), \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) is a function of \(c\) with slope \(-1\), so it is strictly decreasing in \(c\). The interior equilibrium condition is \(G(\lambda) = c\). For a fixed \(\lambda\), this fixes \(c\) at the single value \(G(\lambda)\). So each interior fraction is an equilibrium at exactly one cost. This does not show that a given cost has one equilibrium fraction, which requires monotonicity of \(G\) in \(\lambda\), not proved on this page.

Emp 1 · The informed fraction at cost 0.15

Emp 2 · Which cost gives the fraction 0.2316?

Lab 1, part C, prints \(\lambda^\ast = 0.231615819\) at one of the seven costs. Identify the cost and the printed informativeness at that point.

Solution

The printed line with \(\lambda^\ast = 0.231615819\) is at cost \(0.17\), the sixth of the seven costs, and its printed \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is \(0.013061\). The fraction is smaller than the fraction at cost \(0.16\), which is \(0.480567912\), as the falling pattern requires.

Emp 3 · The gain at the boundary fractions

Lab 1, part B, prints \(G(0)\) and \(G(1)\) at the parameters of Example 1.1. Check both values against the closed forms, and state which of the two is the larger.

Solution

Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0) = \ln(1 + 1)/(2 \cdot 2) = \ln(2)/4 = 0.173287\), which matches the printed value. For \(G(1)\), at \(\lambda = 1\) with \(a = 2\) and unit variances: \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) = 1/5\). Corollary 2.4 gives \(V_I = 1/(1 + 1) = 1/2\), \(b_y = (1/5)/(1 + 1/5) = 1/6\) and \(V_U = 1/(1 + 1/5) = 5/6\). Proposition 3.1 gives \(A = \tau_\varepsilon = 1\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A) = 1/2\), so \(b_s = \beta = 1/2\). Proposition 4.1 gives \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2 \cdot 2 + \kappa^2\beta^2 = 0 + 4 \cdot \tfrac14 = 1\) and \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(1 + 5) = \tfrac19 \cdot 6 = 2/3\). Definition 4.3 then gives \(G(1) = \tfrac14\ln\bigl((1 + 1/(1/2))/(1 + (2/3)/(5/6))\bigr) = \tfrac14\ln(3/(9/5)) = \tfrac14\ln(5/3) = 0.127706\), which matches. The larger value is \(G(0)\).

Research

Res 1 · The noiseless paradox at every cost

Prove that with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at any cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), and state which fraction is an equilibrium when \(c \ge G(0)\).

Solution

This is Proposition 5.1(b). For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), Proposition 5.1(a) gives \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\), so \(N(\lambda) < 0\) and case (iii) fails. At \(\lambda = 1\), \(G(1) = 0 < c\), so case (ii) fails. At \(\lambda = 0\), \(G(0) > c\), so case (i) fails. When \(c \ge G(0)\), Proposition 5.1(c) shows that \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium. There the price is \(\bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), a function of \(u\) alone, which is independent of \(v\), so \(\Corr(v, p)^2 = 0\).

Res 2 · The limit of the gain at zero

Show that \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} \Var(D_U)/V_U = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), using only the formulas of Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 2.4.

Solution

From Proposition 4.1, \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta)\), and \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\). As \(\lambda \to 0^+\), \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\), and the other factors converge to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4\). So \(\Var(D_U) \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), and \(V_U \to \sigma_v^2\), so the ratio tends to \(a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\).

Res 3 · The gain with vanishing noise

Show that \(G(1) \to 0\) as \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), with \(a\), \(\sigma_v^2\) and \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) fixed.

Solution

At \(\lambda = 1\), \(\beta = b_s\), \(\Var(D_I) = \kappa^2 b_s^2\sigma_u^2\), which tends to zero with \(\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed term is \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = (b_y - b_s)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)/V_U\). As \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), \(\tau_\eta \to \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(b_y \to b_s\), so this term tends to zero. Hence both ratios in \(G(1)\) tend to their limits, the numerator to \(1\) and the denominator to \(1\), and \(G(1) \to 0\).
