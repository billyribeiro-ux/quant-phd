Price Informativeness

Some investors pay a cost \(c\) to learn the payoff of a risky asset, and the others learn only from its price. Noise traders, investors who trade for reasons unrelated to the payoff, add a random supply of the asset with standard deviation \(\sigma\), the noise volume, so the price moves for reasons unrelated to the payoff. With the fraction of investors who pay to learn held fixed, more noise makes the price reveal less about the payoff; in the model here, on the interior where that fraction lies strictly between 0 and 1, the fraction rises with the noise, and there the price's informativeness, the squared correlation of the payoff and the price, does not depend on the noise volume. You write the solver that finds the fraction of investors who pay to learn (the informed fraction, \(\lambda\)), and you report how that fraction and the price's informativeness move with the noise and with the cost of information.

"""Rung 1 worked example: one SIMULATED instance, solved step by step.

Standard library only. The two inputs are stated values for this example, a noise volume of 0.60 and an information
cost of 0.13. They are not a draw from the generator in grader/instance.py. The model constants are a = 2, tau_v = 1
and tau_e = 1. Each step prints the quantity it computes, so every printed number comes from the code below.
"""

import math

A = 2.0  # the CARA coefficient
TAU_V = 1.0  # the prior precision of the payoff v
TAU_E = 1.0  # the precision of the signal s = v + eps
SD = 0.60  # the noise volume: the standard deviation of the noise supply u
COST = 0.13  # the information cost c
BISECTION_STEPS = 60  # 60 halvings of [0, 1] reach the floating-point resolution near the root


def price_precision(lam: float) -> float:
    """tau_y, the precision of the price signal, for an informed fraction lam in (0, 1]: lam^2 tau_e^2 /
    (lam^2 tau_e + a^2 SD^2). It is 0 at lam = 0."""
    return lam * lam * TAU_E * TAU_E / (lam * lam * TAU_E + A * A * SD * SD)


def price_coefficients(lam: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """The coefficients b_s and b_u of the linear price p = b_s s + b_u u, for lam in (0, 1]."""
    ty = price_precision(lam)
    denominator = lam * (TAU_V + TAU_E) + (1.0 - lam) * (TAU_V + ty)
    b_s = (lam * TAU_E + (1.0 - lam) * ty) / denominator
    return b_s, -A * b_s / (lam * TAU_E)


def uninformed_term(lam: float) -> float:
    """F_U(lam) for lam in (0, 1]: the Gaussian term of the uninformed investor's expected utility."""
    ty = price_precision(lam)
    b_s, _ = price_coefficients(lam)
    r = ty / (TAU_V + ty) - b_s
    return math.pow(1.0 + r * r * (TAU_V + ty) * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / ty), -0.5)


def informed_term(lam: float) -> float:
    """F_I(lam) for lam in (0, 1]: the Gaussian term of the informed investor's expected utility."""
    b_s, b_u = price_coefficients(lam)
    precision = TAU_V + TAU_E
    slope = TAU_E / precision - b_s
    var_gap = slope * slope * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / TAU_E) + b_u * b_u * SD * SD
    return math.pow(1.0 + precision * var_gap, -0.5)


def gap(lam: float) -> float:
    """The informed-minus-uninformed expected utility at lam in (0, 1]: F_U(lam) - exp(a c) F_I(lam)."""
    return uninformed_term(lam) - math.exp(A * COST) * informed_term(lam)


def gap_at_zero() -> float:
    """The limit of the gap as lam tends to 0: the price then tends to -a u / tau_v."""
    uninformed = math.pow(1.0 + A * A * SD * SD / TAU_V, -0.5)
    informed = math.pow(1.0 + TAU_E / TAU_V + (TAU_V + TAU_E) * A * A * SD * SD / (TAU_V * TAU_V), -0.5)
    return uninformed - math.exp(A * COST) * informed


def main() -> None:
    print("step 1 | inputs: noise volume", SD, "| information cost", COST, "| a", A)
    g0, g1 = gap_at_zero(), gap(1.0)
    print("step 2 | gap as the informed fraction tends to 0:", f"{g0:+.6f}")
    print("step 2 | gap at the informed fraction 1:", f"{g1:+.6f}")
    print("step 2 | the gap changes sign on (0, 1):", g0 > 0.0 and g1 < 0.0)
    low, high = 0.0, 1.0
    for step in range(1, BISECTION_STEPS + 1):
        mid = 0.5 * (low + high)
        if gap(mid) > 0.0:
            low = mid
        else:
            high = mid
        if step in (1, 2, 3):
            print(f"step 3 | after halving {step}: bracket [{low:.6f}, {high:.6f}]")
    root = 0.5 * (low + high)
    print("step 3 | after", BISECTION_STEPS, "halvings the bracket width is", f"{high - low:.3e}")
    print("step 4 | informed fraction lambda* =", f"{root:.8f}")
    print("step 4 | gap at lambda* =", f"{gap(root):+.3e}")
    b_s, b_u = price_coefficients(root)
    print("step 5 | price coefficients b_s =", f"{b_s:.8f}", "b_u =", f"{b_u:.8f}")
    print("step 5 | b_u / b_s =", f"{b_u / b_s:.8f}", "and -a / (lambda* tau_e) =", f"{-A / (root * TAU_E):.8f}")
    ty = price_precision(root)
    print(
        "step 6 | tau_y at lambda* =",
        f"{ty:.8f}",
        "| tau_bar = (tau_v + tau_e) exp(-2 a c) - tau_v =",
        f"{(TAU_V + TAU_E) * math.exp(-2.0 * A * COST) - TAU_V:.8f}",
    )
    pi = ty / (TAU_V + ty)
    print("step 6 | price informativeness tau_y / (tau_v + tau_y) =", f"{pi:.8f}")
    print(
        "step 6 | 1 - exp(2 a c) tau_v / (tau_v + tau_e) =",
        f"{1.0 - math.exp(2.0 * A * COST) * TAU_V / (TAU_V + TAU_E):.8f}",
    )

The model and its equilibrium

The model has one risky asset, two dates and a risk-free return of zero. It is the noisy rational-expectations equilibrium of Lesson 6, and its price informativeness is the measure of Lesson 7. Each result below is derived from the assumptions numbered here, and each proposition states the assumptions it uses.

Notation

\(v\) is the payoff, \(p\) the price, \(s\) the signal, \(\varepsilon\) the noise in the signal, \(u\) the noise-trader supply, \(\sigma\) its standard deviation (the noise volume, noise_sd), \(c\) the information cost (info_cost), \(\lambda\) the informed fraction, \(a\) the CARA coefficient, \(x\) a demand, \(w\) terminal wealth, and \(\tau\) a precision, which is the inverse of a variance. \(\tau_v\) is the prior precision of \(v\), \(\tau_\varepsilon\) the precision of \(\varepsilon\), and \(\tau_y\) the precision of the price signal defined in Proposition 1. \(\Omega = \Var(v-p)\) is the variance of the payoff less the price, and \(V_I\) and \(V_U\) are the conditional variances of \(v\) for an informed and for an uninformed investor. The target precision is \(\bar\tau = (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac}-\tau_v\). This page uses \(\bar\tau\) for this quantity.

Assumptions

  1. A1 · Prior and signal. \(v \sim \Normal(0, 1/\tau_v)\) and \(\varepsilon \sim \Normal(0, 1/\tau_\varepsilon)\), with \(\varepsilon\) independent of \(v\), and \(s = v + \varepsilon\). The prior mean of zero is a normalization: a common prior mean \(\mu\) shifts the payoff, the signal, the conditional means and the price by \(\mu\). For an information set \(\mathcal F\) (Lemma 1), a demand is \((\E[v\mid\mathcal F]-p)/(aV)\), which depends on the shift only through the difference, and an expected utility depends on \(v-p\) alone, so both are unchanged (the economy is the zero-mean one with each variable less \(\mu\)).
  2. A2 · Noise supply. \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma > 0\), independent of \((v, \varepsilon)\). The rational demand absorbs it: \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\).
  3. A3 · Preferences, wealth and cost. Each investor has constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) utility (Definition 6.2 of Lesson 6), \(-\exp(-a w)\), with \(a = 2\). Initial wealth is 0 for every investor, and the risk-free return is 0. An informed investor pays \(c\), in units of terminal wealth, so \(w = x(v-p) - c\). An uninformed investor has \(w = x(v-p)\). A common initial wealth \(w_0\) makes terminal wealth \(w_0+w\), so \(-\exp(-a(w_0+w)) = -\exp(-aw_0)\exp(-aw)\), and it multiplies every expected utility by the same positive factor \(\exp(-aw_0)\), so it changes no comparison below.
  4. A4 · Linear price. The price is \(p = b_s s + b_u u\), with \(b_s \neq 0\) whenever \(\lambda > 0\). The results are for this family. The page does not claim that an equilibrium exists outside it.
  5. A5 · Types and price taking. A fraction \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) of investors is informed. Every informed investor observes the same \(s\). The others observe only \(p\). Each investor takes the price as given, and the average over a type is its expectation, which is the continuum limit.

Definition (equilibrium). At an informed fraction \(\lambda\), an equilibrium is a price and two demand functions, \(x_I(s,p)\) for an informed investor and \(x_U(p)\) for an uninformed one, such that each investor maximizes expected utility given the information of its type, and the market clears: \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\) for every realized \((s,u)\).

Three lemmas

Lemma 1 · CARA demand and expected utility. Assume A3. Let \(\mathcal F\) be an information set for which \(p\) is measurable, let \(m = \E[v\mid\mathcal F]\) (a function of the information), and suppose that \(v\) is conditionally normal given \(\mathcal F\) and that \(V = \Var(v\mid\mathcal F) > 0\) is a constant. Then the optimal demand is \(x^* = (m-p)/(aV)\), and the conditional expected utility at that demand is \(\E[-\exp(-aw)\mid\mathcal F] = -\exp(ac)\exp\!\big(-(m-p)^2/(2V)\big)\) for an informed investor, whose wealth carries the cost \(c\). For an uninformed investor the factor \(\exp(ac)\) is absent.

Proof. Given \(\mathcal F\), the payoff \(v-p\) is normal with mean \(m-p\) and variance \(V\), so \(\E[\exp(-a x(v-p))\mid\mathcal F] = \exp\big(-a x(m-p) + a^2x^2V/2\big)\). For an informed investor \(w = x(v-p)-c\), so \(\E[-\exp(-aw)\mid\mathcal F] = -\exp(ac)\exp\big(-a g(x)\big)\) with \(g(x) = x(m-p) - (a/2)x^2V\). The function \(g\) is strictly concave in \(x\), with \(g'(x) = (m-p) - axV\), which vanishes at \(x^* = (m-p)/(aV)\). Then \(g(x^*) = (m-p)^2/(aV) - (m-p)^2/(2aV) = (m-p)^2/(2aV)\), and \(-a\,g(x^*) = -(m-p)^2/(2V)\). An uninformed investor has no cost, so the factor \(\exp(ac)\) is absent for it. \(\square\)

Lemma 2 · The Gaussian mean. If \(Z \sim \Normal(0, S)\) with \(S \ge 0\) and \(V > 0\), then \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/(2V))] = (V/(V+S))^{1/2}\).

Proof. For \(S = 0\) the value is 1. For \(S > 0\), the expectation is \((2\pi S)^{-1/2}\int \exp\big(-z^2(1/S + 1/V)/2\big)\,dz = (2\pi S)^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\pi SV/(S+V)} = (V/(V+S))^{1/2}\). \(\square\)

Lemma 3 · Total variance of the payoff less the price. Let \(\mathcal F\) be as in Lemma 1, and let \(m = \E[v\mid\mathcal F]\) and \(V = \Var(v\mid\mathcal F)\). Then \(\Var(v-p) = V + \Var(m-p)\).

Proof. Let \(X = v-p\). Since \(p\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable, \(\Var(X\mid\mathcal F) = V\) and \(\E[X\mid\mathcal F] = m - p\). Writing \(\Var(X) = \E[X^2] - (\E X)^2\) and \(\E[X^2] = \E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F) + \E[X\mid\mathcal F]^2]\) gives \(\Var(X) = \E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F)] + \Var(\E[X\mid\mathcal F])\). Here \(\E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F)] = V\), a constant, and \(\Var(\E[X\mid\mathcal F]) = \Var(m-p)\). So \(\Var(v-p) = V + \Var(m-p)\). \(\square\)

Equilibrium, informativeness and the indifference condition

Proposition 1 · The linear equilibrium. Assume A1 to A5 and let \(\lambda \in (0,1]\). The linear equilibrium of A4 exists and is unique. Its coefficients satisfy \[\frac{b_u}{b_s} = -\frac{a}{\lambda\tau_\varepsilon}, \qquad b_s = \frac{\lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y}{\lambda(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)+(1-\lambda)(\tau_v+\tau_y)}, \qquad \tau_y = \frac{\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2}{\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2}.\]

Proof. Let \(\kappa = b_u/b_s\) and \(y = p/b_s = s + \kappa u\). The term \(\varepsilon + \kappa u\) is independent of \(v\), normal with mean 0 and variance \(1/\tau_\varepsilon + \kappa^2\sigma^2\). Define \(\tau_y\) as the reciprocal of that variance; this gives the formula for \(\tau_y\) once \(\kappa\) is known.

(0) Normality. Under A1, A2 and A4, the variables \((v,s,u)\) are jointly normal and \(p\) is a linear function of \((s,u)\), so \(v\) is conditionally normal given \((s,p)\) and given \(p\). This is the hypothesis of Lemma 1 for both investors.

(1) Informed demand. Since \(v\) is independent of \(u\) given \(s\) (A1 and A2), \(\E[v\mid s,u] = \E[v\mid s]\), and the information set generated by \((s,p)\) is contained in the one generated by \((s,u)\), \(\mathcal F_{s,p} \subseteq \mathcal F_{s,u}\), so \(\E[v\mid s,p] = \E[v\mid s] = \tau_\varepsilon s/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(\Var(v\mid s,p) = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\). By Lemma 1, \(x_I = (\tau_\varepsilon s - (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)p)/a\).

(2) Uninformed demand. The uninformed investor observes \(p\), which is equivalent to \(y\) because \(b_s \neq 0\). Since \(y = v + (\varepsilon+\kappa u)\) with \(\varepsilon + \kappa u\) normal and independent of \(v\), the posterior of \(v\) has precision \(\tau_v + \tau_y\) and mean \(\tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). So \(\E[v\mid p] = \tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), \(\Var(v\mid p) = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), and by Lemma 1, \(x_U = (\tau_y y - (\tau_v+\tau_y)p)/a\).

(3) Clearing. Let \(D = \lambda(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)+(1-\lambda)(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). Multiply \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\) by \(a\), substitute \(y = s + \kappa u\) and \(p = b_s s + \kappa b_s u\). The condition becomes \([\lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y - Db_s]\,s + [(1-\lambda)\tau_y\kappa - D b_s\kappa - a]\,u = 0\). Since \(s\) and \(u\) are independent with positive variances, both brackets vanish. The first gives \(b_s = (\lambda\tau_\varepsilon+(1-\lambda)\tau_y)/D\). Substituting \(Db_s = \lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y\) into the second gives \(-\lambda\tau_\varepsilon\kappa = a\), so \(\kappa = -a/(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon)\).

(4) Consistency and uniqueness. The value of \(\kappa\) fixes \(\tau_y = \lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\), and \(b_s\) then follows from (3). Each step is an equivalence, so the coefficients above give the one linear equilibrium. \(D > 0\) because \(\lambda > 0\). \(\square\)

Proposition 2 · Informativeness. Assume A1 to A5. For \(\lambda \in (0,1]\), \(\Corr(v,p)^2 = \tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). At \(\lambda = 0\), \(\Corr(v,p) = 0\).

Proof. With \(p = b_s y\), \(\Cov(v,p) = b_s\Cov(v,y) = b_s/\tau_v\), because \(\Cov(v,y) = \Var(v)\). Also \(\Var(y) = 1/\tau_v + 1/\tau_y\), so \(\Var(p) = b_s^2(1/\tau_v+1/\tau_y)\). Hence \(\Corr(v,p)^2 = (b_s^2/\tau_v^2)/\big((1/\tau_v)\,b_s^2(1/\tau_v+1/\tau_y)\big) = \tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). At \(\lambda = 0\) every investor is uninformed. The regression of \(v\) on \(p\) gives \(\E[v\mid p] = \beta p\) and \(\Var(v\mid p) = V\), with constants \(\beta\) and \(V\), so Lemma 1 gives \(x_U = (\beta-1)p/(aV)\). Clearing, \(x_U = u\), then requires \(p = aVu/(\beta-1)\), a constant times \(u\). So \(b_s = 0\), which A4 allows at \(\lambda = 0\), and \(p\) is independent of \(v\). Then \(\beta = 0\) and \(V = 1/\tau_v\), so \(p = -au/\tau_v\) and \(\Corr(v,p) = 0\). \(\square\)

Proposition 3 · The indifference gap. Assume A1 to A5 and let \(\lambda \in (0,1]\). Let \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). The expected utilities net of cost are \(\E[U_I] = -e^{ac}(V_I/\Omega)^{1/2}\) and \(\E[U_U] = -(V_U/\Omega)^{1/2}\), so the gap \(G(\lambda) = \E[U_I] - \E[U_U] = \Omega^{-1/2}\big(V_U^{1/2} - e^{ac}V_I^{1/2}\big)\). Write \(F_U = (V_U/\Omega)^{1/2}\) and \(F_I = (V_I/\Omega)^{1/2}\), so that \(G = F_U - e^{ac}F_I\). For \(\lambda = 1\) the uninformed term is that of a hypothetical uninformed investor who faces the price of the economy with \(\lambda = 1\). Then \[\operatorname{sign} G(\lambda) = \operatorname{sign}(\bar\tau - \tau_y(\lambda)),\] so \(G(\lambda) > 0\) iff \(\tau_y(\lambda) < \bar\tau\), and \(G\) has at most one zero in \((0,1]\). The limit as \(\lambda\) tends to 0 is \[G(0{+}) = \Big(\tfrac{1}{\tau_v}+\tfrac{a^2\sigma^2}{\tau_v^2}\Big)^{-1/2}\Big(\tfrac{1}{\tau_v^{1/2}} - \tfrac{e^{ac}}{(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)^{1/2}}\Big),\] whose sign is the sign of \(\bar\tau\).

Proof. The conditional normality that Lemma 1 needs holds for both investors, as in step (0) of Proposition 1. For an informed investor, Lemma 1 gives \(\E[U_I\mid s,p] = -e^{ac}\exp(-(m_I-p)^2/(2V_I))\), where \(m_I = \E[v\mid s,p]\) from step (1) of Proposition 1. The variable \(m_I - p\) is a linear function of \((s,u)\) with mean 0 (A1), so Lemma 2 gives \(\E[U_I] = -e^{ac}(V_I/(V_I+S_I))^{1/2}\) with \(S_I = \Var(m_I-p)\). Lemma 3 gives \(V_I + S_I = \Omega\). The uninformed case is identical with \(m_U = \tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), \(V_U\) and \(S_U = \Var(m_U-p)\), and Lemma 3 again gives \(V_U+S_U = \Omega\). Since \(\Omega \ge V_I > 0\), the sign of \(G\) is the sign of \(V_U - e^{2ac}V_I\). Multiplying by \((\tau_v+\tau_y)(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} > 0\) gives \((\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} - (\tau_v+\tau_y) = \bar\tau - \tau_y\). Proposition 1 gives \(\tau_y(\lambda) = \lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon + a^2\sigma^2)\), which is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\), so \(\bar\tau - \tau_y\) changes sign at most once. As \(\lambda\) tends to 0, \(\tau_y\) tends to 0, \(b_s\) tends to 0 and \(b_u\) tends to \(-a/\tau_v\) (Proposition 1, using \(b_s/\lambda \to \tau_\varepsilon/\tau_v\)), so \(p\) tends to \(-au/\tau_v\), \(\Omega\) tends to \(1/\tau_v + a^2\sigma^2/\tau_v^2\) and \(V_U\) to \(1/\tau_v\). Substituting these limits into the formula for \(G\) gives the displayed \(G(0{+})\). \(\square\)

Proposition 4 · The informed fraction. Assume A1 to A5, and let \(\lambda^*\) be the informed fraction defined by the boundary rule: \(\lambda^* = 0\) when \(G(0{+}) \le 0\), \(\lambda^* = 1\) when \(G(1) \ge 0\), and otherwise the zero of \(G\) in \((0,1)\). Then \[\lambda^* = \begin{cases} 0, & \bar\tau \le 0,\\ 1, & \bar\tau \ge \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2),\\ a\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/\big(\tau_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon-\bar\tau)\big)}, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\] In the interior case the price informativeness is \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau) = 1 - e^{2ac}\tau_v/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\), which does not depend on \(\sigma\).

Proof. In the limit formula of Proposition 3 the bracket \(\tau_v^{-1/2} - e^{ac}(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\) is positive exactly when \((\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} > \tau_v\), that is, when \(\bar\tau > 0\); so \(G(0{+}) \le 0\) iff \(\bar\tau \le 0\). By the sign rule of Proposition 3, \(G(1) \ge 0\) iff \(\tau_y(1) \le \bar\tau\), where \(\tau_y(1) = \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\). Otherwise \(G\) has a zero \(\lambda^*\) in \((0,1)\) at which \(\tau_y(\lambda^*) = \bar\tau\). Solving \(\lambda^{*2}\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^{*2}\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2) = \bar\tau\) gives \(\lambda^{*2}(\tau_\varepsilon^2 - \bar\tau\tau_\varepsilon) = \bar\tau a^2\sigma^2\). Here \(\bar\tau < \tau_\varepsilon\), because \(\tau_y(1) < \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(\bar\tau < \tau_y(1)\) in this case, so the division is valid, and the formula follows. The informativeness at \(\lambda^*\) is \(\tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\) at \(\tau_y = \bar\tau\) (Proposition 2), and \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau) = 1 - \tau_v e^{2ac}/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) by substituting \(\bar\tau\). \(\square\)

Proposition 5 · Comparative statics on the interior. Assume A1 to A5, and suppose \(0 < \bar\tau < \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\), so that \(\lambda^*\) is interior. Then \(\partial\lambda^*/\partial\sigma = \lambda^*/\sigma > 0\); over the costs \(c\) at which the interior holds, \(\lambda^*\) and the price informativeness are strictly decreasing in \(c\); and the price informativeness does not depend on \(\sigma\).

Proof. By Proposition 4, \(\lambda^* = a\sigma k(\bar\tau)\) with \(k(t) = \sqrt{t/(\tau_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon-t))}\), which does not depend on \(\sigma\), so \(\partial\lambda^*/\partial\sigma = \lambda^*/\sigma > 0\). The map \(c \mapsto \bar\tau = (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac}-\tau_v\) is strictly decreasing because \(a > 0\), and \(k\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\tau_\varepsilon)\), so \(\lambda^*\) is strictly decreasing in \(c\). The informativeness \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau)\) is strictly increasing in \(\bar\tau\), so it is strictly decreasing in \(c\), and it depends on \(\sigma\) through no term. \(\square\)

With \(\tau_v = \tau_\varepsilon = 1\) and \(a = 2\), the formulas read \(\bar\tau = 2e^{-4c} - 1\), \(\lambda^* = 2\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/(1-\bar\tau)}\) in the interior, and an informativeness of \(1 - e^{4c}/2\). Lab 3 prints these values on its grid, and its last column checks \(\lambda^*\) against the closed form of Proposition 4 at each point. Proposition 5 explains the pattern of the grid: at a fixed cost the informed fraction is proportional to the noise volume, and on the grid the informativeness is the same at every noise volume.

Where the model's limits are. The results need A1 to A5 together. Exponential utility with normal payoffs (A1, A3) is the source of the closed forms, and a utility other than the exponential, or a payoff that is not normal, is outside this page. The noise supply is exogenous (A2), so the model says nothing about why noise traders trade. Investors are price takers in a static, two-date economy (A5), and the price is linear (A4). This page does not estimate how much any of these limits changes the answer.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A risky asset pays an uncertain amount \(v\). Some investors pay a cost \(c\) to observe a signal of \(v\), the others learn only from the price, and a noise-trader supply \(u\) moves the price. The question is how informative the price is, and what fraction of investors chooses to pay for information. The fraction solves an indifference condition, and the informativeness follows from it.
Inputs
No dataset. The solver receives two numbers for each instance: noise_sd, the noise volume \(\sigma\), and info_cost, the cost \(c\). The instance generator in grader/instance.py draws both from the seed, and the draws are SIMULATED. The constants \(a = 2\), \(\tau_v = 1\) and \(\tau_\varepsilon = 1\) are fixed in your module and are not arguments of the interface. The report's grid is given in the report section. Under A1 to A5, every instance the generator draws has one interior root. The generator draws \(c\) from \([0.120, 0.150]\) and \(\sigma\) from \([0.500, 0.750]\), each rounded to three decimals. Here \(\bar\tau = 2e^{-4c}-1\) decreases in \(c\), so \(0.097623 \le \bar\tau \le 0.237567\) on the box (six decimals; \(\bar\tau(0.150) = 0.09762327\ldots\) and \(\bar\tau(0.120) = 0.23756678\ldots\)), and \(\tau_y(1) = 1/(1+4\sigma^2) \ge 1/(1+4\cdot 0.75^2) = 0.30769\ldots\). Therefore \(0 < \bar\tau < \tau_y(1)\) at every point of the box, and Proposition 4 gives an interior \(\lambda^*\) there.
Required outputs
In a repository of your own: solver.py at its root, with the function solve_model(noise_sd: float, info_cost: float) -> dict[str, float], which returns exactly the keys informed_fraction, price_informativeness and indifference_gap, all floats. informed_fraction is \(\lambda^*\) by the boundary rule of Proposition 4, to the stop rule in the constraints below. price_informativeness is \(\Corr(v,p)^2\) at \(\lambda^*\) (Proposition 2). indifference_gap is the gap of Proposition 3 at \(\lambda^*\), with the limit at 0 when \(\lambda^* = 0\). Also report/report.md with the four sections of the brief and its appendix, report/run_case.py, which is Lab 3 with its solve replaced by a call to solve_model and printing the same lines, and report/self_assessment.md produced by the form in the self-assessment section. Start from the starter file, whose signature is complete.
Constraints
Standard library only, with no network. No function prints or warns. The solver works in floating point at full precision, and the gap it reports at fraction 0 agrees with the limit of Proposition 3 to within \(10^{-14}\) relative to the larger of 1 and the absolute value of that limit. The bisection on \(\lambda\) brackets the root in \([0,1]\), evaluates the gap at 0 by the limit of Proposition 3, and stops when the bracket is narrower than \(10^{-12}\) times its upper end, or after 200 halvings. The reported \(\lambda^*\) lies in the final bracket. When the tolerance stop rule ends the search, every point of that bracket is within the stated tolerance of the root. The 200-halving limb can end the search first only when the root is below about \(6\times10^{-49}\), and then the bracket may be wider than the tolerance. These constants are stated here, not arguments of the interface. The boundary rule is part of the answer, and the suite tests it. Invalid input raises the exception named in the exception table, and the message contains the text the table gives. The report displays every number at the precision of the brief and computes each one from unrounded solver output.
Grading mode
Both modes apply. The solver is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which runs 43 checks with warnings as errors, against an oracle that does not read your code and that evaluates the model by moments and quadrature. The report is graded by the rubric in the report rubric, against five criteria with four observable levels each, after you score yourself against two annotated exemplars. The report meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Lab 3 · The reference case: the grid of the report

The inputs are a stated grid, SIMULATED: noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750, and information costs 0.120, 0.135 and 0.150. Expected, stated before running: at each noise volume the informed fraction falls as the cost rises, and at each cost it rises in proportion to the noise volume, so at cost 0.120 it is 0.5582, 0.6978 and 0.8373 for noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750; the informativeness is the same at the three noise volumes of each cost, at 0.1920, 0.1420 and 0.0889 for costs 0.120, 0.135 and 0.150; the gap at each reported point has absolute value below \(10^{-13}\); the bisection agrees with the closed form of Proposition 4 to within \(10^{-12}\); and the gap is positive at fraction 0 and negative at fraction 1 at all 484 points of a 22 by 22 grid over the autograder's box.

"""Rung 3 reference case: the solver on the 3 by 3 grid that the report uses, with stated SIMULATED inputs.

Standard library only. The grid values are stated parameters of the model, not a draw from the generator. The model
constants are a = 2, tau_v = 1 and tau_e = 1. The function solve is the reference: copy this file as report/run_case.py
and replace the body of solve with a call to your module's solve_model; the file must then print the same lines.

Each row gives the informed fraction (bisection on the gap, with the stated tolerance), the price informativeness, the
gap at the reported fraction, and the closed-form fraction of Proposition 4. The last line checks the box of
the autograder's generator on a 22 by 22 grid, which includes the four corners: the gap must be positive at fraction 0
and negative at fraction 1 at every grid point.
"""

import math

A = 2.0  # the CARA coefficient
TAU_V = 1.0  # the prior precision of the payoff v
TAU_E = 1.0  # the precision of the signal s = v + eps
NOISE_GRID = (0.500, 0.625, 0.750)  # the noise volumes the report uses
COST_GRID = (0.120, 0.135, 0.150)  # the information costs the report uses
TOLERANCE = 1e-12  # the bisection stops when the bracket is narrower than this times its upper end
MAX_HALVINGS = 200  # or after this many halvings, the second limb of the stop rule


def price_precision(lam: float, sd: float) -> float:
    """tau_y for lam in (0, 1]: lam^2 tau_e^2 / (lam^2 tau_e + a^2 sd^2)."""
    return lam * lam * TAU_E * TAU_E / (lam * lam * TAU_E + A * A * sd * sd)


def indifference_gap(lam: float, sd: float, cost: float) -> float:
    """F_U(lam) - exp(a c) F_I(lam) for lam in (0, 1]; at lam = 1 the uninformed term is a hypothetical agent's."""
    ty = price_precision(lam, sd)
    denominator = lam * (TAU_V + TAU_E) + (1.0 - lam) * (TAU_V + ty)
    b_s = (lam * TAU_E + (1.0 - lam) * ty) / denominator
    b_u = -A * b_s / (lam * TAU_E)
    r = ty / (TAU_V + ty) - b_s
    f_u = math.pow(1.0 + r * r * (TAU_V + ty) * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / ty), -0.5)
    precision = TAU_V + TAU_E
    slope = TAU_E / precision - b_s
    var_gap = slope * slope * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / TAU_E) + b_u * b_u * sd * sd
    f_i = math.pow(1.0 + precision * var_gap, -0.5)
    return f_u - math.exp(A * cost) * f_i


def gap_at_zero(sd: float, cost: float) -> float:
    """The limit of the gap as the informed fraction tends to 0."""
    uninformed = math.pow(1.0 + A * A * sd * sd / TAU_V, -0.5)
    informed = math.pow(1.0 + TAU_E / TAU_V + (TAU_V + TAU_E) * A * A * sd * sd / (TAU_V * TAU_V), -0.5)
    return uninformed - math.exp(A * cost) * informed


def closed_form_fraction(sd: float, cost: float) -> float:
    """The root in closed form: tau_y(lam) = tau_bar = (tau_v + tau_e) exp(-2 a c) - tau_v, solved for lam, with
    the same boundary rule as solve. Valid as a check of the bisection, not as the solver."""
    t = (TAU_V + TAU_E) * math.exp(-2.0 * A * cost) - TAU_V
    if t <= 0.0:
        return 0.0
    if t >= TAU_E * TAU_E / (TAU_E + A * A * sd * sd):
        return 1.0
    return A * sd * math.sqrt(t / (TAU_E * (TAU_E - t)))


def solve(sd: float, cost: float) -> dict[str, float]:
    """The reference solver: the boundary rule, then bisection on the sign change of the gap."""
    g_0, g_1 = gap_at_zero(sd, cost), indifference_gap(1.0, sd, cost)
    if g_0 <= 0.0:
        lam = 0.0
    elif g_1 >= 0.0:
        lam = 1.0
    else:
        low, high = 0.0, 1.0
        halvings = 0
        while high - low > TOLERANCE * high and halvings < MAX_HALVINGS:
            mid = 0.5 * (low + high)
            if indifference_gap(mid, sd, cost) > 0.0:
                low = mid
            else:
                high = mid
            halvings += 1
        lam = 0.5 * (low + high)
    if lam == 0.0:
        gap, pi = g_0, 0.0
    elif lam == 1.0:
        gap, pi = g_1, price_precision(1.0, sd) / (TAU_V + price_precision(1.0, sd))
    else:
        gap = indifference_gap(lam, sd, cost)
        ty = price_precision(lam, sd)
        pi = ty / (TAU_V + ty)
    return {"informed_fraction": lam, "price_informativeness": pi, "indifference_gap": gap}


def main() -> None:
    print("noise volume | cost | informed fraction | informativeness | gap | closed-form fraction")
    worst = 0.0
    for sd in NOISE_GRID:
        for cost in COST_GRID:
            out = solve(sd, cost)
            closed = closed_form_fraction(sd, cost)
            worst = max(worst, abs(out["informed_fraction"] - closed))
            print(
                f"{sd:.3f} | {cost:.3f} | {out['informed_fraction']:.4f} | {out['price_informativeness']:.4f} | "
                f"{out['indifference_gap']:+.2e} | {closed:.4f}"
            )
    print("largest difference between the bisection and the closed form on the grid:", f"{worst:.1e}")
    inside = 0
    for i in range(22):
        for j in range(22):
            sd = 0.500 + 0.250 * i / 21.0
            cost = 0.120 + 0.030 * j / 21.0
            if gap_at_zero(sd, cost) > 0.0 and indifference_gap(1.0, sd, cost) < 0.0:
                inside += 1
    print("box grid points with an interior fraction (gap positive at 0, negative at 1):", inside, "of", 22 * 22)

Exemplar A

Official scoring: C1 2 · C2 2 · C3 1 · C4 2 · C5 1. The payoff, signal, noise supply and preferences are stated and the equilibrium is given in words; the method and bracket are stated without a tolerance; the informed fraction and informativeness are given with fixed decimals and no rule; the sign given for the noise volume is not the one the grid shows; and no limit of the model is named.

Model

The payoff is normal and the signal is the payoff plus normal noise. The noise supply is normal. Investors have exponential utility. Some investors are informed, and the price clears the market.

  • Meets C1 level 2: states the payoff, the signal, the noise supply and the preferences, and gives the equilibrium in words.
  • Misses C1 level 3: the preferences have no coefficient, and the equilibrium condition is not written as a formula.
Method

The solver finds the informed fraction by bisection on the gap between the informed and uninformed utilities, starting from the interval from 0 to 1.

  • Meets C2 level 2: states the method and the bracket.
  • Misses C2 level 3: no tolerance, no stop rule and no boundary rule.
Results

At a noise volume of 0.625 and a cost of 0.135, the informed fraction is 0.5567 and the price informativeness is 0.1420.

  • Meets C4 level 2: gives numbers with a fixed number of decimals.
  • Misses C4 level 3: no display rule is stated, and the passage does not say that the numbers come from unrounded solver output.
Comparative statics

A higher noise volume makes prices less informative, so the informativeness should fall as the noise rises.

  • Meets C3 level 1: a sign with no grid and no derivation.
  • Misses C3 level 2: no grid is stated, and on the grid of Lab 3 the sign is not the one given, because the informativeness is 0.1420 at each noise volume for the cost 0.135.
Limitations

The results depend on the model, which is a simplified description of a market.

  • Meets C5 level 1: notes a caveat without naming a limit.
  • Misses C5 level 2: names no limit of the model: the utility, the normal payoff and the single asset are not named.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 4. The assumptions are numbered and each is tied to the results it supports; the method, bracket, tolerance, stop rule, boundary rule and gap at each point are stated; the signs are given on the grid with the points they hold on; the display rule names the significant digits; and it says that no claim carries a registry key.

Model

Assumption 1 (prior and signal): the payoff \(v\) is normal with mean 0 and precision 1, and the signal is \(s = v + \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\) normal, independent, of precision 1. Assumption 2 (noise): \(u\) is normal with mean 0 and standard deviation \(\sigma\), independent of \((v,\varepsilon)\), and the market clears, \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\). Assumption 3 (preferences): utility is \(-\exp(-2w)\), initial wealth and the risk-free return are zero, and an informed investor pays \(c\). Assumption 4: the price is linear, \(p = b_s s + b_u u\), with \(b_s \neq 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\). Assumption 5 (types and price taking): a fraction \(\lambda\) of investors is informed and observes \(s\), the others observe only \(p\), and each investor takes the price as given. Assumption 1 is used in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 2 in Propositions 1 to 4, where the market clears in Propositions 1 and 2 (at \(\lambda = 0\) in Proposition 2), the noise is normal in Proposition 3, and the volume \(\sigma\) appears in Proposition 4; Assumption 3 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3, where the cost enters the gap; Assumption 4 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 5 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3. Propositions 4 and 5 state Assumptions 1 to 5 as a block, and are not listed.

  • Meets C1 level 4: lists each assumption with its number and names the results that use it.
Method

The solver brackets the informed fraction in \([0,1]\). It sets the fraction to 0 when the gap at 0, taken as the limit of Proposition 3, is nonpositive, and to 1 when the gap at 1 is nonnegative. Otherwise it bisects on the sign of the gap, with a tolerance of \(10^{-12}\) times the upper end of the bracket, and it stops after at most 200 halvings. The reported fraction is the midpoint of the final bracket. The table below gives the gap at each reported point.

  • Meets C2 level 4: states the method, bracket, tolerance, stop rule and boundary rule, and reports the gap at each point.
Results and comparative statics

Each row is one point of the grid printed by Lab 3. At cost 0.120 the informed fraction is 0.5582, 0.6978 and 0.8373 for noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750, and the informativeness is 0.1920 at all three. At cost 0.135 the informativeness is 0.1420 at all three noise volumes, and at cost 0.150 it is 0.0889. So on the stated grid, at each cost the informativeness is the same at every noise volume, and it falls as the cost rises. The informed fraction rises with the noise volume at each cost, and at cost 0.120 the three fractions are proportional to the noise volumes: the ratios of the unrounded fractions are 1.25 = 0.625 / 0.500 and 1.50 = 0.750 / 0.500 (from the displayed values, 0.6978/0.5582 = 1.2501 and 0.8373/0.5582 = 1.5000). Proposition 5 gives this, since \(\lambda^* = 2\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/(1-\bar\tau)}\) is proportional to \(\sigma\) at a fixed cost. At each noise volume the fraction falls as the cost rises. Every printed gap has absolute value below \(4\times10^{-14}\). Limits and evidence

The model is limited by its exponential utility and its normal payoff, which give the closed forms; by the exogenous noise supply, which the model does not explain; by price taking in a static, two-date economy; and by the linear price, which is the only equilibrium family the page treats. A result for a different utility or a different information structure is outside these propositions. No empirical claim is made. No registry key is attached to any claim, and none is claimed: the results are the derivations of Propositions 1 to 5 and the computed results of Lab 3.

  • Meets C5 level 4: names limits, grades each claim by its derivation or computed result, and states that no registry key is attached because none is claimed.
