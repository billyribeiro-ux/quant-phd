Rational-Expectations Equilibrium

  • Uses: random variable; expectation; conditional expectation; normal distribution
  • Introduces: rational-expectations equilibrium
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or finite check
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A price that traders use to forecast the price itself makes the forecast and the price depend on each other. This lesson states a linear-normal one-period model in which that loop closes exactly. The rational-expectations equilibrium (Definition 1.2) is a fixed point of a map on the forecast's coefficients (Proposition 2.2 in Section 2). The fixed point has a closed form. Iterating the map converges from every start only when \(\varsigma < \beta\), that is, when the supply response is smaller than the demand slope (Proposition 3.2; Definition 1.1 defines both). The lab checks the fixed point and the error ratios (Lemma 3.1) exactly. The model is a stated special case, and the lesson makes no claim about any market.

1A linear-normal model with a public signal

Assumptions
  1. A1 (parameters). The constants \(\alpha\) and \(\delta\) are real, \(\beta > 0\), and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).
  2. A2 (linear forecasts). The forecast of the price given the public signal \(z\), which every trader observes, is restricted to the family of affine functions \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\), with \(c_0, c_1 \in \R\). An affine function is a constant plus a multiple of \(z\).
  3. A3 (independent normal shocks). The public signal \(z\), the demand shock \(u\) and the supply shock \(\upsilon\) are independent. Each has mean zero: \(z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_z^2)\) with \(\sigma_z^2 > 0\), \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\) and \(\upsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\upsilon^2)\) with \(\sigma_u^2, \sigma_\upsilon^2 \ge 0\).
Definition 1.1 (the model's market)

Demand for the risky asset is \(d(p) = \alpha - \beta p + u\), where \(p\) is the price. The public signal \(z\) is observed by every trader. Supply is produced in advance on the basis of the forecast \(m(z)\) of the price, so the total supply is \(q = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\). The market clears when \(d(p) = q\). Given a forecast function \(m\), the clearing price is the unique solution in \(p\) of \(\alpha - \beta p + u = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\), namely

\[ p_m = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\, m(z) - \delta z + u - \upsilon}{\beta}. \]

The solution is unique because \(\beta > 0\). The price \(p_m\) is a function of \((z, u, \upsilon)\). The parameter \(\varsigma\) is the supply response to the forecast, and \(\delta\) is the supply response to the public signal.

Definition 1.2 (rational-expectations equilibrium)

Under A1 to A3, a forecast \(m\) is a rational-expectations equilibrium when the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price the market produces: \(m(z) = \E[\,p_m \mid z\,]\) for every real \(z\). The conditional expectation of \(p_m\) given \(z\) is a function of \(z\) alone. Lemma 2.1 computes it for the affine forecasts that Section 2 uses.

2The conditional law of the price and the fixed point

Lemma 2.1 (the forecast of the market price)

Under A1 to A3, for every affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\),

\[ \E[\,p_m \mid z\,] = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\,(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z}{\beta}, \]

and the conditional law of \(p_m\) given \(z\) is \(\Normal\bigl(\E[p_m \mid z],\ (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\bigr)\).

Proof. Write \(p_m = g(z) + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), where \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\) is a function of \(z\) alone. A function of the conditioning variable is known given that variable, so \(\E[g(z) \mid z] = g(z)\). The variable \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\) by A3, so its conditional expectation given \(z\) equals its unconditional expectation, which is zero. This gives the formula. Next, \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\), so its conditional variance is its unconditional variance, \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). Adding the function \(g(z)\), which is fixed given \(z\), shifts the mean and leaves the variance unchanged. Since \(u\) and \(\upsilon\) are normal and independent of \(z\), the conditional law is normal. \(\square\)

Proposition 2.2 (the fixed point of the coefficient map)

Under A1 to A3, an affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\) is a rational-expectations equilibrium if and only if \((c_0, c_1)\) is a fixed point of the coefficient map

\[ \Psi(c_0, c_1) = \Bigl(\frac{\alpha - \varsigma c_0}{\beta},\ -\frac{\varsigma c_1 + \delta}{\beta}\Bigr). \]

The unique fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\) and \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), and the equilibrium price is \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\).

Proof. By Lemma 2.1, the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price exactly when \(c_0 + c_1 z = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0 - \varsigma c_1 z - \delta z)/\beta\) for every real \(z\). Two affine functions agree for every \(z\) exactly when their constant terms and their slopes agree, so this is the pair of equations \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\) and \(\beta c_1 = -(\varsigma c_1 + \delta)\). These say \(\Psi(c_0, c_1) = (c_0, c_1)\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\) by A1, each equation has exactly one solution, namely \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\). Substituting the fixed point into \(p_m\) gives \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). \(\square\)

Corollary 2.3 (the equilibrium forecast and its error)

Under A1 to A3, at the rational-expectations equilibrium of Proposition 2.2, \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), the forecast error \(p - \E[p \mid z]\) has conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\), and the unconditional variance of \(p\) is \((c_1^\ast)^2 \sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).

Proof. The first two statements follow from Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.1, since \(p - \E[p \mid z] = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). For the third, \(\Var(p) = (c_1^\ast)^2 \Var(z) + \Var\bigl((u - \upsilon)/\beta\bigr)\), because \(z\) and \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) are independent and so uncorrelated, and the constant \(c_0^\ast\) adds nothing to the variance. The variance of \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). \(\square\)

3Iterating the coefficient map

Lemma 3.1 (the error recursion)

Under A1 (so that \(\beta > 0\)), let \(c^\ast = (c_0^\ast, c_1^\ast)\) be the fixed point of Proposition 2.2, and let \(e_k = c_k - c^\ast\) be the error of the iterate \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\). Then each component of the error satisfies \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)\, e_k\) exactly, and therefore \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).

Proof. Since \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\), the first component gives \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)\). Since \(c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta\), the second component gives \(c_{1,k+1} - c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_{1,k} + \delta)/\beta + (\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{1,k} - c_1^\ast)\). Induction gives the power formula. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.2 (when iteration converges)

Under A1 to A3, if \(\varsigma < \beta\), the iterates \(c_k\) converge to \(c^\ast\) from every starting point. If \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) and the starting point differs from \(c^\ast\) in some component, the error in that component does not converge to zero.

Proof. By Lemma 3.1 the error in each component has absolute value \((\varsigma/\beta)^k\) times its starting absolute value. If \(\varsigma < \beta\), then \(\varsigma/\beta < 1\), so \((\varsigma/\beta)^k \to 0\). If \(\varsigma = \beta\), the absolute error stays constant, and it is nonzero when the starting error is nonzero. If \(\varsigma > \beta\), it grows. In both cases the error does not converge to zero. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.3 (a damped step can reach the fixed point at once)

Under A1 to A3, consider the damped step \(c \mapsto (1 - \nu) c + \nu\, \Psi(c)\) with weight \(\nu\). Its error factor in each component is \(1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\). With \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), the factor is zero, so one damped step from any starting point gives \(c^\ast\).

Proof. Subtracting \(c^\ast\) from the damped step and using \(\Psi(c) - c^\ast = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c - c^\ast)\) from Lemma 3.1 gives an error \(\bigl[(1 - \nu) - \nu\varsigma/\beta\bigr](c - c^\ast) = \bigl[1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\bigr](c - c^\ast)\). For \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), \(\nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta) = \frac{\beta}{\beta + \varsigma}\cdot\frac{\beta + \varsigma}{\beta} = 1\), so the factor is zero. \(\square\)

Lab 1 · The fixed point and the iteration, in exact arithmetic

At the base parameters \(\alpha = 10\), \(\beta = 2\), \(\varsigma = 1/2\), \(\delta = 3/4\), the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 4\) and \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the map's residual at it is zero. Iterating from \((0, 0)\), each error ratio is \(-1/4\). With \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\), the ratio is \(-1\) and the \(c_0\) path alternates between 0 and 5. With \(\varsigma = 3\), the ratio is \(-3/2\) and the error grows. A damped step with weight \(4/5\) reaches the fixed point in one step, and the fixed-point identity holds at each of five points of \(z\).

"""E4 L05 lab 1: the coefficient fixed point of a linear rational-expectations model, solved exactly and iterated.

Inputs are illustrative numbers, not data. The equilibrium price is affine in a public signal z, p = c0 + c1 z + noise,
and the traders' forecast of the price given z is the affine function m(z) = c0 + c1 z. The coefficient map sends a
forecast (c0, c1) to the forecast the market produces:

    c0' = (alpha - varsigma * c0) / beta,    c1' = -(varsigma * c1 + delta) / beta.

A rational-expectations equilibrium in this family is a fixed point of the map. The fixed point is
c0* = alpha / (beta + varsigma) and c1* = -delta / (beta + varsigma), which is unique in the family because
beta + varsigma > 0. The error e_k = c_k - c* obeys e_{k+1} = -(varsigma / beta) * e_k exactly, so iteration converges
from any start when varsigma < beta and fails to converge when varsigma >= beta and the start is off the fixed point.

Each parameter set that Parts B to D iterate is checked by check_params before the iteration runs: beta must be
positive and varsigma nonnegative, otherwise the lab raises ValueError.

Part A: the closed-form fixed point, and the residual of the map at it, which is exactly zero.
Part B: iteration from (0, 0) at the base parameters; the ratio of successive errors is -varsigma / beta at every step.
Part C: the same ratio when varsigma equals beta: the iteration does not converge.
Part D: the same ratio when varsigma exceeds beta: the error grows.
Part E: a damped step with weight beta / (beta + varsigma) reaches the fixed point in one step.
Part F: the fixed-point identity m(z) = (alpha - varsigma m(z) - delta z) / beta on a five-point grid of z, exact.

All values are exact Fractions, and no random draws are used, so every run prints the same lines.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction

Coeffs = tuple[Fraction, Fraction]
Params = tuple[Fraction, Fraction, Fraction, Fraction]

BASE: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(1, 2), Fraction(3, 4))
EQUAL: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(2), Fraction(3, 4))
LARGE: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(3), Fraction(3, 4))
Z_GRID: tuple[Fraction, ...] = tuple(Fraction(z) for z in (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2))


def check_params(params: Params) -> None:
    """Raise ValueError unless beta is positive and varsigma is nonnegative."""
    _, beta, varsigma, _ = params
    if beta <= 0:
        raise ValueError("demand slope beta must be positive")
    if varsigma < 0:
        raise ValueError("supply response varsigma must be nonnegative")


def coefficient_map(c: Coeffs, params: Params) -> Coeffs:
    """One step of the map from a forecast (c0, c1) to the forecast the market produces, exact in Fractions."""
    alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = params
    c0, c1 = c
    return ((alpha - varsigma * c0) / beta, -(varsigma * c1 + delta) / beta)


def fixed_point(params: Params) -> Coeffs:
    """The closed-form fixed point (alpha / (beta + varsigma), -delta / (beta + varsigma))."""
    alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = params
    return (alpha / (beta + varsigma), -delta / (beta + varsigma))


def iterate(start: Coeffs, params: Params, steps: int) -> list[Coeffs]:
    """The path of the map from the start: the start, then steps further iterates."""
    path = [start]
    for _ in range(steps):
        path.append(coefficient_map(path[-1], params))
    return path


def error_ratios(path: list[Coeffs], target: Coeffs, index: int) -> list[str]:
    """The ratio of successive errors in coefficient index (0 for c0, 1 for c1), printed exactly.

    A zero error is reported as such, because the ratio is then undefined.
    """
    errors = [c[index] - target[index] for c in path]
    out: list[str] = []
    for k in range(len(errors) - 1):
        if errors[k] == 0:
            out.append("zero-error")
        else:
            out.append(str(errors[k + 1] / errors[k]))
    return out


def show(label: str, params: Params, steps: int) -> None:
    """Print the fixed point, the iterates from (0, 0) and the exact ratio of successive errors in each coefficient."""
    check_params(params)
    target = fixed_point(params)
    path = iterate((Fraction(0), Fraction(0)), params, steps)
    print(f"{label} fixed_point c0 {target[0]} c1 {target[1]}")
    print(f"{label} c0_path {[str(c[0]) for c in path]}")
    print(f"{label} c1_path {[str(c[1]) for c in path]}")
    print(f"{label} c0_error_ratios {error_ratios(path, target, 0)}")
    print(f"{label} c1_error_ratios {error_ratios(path, target, 1)}")


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A to F. Every value is an exact Fraction, so the output is reproducible."""
    alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = BASE
    print("Part A: closed-form fixed point and residual of the map")
    target = fixed_point(BASE)
    residual = coefficient_map(target, BASE)
    print(f"base alpha {alpha} beta {beta} varsigma {varsigma} delta {delta}")
    print(f"fixed_point c0 {target[0]} c1 {target[1]}")
    print(f"residual_c0 {residual[0] - target[0]} residual_c1 {residual[1] - target[1]}")

    print("Part B: iteration at the base parameters from (0, 0)")
    show("base", BASE, 8)

    print("Part C: varsigma equal to beta, the iteration does not converge")
    show("equal", EQUAL, 6)

    print("Part D: varsigma above beta, the error grows")
    show("large", LARGE, 6)

    print("Part E: damped step with weight beta / (beta + varsigma) at the base parameters")
    weight = beta / (beta + varsigma)
    start = (Fraction(0), Fraction(0))
    image = coefficient_map(start, BASE)
    damped = ((1 - weight) * start[0] + weight * image[0], (1 - weight) * start[1] + weight * image[1])
    print(f"weight {weight} damped_step c0 {damped[0]} c1 {damped[1]} equals_fixed_point {damped == target}")

    print("Part F: fixed-point identity on the five-point grid of z, exact")
    c0, c1 = target
    checks: list[bool] = []
    for z in Z_GRID:
        m_z = c0 + c1 * z
        checks.append(m_z == (alpha - varsigma * m_z - delta * z) / beta)
    print(f"z_grid {[str(z) for z in Z_GRID]} identity_holds {checks}")


main()

4Notes on the model

Remark 4.1 (what Proposition 2.2 covers)

The fixed point is found within the affine family of A2. Proposition 2.2 shows that this family contains exactly one rational-expectations equilibrium. It does not show that no non-affine forecast is an equilibrium of the model, because the argument restricts the search to affine forecasts.

5Where the canon breaks

The equilibrium forecast is affine by construction, since A2 restricts the search to affine forecasts. The result is therefore a property of this model, not of rational expectations in general. Three limits apply.

The search is restricted to affine forecasts. A2 is an assumption, not a consequence of rationality. Remark 4.1 records what the restriction does and does not establish.

Existence in the family does not give convergence of naive iteration. Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for all \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\). Proposition 3.2 shows that repeated application of \(\Psi\) converges from every starting point only when \(\varsigma < \beta\). Lab 1 Parts C and D show the two failures. Proposition 3.3 gives a damped step that avoids the failure in this affine case, which decouples into two one-dimensional affine maps.

The forecasts are correct by construction, inside the model. Definition 1.2 takes the traders to know the price rule, the distribution of the shocks and the supply response, because the forecast is the conditional expectation of the price that the model produces. Whether real forecasts have this property is a separate empirical question. This lesson makes no claim about it.

The phrase rational expectations appears in the title of Muth (1961). Only the title is used from that work. The model here is a stated special case, and this lesson uses no result from that paper.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A rational forecast of the price is exact.”

    Corollary 2.3 gives a conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\) for the forecast error. This is zero only when both shocks have zero variance, and A3 allows positive variances. The forecast is unbiased, in the sense that its error has mean zero given \(z\), but it is not exact.

  2. “A rational-expectations equilibrium exists only when repeated iteration of the forecast map converges.”

    Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for every \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\), with \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Proposition 3.2 is about convergence of one particular procedure. Lab 1 Part C has \(\varsigma = \beta\): the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 5/2\), but the iterates alternate between 0 and 5 and never reach it.

  3. “The equilibrium forecast of the price is the unconditional mean of the price.”

    The forecast is \(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), which varies with \(z\) whenever \(\delta \ne 0\), because \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\). At the base parameters of Lab 1 the slope is \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the printed fixed point shows it. The unconditional mean is \(c_0^\ast\), because \(\E z = \E u = \E\upsilon = 0\) by A3 gives \(\E p = c_0^\ast\). It is a different object from the forecast.

7Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Problems in the Empirical tier use the output of Lab 1, and no other data.

Mechanics

M1 · The fixed point at new parameters

Let \(\alpha = 12\), \(\beta = 3\), \(\varsigma = 1\) and \(\delta = 2\). Find \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\), and check that \(\Psi\) maps the fixed point to itself.

Solution

Here \(\beta + \varsigma = 4\), so \(c_0^\ast = 12/4 = 3\) and \(c_1^\ast = -2/4 = -1/2\). Check the first component: \((12 - 1\cdot 3)/3 = 9/3 = 3\). Check the second: \(-(1\cdot(-1/2) + 2)/3 = -(3/2)/3 = -1/2\). Both components are unchanged, so \(\Psi(c^\ast) = c^\ast\).

M2 · One step of the error recursion

With the parameters of M1, start the iteration at \((0, 0)\). Compute the error ratio \(e_1/e_0\) in each component and compare it with \(-\varsigma/\beta\).

Solution

The first iterate is \(\Psi(0,0) = (12/3,\ -2/3) = (4, -2/3)\). The starting error in \(c_0\) is \(0 - 3 = -3\), and the next error is \(4 - 3 = 1\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). In \(c_1\) the starting error is \(0 - (-1/2) = 1/2\), and the next error is \(-2/3 - (-1/2) = -1/6\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). Both equal \(-\varsigma/\beta = -1/3\), as Lemma 3.1 states.

M3 · The conditional law of the price

Let \(\beta = 2\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 4\) and \(\sigma_\upsilon^2 = 1\). Find the conditional variance of the price given \(z\) at the equilibrium of Proposition 2.2.

Solution

By Corollary 2.3 the conditional variance is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (4 + 1)/4 = 5/4\). It does not depend on \(z\), and the conditional law is \(\Normal\bigl(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z,\ 5/4\bigr)\).

Proof

P1 · The conditional expectation in Lemma 2.1

Show that \(\E[\,(u - \upsilon)/\beta \mid z\,] = 0\) under A3, and deduce the formula for \(\E[p_m \mid z]\) in Lemma 2.1.

Solution

Under A3 the vector \((u, \upsilon)\) is independent of \(z\). For an integrable function \(h\) of an independent variable, \(\E[h(u,\upsilon) \mid z] = \E[h(u,\upsilon)]\). With \(h = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), this gives \(\E[(u-\upsilon)/\beta] = (\E u - \E\upsilon)/\beta = 0\). Since \(p_m = g(z) + (u-\upsilon)/\beta\) with \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\), linearity gives \(\E[p_m \mid z] = g(z) + 0\), which is the formula of Lemma 2.1.

P2 · Uniqueness in the affine family

Show that Proposition 2.2 has exactly one fixed point in the affine family, for every \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).

Solution

The first component of the fixed-point condition is \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\), equivalently \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma \ge \beta > 0\), this has exactly one solution \(c_0 = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\). The second is \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which likewise has exactly one solution. So the pair is unique.

P3 · The error recursion

Prove Lemma 3.1 for the first component, starting from \(c_{0,k+1} = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta\) and the identity \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\).

Solution

Subtract the two identities: \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = \bigl[(\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k}) - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)\bigr]/\beta = -\varsigma(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)/\beta\). So \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)e_k\). Induction on \(k\) gives \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).

Empirical

E1 · When the path is within 0.01 of the fixed point

Using Lab 1 Part B at the base parameters, find the first iterate of \(c_0\) that lies within 0.01 of \(c_0^\ast = 4\), and check it against the printed path.

Solution

By Lemma 3.1 the error after \(k\) steps is \(e_k = (-1/4)^k(-4)\), so \(|e_k| = 4/4^k\). We need \(4/4^k < 1/100\), which requires \(4^k > 400\), so \(k \ge 5\). At \(k = 4\), \(|e_4| = 4/256 = 1/64 \approx 0.0156\), which is too large. At \(k = 5\), \(|e_5| = 4/1024 = 1/256 \approx 0.0039\). The printed sixth entry of the \(c_0\) path is \(1025/256\), and \(1025/256 - 4 = 1/256\), which agrees.

E2 · Why the \(\varsigma = \beta\) path does not settle

Lab 1 Part C prints a \(c_0\) path that alternates between 0 and 5 at \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\). Explain the alternation from the error ratio, and state the starting point at which the path would stay put.

Solution

The printed ratio of successive errors in \(c_0\) is \(-1\), which is \(-\varsigma/\beta\) at \(\varsigma = \beta\). Each step flips the sign of the error and keeps its size, so the iterates swap between two values symmetric about the fixed point \(5/2\). The path stays put at \(c_0 = 5/2\): then \(\Psi(5/2) = (10 - 2\cdot 5/2)/2 = 5/2\), so the starting error is zero, and Lemma 3.1 keeps it at zero.

E3 · Growth of the error at \(\varsigma = 3\)

At \(\varsigma = 3\) and \(\beta = 2\), Lab 1 Part D prints a \(c_0\) path starting \(0, 5, -5/2, 35/4\). Verify the error ratio from the path, and compute the error \(e_3\).

Solution

Here \(c_0^\ast = 10/(2 + 3) = 2\). The errors are \(e_0 = -2\), \(e_1 = 3\), \(e_2 = -9/2\) and \(e_3 = 35/4 - 2 = 27/4\). Each ratio is \(-3/2\), which equals \(-\varsigma/\beta = -3/2\) by Lemma 3.1. The error grows by a factor of \(3/2\) and alternates in sign at each step.

Research

R1 · The damped weight at \(\varsigma = \beta\)

At \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\) and \(\alpha = 10\), find the damping weight of Proposition 3.3, and show that one damped step from \((0,0)\) gives the fixed point in the first component.

Solution

The weight is \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma) = 2/4 = 1/2\). The first entry of \(\Psi(0,0)\) is \(10/2 = 5\). The damped step gives \((1 - 1/2)\cdot 0 + (1/2)\cdot 5 = 5/2\), which is \(c_0^\ast = 10/4 = 5/2\).

R2 · The price with no supply response to the signal

Let \(\delta = 0\). Show that the equilibrium forecast \(\E[p \mid z]\) does not depend on \(z\), and find the unconditional variance of the price.

Solution

With \(\delta = 0\), \(c_1^\ast = -0/(\beta + \varsigma) = 0\), so \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast\) for every \(z\), which does not depend on \(z\). By Corollary 2.3 the unconditional variance is \((c_1^\ast)^2\sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).

R3 · Existence and convergence are different questions

Explain, in one paragraph and with reference to Propositions 2.2 and 3.2, why a rational-expectations equilibrium can exist in the affine family even when repeated iteration of \(\Psi\) does not converge.

Solution

Proposition 2.2 finds the fixed point from the two linear equations \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\) and \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which have a solution whenever \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Existence therefore needs only the algebra of those equations. Proposition 3.2 concerns a different question, namely whether the particular sequence \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\) reaches the fixed point. With \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) the error in each component does not shrink, so this sequence fails to converge, while the fixed point is still there to be found by other means, such as the damped step of Proposition 3.3.

9Retrieval quiz

  1. In Proposition 2.2, what is the fixed point \(c_0^\ast\) of the coefficient map?

    Options

    The first component of the fixed-point condition is \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\). The supply response \(\varsigma\) enters the denominator.

  2. At the base parameters \(\alpha = 10\), \(\beta = 2\), \(\varsigma = 1/2\), what is \(c_0^\ast\)?

    \(c_0^\ast = 10/(2 + 1/2) = 10/(5/2) = 4\), as Lab 1 Part A prints.

  3. Under A1 to A3, when does repeated iteration of the coefficient map converge from every starting point?

    Options

    Lemma 3.1 gives the error factor \(-\varsigma/\beta\), and the error vanishes in the limit only when \(|\varsigma/\beta| < 1\), that is, \(\varsigma < \beta\) (Proposition 3.2).

  4. At the base parameters, what is the ratio of successive errors in \(c_0\) under the iteration from \((0,0)\)?

    The ratio is \(-\varsigma/\beta = -(1/2)/2 = -1/4\), which Lab 1 Part B prints at every step.

  5. At \(\varsigma = \beta\), what happens to the error of the iteration started at \((0,0)\)?

    Options

    With \(\varsigma = \beta\) the error factor is \(-1\). Lab 1 Part C prints the ratio \(-1\) and the alternating path 0, 5, 0, 5.

  6. For the damped step \(c \mapsto (1 - \nu)c + \nu \Psi(c)\), which weight gives the fixed point in one step?

    Options

    The error factor is \(1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\), which is zero exactly at \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\) (Proposition 3.3). At \(\nu = 1\) the factor is \(-\varsigma/\beta\), which is the plain iteration.

  7. With \(\sigma_u^2 = 4\), \(\sigma_\upsilon^2 = 1\) and \(\beta = 2\), what is the conditional variance of the price given \(z\)?

    The conditional variance is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (4 + 1)/4 = 5/4 = 1.25\) (Corollary 2.3 and Problem M3).

  8. When \(\delta = 0\), what is the equilibrium forecast \(\E[p \mid z]\)?

    Options

    With \(\delta = 0\), \(c_1^\ast = 0\), so the forecast is the constant \(c_0^\ast\) (Problem R2).
