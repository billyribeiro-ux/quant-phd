Market-Structure Engine

An exchange charges a fee per trade. You will write the module that computes what a monopoly, two exchanges competing on quantity, and two exchanges competing on fee each produce: the trade volume, the fee, the exchange profit, the consumer surplus, the welfare and the deadweight loss. All money is in whole cents. You will prove the outcomes the module reports, test the module, and write a memo that recommends a structure.

"""Rung 1 worked example: the monopoly, Cournot and Bertrand outcomes of the reference case a = 120, b = 1, c = 20.

Standard library only (fractions, math). No randomness and no network. The three inputs are the reference case of the
project specification: a = 120 trades at a fee of zero, b = 1 trade per cent, c = 20 cents per trade. They are inputs
of the specification, not market data. The gap is d = a - b*c = 100. The lab computes each structure's exact volume
and fee, then profit, consumer surplus, welfare and deadweight loss as Fractions, and rounds each output once, a tie
going up. It prints the exact value and the rounded value. Expected: monopoly volume 50 trades and fee 70 cents;
Cournot volume 200/3 trades (67 after rounding) and fee 160/3 cents (53 after rounding); Bertrand volume 100 trades and
fee 20 cents. Profit is (fee - c) times volume. Consumer surplus is Q^2/(2b). Welfare is consumer surplus plus profit.
The deadweight loss is (d - Q)^2/(2b), which is zero for Bertrand. The lab checks the reference case only.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from fractions import Fraction

A = 120  # trades at a fee of zero
B = 1  # trades per cent that the fee removes
C = 20  # cents per trade, the cost to the exchange


def half_up(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole number to a non-negative exact value, a tie going up."""
    return math.floor(x + Fraction(1, 2))


def volume_and_fee(structure: str) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction]:
    """The exact volume in trades and fee in cents of one structure, from the closed forms of the specification."""
    d = Fraction(A - B * C)
    if structure == "monopoly":
        return d / 2, Fraction(A + B * C, 2 * B)
    if structure == "cournot":
        return 2 * d / 3, Fraction(A + 2 * B * C, 3 * B)
    return d, Fraction(C)


def main() -> None:
    d = A - B * C
    # Step 1. The gap d = a - b*c is the trade volume at which the fee equals the cost, the competitive benchmark.
    print(f"step 1: gap d = a - b*c = {d} trades")
    for structure in ("monopoly", "cournot", "bertrand"):
        volume, fee = volume_and_fee(structure)
        # Step 2. Profit per trade is fee minus cost, and profit is that margin times the volume.
        profit = (fee - C) * volume
        # Step 3. Consumer surplus is the area under the inverse demand (a - t)/b above the fee: Q^2/(2b).
        consumer = volume * volume / (2 * B)
        # Step 4. Welfare is consumer surplus plus profit, and the deadweight loss is the gap from the benchmark.
        welfare = consumer + profit
        deadweight = (d - volume) ** 2 / (2 * B)
        print(f"{structure}: volume {volume} trades -> {half_up(volume)}; fee {fee} cents -> {half_up(fee)}")
        print(
            f"{structure}: profit {profit} -> {half_up(profit)}; consumer surplus {consumer} -> {half_up(consumer)}; "
            f"welfare {welfare} -> {half_up(welfare)}; deadweight loss {deadweight} -> {half_up(deadweight)} cents"
        )
    # Step 5. The Bertrand fee equals the cost, so the Bertrand exchanges earn nothing and the market is competitive.
    bertrand_volume, bertrand_fee = volume_and_fee("bertrand")
    print("bertrand fee equals cost:", bertrand_fee == C, "| volume equals the gap:", bertrand_volume == d)

Rung 3 · The model and its closed forms

The project asks how much the market structure matters, so each result it reports is derived from one model. You prove the closed forms in this section and use them in the module and the memo. The model is a proposal the project adopts. It is not estimated from data.

Definitions

  • Model. The inputs are whole numbers \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) with \(a > bc > 0\). \(a\) is the trade volume at a fee of zero, in trades. \(b\) is the number of trades that the fee removes per cent, in trades per cent. \(c\) is the constant cost to the exchange of one trade, in cents per trade. The gap is \(d = a - bc > 0\), in trades.
  • Inverse demand. At total volume \(Q\) trades, the fee that clears the market is \(f(Q) = (a - Q)/b\), in cents per trade. The volume at fee \(f\) is \(a - bf\).
  • Monopoly. One exchange chooses the volume \(Q\) and so the fee \(f(Q)\). Its profit is \((f(Q) - c)\,Q\), in cents.
  • Cournot. Two exchanges choose their volumes \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) at the same time, each without observing the other. The total volume is \(Q = q_1 + q_2\), and the fee is \(f(Q)\). The profit of exchange \(i\) is \((f(Q) - c)\,q_i\).
  • Bertrand. Two identical exchanges choose their fees \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) at the same time, each in the interval from 0 to \(a/b\). The exchange with the lower fee serves the whole demand \(a - bf\) at its fee, and the two split the demand equally at a tie.
  • Best response. The best response of an exchange to a rival's choice is the choice that maximises its own profit given that rival choice.
  • Equilibrium, in words. A profile of choices is an equilibrium when each exchange's choice is its best response to the other's choice. No exchange can gain by changing its choice alone.
  • Consumer surplus. The area between the inverse demand and the fee, over the volume: \(\int_0^Q \big(f(t) - f(Q)\big)\,dt\), in cents.
  • Welfare. In this project, welfare is total surplus: consumer surplus plus exchange profit. It is the gross value of the volume, \(\int_0^Q f(t)\,dt\), less the cost \(cQ\). Welfare here is the total surplus of this one market, not a general welfare criterion.
  • Deadweight loss. The welfare that the competitive benchmark produces, less the welfare at the volume \(Q\). The benchmark is volume \(d\), at which the fee equals the cost.

The project covers three structures: monopoly, Cournot and Bertrand. Monopolistic competition, and entry by new exchanges, are outside the project.

Propositions

Each proposition lists the assumptions its proof uses. Each proof is complete, and each is graded T, the grade of a result proved on the page.

Proposition 1 (monopoly). Assume \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), a single exchange, the inverse demand \(f(Q) = (a - Q)/b\), and constant cost \(c\). Then the profit \(\pi(Q) = ((a - Q)/b - c)Q\) has a unique maximiser \(Q^* = d/2\) on \(Q \ge 0\). The fee is \(f(Q^*) = (a + bc)/(2b)\), and the maximal profit is \(d^2/(4b)\).

Proof. Expanding, \(\pi(Q) = -Q^2/b + ((a - bc)/b)\,Q\). Its derivative is \(\pi'(Q) = (a - bc - 2Q)/b = (d - 2Q)/b\), which is zero at \(Q = d/2\). The second derivative is \(-2/b < 0\) for every \(Q\), so \(\pi'\) is strictly decreasing and \(Q = d/2\) is the unique maximiser on \(Q \ge 0\). At \(Q = d/2\) the fee is \(f = (a - d/2)/b = (a + bc)/(2b)\), and the profit is \((f - c)\,d/2 = (d/(2b))(d/2) = d^2/(4b)\), because \(f - c = (a + bc - 2bc)/(2b) = d/(2b)\). \(\square\)

Proposition 2 (Cournot). Assume \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), two exchanges that choose \(q_1, q_2 \ge 0\) at the same time, the inverse demand \(f(Q) = (a - Q)/b\) with \(Q = q_1 + q_2\), and constant cost \(c\). Then the choice of each exchange is \(\max(0, (d - q_j)/2)\) against the rival's \(q_j\). The unique equilibrium is \(q_1 = q_2 = d/3\), with total volume \(Q = 2d/3\), fee \((a + 2bc)/(3b)\), and total exchange profit \(2d^2/(9b)\). After \(k\) steps of the iteration \(q_{k+1} = (d - q_k)/2\) from \(q_0 = 0\), the error is \(|q_k - d/3| = d/(3 \cdot 2^k)\).

Proof. The profit of exchange \(i\) is \(\pi_i = ((a - q_i - q_j)/b - c)\,q_i\), whose derivative in \(q_i\) is \((d - 2q_i - q_j)/b\). Its second derivative is \(-2/b < 0\), so the best response on \(q_i \ge 0\) is the zero of that derivative, or zero if the zero is negative: \(\max(0, (d - q_j)/2)\). Let \((q_1, q_2)\) be an equilibrium. If \(q_1 = 0\), then \(q_2 = \max(0, d/2) = d/2 > 0\), and then \(q_1 = \max(0, (d - d/2)/2) = d/4 > 0\), a contradiction. So \(q_1 > 0\), and by the same argument \(q_2 > 0\). Both are therefore interior, and \(q_i = (d - q_j)/2\) holds for both. Subtracting the two equations gives \(q_1 - q_2 = -(q_1 - q_2)/2\), so \(q_1 = q_2 = q\), and \(2q = d - q\) gives \(q = d/3\). For the iteration, let \(e_k = q_k - d/3\). Then \(e_{k+1} = (d - q_k)/2 - d/3 = -e_k/2\), and \(e_0 = -d/3\), so \(|e_k| = d/(3 \cdot 2^k)\). The total volume is \(2d/3\), the fee is \(f = (a - 2d/3)/b = (a + 2bc)/(3b)\), and each exchange earns \((f - c)\,d/3 = (d/(3b))(d/3) = d^2/(9b)\). \(\square\)

Proposition 3 (Bertrand). Assume \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), two identical exchanges with constant cost \(c\), fees in \([0, a/b]\), and the demand rule of the Definitions: the lower fee takes the whole demand \(a - bf\), and equal fees split it equally. Then the profile \((c, c)\) is an equilibrium, both exchanges earn zero, and the volume is \(d\). No other profile is an equilibrium.

Proof. The profile \((c, c)\) is an equilibrium. At \((c, c)\) the demand \(a - bc = d\) is split equally, so each exchange earns \((c - c)\,d/2 = 0\). A higher fee serves no demand and earns zero, so it gains nothing. A lower fee \(f < c\) serves demand \(a - bf > 0\) at a negative margin, so its profit \((f - c)(a - bf)\) is negative. No other profile is an equilibrium. Let \(f_1 \le f_2\) be the fees of an equilibrium, with \(f_1\) the lower. If \(f_1 < c\), exchange 1 serves a positive share of demand at a negative margin, so its profit is negative. Raising its fee to \(c\) gives it a share of demand at zero margin, or no demand, so its profit is then zero, a gain. Hence \(f_1 \ge c\). If \(f_1 > c\) and \(f_2 > f_1\), exchange 2 earns zero, and charging \((c + f_1)/2\), which lies in \((c, f_1)\) and so below \(f_1\), takes the whole demand at a positive margin, a gain. If \(f_1 = f_2 = f > c\), each earns \((f - c)(a - bf)/2 > 0\). Charging \(g = (f + c)/2\), with \(c < g < f\), takes the whole demand \(a - bg > a - bf\) at margin \(g - c = (f - c)/2\), which earns \((f - c)(a - bg)/2 > (f - c)(a - bf)/2\), a gain. The remaining case, \(f_1 = c < f_2\), is the second case with \(f_1\) replaced by \(c\): exchange 2 earns zero, and charging \((c + f_2)/2 \) gains. So \(f_1 = c\) and \(f_2 = c\). \(\square\)

Proposition 4 (surplus identities). Assume \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), and let \(Q \in [0, d]\) be a volume at the inverse demand \(f(Q) = (a - Q)/b\). Then the consumer surplus is \(Q^2/(2b)\), the exchange profit is \((f(Q) - c)\,Q\), the welfare is their sum, and the deadweight loss is \((d - Q)^2/(2b)\). The deadweight loss is zero at \(Q = d\).

Proof. The consumer surplus is \(\int_0^Q \big((a - t)/b - (a - Q)/b\big)\,dt = \int_0^Q (Q - t)/b\,dt = Q^2/(2b)\). The gross value is \(G(x) = \int_0^x (a - t)/b\,dt = (ax - x^2/2)/b\), so the welfare at volume \(x\) is \(W(x) = G(x) - cx\). Its derivative is \(W'(x) = (a - x)/b - c = (d - x)/b\). Hence \(W(d) - W(Q) = \int_Q^d (d - t)/b\,dt = (d - Q)^2/(2b)\). The welfare at \(Q\) equals consumer surplus plus profit, since \(G(Q) - cQ = Q^2/(2b) + (f(Q) - c)Q\) by the consumer surplus formula and \(f(Q)\,Q = G(Q) - Q^2/(2b)\). \(\square\)

Proposition 5 (ordering). Assume \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\). Then the exact volumes satisfy \(d/2 < 2d/3 < d\), the exact fees satisfy \((a + bc)/(2b) > (a + 2bc)/(3b) > c\), the exact deadweight losses satisfy \(d^2/(8b) > d^2/(18b) > 0\), the exact welfare levels satisfy \(W(d/2) < W(2d/3) < W(d)\) with \(W\) as in Proposition 4, and the monopoly profit exceeds the Cournot total by \(d^2/(36b)\). If \(d > 6b\), the differences of the printed integers keep the same signs.

Proof. Since \(d > 0\), \(2d/3 - d/2 = d/6 > 0\) and \(d - 2d/3 = d/3 > 0\). The fee difference is \((a + bc)/(2b) - (a + 2bc)/(3b) = (3a + 3bc - 2a - 4bc)/(6b) = (a - bc)/(6b) = d/(6b) > 0\), and \((a + 2bc)/(3b) - c = d/(3b) > 0\). The deadweight losses are \((d/2)^2/(2b) = d^2/(8b)\) and \((d/3)^2/(2b) = d^2/(18b)\), and the Bertrand loss is zero. The profit difference is \(d^2/(4b) - 2d^2/(9b) = d^2(9 - 8)/(36b) = d^2/(36b)\). The welfare levels are \(W(x) = G(x) - cx\), with \(W'(x) = (d - x)/b > 0\) on \((0, d)\), so \(W\) increases there and \(W(d/2) < W(2d/3) < W(d)\). Proposition 4 gives \(W(d) - W(2d/3) = d^2/(18b)\) and \(W(d) - W(d/2) = d^2/(8b)\), so \(W(2d/3) - W(d/2) = 5d^2/(72b)\). Rounding moves each exact value by at most one half, so a difference of exact values greater than 1 keeps its sign after rounding. The differences here are \(d/6\), \(d/(6b)\), \(d/3\) and \(5d^2/(72b)\), and \(d/(6b)\) exceeds 1 when \(d > 6b\), which gives the other three too: \(d > 6b\) implies \(d > 6\), and \(d^2 > 36b^2 \ge 14.4\,b\) gives \(5d^2/(72b) > 1\). For \(d^2/(36b)\), the instance guarantees \(d \ge 60\) and \(b \le 3\), so \(d^2/(36b) \ge 33\). \(\square\)

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
An exchange charges a fee per trade for access to its market. The number of exchanges and the way they compete change the trade volume, the fee, the exchange profit and the split of the surplus between traders and exchanges. You quantify how much, for three structures: one exchange (monopoly), two exchanges competing on quantity (Cournot), and two exchanges competing on fee (Bertrand). The engine you write gives the same six outputs for each structure, so that a memo can compare them.
Inputs
Three whole numbers, \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\), with \(a > bc > 0\): \(a\) in trades at a fee of zero, \(b\) in trades per cent, and \(c\) in cents per trade. The autograder's instance comes from grader/instance.py, seeded by your workspace ID. It is SIMULATED: the generator draws \(b\) from 1 to 3, \(c\) from 5 to 60 and the gap \(d = a - bc\) from 60 to 400. None of it is exchange data. The memo uses the reference case, \(a = 120\), \(b = 1\), \(c = 20\), which is an input and not data.
Required outputs
In a repository of your own: market_structure.py at its root, with outcome(structure: str, a: int, b: int, c: int) -> dict[str, int]. The argument structure is one of the strings "monopoly", "cournot" or "bertrand". The returned dictionary has exactly the keys volume_trades, fee_cents, profit_cents, consumer_surplus_cents, welfare_cents and deadweight_loss_cents, and every value is an int. Start from the starter file, whose signature is complete. Also memo.md, the memo described in the brief; run_report.py, which is Lab 3 with its closed-form call replaced by a call to outcome; and self_assessment.md, the summary that the form in the self-assessment section produces.
Constraints
Standard library only. The module prints nothing and warns about nothing. A run in your browser stops after 120 seconds. Each money output is an exact fraction computed from \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\), rounded once to the nearest whole cent, with a tie going up (the value of \(\lfloor x + 1/2 \rfloor\)). volume_trades is the exact volume rounded once, by the same rule, to the nearest whole trade. No output is computed from another output's rounded value. The consumer surplus, the welfare and the deadweight loss use the exact volume. The inputs that raise ValueError are these, and the message states the condition: a structure that is not one of the three names, with the message naming all three; \(a \le bc\), with the message a must exceed b*c; \(b \le 0\), with the message b must be positive; and \(c \le 0\), with the message c must be positive. Any other invalid input may raise any exception or return a value.
Grading mode
Both modes apply. The code is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which runs 57 checks with warnings as errors: the six outputs of each structure against exact closed forms computed separately, the ordering of the outputs, the exception table, the integer types, the rounding of a tie, the purity of the module, and a second instance. The same file runs in your browser and on your machine. It is not secret: its integrity comes from your seeded instance and from a second instance that a fixed answer would also have to match. The memo is graded by rubric, against the six criteria of the memo rubric, each with four observable levels. You score it yourself with the form in the self-assessment section, and you calibrate that scoring on two annotated exemplars.

Conventions

  • Each volume is in trades, each fee and each money value is in cents, and a rate such as \(b\) is in trades per cent. Each output of outcome is an int.
  • The closed forms of Propositions 1 to 3 are the values the module reports. Monopoly: volume \(d/2\), fee \((a + bc)/(2b)\). Cournot: volume \(2d/3\), fee \((a + 2bc)/(3b)\). Bertrand: volume \(d\), fee \(c\). Profit is \((f - c)Q\) for the structure's fee \(f\) and volume \(Q\), so the Cournot profit is the total of the two exchanges.
  • The rounding rule is the one of the Constraints. For the reference case, the printed values are those of Lab 3.

Rung 3 · The memo

The memo runs your module on the reference case, states what the numbers mean, and recommends a structure. Its numbers are the ones Lab 3 prints, so you can check each one before you write it.

The brief

Write for a regulator who approves exchange fee schedules and knows Lessons 5 to 7. Use these five sections, in this order and under these headings, followed by the appendix.

  1. Summary: the case, the three fees, and the one number that most separates the structures, in at most 80 words.
  2. Model and conventions: the inputs with their units, the three structures and their timing, and the rounding rule, with the function of your module that produces each number.
  3. Outcomes: the volume, the fee, the exchange profit, the consumer surplus, the welfare and the deadweight loss of each structure.
  4. Comparison and recommendation: the order of the three outcomes with the sign and the number of each difference, the structure to accept, and what would change that choice.
  5. Limits: linear demand, constant cost and two exchanges, and for each, the direction in which the fee or the volume would move without it.

Appendix A · Proofs. Propositions 1 to 5 of this page, restated in your words, each with its hypotheses and its proof. A proof may use an earlier one.

Length. The body (the five sections) runs 500 to 1000 words. The appendix has no limit. A word is a run of characters between spaces.

Numbers. Every number the memo quotes is either printed by run_report.py with its unit, or derived on the memo's page from the symbols of the model. A money value is quoted as the integer cents the run prints. A volume is quoted as trades, and say when it is rounded.

Scoring. Each criterion scores the highest level whose every element the memo shows. Each level contains the one below it. The memo meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4 · C4 4 · C5 3 · C6 3. The model states each input with its unit and each structure's timing; each closed form is derived with its hypotheses; each money value is printed by the run or derived on the page, and its rounding is named; the comparison gives the sign and number of each difference. It falls short of level 4 for C5, because the one condition it names is a tie rule and not a number whose change would move the choice, and for C6, because the memo does not say in which direction the fee would move under another tie rule.

Summary

For the reference case, \(a = 120\), \(b = 1\), \(c = 20\), the monopoly fee is 70 cents and its welfare 3750 cents. Cournot raises the volume to 67 trades (exact \(200/3\)), lowers the fee to 53 cents and raises the welfare to 4444 cents. Bertrand gives the full volume of 100 trades at 20 cents and the welfare of 5000 cents. I recommend accepting the Bertrand structure, because it has no deadweight loss.

  • Meets C4 level 3 in part: the Summary gives the three fees and the welfare of the monopoly and Cournot, as printed, so the comparison is visible before its section.
Model

The inputs are \(a = 120\) trades at a fee of zero, \(b = 1\) trade per cent and \(c = 20\) cents per trade, with \(d = a - bc = 100\) trades. Monopoly: one exchange chooses the volume. Cournot: two exchanges choose their volumes at the same time. Bertrand: two exchanges choose their fees at the same time. Demand is the inverse demand \((a - Q)/b\), and the cost is constant at \(c\).

  • Meets C1 level 4: lists the inputs with their units and gives the timing of each structure.
Derivations

The monopoly profit \(((a - Q)/b - c)\,Q\) has derivative \((d - 2Q)/b\), which is zero at \(Q = d/2 = 50\), so the fee is \((a + bc)/(2b) = 70\) cents. An exchange's Cournot choice against a rival's \(q_j\) solves \((d - 2q_i - q_j)/b = 0\), that is \(q_i = (d - q_j)/2\). The symmetric solution is \(q = d/3\), so the volume is \(2d/3 = 200/3\) trades and the fee \((a + 2bc)/(3b) = 160/3\) cents. For an equilibrium, the difference \(x = q_1 - q_2\) satisfies \(x = -x/2\), so \(x = 0\), and the symmetric point is the only equilibrium.

  • Meets C2 level 4: shows the case analysis that excludes a zero quantity and the symmetric solution.
Welfare

The deadweight loss is \((d - Q)^2/(2b)\), computed on the exact volume. Monopoly: \(50^2/2 = 1250\) cents, printed as 1250. Cournot: \((100/3)^2/2 = 5000/9\), which is 555.56 and is printed as 556, the nearest cent. Bertrand: 0 cents. Each money value is an exact fraction rounded once to the nearest cent, with a tie going up.

  • Meets C3 level 4: states which values are exact before rounding, and that the loss uses the exact volume.
Comparison

The volumes rise from 50 to 67 to 100 trades as printed (exact 50, \(200/3\) and 100), with exact differences \(50/3\) and \(100/3\), both positive. The fees fall from 70 to 53 to 20 cents as printed (exact 70, \(160/3\) and 20): the monopoly fee exceeds the Cournot fee by \(50/3 = d/(6b)\), a positive difference. The welfare rises from 3750 cents under monopoly to 4444 under Cournot and 5000 under Bertrand, and each difference is positive. The monopoly profit is 2500 cents and the Cournot total is 2222 cents as printed (exact \(20000/9\)), so the monopoly profit exceeds it by \(d^2/(36b) = 10000/36 = 277.8\) cents, a positive difference.

  • Meets C4 level 4: orders the three volumes, fees and welfare levels, with the sign and printed number of each difference, and states the exact profit difference \(d^2/(36b)\) with its sign and value.
Recommendation

I recommend that the regulator accept the Bertrand structure. Of the three, it gives the highest welfare, 5000 cents, and no deadweight loss, and its fee is the cost, 20 cents. The choice would change under a different tie rule, the condition that the Limits section names.

  • Meets C5 level 3: names the structure, justifies it from the printed welfare and fee numbers, and names one condition under which the choice would change.
Limits

Linear demand: if the demand were curved, the volumes and fees would change, and the closed forms here would not hold. Constant cost: a higher \(c\) raises each of the three fees, since the monopoly fee \((a + bc)/(2b)\), the Cournot fee \((a + 2bc)/(3b)\) and the Bertrand fee \(c\) all increase in \(c\). Two exchanges: the Cournot values are for exactly two exchanges, and the memo draws no conclusion for other numbers of exchanges. Tie rule: the Bertrand result uses the equal split of Proposition 3, and the memo does not say how the fee would move under another rule.

  • Meets C6 level 3: names the three limits, and for each, what changes without it.
  • Misses C6 level 4: the direction in which the fee would move without the tie rule is not stated.

Rung 4 · Stretch

  • Three Cournot exchanges. State the symmetric Cournot volume for \(n\) exchanges with inverse demand \((a - Q)/b\) and constant cost \(c\), prove it from the reaction functions, and check it against the two-exchange closed form.
  • A curved demand. Replace the linear inverse demand by a second one, with the same choke fee \(a/b\), and state as a hypothesis how the monopoly fee moves. Test the hypothesis on a grid.
  • Ties in Bertrand. Give the Bertrand outcome under a rule that splits demand in proportion to capacity, and prove which structure then has the lowest deadweight loss.
