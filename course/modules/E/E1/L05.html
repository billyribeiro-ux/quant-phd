A seller that faces the whole market demand chooses its own price, and buyers respond along that demand curve. This lesson solves that choice for linear demand and a constant marginal cost. Monopoly pricing, defined in §1, sets a price above cost, and its markup, the Lerner index (defined in §1), equals the inverse of the absolute price elasticity, \(1/|\varepsilon|\), from Lesson L04 at the chosen price. The profit is quadratic in price, so completing the square (Proposition 5.2) solves it. Lab 1 checks the closed form by a grid search in exact arithmetic, and checks the markup identity for three cases. Lesson L06 changes the setting to two sellers.

1The monopoly problem and its solution for linear demand

Definition 5.1 (monopoly pricing)

Monopoly pricing is the choice of a single seller that faces the market demand curve \(Q(p)\) and sets the price \(p\) at which it sells. Its profit at price \(p\) is \(\pi(p) = (p - c)\,Q(p)\), where \(c\) is the constant marginal cost in cents per unit, with no fixed cost. The seller chooses \(p\) to maximize \(\pi(p)\) over the prices at which \(Q(p) \ge 0\). A seller that chooses a price in this way is a monopolist. The model has one price for all the units the seller sells.

Assumption M (linear demand and constant cost)

Let \(a > 0\), \(b > 0\) and \(c \ge 0\), with \(a > bc\). Demand is \(Q(p) = a - bp\) on the prices \(0 \le p \le a/b\), where \(Q(p) \ge 0\). The price \(a/b\), at which demand is zero, is the choke price. The cost is \(c\) per unit. The condition \(a > bc\) says that the quantity sold at marginal cost is positive. Units are cents per unit for prices and units for quantities, so \(\pi\) is in cents.

Proposition 5.2 (the monopoly price, quantity and profit)

Under Assumption M, the profit \(\pi\) on \(0 \le p \le a/b\) has a unique maximizer \[ p_m = \frac{a + bc}{2b}. \] At \(p_m\), the quantity is \(Q_m = (a - bc)/2\) and the profit is \(\pi_m = (a - bc)^{2}/(4b)\). The monopoly price lies strictly between \(c\) and the choke price \(a/b\).

Proof

Expanding the profit gives \(\pi(p) = (p - c)(a - bp) = -bp^{2} + (a + bc)p - ac\). Let \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b)\). Then \[ -b(p - p_m)^{2} = -bp^{2} + 2bp_m\,p - bp_m^{2} = -bp^{2} + (a + bc)\,p - bp_m^{2}, \] so \(\pi(p) = -b(p - p_m)^{2} + \bigl(bp_m^{2} - ac\bigr)\). Since \(b\,p_m^{2} = (a + bc)^{2}/(4b)\), the constant is \(bp_m^{2} - ac = \bigl[(a + bc)^{2} - 4abc\bigr]/(4b) = (a - bc)^{2}/(4b)\), because \((a + bc)^{2} - 4abc = (a - bc)^{2}\). So \[ \pi(p) = \frac{(a - bc)^{2}}{4b} - b\,(p - p_m)^{2}. \] Because \(b > 0\), the term \(-b(p - p_m)^{2}\) is zero at \(p = p_m\) and strictly negative elsewhere, so \(p_m\) is the unique maximizer on the whole real line, and in particular on \([0, a/b]\) once \(p_m\) lies in that interval. It does. \(p_m \ge 0\) because \(a + bc > 0\), and \(p_m < a/b\) because \(a + bc < 2a\), which is \(bc < a\). Also \(p_m - c = (a + bc)/(2b) - c = (a - bc)/(2b) > 0\) because \(a > bc\), so \(p_m\) lies strictly between \(c\) and \(a/b\). The quantity is \(Q_m = a - bp_m = a - (a + bc)/2 = (a - bc)/2\), and the profit is \(\pi_m = (a - bc)^{2}/(4b)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 5.3 (with positive cost, the monopoly price is above the revenue-maximising price)

Revenue is \(R(p) = p(a - bp) = ap - bp^{2}\), and \(R'(p) = a - 2bp\) vanishes at \(p = a/(2b)\), the price of Proposition 1.4 (in L04) with \(|\varepsilon| = 1\). The monopoly price exceeds that point by a fixed amount: \[ p_m - \frac{a}{2b} = \frac{c}{2}. \] With \(c > 0\), the monopolist prices above the revenue-maximising price. Its price lies in the elastic region of demand, where revenue falls as price rises. The monopolist maximizes profit, which subtracts cost, and not revenue.

Proposition 5.4 (the Lerner index equals the inverse absolute elasticity at an interior optimum)

Let \(Q\) be differentiable, with \(Q(p) > 0\) and \(Q'(p) < 0\) on an open interval \(I\), and let \(p^{*} > 0\) be an interior maximizer of \(\pi(p) = (p - c)Q(p)\) in \(I\). Then \[ \frac{p^{*} - c}{p^{*}} = \frac{1}{|\varepsilon(p^{*})|}, \] where \(\varepsilon\) is the price elasticity of Definition 1.1 in L04. The left side is the Lerner index, the markup of price over cost as a share of price.

Proof

The function \(\pi\) is differentiable on \(I\). At an interior maximizer, \(\pi'(p^{*}) = 0\). The reason is that if \(\pi'(p^{*}) > 0\), then \(\pi(p^{*} + h) > \pi(p^{*})\) for all small \(h > 0\), and if \(\pi'(p^{*}) < 0\), then \(\pi(p^{*} - h) > \pi(p^{*})\) for all small \(h > 0\). Both contradict maximality. By the product rule, \(\pi'(p^{*}) = Q(p^{*}) + (p^{*} - c)Q'(p^{*}) = 0\). Divide by \(Q(p^{*}) > 0\) to get \((p^{*} - c)\,Q'(p^{*})/Q(p^{*}) = -1\). Write \(Q'(p^{*})/Q(p^{*}) = \varepsilon(p^{*})/p^{*}\), by the definition \(\varepsilon(p^{*}) = p^{*}Q'(p^{*})/Q(p^{*})\), which gives \(\dfrac{p^{*} - c}{p^{*}}\,\varepsilon(p^{*}) = -1\). Since \(Q' < 0\), \(\varepsilon(p^{*}) < 0\), so \(\dfrac{p^{*} - c}{p^{*}} = -1/\varepsilon(p^{*}) = 1/|\varepsilon(p^{*})|\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 5.5 (the Lerner index for linear demand)

Under Assumption M, \(|\varepsilon(p_m)| = (a + bc)/(a - bc)\) and the Lerner index at the monopoly price is \[ \frac{p_m - c}{p_m} = \frac{a - bc}{a + bc} = \frac{1}{|\varepsilon(p_m)|}. \]

Proof

From Proposition 5.2, \(p_m - c = (a + bc)/(2b) - c = (a - bc)/(2b)\). Dividing by \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b)\) gives the Lerner index \((a - bc)/(a + bc)\). The demand elasticity at \(p_m\) is \(\varepsilon(p_m) = -bp_m/(a - bp_m)\). Here \(bp_m = (a + bc)/2\) and \(a - bp_m = (a - bc)/2\), so \(\varepsilon(p_m) = -(a + bc)/(a - bc)\). Both quantities are positive, because \(a > bc\). The two displayed expressions agree. The hypotheses of Proposition 5.4 hold on \(I = (0, a/b)\): \(Q > 0\) there, and \(Q' = -b < 0\). The point \(p_m\) lies in \(I\), because \(p_m > 0\) (since \(a + bc > 0\)) and \(p_m < a/b\) (since \(bc < a\)). By Proposition 5.2 it is the maximizer of \(\pi\) on \(I\). \(\blacksquare\)

Lab 1 · The monopoly price on linear demand, and the Lerner index against 1/|elasticity|

Stated before running. The inputs are illustrative, not data: three cases \((a, b, c)\), with prices in cents per unit. The reference case is \((120, 1, 20)\). The cases \((90, 3, 10)\) and \((200, 4, 5)\) test the same identities away from it. Proposition 5.2 predicts the monopoly prices 70, 20 and 27.5 cents, the quantities 50, 30 and 90, and the profits \((a - bc)^{2}/(4b)\), which are 2500, 300 and 2025 cents. Corollary 5.5 predicts that the Lerner index at the optimum equals \(1/|\varepsilon|\), with values 5/7, 1/2 and 9/11. The grid search of Part A should find the closed-form price in each case, since each price lies on the grid of steps of 1/10 cent. The code uses no random draws and reads no file.

"""E1 L05 lab 1: the monopoly price on linear demand, and the Lerner index against 1/|elasticity| at the optimum.

Inputs are illustrative parameters, not data. Demand is Q(p) = a - b p units, with the price p in cents per unit.
The monopolist has a constant marginal cost of c cents per unit. Three cases are run: the reference case
(a, b, c) = (120, 1, 20), and two further cases that test the same identities away from it. No random draws are
used, and no file is read.

Part A: the monopoly profit (p - c) Q(p) in cents is evaluated on a grid of prices in steps of 1/10 cent, from 0 to
the choke price a/b, in exact Fractions. The grid argmax is compared with the closed form p_m = (a + b c) / (2 b).
Part B: at p_m, the Lerner index (p - c) / p is compared with 1/|e|, where e = -b p / (a - b p) is the price
elasticity of demand. The two are exact Fractions, and the lab checks that they are equal.
Part C: the monopoly quantity, the profit, the slope of the profit at p_m, the second difference of the profit, and
the revenue-maximising price a / (2 b) are printed, and the profit is compared with (a - b c)^2 / (4 b).
The second difference is exact for a quadratic profit.

Fractions are printed as decimals with ten places. The comparisons are made on the exact values.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction

GRID_STEP = Fraction(1, 10)
CASES = ((120, 1, 20), (90, 3, 10), (200, 4, 5))  # (a, b, c): demand intercept, slope, marginal cost


def demand(price: Fraction, a: int, b: int) -> Fraction:
    """Quantity demanded in units at a price in cents per unit: a - b p."""
    return a - b * price


def profit(price: Fraction, a: int, b: int, c: int) -> Fraction:
    """Monopoly profit in cents: (p - c) (a - b p), for a constant marginal cost c cents per unit."""
    return (price - c) * demand(price, a, b)


def closed_form_price(a: int, b: int, c: int) -> Fraction:
    """The monopoly price (a + b c) / (2 b) in cents per unit, from the first-order condition a - 2 b p + b c = 0."""
    return Fraction(a + b * c, 2 * b)


def elasticity(price: Fraction, a: int, b: int) -> Fraction:
    """The price elasticity of demand -b p / (a - b p), a dimensionless ratio."""
    return -b * price / demand(price, a, b)


def grid_prices(a: int, b: int) -> list[Fraction]:
    """Prices from 0 to the choke price a/b in steps of 1/10 cent."""
    last = int(Fraction(a, b) * 10)
    return [Fraction(k, 10) for k in range(last + 1)]


def main() -> None:
    """Print Parts A, B and C for the three cases. Each line is a fixed label or a number."""
    print("Part A: grid search of the monopoly profit in steps of 1/10 cent, against the closed form")
    for a, b, c in CASES:
        best = max(grid_prices(a, b), key=lambda p: profit(p, a, b, c))
        closed = closed_form_price(a, b, c)
        print(
            f"case a {a} b {b} c {c} grid_argmax {float(best):.10f} closed_form {float(closed):.10f} "
            f"agree {best == closed}"
        )

    print("Part B: Lerner index at the monopoly price against 1/|elasticity|")
    for a, b, c in CASES:
        p_m = closed_form_price(a, b, c)
        lerner = (p_m - c) / p_m
        inverse_abs_elasticity = 1 / abs(elasticity(p_m, a, b))
        print(
            f"case a {a} b {b} c {c} lerner_index {float(lerner):.10f} "
            f"inverse_abs_elasticity {float(inverse_abs_elasticity):.10f} "
            f"equal {lerner == inverse_abs_elasticity}"
        )

    print("Part C: quantity, profit, slope and curvature of the profit at the monopoly price")
    for a, b, c in CASES:
        p_m = closed_form_price(a, b, c)
        quantity = demand(p_m, a, b)
        slope = (profit(p_m + GRID_STEP, a, b, c) - profit(p_m - GRID_STEP, a, b, c)) / (2 * GRID_STEP)
        curvature = (profit(p_m + GRID_STEP, a, b, c) - 2 * profit(p_m, a, b, c) + profit(p_m - GRID_STEP, a, b, c)) / (
            GRID_STEP * GRID_STEP
        )
        revenue_price = Fraction(a, 2 * b)
        print(
            f"case a {a} b {b} c {c} quantity {float(quantity):.10f} profit_cents {float(profit(p_m, a, b, c)):.10f} "
            f"slope_at_optimum {float(slope):.10f} second_difference {float(curvature):.10f} "
            f"monopoly_minus_revenue_price {float(p_m - revenue_price):.10f} "
            f"half_cost {p_m - revenue_price == Fraction(c, 2)} "
            f"profit_closed_form {profit(p_m, a, b, c) == Fraction((a - b * c) ** 2, 4 * b)}"
        )


main()

2Where the canon breaks

  • The closed forms need linear demand and constant cost. Proposition 5.4 holds for any differentiable demand with the stated sign conditions, but the explicit price of Proposition 5.2 does not. Problem R1 gives a constant-elasticity demand that has its own closed form.
  • One seller and no entry. Definition 5.1 has one seller, with no rival and no entry. Lesson L06 treats two sellers, and the price changes.
  • One price for every unit. The seller here sets one price and cannot charge buyers different prices. Price discrimination is outside this lesson.
  • The seller knows the demand curve. The solution uses the demand curve \(Q(p) = a - bp\) as known. A seller that does not know it is outside this model, and the lab does not treat that case.
  • The Lerner index is a markup, not a welfare measure. The index measures how far the price exceeds cost, as a share of price. It does not state whether that gap is a loss to society. Lesson L07 measures the welfare cost of this price.
  • The cases are illustrative. The three cases of Lab 1 are chosen for exact arithmetic. The lab makes no claim about any real seller.

3Misconceptions

  1. “A monopolist sets the price where demand is unit elastic, because that maximizes revenue.”

    For linear demand, the price with unit elasticity maximizes revenue (Remark 5.3). A monopolist with a positive constant cost prices above that point. Its price is \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b)\), which exceeds \(a/(2b)\) by \(c/2\), so \(|\varepsilon(p_m)| > 1\). In the reference case of Lab 1, \(a = 120\), \(b = 1\), \(c = 20\): the price is 70 cents, and Part B prints \(1/|\varepsilon| = 0.7142857143\), so \(|\varepsilon(70)| = 1.4\).

  2. “A monopolist sells the quantity that a competitive market would sell at the same cost.”

    If price equals marginal cost \(c\), the price-taking benchmark of L02 and L03, the quantity is \(a - bc\). The monopoly quantity is \((a - bc)/2\) (Proposition 5.2), which is half of it for every \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) in Assumption M. In the reference case the monopoly quantity is 50 units (Part C of Lab 1), and the benchmark quantity is \(120 - 20 = 100\) units.

  3. “The markup over cost is a fixed share of cost, so demand does not affect it.”

    The markup \((p - c)/p\) equals \(1/|\varepsilon(p_m)|\) (Proposition 5.4), so it depends on the demand curve at the chosen price. With the cost fixed at \(c = 20\) and \(b = 1\), Corollary 5.5 gives \((a - bc)/(a + bc) = 100/140 = 5/7\) at \(a = 120\), as Part B of Lab 1 prints, and \(180/220 = 9/11\) at \(a = 200\).

4Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · The monopoly price and profit with \(a = 100\), \(b = 2\), \(c = 10\)

Demand is \(Q(p) = 100 - 2p\), and the marginal cost is 10 cents per unit. Find the monopoly price, the quantity and the profit in cents.

Solution

Proposition 5.2 gives \(p_m = (100 + 2 \cdot 10)/(2 \cdot 2) = 120/4 = 30\) cents. The quantity is \(Q(30) = 100 - 60 = 40\) units, which equals \((a - bc)/2 = (100 - 20)/2 = 40\). The profit is \((30 - 10) \cdot 40 = 800\) cents, which equals \((a - bc)^{2}/(4b) = 80^{2}/8 = 800\).

M2 · The Lerner index for \(a = 60\), \(b = 3\), \(c = 4\)

Find the monopoly price, the Lerner index and the magnitude of the elasticity at the monopoly price, and check that they satisfy Proposition 5.4.

Solution

\(p_m = (60 + 12)/6 = 12\) cents, and \(Q(12) = 60 - 36 = 24\) units. The Lerner index is \((12 - 4)/12 = 2/3\). The elasticity is \(\varepsilon(12) = -3 \cdot 12/24 = -3/2\), with magnitude \(3/2\), so \(1/|\varepsilon| = 2/3\). This agrees with Corollary 5.5: \((a - bc)/(a + bc) = 48/72 = 2/3\).

M3 · Zero cost puts the monopoly on the unit-elastic point

For \(a = 50\), \(b = 1\) and \(c = 0\), find \(p_m\), and show that \(|\varepsilon(p_m)| = 1\).

Solution

With \(c = 0\), \(p_m = a/(2b) = 50/2 = 25\) cents. The quantity is \(Q(25) = 50 - 25 = 25\), and \(\varepsilon(25) = -1 \cdot 25/25 = -1\), so \(|\varepsilon| = 1\). Corollary 5.5 gives \((a + bc)/(a - bc) = 1\), as it must. Remark 5.3 says that the monopoly price is above the revenue-maximising price by \(c/2 = 0\), so with zero cost the two prices coincide.

Proof

P1 · The completed square, by expansion

Show that \((a - bc)^{2}/(4b) - b(p - p_m)^{2}\), with \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b)\), equals \((p - c)(a - bp)\) for every \(p\).

Solution

Expand the second term: \(-b(p - p_m)^{2} = -bp^{2} + 2bp_m p - bp_m^{2} = -bp^{2} + (a + bc)p - (a + bc)^{2}/(4b)\). Add \((a - bc)^{2}/(4b)\): the constant term becomes \(\bigl[(a - bc)^{2} - (a + bc)^{2}\bigr]/(4b) = -4abc/(4b) = -ac\). So the whole expression is \(-bp^{2} + (a + bc)p - ac\). On the other side, \((p - c)(a - bp) = ap - bp^{2} - ac + bcp = -bp^{2} + (a + bc)p - ac\). The two agree.

P2 · The monopoly price is the revenue maximiser when cost is zero

Show that with \(c = 0\), the monopoly price maximizes revenue \(R(p) = p(a - bp)\) over \(0 \le p \le a/b\).

Solution

With \(c = 0\), the profit is \(\pi(p) = pQ(p) = R(p)\), so the monopoly problem is the revenue problem. By Proposition 5.2 the maximizer is \(p_m = a/(2b)\), the unique point where \(R'(p) = a - 2bp = 0\). Its revenue is \(R(a/(2b)) = a^{2}/(4b)\), which equals \(\pi_m = (a - 0)^{2}/(4b)\).

P3 · Strict concavity of the profit without convexity theory

For Assumption M, show that the second derivative of \(\pi\) is \(-2b\) at every price, and use this to state the sign of the profit's curvature.

Solution

The profit is \(\pi(p) = -bp^{2} + (a + bc)p - ac\). Its first derivative is \(\pi'(p) = -2bp + (a + bc)\), and its second derivative is \(\pi''(p) = -2b\). Because \(b > 0\), \(\pi'' < 0\) everywhere, so \(\pi\) is strictly concave. This second-order fact confirms the maximum that Proposition 5.2 finds by completing the square; Proposition 5.2 does not use it, and it needs no convexity theory. The first-order condition \(\pi'(p) = 0\) gives \(p = (a + bc)/(2b)\), which is \(p_m\).

Empirical

E1 · Why the grid finds the closed form exactly

Part A of Lab 1 reports agree True for \((90, 3, 10)\), with the grid argmax 20. Explain why the grid search returns the closed-form price exactly, and not an approximation.

Solution

The closed form is \(p_m = 20\), and 20 is a multiple of the grid step 1/10, so it is one of the grid prices. The profit has a unique maximum (Proposition 5.2), and the grid contains the maximizer, so the best grid price is the maximizer. The code compares the two exact Fractions, so the agreement is exact and not a tolerance.

E2 · The Lerner index for \((200, 4, 5)\)

Part B of Lab 1 prints 0.8181818182 for \((200, 4, 5)\). Derive this value from the closed forms and check it.

Solution

Here \(p_m = (200 + 20)/8 = 27.5\) cents and \(Q(27.5) = 200 - 110 = 90\) units. The Lerner index is \((27.5 - 5)/27.5 = 22.5/27.5 = 9/11\). The elasticity is \(\varepsilon = -4 \cdot 27.5/90 = -110/90 = -11/9\), so \(1/|\varepsilon| = 9/11\). The printed value 0.8181818182 is \(9/11\) to ten places, and it equals the closed form of Corollary 5.5, \((200 - 20)/(200 + 20) = 180/220 = 9/11\).

E3 · The gap between the monopoly and revenue prices

Part C of Lab 1 prints the monopoly price minus \(a/(2b)\) as 10, 5 and 2.5 cents. Show that these equal \(c/2\) for the three cases.

Solution

For \((120, 1, 20)\), \(a/(2b) = 60\), and \(70 - 60 = 10 = 20/2\). For \((90, 3, 10)\), \(a/(2b) = 15\), and \(20 - 15 = 5 = 10/2\). For \((200, 4, 5)\), \(a/(2b) = 25\), and \(27.5 - 25 = 2.5 = 5/2\). In each case the gap is \(c/2\), as Remark 5.3 states.

Research

R1 · A constant-elasticity demand curve

Let \(Q(p) = A\,p^{-\beta}\) with \(A > 0\) and \(\beta > 1\), and let the marginal cost be \(c > 0\). Show that the monopoly price is \(p_m = \beta c/(\beta - 1)\), that the Lerner index at \(p_m\) is \(1/\beta\), and that the profit has a unique maximizer.

Solution

The derivative of the profit is \(\pi'(p) = Q(p) + (p - c)Q'(p) = Ap^{-\beta} - \beta(p - c)Ap^{-\beta - 1} = Ap^{-\beta - 1}\bigl[p - \beta(p - c)\bigr] = Ap^{-\beta - 1}\bigl[\beta c - (\beta - 1)p\bigr]\). For \(p > 0\) the prefactor is positive, so \(\pi'\) is positive for \(p < \beta c/(\beta - 1)\) and negative above it. The profit rises and then falls, so the unique maximizer is \(p_m = \beta c/(\beta - 1)\). The Lerner index is \((p_m - c)/p_m = 1 - c/p_m = 1 - (\beta - 1)/\beta = 1/\beta\). The elasticity of this demand is \(-\beta\) at every price, so \(1/|\varepsilon| = 1/\beta\), which agrees with Proposition 5.4. The condition \(\beta > 1\) is needed for \(p_m\) to be positive and finite.

R2 · The monopoly price is the midpoint of cost and the choke price

Under Assumption M, show that \(p_m\) is the midpoint of \(c\) and the choke price \(a/b\), and state what this means for how far the monopoly price lies above cost.

Solution

The midpoint of \(c\) and \(a/b\) is \(\tfrac12(c + a/b) = (bc + a)/(2b) = p_m\). So the monopoly price lies halfway between the cost and the price at which demand falls to zero. The distance from cost to the monopoly price is \(p_m - c = (a - bc)/(2b)\), which is half the distance from cost to the choke price, \(a/b - c = (a - bc)/b\).

R3 · Positive cost puts the optimum in the elastic region

Under Assumption M with \(c > 0\), show that \(|\varepsilon(p_m)| > 1\), using only Proposition 1.4 of L04 and the formula for \(p_m\).

Solution

By Proposition 5.2, \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b)\), and \(a/(2b)\) is the midpoint of \(0\) and the choke price \(a/b\), the interval of Proposition 1.4 of L04. The difference \(p_m - a/(2b) = c/2\) is positive when \(c > 0\). Proposition 1.4 says \(|\varepsilon| > 1\) when \(p > a/(2b)\), with \(0 < p < a/b\). Here \(0 < p_m < a/b\) (Proposition 5.2), so \(|\varepsilon(p_m)| > 1\). The same conclusion follows from Corollary 5.5, since \((a + bc)/(a - bc) > 1\) when \(b c > 0\).

6Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson’s cards to your review deck.

  1. A monopolist faces \(Q = 100 - 2p\) and has a constant marginal cost of 10 cents per unit. What is its profit-maximizing price?

    Options

    \(p_m = (a + bc)/(2b) = (100 + 20)/4 = 30\) (Proposition 5.2, Problem M1).

  2. For the same monopolist, how many units does it sell at that price?

    \(Q(30) = 100 - 60 = 40\) units, which is \((a - bc)/2 = 40\) (Proposition 5.2).

  3. With a positive constant marginal cost, where is a monopolist’s price relative to the unit-elastic point \(a/(2b)\) of linear demand?

    Options

    \(p_m - a/(2b) = c/2 > 0\) (Remark 5.3), and \(|\varepsilon| > 1\) above \(a/(2b)\) (Proposition 1.4 of L04).

  4. In the reference case \((a, b, c) = (120, 1, 20)\), what is the Lerner index at the monopoly price, to seven decimal places?

    \((70 - 20)/70 = 5/7 = 0.7142857\) (Corollary 5.5, Part B of Lab 1).

  5. Which identity holds at an interior monopoly optimum with \(Q' < 0\)?

    Options

    Proposition 5.4: dividing the first-order condition \(Q + (p - c)Q' = 0\) by \(Q\) gives \((p - c)Q'/Q = -1\), and substituting \(Q'/Q = \varepsilon/p\) from \(\varepsilon = pQ'/Q\) gives \(\frac{p - c}{p}\,\varepsilon = -1\), which is this identity.

  6. What is the monopoly profit in cents in the reference case \((120, 1, 20)\)?

    \((a - bc)^{2}/(4b) = 100^{2}/4 = 2500\) cents (Proposition 5.2, Part C of Lab 1).

  7. For linear demand \(Q = a - bp\), at which price does revenue \(p(a - bp)\) reach its maximum?

    Options

    \(R'(p) = a - 2bp\) vanishes at \(a/(2b)\) (Remark 5.3). The monopoly price \((a + bc)/(2b)\) is a different point when \(c > 0\).

  8. A monopolist faces \(Q = 90 - 3p\) with a marginal cost of 10 cents per unit. What is its profit-maximizing price, in cents?

    \(p_m = (90 + 3 \cdot 10)/(2 \cdot 3) = 120/6 = 20\) (Proposition 5.2, Part A of Lab 1).

  9. For constant-elasticity demand \(Q = Ap^{-\beta}\) with \(\beta > 1\) and marginal cost \(c > 0\), what is the monopoly price?

    Options

    Problem R1: the first derivative of the profit is positive below \(\beta c/(\beta - 1)\) and negative above it.

  10. For \((a, b, c) = (200, 4, 5)\), what is the magnitude \(|\varepsilon(p_m)|\) at the monopoly price, to seven decimal places?

    \(|\varepsilon(27.5)| = 110/90 = 11/9 = 1.2222222\) (Problem E2, Corollary 5.5).
