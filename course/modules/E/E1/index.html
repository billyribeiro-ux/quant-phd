Microeconomics

A price is where buyers' choices meet sellers' choices. The exchange-fee question in this program's market-structure project is a question about that meeting. This module teaches the theory of the consumer and the firm, the competitive equilibrium that joins them, the elasticity of demand and the comparative statics of an equilibrium, and the three market structures this module treats: monopoly, Cournot competition and Bertrand competition. It then measures what each structure does to surplus and to efficiency, and it ends with the external costs and public goods that a market does not price. You leave able to derive an optimum from its first-order conditions, to compute an equilibrium exactly, and to model an exchange-fee market under three structures and compare their outcomes in a tested engine.

  • Prerequisites: M2 Single & Multivariable Calculus. M2 requires M1, and M1 requires P0.1, so both are in the closure too. Secondary-school algebra is assumed.
  • Project: Market-structure lab, graded by an autograder and a rubric
  • Data: none. The labs compute from formulas in exact fractions, except where a lab says it uses floating point, and no lab reads a data file. The project's generated instances are labelled SIMULATED on the project page.

90 h lessons · 30 h project (25%)

Objectives

By the end of E1 you can:

  1. Derive the optimal bundle of a consumer with the utility \(xy\) from the budget line, and show that the marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio there (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the profit-maximizing output of a price-taking firm from its cost function, and show that price equals marginal cost at a positive output (Lesson 2).
  3. Compute the competitive equilibrium of a linear market exactly, and prove that it is unique (Lesson 3).
  4. Compute the price elasticity of demand, and derive the comparative-static effect of a demand shift on the equilibrium by implicit differentiation, checked against a finite difference (Lesson 4).
  5. Derive the monopoly price on linear demand, compare the Cournot and Bertrand outcomes with it, and state the Lerner index (Lessons 5 and 6).
  6. Compute consumer and producer surplus and the deadweight loss of each structure against the competitive benchmark, and state the Pigouvian wedge and the public-good condition (Lessons 7 to 9).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven test the M1 and M2 prerequisites, and two skill checks, marked as such, test what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for nine items means eight correct: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks correctly. The prerequisites are taught in M1 and M2. A result below the bar means those items need review before rung 1.

  1. What is the derivative of \(x^3\) at \(x = 2\)?

    The derivative is \(3x^2\), which equals 12 at \(x = 2\) (derivative, M2).

  2. For \(f(x, y) = x^2 y\), what is the partial derivative with respect to \(x\) at \((x, y) = (1, 3)\)?

    \(\partial f/\partial x = 2xy\), which equals \(2 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = 6\) (partial derivative, M2).

  3. The curve \(y^2 + x = 4\) passes through \((x, y) = (3, 1)\). What is \(dy/dx\) there? Give it as a decimal.

    Differentiating implicitly, \(2y\,dy/dx + 1 = 0\), so \(dy/dx = -1/(2y) = -1/2\) at \(y = 1\) (implicit-function theorem, M2).

  4. At a continuously compounded rate of 0.05, what is \(e^{0.05 \cdot 10}\), the growth factor after 10 years, to four decimals?

    \(e^{0.5} = 1.6487\) to four decimals (exponential and logarithm, M1).

  5. To prove by direct proof that if \(n\) is even then \(n^2\) is even, what is the first step?

    Options

    A direct proof starts from the hypothesis, here \(n\) even, written as \(n = 2k\) (direct proof, M1).

  6. For \(f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2\), what is the gradient at \((1, 2)\)?

    Options

    The gradient is \((2x, 2y)\), which equals \((2, 4)\) at \((1, 2)\) (gradient, M2).

  7. A payment of 100 is received at time 2 and discounted at a continuously compounded rate of 0.05. What is its present value, to two decimals?

    \(100 e^{-0.05 \cdot 2} = 100 e^{-0.1} = 90.48\) to two decimals (present value and discount factor, M1).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): for the monopoly profit \(((a - Q)/b - c)\,Q\) with \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), where \((a - Q)/b\) is the inverse demand, which equation is the first-order condition?

    Options

    The derivative of \(((a - Q)/b - c)Q\) with respect to \(Q\) is \((a - bc - 2Q)/b\), which is set to zero, giving \(a - bc - 2Q = 0\). This is the monopoly step of the worked example in rung 1.

  9. Skill check (rung 2): for the Cournot profit \(((a - q_1 - q_2)/b - c)\,q_1\) with \(a > bc > 0\) and \(b > 0\), which expression is firm 1's best response to \(q_2\) when the response is interior?

    Options

    The derivative in \(q_1\) is \((a - 2q_1 - q_2 - bc)/b\), which is zero at \(q_1 = (a - bc - q_2)/2\). This is the completion step of rung 2.

Lessons

Nine lessons of ten study hours each, 90 hours in total. Lessons 1 to 3 cover the consumer, the firm and the competitive equilibrium. Lesson 4 covers elasticity and comparative statics. Lessons 5 and 6 cover monopoly, Cournot and Bertrand competition. Lessons 7 to 9 cover surplus, efficiency, externalities and public goods. Each lesson page contains graded results, labs inline in the argument, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 10 hConsumer ChoiceThe optimal bundle for the utility \(xy\) on a budget line, the marginal rate of substitution, and the tangency of the MRS with the price ratio. L02 · 10 hProducer Choice and CostProfit maximization for a price-taking firm with a fixed cost and a quadratic cost, the output at which price equals marginal cost, and why a fixed cost does not change it. L03 · 10 hCompetitive EquilibriumThe linear competitive equilibrium: existence, uniqueness and the closed form, computed in exact fractions and checked on a grid of prices. L04 · 10 hDemand Responsiveness and Comparative StaticsThe price elasticity of demand, and the change in an equilibrium from a demand shift, by implicit differentiation checked against a finite difference. L05 · 10 hMonopoly PricingThe monopoly price on linear demand, the Lerner index, and the second-order condition checked by the sign of the second derivative. L06 · 10 hOligopoly: Cournot and BertrandCournot quantities from reaction functions, Bertrand fees at marginal cost, and the ordering of the three outcomes. L07 · 10 hSurplus and Deadweight LossConsumer and producer surplus as definite integrals, and the deadweight loss of monopoly and Cournot against the competitive benchmark. L08 · 10 hPareto EfficiencyPareto improvements in a two-consumer exchange economy, and the allocations on a grid that no other grid allocation improves, which need not be efficient. L09 · 10 hExternalities and Public GoodsThe Pigouvian wedge equal to the marginal external cost, and the vertical summation of willingness to pay for a public good.

The project ladder

The unit ends in the market-structure lab. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated monopoly, Cournot and Bertrand outcome for the reference case \(a = 120\), \(b = 1\), \(c = 20\), with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same task with the Cournot reaction step and the welfare line removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement market_structure.py to the stated interface, and write the memo. Graded by the autograder against generated inputs and by the rubric. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a third symmetric exchange with the closed form stated and tested, or a demand curve that is not linear with the outcome comparison stated as a hypothesis, suitable for your portfolio.
