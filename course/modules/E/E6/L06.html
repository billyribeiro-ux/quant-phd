The Method of Undetermined Coefficients

Linear rational expectations systems cannot be solved by simple backward induction because current decisions depend on forward-looking expectations. The method of undetermined coefficients transforms expectational difference equations into algebraic systems by guessing linear policy rules.

1. Guess-and-Verify in Linear Rational Expectations

In linear dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models, forward-looking endogenous variables depend on current structural states and anticipated future shocks. When the structural equations and shock processes are linear, the minimum state variable (MSV) solution is also linear. The method of undetermined coefficients postulates an affine relationship between endogenous non-predetermined jump variables and exogenous or predetermined state variables, solving for the unknown matrix mappings by equating coefficients.

This technique, formalizing the general equilibrium solution of the New Keynesian model, draws directly on the properties of a linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces.

Definition 6.1 (Method of Undetermined Coefficients) Given an expectational difference system \(\mathbf{A}_0 \mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{A}_1 \E_t [\mathbf{y}_{t+1}] + \mathbf{B} \mathbf{s}_t\) driven by a Markov state vector \(\mathbf{s}_t = \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{s}_{t-1} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t\), the method posits a candidate solution \(\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{s}_t\), evaluates the conditional expectation \(\E_t [\mathbf{y}_{t+1}] = \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{s}_t\), and solves the algebraic matrix equation: \[ \begin{aligned} (\mathbf{A}_0 \mathbf{\Gamma} - \mathbf{A}_1 \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{\Phi} - \mathbf{B}) \mathbf{s}_t &= \mathbf{0} \quad \forall \mathbf{s}_t \end{aligned} \] requiring the operator residual matrix \(\mathbf{A}_0 \mathbf{\Gamma} - \mathbf{A}_1 \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{\Phi} - \mathbf{B}\) to be identically zero.

2. Solving the Three-Equation System for a Demand Shock

Consider the canonical New Keynesian economy where the only stochastic disturbance is an AR(1) natural real interest rate shock \(u_t \equiv r^n_t\):

\[ \begin{aligned} u_t &= \rho u_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t, \quad |\rho| < 1, \quad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathrm{WN}(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2) \end{aligned} \]

The structural equations are:

\[ \begin{aligned} x_t &= \E_t [x_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma} (i_t - \E_t [\pi_{t+1}] - u_t) \\ \pi_t &= \beta \E_t [\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa x_t \\ i_t &= \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y x_t \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 6.2 (Analytical Coefficients for Demand Shock) Positing the linear policy functions \(\pi_t = a u_t\) and \(x_t = b u_t\), the unique bounded linear rational expectations equilibrium coefficients are: \[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{\kappa}{\Delta}, \quad b = \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{\Delta} \end{aligned} \] where the determinant denominator is: \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta &= (1 - \beta\rho)(\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y) + \kappa(\phi_\pi - \rho) \end{aligned} \] provided \(\Delta \ne 0\).

Proof. Under the candidate policy rules \(\pi_t = a u_t\) and \(x_t = b u_t\), the conditional expectations are: \[ \begin{aligned} \E_t [\pi_{t+1}] &= a \E_t [u_{t+1}] = a \rho u_t \\ \E_t [x_{t+1}] &= b \E_t [u_{t+1}] = b \rho u_t \end{aligned} \] Substituting these expectations and the policy rule \(i_t = \phi_\pi a u_t + \phi_y b u_t\) into the NKPC: \[ \begin{aligned} a u_t &= \beta (a \rho u_t) + \kappa (b u_t) \\ (1 - \beta\rho) a - \kappa b &= 0 \end{aligned} \] Substituting into the dynamic IS curve: \[ \begin{aligned} b u_t &= b \rho u_t - \frac{1}{\sigma} \left[ (\phi_\pi a + \phi_y b) u_t - a \rho u_t - u_t \right] \\ \sigma(1 - \rho) b &= -(\phi_\pi - \rho) a - \phi_y b + 1 \\ (\phi_\pi - \rho) a + [\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y] b &= 1 \end{aligned} \] This constitutes a \(2 \times 2\) linear system \(\mathbf{M} \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) where: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{M} &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 - \beta\rho & -\kappa \\ \phi_\pi - \rho & \sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y \end{bmatrix} \end{aligned} \] The determinant of \(\mathbf{M}\) is \(\det(\mathbf{M}) = (1 - \beta\rho)[\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y] - (-\kappa)(\phi_\pi - \rho) = \Delta\). Applying Cramer's rule: \[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{\det \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\kappa \\ 1 & \sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y \end{bmatrix}}{\Delta} = \frac{\kappa}{\Delta} \\ b &= \frac{\det \begin{bmatrix} 1 - \beta\rho & 0 \\ \phi_\pi - \rho & 1 \end{bmatrix}}{\Delta} = \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{\Delta} \end{aligned} \] which completes the derivation.

Remark 6.1 (Exact Rational Solutions) When model parameters are specified as rational numbers (\(\beta, \sigma, \kappa, \phi_\pi, \phi_y, \rho \in \mathbb{Q}\)), the determinant \(\Delta\) and the coefficients \(a\) and \(b\) are exact rational fractions in \(\mathbb{Q}\). This enables exact integer-ratio arithmetic without floating-point rounding error, providing a definitive ground truth for computational solver validation.

Example 6.3 (Numerical Verification of Rational Coefficients) Let \(\beta = 99/100\), \(\sigma = 1\), \(\kappa = 1/10\), \(\phi_\pi = 3/2\), \(\phi_y = 1/2\), and \(\rho = 1/2\). The matrix elements are: \[ \begin{aligned} m_{11} &= 1 - \frac{99}{100}\frac{1}{2} = \frac{101}{200} \\ m_{12} &= -\frac{1}{10} = -\frac{20}{200} \\ m_{21} &= \frac{3}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = 1 \\ m_{22} &= 1\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) + \frac{1}{2} = 1 \end{aligned} \] The determinant is: \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta &= \frac{101}{200}(1) - \left(-\frac{1}{10}\right)(1) = \frac{101}{200} + \frac{20}{200} = \frac{121}{200} = 0.605 \end{aligned} \] The undetermined coefficients are: \[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{1/10}{121/200} = \frac{20}{121} \approx 0.16528926 \\ b &= \frac{101/200}{121/200} = \frac{101}{121} \approx 0.83471074 \end{aligned} \] Substituting back: \(m_{11} a + m_{12} b = \frac{101}{200}\frac{20}{121} - \frac{1}{10}\frac{101}{121} = \frac{2020}{24200} - \frac{2020}{24200} = 0\).

3. Computational Lab: Exact Rational Undetermined Coefficients

The lab below sets up the \(2 \times 2\) linear system in Python using exact rational arithmetic (`fractions.Fraction`) and floating point, verifying that the substitution residuals for both the NKPC and dynamic IS equations equal zero exactly.

Lab 1 · Undetermined Coefficients in Exact Arithmetic

The method of undetermined coefficients yields exact rational solutions whose algebraic residuals are identically zero in rational arithmetic and zero within double-precision machine epsilon. Model output, no data.

"""Lab E6-L06-1: Solving linear rational-expectations systems by undetermined coefficients.

Sets up the 2x2 linear system for the undetermined coefficients (a, b) mapping the
natural real rate shock u_t to inflation (pi_t = a * u_t) and the output gap (x_t = b * u_t).
Solves the system in exact rational arithmetic (fractions.Fraction) and standard floats,
and verifies exact equation substitution residuals.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction


def solve_linear_re_fraction(
    beta: Fraction,
    sigma: Fraction,
    kappa: Fraction,
    phi_pi: Fraction,
    phi_y: Fraction,
    rho: Fraction,
) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction, Fraction]:
    """Solve undetermined coefficients in exact arithmetic."""
    # Matrix elements for M * [a, b]^T = [0, 1]^T
    m11 = Fraction(1) - beta * rho
    m12 = -kappa
    m21 = phi_pi - rho
    m22 = sigma * (Fraction(1) - rho) + phi_y

    det = m11 * m22 - m12 * m21
    if det == Fraction(0):
        raise ValueError("Singular system: cannot solve undetermined coefficients.")

    # Cramer's rule / 2x2 matrix inversion
    a = kappa / det
    b = m11 / det
    return a, b, det


def main() -> None:
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Exact rational solve
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    beta_q = Fraction(99, 100)
    sigma_q = Fraction(1, 1)
    kappa_q = Fraction(1, 10)
    phi_pi_q = Fraction(3, 2)
    phi_y_q = Fraction(1, 2)
    rho_q = Fraction(1, 2)

    a_q, b_q, det_q = solve_linear_re_fraction(beta_q, sigma_q, kappa_q, phi_pi_q, phi_y_q, rho_q)

    # Substitution check: M * [a, b]^T - [0, 1]^T == [0, 0]^T
    m11_q = Fraction(1) - beta_q * rho_q
    m12_q = -kappa_q
    m21_q = phi_pi_q - rho_q
    m22_q = sigma_q * (Fraction(1) - rho_q) + phi_y_q

    res1_q = m11_q * a_q + m12_q * b_q
    res2_q = m21_q * a_q + m22_q * b_q - Fraction(1)

    print("Exact rational solution (fractions.Fraction):")
    print(f"  Determinant Delta: {det_q}")
    print(f"  Inflation coefficient a: {a_q} ({float(a_q):.8f})")
    print(f"  Output gap coefficient b: {b_q} ({float(b_q):.8f})")
    print(f"  System residual 1 (NKPC): {res1_q}")
    print(f"  System residual 2 (IS-TR): {res2_q}")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Floating point comparison
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    a_f = float(a_q)
    b_f = float(b_q)
    m11_f = float(m11_q)
    m12_f = float(m12_q)
    m21_f = float(m21_q)
    m22_f = float(m22_q)

    res1_f = m11_f * a_f + m12_f * b_f
    res2_f = m21_f * a_f + m22_f * b_f - 1.0

    print("\nFloating point evaluation and substitution residuals:")
    print(f"  Residual 1: {res1_f:.2e}")
    print(f"  Residual 2: {res2_f:.2e}")


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The method of undetermined coefficients is simple and transparent, but has notable structural limitations:

  • Curse of Dimensionality in State Variables: When the macroeconomic model includes multiple endogenous capital stocks, habit persistence, and lagged policy rates, the guess-and-verify technique leads to non-linear matrix quadratic equations (matrix Riccati equations) that cannot be solved by linear Cramer's rule.
  • Inability to Detect Indeterminacy Directly: Undetermined coefficients posits an equilibrium of a particular form. If sunspot equilibria exist (multiplicity), the linear guess finds one solution but fails to detect the continuum of other stationary equilibria.
  • Strict Linearity: The method cannot solve models with occasionally binding constraints, such as the zero lower bound on nominal interest rates or collateral borrowing constraints.

Misconceptions

  1. The method of undetermined coefficients proves that the solution found is the unique equilibrium.

    The method merely verifies that the conjectured solution satisfies the equilibrium conditions. Establishing uniqueness requires verifying that the model's companion matrix satisfies the Blanchard-Kahn rank condition (covered in Lesson 7).

  2. Undetermined coefficients can be applied directly to non-linear DSGE models without linearization.

    The method relies strictly on the linear conditional expectation operator \(\E_t[\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{s}_{t+1}] = \mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{s}_t\). Non-linear models require projection methods, value function iteration, or perturbation expansions.

  3. The undetermined coefficients \(a\) and \(b\) depend on the variance of the shock innovation \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\).

    In first-order linear approximations, certainty equivalence holds: the slope coefficients depend only on the persistence \(\rho\) and structural model parameters, completely independent of the innovation variance \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\).

Problem Set

Problem 6.1 (Purely Temporary Demand Shock). Set \(\rho = 0\) in the natural rate process. Calculate the analytical values of \(\Delta\), \(a\), and \(b\) as functions of \((\beta, \sigma, \kappa, \phi_\pi, \phi_y)\).

Solution

Substituting \(\rho = 0\): \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta &= (1 - 0)(\sigma(1 - 0) + \phi_y) + \kappa(\phi_\pi - 0) = \sigma + \phi_y + \kappa\phi_\pi \\ a &= \frac{\kappa}{\sigma + \phi_y + \kappa\phi_\pi} \\ b &= \frac{1}{\sigma + \phi_y + \kappa\phi_\pi} \end{aligned} \] When the shock has no persistence, future expectations vanish (\(\E_t[\pi_{t+1}] = \E_t[x_{t+1}] = 0\)), reducing the system to static algebra.

Problem 6.2 (Monetary Policy Shock Derivation). Let the monetary policy rule be \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_y x_t + v_t\), where \(v_t = \rho_v v_{t-1} + \varepsilon_{v,t}\), with \(r^n_t = e_t = 0\). Using undetermined coefficients \(x_t = b_v v_t\) and \(\pi_t = a_v v_t\), set up the \(2 \times 2\) system for \((a_v, b_v)\).

Solution

Substitute \(\E_t[\pi_{t+1}] = a_v \rho_v v_t\) and \(\E_t[x_{t+1}] = b_v \rho_v v_t\) into the NKPC and dynamic IS: \[ \begin{aligned} a_v v_t &= \beta a_v \rho_v v_t + \kappa b_v v_t \implies (1 - \beta\rho_v) a_v - \kappa b_v = 0 \\ b_v v_t &= b_v \rho_v v_t - \frac{1}{\sigma} \left[ (\phi_\pi a_v + \phi_y b_v + 1) v_t - a_v \rho_v v_t \right] \end{aligned} \] Rearranging: \[ \begin{aligned} (\phi_\pi - \rho_v) a_v + [\sigma(1 - \rho_v) + \phi_y] b_v = -1 \end{aligned} \] In matrix form: \[ \begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - \beta\rho_v & -\kappa \\ \phi_\pi - \rho_v & \sigma(1 - \rho_v) + \phi_y \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a_v \\ b_v \end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \end{aligned} \] The solution is \(a_v = -\kappa / \Delta_v\) and \(b_v = -(1 - \beta\rho_v) / \Delta_v\).

Problem 6.3 (Cost-Push Markup Shock System). Let the NKPC include an AR(1) cost-push shock \(e_t = \rho_e e_{t-1} + \varepsilon_{e,t}\). Formulate the linear system for undetermined coefficients \(x_t = b_e e_t\) and \(\pi_t = a_e e_t\).

Solution

Substituting into the NKPC: \[ \begin{aligned} a_e e_t = \beta \rho_e a_e e_t + \kappa b_e e_t + e_t \implies (1 - \beta\rho_e) a_e - \kappa b_e = 1 \end{aligned} \] From the dynamic IS curve: \[ \begin{aligned} (\phi_\pi - \rho_e) a_e + [\sigma(1 - \rho_e) + \phi_y] b_e = 0 \end{aligned} \] In matrix form: \[ \begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - \beta\rho_e & -\kappa \\ \phi_\pi - \rho_e & \sigma(1 - \rho_e) + \phi_y \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a_e \\ b_e \end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{aligned} \] Applying Cramer's rule yields \(a_e = \frac{\sigma(1 - \rho_e) + \phi_y}{\Delta_e}\) and \(b_e = -\frac{\phi_\pi - \rho_e}{\Delta_e}\).

Problem 6.4 (Positivity of Output Gap Multiplier). Prove that for any \(\beta \in (0,1)\), \(\rho \in [0,1)\), \(\kappa > 0\), \(\sigma > 0\), \(\phi_y \ge 0\), and \(\phi_\pi > \rho\), the natural rate multiplier on the output gap \(b\) satisfies \(0 < b < \frac{1}{\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y}\).

Solution

We have \(b = \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{\Delta}\) with \(\Delta = (1 - \beta\rho)[\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y] + \kappa(\phi_\pi - \rho)\). Since \(\beta \rho \in [0,1)\), the numerator is strictly positive: \(1 - \beta\rho > 0\). Since \(\kappa > 0\) and \(\phi_\pi - \rho > 0\), we have \(\kappa(\phi_\pi - \rho) > 0\). Thus: \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta > (1 - \beta\rho)[\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y] > 0 \end{aligned} \] Dividing the numerator \(1 - \beta\rho\) by \(\Delta\) yields: \[ \begin{aligned} b = \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{\Delta} < \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{(1 - \beta\rho)[\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y]} = \frac{1}{\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y} \end{aligned} \] Since the numerator and denominator are strictly positive, \(b > 0\), which completes the proof.

Problem 6.5 (Equivalence of Matrix Inversion and Undetermined Coefficients). Prove that the undetermined coefficient solution is mathematically equivalent to the spectral projection of the forward-looking companion matrix onto the stable eigenspace.

Solution

Write the model as \(\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B} \E_t [\mathbf{y}_{t+1}] + \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{C} u_t\). If all eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B}\) have moduli strictly less than unity (so \(\mathbf{B}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\) has eigenvalues outside the unit circle), the unique forward-stable solution is: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{y}_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty (\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B})^j \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{C} \E_t [u_{t+j}] \end{aligned} \] Since \(\E_t [u_{t+j}] = \rho^j u_t\), this infinite sum evaluates to: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{y}_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty (\rho \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B})^j \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{C} u_t = [\mathbf{I} - \rho \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B}]^{-1} \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{C} u_t = [\mathbf{A} - \rho \mathbf{B}]^{-1} \mathbf{C} u_t \end{aligned} \] The matrix \(\mathbf{A} - \rho \mathbf{B}\) is precisely the matrix \(\mathbf{M}\) formed in the undetermined coefficients formulation. Hence \(\mathbf{\Gamma} = \mathbf{M}^{-1} \mathbf{C}\), proving exact equivalence.

Problem 6.6 (Certainty Equivalence). Prove that the undetermined coefficients \((a, b)\) are independent of the innovation variance \(\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_t) = \sigma_\varepsilon^2\).

Solution

Under rational expectations with linear structural equations, the conditional expectation operator is a linear functional: \(\E_t [u_{t+1}] = \E_t [\rho u_t + \varepsilon_{t+1}] = \rho u_t + \E_t[\varepsilon_{t+1}] = \rho u_t\). Because \(\E_t [\varepsilon_{t+1}] = 0\) by definition of white noise, higher central moments \(\E_t [(\varepsilon_{t+1} - 0)^k]\) for \(k \ge 2\) do not enter the first-order expectational equations. The linear algebraic system \(\mathbf{M} \mathbf{\gamma} = \mathbf{d}\) contains only structural parameters and \(\rho\), proving certainty equivalence.

Problem 6.7 (Empirical Parameter Sensitivity). For baseline parameters \(\beta = 0.99, \sigma = 1.0, \kappa = 0.1, \phi_\pi = 1.5, \phi_y = 0.5\), evaluate how the inflation coefficient \(a\) changes as persistence \(\rho\) increases from \(0.3\) to \(0.8\).

Solution

For \(\rho = 0.3\): \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta(0.3) &= (1 - 0.99 \times 0.3)(1.0 \times 0.7 + 0.5) + 0.1(1.5 - 0.3) \\ &= (0.703)(1.2) + 0.1(1.2) = 0.8436 + 0.12 = 0.9636 \\ a(0.3) &= \frac{0.1}{0.9636} \approx 0.103777 \end{aligned} \] For \(\rho = 0.8\): \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta(0.8) &= (1 - 0.99 \times 0.8)(1.0 \times 0.2 + 0.5) + 0.1(1.5 - 0.8) \\ &= (0.208)(0.7) + 0.1(0.7) = 0.1456 + 0.07 = 0.2156 \\ a(0.8) &= \frac{0.1}{0.2156} \approx 0.463822 \end{aligned} \] As persistence increases from 0.3 to 0.8, the inflation response increases more than fourfold (from 0.104 to 0.464) due to strong forward-looking accumulation of real rate gaps.

Problem 6.8 (Policy Aggressiveness and Disinflation). Suppose the central bank raises its inflation coefficient from \(\phi_\pi = 1.5\) to \(\phi_\pi = 2.5\). Holding other parameters at \(\beta = 0.99, \sigma = 1.0, \kappa = 0.1, \phi_y = 0.5, \rho = 0.5\), calculate the percentage reduction in the inflation coefficient \(a\).

Solution

At \(\phi_\pi = 1.5\), \(\Delta = 0.605\) and \(a = 0.1 / 0.605 \approx 0.165289\). At \(\phi_\pi = 2.5\): \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_{new} &= (1 - 0.495)(0.5 + 0.5) + 0.1(2.5 - 0.5) = 0.505(1.0) + 0.1(2.0) = 0.505 + 0.20 = 0.705 \\ a_{new} &= \frac{0.1}{0.705} \approx 0.141844 \end{aligned} \] The percentage reduction in the inflation multiplier is: \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{0.165289 - 0.141844}{0.165289} = \frac{0.023445}{0.165289} \approx 14.18\% \end{aligned} \] More aggressive policy feedback dampens inflation volatility by anchoring forward expectations.

Problem 6.9 (Macroeconomic Fit of AR(1) Natural Rate). Empirical estimates (e.g., Holston-Laubach-Williams) find that natural interest rate shocks have quarterly persistence \(\rho \in [0.85, 0.95]\). Explain the implications of such high persistence for output gap volatility under undetermined coefficients.

Solution

As \(\rho \to 1\), \(\sigma(1 - \rho) \to 0\). The term \(1 - \beta\rho\) approaches \(1 - \beta \approx 0.01\), making \(\Delta\) very small: \(\Delta \approx (1 - \beta)\phi_y + \kappa(\phi_\pi - 1)\). The output gap coefficient \(b = \frac{1 - \beta\rho}{\Delta} \to \frac{1 - \beta}{(1 - \beta)\phi_y + \kappa(\phi_\pi - 1)}\). If \(\kappa\) is small (strong price rigidity), the denominator is small, so high persistence magnifies output gap volatility unless the monetary authority actively leans against it.

Problem 6.10 (Multiple Exogenous Shocks and Additive Linearity). Suppose both an AR(1) natural rate shock \(u_t\) and an AR(1) cost-push shock \(e_t\) are present, with uncorrelated innovations. Prove that the undetermined coefficient solution is strictly additive: \(x_t = b_u u_t + b_e e_t\) and \(\pi_t = a_u u_t + a_e e_t\).

Solution

Because the structural system is linear, the principle of superposition applies. Let the structural operator be \(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}_t) = \mathbf{B}_u u_t + \mathbf{B}_e e_t\). Since \(\E_t[u_{t+1}] = \rho_u u_t\) and \(\E_t[e_{t+1}] = \rho_e e_t\) are mutually independent linear projections, \(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_u u_t + \mathbf{\Gamma}_e e_t) = \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_u u_t) + \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_e e_t) = \mathbf{B}_u u_t + \mathbf{B}_e e_t\). By uniqueness of the linear map in a non-singular system, the joint solution is the exact sum of the individual shock solutions.

Problem 6.11 (General Companion Form Algorithm). Outline McCallum's generalized algorithm of undetermined coefficients for a general vector linear difference system with predetermined state variables \(\mathbf{k}_{t-1}\) and jump variables \(\mathbf{y}_t\).

Solution

McCallum writes the system as \(\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{P} \mathbf{k}_{t-1} + \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{u}_t\) and \(\mathbf{k}_t = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{k}_{t-1} + \mathbf{S} \mathbf{u}_t\). Substituting these conjectures into structural equations generates a matrix quadratic equation for \(\mathbf{P}\) and \(\mathbf{R}\): \(\mathbf{F} \mathbf{R}^2 + \mathbf{G} \mathbf{R} + \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0}\). The stable equilibrium selects the unique matrix root \(\mathbf{R}\) whose eigenvalues lie strictly inside the unit circle, after which \(\mathbf{Q}\) and \(\mathbf{S}\) are solved via linear matrix equations.

Problem 6.12 (Sunspot Solutions and Free Parameters). When \(\det(\mathbf{M}) = 0\), show how undetermined coefficients fails and explain how a non-fundamental sunspot shock \(\xi_t\) enters the solution space.

Solution

When \(\det(\mathbf{M}) = 0\), the linear system \(\mathbf{M} \mathbf{\gamma} = \mathbf{d}\) either has no solutions or an infinite number of solutions. If the kernel of \(\mathbf{M}\) is non-trivial, there exists an eigenvector \(\mathbf{v} \ne \mathbf{0}\) such that \(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}\). An arbitrary expectational error \(\xi_t = \mathbf{y}_t - \E_{t-1}[\mathbf{y}_t]\) satisfying \(\E_t[\xi_{t+1}] = 0\) can be added along this null vector (\(\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{u}_t + \mathbf{v} \xi_t\)) without violating the equilibrium conditions, generating indeterminate, self-fulfilling volatility.

  3. With \(\Delta = 0.605\) and \(1 - \beta\rho = 1 - (0.99)(0.5) = 0.505\), what is the output gap multiplier \(b\)?

    \(b = 0.505 / 0.605 \approx 0.834711\).

  4. When does the method of undetermined coefficients fail to guarantee a unique macroeconomic path?

    Options

    Undetermined coefficients finds a candidate solution within the conjectured linear class, but cannot rule out additional sunspot solutions if determinacy conditions fail.

  5. In the three-equation NK model, what matrix equation is solved for \((a, b)\)?

  6. What happens to the inflation coefficient \(a\) as price stickiness disappears (\(\kappa \to \infty\))?
