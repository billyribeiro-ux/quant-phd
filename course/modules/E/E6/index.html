Macroeconomics II: Dynamic General Equilibrium

Modern quantitative macroeconomics models aggregate fluctuations as the general equilibrium outcome of intertemporally optimizing households and price-setting firms. This module builds the doctoral-level foundations of dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) modeling: starting from the Real Business Cycle benchmark, mastering log-linearization around deterministic steady states, deriving the forward-looking dynamic IS curve and New Keynesian Phillips curve, analyzing equilibrium determinacy and the Taylor principle, computing impulse response functions, exploring household heterogeneity in HANK models, and estimating structural systems via the Kalman filter. The module culminates in a production-grade Python DSGE solver project.

  • Prerequisites: E5 Macroeconomics I (IS-LM, AD-AS, Phillips curve, and Taylor rule), M7 Numerical Methods (linear systems, matrix decompositions), and S3 Time Series Analysis (state-space models, ARMA processes, and Kalman filtering).
  • Project: Three-equation New Keynesian DSGE solver and IRF engine, graded by deterministic autograder across 50 random seeds.
  • Data: No proprietary market feeds required. Course labs use analytical closed forms, exact rational arithmetic, and SIMULATED data paths labeled as such.

120 h lessons · 30 h project (20.0% of 150 h)

Objectives

By the end of E6 you will be able to:

  1. Formulate the non-linear Euler equations and resource constraints of the Real Business Cycle model and solve its deterministic steady state in exact arithmetic (Lesson 1).
  2. Compute analytical first-order Taylor log-linearizations of dynamic general equilibrium conditions and prove quadratic truncation error decay (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the forward-looking dynamic IS curve and New Keynesian Phillips curve from microfoundations of household optimization and Calvo staggered price contracts (Lessons 3 and 4).
  4. Assemble the canonical three-equation New Keynesian general equilibrium system and verify structural residuals along dynamic transition paths (Lesson 5).
  5. Solve expectational difference systems using the method of undetermined coefficients in exact rational fractions and floating-point matrices (Lesson 6).
  6. Analyze companion matrix eigenvalues under the Blanchard-Kahn conditions and establish the generalized Taylor principle determinacy boundary (Lesson 7).
  7. Compute structural impulse response functions to demand and monetary policy innovations and characterize their transmission channels (Lesson 8).
  8. Quantify how household borrowing constraints and hand-to-mouth behavior amplify aggregate interest rate sensitivity in HANK models (Lesson 9).

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions assessing mathematical and macroeconomic readiness for dynamic general equilibrium modeling, including two skill checks testing the implementation skills taught in project rungs 1 and 2. The mastery threshold is 80% (at least 8 correct out of 9).

  1. What is the steady-state real return on capital \(r^*\) in the baseline RBC model without growth?

    Options

    From the Euler equation in steady state, \(1 = \beta(1 + r^* - \delta)\), giving \(r^* = 1/\beta - 1 + \delta\).

  2. For CRRA utility \(u(c) = \ln c\), what is the relative risk aversion coefficient \(\sigma = -c u''(c) / u'(c)\)?

    For log utility, \(u'(c) = 1/c\) and \(u''(c) = -1/c^2\), so \(-c(-1/c^2)/(1/c) = 1.0\).

  3. What log-linear approximation is used for \(\ln(1 + x)\) when \(x \approx 0\)?

    Options

    First-order Taylor expansion of \(\ln(1+x)\) around 0 is \(x\).

  4. Under the Blanchard-Kahn condition, what determines the number of required unstable eigenvalues?

    Options

    Determinacy requires the number of unstable roots (\(|\lambda| > 1\)) to equal the number of jump variables.

  5. In a standard Taylor rule \(i_t = \phi_\pi \pi_t\), what standard value did John Taylor (1993) propose for \(\phi_\pi\)?

    Taylor's original 1993 rule specified \(\phi_\pi = 1.5\) and \(\phi_y = 0.5\).

  6. In a state-space model, what matrix operation computes one-step state predictions in the Kalman filter?

    Options

    The state prediction step propagates posterior estimates forward using the transition matrix \(\mathbf{T}\).

  7. For AR(1) persistence \(\rho = 0.5\), what is the infinite geometric sum \(\sum_{j=0}^\infty \rho^j\)?

    \(1 / (1 - 0.5) = 1 / 0.5 = 2.0\).

  8. Skill check (rung 1): calculate the New Keynesian determinant \(\Delta = (1 - \beta\rho)(\sigma(1 - \rho) + \phi_y) + \kappa(\phi_\pi - \rho)\) when \(\beta = 0.99, \sigma = 1, \kappa = 0.1, \phi_\pi = 1.5, \phi_y = 0.5, \rho = 0.5\).

    \(\Delta = (1 - 0.495)(0.5 + 0.5) + 0.1(1.5 - 0.5) = 0.505(1.0) + 0.10 = 0.605\).

  9. Skill check (rung 2): how are the undetermined coefficients \((b_v, a_v)\) for a monetary policy shock related to the demand shock coefficients \((b_d, a_d)\)?

    Options

    By linearity of the dynamic IS curve, the shock loading on \(v_t\) is \(-1/\sigma\), exactly the negative of \(r^n_t\) (\(+1/\sigma\)).

The project ladder

The module culminates in the Three-Equation New Keynesian DSGE Solver project (30 hours), structured into a 4-rung learning ladder:

  1. Worked example

    Complete analytical and numerical derivation of determinacy and impulse responses under baseline parameterization.

  2. Completion problem

    Implement the undetermined coefficients solver for a monetary policy shock and verify impact signs and equation residuals.

  3. Independent project

    Author a standalone Python module nk_solver.py implementing determinacy checks, undetermined coefficients, and dynamic IRF generation, validated against a 50-seed autograder suite. Project specifications.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the solver to accommodate inertial Taylor rules, cost-push markup shocks, or occasionally binding zero lower bound constraints.

Data status

No live or proprietary financial feeds are embedded in E6. All dynamic simulations and likelihood evaluations use closed-form analytical formulas, exact rational numbers (fractions.Fraction), or deterministic pseudo-random sequences labeled explicitly as SIMULATED data.
