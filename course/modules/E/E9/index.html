Sovereign Debt, Crises & Political Economy

Sovereign governments borrow without external collateral, subject to willingness-to-pay constraints and the discipline of international capital markets. This doctoral module provides rigorous theoretical derivations, empirical evaluations, and computational frameworks for sovereign debt sustainability, currency and banking crises, and macroeconomic forensics. Key topics include public-debt dynamics under \(r - g\), the Eaton–Gersovitz sovereign default model, first- and second-generation currency crisis models, Calvo sudden stops, twin banking-currency crises, stochastic debt sustainability analysis (DSA), fan chart risk distributions, and the Reinhart–Rogoff replication episode.

  • Prerequisites: E7 Monetary Economics & Central Banking (policy rates, term structure, fiscal theory of price level) and E8 International Macro & the Dollar (balance of payments, interest parity, safe asset convenience yields). Foundational econometrics (S2) and discounting (M1) are utilized throughout.
  • Project: Sovereign Debt Sustainability Analysis & Fan Chart Engine (32 hours, graded by autograder and sovereign risk memo rubric).

48 h lessons · 32 h project (40.0% of 80 h)

Objectives

By the end of E9, you will be able to:

  1. Formulate exact discrete-time public debt dynamics and evaluate interest-rate growth differentials \((r - g)\) under low-rate regimes and sign-flip reversal risks (Lesson 1).
  2. Solve the Eaton–Gersovitz sovereign default model, derive the endogenous bond price schedule \(q(b')\), and locate the peak of the sovereign Debt Laffer curve (Lesson 2).
  3. Analyze first- and second-generation currency crisis models, compute Krugman continuous-time reserve collapse timing, and determine Obstfeld self-fulfilling escape clause zones (Lesson 3).
  4. Model Calvo sudden stops, relative price collapses of non-traded goods, balance-sheet currency mismatches, and banking crisis insolvency boundaries (Lesson 4).
  5. Construct a stochastic debt sustainability analysis (DSA) engine, simulate Monte Carlo fan charts, and compute debt ceiling breach probabilities (Lesson 5).
  6. Replicate historical debt-growth datasets, correct spreadsheet formula omissions and weighting distortions, and critique empirical sovereign debt threshold claims (Lesson 6).

Placement Diagnostic

Ten questions: eight on the prerequisite foundations (E7 monetary theory, E8 international finance, S2 econometrics, M1 discounting) and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. Mastery bar is 80 percent (at least eight of ten correct) including both marked skill checks to qualify for skipping rungs 1 and 2.

  1. Skill check (Rung 1): A government begins period \(t=0\) with debt-to-GDP ratio \(b_0 = 0.90\). In period \(t=1\), real effective interest rate is \(r = 3.0\%\), real GDP growth is \(g = 2.0\%\), and the government runs a primary deficit of \(1.5\%\) (\(s = -0.015\)). What is the exact debt-to-GDP ratio \(b_1\) at the end of period 1?

    Options

    Using exact discrete-time accumulation: \(b_1 = \frac{1 + r}{1 + g} b_0 - s = \frac{1.03}{1.02} \times 0.90 - (-0.015) = 1.009804 \times 0.90 + 0.015 = 0.90882 + 0.015 = 0.9238\).

  2. Skill check (Rung 2): If a sovereign wishes to stabilize its public debt ratio at target \(b^* = 0.80\) under macroeconomic conditions \(r = 4.0\%\) and \(g = 1.0\%\), and its current primary balance is a deficit of \(1.0\%\) (\(s = -0.01\)), what is the required primary fiscal gap \(\mathrm{gap} = s^* - s\)?

    Options

    Debt-stabilizing primary surplus is \(s^* = \frac{r - g}{1 + g} b^* = \frac{0.03}{1.01} \times 0.80 = 0.02376 = 2.38\%\). The gap is \(s^* - s = 2.38\% - (-1.00\%) = 3.38\%\) of GDP.

  3. Under the balance-of-payments double-entry accounting identity (E8), if an economy experiences a current account deficit (\(\mathrm{CA} < 0\)) and zero capital account transfers, how must this deficit be balanced in the external accounts?

    Options

    The BoP identity requires \(\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{FA} = 0\), so a trade and income deficit requires foreign financial inflows or drawing down reserve assets.

  4. According to the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (E7), what determines the domestic price level when monetary policy is passive?

    Options

    FTPL states that \(B_t / P_t = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \beta^j \E_t[S_{t+j}]\), determining \(P_t\) directly from debt and expected primary surpluses.

  5. Why do sovereign safe assets command a convenience yield (E8, Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen 2012)?

    Options

    Safe asset convenience yields reflect non-pecuniary service flows, including collateral eligibility, regulatory compliance, and near-cash liquidity.

  6. If the real effective interest rate is \(r = 2.0\%\) and real GDP growth is \(g = 3.0\%\) (\(r < g\)), what steady-state debt ratio \(\bar{b}\) is sustained by a permanent primary deficit of \(1.2125\%\) of GDP (\(s = -0.012125\))?

    At steady state, \(\bar{b} = \frac{-s}{1 - (1+r)/(1+g)} = \frac{0.012125}{1 - 1.02/1.03} = \frac{0.012125}{0.0097087} = 1.25\) (or 125% of GDP).

  7. In econometric panel analysis with Newey-West standard errors (S2), why must autocovariance lags be included when evaluating multi-year debt forecast errors?

    Options

    Overlapping forecast horizons induce MA errors of order \(H-1\), rendering classical standard errors biased downward without HAC corrections.

  8. Under the Uncovered Interest Parity condition (E8), what does an increase in the foreign interest rate relative to the domestic rate imply under risk neutrality?

    Options

    UIP states \(\E[\Delta s] = i - i^*\). If \(i^* > i\), the expected change in exchange rate is negative (foreign currency expected to depreciate).

  9. If the shadow price of a dealer's balance-sheet constraint is \(\lambda = 12\) basis points, what is the theoretical cross-currency basis in basis points under intermediary pricing frictions (E8)?

    The basis directly measures the shadow cost of balance-sheet expansion: \(x = -\lambda = -12.0\) bp (magnitude 12.0 bp).

  10. Why cannot international creditors easily enforce sovereign debt contracts through domestic asset seizure in the borrower's jurisdiction?

    Options

    Sovereign immunity prevents foreign courts from seizing in-country infrastructure and state property, making debt sustainability dependent on willingness to pay.

Lesson Map (48 Study Hours)

The 48 lesson hours comprise 6 sequential 8-hour lessons covering theoretical foundations, empirical evidence, and quantitative methods for sovereign crises:

  1. L01 · Debt Dynamics and \(r - g\) (8 h) — Public-debt dynamics, government budget constraints, the Blanchard (2019) low-interest rate thesis, safe-asset yields, and reversal risks.
  2. L02 · Sovereign Default & Willingness to Pay (8 h) — The Eaton–Gersovitz (1981) model, sovereign immunity, default thresholds, endogenous bond price schedules, and the Debt Laffer curve.
  3. L03 · Currency Crises: Fundamentals vs. Self-Fulfilling Runs (8 h) — First-generation models (Krugman 1979), shadow exchange rates, attack timing, and second-generation escape clauses (Obstfeld 1996).
  4. L04 · Sudden Stops & Twin Crises (8 h) — Calvo (1998) capital flow reversals, relative price collapse of non-traded goods, balance-sheet mismatches, and banking crisis amplification (Reinhart & Rogoff 2009).
  5. L05 · Debt Sustainability Analysis & Fan Charts (8 h) — IMF/institutional DSA frameworks, multivariate VAR macroeconomic shocks, Monte Carlo fan charts, and fiscal stabilization gaps.
  6. L06 · The Reinhart-Rogoff Controversy & Replication (8 h) — Forensic analysis of the 90% debt threshold, spreadsheet formula errors, selective exclusions, weighting methods, and Herndon, Ash & Pollin (2014).
