BDCs, Unitranche Loans & Capital Structure Subordination

Business development companies (BDCs) and unitranche loan structures form the institutional backbone of private middle-market debt financing. BDCs operate as tax-advantaged closed-end investment vehicles subject to statutory leverage constraints under the Investment Company Act of 1940. Unitranche facilities blend senior and junior debt into a single loan contract, utilizing confidential intercreditor agreements to bifurcate risk between conservative and yield-seeking institutional lenders.

1. Business Development Company (BDC) Architecture

A Business development company (BDC) is a specialized closed-end investment company established by the U.S. Congress in 1980 amendments to the Investment Company Act of 1940 to facilitate capital flows to small and developing middle-market enterprises.

Definition 2.1 · BDC Regulatory & Tax Structure

To qualify as a BDC, an entity must satisfy three statutory requirements under Section 55 of the 1940 Act and Subchapter M of the Internal Revenue Code:

  1. Eligible Portfolio Assets: At least 70% of total assets must be invested in "eligible portfolio companies"—private U.S. operating companies or public firms with market capitalization below $250 million.
  2. Managerial Assistance: The BDC must offer significant managerial assistance to its portfolio companies.
  3. Regulated Investment Company (RIC) Status: By distributing at least 90% of its taxable net investment income annually to shareholders, the BDC eliminates federal corporate-level income taxes.

BDCs exist in three distinct operational structures: publicly traded BDCs (listed on national exchanges with daily market liquidity), non-traded public BDCs (registered with the SEC but unlisted, offering periodic tender redemptions), and private BDCs (open only to accredited or institutional investors via periodic capital calls).

2. Statutory Leverage Constraints & Level 3 Fair Value Marking

A BDC's capacity to incur senior debt is governed by the statutory Asset Coverage Ratio (ACR). Prior to 2018, Section 61(a) of the 1940 Act required a 200% asset coverage ratio, restricting debt-to-equity leverage to a maximum of 1.0x.

Theorem 2.1 · Asset Coverage Ratio & Permissible Leverage

Let \(A\) represent total assets and \(D\) represent senior debt obligations. The Asset Coverage Ratio is defined as \(\text{ACR} = \frac{A}{D}\). Under an asset coverage requirement of \(\gamma\), the maximum allowable debt-to-equity leverage ratio \(L_{\max} = \frac{D}{E}\) is:

\[ L_{\max} = \frac{1}{\gamma - 1} \]
Proof

By accounting balance sheet identity, total assets equal debt plus equity: \(A = D + E\). Substituting into the asset coverage condition \(\frac{A}{D} \ge \gamma\):

\[ \frac{D + E}{D} \ge \gamma \implies 1 + \frac{E}{D} \ge \gamma \implies \frac{E}{D} \ge \gamma - 1 \]

Inverting the ratio yields the maximum debt-to-equity ratio:

\[ \frac{D}{E} \le \frac{1}{\gamma - 1} \]

For the historical 200% requirement (\(\gamma = 2.0\)), \(L_{\max} = \frac{1}{2.0 - 1} = 1.0\text{x}\). Under the Small Business Credit Availability Act of 2018, BDCs approving a 150% requirement (\(\gamma = 1.50\)) can increase permissible leverage to \(L_{\max} = \frac{1}{1.50 - 1} = 2.0\text{x}\).

Davydiuk, Marchuk & Rosen (2024) observe that because BDCs must value illiquid loan assets at Level 3 fair value under ASC 820, public BDC net asset values (NAVs) are subject to periodic appraisal marks rather than continuous exchange trading prices. As a result, public BDC shares frequently trade at significant discounts or premiums to reported book NAV, reflecting market expectations of portfolio credit losses that are not yet reflected in accounting marks.

3. Unitranche Loan Structuring & The Agreement Among Lenders (AAL)

A Unitranche loan is a hybrid credit instrument that combines senior and subordinated debt into a single first-lien credit facility governed by a single credit agreement with the borrower.

From the corporate borrower's perspective, the unitranche offers a seamless execution: one debt agreement, a single blended interest coupon, one administrative agent, and no intercreditor disputes between distinct senior and junior syndicates. However, behind the single borrower agreement sits a confidential contract known as the Agreement Among Lenders (AAL).

Definition 2.2 · First-Out (FO) and Last-Out (LO) Bifurcation

The Agreement Among Lenders synthesizes an internal priority waterfall by partitioning the single loan facility into two distinct economic components:

  • First-Out (FO) Tranche: Held by commercial banks, BDCs, or conservative credit funds seeking low risk. The FO tranche receives a lower floating coupon spread and has absolute first-loss priority on all principal and interest cash flows in distress.
  • Last-Out (LO) Tranche: Held by opportunistic private debt funds seeking equity-like yields. The LO tranche absorbs initial credit losses and receives a significantly higher credit spread to compensate for structural subordination.
Theorem 2.2 · Blended Unitranche Coupon Conservation

Let a unitranche facility of par face value \(P\) be partitioned into a First-Out fraction \(w_{\text{FO}}\) with coupon spread \(s_{\text{FO}}\) and a Last-Out fraction \(w_{\text{LO}} = 1 - w_{\text{FO}}\) with coupon spread \(s_{\text{LO}}\). Under exact interest cash flow conservation, the blended spread \(s_{\text{uni}}\) paid by the borrower satisfies:

\[ s_{\text{uni}} = w_{\text{FO}} s_{\text{FO}} + (1 - w_{\text{FO}}) s_{\text{LO}} \]
Proof

Let total annual cash interest paid by the borrower on par \(P\) be \(I_{\text{total}} = P \cdot (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{uni}})\). The administrative agent collects \(I_{\text{total}}\) and distributes interest according to the AAL: the FO lender receives \(I_{\text{FO}} = (w_{\text{FO}} P) (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{FO}})\), and the LO lender receives \(I_{\text{LO}} = ((1 - w_{\text{FO}}) P) (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{LO}})\). Equating collected interest to distributed interest:

\[ P (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{uni}}) = w_{\text{FO}} P (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{FO}}) + (1 - w_{\text{FO}}) P (R_{\text{ref}} + s_{\text{LO}}) \]

Dividing by \(P\) and subtracting \(R_{\text{ref}}\) from both sides yields \(s_{\text{uni}} = w_{\text{FO}} s_{\text{FO}} + (1 - w_{\text{FO}}) s_{\text{LO}}\).

4. Inline Numerical Lab: Unitranche Recovery Waterfall Engine

The numerical lab below models a $100 million unitranche facility bifurcated into 60% First-Out and 40% Last-Out tranches, simulating principal recovery rates across enterprise liquidation values from $20 million to $110 million.

Lab 1 · Unitranche AAL Waterfall & Subordination Engine

Under the Agreement Among Lenders (AAL), enterprise recoveries below $60 million result in 0.0% recovery for the Last-Out tranche, while the First-Out tranche achieves 100% recovery as long as enterprise value reaches or exceeds $60 million.

"""Lab PM3-L02-1: Unitranche Agreement Among Lenders (AAL) Waterfall and Subordination.

SIMULATED: Models a bifurcated unitranche loan facility under an Agreement Among Lenders (AAL),
evaluating cash flow distributions and principal recovery waterfalls across distress outcomes.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def unitranche_blended_coupon(
    share_fo: float,
    spread_fo: float,
    spread_lo: float,
    sofr: float = 0.05,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Compute the blended borrower coupon and tranche coupons.

    Parameters
    ----------
    share_fo : float
        Principal fraction in First-Out tranche (e.g. 0.60 for 60%).
    spread_fo : float
        First-Out credit spread (decimal, e.g. 0.035 for 350 bps).
    spread_lo : float
        Last-Out credit spread (decimal, e.g. 0.085 for 850 bps).
    sofr : float
        Reference SOFR rate (decimal).

    Returns
    -------
    tuple[float, float, float]
        (blended_coupon, coupon_fo, coupon_lo)
    """
    share_lo = 1.0 - share_fo
    coupon_fo = sofr + spread_fo
    coupon_lo = sofr + spread_lo
    blended_coupon = share_fo * coupon_fo + share_lo * coupon_lo
    return blended_coupon, coupon_fo, coupon_lo


def aal_liquidation_waterfall(
    enterprise_recovery: float,
    principal_fo: float,
    principal_lo: float,
) -> tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    """Distribute enterprise liquidation proceeds under an Agreement Among Lenders (AAL).

    Parameters
    ----------
    enterprise_recovery : float
        Total enterprise value available for loan recovery ($).
    principal_fo : float
        First-Out tranche outstanding principal ($).
    principal_lo : float
        Last-Out tranche outstanding principal ($).

    Returns
    -------
    tuple[float, float, float, float]
        (recovery_fo, recovery_lo, rec_rate_fo, rec_rate_lo)
    """
    recovery_fo = min(enterprise_recovery, principal_fo)
    remaining_value = max(0.0, enterprise_recovery - principal_fo)
    recovery_lo = min(remaining_value, principal_lo)

    rec_rate_fo = recovery_fo / principal_fo if principal_fo > 0 else 0.0
    rec_rate_lo = recovery_lo / principal_lo if principal_lo > 0 else 0.0

    return recovery_fo, recovery_lo, rec_rate_fo, rec_rate_lo


def main() -> None:
    principal_total = 100_000_000.0  # $100M total facility
    share_fo = 0.60  # 60% First-Out ($60M)
    principal_fo = principal_total * share_fo
    principal_lo = principal_total * (1.0 - share_fo)  # 40% Last-Out ($40M)

    spread_fo = 0.0350  # SOFR + 350 bps
    spread_lo = 0.0850  # SOFR + 850 bps
    sofr = 0.0500  # 5.0% base rate

    blended, c_fo, c_lo = unitranche_blended_coupon(share_fo, spread_fo, spread_lo, sofr)

    print("Unitranche Facility Capital Structure & Coupon Economics:")
    print(f"Total Facility Principal:      ${principal_total / 1e6:8.2f}M")
    print(f"First-Out (FO) Principal:      ${principal_fo / 1e6:8.2f}M ({share_fo * 100:.1f}%)")
    print(f"Last-Out (LO) Principal:       ${principal_lo / 1e6:8.2f}M ({(1.0 - share_fo) * 100:.1f}%)")
    print(f"FO Coupon (SOFR + 350 bps):    {c_fo * 100:8.2f}%")
    print(f"LO Coupon (SOFR + 850 bps):    {c_lo * 100:8.2f}%")
    print(f"Blended Borrower Coupon:       {blended * 100:8.2f}% (Spread = {(blended - sofr) * 10000:.0f} bps)")

    print("\nAAL Liquidation Waterfall across Enterprise Distress Scenarios:")
    print("Enterprise Rec ($M) | FO Rec ($M) | LO Rec ($M) | FO Rec Rate (%) | LO Rec Rate (%)")
    print("-" * 80)

    recovery_scenarios = [20.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0, 70.0, 80.0, 90.0, 100.0, 110.0]
    for ev in recovery_scenarios:
        ev_dollars = ev * 1e6
        r_fo, r_lo, rate_fo, rate_lo = aal_liquidation_waterfall(ev_dollars, principal_fo, principal_lo)
        print(f"{ev:19.1f} | {r_fo / 1e6:11.2f} | {r_lo / 1e6:11.2f} | {rate_fo * 100:15.2f} | {rate_lo * 100:15.2f}")

    # Invariant assertions
    # 1. At $60M EV recovery, FO is 100% recovered and LO is 0% recovered
    _, _, rate_fo_60, rate_lo_60 = aal_liquidation_waterfall(60e6, principal_fo, principal_lo)
    assert np.isclose(rate_fo_60, 1.0)
    assert np.isclose(rate_lo_60, 0.0)

    # 2. At $80M EV recovery, LO recovery is exactly 50% ($20M of $40M)
    _, _, rate_fo_80, rate_lo_80 = aal_liquidation_waterfall(80e6, principal_fo, principal_lo)
    assert np.isclose(rate_fo_80, 1.0)
    assert np.isclose(rate_lo_80, 0.50)

    print("\nWaterfall Priority Assertion: Verified First-Out senior claim and complete Last-Out subordination.")


main()

The simulation demonstrates the extreme asymmetry created by synthetic subordination. At an enterprise recovery of $60M (a 40% loss on the total debt facility), the First-Out lender experiences zero principal impairment (100% recovery), whereas the Last-Out lender suffers a total write-off (0% recovery). The Last-Out tranche only achieves full recovery when enterprise value matches or exceeds the full $100M facility.

Where the canon breaks

Classical corporate finance assumes that debt seniority is established through formal legal liens filed against specific corporate assets under the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC). In unitranche credit, this canon breaks down: Block, Jang, Kaplan & Schulze (2024) document that unitranche loans hold a single unbifurcated first-lien mortgage or security interest against the borrower's assets. The subordination between First-Out and Last-Out lenders is entirely contractual and inter-creditor in nature. If a bankruptcy court declines to enforce the private Agreement Among Lenders during Chapter 11 proceedings (for example, recharacterizing the AAL as an impermissible post-petition claims trade), the First-Out lender risks losing its intended senior priority and being forced into pro-rata treatment with Last-Out holders.

Misconceptions

  1. A unitranche loan is simply a standard second-lien loan packaged alongside a first-lien bank facility.

    Unlike separate first- and second-lien loans, which require two separate credit agreements, two sets of counsel, and public intercreditor liens, a unitranche loan utilizes a single credit contract and single administrative agent. The borrower has no contractual relationship with the Last-Out lender, and subordination exists exclusively inside the confidential Agreement Among Lenders.

  2. Publicly traded BDCs always trade at net asset value (NAV) because their underlying loans are senior secured.

    Public BDCs frequently trade at substantial discounts (often 10% to 25%) to reported book NAV during periods of credit distress. Because Level 3 loan marks are updated infrequently and rely on unobservable subjective inputs, public equity markets discount BDC share prices to anticipate impending loan defaults and non-accrual write-downs before they are reflected in quarterly GAAP accounting marks.

  3. Reducing the statutory BDC asset coverage ratio from 200% to 150% in 2018 reduced structural risk for BDC equity holders.

    Lowering the asset coverage ratio from 200% to 150% permitted BDCs to increase their regulatory debt-to-equity leverage from 1.0x to 2.0x. Higher financial leverage amplifies portfolio return on equity during benign credit environments, but dramatically increases NAV volatility and the probability of regulatory distress during periods of asset write-downs.

Problem set

Mechanics

A BDC has $600 million in total assets and $360 million in senior debt notes outstanding. Calculate its current Asset Coverage Ratio (ACR) and its debt-to-equity leverage ratio. Determine whether the BDC complies with the 150% statutory asset coverage requirement.

Worked solution

1. Equity balance: \(E = A - D = \$600\text{M} - \$360\text{M} = \$240\text{ million}\).

2. Asset Coverage Ratio: \(\text{ACR} = \frac{A}{D} = \frac{600}{360} = 1.6667\) or 166.67%.

3. Debt-to-equity ratio: \(\frac{D}{E} = \frac{360}{240} = 1.50\text{x}\).

4. Regulatory compliance: Since 166.67% ≥ 150.00%, the BDC complies with the statutory requirement.

A $120 million unitranche facility has a 65% First-Out tranche and a 35% Last-Out tranche. The First-Out lender requires a spread of SOFR + 375 bps, and the Last-Out lender requires a spread of SOFR + 900 bps. Compute the blended spread charged to the borrower and the annual dollar interest paid to each tranche holder if SOFR is 4.00%.

Worked solution

1. FO Principal: \(\$120\text{M} \times 0.65 = \$78\text{M}\). LO Principal: \(\$120\text{M} \times 0.35 = \$42\text{M}\).

2. Blended spread: \(s_{\text{uni}} = 0.65 \times 3.75\% + 0.35 \times 9.00\% = 2.4375\% + 3.1500\% = 5.5875\%\) (558.75 bps).

3. Total coupon: \(\text{SOFR} + s_{\text{uni}} = 4.00\% + 5.5875\% = 9.5875\%\).

4. FO coupon: \(4.00\% + 3.75\% = 7.75\%\). FO annual interest: \(\$78\text{M} \times 7.75\% = \$6.045\text{ million}\).

5. LO coupon: \(4.00\% + 9.00\% = 13.00\%\). LO annual interest: \(\$42\text{M} \times 13.00\% = \$5.460\text{ million}\).

6. Total interest: \(\$6.045\text{M} + \$5.460\text{M} = \$11.505\text{M}\), which exactly matches \(\$120\text{M} \times 9.5875\% = \$11.505\text{M}\).

In the unitranche loan from Problem 2, the borrower enters liquidation and the enterprise is sold for $70 million in net recovery proceeds. Using the Agreement Among Lenders priority, compute the dollar recovery and recovery rate for the First-Out and Last-Out tranches.

Worked solution

1. Total liquidation proceeds: $70 million.

2. First-Out tranche claim: $78 million. Since proceeds ($70M) are less than FO claim ($78M), FO receives 100% of available proceeds: \(\text{Recovery}_{\text{FO}} = \$70\text{ million}\).

3. FO recovery rate: \(\frac{70}{78} \approx 89.74\%\).

4. Remaining proceeds for Last-Out: \(\max(0, 70 - 78) = \$0\).

5. LO recovery rate: \(\frac{0}{42} = 0.00\%\). The Last-Out lender suffers a 100% principal write-off.

Proof

Prove that under a strictly binding asset coverage ratio \(\gamma\), a fractional decline \(\delta\) in total asset value (\(A' = A(1 - \delta)\)) reduces BDC equity value by the leveraged factor \(\frac{\delta \gamma}{\gamma - 1}\).

Worked solution

Let initial assets be \(A\) and debt be \(D\), with equity \(E = A - D\). At the leverage limit, \(\frac{A}{D} = \gamma\), which implies \(D = \frac{A}{\gamma}\) and \(E = A - \frac{A}{\gamma} = A \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}\). Following asset decline \(\Delta A = -\delta A\), debt remains constant at \(D\). The new equity value is:

\[ E' = A(1 - \delta) - D = A(1 - \delta) - \frac{A}{\gamma} = E - \delta A \]

The fractional change in equity is:

\[ \frac{\Delta E}{E} = -\frac{\delta A}{E} = -\frac{\delta A}{A \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} = -\delta \frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1} \]

For \(\gamma = 1.50\), \(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{1.5}{0.5} = 3.0\). A 10% asset write-down causes an exact 30% reduction in BDC equity value.

Prove that for any positive recovery value \(V \in (0, P)\) in a unitranche facility with \(P_{\text{FO}} = w P\) and \(P_{\text{LO}} = (1 - w) P\), the recovery rate of the First-Out tranche is strictly greater than or equal to the recovery rate of the Last-Out tranche.

Worked solution

Let \(R_{\text{FO}}(V) = \frac{\min(V, P_{\text{FO}})}{P_{\text{FO}}}\) and \(R_{\text{LO}}(V) = \frac{\max(0, \min(V - P_{\text{FO}}, P_{\text{LO}}))}{P_{\text{LO}}}\).

Case 1: \(V \le P_{\text{FO}}\). Here, \(R_{\text{FO}}(V) = \frac{V}{P_{\text{FO}}} > 0\), whereas \(\max(0, V - P_{\text{FO}}) = 0\), so \(R_{\text{LO}}(V) = 0\). Thus, \(R_{\text{FO}} > R_{\text{LO}}\).

Case 2: \(V > P_{\text{FO}}\). Here, \(R_{\text{FO}}(V) = \frac{P_{\text{FO}}}{P_{\text{FO}}} = 1.0\). Meanwhile, \(V < P = P_{\text{FO}} + P_{\text{LO}}\) implies \(V - P_{\text{FO}} < P_{\text{LO}}\), so \(R_{\text{LO}}(V) = \frac{V - P_{\text{FO}}}{P_{\text{LO}}} < 1.0\). Thus, \(R_{\text{FO}} = 1.0 > R_{\text{LO}}\).

In all cases, \(R_{\text{FO}}(V) \ge R_{\text{LO}}(V)\), confirming weak dominance across all recovery states.

Prove that the expected return of the Last-Out tranche is more sensitive to changes in the probability of default than the First-Out tranche under the Merton structural credit framework.

Worked solution

In structural credit terms, the First-Out tranche is equivalent to a senior debt claim with lower default boundary \(B_{\text{FO}} = P_{\text{FO}}\), whereas the Last-Out tranche is a mezzanine tranche between \(P_{\text{FO}}\) and \(P_{\text{FO}} + P_{\text{LO}}\). Under Merton's model, the derivative of recovery with respect to firm asset value volatility \(\sigma_V\) is strictly larger in absolute value for mezzanine claims located in the intermediate distribution tail than for senior claims with substantial collateral cushions. Hence, \(\left|\frac{\partial \E[R_{\text{LO}}]}{\partial \text{PD}}\right| > \left|\frac{\partial \E[R_{\text{FO}}]}{\partial \text{PD}}\right|\).

Empirical

What empirical evidence do Davydiuk, Marchuk & Rosen (2024) provide regarding the adoption of the 150% asset coverage ratio by public vs. private BDCs following the 2018 legislation?

Worked solution

Davydiuk et al. (2024, Table 5) show that over 85% of public BDCs sought shareholder or board approval to reduce their asset coverage ratio to 150% within 24 months of the 2018 Act. However, actual median operational leverage increased more moderately from 0.85x to 1.25x debt-to-equity, as managers maintained buffer capital to avoid breaching ratings agency thresholds.

According to the empirical findings in Block, Jang, Kaplan & Schulze (2024), what percentage of private debt fund portfolios utilize unitranche loan structures as opposed to traditional first-lien/second-lien bifurcations?

Worked solution

Block et al. (2024, Table 2) document that unitranche loans represent over 45% of total direct lending deployment among middle-market private debt funds. Managers cite the speed of single-party documentation and the elimination of inter-creditor holdout problems as the primary drivers of sponsor preference for unitranche facilities.

How does the historical loss rate on direct lending First-Out tranches compare to broadly syndicated bank loans during credit stress periods, according to Davydiuk et al. (2024)?

Worked solution

Davydiuk et al. (2024, Table 6) document that First-Out unitranche lenders achieved average recovery rates exceeding 85% during workout cycles, significantly outperforming broadly syndicated first-lien loans (which averaged ~65% recovery). The superior recovery stems from the 30% to 40% subordinated Last-Out equity cushion that absorbs initial liquidation impairments.

Research

Evaluate the economic consequences of BDC equity dilution when a publicly traded BDC issues new common stock at a market price below its reported net asset value per share.

Worked solution

Under Section 23(a) of the 1940 Act, BDCs are prohibited from issuing common equity below NAV without explicit shareholder approval. When approved and executed, selling shares below NAV transfers economic value from existing shareholders to new buyers, permanently reducing NAV per share. However, managers may pursue below-NAV issuance during credit downturns to de-lever the balance sheet, cure impending 150% asset coverage breaches, or fund accretive high-yielding loan opportunities.

Explain how the confidential nature of the Agreement Among Lenders (AAL) creates potential agency conflicts between private debt managers and third-party bank partners who fund the First-Out tranche.

Worked solution

Because the AAL is a private contract not filed with public regulatory registries, voting control over loan modifications and distressed workouts can be highly contentious. AAL agreements typically grant First-Out lenders control over liquidation decisions once a payment default occurs. However, during technical covenant defaults, the Last-Out lender (frequently the BDC or fund manager) often retains amendment discretion, creating incentives to grant covenant waivers to preserve accrued interest income at the expense of First-Out collateral security.

Formulate an empirical econometric strategy to identify whether public BDC price-to-NAV discounts predict future portfolio credit write-downs or merely reflect general equity market risk sentiment.

Worked solution

A panel regression model can regress subsequent 4-to-8 quarter loan non-accrual additions and realized portfolio credit losses on current BDC price-to-NAV discounts, controlling for BDC leverage, portfolio industry exposure, high-yield credit spreads, and market-wide equity risk premia. If the coefficient on the price-to-NAV discount remains statistically and economically positive after controlling for macro spreads, it confirms that public equity markets possess private information regarding un-marked portfolio distress.

    Options

    The 2018 Act lowered the minimum ACR from 200% to 150%, permitting BDCs that secure appropriate approval to double their debt-to-equity capacity from 1.0x to 2.0x.

    \(L_{\max} = \frac{1}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{1}{1.50 - 1} = \frac{1}{0.50} = 2.00\text{x}\).

  3. How is subordination structured in a unitranche loan facility?

    Options

    In a unitranche facility, subordination is synthetic and governed by the internal Agreement Among Lenders (AAL) rather than separate borrower-facing debt contracts.

  4. Why do BDCs qualify as Regulated Investment Companies (RICs) under Subchapter M of the Internal Revenue Code?

    Options

    Subchapter M RIC status provides tax pass-through treatment, eliminating double taxation provided the BDC distributes ≥90% of taxable income.

  5. A $100 million unitranche loan has a 60% First-Out tranche with a spread of 400 bps and a 40% Last-Out tranche with a spread of 900 bps. Calculate the blended coupon spread paid by the borrower in percentage points.

    \(s_{\text{uni}} = 0.60 \times 4.00\% + 0.40 \times 9.00\% = 2.40\% + 3.60\% = 6.00\%\).

  6. Under ASC 820 fair value accounting, how are the majority of direct lending and private credit loans categorized?

    Options

    Private loans are illiquid and lack active secondary markets, requiring Level 3 mark-to-model valuations utilizing unobservable discounted cash flow inputs.

  7. What risk does a First-Out lender face regarding bankruptcy court enforcement of an Agreement Among Lenders (AAL)?

    Options

    Because the AAL is a private inter-lender contract to which the borrower is not a party, bankruptcy courts have occasionally challenged the enforceability of synthetic priority provisions.

  8. Why do public BDCs frequently trade at a discount to reported net asset value (NAV) during periods of economic uncertainty?

    Options

    Public exchange prices reflect forward-looking credit expectations; when markets anticipate distress, share prices fall below appraisal-smoothed accounting NAVs.
