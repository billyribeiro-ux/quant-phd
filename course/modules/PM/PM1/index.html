Private Equity

Private equity manages over four trillion dollars of institutional capital through closed-end limited partnerships. This module establishes the structural, contract-theoretic, and econometric foundations of private capital: limited partnership agreement mechanics, management fees and carried interest waterfalls, money-weighted cash flow benchmarking via Public Market Equivalents, and the distortionary leverage dynamics of subscription credit lines.

  • Prerequisites: CF5 Leveraged Buyouts and F7 Performance Evaluation
  • Unlocks: PM2 Venture Capital and PM3 Private Debt
  • Project: PME engine, autograder

Objectives

  1. Model the 10-year closed-end private fund life cycle, drawdown pacing, and European versus American distribution waterfalls under strict cash flow conservation.
  2. Decompose general partner compensation into fixed management fee annuities and performance-contingent carried interest, proving the conditions for clawback liabilities.
  3. Formulate the Kaplan & Schoar (2005) Public Market Equivalent (KS-PME) and prove its economic interpretation as an exact wealth ratio relative to public market indexing.
  4. Derive the continuous Direct Alpha metric of Gredil, Griffiths & Stucke (2014) and prove that Direct Alpha is zero if and only if KS-PME equals unity.
  5. Analyze the index replication mechanics of Long & Nickels (1996) PME and isolate the synthetic short-position failure mode on outperforming buyout funds.
  6. Quantify the internal rate of return (IRR) inflation produced by short-term subscription lines of credit and distinguish genuine value creation from capital call timing compression.
  7. Evaluate the empirical debate on net private equity outperformance, sample selection biases, living-dead fund write-downs, and factor risk adjustments.

Prerequisites

This module assumes mastery of corporate finance and empirical asset pricing:

  • From CF5: leveraged buyout mechanics, multiple on invested capital (MOIC), sources and uses, and cash sweep covenants.
  • From F7: factor-adjusted alpha, alpha inference, return smoothing, and return unsmoothing.
  • From CF1: internal rate of return, present value, and discount factors.

Placement diagnostic

Achieve an 80% score and correctly solve both practical skill items to validate prerequisite mastery and unlock Rung 3 of the project ladder directly.

  1. A private fund calls 100 million dollars at \(t=0\) when the benchmark index is 100. At \(t=3\), the fund distributes 150 million dollars when the index is 120. There is no residual NAV. Calculate the Kaplan & Schoar (2005) Public Market Equivalent (KS-PME) of the fund.

    Discounted distribution: \(150 / 120 = 1.25\). Discounted call: \(100 / 100 = 1.0\). \(\mathrm{KS\text{-}PME} = 1.25 / 1.0 = 1.25\).

  2. In a Long & Nickels PME synthetic replication, the synthetic NAV at \(t=1\) is 100 million dollars. Between \(t=1\) and \(t=2\), the index rises from 100 to 110. At \(t=2\), the private fund distributes 120 million dollars and calls zero capital. Calculate the synthetic portfolio NAV immediately after the \(t=2\) distribution.

    Before cash flow, synthetic NAV grows to \(100 \times (110 / 100) = 110\). After the distribution of 120, NAV is \(110 - 120 = -10.0\) million dollars, demonstrating the short-position failure mode.

  3. Under a standard European (whole-fund) waterfall with an 8% preferred return and a 100% GP catch-up to a 20% carry, when does the General Partner receive their first carried interest dollar?

    Options

    A European waterfall requires full return of all drawn capital across all deals plus the preferred return before the GP participates in any carried interest.

  4. Why does delaying capital calls using a subscription credit facility inflate fund internal rate of return (IRR)?

    Options

    IRR is highly sensitive to the holding time of capital; bridging calls with credit lines shortens the LP cash investment window, mathematically increasing the annualized IRR even as facility interest slightly reduces total MOIC.

  5. In Metrick & Yasuda (2010), what proportion of total expected general partner revenue in buyout funds is accounted for by fixed management fees versus variable carried interest?

    Options

    Metrick & Yasuda (2010) show that fixed management fees provide guaranteed base compensation representing roughly two-thirds of expected revenue for large buyout funds.

Lessons

L01 · 7 h Private Equity Fund Structure and LPA Mechanics The GP/LP limited partnership structure, 10-year term, capital call notices, distribution mechanics, committed versus invested fee bases, and European versus American waterfalls. L02 · 7 h Carried Interest and Waterfall Economics Preferred return hurdle rates, GP catch-up schedules, carry tier splits, clawback obligations, escrow reserves, and the Metrick–Yasuda fee revenue decomposition. L03 · 7 h The Public Market Equivalent and Cash Flow Benchmarking Limitations of IRR and MOIC, Kaplan & Schoar (2005) KS-PME formulation, economic wealth ratios, public market discounting, and empirical findings. L04 · 7 h Generalized PME Frameworks: Long–Nickels and Direct Alpha Long & Nickels (1996) synthetic NAV replication, the short-position failure mode, Gredil, Griffiths & Stucke (2014) Direct Alpha derivation, and liquidity adjustments. L05 · 8 h Subscription Lines of Credit and the Private Equity Debate Capital call credit facilities, balance sheet mechanics, IRR distortion via timing compression, and the debate on whether private equity outperforms public markets after fees.
