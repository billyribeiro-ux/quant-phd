Carried interest aligns General Partner incentives with Limited Partner wealth creation by allocating 20% of net profits to the manager. However, the exact timing and magnitude of carried interest depend on complex non-linear contractual mechanisms: preferred return hurdle rates, General Partner catch-up schedules, clawback provisions, and escrow accounts. Rigorous modeling of these tiers is essential to forecast net LP cash flows and assess managerial option payoffs.

1The Preferred Return Hurdle and GP Catch-Up Mechanics

Definition 2.1 (Carried Interest and the Preferred Return)

Carried interest (or “carry”) is the performance fee share—contractually established at \(\gamma = 20\%\) in institutional private equity—allocated to the General Partner from net partnership profits above a specified benchmark.

Before the GP participates in any carried interest, the private-equity fund structure requires limited partners to receive a preferred return (or “hurdle rate”), conventionally set at an annualized rate of \(r_h = 8\%\). For a capital draw \(C_i\) called at time \(t_i\) and realized at time \(t\), the accrued hurdle dollar obligation \(H_i(t)\) is:

\[ H_i(t) = C_i \left[(1 + r_h)^{t - t_i} - 1\right] \]

Once limited partners receive their full drawn capital plus the accrued preferred return, the distribution schedule enters the GP catch-up tier.

Definition 2.2 (The GP Catch-Up Mechanism)

The GP catch-up is an accelerated interim distribution designed to restore the GP's carried interest share to exactly \(\gamma\) (typically 20%) of all cumulative profits distributed above drawn capital. Let \(H\) denote the total preferred return distributed to LPs in Tier 2. The catch-up allocation operates as follows:

  1. 100% Catch-Up: The GP receives 100% of all subsequent distribution dollars until cumulative GP carry equals 20% of total profits distributed above capital. The dollar catch-up amount \(C_{\text{catch}}\) solves: \[ \frac{C_{\text{catch}}}{H + C_{\text{catch}}} = \gamma \implies C_{\text{catch}} = \frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} H \] For \(\gamma = 0.20\), \(\frac{0.20}{0.80} = 0.25\). Thus, the GP receives exactly \(\$0.25\) for every \(\$1.00\) of preferred return paid to LPs.
  2. 50% (or 80/20) Catch-Up: Distributions are split (e.g. 50% to GP, 50% to LP) until the 20% aggregate threshold is reached, softening the cash diversion from LPs over a wider dollar band.
Theorem 2.1 (The Catch-Up Invariant)

Let total net distribution proceeds above drawn capital be \(\Pi = D - C_{\text{drawn}}\). If \(\Pi \ge \frac{H}{1 - \gamma}\), then under a complete catch-up mechanism, the total carried interest paid to the GP is identically:

\[ C_{\text{carry}} = \gamma \cdot \Pi \]

and the net profit received by limited partners is identically \((1 - \gamma) \cdot \Pi\).

Proof

In Tier 2, LPs receive \(H\), while GP receives 0. In Tier 3, the GP receives \(C_{\text{catch}} = \frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} H\). Total profit distributed through Tier 3 is \(\Pi_3 = H + C_{\text{catch}} = H + \frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} H = \frac{1}{1 - \gamma} H\). At this point, the GP's share of distributed profit is \(\frac{C_{\text{catch}}}{\Pi_3} = \frac{\frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} H}{\frac{1}{1 - \gamma} H} = \gamma\). Any residual profit \(\Pi_{\text{res}} = \Pi - \Pi_3 \ge 0\) in Tier 4 is contractually split \(\gamma\) to GP and \((1 - \gamma)\) to LPs. Therefore, total GP carry is \(C_{\text{carry}} = C_{\text{catch}} + \gamma \Pi_{\text{res}} = \gamma \Pi_3 + \gamma (\Pi - \Pi_3) = \gamma \Pi\), which proves the claim.

2The Four Tiers of an Institutional Waterfall

Institutional private equity waterfalls govern cash priority through four sequential gates under strict cash sweep conservation:

Tier Name LP Share GP Share Termination Condition
Tier 1 Return of Capital 100% 0% Cumulative LP distributions equal cumulative drawn capital (\(C_{\text{drawn}}\)).
Tier 2 Preferred Return 100% 0% Limited partners achieve an 8.0% compounded annual return on drawn capital.
Tier 3 GP Catch-Up 0% (or 50%) 100% (or 50%) GP receives 20% of total profits distributed in Tiers 2 and 3 (\(C_{\text{catch}} = 0.25 H\)).
Tier 4 Carried Interest Split 80% 20% Applies to all remaining residual proceeds until final partnership liquidation.

3Clawback Provisions and Escrow Reserves

In American (deal-by-deal) waterfalls, the GP takes carried interest on early profitable exits. If subsequent portfolio company investments incur write-downs or total bankruptcies, the GP will have received more than 20% of cumulative net fund profits.

Definition 2.3 (Clawback Obligation)

A clawback provision is an enforceable covenant in the Limited Partnership Agreement requiring the General Partner to repay excess carried interest upon fund termination. Let \(C_{\text{paid}}\) denote the cumulative gross carried interest distributed to the GP across all prior years, and let \(C_{\text{allowed}}\) denote the maximum carried interest permissible on the whole-fund net outcome:

\[ C_{\text{allowed}} = \max\left(0, \gamma \cdot \max(0, D_{\text{total}} - C_{\text{drawn}} - H_{\text{total}})\right) \]

The contractual clawback liability \(L_{\text{clawback}}\) owed by the GP to the LPs is:

\[ L_{\text{clawback}} = \max\left(0, C_{\text{paid}} - C_{\text{allowed}}\right) \]
Remark 2.1 (Escrow Accounts and Tax Leakage)

To secure clawback enforceability, institutional LPAs mandate that a portion of interim carried interest (typically 30% to 50%) be deposited into a segregated third-party escrow account. Furthermore, LPAs specify whether clawbacks are calculated on a pre-tax or net-of-tax basis. Under a net-of-tax clawback, the GP deducts hypothetical taxes paid on historical carry distributions, leaving limited partners bearing the tax friction if the GP cannot recover tax refunds from revenue authorities.

4The Metrick & Yasuda (2010) Revenue Decomposition

Metrick & Yasuda (2010) develop a comprehensive simulation model to value the expected components of GP compensation across buyout and venture capital partnerships.

Metrick & Yasuda (2010) prove that for a median buyout fund with a 2% management fee and 20% carry, the present value of management fees is approximately $10.35 per $100 of committed capital, whereas the expected present value of carried interest is $6.65 per $100 of commitment. Consequently, fixed management fees account for approximately 60.9% of total expected GP revenue, demonstrating that private equity managers receive substantial guaranteed baseline compensation independent of market outperformance.

Lab 1 · Waterfall Distribution and Clawback Liability Quantification

A SIMULATED fund invests $100M across 3 deals: Deal 1 exits for $90M ($30M cost), Deal 2 exits for $30M ($30M cost), and Deal 3 collapses to $0 ($40M cost). We test whether a European waterfall pays $0 carry, while an American waterfall over-distributes $12.00M to the GP, creating an exact $12.00M clawback liability.

"""Lab 1 for PM1 L02: Carried Interest Waterfall and Clawback Mechanics.

SIMULATED: a private equity fund evaluating European vs. American waterfalls across 3 deals.
Deal 1: $30M invested, exits in year 3 for $90M (+$60M profit).
Deal 2: $30M invested, exits in year 5 for $30M ($0 profit).
Deal 3: $40M invested, written down to $0 in year 7 (-$40M loss).
Total invested: $100M. Total gross exit proceeds: $120M (Net fund profit = +$20M).
Hurdle: 8% per annum compounded. Carry: 20% with 100% GP catch-up.
Demonstrates GP over-distribution and clawback liability under American waterfall.
"""

from __future__ import annotations


def main() -> None:
    # 1. European (Whole-Fund) Waterfall at Fund Liquidation (Year 7)
    # Total drawn capital = $100M, total proceeds = $120M
    # Net profit = $20M
    total_drawn = 100.0
    total_proceeds = 120.0
    hurdle_rate = 0.08

    # Preferred return on $100M drawn over average hold of ~5 years:
    # For simplicity of illustration, suppose accrued preferred return is $25.0M
    accrued_hurdle = 25.0

    # In European waterfall:
    # Tier 1: Return of Capital = min($120M, $100M) = $100M to LP
    # Remaining proceeds = $20M
    # Tier 2: Preferred Return = min($20M, $25M) = $20M to LP
    # Remaining proceeds = $0M
    # GP receives $0 carried interest because hurdle ($25M) was not met!
    euro_lp_capital = total_drawn
    euro_lp_hurdle = total_proceeds - euro_lp_capital
    euro_gp_carry = 0.0

    print("--- European (Whole-Fund) Waterfall ---")
    print(f"LP Return of Capital: ${euro_lp_capital:.2f}M")
    print(f"LP Preferred Return: ${euro_lp_hurdle:.2f}M (Hurdle shortfall: ${accrued_hurdle - euro_lp_hurdle:.2f}M)")
    print(f"GP Carried Interest: ${euro_gp_carry:.2f}M")
    print("Clawback Liability: $0.00M")

    # 2. American (Deal-by-Deal) Waterfall
    # Deal 1 exits in Year 3 for $90M on $30M cost basis (Profit = $60M)
    # Hurdle on Deal 1: $30M * ((1.08)^3 - 1) = $30M * 0.2597 = $7.79M
    d1_cost = 30.0
    d1_proceeds = 90.0
    d1_hurdle = d1_cost * ((1.0 + hurdle_rate) ** 3 - 1.0)  # $7.79M
    d1_profit_above_cost = d1_proceeds - d1_cost  # $60M
    d1_profit_above_hurdle = d1_profit_above_cost - d1_hurdle  # $52.21M

    # Under 100% GP catch-up:
    # GP receives 100% of cash until GP carry = 20% of total profit (hurdle + catch-up)
    # GP catch-up amount: C / (d1_hurdle + C) = 0.20 => C = 0.20 / 0.80 * d1_hurdle = 0.25 * d1_hurdle
    d1_gp_catchup = 0.25 * d1_hurdle
    # Remaining profit split 80% LP / 20% GP
    d1_residual_profit = d1_profit_above_hurdle - d1_gp_catchup
    d1_gp_residual = 0.20 * d1_residual_profit
    d1_gp_carry = d1_gp_catchup + d1_gp_residual

    # Deal 2 exits for $30M on $30M cost (no carry, no loss)
    # Deal 3 collapses to $0 on $40M cost (total loss of $40M)
    # Cumulative fund profit across all 3 deals = $20M
    # Maximum allowable GP carry on whole-fund basis = $0 (since net profit $20M < total hurdle $25M)
    clawback_liability = d1_gp_carry

    print("\n--- American (Deal-by-Deal) Waterfall ---")
    print(f"Deal 1 Exit Proceeds: ${d1_proceeds:.2f}M on ${d1_cost:.2f}M cost")
    print(f"Deal 1 GP Carry Distributed: ${d1_gp_carry:.2f}M")
    print("Deal 3 Loss: -$40.00M")
    print(f"Interim GP Carry Over-Distribution: ${d1_gp_carry:.2f}M")
    print(f"Final Clawback Owed by GP to LPs: ${clawback_liability:.2f}M")


main()
--- European (Whole-Fund) Waterfall ---
LP Return of Capital: $100.00M
LP Preferred Return: $20.00M (Hurdle shortfall: $5.00M)
GP Carried Interest: $0.00M
Clawback Liability: $0.00M

--- American (Deal-by-Deal) Waterfall ---
Deal 1 Exit Proceeds: $90.00M on $30.00M cost
Deal 1 GP Carry Distributed: $12.00M
Deal 3 Loss: -$40.00M
Interim GP Carry Over-Distribution: $12.00M
Final Clawback Owed by GP to LPs: $12.00M

The experiment confirms that under a European waterfall, the GP receives $0.00M in carried interest because cumulative fund profit ($20M) fails to clear the partnership hurdle ($25M). Under an American waterfall, Deal 1 distributes $12.00M in carry immediately; when Deal 3 collapses, the GP owes an exact $12.00M clawback obligation back to LPs, demonstrating the structural moral hazard of deal-by-deal carry.

5Where the canon breaks

Textbook principal-agent theory models carried interest as an optimal call option aligning the manager's effort with limited partner returns. In practice, the option-like nature of carried interest introduces severe risk-shifting incentives: because the GP shares in 20% of the upside but has limited personal liability for downside losses beyond their modest co-investment, a GP managing an underwater fund late in its lifecycle has a strong incentive to engage in excessive risk-taking on remaining deals ("resurrection risk"), attempting high-beta or highly leveraged gambles to cross the preferred return hurdle before liquidation.

6Misconceptions

  1. “Carried interest is calculated as 20% of gross portfolio sales proceeds.”

    Carried interest is calculated strictly on net profits above drawn capital and the preferred return hurdle, never on gross transaction revenue or total exit proceeds.

  2. “A 100% GP catch-up means the GP receives 100% of all fund profits forever.”

    The 100% catch-up applies only over a narrow interim dollar band—specifically until cumulative GP carry equals 20% of total profits distributed above capital. Once the 20% aggregate threshold is achieved, all subsequent proceeds are split 80% to LP and 20% to GP.

  3. “Clawback provisions guarantee that limited partners will always recover excess carried interest.”

    Clawbacks are general unsecured liabilities of the GP entity. If the GP distributed carry to individual deal partners who spent the proceeds, departed the firm, or filed for personal bankruptcy, limited partners face significant collection risk unless protected by funded escrow accounts.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Preferred Return Hurdle Compounding

A capital call of $50M is drawn on Day 0. The fund specifies an 8.0% annual compounded preferred return. Calculate the accrued preferred return dollar amount at the end of Year 4 before any distributions occur.

Solution

The compound growth factor is \((1.08)^4 = 1.360489\). Cumulative preferred return is \(H = \$50\text{M} \times (1.360489 - 1) = \$50\text{M} \times 0.360489 = \$18.024\text{M}\). Limited partners must receive \(\$50\text{M} + \$18.024\text{M} = \$68.024\text{M}\) before the GP can receive any carried interest.

M2 · 100% Catch-Up Band Width

In a European waterfall, limited partners have received $100M of returned capital and $20M of preferred return. The LPA specifies 20% carry with a 100% GP catch-up. How many dollars must be distributed to the GP in Tier 3 to complete the catch-up?

Solution

Under a 100% catch-up to a 20% carry, the catch-up amount is \(C_{\text{catch}} = \frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} H = \frac{0.20}{0.80} \times \$20\text{M} = 0.25 \times \$20\text{M} = \$5.0\text{M}\). After the GP receives $5.0M, total profit distributed is \(\$20\text{M} + \$5\text{M} = \$25\text{M}\), and GP carry is \(5 / 25 = 20.0\%\).

M3 · Residual Tier Split

Following the completion of the catch-up in problem M2, the fund distributes an additional $40M from subsequent exits. Calculate the exact dollar allocation of this $40M between LPs and the GP.

Solution

In Tier 4, residual distributions are split 80% to LPs and 20% to the GP. The LP allocation is \(0.80 \times \$40\text{M} = \$32.0\text{M}\). The GP allocation is \(0.20 \times \$40\text{M} = \$8.0\text{M}\).

Proof

P1 · Catch-Up Equality with 50% Catch-Up

Prove that under a 50% GP catch-up mechanism, the total dollar distribution \(\Delta D\) required to achieve full catch-up on preferred return \(H\) is \(\Delta D = \frac{2\gamma}{1 - 2\gamma} H\) when \(\gamma < 0.5\), and find the exact dollar amounts received by LPs and GP during the catch-up tier.

Solution

In the 50% catch-up tier, each dollar is split 50% to GP and 50% to LP. Let \(C\) be the dollars distributed to GP; an identical amount \(C\) is distributed to LP. Cumulative LP profit is \(H + C\), and cumulative GP profit is \(C\). Full catch-up requires \(\frac{C}{(H + C) + C} = \gamma \implies \frac{C}{H + 2C} = \gamma \implies C = \gamma H + 2\gamma C \implies C(1 - 2\gamma) = \gamma H \implies C = \frac{\gamma}{1 - 2\gamma} H\). Total cash required is \(\Delta D = 2C = \frac{2\gamma}{1 - 2\gamma} H\). For \(\gamma = 0.20\), \(C = \frac{0.20}{0.60} H = \frac{1}{3} H\), and total tier cash is \(\Delta D = \frac{2}{3} H\).

P2 · Bound on Interim American Clawback Exposure

Consider a fund with \(K\) deals, each with cost \(C_k\) and proceeds \(X_k\). Prove that the maximum clawback liability under an American waterfall cannot exceed \(\gamma \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} (X_k - C_k)\), where \(\mathcal{P} = \{k : X_k > C_k\}\) is the set of profitable deals.

Solution

Under an American waterfall, carry is collected only on deals in \(\mathcal{P}\). The maximum carry distributed on deal \(k \in \mathcal{P}\) is bounded by \(\gamma (X_k - C_k)\). Hence, cumulative carry paid to the GP across all deals satisfies \(C_{\text{paid}} \le \gamma \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} (X_k - C_k)\). Since allowable whole-fund carry is non-negative (\(C_{\text{allowed}} \ge 0\)), the clawback liability \(L = \max(0, C_{\text{paid}} - C_{\text{allowed}}) \le C_{\text{paid}} \le \gamma \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} (X_k - C_k)\), establishing the upper bound.

P3 · Pareto Efficiency of Whole-Fund Waterfalls

Prove that for risk-averse Limited Partners and a risk-neutral General Partner with zero insolvency friction, an American waterfall is weakly Pareto-dominated by a European waterfall when holding total expected carry constant.

Solution

Under an American waterfall, the timing of LP distributions is delayed relative to a European waterfall, and LPs bear non-zero default risk on the GP clawback obligation if subsequent deals collapse. Because LPs are strictly risk-averse, the lottery over whether the clawback will be paid in full yields strictly lower expected utility than receiving the cash upfront with certainty under a European waterfall. Since the risk-neutral GP has identical expected nominal compensation, the European waterfall weakly Pareto-dominates.

Empirical

E1 · Metrick & Yasuda (2010) Buyout vs. Venture Capital Carry

Compare the relative share of carried interest in total GP revenue between buyout funds and venture capital funds reported by Metrick & Yasuda (2010).

Solution

Metrick & Yasuda (2010) find that carried interest constitutes a materially higher fraction of total GP revenue in venture capital funds (~46% to 50%) than in large buyout funds (~35% to 40%). Because VC returns exhibit higher right-tail skewness and smaller fund sizes relative to deal count, option-like carry provides a larger share of compensation compared to the large asset-gathering management fees of mega-buyout funds.

E2 · Market Adoption of European Waterfalls

What structural factors driven by institutional limited partners caused the widespread shift from American to European waterfalls in US private equity following the 2008 global financial crisis?

Solution

During the 2008 financial crisis, many 2005–2007 vintage funds that had distributed substantial deal-by-deal carry on early exits suffered catastrophic write-downs on later cyclical investments. When funds liquidated, LPs faced massive clawback deficits that GPs could not repay due to departed partners and illiquidity. Institutional LP consortia (such as the Institutional Limited Partners Association, ILPA) mandated European waterfalls as standard contractual policy to eradicate clawback collection risk.

E3 · Escrow Retention Rates in Practice

What is the standard market percentage of carried interest withheld in escrow in modern deal-by-deal LPAs, and how is it released?

Solution

Modern deal-by-deal LPAs typically mandate a 30% to 50% escrow withholding on all gross carry distributions. Escrow balances remain segregated until the fund achieves aggregate net profitability across all realized investments, with remaining unrealized assets written down to fair value, providing a cash reserve to satisfy potential clawback obligations.

Research

R1 · Net-of-Tax Clawbacks and Moral Hazard

Evaluate the economic inefficiency of "net-of-tax" clawback clauses where the GP's clawback obligation is reduced by the taxes paid by individual partners.

Solution

Under a net-of-tax clause, if a GP over-distributes $10M of carry and partners pay $3.7M in capital gains taxes, the GP is only required to repay $6.3M to LPs upon clawback. Limited partners bear a permanent $3.7M deadweight loss, effectively subsidizing the personal income taxes of GP executives. This creates moral hazard by encouraging GPs to aggressively recognize early carry distributions even when later deals carry high risk.

R2 · End-of-Fund Resurrection Risk

Model the portfolio manager's asset selection problem when a fund in Year 8 has exhausted its management fee base and sits 200 basis points below the preferred return hurdle.

Solution

Because the GP receives zero carry unless the preferred return hurdle is cleared, the GP's payoff function is convex: \(\max(0, 0.20 \cdot (\Pi - H))\). If the fund is slightly below the hurdle, a safe investment yielding 5% leaves the GP with $0 carry. However, a high-variance, speculative investment (e.g. distressed debt, unhedged operational restructuring) that has a 30% chance of yielding 30% and a 70% chance of total loss provides positive expected carry to the GP, while imposing downside losses entirely on LPs.

R3 · Tiered Carried Interest and Super-Carry

Some mega-funds incorporate "tiered carry" (e.g. 20% carry up to 2.0x MOIC, stepping up to 25% or 30% carry above 3.0x MOIC). Analyze how tiered carry alters the GP's risk incentives relative to flat 20% carry.

Solution

Tiered carry increases the convexity of the GP payoff at high return thresholds. While it incentivizes managers to maximize home-run outcomes on winner investments, it also amplifies incentives to apply extreme leverage to portfolio companies to push returns past the 2.5x or 3.0x super-carry threshold, elevating bankruptcy risk for portfolio firms.

9Retrieval quiz

  1. What is the standard institutional preferred return hurdle rate in private equity?

    Options

    An 8% per annum compounded hurdle is the universal market standard in institutional private equity agreements.

  2. Under a 100% GP catch-up mechanism to a 20% carry, how many dollars of catch-up does the GP receive if the preferred return paid to LPs is $10.0M?

    \(C_{\text{catch}} = \frac{0.20}{0.80} \times \$10.0\text{M} = 0.25 \times \$10.0\text{M} = \$2.5\text{M}\).

  3. In a standard European waterfall, what happens in Tier 1?

    Options

    Tier 1 allocates 100% of cash to limited partners until cumulative distributions equal 100% of drawn capital across the whole fund.

  4. What is a clawback provision in a private equity partnership?

    Options

    A clawback requires the GP to return over-distributed carried interest upon fund termination so that cumulative carry does not exceed 20% of net profits above hurdle.

  5. Once the preferred return hurdle and the 100% GP catch-up tier are completely satisfied, what percentage of each subsequent distribution dollar goes to the General Partner?

    In the residual tier (Tier 4), proceeds are split 80% to LPs and 20% to the GP.

  6. Why do institutional limited partners strongly favor a European waterfall over an American waterfall?

    Options

    European waterfalls protect LPs from interim over-distribution and clawback default risk by aggregating performance across the entire partnership.

  7. In Metrick & Yasuda (2010), what is the expected present value of carried interest per $100 of commitment for a median buyout fund (where total revenue is $17.00 and management fees are $10.35)?

    \(\$17.00 - \$10.35 = \$6.65\) per $100 of commitment.

  8. What risk incentive is introduced when an underwater private equity fund nears the end of its 10-year term?

    Options

    Due to the call-option structure of carry, underwater GPs have an incentive to take extreme risk on remaining assets because upside yields 20% carry while downside falls on LPs.
