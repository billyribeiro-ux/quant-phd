Venture Capital

Venture capital finances high-uncertainty, intangible-intensive entrepreneurial enterprises through dynamic financial contracting. This module establishes the structural, contract-theoretic, and econometric foundations of venture capital: the valuation of staged financing as compound real options, the empirical anatomy of venture contracts separating control rights from cash flows, capitalization table dynamics across down-rounds and option pool expansions, portfolio construction under extreme power-law return distributions, and econometric corrections for endogenous round refinancing and transaction censoring.

  • Prerequisites: CF6 Equity Capital Markets and PM1 Private Equity
  • Project: Term-sheet economics, autograder
  • Reading: Kaplan & Strömberg (2003), Korteweg & Sorensen (2010), Gompers & Lerner (2004)

Objectives

  1. Formulate staged venture capital financing as a compound real option to abandon or expand, proving the conditions under which staging transforms negative-NPV upfront commitments into positive-NPV sequential investments.
  2. Decompose venture capital contracts into cash flow rights, voting rights, board seats, and liquidation rights following Kaplan & Strömberg (2003), proving the state-contingent control-shift mechanism.
  3. Model complete capitalization table progressions through seed, Series A, Series B, and down-round Series C financing, deriving closed-form solutions for pre-money option pool shuffles.
  4. Derive conversion price adjustments under Full Ratchet, Broad-Based Weighted Average, and Narrow-Based Weighted Average anti-dilution provisions, establishing their mathematical dilution hierarchy.
  5. Construct multi-tier liquidation preference waterfalls across standard (LIFO) and pari passu seniority regimes, solving simultaneous Nash equilibria for rational preferred conversion decisions.
  6. Analyze power-law venture returns and portfolio skewness, proving why traditional mean-variance diversification fails and quantifying fund-maker probability as a function of portfolio deal count.
  7. Estimate selection-corrected venture capital risk and return characteristics following Korteweg & Sorensen (2010), isolating the upward return bias produced by endogenous round refinancing and transaction censoring.

Placement diagnostic

A score of 80% or higher waives Rungs 1 and 2 of the capstone project. Items 1 and 2 directly test the skills taught in Rungs 1 and 2.

  1. In an option pool shuffle, a venture capital firm invests $5.0M at a $15.0M pre-money valuation ($20.0M post-money), requiring a 15% unallocated option pool on a post-money fully diluted basis. The founders hold 10,000,000 common shares prior to the round. How many total shares must exist post-closing?

    Select the correct post-closing share count:

    Let \(N_{\text{post}}\) be post-closing shares. The investor purchases \(I/V_{\text{post}} = 5/20 = 25\%\). The option pool takes 15%. Founders retain \(100\% - 25\% - 15\% = 60\%\). Therefore, \(N_{\text{post}} = 10{,}000{,}000 / 0.60 = 16{,}666{,}667\) shares. Pre-money share price is \(\$20\text{M} / 16{,}666{,}667 = \$1.20\).

  2. Series A preferred shares were issued at $2.00 per share. In a down-round, Series B issues new shares at $1.00 each, raising $5.0M (5,000,000 new shares). Immediately prior to Series B, the fully diluted share count is 10,000,000 shares. Under Broad-Based Weighted Average anti-dilution, what is the adjusted Series A conversion price?

    Select the adjusted conversion price:

    Hypothetical shares issuable at old conversion price: \(\$5\text{M} / \$2.00 = 2{,}500{,}000\). Adjusted conversion price: \(CP_{\text{new}} = \$2.00 \times \frac{10\text{M} + 2.5\text{M}}{10\text{M} + 5.0\text{M}} = \$2.00 \times \frac{12.5\text{M}}{15.0\text{M}} = \$1.6667\).

  3. According to Rock's (1986) winner's-curse model of IPO underpricing (from CF6), why must initial public offerings be underpriced on average?

    Select the economic rationale:

    Rock (1986) proves that uninformed investors are crowded out of underpriced IPOs by informed bidders, suffering the winner's curse. Average underpricing is required to ensure uninformed capital enters the primary equity market.

  4. In private equity fund performance evaluation (from PM1), which mathematical property distinguishes Kaplan–Schoar Public Market Equivalent (KS-PME) from the Internal Rate of Return (IRR)?

    Select the correct distinction:

    Kaplan & Schoar (2005) formulate KS-PME as the ratio of benchmark-discounted distributions (plus residual NAV) to benchmark-discounted capital calls, representing the terminal wealth ratio of investing in the fund versus public markets.

  5. In corporate capital budgeting (from CF4), how does staged financing alter project valuation compared to an upfront lump-sum commitment?

    Select the real option mechanism:

    Staged financing decomposes an uncommitted project into a sequential compound call option, giving the investor the right to abandon without deploying subsequent expansion tranches.

Lessons

L01 · 6 h Staged Financing & Real Options in Ventures Sequential capital allocation, milestone-based investing, agency costs of free cash flow, real options to abandon or delay, and Gompers & Lerner (2004) staging dynamics. L02 · 6 h Venture Contracts & Governance Rights The empirical anatomy of VC term sheets: separating cash flow rights, voting rights, board composition, and founder vesting schedules following Kaplan & Strömberg (2003). L03 · 7 h Cap Tables, Liquidation Preferences & Anti-Dilution Option pool shuffle algebra, convertible vs. participating preferred, liquidation preference seniority waterfalls, and Full Ratchet versus Broad-Based anti-dilution adjustments. L04 · 7 h Power-Law Returns & Portfolio Skewness Pareto distributions in venture outcomes, return asymmetry, failure of mean-variance diversification, the Babe Ruth effect, and fund size scaling dynamics. L05 · 6 h Selection Bias & Censoring in VC Return Data Endogenous round refinancing, transaction censoring, living-dead write-down lags, and the Korteweg & Sorensen (2010) econometric selection correction.
