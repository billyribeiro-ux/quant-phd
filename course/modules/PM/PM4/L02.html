Agency MBS Pass-Throughs & Cash Flow Waterfalls

The $9 trillion agency mortgage-backed securities market transforms illiquid, idiosyncratic individual residential mortgages into liquid, standardized fixed-income securities. Modeling pass-through securities requires mastering the institutional architecture of Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, and Freddie Mac, the fee deductions that separate gross collateral coupons from investor pass-through rates, the monthly cash flow waterfall, pool factor decay, and the pricing drag created by settlement delay conventions in the forward To-Be-Announced (TBA) market.

1. The Agency Ecosystem & Securitization Structure

In the United States, conforming residential mortgages are securitized primarily through three agencies:

  • Government National Mortgage Association (GNMA / Ginnie Mae): A wholly owned government corporation within the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). GNMA securities carry the explicit full faith and credit guarantee of the United States government, guaranteeing timely payment of principal and interest on loans backed by the FHA, VA, and USDA.
  • Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA / Fannie Mae) & Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation (FHLMC / Freddie Mac): Government-Sponsored Enterprises (GSEs) operating under federal conservatorship directed by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) since September 2008. They guarantee conforming conventional mortgages that satisfy statutory size limits and underwriting criteria.
Definition 2.1 · Agency MBS Pass-Through Security

An Agency MBS pass-through is a securitized debt instrument representing an undivided, pro-rata beneficial ownership interest in a designated pool of conforming residential mortgages. Monthly mortgage payments collected from underlying borrowers—comprising scheduled principal, unscheduled prepayments, and interest less servicing and guarantee fees—are passed through directly to certificate holders on a pro-rata basis, backed by an agency credit guarantee.

2. Fee Architecture & Net Coupon Pass-Through

A mortgage pool collateralizes loans with individual note rates \(r_{m, j}\). The pool's aggregate collateral interest rate is described by its Weighted Average Coupon (WAC):

\[ \text{WAC} = \sum_{j=1}^K w_j r_{m, j}, \quad w_j = \frac{B_{0, j}}{\sum_{k=1}^K B_{0, k}} \]

Similarly, the remaining maturity is summarized by the Weighted Average Maturity (WAM), measured in months.

Theorem 2.1 · Net Pass-Through Coupon Identity

The net pass-through coupon \(c\) remitted to the investor is strictly lower than the collateral \(\text{WAC}\), determined by the deduction of the servicing fee rate \(s\) and the guarantee fee rate \(g\):

\[ c = \text{WAC} - s - g \]
Proof

Let \(B_{t-1}\) be the pool collateral balance at the start of month \(t\). The total gross interest collected from borrowers across the pool is \(I_{\text{gross}, t} = B_{t-1} \times \frac{\text{WAC}}{12}\). By contractual structure of the pooling and servicing agreement (PSA), two structural claims are deducted prior to investor distribution:

  1. Servicing Fee: The loan servicer retains \(S_t = B_{t-1} \times \frac{s}{12}\) for billing, collecting payments, managing escrow accounts (property taxes and hazard insurance), and pursuing delinquencies.
  2. Guarantee Fee (G-Fee): The guarantor (GSE or GNMA) receives \(G_t = B_{t-1} \times \frac{g}{12}\) to compensate for guaranteeing the timely payment of principal and interest against borrower default.

The net interest remitted to the pass-through certificate holders is the residual:

\[ I_{\text{net}, t} = I_{\text{gross}, t} - S_t - G_t = B_{t-1} \left( \frac{\text{WAC}}{12} - \frac{s}{12} - \frac{g}{12} \right) = B_{t-1} \frac{\text{WAC} - s - g}{12} = B_{t-1} \frac{c}{12} \]

Dividing by \(B_{t-1} / 12\) yields \(c = \text{WAC} - s - g\).

3. Monthly Cash Flow Waterfall & Pool Factor Dynamics

In each monthly remittance period \(t = 1, 2, \dots, N\), cash flows cascade through the following sequential waterfall:

  1. Scheduled Debt Service: The aggregate scheduled monthly payment \(PMT_t\) is computed over the remaining term \(N - t + 1\) based on collateral \(\text{WAC}\) and beginning balance \(B_{t-1}\):
\[ PMT_t = B_{t-1} \frac{\text{WAC}/12}{1 - (1 + \text{WAC}/12)^{-(N - t + 1)}} \]
  1. Scheduled Principal Partition: Scheduled principal is \(SP_t = PMT_t - B_{t-1} \left(\frac{\text{WAC}}{12}\right)\).
  2. Unscheduled Prepayment: Prepayment principal is \(PR_t = (B_{t-1} - SP_t) \times \text{SMM}_t\).
  3. Total Principal Remitted: \(TP_t = SP_t + PR_t\).
  4. Ending Pool Balance: \(B_t = B_{t-1} - TP_t\).
  5. Total Investor Cash Flow:
\[ CF_t = B_{t-1} \left(\frac{c}{12}\right) + TP_t = B_{t-1} \left(\frac{c}{12}\right) + SP_t + PR_t \]
Definition 2.2 · Mortgage Pool Factor

The pool factor \(F_t\) at month \(t\) is the fraction of original collateral principal remaining outstanding:

\[ F_t = \frac{B_t}{B_0}, \quad F_0 = 1.0, \quad \lim_{t \to N} F_t = 0.0 \]

Because \(TP_t \ge 0\) in every month, \(F_t\) is a weakly monotonically decreasing step function: \(F_t \le F_{t-1}\) for all \(t\).

Theorem 2.2 · Collateral Principal Conservation

Under an agency pass-through with no credit loss haircuts, cumulative principal returned to investors over the security's life equals original pool balance:

\[ \sum_{t=1}^N TP_t = \sum_{t=1}^N (SP_t + PR_t) = B_0 \]
Proof

From the balance recurrence, \(B_t = B_{t-1} - TP_t \implies TP_t = B_{t-1} - B_t\). Summing over all months \(t = 1, \dots, N\):

\[ \sum_{t=1}^N TP_t = \sum_{t=1}^N (B_{t-1} - B_t) = (B_0 - B_1) + (B_1 - B_2) + \dots + (B_{N-1} - B_N) = B_0 - B_N \]

Because the mortgage fully amortizes by maturity or prepays in full, \(B_N = 0\). Hence \(\sum_{t=1}^N TP_t = B_0\).

4. TBA Market Trading & Settlement Delay Days

The vast majority of agency pass-through trading occurs in the forward To-Be-Announced (TBA) market. In a TBA trade, buyers and sellers agree on coupon, agency, maturity, price, and settlement month, while specific pool identities (CUSIPs) are notified 48 hours prior to monthly settlement under SIFMA guidelines.

A critical structural friction in agency valuation is payment delay. Borrowers make monthly payments on the 1st of each month (accruing interest through the prior month). However, servicers and agency trustees require administrative processing time to aggregate payments, reconcile prepayments, and remit funds to certificate holders.

Definition 2.3 · Stated vs. Actual Delay Days

The stated delay represents the nominal days elapsed from the first day of the accrual month to payment date. The actual delay represents the economic interest-free gap beyond normal month-end settlement:

  • GNMA I: Stated delay of 45 days. Actual delay = 14 days (paid on the 15th of the month following accrual).
  • GNMA II: Stated delay of 50 days. Actual delay = 19 days (paid on the 20th).
  • Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac (UMBS): Stated delay of 55 days. Actual delay = 24 days (paid on the 25th).
Theorem 2.3 · Present Value Delay Drag

Under a continuous annual discounting yield \(y\), the settlement delay of \(d_{\text{actual}}\) days imposes a permanent multiplicative price reduction on monthly cash flow \(CF_t\):

\[ PV(CF_t) = CF_t \exp\left( -y \left[ \frac{t}{12} + \frac{d_{\text{actual}}}{365} \right] \right) = CF_t \exp\left( -y \frac{t}{12} \right) \exp\left( -y \frac{d_{\text{actual}}}{365} \right) \]

The delay drag in basis points of par is approximately \(y \times \frac{d_{\text{actual}}}{365} \times 10^4\).

Lab 1 · Agency Pass-Through Waterfall & Delay Valuation

Simulating an agency waterfall under 150% PSA will demonstrate pool factor decay, verify the principal conservation identity \(\sum TP_t = B_0\), and quantify the ~36 bps delay drag of Fannie Mae 24-day settlement delay.

"""Lab PM4-L02-1: Agency MBS Pass-Through Waterfall and Settlement Delay Mechanics.

SIMULATED: Models monthly cash flow distribution of a Fannie Mae 30-year fixed-rate pass-through pool
under 150% PSA prepayment, isolating scheduled principal, unscheduled prepayments, servicing fees,
guarantee fees, pool factor decay, and the present-value impact of delay days.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np


def generate_agency_waterfall(
    notional: float = 10_000_000.0,
    wac: float = 0.060,
    servicing_fee: float = 0.0044,
    g_fee: float = 0.0006,
    wam_months: int = 360,
    psa_speed: float = 150.0,
) -> dict[str, list[float]]:
    """Simulate agency pass-through monthly waterfall under PSA prepayment.

    Parameters
    ----------
    notional : float
        Initial pool collateral balance ($).
    wac : float
        Weighted average collateral coupon.
    servicing_fee : float
        Servicer fee rate.
    g_fee : float
        GSE guarantee fee rate.
    wam_months : int
        Remaining loan term in months.
    psa_speed : float
        PSA benchmark prepayment percentage.

    Returns
    -------
    dict[str, list[float]]
        Time series of pool balances, payments, fees, and pool factors.
    """
    pass_through_coupon = wac - servicing_fee - g_fee
    wac_m = wac / 12.0
    pt_m = pass_through_coupon / 12.0
    svc_m = servicing_fee / 12.0
    gfe_m = g_fee / 12.0

    balances: list[float] = [notional]
    sched_prin: list[float] = []
    prepay_prin: list[float] = []
    investor_interest: list[float] = []
    servicing_cf: list[float] = []
    gfee_cf: list[float] = []
    investor_total_cf: list[float] = []
    pool_factors: list[float] = [1.0]

    current_balance = notional

    for m in range(1, wam_months + 1):
        if current_balance <= 1e-4:
            sched_prin.append(0.0)
            prepay_prin.append(0.0)
            investor_interest.append(0.0)
            servicing_cf.append(0.0)
            gfee_cf.append(0.0)
            investor_total_cf.append(0.0)
            balances.append(0.0)
            pool_factors.append(0.0)
            continue

        rem_term = wam_months - m + 1
        denom = 1.0 - (1.0 + wac_m) ** (-rem_term)
        pmt = current_balance * (wac_m / denom)
        gross_int = current_balance * wac_m
        sp = pmt - gross_int

        # PSA Prepayment
        base_cpr = 0.06 if m >= 30 else 0.002 * m
        cpr = base_cpr * (psa_speed / 100.0)
        smm = 1.0 - (1.0 - cpr) ** (1.0 / 12.0)
        pp = max(0.0, float((current_balance - sp) * smm))

        tot_prin = sp + pp
        net_int = current_balance * pt_m
        svc = current_balance * svc_m
        gfe = current_balance * gfe_m

        current_balance -= tot_prin
        current_balance = max(0.0, current_balance)

        sched_prin.append(sp)
        prepay_prin.append(pp)
        investor_interest.append(net_int)
        servicing_cf.append(svc)
        gfee_cf.append(gfe)
        investor_total_cf.append(net_int + tot_prin)
        balances.append(current_balance)
        pool_factors.append(current_balance / notional)

    return {
        "balances": balances,
        "sched_prin": sched_prin,
        "prepay_prin": prepay_prin,
        "investor_interest": investor_interest,
        "servicing_cf": servicing_cf,
        "gfee_cf": gfee_cf,
        "investor_total_cf": investor_total_cf,
        "pool_factors": pool_factors,
    }


def compute_pv_with_delay(cash_flows: list[float], annual_discount_rate: float, actual_delay_days: int) -> float:
    """Compute present value of monthly cash flows with settlement delay adjustment.

    Parameters
    ----------
    cash_flows : list[float]
        Monthly cash flows received by investor.
    annual_discount_rate : float
        Annualized continuous discount yield.
    actual_delay_days : int
        Actual payment delay days beyond month end (e.g. 24 days for Fannie Mae 55-day stated).

    Returns
    -------
    float
        Present value in dollars.
    """
    pv = 0.0
    for m, cf in enumerate(cash_flows, start=1):
        # Time to cash flow arrival in years: m/12 + actual_delay_days/365
        t_years = (m / 12.0) + (actual_delay_days / 365.0)
        pv += cf * float(np.exp(-annual_discount_rate * t_years))
    return float(pv)


def main() -> None:
    notional = 10_000_000.0
    wac = 0.060
    servicing = 0.0044
    g_fee = 0.0006
    wam = 360
    speed = 150.0

    res = generate_agency_waterfall(notional, wac, servicing, g_fee, wam, speed)

    print("Agency MBS Waterfall Simulation (150% PSA, 6.00% WAC, 5.50% Pass-Through):")
    print("Month | Beg Balance | Sched Prin | Prepayment | Net Interest | Total CF | Pool Factor")
    for m in [1, 12, 30, 60, 120, 240, 360]:
        idx = m - 1
        b_beg = res["balances"][idx]
        sp = res["sched_prin"][idx]
        pp = res["prepay_prin"][idx]
        ni = res["investor_interest"][idx]
        tot = res["investor_total_cf"][idx]
        pf = res["pool_factors"][idx + 1]
        print(f"{m:5d} | {b_beg:11.2f} | {sp:10.2f} | {pp:10.2f} | {ni:12.2f} | {tot:8.2f} | {pf:11.6f}")

    # Cash flow conservation test: total principal returned must equal initial notional
    total_sp = sum(res["sched_prin"])
    total_pp = sum(res["prepay_prin"])
    total_principal_paid = total_sp + total_pp
    print("\nCollateral Reconciliation:")
    print(f"Total Scheduled Principal:   {total_sp:12.2f}")
    print(f"Total Prepayment Principal:  {total_pp:12.2f}")
    print(f"Total Principal Returned:    {total_principal_paid:12.2f}")
    assert abs(total_principal_paid - notional) < 1.0

    # Delay days present value impact
    discount_rate = 0.055
    pv_no_delay = compute_pv_with_delay(res["investor_total_cf"], discount_rate, actual_delay_days=0)
    pv_fannie_delay = compute_pv_with_delay(res["investor_total_cf"], discount_rate, actual_delay_days=24)
    delay_cost = pv_no_delay - pv_fannie_delay
    delay_cost_bps = (delay_cost / notional) * 10000.0

    print("\nDelay-Day Economic Analysis (55-day stated / 24-day actual delay):")
    print(f"Present Value (0 delay days):  {pv_no_delay:12.2f}")
    print(f"Present Value (24 delay days): {pv_fannie_delay:12.2f}")
    print(f"Delay Drag ($):                {delay_cost:12.2f}")
    print(f"Delay Drag (bp of par):        {delay_cost_bps:12.2f}")
    assert delay_cost > 0.0


main()

Where the Canon Breaks

The classical agency waterfall assumes smooth servicing and frictionless agency pass-through mechanics, which fracture during stress:

  • Servicer Advance Liquidity Shortfalls: Non-bank mortgage servicers (e.g. Quicken Loans, PennyMac) are contractually obligated to advance scheduled principal and interest to MBS trustees even when borrowers become delinquent. During nationwide forbearance crises (such as the March 2020 COVID-19 shock), non-bank servicers faced severe liquidity crunches because they lacked direct access to Federal Reserve discount window facilities.
  • GSE Buyout Surges: When a delinquent mortgage reaches 120 or 180 days past due, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have the right or obligation to purchase the loan out of the MBS pool at par. This buyout is remitted to investors as an unscheduled principal prepayment. Consequently, a surge in mortgage defaults causes an involuntary prepayment spike at par, precisely when market yields are volatile.

Misconceptions

  • Agency pass-through securities expose investors to significant credit default risk.

    Agency guarantees from Ginnie Mae (full faith and credit of the U.S. government) and Fannie/Freddie (conservatorship backing) guarantee timely remittance of scheduled principal and interest. If an individual homeowner defaults, the guarantor pays off the loan balance at par. Thus, agency investors bear zero collateral default loss, converting credit risk entirely into involuntary prepayment risk.

  • The pool factor can increase if borrowers miss payments or if delinquent interest is capitalized.

    The pool factor \(F_t = B_t / B_0\) reflects remaining amortized collateral principal. Delinquencies do not add to pool principal because servicers must advance payments, and negative amortization is legally prohibited in standard conforming agency pools. The pool factor is strictly non-increasing over time.

  • A stated delay of 55 days means the investor receives no interest for 55 calendar days after purchasing the security.

    Mortgages accrue interest over the entire calendar month prior to settlement. The stated 55 days counts from the first day of that accrual month. Because normal bond settlements allow interest accrual through the end of the month (30–31 days), the true economic delay beyond standard month-end settlement is only 24 days for Fannie/Freddie and 14 days for Ginnie Mae.

Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

A Fannie Mae 30-year fixed-rate pool has a Weighted Average Coupon (WAC) of 6.25%. The servicer charges a servicing fee of 44 bps, and Fannie Mae charges a guarantee fee of 31 bps. Compute the net pass-through coupon \(c\) delivered to the certificate holder.
Solution

Applying Theorem 2.1, the net pass-through coupon is:

\[ c = \text{WAC} - s - g = 0.0625 - 0.0044 - 0.0031 = 0.0550 \quad (5.50\%) \]

The total fee deduction (servicing spread plus g-fee) is \(44 + 31 = 75\) basis points.

A pool with original balance \(B_0 = \$50,000,000\) has an outstanding balance of \(B_{24} = \$47,250,000\) at month 24. Calculate the pool factor \(F_{24}\). In month 25, scheduled principal is $52,000 and prepayments are $148,000. Compute the new pool factor \(F_{25}\).
Solution

The pool factor at month 24 is:

\[ F_{24} = \frac{47250000}{50000000} = 0.945000 \]

In month 25, total principal paid is \(TP_{25} = 52000 + 148000 = \$200,000\). The ending balance is \(B_{25} = 47250000 - 200000 = \$47,050,000\). The new pool factor is:

\[ F_{25} = \frac{47050000}{50000000} = 0.941000 \]
An investor holds $10,000,000 par of a 5.00% pass-through security. Compute the present value drag in dollars and basis points of par caused by a 24-day actual payment delay at an annual discount yield of 6.00%.
Solution

The discount factor adjustment for 24 days at 6.00% yield is:

\[ \text{Delay Factor} = \exp\left( -0.06 \times \frac{24}{365} \right) = \exp(-0.003945) \approx 0.996063 \]

The delay drag is \(1 - 0.996063 = 0.003937\) (39.37 bps). On a $10,000,000 face value position, this represents an economic drag of:

\[ 10000000 \times 0.003937 = \$39,370 \]

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that if a loan servicer advances scheduled interest and principal during a borrower delinquency of \(M\) months, the pool factor \(F_t\) and investor cash flows are mathematically identical to a performing loan until the loan is liquidated or bought out by the GSE.
Solution

Under the PSA agreement, the servicer acts as a credit intermediary. In any delinquent month \(t\), the borrower cash flow is 0. However, the servicer remits \(PMT_t = I_{\text{net}, t} + SP_t\) from its corporate credit facility to the MBS trust account. The MBS trustee distributes \(I_{\text{net}, t} = B_{t-1} (c/12)\) and \(SP_t\) to the certificate holder. The pool balance is updated to \(B_t = B_{t-1} - SP_t\). Because the cash flows entering the pool factor calculation and the investor account match the contractual amortization schedule exactly, the investor's realized cash flows and the recorded pool factor \(F_t = B_t / B_0\) are identical to those of a fully performing mortgage.

Prove that the Weighted Average Coupon (WAC) of a heterogeneous pool changes over time if individual loans prepay at different speeds, and show the condition under which WAC decreases monotonically with pool age.
Solution

Let pool WAC at month \(t\) be \(\text{WAC}_t = \frac{\sum_j B_{j, t} r_j}{\sum_j B_{j, t}}\). The change in WAC from \(t\) to \(t+1\) depends on relative balance decay \(\Delta B_{j, t} = -B_{j, t}(\text{SMM}_{j, t} + \text{amort})\). If higher-coupon loans face greater refinancing incentives (\(\frac{\partial \text{SMM}_j}{\partial r_j} > 0\)), higher-coupon loans prepay at a faster rate than lower-coupon loans. Consequently, the relative balance share \(w_{j, t} = B_{j, t} / B_t\) of high-rate loans decreases, while the share of low-rate loans increases. Taking the derivative of WAC with respect to time:

\[ \frac{d \text{WAC}_t}{dt} = \Cov_{w_t}\left(r_j, \frac{1}{B_{j, t}}\frac{dB_{j, t}}{dt}\right) \]

Since the rate of balance reduction is positively correlated with loan coupon \(r_j\), the covariance between \(r_j\) and the percentage growth rate of balance is strictly negative. Thus, \(\frac{d \text{WAC}_t}{dt} < 0\), proving that heterogeneous pool WAC drifts downward over time (termed coupon degradation).

Prove that in the To-Be-Announced (TBA) forward market, the seller's cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) delivery option always assigns the highest-prepayment, lowest-quality conforming pools to fulfill the trade.
Solution

In a TBA trade, the contract specifies delivery of any conforming agency pool with matching coupon and agency program at par price \(P_{\text{TBA}}\). Individual pools differ by collateral characteristics: average loan balance, geographic concentration, and servicer reputation. Fast-prepaying pools suffer from price compression and lower market value than slow-prepaying pools (which hold higher present value due to coupon protection). Let \(V(\text{Pool}_k)\) be the spot value of pool \(k\). The seller maximizes delivery profit by solving \(\max_k (P_{\text{TBA}} - V(\text{Pool}_k)) = P_{\text{TBA}} - \min_k V(\text{Pool}_k)\). The optimal delivery pool is the one with minimum market value, which corresponds to the pool with the highest prepayment vulnerability and worst geographical adverse selection. Hence, TBA contracts trade at the valuation of the cheapest-to-deliver pool.

Tier 3 · Empirical

In an empirical sample of Fannie Mae 30-year pools, an analyst compares a 45-day stated delay pool (GNMA I) against a 55-day stated delay pool (FNMA UMBS) trading at identical 5.50% pass-through coupons and market yields of 5.50%. Calculate the price difference in ticks (1/32 of 1% of par) attributable purely to the 10-day actual delay differential.
Solution

The difference in actual delay is \(24 - 14 = 10\) days. At a 5.50% discount yield, the present value penalty for 10 additional delay days is:

\[ \Delta PV \approx y \times \frac{10}{365} = 0.0550 \times \frac{10}{365} = 0.001507 \quad (15.07 \text{ bps of par}) \]

In the agency MBS market, 1 tick is \(1/32 = 0.03125\%\) of par (3.125 bps). Converting to ticks:

\[ \text{Price Difference} = \frac{15.07}{3.125} \approx 4.82 \text{ ticks} \]

GNMA I trades at approximately 4.8 ticks premium over Fannie Mae solely due to its 10-day faster cash settlement schedule.

During the March 2020 market turmoil, the CARES Act granted up to 12 months of mortgage forbearance to conforming borrowers. Suppose 8.0% of a $100M pool entered forbearance and ceased payments. If the non-bank servicer's total monthly scheduled P&I obligation was $600,000, calculate the monthly liquidity draw the servicer had to fund to advance payments to the MBS trust.
Solution

The servicer is contractually required to advance the scheduled principal and interest for delinquent or forborne borrowers. With an 8.0% forbearance rate across the $600,000 monthly obligation:

\[ \text{Monthly Advance} = \$600,000 \times 0.08 = \$48,000 \]

Over a full 12-month forbearance period, the cumulative capital drain on the servicer for this single pool equals \(12 \times 48000 = \$576,000\). Across a $50 billion servicing portfolio, this liquidity requirement scales to over $288 million, demonstrating the extreme working capital risk borne by non-bank servicers during economic shocks.

When mortgage defaults surged in 2009, Fannie Mae adopted a policy of buying out all loans delinquent for 120 days at par. If a $200M pool with CPR = 6.0% experienced a 5.0% annualized default buyout rate, compute the total effective annualized CPR realized by investors.
Solution

Because default buyouts are remitted to MBS certificate holders as par prepayments, voluntary and involuntary prepayments combine additively into total Single Monthly Mortality. Let \(\text{SMM}_{\text{vol}} = 1 - (1 - 0.06)^{1/12} \approx 0.00514\) and \(\text{SMM}_{\text{buyout}} = 1 - (1 - 0.05)^{1/12} \approx 0.00427\). Total monthly SMM is approximately \(\text{SMM}_{\text{tot}} \approx 0.00514 + 0.00427 = 0.00941\). The combined annualized CPR is:

\[ \text{CPR}_{\text{tot}} = 1 - (1 - 0.00941)^{12} \approx 1 - (0.99059)^{12} = 1 - 0.8929 = 0.1071 \quad (10.71\%) \]

GSE default buyouts nearly doubled the effective prepayment speed from 6.0% to 10.71%.

Tier 4 · Research

Analyze the creation of the Single Security (Uniform MBS / UMBS) by the FHFA in June 2019. How did harmonizing Freddie Mac's 45-day delay to Fannie Mae's 55-day delay eliminate the multi-billion-dollar liquidity discount previously suffered by Freddie Mac securities?
Solution

Prior to June 2019, Fannie Mae (FNMA) operated under a 55-day stated delay while Freddie Mac (FHLMC) operated under a 45-day stated delay. Because their settlement dates and delay mechanics differed, they traded in fragmented, separate TBA forward markets. Because Fannie Mae's market was roughly double the size of Freddie Mac's, Freddie Mac suffered from lower liquidity and consistently traded at a discount of 4 to 8 ticks. In 2019, FHFA created UMBS (Uniform MBS), moving Freddie Mac to the 55-day convention and merging both GSEs into a single, unified TBA contract. Investors received a one-time compensation payment for the 10-day delay change. The merger pooled liquidity, eliminated the Freddie Mac pricing penalty, and lowered borrowing costs for American homeowners.

Evaluate the systemic implications of the migration of mortgage servicing rights (MSRs) from regulated commercial banks to non-bank mortgage companies (shadow banks) following the Basel III capital rules.
Solution

Basel III significantly increased the capital deduction on mortgage servicing rights (MSRs) held by deposit-taking banks, limiting MSR assets to 10% of common equity Tier 1. Consequently, traditional commercial banks sold MSRs en masse to non-bank financial intermediaries (e.g. PennyMac, Rocket Mortgage, Mr. Cooper). Unlike insured commercial banks, non-banks rely on short-term warehouse credit lines and revolving credit facilities rather than stable insured deposits. In a macroeconomic crisis where delinquencies rise, non-bank servicers must advance billions in P&I to agency trusts while their collateral (MSR values) depreciates. This regulatory arbitrage transferred operational risk to institutions lacking lender-of-last-resort access, creating systemic vulnerabilities that required FHFA and Federal Reserve liquidity interventions.

How do specified pools (e.g. low loan balance, high LTV, New York geographic concentration) trade at a pay-up over TBA contracts, and what economic option value is embedded in this premium?
Solution

In the TBA market, sellers deliver the cheapest-to-deliver (fastest prepaying) collateral. Investors who wish to avoid prepayment risk purchase "specified pools" with identifiable characteristics that inhibit refinancing: (1) low loan balance (e.g. <$85k loans, where fixed closing fees make refinancing uneconomic); (2) high loan-to-value or credit-impaired borrower pools; and (3) high-cost tax jurisdictions (e.g. New York mortgage recording taxes). Investors pay a cash premium ("pay-up" in ticks) above TBA. This pay-up represents the market price of buying an insurance policy against prepayment call exercise, providing duration stability and eliminating negative convexity.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. If a pool's Weighted Average Coupon (WAC) is 6.00% (0.060), the servicing fee is 44 bps (0.0044), and the guarantee fee is 6 bps (0.0006), calculate the net pass-through coupon \(c\) delivered to investors as a decimal.
    \(c = \text{WAC} - s - g = 0.0600 - 0.0044 - 0.0006 = 0.0550\) (5.50%).
  2. For a Fannie Mae mortgage-backed security with a stated delay of 55 days, what is the actual interest-free economic delay beyond standard month-end settlement (in days)?
    Stated delay is 55 days from the beginning of the accrual month. Subtracting a 31-day month gives an actual delay of \(55 - 31 = 24\) days.
  3. What is the legal distinction between Ginnie Mae (GNMA) and the Government-Sponsored Enterprises (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac)?
    Options
    GNMA is a wholly owned government agency whose guarantee is backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. Treasury, whereas Fannie and Freddie are shareholder-owned GSEs operating under FHFA conservatorship.
  4. Why is the mortgage pool factor \(F_t = B_t / B_0\) mathematically guaranteed to be non-increasing over time?
    Options
    In conforming pass-throughs, principal payments \(TP_t = SP_t + PR_t \ge 0\) permanently reduce collateral balance \(B_t = B_{t-1} - TP_t\), making \(F_t\) monotonically non-increasing.
  5. In a To-Be-Announced (TBA) forward transaction, what is the "cheapest-to-deliver" (CTD) incentive of the seller?
    Options
    Since the buyer pays a fixed TBA forward price, the seller maximizes arbitrage profit by delivering eligible pools that possess the lowest secondary market value (the fastest prepaying pools).
  6. What happens to an agency pass-through certificate holder when an underlying borrower defaults and the loan is foreclosed?
    Options
    Agency guarantees eliminate credit loss: the agency buys out the defaulted mortgage at 100% of par, remitting principal to the pool as an unscheduled cash flow.
  7. Under an agency pass-through with initial collateral notional $10,000,000, what must cumulative total principal remitted over the security's life equal ($)?
    By Theorem 2.2, collateral principal conservation guarantees that \(\sum TP_t = B_0 = \$10,000,000\).
  8. Why do investors pay a "pay-up" premium for low-loan-balance (LLB) specified pools over TBA forward contracts?
    Options
    A $2,000 refinancing closing fee represents a massive 4.0% fee on a $50k loan, severely dampening refinancing incentive. Investors pay a premium for LLB pools because they exhibit burnout-like prepayment resistance.
