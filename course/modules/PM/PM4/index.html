Real Estate Finance & Mortgage-Backed Securities

Master the mathematical, institutional, and empirical foundations of real estate finance and structured debt. You will derive closed-form mortgage amortization recurrences, model endogenous borrower prepayments using proportional hazard frameworks, price agency pass-through securities by Monte Carlo simulation, calibrate Option-Adjusted Spreads (OAS), manage negative convexity hedging risks, evaluate REIT Net Asset Value structures, and analyze the microeconomic credit supply channels of the 2000s subprime mortgage expansion.

  • Prerequisites: FI1 (Bond Math & Curves), CF4 (Valuation), DV1 (Continuous-Time Arbitrage)
  • Hours: 48 hours lessons across 6 lessons + 32 hours project
  • Project: MBS pricer (Monte Carlo OAS & effective Greeks engine), autograder

Objectives

  1. Formulate exact closed-form amortization recurrences, Single Monthly Mortality (SMM), and empirical hazard rate prepayment models under interest rate incentive shifts.
  2. Map agency mortgage pass-through payment waterfalls, pool factor dynamics, servicing fees, and settlement delay adjustments across GSE structures.
  3. Characterize the negative convexity and price compression of mortgage debt relative to option-free fixed-income benchmarks.
  4. Calibrate Option-Adjusted Spread (OAS) and calculate effective duration and convexity via risk-neutral Monte Carlo simulation.
  5. Value commercial real estate cash flows using capitalization rates, and bridge public REIT balance sheets to Net Asset Value per share.
  6. Evaluate empirical evidence on subprime mortgage expansion, credit supply shocks, and foreclosure contagion during the 2000s housing crisis.

Prerequisites

This module builds upon fixed income mathematics, corporate valuation, and continuous-time derivatives:

  • Fixed Income Fundamentals (FI1): Clean and dirty pricing, bond price-yield convexity, Macaulay and modified duration (duration, convexity), spot zero curves (spot-rate), and forward rates.
  • Valuation (CF4): Discounted cash flow (DCF) mechanics, capitalization of cash flows, terminal value calculations (terminal-value), and equity value bridge accounting.
  • Continuous-Time Arbitrage Pricing (DV1): Black-Scholes-Merton framework (bsm-pde), risk-neutral pricing measure, and option sensitivity Greeks (option-greeks).

Placement diagnostic

Test your preparation across prerequisite fixed income concepts and the core mortgage modeling skills taught in Rungs 1 and 2. Score 80% or higher and answer all designated skill questions correctly to unlock direct placement into Rung 3.

  1. An agency pass-through with baseline price \(P_0 = 103.3226\) per 100 par is priced under parallel shifts \(\Delta y = 0.0025\) (25 bps). The pricer yields \(P_+ = 102.5213\) and \(P_- = 104.0800\). Compute the effective duration \(D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_- - P_+}{2 P_0 \Delta y}\) in years.
    Substituting into the central finite-difference formula gives \(D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{104.0800 - 102.5213}{2 \times 103.3226 \times 0.0025} = \frac{1.5587}{0.5166} \approx 3.0172\) years.
  2. Calculate the exact monthly Single Monthly Mortality (SMM) corresponding to a 6.00% annualized Conditional Prepayment Rate (CPR), unrounded to five decimal places.
    SMM is calculated via compound conversion: \(\text{SMM} = 1 - (1 - 0.06)^{1/12} = 1 - (0.94)^{0.08333} \approx 0.00514\).
  3. Why does an option-free bullet Treasury bond exhibit positive convexity (\(d^2P/dy^2 > 0\)) while an agency pass-through security exhibits negative convexity (\(d^2P/dy^2 < 0\)) in low interest rate regimes?
    1. Borrowers hold an embedded call option to prepay at par, which moves in-the-money as rates decline, capping MBS upside and compressing capital appreciation.
    2. Treasuries pay semiannual coupons while mortgage pools pay monthly coupons.
    3. Agency MBS are subject to default credit risk that rises when interest rates drop.
    4. Servicing fees increase nonlinearly as market yields decline.
    Pass-through investors are short an embedded American call option held by mortgagors. When market rates fall below the note rate, prepayment speeds accelerate, truncating price gains near par and creating local concavity.
  4. What distinguishes the Option-Adjusted Spread (OAS) from the static zero-volatility spread (Z-spread) of a mortgage-backed security?
    1. OAS accounts for interest rate volatility by averaging discounted cash flows across stochastic paths with state-dependent prepayments, whereas Z-spread discounts along a single deterministic curve.
    2. OAS includes a credit default risk premium while Z-spread assumes default-free collateral.
    3. OAS is computed exclusively on historical transaction data without an interest rate model.
    4. Z-spread adjusts for prepayment burnout while OAS assumes constant PSA speed.
    Z-spread ignores rate volatility and option exercise. OAS dynamically adjusts for the prepayment option by simulating cash flows along multiple interest rate paths under the risk-neutral measure.
  5. Under the Gordon Growth framework, if an institutional office property generates a forward 12-month Net Operating Income (NOI) of $10,000,000 and is valued at a capitalization rate of 5.00% with expected long-term NOI growth of 2.00%, what is the implied required total discount rate?
    1. 7.00%
    2. 3.00%
    3. 10.00%
    4. 5.00%
    In a constant-growth perpetuity, \(V_0 = \frac{\text{NOI}_1}{r - g}\), so the cap rate is \(R = \frac{\text{NOI}_1}{V_0} = r - g\). Thus, \(r = R + g = 5.00\% + 2.00\% = 7.00\%\).
  6. According to the empirical findings of Mian & Sufi (2009), what was the defining cross-sectional characteristic of mortgage credit expansion during the 2002–2005 housing boom?
    1. Mortgage credit growth was negatively or weakly correlated with income growth, expanding most aggressively in zip codes with high latent subprime shares.
    2. Credit expanded exclusively in high-income zip codes with strong real wage growth.
    3. Subprime lending was concentrated in metropolitan areas with zero house price appreciation.
    4. Mortgage denial rates rose across all demographics due to stricter bank lending standards.
    Mian & Sufi (2009) established that mortgage credit expansion was driven by a credit supply shock through securitization, decoupling credit growth from local economic fundamentals and income growth.
  7. A mortgage pool begins with balance \(B_0 = \$100,000,000\). Over its 360-month life, cumulative scheduled principal paid is \$24,988,400. In the absence of default losses, what must cumulative unscheduled prepayment principal plus scheduled principal sum to ($)?
    By collateral cash flow conservation, total principal returned to investors must exactly equal the initial balance of \$100,000,000.
  8. What structural requirement must a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) satisfy under Internal Revenue Code Section 856 to eliminate corporate income tax?
    1. Distribute at least 90% of taxable income to shareholders annually and invest at least 75% of assets in real estate.
    2. Maintain leverage below 25% of gross asset value and operate for at least 10 years.
    3. Hold only triple-net leased single-tenant retail properties.
    4. Pay dividends exclusively from capital gains rather than operating cash flows.
    Under IRC Section 856, REITs avoid entity-level corporate taxation by passing through at least 90% of annual taxable income as shareholder dividends and holding at least 75% of assets in real estate.

Introduces housing-boom-bust.

  3. Independent project

  4. Stretch

    Open-ended extensions: multi-factor term structure models, stochastic housing equity option default, and sequential-pay CMO tranche waterfalls.
