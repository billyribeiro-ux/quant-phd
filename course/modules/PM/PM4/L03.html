Negative Convexity & Price Compression of Mortgages

Because mortgagors hold an embedded call option to extinguish debt at par, mortgage pass-through prices compress into an asymptotic ceiling when discount rates drop, producing negative convexity and duration extension shocks that force multi-trillion-dollar dynamic hedging spirals across sovereign debt markets.

1. Structural Callable Bond Decomposition

A standard fixed-income bullet bond exhibits positive convexity everywhere: price appreciation accelerates as interest rates fall, while price depreciation decelerates as rates rise. In contrast, agency mortgage-backed securities violently invert this curvature. The economic root of this inversion lies in the borrower's legal entitlement to prepay at par without penalty.

Definition 3.1 · Negative Convexity

A fixed-income security exhibits negative convexity over a yield domain \(Y \subset \R_+\) if its price-yield function \(P(y)\) is locally concave, satisfying:

\[ \frac{\partial^2 P(y)}{\partial y^2} < 0 \quad \forall y \in Y \]

Equivalently, its effective convexity \(C_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{P(y)} \frac{\partial^2 P(y)}{\partial y^2}\) is strictly negative.

Theorem 3.1 · Structural Callable Bond Decomposition

An agency MBS pass-through security with market price \(P_{\text{MBS}}(y)\) can be decomposed into an option-free host amortizing bullet bond \(P_{\text{bullet}}(y)\) minus an American call option \(C_{\text{call}}(y; K = \text{Par})\) held by the homeowner pool:

\[ P_{\text{MBS}}(y) = P_{\text{bullet}}(y) - C_{\text{call}}(y; K = \text{Par}) \]
Proof

At any time \(t\), the mortgagor has the contractual obligation to service scheduled debt payments, generating cash flow stream \(\{CF_t^{\text{sched}}\}\). The present value of these contractual cash flows under zero prepayment is \(P_{\text{bullet}}(y) = \sum_{t=1}^N CF_t^{\text{sched}} e^{-y t/12}\). Concurrently, the mortgagor holds the continuous right to extinguish the debt balance \(B_t\) by paying par \(B_t\). The homeowner exercises this right whenever the market value of the remaining debt exceeds \(B_t\) plus refinancing frictions. The fair value of this prepayment right to the borrower is an American call option \(C_{\text{call}}(y; \text{Par})\) on the underlying loan collateral. By the law of one price, the value of the mortgage held by the investor is the value of the unencumbered cash flows minus the value of the borrower's call option:

\[ P_{\text{MBS}}(y) = P_{\text{bullet}}(y) - C_{\text{call}}(y; \text{Par}) \]

Because the investor is short the embedded call option, when interest rates drop (\(y \downarrow\)), the value of the call option surges (\(\frac{\partial C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y} < 0\)). As the option moves deep into the money, \(\frac{\partial^2 C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y^2} > \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{bullet}}}{\partial y^2}\), causing the second derivative \(\frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{MBS}}}{\partial y^2} = \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{bullet}}}{\partial y^2} - \frac{\partial^2 C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y^2}\) to become strictly negative.

2. Price Compression, Contraction, and Extension

Negative convexity alters the duration dynamics of mortgage securities across interest rate cycles. While option-free bonds display smooth, monotonic duration variations, mortgage duration contracts during rallies and extends during market sell-offs.

Theorem 3.2 · Duration-Yield Sensitivity Identity

The derivative of effective duration with respect to discount yield satisfies the exact identity:

\[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = D_{\text{eff}}^2 - C_{\text{eff}} \]

Consequently, whenever a security exhibits negative convexity (\(C_{\text{eff}} < 0\)), its duration strictly extends as yields rise:

\[ C_{\text{eff}} < 0 \implies \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} > 0 \]
Proof

Recall the definition of effective duration: \(D_{\text{eff}} = -\frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = -P^{-1} P_y\). Differentiating with respect to yield \(y\):

\[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( P^{-1} P_y \right) = - \left( -P^{-2} (P_y)^2 + P^{-1} P_{yy} \right) \]

Recognizing that \(P^{-1} P_y = -D_{\text{eff}}\) and \(P^{-1} P_{yy} = C_{\text{eff}}\):

\[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = \left( -P^{-1} P_y \right)^2 - P^{-1} P_{yy} = D_{\text{eff}}^2 - C_{\text{eff}} \]

Because \(D_{\text{eff}}^2 \ge 0\), if \(C_{\text{eff}} < 0\), then \(-C_{\text{eff}} > 0\), guaranteeing that \(\frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} > 0\).

Example 3.1 · Price Asymmetry in Rate Rallies vs Sell-Offs

Consider an agency pass-through trading at par ($100.00) with \(D_{\text{eff}} = 4.0\) years and \(C_{\text{eff}} = -100.0\). Using a second-order Taylor expansion \(\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2\):

  • 100 bps rate drop (\(\Delta y = -0.01\)): \(\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -4.0(-0.01) + \frac{1}{2}(-100.0)(-0.01)^2 = +0.0400 - 0.0050 = +3.50\%\) ($103.50).
  • 100 bps rate spike (\(\Delta y = +0.01\)): \(\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -4.0(+0.01) + \frac{1}{2}(-100.0)(+0.01)^2 = -0.0400 - 0.0050 = -4.50\%\) ($95.50).

The investor experiences 450 bps of loss on the sell-off but only 350 bps of gain on the rally. The negative convexity tax costs 50 bps in both directions.

3. Hedging Spirals and Convexity Feedback Loops

Institutional mortgage hedgers (GSE retained portfolios, bank treasury desks, mortgage servicers, and REITs) dynamically hedge duration risk using Treasury futures and interest rate swaps. To maintain duration neutrality for a mortgage position of notional \(N_{\text{mbs}}\), the required short Treasury hedge notional \(N_T\) is:

\[ N_T(y) = N_{\text{mbs}} \frac{D_{\text{mbs}}(y)}{D_T} \]

Because mortgage duration extends as yields rise (\(\frac{\partial D_{\text{mbs}}}{\partial y} > 0\)), differentiating the hedge ratio reveals the destabilizing feedback mechanism:

\[ \frac{\partial N_T}{\partial y} = \frac{N_{\text{mbs}}}{D_T} \frac{\partial D_{\text{mbs}}}{\partial y} > 0 \]

As interest rates rise, mortgage duration lengthens, forcing institutional hedgers to sell additional Treasuries or pay fixed on additional interest rate swaps. This forced selling into a falling market drives bond prices lower and yields higher, triggering further duration extension across the entire multi-trillion dollar market.

Lab 1 · Negative Convexity & Price Compression Simulation

Simulating MBS pass-through cash flows against an option-free bullet bond across yields from 3.5% to 9.5% will verify price compression near par, demonstrate negative effective convexity in the refinancing cusp (5.5%–6.5%), and show positive bullet convexity everywhere.

"""Lab PM4-L03-1: Negative Convexity and Price Compression of Mortgages.

SIMULATED: Demonstrates price compression, negative convexity, and duration extension
of an agency mortgage pass-through compared to an option-free bullet bond across a wide yield spectrum.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from collections.abc import Callable

import numpy as np


def price_bullet_bond(
    coupon_rate: float,
    yield_rate: float,
    maturity_years: float = 10.0,
    face_value: float = 100.0,
    frequency: int = 2,
) -> float:
    """Price an option-free bullet coupon bond."""
    n_periods = int(maturity_years * frequency)
    periodic_c = coupon_rate / frequency
    periodic_y = yield_rate / frequency
    pv_coupons = (face_value * periodic_c) * (1.0 - (1.0 + periodic_y) ** (-n_periods)) / periodic_y
    pv_face = face_value * ((1.0 + periodic_y) ** (-n_periods))
    return float(pv_coupons + pv_face)


def price_mbs_pass_through(
    gross_wac: float,
    net_coupon: float,
    market_yield: float,
    wam_months: int = 360,
    notional: float = 100.0,
) -> float:
    """Price a mortgage pass-through under state-dependent prepayment."""
    wac_m = gross_wac / 12.0
    net_m = net_coupon / 12.0
    disc_m = market_yield / 12.0

    current_balance = notional
    pv = 0.0

    for m in range(1, wam_months + 1):
        if current_balance <= 1e-4:
            break

        rem_term = wam_months - m + 1
        denom = 1.0 - (1.0 + wac_m) ** (-rem_term)
        pmt = current_balance * (wac_m / denom)
        sp = pmt - current_balance * wac_m

        # Refinancing spread incentive: WAC - market yield
        spread = gross_wac - market_yield
        # Prepayment S-curve: base 6% CPR plus surge up to 50% CPR when in the money
        cpr = 0.06 + 0.44 / (1.0 + float(np.exp(-45.0 * (spread - 0.005))))
        smm = 1.0 - (1.0 - cpr) ** (1.0 / 12.0)
        pp = max(0.0, (current_balance - sp) * smm)

        tot_prin = sp + pp
        net_int = current_balance * net_m
        tot_cf = net_int + tot_prin

        # Discount cash flow
        pv += tot_cf / ((1.0 + disc_m) ** m)
        current_balance -= tot_prin

    return float(pv)


def compute_duration_and_convexity(
    pricer_func: Callable[[float], float],
    base_yield: float,
    dy: float = 0.0020,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Compute numerical price, effective duration, and effective convexity via finite differences."""
    p0 = float(pricer_func(base_yield))
    p_up = float(pricer_func(base_yield + dy))
    p_down = float(pricer_func(base_yield - dy))

    eff_duration = (p_down - p_up) / (2.0 * p0 * dy)
    eff_convexity = (p_up + p_down - 2.0 * p0) / (p0 * (dy**2))
    return p0, eff_duration, eff_convexity


def main() -> None:
    wac = 0.065
    net_c = 0.060
    bullet_coupon = 0.060
    yields = [0.035, 0.045, 0.055, 0.065, 0.075, 0.085, 0.095]

    print("MBS Pass-Through vs Option-Free Bullet Bond Across Yield Levels:")
    print("Yield (%) | MBS Price | Bullet Price | MBS Eff Dur | Bullet Dur | MBS Convexity | Bullet Cvx")

    mbs_convexities: list[float] = []

    for y in yields:
        p_mbs, d_mbs, c_mbs = compute_duration_and_convexity(
            lambda yld: price_mbs_pass_through(wac, net_c, yld), y, dy=0.0010
        )
        p_bul, d_bul, c_bul = compute_duration_and_convexity(
            lambda yld: price_bullet_bond(bullet_coupon, yld, maturity_years=10.0), y, dy=0.0010
        )
        mbs_convexities.append(c_mbs)
        row = (
            f"{y * 100:9.2f} | {p_mbs:9.2f} | {p_bul:12.2f} | "
            f"{d_mbs:11.2f} | {d_bul:10.2f} | {c_mbs:13.2f} | {c_bul:10.2f}"
        )
        print(row)

    # Validate core theorems of negative convexity
    # 1. MBS price is compressed (capped) near par relative to bullet when rates collapse to 3.5%
    p_mbs_low, _, _ = compute_duration_and_convexity(
        lambda yld: price_mbs_pass_through(wac, net_c, yld), 0.035, dy=0.0010
    )
    p_bul_low, _, _ = compute_duration_and_convexity(
        lambda yld: price_bullet_bond(bullet_coupon, yld, maturity_years=10.0), 0.035, dy=0.0010
    )
    print("\nPrice Compression at 3.50% Yield:")
    print(f"MBS Price:    {p_mbs_low:6.2f}")
    print(f"Bullet Price: {p_bul_low:6.2f}")
    assert p_mbs_low < p_bul_low

    # 2. MBS exhibits negative convexity in the refinancing zone (e.g. 5.5% - 6.5%)
    min_mbs_cvx = min(mbs_convexities)
    print(f"\nMinimum MBS Effective Convexity: {min_mbs_cvx:6.2f}")
    assert min_mbs_cvx < 0.0

    # 3. Bullet bond has positive convexity everywhere
    for y in yields:
        _, _, c_bul = compute_duration_and_convexity(
            lambda yld: price_bullet_bond(bullet_coupon, yld, maturity_years=10.0), y, dy=0.0010
        )
        assert c_bul > 0.0

Where the Canon Breaks

Textbook models of callable bonds assume frictionless continuous exercise boundaries, but empirical agency mortgage markets exhibit substantial structural departures:

  • Burnout Hysteresis Flips Convexity Signs: In seasoned pools that have survived multiple refinancing waves, the remaining collateral consists of inert, low-balance, or credit-impaired borrowers. The embedded call option becomes economically dormant, restoring positive convexity even during strong interest rate rallies (Stanton 1995).
  • Primary-Secondary Spread Bottlenecks: When secondary Treasury yields collapse rapidly, mortgage originator capacity limits and compliance processing queues cause retail mortgage rates to remain elevated. This temporary spread blowout delays borrower refinancing, insulating MBS prices from immediate negative convexity shocks.
  • Tranche Stripping and Negative Duration: When pass-through collateral is stripped into Interest-Only (IO) and Principal-Only (PO) securities, the PO strip captures massive positive convexity, while the IO strip displays negative duration across most market yields, falling in price when interest rates drop.

Misconceptions

  • Negative convexity implies that mortgage prices fall when benchmark interest rates decrease.

    Pass-through duration remains strictly positive; thus, falling interest rates increase mortgage prices (\(\partial P / \partial y \le 0\)). Negative convexity means the rate of price appreciation slows down (concave curvature), compressing upside gains relative to an option-free bullet bond.

  • Mortgage pass-through securities exhibit negative convexity across all interest rate environments.

    Negative convexity is concentrated in the refinancing cusp. When yields are extremely high relative to coupon, prepayments hit their baseline demographic floor and the bond displays positive convexity. When yields collapse to near zero, prepayment speed hits its physical ceiling, and the bond behaves like a short-term cash equivalent with positive convexity.

  • A duration-matched hedge using 10-year Treasury futures completely immunizes an MBS portfolio against interest rate shocks.

    Because MBS convexity is negative while Treasury convexity is positive, a duration hedge is valid only for infinitesimal yield moves. A significant rate increase extends MBS duration and shortens Treasury hedge duration, leaving the portfolio net long duration and suffering unhedged capital losses.

Problem Set

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An agency MBS pass-through trades at base price \(P_0 = 101.50\). Under yield shifts of \(\Delta y = 25\text{ bps} = 0.0025\), the up-shifted price is \(P_+ = 100.20\) and the down-shifted price is \(P_- = 102.30\). Calculate the effective duration \(D_{\text{eff}}\) and effective convexity \(C_{\text{eff}}\).
Solution

Effective duration is given by the symmetric difference quotient: \[ D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_- - P_+}{2 P_0 \Delta y} = \frac{102.30 - 100.20}{2 \times 101.50 \times 0.0025} = \frac{2.10}{0.5075} \approx 4.138\text{ years} \] Effective convexity is computed from the second central difference: \[ C_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_+ + P_- - 2P_0}{P_0 (\Delta y)^2} = \frac{100.20 + 102.30 - 2(101.50)}{101.50 \times (0.0025)^2} = \frac{-0.50}{101.50 \times 0.00000625} \approx -788.18 \] The negative sign on convexity confirms that price upside is heavily compressed relative to downside risk.

A bullet bond has duration \(D_B = 6.0\) and convexity \(C_B = +50.0\). An MBS has duration \(D_M = 4.0\) and convexity \(C_M = -120.0\). Using second-order Taylor expansion, calculate the percentage price change for both bonds when interest rates decline by 100 bps (\(\Delta y = -0.01\)).
Solution

Applying the second-order Taylor approximation \(\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2\): For the bullet bond: \[ \frac{\Delta P_B}{P_B} \approx -6.0(-0.01) + \frac{1}{2}(50.0)(-0.01)^2 = +0.0600 + 0.0025 = +6.25\% \] For the MBS: \[ \frac{\Delta P_M}{P_M} \approx -4.0(-0.01) + \frac{1}{2}(-120.0)(-0.01)^2 = +0.0400 - 0.0060 = +3.40\% \] Negative convexity reduces MBS capital appreciation by 60 basis points from its linear duration estimate, illustrating upside price compression.

A mortgage portfolio with balance $500 million experiences a rate decline that causes its effective duration to contract from 5.2 years to 2.8 years. If 10-year Treasury futures have duration \(D_T = 7.0\) years, calculate the change in Treasury futures hedge notional required to maintain duration neutrality.
Solution

Initial dollar duration is \(\text{DD}_{\text{initial}} = \$500\text{M} \times 5.2 = \$2,600\text{M-years}\), requiring short Treasury notional \(N_{T, 1} = \frac{\$2,600\text{M}}{7.0} = \$371.43\text{M}\). Following the rate decline, new dollar duration is \(\text{DD}_{\text{new}} = \$500\text{M} \times 2.8 = \$1,400\text{M-years}\), requiring short Treasury notional \(N_{T, 2} = \frac{\$1,400\text{M}}{7.0} = \$200.00\text{M}\). The portfolio manager must buy back \(\$371.43\text{M} - \$200.00\text{M} = \$171.43\text{M}\) of Treasury futures. This buying into a bond market rally exemplifies dynamic hedging feedback.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that if an MBS price satisfies \(P_{\text{MBS}}(y) = P_{\text{bullet}}(y) - C_{\text{call}}(y)\), and the embedded option satisfies \(\frac{\partial^2 C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y^2} > \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{bullet}}}{\partial y^2} > 0\), the pass-through security must exhibit strictly negative convexity.
Solution

By definition, effective convexity of the MBS is: \[ C_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{P_{\text{MBS}}} \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{MBS}}}{\partial y^2} \] Differentiating the callable bond decomposition twice with respect to discount yield \(y\): \[ \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{MBS}}}{\partial y^2} = \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{bullet}}}{\partial y^2} - \frac{\partial^2 C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y^2} \] Substituting the given hypothesis \(\frac{\partial^2 C_{\text{call}}}{\partial y^2} > \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{bullet}}}{\partial y^2}\): \[ \frac{\partial^2 P_{\text{MBS}}}{\partial y^2} < 0 \] Since market price is strictly positive (\(P_{\text{MBS}} > 0\)), dividing by \(P_{\text{MBS}}\) maintains the strict inequality \(C_{\text{eff}} < 0\), proving strictly negative convexity.

Prove that the yield derivative of effective duration satisfies \(\frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = D_{\text{eff}}^2 - C_{\text{eff}}\).
Solution

Effective duration is defined as \(D_{\text{eff}} = -\frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\). Applying the product rule to differentiate \(D_{\text{eff}}\) with respect to \(y\): \[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( P^{-1} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) = - \left[ (-P^{-2}) \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right)^2 + P^{-1} \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial y^2} \right] \] Distributing the negative sign: \[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = \left( \frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right)^2 - \frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial y^2} \] Recognizing that \(\frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = -D_{\text{eff}}\) and \(\frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial y^2} = C_{\text{eff}}\), this yields: \[ \frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = (-D_{\text{eff}})^2 - C_{\text{eff}} = D_{\text{eff}}^2 - C_{\text{eff}} \] This completes the analytical proof.

Let a mortgage pool be stripped into a Principal-Only (PO) tranche and an Interest-Only (IO) tranche such that \(P_{\text{MBS}} = P_{\text{PO}} + P_{\text{IO}}\). Prove that if \(D_{\text{MBS}} > 0\) and \(D_{\text{IO}} < 0\), then \(D_{\text{PO}} > D_{\text{MBS}}\).
Solution

By linearity of the price derivative: \[ \frac{\partial P_{\text{MBS}}}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial P_{\text{PO}}}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial P_{\text{IO}}}{\partial y} \] Multiplying both sides by \(-\frac{1}{P_{\text{MBS}}}\) and expressing in terms of value weights \(w_{\text{PO}} = \frac{P_{\text{PO}}}{P_{\text{MBS}}}\) and \(w_{\text{IO}} = \frac{P_{\text{IO}}}{P_{\text{MBS}}}\) where \(w_{\text{PO}} + w_{\text{IO}} = 1\): \[ D_{\text{MBS}} = w_{\text{PO}} D_{\text{PO}} + w_{\text{IO}} D_{\text{IO}} \implies w_{\text{PO}} D_{\text{PO}} = D_{\text{MBS}} - w_{\text{IO}} D_{\text{IO}} \] Since \(D_{\text{IO}} < 0\) and \(w_{\text{IO}} > 0\), the term \(-w_{\text{IO}} D_{\text{IO}} > 0\). Therefore: \[ w_{\text{PO}} D_{\text{PO}} > D_{\text{MBS}} \] Dividing by \(w_{\text{PO}} \in (0, 1)\) strictly amplifies the inequality: \[ D_{\text{PO}} > \frac{D_{\text{MBS}}}{w_{\text{PO}}} > D_{\text{MBS}} \] Thus, a Principal-Only strip must possess a duration strictly exceeding that of the underlying pass-through.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Schwartz & Torous (1989) estimate a proportional hazard model where prepayment intensity increases from \(\lambda_1 = 0.05\) at par to \(\lambda_2 = 0.40\) when refinancing incentive reaches +200 bps. On a $100M pool with $120,000 scheduled monthly principal, compute the monthly unscheduled prepayments under both states.
Solution

Convert the continuous hazard intensities to Single Monthly Mortality (\(\text{SMM}\)): \[ \text{SMM} = 1 - e^{-\lambda / 12} \] For \(\lambda_1 = 0.05\): \(\text{SMM}_1 = 1 - e^{-0.05 / 12} = 1 - e^{-0.004167} \approx 0.004158\). For \(\lambda_2 = 0.40\): \(\text{SMM}_2 = 1 - e^{-0.40 / 12} = 1 - e^{-0.033333} \approx 0.032788\). The balance remaining after scheduled principal is \(B_{\text{rem}} = \$100,000,000 - \$120,000 = \$99,880,000\). In State 1 (par): \(\text{Unscheduled}_1 = \$99,880,000 \times 0.004158 \approx \$415,301\). In State 2 (+200 bps): \(\text{Unscheduled}_2 = \$99,880,000 \times 0.032788 \approx \$3,274,865\). Prepayments surge by \(7.88\times\), returning over $3.27M in capital during a single month into depressed reinvestment yields.

During the summer 2003 rate spike, aggregate agency MBS duration extended from 1.8 years to 4.5 years across an $8 trillion market. Compute the total increase in dollar duration and the required short Treasury futures notional (\(D_T = 7.0\)) to rebalance.
Solution

The duration extension across the market was \(\Delta D = 4.5 - 1.8 = 2.7\text{ years}\). The resulting increase in total dollar duration is: \[ \Delta \text{Dollar Duration} = \$8,000,000,000,000 \times 2.7 = \$21.6\text{ trillion-years} \] To neutralize this interest rate exposure using 10-year Treasury futures (\(D_T = 7.0\)), institutional hedgers were forced to short: \[ N_T = \frac{\$21.6\text{ trillion-years}}{7.0\text{ years}} \approx \$3.086\text{ trillion} \] The market requirement to absorb over $3 trillion in 10-year Treasury short sales accelerated the bond market sell-off.

A pass-through bond currently trades at $101.00 at a 5.0% yield. At 4.0% yield it trades at $103.20, and at 6.0% yield it trades at $96.50. Calculate the downside-to-upside asymmetry ratio and explain why it indicates negative convexity.
Solution

During the 100 bps rally (\(y\) drops to 4.0%): \[ \Delta P_{\text{up}} = 103.20 - 101.00 = +\$2.20 \] During the 100 bps sell-off (\(y\) rises to 6.0%): \[ \Delta P_{\text{down}} = 96.50 - 101.00 = -\$4.50 \] The asymmetry ratio of losses to gains is: \[ \text{Asymmetry Ratio} = \frac{|\Delta P_{\text{down}}|}{\Delta P_{\text{up}}} = \frac{4.50}{2.20} \approx 2.045 \] Because losses during a rate increase are more than double the gains during an identical rate decrease, the bond displays severe price concavity (negative convexity).

Tier 4 · Research

Formulate Stanton's (1995) rational prepayment decision rule where mortgagors face fixed closing costs \(F\) and unobserved non-monetary friction costs \(\xi\). Explain why pool-level prepayment exhibits a smooth S-curve rather than an instantaneous step function.
Solution

In Stanton (1995), let \(V(r_t, t)\) denote the market value of the current mortgage at short rate \(r_t\), and \(B_t\) denote the outstanding balance. The net present value of refinancing is \(\text{NPV} = V(r_t, t) - [B_t + F + \xi]\). A rational mortgagor exercises the call option when \(V(r_t, t) - B_t \ge F + \xi\). Because the non-monetary transaction friction \(\xi\) is heterogeneous across borrowers with continuous distribution \(\Phi(\xi)\), the fraction of the pool refinancing is \(\Prob(\xi \le V(r_t, t) - B_t - F)\). Combined with Poisson decision review arrivals \(\lambda\), this smooths the aggregate prepayment response into an empirical S-curve across interest rate spreads.

Analyze the macroeconomic impact of the Federal Reserve's purchase of $1.25 trillion in agency MBS under Quantitative Easing (QE1) on aggregate mortgage convexity risk in the private financial sector.
Solution

Under private institutional ownership, mortgage negative convexity generates dynamic duration hedging flows in Treasuries and swaps, amplifying rate volatility. Because the Federal Reserve is an unhedged buy-and-hold sovereign balance sheet, absorbing $1.25 trillion of agency MBS permanently extracted negative convexity risk from the private market. This eliminated billions of dollars in dynamic duration rebalancing needs, suppressing interest rate volatility and narrowing the primary-secondary mortgage spread.

Explain how Mortgage Servicing Rights (MSR) act as a natural structural hedge against agency pass-through negative convexity, and derive the portfolio duration formula for a mortgage institution holding both assets.
Solution

Mortgage Servicing Rights entitle the holder to retain a periodic servicing fee (typically 25 to 50 bps) on remaining balance. When interest rates fall, prepayment waves rapidly extinguish the underlying loans, destroying servicing cash flows. Consequently, MSRs possess negative duration (\(D_{\text{MSR}} < 0\)) and positive convexity during rate rallies. In a combined portfolio with value weights \(w_{\text{MBS}}\) and \(w_{\text{MSR}}\), total duration is \(D_P = w_{\text{MBS}} D_{\text{MBS}} + w_{\text{MSR}} D_{\text{MSR}}\). Because \(D_{\text{MSR}} < 0\), long MSR positions offset the positive duration of pass-throughs while their rapid cash flow contraction counters the negative convexity of the MBS.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. In the callable bond decomposition \(P_{\text{MBS}} = P_{\text{bullet}} - C_{\text{call}}\), who is the holder of the embedded call option?

    Options
    Homeowners hold the unpenalized statutory right to prepay their mortgage at par, which is structurally equivalent to a short call option for the MBS investor.

  2. What happens to the effective duration of an agency pass-through when benchmark interest rates drop substantially?

    Options
    Surging prepayments return principal rapidly, shortening the weighted-average life of cash flows and contracting duration.

  3. What is the economic phenomenon termed "price compression" in MBS markets?

    Options
    Because mortgagors prepay at par, investors refuse to pay a significant premium over par, compressing prices into an asymptotic ceiling.

  4. Why does dynamic duration hedging of MBS generate destabilizing feedback spirals in bond markets?

    Options
    Duration extension increases required short hedges, forcing institutions to sell Treasuries into falling markets, driving rates higher.

  5. Under what interest rate conditions does an agency MBS pass-through exhibit positive convexity?

    Options
    When market yields are far above the note rate, the borrower call option is deep out-of-the-money and the security behaves like an option-free amortizing bond.

  6. Which stripped mortgage derivative tranche exhibits negative effective duration across most yield environments?

    Options
    An IO strip receives only interest; falling rates trigger prepayments that extinguish the balance, destroying future interest and causing IO prices to collapse.
  7. If an MBS has effective duration \(D_{\text{eff}} = 4.0\) and effective convexity \(C_{\text{eff}} = -150.0\), compute the analytical derivative \(\frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y}\).
    \(\frac{\partial D_{\text{eff}}}{\partial y} = D_{\text{eff}}^2 - C_{\text{eff}} = 4.0^2 - (-150.0) = 16.0 + 150.0 = 166.0\).
  8. A pass-through trades at $101.50. Under \(\Delta y = 0.0025\), \(P_+ = 100.20\) and \(P_- = 102.30\). Compute its effective duration to three decimal places.
    \(D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{102.30 - 100.20}{2 \times 101.50 \times 0.0025} = \frac{2.10}{0.5075} \approx 4.138\) years.
