Macro Liquidity Regimes & Bitcoin Beta Switching

In its first decade, cryptocurrency traded largely decoupled from the global macro economy, exhibiting near-zero beta to equity indices, real interest rates, and foreign exchange. Following the monetary expansions of 2020 and institutional capital entry, digital assets experienced a permanent structural shift into a macro liquidity proxy. Today, Bitcoin and high-beta altcoins exhibit sharp regime-switching behavior: in liquidity-expansion regimes, they behave as supercharged growth equities; during monetary tightening and real-yield surges, correlations spike and liquidity evaporates. Quantitative macro traders must model these regime transitions to prevent catastrophic diversification failure during liquidity contractions.

1The 2020 Structural Break: From Idiosyncratic Asset to Macro Proxy

Between 2010 and 2019, empirical asset pricing literature consistently found that Bitcoin returns were disconnected from standard macroeconomic risk factors. In their comprehensive study, Liu & Tsyvinski (2021) demonstrated that daily and weekly cryptocurrency returns showed zero statistically significant exposure to market equity factors, currencies, commodity indices, or macroeconomic news announcements. Digital asset returns were driven overwhelmingly by internal crypto-market momentum and user adoption metrics.

This dynamic underwent a fundamental structural break in March 2020. The coordinated monetary policy response to the COVID-19 pandemic—unprecedented expansion of the Federal Reserve balance sheet (quantitative easing), rapid M2 growth, and zero interest rate policy—coincided with an influx of institutional capital into crypto markets.

Definition 1.1 · Crypto Correlation Regimes
Crypto correlation regimes denote discrete, persistent macroeconomic states characterizing the statistical dependence between digital asset returns \(r_{\text{crypto}, t}\) and traditional risk factors \(f_{\text{macro}, t}\) (such as tech equity returns \(r_{\text{equity}, t}\) and real interest rate shocks \(\Delta y_{\text{real}, t}\)). Formally, the return generation process obeys a state-dependent linear factor model: \[\begin{aligned} r_{\text{crypto}, t} &= \alpha_{S_t} + \beta_{S_t}^{\top} f_{\text{macro}, t} + \sigma_{S_t} \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1), \end{aligned}\] where \(S_t \in \{0, 1\}\) denotes the latent regime at date \(t\). In Regime 0 (Idiosyncratic Era), \(\beta_0 \approx \mathbf{0}\) and asset returns are dominated by idiosyncratic variance \(\sigma_0^2\). In Regime 1 (Macro Liquidity Era), \(\beta_1 \gg \mathbf{0}\), exhibiting high sensitivity to tech equities and severe negative sensitivity to real interest rate tightening.

As documented in modern empirical research, the unconditional correlation between Bitcoin and the Nasdaq 100 shifted from \(\rho \approx 0.05\) (pre-2020) to \(\rho \in [0.50, 0.75]\) during the 2020–2023 macro liquidity cycle.

2Monetary Policy Channels: Real Yields and Global Fiat Liquidity

Why did digital assets become hyper-sensitive to macro conditions? The economic mechanism operates through two primary transmission channels:

  1. The Discount Rate Channel (Real Yields): As non-yielding, perpetual growth assets, cryptocurrencies possess extremely long effective duration. In a discounted cash flow or utility-based pricing framework, the discount rate applied to distant adoption value is tied to the real risk-free rate \(y_{\text{real}} = y_{\text{nominal}} - \pi^e\). When 10-year Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) real yields rise (e.g. from \(-1.00\%\) in 2021 to \(+2.50\%\) in 2023), the present value of speculative growth assets experiences steep contraction.
  2. Global Fiat Liquidity (Central Bank Balance Sheets & M2): Cryptocurrencies serve as a marginal liquidity sponge. When global central bank balance sheets expand, excess fiat reserves flow out the risk curve into speculative, high-volatility assets. When central banks initiate Quantitative Tightening (QT) and drain reserves, institutional market participants de-risk by liquidating their most volatile, non-cash-flow holdings.

Theorem 2.1 · Real Rate Elasticity of Digital Asset Valuations
Let \(P_t = \E_t \left[ \sum_{s=1}^{\infty} \frac{U_s}{(1 + r_f + \eta)^s} \right]\) represent the fundamental valuation of a digital asset providing speculative consumption utility \(U_s\), where \(r_f\) is the real risk-free rate and \(\eta\) is the crypto risk premium. The percentage price sensitivity to a shock in the real rate \(\Delta r_f\) is given by the effective modified duration \(\mathcal{D}^*\): \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\Delta P_t}{P_t} &\approx -\mathcal{D}^* \Delta r_f. \end{aligned}\] For perpetual growth assets with zero interim cash flows and adoption cash-flow horizons \(T \gg 15\) years, \(\mathcal{D}^* > 20\), implying that a \(+100\) bps increase in real yields induces an expected contraction exceeding \(-20\%\) in equilibrium price.

Proof
Consider a perpetual cash flow stream \(C_s = C_0 (1 + g)^s\) discounted at rate \(k = r_f + \eta\). By the Gordon growth formula, \(P = \frac{C_1}{k - g} = \frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{r_f + \eta - g}\). Taking the derivative with respect to \(r_f\): \[\begin{aligned} \frac{dP}{dr_f} &= -\frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{(r_f + \eta - g)^2} = -\frac{P}{r_f + \eta - g}. \end{aligned}\] Dividing both sides by \(P\) yields the modified duration: \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{D}^* &= -\frac{1}{P} \frac{dP}{dr_f} = \frac{1}{r_f + \eta - g}. \end{aligned}\] When the net capitalization discount rate \(r_f + \eta - g\) is small (e.g. \(4\%\) to \(5\%\)), \(\mathcal{D}^* = \frac{1}{0.04} = 25\). Thus, a 1% (100 bps) upward shift in \(r_f\) causes a 25% drop in asset price, proving the high-duration vulnerability of digital assets.

3Rolling OLS Beta and Dynamic Factor Exposures

To quantify how digital asset macro exposures evolve over time, we estimate rolling-window regressions of crypto asset returns on benchmark macro factors. For a fixed estimation window of \(W\) trading days (e.g. \(W = 60\) days): \[\begin{aligned} \hat{\beta}_{t, \text{Nasdaq}} &= \frac{\sum_{\tau = t - W + 1}^{t} (r_{\text{crypto}, \tau} - \bar{r}_{\text{crypto}}) (r_{\text{nasdaq}, \tau} - \bar{r}_{\text{nasdaq}})}{\sum_{\tau = t - W + 1}^{t} (r_{\text{nasdaq}, \tau} - \bar{r}_{\text{nasdaq}})^2}, \end{aligned}\] and rolling Pearson correlation: \[\begin{aligned} \hat{\rho}_{t} &= \frac{\hat{\Cov}_{t}(r_{\text{crypto}}, r_{\text{nasdaq}})}{\hat{\sigma}_{t, \text{crypto}} \hat{\sigma}_{t, \text{nasdaq}}}. \end{aligned}\]

Empirical analysis reveals that during risk-off equity drawdowns triggered by rate shocks (e.g. 2022 Fed tightening), \(\hat{\beta}_{t, \text{Nasdaq}}\) frequently exceeded 1.50 to 2.00, demonstrating that crypto assets acted not as an uncorrelated hedge, but as leveraged tech beta.

4Markov Regime-Switching Models of Crypto Beta

While rolling OLS windows smooth over structural shifts, the underlying data-generating process is more accurately characterized by discrete state transitions. We formalize this using the Hamilton (1989) Markov-switching autoregression framework.

Definition 4.1 · Two-State Markov Switching Regression
Let \(S_t \in \{0, 1\}\) follow a first-order, two-state ergodic Markov chain with transition probability matrix \(\mathbf{P}\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathbf{P} &= \begin{pmatrix} p_{00} & p_{01} \\ p_{10} & p_{11} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} p & 1-p \\ 1-q & q \end{pmatrix}, \end{aligned}\] where \(p_{ij} = \Prob(S_t = j \mid S_{t-1} = i)\). Conditional on regime \(S_t\), crypto return \(y_t\) is generated by: \[\begin{aligned} y_t &= \alpha_{S_t} + \beta_{S_t} x_t + \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \mid S_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{S_t}^2), \end{aligned}\] where \(x_t\) is the equity market return vector.

The unconditional expected duration of regime \(j\) is: \[\begin{aligned} \E[D_j] &= \frac{1}{1 - p_{jj}}. \end{aligned}\] For instance, if \(p_{11} = 0.98\) (the Macro Liquidity regime exhibits a 98% daily continuation probability), the expected duration of the regime is \(\frac{1}{1 - 0.98} = 50\) trading days (~2.5 months).

The regime filtered probabilities \(\xi_{t \mid t} = \Prob(S_t = j \mid \mathcal{F}_t)\) and smoothed probabilities \(\xi_{t \mid T} = \Prob(S_t = j \mid \mathcal{F}_T)\) are obtained using Hamilton's recursive filter and Kim's backward smoothing algorithm.

5Computational Lab: Estimating Regime Shifts and Rolling Betas

The following laboratory script simulates daily digital asset returns across two distinct macroeconomic regimes (Regime 0: early idiosyncratic asset; Regime 1: macro liquidity asset) and quantifies rolling beta dynamics and interest rate sensitivities.

Lab 1 · Macro liquidity regimes and Bitcoin beta switching

Simulating digital asset returns across two macroeconomic regimes will verify that: (1) equity beta and correlation surge substantially from the early era to the macro liquidity era; (2) rolling 60-day beta shifts upward by more than 0.80 following the monetary regime break.

"""Lab X4-L03-1: Macro Liquidity Regimes & Bitcoin Beta Switching.

SIMULATED daily digital asset returns, Nasdaq 100 tech equity returns, and real interest rate shocks.
Estimates rolling correlations, beta to equities, and a 2-state regime classification
identifying the structural shift from early idiosyncratic asset to high-beta macro liquidity asset.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def rolling_ols_beta(y: list[float], x: list[float], window: int) -> list[float]:
    """Compute rolling window beta of y on x."""
    n = len(y)
    betas: list[float] = []
    for t in range(window, n + 1):
        y_win = y[t - window : t]
        x_win = x[t - window : t]
        m_x = sum(x_win) / window
        m_y = sum(y_win) / window
        cov = sum((xi - m_x) * (yi - m_y) for xi, yi in zip(x_win, y_win, strict=True))
        var = sum((xi - m_x) ** 2 for xi in x_win)
        b = cov / var if var > 1e-12 else 0.0
        betas.append(b)
    return betas


def rolling_correlation(y: list[float], x: list[float], window: int) -> list[float]:
    """Compute rolling Pearson correlation between y and x."""
    n = len(y)
    corrs: list[float] = []
    for t in range(window, n + 1):
        y_win = y[t - window : t]
        x_win = x[t - window : t]
        m_x = sum(x_win) / window
        m_y = sum(y_win) / window
        cov = sum((xi - m_x) * (yi - m_y) for xi, yi in zip(x_win, y_win, strict=True))
        var_x = sum((xi - m_x) ** 2 for xi in x_win)
        var_y = sum((yi - m_y) ** 2 for yi in y_win)
        denom = math.sqrt(var_x * var_y)
        corr = cov / denom if denom > 1e-12 else 0.0
        corrs.append(corr)
    return corrs


def main() -> None:
    print("Lab X4-L03-1 | Macro Liquidity Regimes & Digital Asset Beta")
    print("-----------------------------------------------------------")

    rng = random.Random(2024)
    n_days = 800  # 400 days in Regime 0, 400 days in Regime 1

    # Macro factors: Tech equity return (nasdaq) and real rate shock (delta_real_yield)
    nasdaq_returns: list[float] = []
    real_rate_shocks: list[float] = []
    btc_returns: list[float] = []

    regime_labels: list[int] = []

    for t in range(n_days):
        ret_nasdaq = rng.gauss(0.0004, 0.012)
        shock_rate = rng.gauss(0.0000, 0.0005)  # 5 bps daily shock
        nasdaq_returns.append(ret_nasdaq)
        real_rate_shocks.append(shock_rate)

        if t < 400:
            # Regime 0: Early Idiosyncratic Era (2015-2019)
            # Low equity beta (0.12), zero interest rate sensitivity
            idio = rng.gauss(0.001, 0.040)
            ret_btc = 0.12 * ret_nasdaq - 1.5 * shock_rate + idio
            regime_labels.append(0)
        else:
            # Regime 1: Macro Liquidity Era (2020-present)
            # High equity beta (1.55), severe real rate sensitivity (-25.0)
            idio = rng.gauss(0.0005, 0.025)
            ret_btc = 1.55 * ret_nasdaq - 25.0 * shock_rate + idio
            regime_labels.append(1)

        btc_returns.append(ret_btc)

    # 1. Full Sample vs. Regime-Specific OLS
    def estimate_regime_stats(indices: list[int], name: str) -> None:
        y_sub = [btc_returns[i] for i in indices]
        x_sub = [nasdaq_returns[i] for i in indices]
        r_sub = [real_rate_shocks[i] for i in indices]
        m = len(indices)

        m_y = sum(y_sub) / m
        m_x = sum(x_sub) / m
        cov_yx = sum((y - m_y) * (x - m_x) for y, x in zip(y_sub, x_sub, strict=True)) / m
        var_x = sum((x - m_x) ** 2 for x in x_sub) / m
        var_y = sum((y - m_y) ** 2 for y in y_sub) / m
        beta_nasdaq = cov_yx / var_x
        corr_nasdaq = cov_yx / math.sqrt(var_x * var_y)

        cov_yr = sum((y - m_y) * (r - sum(r_sub) / m) for y, r in zip(y_sub, r_sub, strict=True)) / m
        var_r = sum((r - sum(r_sub) / m) ** 2 for r in r_sub) / m
        beta_rate = cov_yr / var_r

        print(f"{name}:")
        print(f"  Nasdaq Beta:        {beta_nasdaq:.4f}")
        print(f"  Nasdaq Correlation: {corr_nasdaq:.4f}")
        print(f"  Real Rate Beta:     {beta_rate:.2f}")

    estimate_regime_stats(list(range(400)), "Regime 0 (Early Idiosyncratic Era)")
    print()
    estimate_regime_stats(list(range(400, 800)), "Regime 1 (Macro Liquidity Era)")

    # 2. Rolling Beta and Correlation Dynamics (60-day window)
    window = 60
    rolling_betas = rolling_ols_beta(btc_returns, nasdaq_returns, window)
    rolling_corrs = rolling_correlation(btc_returns, nasdaq_returns, window)

    early_avg_beta = sum(rolling_betas[:300]) / 300
    late_avg_beta = sum(rolling_betas[400:]) / len(rolling_betas[400:])
    late_avg_corr = sum(rolling_corrs[400:]) / len(rolling_corrs[400:])

    print("\nRolling 60-day Beta Shift:")
    print(f"  Average Beta in Early Regime: {early_avg_beta:.4f}")
    print(f"  Average Beta in Late Regime:  {late_avg_beta:.4f}")
    print(f"  Average Corr in Late Regime:  {late_avg_corr:.4f}")

    assert late_avg_beta > early_avg_beta + 0.80
    assert late_avg_corr > 0.40
    print("\nVerified structural break: Bitcoin transformed into a high-beta macro liquidity asset.")


main()

Where the canon breaks

Traditional multi-asset portfolio construction relies on the premise of stable, unconditional diversification benefits:

  1. Unconditional Correlation Invariance: Mean-variance portfolio optimization assumes correlation matrices are stationary over multi-year horizons. In crypto, correlations with risk equities swing from 0.05 to 0.70 across monetary regimes, rendering historical covariance matrices misleading.
  2. Digital Gold Safe Haven Hypothesis: Retail narratives often brand Bitcoin as "digital gold," predicting that during equity crashes or inflation shocks, digital assets will appreciate. Empirically, during liquidity squeeze events (e.g. March 2020 dash for cash or June 2022 rate hike cycles), Bitcoin sells off aggressively alongside high-beta tech equities.
  3. Homogeneous Asset Class Beta: Practitioners frequently assume all cryptocurrencies possess identical macro sensitivity. In reality, large caps (Bitcoin, Ethereum) exhibit high macro liquidity beta, whereas micro-cap altcoins exhibit idiosyncratic momentum and severe liquidity haircuts.

Misconceptions

  • Bitcoin is an immutable inflation hedge that always appreciates when inflation rises.

    A widespread belief is that because Bitcoin has a hard supply cap of 21 million units, its price must appreciate when headline consumer inflation rises. In reality, central banks respond to persistent inflation by aggressively raising real policy rates and draining balance sheet reserves. Because digital assets have massive effective duration and depend on fiat liquidity availability, rate hikes cause severe valuation compressions that overwhelm any inflation-hedging properties.

  • Crypto beta to traditional equities is constant over time.

    Portfolio managers often calibrate risk models using 5-year full-sample OLS regressions, reporting a modest equity beta of ~0.40. This pools the pre-2020 idiosyncratic era (\(\beta \approx 0.10\)) with the post-2020 institutional era (\(\beta \approx 1.50\)). Using an unconditional average dramatically underestimates tail risk and portfolio drawdown vulnerability during equity sell-offs.

  • Nominal Federal Reserve interest rates matter more than real Treasury yields.

    Investors frequently focus exclusively on the nominal Federal Funds target rate. However, asset valuations are discounted against expected real yields (TIPS). When nominal rates rise alongside high inflation expectations, real rates may remain negative, preserving asset valuations. Conversely, when real yields spike due to quantitative tightening and falling inflation expectations, digital asset prices drop precipitously regardless of the nominal rate level.

Problem set

Mechanics

Problem M1 · Markov Chain Transition Duration

A two-state Markov chain models crypto liquidity regimes. The continuation probability of the Macro Liquidity regime is \(p_{11} = 0.96\), while the continuation probability of the Idiosyncratic regime is \(p_{00} = 0.90\). Calculate the expected duration (in trading days) of both regimes.

Worked solution

The expected duration of state \(j\) in a discrete-time Markov chain is given by: \[\begin{aligned} \E[D_j] &= \frac{1}{1 - p_{jj}}. \end{aligned}\] For Regime 0 (Idiosyncratic): \[\begin{aligned} \E[D_0] &= \frac{1}{1 - 0.90} = \frac{1}{0.10} = 10 \text{ trading days}. \end{aligned}\] For Regime 1 (Macro Liquidity): \[\begin{aligned} \E[D_1] &= \frac{1}{1 - 0.96} = \frac{1}{0.04} = 25 \text{ trading days}. \end{aligned}\] Regime 1 is substantially more persistent, lasting on average 2.5 times longer than Regime 0.

Problem M2 · Calculating Portfolio Beta Under Regime Shift

A multi-asset fund allocates 5% to Bitcoin and 95% to a market index (equity beta = 1.0). In Regime 0, Bitcoin's equity beta is \(\beta_0 = 0.15\). In Regime 1, its beta shifts to \(\beta_1 = 1.80\). Compute the fund's total equity beta in each regime.

Worked solution

The portfolio beta is the weighted sum of constituent betas: \[\begin{aligned} \beta_p &= w_{\text{mkt}} \beta_{\text{mkt}} + w_{\text{btc}} \beta_{\text{btc}} = 0.95(1.0) + 0.05 \beta_{\text{btc}}. \end{aligned}\] In Regime 0: \[\begin{aligned} \beta_{p, 0} &= 0.95(1.0) + 0.05(0.15) = 0.95 + 0.0075 = 0.9575. \end{aligned}\] In Regime 1: \[\begin{aligned} \beta_{p, 1} &= 0.95(1.0) + 0.05(1.80) = 0.95 + 0.0900 = 1.0400. \end{aligned}\] The 5% crypto allocation increases the portfolio's active equity beta exposure by 8.25 percentage points across the regime transition.

Problem M3 · Ergodic Steady-State Distribution

For the transition matrix \(\mathbf{P} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.90 & 0.10 \\ 0.05 & 0.95 \end{pmatrix}\), compute the stationary distribution \(\boldsymbol{\pi} = (\pi_0, \pi_1)\).

Worked solution

The stationary probabilities satisfy \(\boldsymbol{\pi} \mathbf{P} = \boldsymbol{\pi}\) with \(\pi_0 + \pi_1 = 1\). From \(\pi_0 p_{01} = \pi_1 p_{10}\): \[\begin{aligned} 0.10 \pi_0 &= 0.05 \pi_1 \implies \pi_1 = 2 \pi_0. \end{aligned}\] Substituting into the normalization condition: \[\begin{aligned} \pi_0 + 2\pi_0 &= 1 \implies 3\pi_0 = 1 \implies \pi_0 = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333, \quad \pi_1 = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667. \end{aligned}\] The market spends two-thirds of its time in the macro-sensitive regime.

Proof

Problem P1 · Proof of Modified Duration Formula for Perpetual Growth Valuations

Prove that for an asset whose expected future benefits grow at constant rate \(g\) and are discounted at rate \(k = r_f + \eta > g\), the modified duration with respect to the discount rate \(r_f\) is exactly \(\mathcal{D}^* = \frac{1}{r_f + \eta - g}\).

Worked solution

The asset price is: \[\begin{aligned} P(r_f) &= \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{C_0 (1 + g)^t}{(1 + r_f + \eta)^t} = \frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{r_f + \eta - g}. \end{aligned}\] By definition, modified duration is \(\mathcal{D}^* = -\frac{1}{P} \frac{dP}{dr_f}\). Differentiating \(P(r_f)\) with respect to \(r_f\): \[\begin{aligned} \frac{dP}{dr_f} &= -\frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{(r_f + \eta - g)^2}. \end{aligned}\] Dividing by \(P\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{D}^* &= -\frac{1}{P} \left[ -\frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{(r_f + \eta - g)^2} \right] = \frac{C_0 (1 + g)}{(r_f + \eta - g)^2} \cdot \frac{r_f + \eta - g}{C_0 (1 + g)} = \frac{1}{r_f + \eta - g}. \end{aligned}\] This completes the proof.

Problem P2 · Proof of Ergodic Unconditional Mean under Markov Switching

Consider a two-state process \(y_t = \mu_{S_t} + \epsilon_t\), where \(\E[\epsilon_t \mid S_t] = 0\) and \(S_t\) has stationary probabilities \(\pi_0\) and \(\pi_1 = 1 - \pi_0\). Prove that the unconditional mean is \(\E[y_t] = \pi_0 \mu_0 + \pi_1 \mu_1\) and compute its unconditional variance.

Worked solution

By the law of total expectation: \[\begin{aligned} \E[y_t] &= \E[\E[y_t \mid S_t]] = \Prob(S_t = 0)\E[y_t \mid S_t=0] + \Prob(S_t = 1)\E[y_t \mid S_t=1] \\ &= \pi_0 \mu_0 + \pi_1 \mu_1. \end{aligned}\] For the unconditional variance, by the law of total variance: \[\begin{aligned} \Var(y_t) &= \E[\Var(y_t \mid S_t)] + \Var(\E[y_t \mid S_t]). \end{aligned}\] The first term is \(\pi_0 \sigma_0^2 + \pi_1 \sigma_1^2\). The second term is the variance of a discrete random variable taking value \(\mu_0\) with probability \(\pi_0\) and \(\mu_1\) with probability \(\pi_1\): \[\begin{aligned} \Var(\E[y_t \mid S_t]) &= \pi_0 (\mu_0 - \bar{\mu})^2 + \pi_1 (\mu_1 - \bar{\mu})^2 = \pi_0 \pi_1 (\mu_0 - \mu_1)^2. \end{aligned}\] Therefore: \[\begin{aligned} \Var(y_t) &= \pi_0 \sigma_0^2 + \pi_1 \sigma_1^2 + \pi_0 \pi_1 (\mu_0 - \mu_1)^2. \end{aligned}\] The unconditional variance is strictly greater than the weighted average of state variances whenever \(\mu_0 \neq \mu_1\).

Problem P3 · Proof of Downside Beta Decomposition

Let \(r\) and \(m\) have zero mean. Show that the unconditional regression beta \(\beta = \frac{\Cov(r, m)}{\Var(m)}\) can be decomposed into up-market beta \(\beta^+\) and down-market beta \(\beta^-\), weighted by the downside and upside semi-variances of \(m\).

Worked solution

Partition the sample space into \(I^+ = \{m \ge 0\}\) and \(I^- = \{m < 0\}\). The covariance decomposes as: \[\begin{aligned} \Cov(r, m) &= \E[r m] = \E[r m \cdot \mathbf{1}_{m \ge 0}] + \E[r m \cdot \mathbf{1}_{m < 0}]. \end{aligned}\] Define the conditional betas: \[\begin{aligned} \beta^+ &= \frac{\E[r m \mid m \ge 0]}{\E[m^2 \mid m \ge 0]}, \quad \beta^- = \frac{\E[r m \mid m < 0]}{\E[m^2 \mid m < 0]}. \end{aligned}\] Let \(p^+ = \Prob(m \ge 0)\) and \(p^- = \Prob(m < 0)\), and denote \(s_+^2 = \E[m^2 \mid m \ge 0]\), \(s_-^2 = \E[m^2 \mid m < 0]\). Then: \[\begin{aligned} \Cov(r, m) &= p^+ s_+^2 \beta^+ + p^- s_-^2 \beta^-. \end{aligned}\] Dividing by \(\Var(m) = p^+ s_+^2 + p^- s_-^2\): \[\begin{aligned} \beta &= \frac{p^+ s_+^2}{\Var(m)} \beta^+ + \frac{p^- s_-^2}{\Var(m)} \beta^-. \end{aligned}\] Thus, unconditional beta is an exact convex combination of up-beta and down-beta weighted by semi-variances.

Empirical

Problem E1 · Contrasting Early Liu-Tsyvinski (2021) with Modern Regimes

Liu & Tsyvinski (2021, Table 2) reported that Bitcoin's beta to the US equity market was statistically indistinguishable from zero (\(\beta = 0.08, t = 0.72\)) for the 2011–2018 period. In our modern regime lab, what was the estimated equity beta in Regime 1, and what macro factors explain this divergence?

Worked solution

In the modern Regime 1 lab simulation, the estimated equity beta rose to \(\approx 1.55\) (and Nasdaq correlation to \(\approx 0.60\)). Key macro explanatory factors: (1) Institutional capital inflow: hedge funds, institutional asset managers, and corporate treasuries (e.g. MicroStrategy, Tesla) entered after 2020, managing crypto as a risk-budgeted high-beta tech asset; (2) Liquidity commonality: digital assets became part of the broader risk-on / risk-off macro asset allocation basket, driven by global M2 and Fed balance sheet shifts; (3) CME Bitcoin futures and spot ETF infrastructure integrated crypto into traditional margin and prime brokerage financing networks.

Problem E2 · Asymmetric Beta: Up-Market vs. Down-Market Beta

Suppose Bitcoin exhibits asymmetric beta to the Nasdaq: \(\beta^+ = 1.10\) when Nasdaq return is positive (\(r_{\text{nasdaq}} > 0\)), but \(\beta^- = 2.10\) when Nasdaq return is negative (\(r_{\text{nasdaq}} < 0\)). What is the implication for downside risk and portfolio Sortino ratio?

Worked solution

Asymmetric beta (\(\beta^- > \beta^+\)) produces negative skewness and severe downside tail risk. During equity market rallies, crypto participates moderately (\(1.10\times\)), but during equity market sell-offs, crypto plunges with severe leverage (\(2.10\times\)). This downside amplification sharply reduces the Sortino ratio (return per unit of downside deviation) and renders portfolio insurance strategies significantly more expensive.

Problem E3 · High Real Rate Environments and Crypto Staking Yields

How does a rise in 10-year Treasury yields from 1.5% to 5.0% alter the hurdle rate and relative attractiveness of Ethereum staking yields (~3.5%)?

Worked solution

When Treasury yields are 1.5%, a 3.5% staking yield offers a positive spread (+200 bps) over nominal risk-free cash, attracting yield-seeking capital despite smart contract and token volatility risks. When risk-free Treasury yields rise to 5.0%, the spread turns negative (-150 bps). Investors can earn higher yields in zero-credit-risk, capital-guaranteed sovereign bills, triggering institutional outflows from staked crypto protocols into risk-free fixed income.

Problem R1 · Estimating Filtered Probabilities in Real Time

Explain the formulation of Hamilton's filter for real-time regime inference. Why do filtered probabilities \(\Prob(S_t = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_t)\) lag smoothed probabilities \(\Prob(S_t = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_T)\), and how can a quant mitigate this lag in live trading?

Worked solution

Hamilton's filter computes \(\xi_{t \mid t} \propto f(y_t \mid S_t, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}) \sum_i p_{it} \xi_{t-1 \mid t-1}\) using only past and contemporaneous information \(\mathcal{F}_t\). Smoothed probabilities \(\xi_{t \mid T}\) condition on the entire future trajectory up to date \(T\), removing estimation noise. Filtered probabilities exhibit lag because incoming data must overcome the persistence prior \(p_{00}\). To mitigate lag in live execution: (1) Incorporate leading high-frequency covariates (e.g. 5-minute order book depth, cross-currency basis, perpetual funding rates); (2) Utilize Bayesian changepoint detection algorithms (e.g. Adams & MacKay 2007) that update run-length probabilities adaptively.

Problem R2 · Cross-Asset Spillovers: Dollar Index (DXY) vs. Bitcoin

Empirically, Bitcoin exhibits strong negative correlation with the US Dollar Index (DXY). Formulate an econometrically sound vector autoregression (VAR) or VECM model to test whether DXY moves Granger-cause Bitcoin returns or vice versa.

Worked solution

Formulation: (1) Specify a bivariate \(\text{VAR}(p)\): \[\begin{pmatrix} r_{\text{btc}, t} \\ \Delta \ln \text{DXY}_t \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{c} + \sum_{k=1}^p \mathbf{\Phi}_k \begin{pmatrix} r_{\text{btc}, t-k} \\ \Delta \ln \text{DXY}_{t-k} \end{pmatrix} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t.\] (2) Test stationarity via ADF and KPSS tests; if log levels are \(I(1)\) and cointegrated, estimate a Vector Error Correction Model (VECM). (3) Perform Granger causality Wald tests on the off-diagonal lag coefficients. (4) Identify structural shocks using Cholesky or sign restrictions (e.g. global liquidity shock causes dollar appreciation and crypto depreciation). Modern empirical findings show DXY and global liquidity strongly Granger-cause crypto returns, while reverse causality from crypto to the dollar is economically negligible.

Problem R3 · Dynamic Macro Hedging of a Crypto Derivatives Book

A market maker holds a delta-neutral crypto options book with substantial positive vega and negative gamma. As macro regime shifts occur, how should the desk dynamically adjust its hedge ratio between crypto perpetuals and Nasdaq futures (NQ)?

Worked solution

Hedging protocol: (1) In Regime 0 (low macro correlation), basis and delta risks are purely crypto-native. The desk hedges spot/delta exposure exclusively using crypto perpetual contracts. (2) In Regime 1 (high macro liquidity correlation), cross-asset margin and liquidity considerations arise. When crypto perpetual open interest dries up or funding rates spike, the desk hedges directional macro tail risk using liquid Nasdaq 100 E-mini futures (NQ) scaled by the filtered regime beta: \[N_{\text{NQ}} = -\frac{\hat{\beta}_{S_t, \text{NQ}} \times \Delta_{\text{crypto}}}{\text{Contract Multiplier}_{\text{NQ}} \times P_{\text{NQ}}}.\] (3) To guard against regime-switch slippage, the desk dynamically monitors smoothed transition probabilities \(\xi_{t \mid t}\) and blends the hedge between pure-crypto and macro futures.
