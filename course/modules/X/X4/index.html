Digital Assets as a Macro Asset

Digital assets have evolved from an isolated cryptographic novelty into a multi-trillion dollar asset class intimately connected to global monetary policy, systemic funding liquidity, and banking fragility. This module rigorously investigates the empirical asset pricing of cryptocurrencies and the balance-sheet stability of fiat-backed stablecoins. Across four doctoral-level lessons, we examine the extreme statistical moments and time-series return predictability of major tokens (Lesson 1); the cross-sectional risk factors spanning cryptocurrency returns including market, size, and momentum (Lesson 2); the post-2020 structural regime shift linking digital asset valuations to central bank balance sheets, real interest rates, and tech equity betas (Lesson 3); and the run dynamics, maturity mismatch, and de-peg triggers of fiat-backed stablecoin reserve portfolios (Lesson 4). The module concludes with a 16-hour independent stress-testing project modeling liquidation waterfalls and finding critical peg-breaking run thresholds.

  • Prerequisites: X1 FX Markets & Parity Conditions, and F2 The Classic Factors.
  • Introduces: Cross-sectional crypto factors (crypto-risk-factors), stablecoin reserve runs and peg breaks (stablecoin-reserve-run), and macro correlation regimes (crypto-correlation-regimes).
  • Project: Stablecoin reserve stress test: an autograded liquidation engine (stablecoin_stress.py) and executive risk memo (memo.md) evaluated by rubric.
  • Data: Pinned deterministic problem instances and empirical return series.

24 h lessons · 16 h project (40%)

Objectives

By the end of X4 you can:

  1. Estimate higher empirical return moments (variance, skewness, excess kurtosis) and evaluate time-series momentum vs. network user growth predictive regressions (Lesson 1).
  2. Construct the Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu cryptocurrency factor portfolios (CMKT, CSMB, CMOM) and estimate cross-sectional risk premia via Fama–MacBeth two-pass regressions (Lesson 2).
  3. Perform factor spanning regressions to test whether candidate network and valuation anomalies deliver statistically significant abnormal alpha (Lesson 2).
  4. Identify the 2020 structural break in Bitcoin macro sensitivity and estimate rolling beta to technology equities and real Treasury yields (Lesson 3).
  5. Model cryptocurrency correlation regimes using Markov regime-switching and threshold specifications conditioned on central bank balance sheet expansion (Lesson 3).
  6. Deconstruct stablecoin balance sheets into tiered reserve assets, calculate mark-to-market duration losses under monetary tightening, and simulate multi-tier liquidation waterfalls (Lesson 4).
  7. Build a deterministic balance-sheet stress test engine that executes liquidation waterfalls and solves for the critical run size \(R^*\) that breaks the secondary market peg (Project).

Prerequisites

X4 builds directly on the empirical asset pricing foundations established in Fama–French three-factor model, Carhart four-factor model, size effect, cross-sectional momentum, and spanning regression from F2. From X1 FX Markets & Parity Conditions, we apply covered interest parity, no-arbitrage bounds, and the random-walk benchmark. From linear models and time series in S2 and S3, X4 utilizes ordinary least squares, weak stationarity, and rolling regression estimators. From mathematical foundations in M4, we draw upon standardized moments including skewness, kurtosis, and the Student-t distribution.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on prerequisite concepts from F2, X1, and S3, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In empirical factor pricing (F2), what is the primary econometric purpose of a factor spanning regression?

    Options

    A spanning regression regresses a candidate factor on benchmark factors. If the intercept \(\alpha\) is statistically indistinguishable from zero, the candidate factor adds no incremental pricing information and is spanned by the benchmark model.

  2. How is the classic Fama–French Small Minus Big (SMB) size factor constructed (F2)?

    Options

    SMB reflects the return difference between small-capitalization and large-capitalization asset portfolios, capturing the size premium.

  3. What is the excess kurtosis of a standard Gaussian (normal) distribution?

    By definition, kurtosis of a normal distribution is 3.0, so excess kurtosis equals \(\text{Kurtosis} - 3.0 = 0.0\).

  4. Under Covered Interest Parity (X1), what prevents an arbitrageur from generating riskless profits if forward exchange rates deviate from interest rate differentials?

    Options

    Bid–ask spreads across spot, forward, and deposit markets create a band of inaction where small deviations cannot be profitably arbitraged.

  5. In Diamond & Dybvig (1983), why are fractional reserve financial intermediaries vulnerable to self-fulfilling depositor runs?

    Options

    Maturity transformation creates a sequential-service first-mover advantage: because illiquid assets can only be liquidated at a fire-sale loss, early redeemers get par while late redeemers suffer losses, inducing a run.

  6. In modern empirical macroeconomics (E8, X1), what defines a safe asset?

    Options

    Safe assets (e.g. US Treasuries) are information-insensitive debt instruments that maintain liquidity and store of value during systemic distress.

  7. Skill check 1: A stablecoin issuer needs to raise $19.8M in cash to satisfy redemptions by selling Treasury bills subject to a 1.0% liquidation haircut. What par market value of Treasury bills must be liquidated (\(MV = \text{Cash} / (1 - h)\))?

    \(MV = \frac{19.8}{1.0 - 0.01} = \frac{19.8}{0.99} = 20.0\) million dollars.

  8. Skill check 2: A stablecoin has 5,000M unredeemed tokens outstanding following a run. Its remaining reserve assets have a mark-to-market value of $4,900M. What is the remaining Net Asset Value (NAV) per token (\(NAV = \text{Assets} / \text{Tokens}\))?

    \(NAV = \frac{4900}{5000} = 0.980\) dollars per token.

Lesson Map

  1. Lesson 01 · Statistical Moments & Time-Series Return Predictability

    Non-Gaussian return distributions, extreme volatility, positive skewness, excess kurtosis, time-series momentum, and investor attention proxies following Liu & Tsyvinski (2021). 6 study hours.

  2. Lesson 02 · Cross-Sectional Crypto Risk Factors & Spanning

    The cross-section of cryptocurrencies, constructing CMKT, CSMB, and CMOM factors, Fama–MacBeth risk premia estimation, and factor spanning tests following Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2022). Introduces crypto-risk-factors. 6 study hours.

  3. Lesson 03 · Macro Liquidity Regimes & Bitcoin Beta Switching

    The 2020 structural shift from idiosyncratic alternative asset to high-beta macro liquidity proxy, rolling correlations with technology equities, and Markov regime-switching models. Introduces crypto-correlation-regimes. 6 study hours.

  4. Lesson 04 · Stablecoin Mechanics, Reserve Backing & Peg Run Dynamics

    Balance-sheet mechanics of fiat-backed stablecoins, Diamond–Dybvig bank run dynamics, maturity/liquidity mismatch, mark-to-market duration losses, and liquidation waterfalls. Introduces stablecoin-reserve-run. 6 study hours.

Project Ladder

The 16-hour capstone project evaluates institutional stablecoin reserve resilience under severe redemption pressure and macroeconomic interest rate shocks:

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    Trace a multi-tier liquidation waterfall on a $20B stablecoin balance sheet subjected to a 40% redemption shock and 125 bps interest rate spike.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Implement the core liquidation step formulas for market value consumption, haircut loss accounting, and post-run secondary market NAV.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Implement the complete stress-testing engine stablecoin_stress.py satisfying the 50-seed autograder suite and author an executive risk memo memo.md evaluated by rubric.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Incorporate endogenous redemption velocity driven by secondary decentralized exchange (DEX) liquidity pool discounts and fire-sale contagion.
