Currency Risk Premia

International currency markets offer predictable cross-sectional excess returns that systematically contradict the classical uncovered interest parity hypothesis. This module establishes the structural asset pricing architecture of foreign exchange risk premia. Across six intensive lessons, we investigate the empirical mechanics of the currency carry trade and the forward discount puzzle (Lesson 1); the devastating left-tail crash risk generated by margin constraints and asymmetric liquidity spirals (Lesson 2); the econometric small-sample upward bias induced by rare disaster peso problems (Lesson 3); the foundational two-factor decomposition of Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011) into the Dollar level factor and the High-Minus-Low FX carry slope factor (Lesson 4); the pricing of global foreign exchange volatility risk innovations following Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling & Schrimpf (2012) (Lesson 5); and the orthogonal dynamics of cross-sectional currency momentum and purchasing power parity value (Lesson 6). The module culminates in an 18-hour empirical research project replicating core currency factors on open market data and testing cross-sectional pricing models.

  • Prerequisites: X1 FX Markets & Parity Conditions and F3 Factors Across Asset Classes.
  • Unlocks: Studio · Carry Book with Crash-Risk Overlay and advanced quantitative asset pricing electives.
  • Project: Currency factor replication: an autograded empirical pricing engine (currency_factors.py) replicating Dollar and HML-FX factors and testing global FX volatility pricing, accompanied by an analytical research memo (report.md).
  • Data: Embedded daily and monthly market series (fx_daily.csv, fx_monthly.csv, ust_daily.csv) alongside deterministic simulated problem instances.

52 h lessons · 18 h project (26%)

Objectives

By the end of X2 you can:

  1. Formulate the currency carry trade strategy, quantify historical Sharpe ratios, and decompose the forward discount puzzle in the cross-section of global currencies (Lesson 1).
  2. Analyze carry crash risk, quantify negative return skewness and fat left tails, and model asymmetric margin-induced funding liquidity spirals (Lesson 2).
  3. Quantify the econometric peso problem, derive small-sample upward bias in estimated Sharpe ratios, and evaluate rare catastrophe disaster risk models in currency markets (Lesson 3).
  4. Decompose currency excess returns into the Dollar level factor (\(R_{\mathrm{DOL}}\)) and the High-Minus-Low FX carry slope factor (\(\mathrm{HML}_{\mathrm{FX}}\)), and derive the no-arbitrage affine stochastic discount factor (Lesson 4).
  5. Estimate the global foreign exchange volatility innovation factor (\(\Delta \mathrm{Vol}_{\mathrm{FX}}\)) and test whether exposure to aggregate volatility risk prices the cross-section of currency carry portfolios (Lesson 5).
  6. Construct cross-sectional and time-series currency momentum and purchasing power parity value factors, and evaluate the diversification benefits of multi-factor currency allocations (Lesson 6).
  7. Replicate the Dollar factor and HML-FX on open data, and execute two-pass Fama–MacBeth cross-sectional asset pricing regressions (Project).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on prerequisite concepts from X1, F3, and S5, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under Covered Interest Parity (CIP), how is the log forward discount \(fd_t = \ln F_t - \ln S_t\) related to the domestic risk-free rate \(i_t\) and the foreign risk-free rate \(i_t^*\)?

    Options

    By CIP, \(F_t / S_t = (1 + i_t) / (1 + i_t^*)\). In continuous logs, \(\ln(F_t / S_t) \approx i_t - i_t^*\). High-interest foreign currencies have \(i_t^* > i_t\), so \(fd_t < 0\) (forward discount).

  2. What is the empirical finding of the classic Fama (1984) regression \(\Delta s_{t+1} = \alpha + \beta (f_t - s_t) + \varepsilon_{t+1}\)?

    Options

    Fama (1984) showed that empirical estimates of \(\beta\) across major currency pairs are systematically negative (often between \(-0.5\) and \(-2.0\)), meaning high-interest currencies tend to appreciate rather than depreciate.

  3. In cross-asset factor investing (F3), how is generalized carry defined for a futures contract or financial asset?

    Options

    As formalized by Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt (2018), carry is the expected return of a position if the market price does not change.

  4. What did Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013) discover regarding the empirical correlation between value factors and momentum factors across asset classes?

    Options

    Asness et al. (2013) demonstrated ubiquitous negative correlation between value and momentum strategies across eight diverse asset classes.

  5. In two-pass Fama–MacBeth cross-sectional asset pricing regressions (S5), what is estimated in Stage 1 versus Stage 2?

    Options

    Fama–MacBeth first runs time-series regressions of asset returns on factors to obtain factor betas \(\beta_i\), and then runs cross-sectional regressions of mean asset returns on betas to estimate risk premia \(\lambda\).

  6. In Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan (2011), what is the economic interpretation of the Dollar factor (\(R_{\mathrm{DOL}}\))?

    Options

    The Dollar factor \(R_{\mathrm{DOL}}\) is the unweighted average return of all sorted foreign currencies against the US dollar, functioning as an aggregate level factor.

  7. Skill check (Rung 1 · Factor Construction): In a 5-portfolio currency carry sort, Portfolio 1 (lowest carry) earns an annualized excess return of \(-4.00\%\), and Portfolio 5 (highest carry) earns \(+8.00\%\). What is the annualized return of the High-Minus-Low FX (\(\mathrm{HML}_{\mathrm{FX}}\)) carry factor in percent?

    \(\mathrm{HML}_{\mathrm{FX}} = R_5 - R_1 = 8.00\% - (-4.00\%) = 12.00\%\) annualized carry spread.

  8. Skill check (Rung 2 · Fama–MacBeth): In a univariate cross-sectional regression without an intercept of portfolio mean excess returns \(\bar{R}\) on factor betas \(\beta\), the vector of betas is \(\beta = [-0.5, 0.5]\) and the vector of mean returns is \(\bar{R} = [-0.0040, 0.0040]\). What is the estimated factor risk premium \(\hat{\lambda} = (\beta' \beta)^{-1} \beta' \bar{R}\)?

    \(\beta'\beta = (-0.5)^2 + 0.5^2 = 0.50\). \(\beta'\bar{R} = (-0.5)(-0.0040) + (0.5)(0.0040) = 0.0020 + 0.0020 = 0.0040\). \(\hat{\lambda} = 0.0040 / 0.50 = 0.0080\).

Project ladder

The module culminates in the Currency factor replication project (18 hours), where you implement an econometric factor replication engine testing whether global FX volatility prices the carry cross-section across 50 seeded instances.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Replicate the Dollar factor and HML-FX on sample currency series, tracing portfolio sorting, factor regressions, and two-pass Fama–MacBeth pricing.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Complete the missing factor construction and cross-sectional regression formulas for Dollar return, HML-FX spread, and risk price estimation.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Implement the complete solver currency_factors.py passing the 50-seed autograder suite and author the analytical research memo report.md evaluated by rubric.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Evaluate the impact of transaction costs, dynamic turnover friction, and bid-ask spreads on net factor Sharpe ratios, solving for breakeven execution costs.
