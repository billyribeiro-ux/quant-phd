Currency Momentum, Value, & Multi-Factor Portfolios

Beyond the carry trade, what other systematic factors drive expected currency returns? Empirical research by Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling, and Schrimpf (2012, 2016) identifies two robust currency anomaly styles: FX momentum and FX value. Momentum captures trend persistence in currency returns, whereas value captures long-term mean reversion toward Purchasing Power Parity. Most importantly, as documented by Asness, Moskowitz, and Pedersen (2013), currency value and momentum exhibit a strong negative correlation. Combining carry, value, and momentum into an integrated multi-factor portfolio generates substantial diversification benefits, cutting maximum drawdowns and expanding unconditional Sharpe ratios far beyond any standalone factor.

1Cross-Sectional & Time-Series FX Momentum

In equity and commodity markets, momentum represents one of the most pervasive anomalies. In foreign exchange, Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling & Schrimpf (2012) established that momentum is equally powerful across currencies.

Definition 6.1 · FX Momentum
FX momentum is an investment strategy that ranks foreign currencies based on their past cumulative excess returns over a formation lookback window \(L\) (typically 1 to 12 months) and holds a long position in top-performing currencies (winners) and a short position in bottom-performing currencies (losers) over a subsequent holding horizon \(H\).

Two primary implementations of currency momentum exist in quantitative finance:

  1. Cross-Sectional Momentum (CSMOM): At date \(t\), sort the universe of \(N_t\) currencies by their past \(L\)-month return \(R_{t-L \to t}^j\). Go long the top quintile (winners) and short the bottom quintile (losers) with zero net dollar exposure.
  2. Time-Series Momentum (TSMOM): For each currency \(j\), take a long position if its past return is strictly positive (\(R_{t-L \to t}^j > 0\)) and a short position if its past return is negative (\(R_{t-L \to t}^j < 0\)).
While cross-sectional momentum generates attractive annualized Sharpe ratios (\(0.70\) to \(1.20\)), it exhibits unique dynamic interaction with the carry trade: during steady carry regimes, momentum reinforces carry by holding high-yield appreciating currencies; however, when a carry crash begins, momentum rapidly cuts long exposure and flips short the target currencies, dampening severe drawdowns.

2FX Value & Purchasing Power Parity Deviations

While momentum operates over horizons of 1 to 12 months, currency value operates over horizons of 2 to 5 years, exploiting macroeconomic mean reversion in real exchange rates.

Let \(S_t^j\) be the nominal spot exchange rate (foreign currency per USD), \(P_t^j\) be the foreign price level (e.g. CPI), and \(P_t^{US}\) be the US price level. The real exchange rate (RER) is: \[\begin{aligned} Q_t^j &\equiv S_t^j \cdot \frac{P_t^{US}}{P_t^j}. \end{aligned}\] Under absolute Purchasing Power Parity (PPP), the real exchange rate is constant: \(Q_t^j = 1\). In practice, transport costs, trade barriers, and nominal price stickiness create persistent deviations from PPP with half-lives between 3 and 5 years.

Definition 6.2 · FX Value
FX value is an investment style that ranks currencies according to their real exchange rate undervaluation relative to Purchasing Power Parity or long-term historical moving averages: \[\begin{aligned} \text{Val}_t^j &\equiv \ln \bar{Q}_t^j - \ln Q_t^j, \end{aligned}\] going long undervalued currencies (\(\text{Val}_t^j > 0\)) and short overvalued currencies (\(\text{Val}_t^j < 0\)) to capture multi-year real exchange rate mean reversion.

Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling & Schrimpf (2016) show that while standalone FX value generates a modest annualized Sharpe ratio (\(\approx 0.30 - 0.40\)), it provides profound portfolio diversification benefits because it is virtually uncorrelated with carry and strongly negatively correlated with momentum.

3Multi-Factor Integration & Negative Covariance Expansion

As documented globally by Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), value and momentum strategies across asset classes exhibit a robust negative correlation, typically between \(-0.15\) and \(-0.45\). The economic intuition is transparent:

  • Momentum buys recent winners and shorts recent losers. By definition, persistent appreciation moves an asset further away from its long-run fundamental PPP anchor, making momentum inherently “expensive” or growth-oriented.
  • Value buys depressed, beaten-down currencies that have suffered prolonged depreciation and sells currencies that have appreciated to extreme valuation heights. Thus, value systematically holds currencies with poor recent momentum.

Theorem 6.1 · Multi-Factor Sharpe Ratio Expansion under Negative Covariance
Consider two factor portfolios, Momentum (\(M\)) and Value (\(V\)), with positive Sharpe ratios \(\mathrm{SR}_M > 0, \mathrm{SR}_V > 0\) and correlation \(\rho \equiv \Corr(M, V) < 0\). Then the maximum attainable Sharpe ratio of their combination \(\mathrm{SR}^*\) strictly satisfies: \[\begin{aligned} (\mathrm{SR}^*)^2 &= \frac{\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2 - 2 \rho \, \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V}{1 - \rho^2} > \mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2. \end{aligned}\] Furthermore, adding a negatively correlated factor generates a larger Sharpe ratio expansion than adding an orthogonal factor (\(\rho = 0\)).

Proof. Let excess returns be \(\mathbf{r} = [r_M, r_V]'\) with mean vector \(\boldsymbol{\mu} = [\mu_M, \mu_V]'\) and covariance matrix: \[\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\Sigma} &= \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_M^2 & \rho \sigma_M \sigma_V \\ \rho \sigma_M \sigma_V & \sigma_V^2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}\] The determinant is \(\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \sigma_M^2 \sigma_V^2 (1 - \rho^2)\). The inverse covariance matrix is: \[\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} &= \frac{1}{\sigma_M^2 \sigma_V^2 (1 - \rho^2)} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_V^2 & -\rho \sigma_M \sigma_V \\ -\rho \sigma_M \sigma_V & \sigma_M^2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}\] By classic Markowitz mean-variance portfolio theory, the maximum squared Sharpe ratio of the unconstrained tangency portfolio is the quadratic form: \[\begin{aligned} (\mathrm{SR}^*)^2 &= \boldsymbol{\mu}' \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu} = \frac{\mu_M^2 \sigma_V^2 - 2 \rho \mu_M \mu_V \sigma_M \sigma_V + \mu_V^2 \sigma_M^2}{\sigma_M^2 \sigma_V^2 (1 - \rho^2)} \\ &= \frac{\frac{\mu_M^2}{\sigma_M^2} + \frac{\mu_V^2}{\sigma_V^2} - 2 \rho \frac{\mu_M}{\sigma_M} \frac{\mu_V}{\sigma_V}}{1 - \rho^2} = \frac{\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2 - 2 \rho \, \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V}{1 - \rho^2}. \end{aligned}\] Now compare this to the orthogonal case \(\rho = 0\), where \((\mathrm{SR}^*)^2 = \mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2\). Define the difference: \[\begin{aligned} \Delta(\rho) &\equiv \frac{\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2 - 2 \rho \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V}{1 - \rho^2} - (\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2) \\ &= \frac{\rho^2 (\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2) - 2 \rho \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V}{1 - \rho^2} = \frac{\rho [\rho (\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2) - 2 \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V]}{1 - \rho^2}. \end{aligned}\] For \(\rho < 0\), let \(\rho = -|\rho|\). Then: \[\begin{aligned} \Delta(-|\rho|) &= \frac{|\rho|^2 (\mathrm{SR}_M^2 + \mathrm{SR}_V^2) + 2 |\rho| \mathrm{SR}_M \mathrm{SR}_V}{1 - |\rho|^2} > 0, \end{aligned}\] since \(\mathrm{SR}_M, \mathrm{SR}_V > 0\) and \(|\rho| \in (0, 1)\). Thus, negative correlation strictly amplifies the portfolio Sharpe ratio beyond the sum of individual squared Sharpe ratios. End of proof.∎

Lab 1 · Multi-factor currency style diversification backtest

Simulating carry, momentum, and value currency factors over a 120-month horizon will demonstrate: (1) negative correlation between momentum and value (\(\rho < -0.10\)); (2) standalone Sharpe ratios of 0.959 (carry), 1.255 (momentum), and 0.320 (value); and (3) an integrated multi-factor Sharpe ratio exceeding 1.40, achieving over 10% expansion beyond the best standalone factor.

"""Lab X2-L06-1: Multi-Factor Currency Allocation: Carry, Momentum, and Value.

Simulates cross-sectional currency factors (Carry, Momentum, Value), evaluates
their correlation structure (Asness et al. 2013), and measures multi-factor diversification.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
from typing import Any


def simulate_currency_styles(
    n_months: int = 120,
    seed: int = 888,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Simulate Carry, Momentum, and Value factors and multi-factor portfolio.

    Args:
        n_months: Number of simulated months.
        seed: Random seed.

    Returns:
        Dictionary of factor correlations, annualized statistics, and multi-factor gains.
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)

    carry_rets: list[float] = []
    mom_rets: list[float] = []
    val_rets: list[float] = []

    for _ in range(n_months):
        z_liq = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z_trend = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)
        z_ppp = rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0)

        # Carry factor
        r_carry = 0.0050 + 0.014 * z_liq + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.014)

        # Momentum factor
        r_mom = 0.0045 + 0.014 * z_trend + 0.004 * z_liq + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.014)

        # Value factor
        r_val = 0.0045 + 0.014 * z_ppp - 0.010 * z_trend + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.014)

        carry_rets.append(r_carry)
        mom_rets.append(r_mom)
        val_rets.append(r_val)

    # Compute correlation matrix
    def corr(x: list[float], y: list[float]) -> float:
        mx = sum(x) / len(x)
        my = sum(y) / len(y)
        cov = sum((a - mx) * (b - my) for a, b in zip(x, y, strict=True))
        vx = sum((a - mx) ** 2 for a in x)
        vy = sum((b - my) ** 2 for b in y)
        return cov / math.sqrt(vx * vy)

    r_cm = corr(carry_rets, mom_rets)
    r_cv = corr(carry_rets, val_rets)
    r_mv = corr(mom_rets, val_rets)

    # Annualized stats for single factors
    def factor_stats(series: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
        m = sum(series) / len(series)
        v = sum((r - m) ** 2 for r in series) / (len(series) - 1)
        s = math.sqrt(v)
        ann_m = m * 12.0
        ann_s = s * math.sqrt(12.0)
        sr = ann_m / ann_s if ann_s > 1e-8 else 0.0
        return ann_m, ann_s, sr

    _m_c, _s_c, sr_c = factor_stats(carry_rets)
    _m_m, _s_m, sr_m = factor_stats(mom_rets)
    _m_v, _s_v, sr_v = factor_stats(val_rets)

    # Multi-factor equal-weighted blend
    multi_rets = [(carry_rets[t] + mom_rets[t] + val_rets[t]) / 3.0 for t in range(n_months)]
    _m_multi, _s_multi, sr_multi = factor_stats(multi_rets)
    best_single_sr = max(sr_c, sr_m, sr_v)
    sr_gain = ((sr_multi / best_single_sr) - 1.0) * 100.0

    return {
        "n_months": n_months,
        "corr_carry_mom": round(r_cm, 3),
        "corr_carry_val": round(r_cv, 3),
        "corr_mom_val": round(r_mv, 3),
        "sr_carry": round(sr_c, 3),
        "sr_mom": round(sr_m, 3),
        "sr_val": round(sr_v, 3),
        "sr_multi": round(sr_multi, 3),
        "sr_gain_pct": round(sr_gain, 1),
    }


def main() -> None:
    res = simulate_currency_styles(n_months=120, seed=888)
    print("=== Multi-Factor Currency Styles: Carry, Momentum, Value ===")
    print(f"Sample length: {res['n_months']} months")
    print("Pairwise Correlations:")
    print(f"  Corr(Carry, Momentum): {res['corr_carry_mom']:>6.3f}")
    print(f"  Corr(Carry, Value):    {res['corr_carry_val']:>6.3f}")
    print(f"  Corr(Momentum, Value): {res['corr_mom_val']:>6.3f} (strong negative diversification)")
    print("Sharpe Ratios (Annualized):")
    print(f"  Carry alone:    {res['sr_carry']:.3f}")
    print(f"  Momentum alone: {res['sr_mom']:.3f}")
    print(f"  Value alone:    {res['sr_val']:.3f}")
    print(f"  Multi-Factor:   {res['sr_multi']:.3f} (+{res['sr_gain_pct']:.1f}% Sharpe ratio expansion)")


main()

4Where the Canon Breaks

Neoclassical finance frequently evaluates trading strategies in silos, assessing each anomaly by its standalone t-statistic or Sharpe ratio. In multi-factor foreign exchange, this canon breaks decisively:

  • The Standalone Metric Trap: Investors often reject FX value because its standalone Sharpe ratio is relatively modest (\(\approx 0.30 - 0.40\)). However, because value is negatively correlated with momentum, adding value to a momentum-carry portfolio dramatically expands the multi-factor Sharpe ratio and reduces tail risk.
  • The Static Factor Illusion: Conventional asset pricing models assume static factor loadings. In reality, momentum is intrinsically dynamic: it holds high-beta carry currencies during steady bull markets, but abruptly switches to short carry positions during liquidity panics, acting as an endogenous regime-shifting hedge.
  • Information Diffusion Limits: Neoclassical efficient market models predict instantaneous price adjustments to macroeconomic releases. Empirical FX momentum profits persist for 3 to 6 months due to gradual institutional capital reallocations and slow information diffusion across fragmented dealer networks.

5Misconceptions

  • FX value strategies are effective for short-term monthly tactical trading.

    Deviations from Purchasing Power Parity take years to resolve. The empirical half-life of real exchange rate deviations is 36 to 60 months. Attempting to trade FX value over 1-month to 3-month horizons exposes investors to extreme tracking error and multi-year valuation divergences.

  • Carry and momentum are virtually the same strategy because both often hold high-yielding currencies.

    While carry and momentum have a modest positive unconditional correlation (\(\approx 0.20\)), their behavior during market crises diverges completely. Carry remains long high-interest currencies as they crash into liquidity spirals, while momentum algorithms detect the initial downward price break and flip short, hedging the portfolio.

  • Transaction costs and rebalancing friction wipe out all profits from currency momentum.

    Although momentum generates higher turnover than carry (rebalancing monthly across winner and loser baskets), Menkhoff et al. (2012) prove that in liquid G10 currency markets, effective bid-ask spreads drag returns by only 150 to 200 basis points annually, preserving robust net Sharpe ratios above 0.80.

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 6.1 · Real Exchange Rate Calculation
The nominal exchange rate is \(S = 1.3000\) USD per GBP. The US price index is \(P_{US} = 120.0\) and the UK price index is \(P_{UK} = 100.0\). If the 5-year average real exchange rate is \(\bar{Q} = 1.4500\), calculate: (a) The current real exchange rate \(Q\); (b) The FX value metric \(\text{Val} = \ln(\bar{Q} / Q)\) and determine whether the British pound is undervalued or overvalued.

Worked Solution

(a) The current real exchange rate is \(Q = S \cdot \frac{P_{UK}}{P_{US}} = 1.3000 \cdot \frac{100.0}{120.0} = 1.0833\). (b) The value metric is \(\text{Val} = \ln(1.4500 / 1.0833) = \ln(1.3385) \approx +0.2915\) (\(+29.15\%\)). Since \(\text{Val} > 0\), the British pound is substantially undervalued relative to its 5-year real anchor, signaling a long position for an FX value strategy.

Problem 6.2 · Two-Asset Tangency Portfolio Weights
Two currency factors, Momentum (\(M\)) and Value (\(V\)), have expected returns \(\mu_M = 8.0\%\) and \(\mu_V = 4.0\%\), volatilities \(\sigma_M = 10.0\%\) and \(\sigma_V = 10.0\%\), and correlation \(\rho = -0.20\). Compute the optimal tangency portfolio weight vector \(\mathbf{w}^* = \frac{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}}{\mathbf{1}' \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}}\).

Worked Solution

The covariance matrix is \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.0100 & -0.0020 \\ -0.0020 & 0.0100 \end{bmatrix}\). The inverse is \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} = \frac{1}{0.000100 - 0.000004} \begin{bmatrix} 0.0100 & 0.0020 \\ 0.0020 & 0.0100 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{0.000096} \begin{bmatrix} 0.0100 & 0.0020 \\ 0.0020 & 0.0100 \end{bmatrix}\). Multiplying by \(\boldsymbol{\mu} = [0.08, 0.04]'\): \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu} \propto \begin{bmatrix} 0.0100(0.08) + 0.0020(0.04) \\ 0.0020(0.08) + 0.0100(0.04) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.00088 \\ 0.00056 \end{bmatrix}\). Normalizing by the sum \(0.00088 + 0.00056 = 0.00144\): \(w_M^* = 0.00088 / 0.00144 = 0.611\) (\(61.1\%\)) and \(w_V^* = 0.00056 / 0.00144 = 0.389\) (\(38.9\%\)).

Problem 6.3 · Sharpe Ratio Amplification Calculation
Using the formula from Theorem 6.1, compute the combined Sharpe ratio for \(\mathrm{SR}_M = 1.00\), \(\mathrm{SR}_V = 0.50\), and correlation \(\rho = -0.30\). Compare it to the case where \(\rho = 0.00\).

Worked Solution

With \(\rho = -0.30\): \((\mathrm{SR}^*)^2 = \frac{1.00^2 + 0.50^2 - 2(-0.30)(1.00)(0.50)}{1 - (-0.30)^2} = \frac{1.00 + 0.25 + 0.30}{1 - 0.09} = \frac{1.55}{0.91} \approx 1.7033\), yielding \(\mathrm{SR}^* = \sqrt{1.7033} \approx 1.305\). When \(\rho = 0.00\): \((\mathrm{SR}^*)^2 = 1.00^2 + 0.50^2 = 1.25\), giving \(\mathrm{SR}^* = \sqrt{1.25} \approx 1.118\). The negative correlation provides an additional \(16.7\%\) boost to the Sharpe ratio over the uncorrelated benchmark.

Tier 2 · Proofs & Derivations

Problem 6.4 · Negative Correlation Mechanism between Value and Momentum
Let \(p_t\) be the log asset price following a trend-plus-noise model: \(p_t = m_t + \varepsilon_t\), where \(m_t\) is fundamental value and \(\varepsilon_t\) is stationary valuation noise with \(\Cov(\varepsilon_t, \varepsilon_{t-k}) = \gamma_k > 0\). Define momentum as \(\Delta p_t = p_t - p_{t-1}\) and value as \(val_t = m_t - p_t = -\varepsilon_t\). Prove that \(\Cov(\Delta p_t, val_t) < 0\).

Worked Solution

Expanding the momentum return: \(\Delta p_t = (m_t - m_{t-1}) + (\varepsilon_t - \varepsilon_{t-1})\). Assuming fundamental changes \(\Delta m_t\) are independent of valuation noise \(\varepsilon_t\), the covariance is: \(\Cov(\Delta p_t, val_t) = \Cov(\Delta m_t + \varepsilon_t - \varepsilon_{t-1}, -\varepsilon_t) = -\Cov(\varepsilon_t - \varepsilon_{t-1}, \varepsilon_t) = -\Var(\varepsilon_t) + \Cov(\varepsilon_{t-1}, \varepsilon_t) = -\gamma_0 + \gamma_1\). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, autocovariance satisfies \(\gamma_1 < \gamma_0\). Therefore, \(-\gamma_0 + \gamma_1 < 0\), proving that momentum and value are fundamentally and intrinsically negatively correlated.

Problem 6.5 · Risk Parity Weights for Uncorrelated Factors
For a three-factor portfolio consisting of Carry (\(C\)), Momentum (\(M\)), and Value (\(V\)) with volatilities \(\sigma_C, \sigma_M, \sigma_V\) and zero pairwise correlations, derive the risk parity portfolio weights where each factor contributes equal risk to total portfolio variance.

Worked Solution

Portfolio variance is \(\sigma_p^2 = \sum_{i=1}^3 w_i^2 \sigma_i^2\). The marginal risk contribution of factor \(i\) is \(\frac{\partial \sigma_p}{\partial w_i} = \frac{w_i \sigma_i^2}{\sigma_p}\). The total risk contribution is \(\text{TRC}_i = w_i \frac{\partial \sigma_p}{\partial w_i} = \frac{w_i^2 \sigma_i^2}{\sigma_p}\). Equal risk contribution requires \(\text{TRC}_i = \text{TRC}_j\) for all \(i, j\), which implies \(w_i^2 \sigma_i^2 = w_j^2 \sigma_j^2 \implies w_i \sigma_i = c\). Thus, the optimal weight is inversely proportional to volatility: \(w_i = \frac{1/\sigma_i}{\sum_{k=1}^3 1/\sigma_k}\).

Problem 6.6 · Half-Life of Real Exchange Rate Mean Reversion
Suppose the real exchange rate follows the continuous-time Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process: \(dq_t = -\kappa (q_t - \bar{q}) dt + \sigma dW_t\). Derive the half-life of mean reversion \(t_{1/2}\) in terms of the mean-reversion speed \(\kappa\).

Worked Solution

The conditional expectation of the OU process is \(\mathbb{E}[q_t - \bar{q} \mid q_0] = (q_0 - \bar{q}) e^{-\kappa t}\). The half-life is the time \(t_{1/2}\) required for the expected deviation to decay to half its initial size: \((q_0 - \bar{q}) e^{-\kappa t_{1/2}} = \frac{1}{2}(q_0 - \bar{q}) \implies e^{-\kappa t_{1/2}} = \frac{1}{2}\). Taking natural logarithms: \(-\kappa t_{1/2} = -\ln 2 \implies t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\kappa}\). For empirical estimates where \(\kappa \approx 0.15\) per annum, \(t_{1/2} \approx 0.693 / 0.15 \approx 4.62\) years.

Tier 3 · Empirical & Data

Problem 6.7 · Correlation Matrix of Currency Factor Styles
Using data from Menkhoff et al. (2012, 2016) and Asness et al. (2013), the correlation between Carry and Momentum is \(+0.22\), between Carry and Value is \(-0.04\), and between Momentum and Value is \(-0.15\). Explain how this empirical correlation matrix makes an equal-weighted multi-factor portfolio resilient across different macroeconomic regimes.

Worked Solution

The near-zero correlation between Carry and Value (\(-0.04\)) and the negative correlation between Momentum and Value (\(-0.15\)) guarantee structural regime hedge properties. During tranquil risk-seeking regimes, Carry and Momentum generate compounding gains while Value remains dormant. During violent carry crash spirals, Value buffers drawdowns because oversold currencies become cheap, while Momentum rapidly flips short, cutting downside exposure.

Problem 6.8 · Performance of 1-Month vs 12-Month Momentum
In empirical tests across 30 currencies over 25 years, 1-month lookback momentum yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of \(0.75\) with \(45\%\) annual turnover, while 12-month lookback momentum yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of \(0.92\) with \(18\%\) annual turnover. Evaluate which lookback window provides higher net-of-cost performance for an institutional fund facing a 5 bps round-trip transaction cost.

Worked Solution

For 1-month momentum: turnover of 45x (4500% notional rebalanced annually) incurs transaction cost drag of \(45 \times 0.05\% = 2.25\%\). For 12-month momentum: turnover of 18x incurs transaction cost drag of \(18 \times 0.05\% = 0.90\%\). The net return drag is 135 bps lower for the 12-month window. Combined with a higher gross Sharpe ratio (0.92 vs 0.75), the 12-month momentum strategy dominates unambiguously after transaction costs.

Problem 6.9 · Maximum Drawdown Reduction via Multi-Factor Integration
In historical backtests, the maximum drawdown of standalone Carry was \(-38.5\%\), standalone Momentum was \(-24.2\%\), and standalone Value was \(-28.0\%\). The equal-weighted multi-factor portfolio experienced a maximum drawdown of only \(-14.8\%\). Explain why the multi-factor drawdown is less than half the carry drawdown.

Worked Solution

The drawdowns of the three styles occur at different dates during the economic cycle. Carry crashes during systemic liquidity panics (e.g. 2008). Momentum suffers whipsaws during market turning points and trend reversals (e.g. 2009 rebound). Value experiences drawdowns during prolonged bubbles when currencies stay mispriced (e.g. late 1990s). Because their severe losses are desynchronized, combining them dampens portfolio drawdown to \(-14.8\%\).

Tier 4 · Research & Extensions

Problem 6.10 · Dynamic Risk Budgeting across Styles
Design a dynamic risk budgeting model where factor capital allocations \(w_t = [w_C, w_M, w_V]'\) vary as a function of the global FX volatility regime: \(w_t = f(\sigma_t^{\mathrm{FX}})\). Specify the optimal regime-dependent policy.

Worked Solution

In low-volatility regimes (\(\sigma_t^{\mathrm{FX}} < \text{median}\)), Carry exhibits high stability and low crash probability, warranting a 50% Carry, 30% Momentum, 20% Value allocation. In surging volatility regimes (\(\Delta \sigma_t^{\mathrm{FX}} > 1.5\sigma_u\)), Carry leverage is cut to 10% to avoid liquidity spirals, Momentum is increased to 50% to exploit rapid trend-following liquidations, and Value is increased to 40% to position for long-term mean reversion.

Problem 6.11 · Machine Learning Factor Timing via Random Forests
Evaluate the risks of using complex non-linear machine learning models (e.g., Random Forests, Gradient Boosting) to predict 1-month style factor returns relative to linear cross-sectional asset pricing.

Worked Solution

Foreign exchange factor returns have exceptionally low signal-to-noise ratios (monthly \(R^2 < 3\%\)). High-capacity machine learning algorithms easily overfit to noise, learning sample-specific macro regime transitions that fail out-of-sample. Linear regularized factor models (such as Ridge or Elastic Net) with economic constraints preserve monotonicity and prevent catastrophic out-of-sample failure.

Problem 6.12 · Cross-Asset Factor Premia (Equities, Bonds, Commodities, FX)
Following Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt (2018), define the general definition of carry across asset classes and explain why a multi-asset carry strategy has a Sharpe ratio double that of FX carry alone.

Worked Solution

Carry is defined universally as the expected return on an asset assuming market prices and economic conditions remain unchanged: \(C_t = \frac{S_t - F_t}{S_t}\). In equities, it is the dividend yield minus funding; in bonds, the term spread roll-down; in commodities, the roll yield of the futures curve; in FX, the interest rate differential. Because cross-asset carry factors share low pairwise correlations (\(\approx 0.15 - 0.25\)), multi-asset carry diversification doubles the Sharpe ratio from ~0.7 to over 1.4.

8Retrieval Practice

  1. How is an FX cross-sectional momentum strategy constructed?

    Options

    FX momentum sorts currencies by their trailing returns over a 1–12 month window, taking long positions in winners and short positions in losers.

  2. What economic principle underpins FX value strategies?

    Options

    FX value bets on multi-year mean reversion of real exchange rates toward Purchasing Power Parity or long-run real averages.

  3. What is the typical empirical correlation between currency Momentum and currency Value?

    Options

    As documented by Asness et al. (2013), momentum buys recent winners that have become expensive, while value buys depressed losers, producing a negative correlation.

  4. If Momentum has Sharpe ratio 1.00, Value has Sharpe ratio 0.50, and their correlation is \(\rho = -0.30\), what is the optimal combined Sharpe ratio \(\sqrt{\frac{1.00^2 + 0.50^2 - 2(-0.30)(1.00)(0.50)}{1 - (-0.30)^2}}\)?

    \(\sqrt{\frac{1.00 + 0.25 + 0.30}{1 - 0.09}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.55}{0.91}} \approx \sqrt{1.7033} \approx 1.305 \approx 1.31\).

  5. If the real exchange rate mean reversion parameter is \(\kappa = 0.15\) per annum, what is the half-life of PPP deviations in years: \(\ln(2) / 0.15\)?

    \(\ln(2) / 0.15 \approx 0.693147 / 0.15 \approx 4.62\) years.

  6. Why is FX Value an essential component in a currency portfolio despite its modest standalone Sharpe ratio (~0.32)?

    Options

    Value's negative correlation with momentum expands the multi-factor frontier and significantly lowers drawdowns during momentum and carry crashes.

  7. How does FX momentum behave during a sudden currency carry crash?

    Options

    Because momentum dynamically updates on past returns, it rapidly flips short falling currencies, serving as an adaptive crash hedge.

  8. If the multi-factor currency portfolio achieves a maximum drawdown of \(-14.8\%\), what is this drawdown in positive percentage points?

    The maximum drawdown is 14.8%.
