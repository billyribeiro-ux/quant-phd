FX Markets & Parity Conditions

Foreign exchange represents the largest financial market in the world, with over $7.5 trillion in daily turnover coordinated through over-the-counter dealer networks, continuous electronic brokerages, and algorithmic execution venues. This module establishes the structural foundations of international currency pricing and rigorously examines classical parity conditions. Across six intensive lessons, we analyze FX market microstructure, CLS settlement, and covered interest parity (Lesson 1); the post-2008 emergence of persistent non-zero cross-currency bases and regulatory balance-sheet constraints (Lesson 2); the empirical failure of uncovered interest parity across short horizons (Lesson 3); the forward-premium anomaly and Fama variance decompositions (Lesson 4); purchasing power parity, trade cost bands of inaction, and the 3-to-5-year half-life persistence puzzle (Lesson 5); and real exchange rate modeling culminating in the Meese–Rogoff random-walk forecasting benchmark (Lesson 6). The module concludes with a 18-hour empirical project re-running the Meese–Rogoff horse race with modern econometric discipline.

  • Prerequisites: E8 International Macro & the Dollar, and FI1 Fixed Income Fundamentals.
  • Unlocks: X2 Currency Risk Premia.
  • Project: Meese–Rogoff re-run: an autograded out-of-sample forecasting engine (meese_rogoff.py) evaluating random walk, fundamentals, and UIP models across horizons, accompanied by an analytical research memo (report.md).
  • Data: Embedded daily and monthly market series (fx_daily.csv, fx_monthly.csv) alongside deterministic simulated problem instances.

42 h lessons · 18 h project (30%)

Objectives

By the end of X1 you can:

  1. Analyze global FX market structure, vehicle currency tiering, two-tier OTC dealer networks, and settlement netting mechanics under Continuous Linked Settlement (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive covered interest parity (CIP), calculate forward points, and quantify cross-currency bases driven by intermediary balance-sheet constraints and the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (Lesson 2).
  3. Formulate the uncovered interest parity (UIP) condition and evaluate econometric regressions proving its systematic empirical breakdown over short and intermediate horizons (Lesson 3).
  4. Decompose the forward premium into expected currency depreciation and time-varying risk premia to prove Fama's necessary conditions for a negative regression slope (Lesson 4).
  5. Formulate absolute and relative purchasing power parity (PPP), estimate the half-life of real exchange rate deviations, and critique explanations for the PPP persistence puzzle (Lesson 5).
  6. Model real exchange rate dynamics and evaluate out-of-sample predictive accuracy against the Meese–Rogoff driftless random-walk benchmark (Lesson 6).
  7. Re-run the Meese–Rogoff forecasting horse race across multiple horizons, computing Theil's U and Diebold–Mariano test statistics under rolling-window real-time discipline (Project).

Prerequisites

X1 builds directly upon the open-economy macroeconomic foundations established in balance of payments accounting, uncovered interest parity, and covered interest parity from E8 International Macro & the Dollar. From E8, we take as established the forward-premium puzzle, the post-crisis cross-currency basis, the impossible trinity, and Dornbusch overshooting. From time series econometrics in S3, X1 applies autoregressive AR(1) estimation, unit root testing, and out-of-sample forecast evaluation. From linear regression in S2, X1 uses ordinary least squares (OLS), standard error formulas, and hypothesis testing.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on prerequisite concepts from E8, S2, and S3, and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under Covered Interest Parity (CIP, E8), if the 1-year US interest rate is 5.0%, the 1-year Eurozone interest rate is 3.0%, and the spot exchange rate is \(S = 1.0800\) USD per EUR, what is the theoretical 1-year forward rate \(F\)?

    Options

    By CIP, \(F = S \times \frac{1 + i_{\$}}{1 + i_{\text{EUR}}} = 1.0800 \times \frac{1.05}{1.03} \approx 1.10097 \approx 1.1010\).

  2. What does a negative cross-currency basis on EUR/USD (E8) indicate about international funding markets?

    Options

    A negative basis indicates that synthetic dollar borrowing via FX swaps requires paying a premium over cash dollar rates, reflecting balance-sheet constraints of global arbitrageurs (Du et al. 2018).

  3. In the standard Fama (1984) regression \(\Delta s_{t+1} = \alpha + \beta (f_t - s_t) + \epsilon_{t+1}\), what is the theoretical value of \(\beta\) under the joint null hypothesis of Uncovered Interest Parity and Rational Expectations?

    Under UIP and rational expectations, \(\E_t[\Delta s_{t+1}] = f_t - s_t\), which implies \(\alpha = 0\) and \(\beta = 1.0\).

  4. In Dornbusch's (1976) overshooting model (E8), why does the nominal exchange rate overshoot its long-run equilibrium following an unanticipated monetary expansion?

    Options

    Because goods prices adjust sluggishly, the monetary injection drives down nominal interest rates. To satisfy UIP, the currency must depreciate past its long-run level so that investors expect future appreciation.

  5. If the monthly persistence parameter of a real exchange rate AR(1) process is \(\rho = 0.98\), what is the estimated half-life of deviations in months (\(\text{half-life} = \ln(0.5)/\ln(\rho)\))?

    \(\text{half-life} = \frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln(0.98)} = \frac{-0.69315}{-0.02020} \approx 34.31\) months (approx 2.86 years).

  6. In time series forecasting (S3), what is Theil's U inequality coefficient comparing model forecast RMSE to a random walk benchmark RMSE?

    Options

    Theil's U is defined as \(\text{RMSE}_{\text{model}} / \text{RMSE}_{\text{RW}}\). A value greater than 1 indicates that the benchmark random walk achieves lower forecast error than the model.

  7. Skill check (Rung 1 · Project): An out-of-sample horse race evaluates 1-month-ahead forecasts. The structural fundamentals model achieves an out-of-sample \(\text{RMSE} = 0.0450\). The driftless random walk benchmark achieves \(\text{RMSE} = 0.0300\). What is Theil's U statistic for the structural model?

    \(\text{Theil's } U = \frac{\text{RMSE}_{\text{fund}}}{\text{RMSE}_{\text{RW}}} = \frac{0.0450}{0.0300} = 1.50\). Because \(U = 1.50 > 1.0\), the random walk outperforms the structural model by 33% lower RMSE.

  8. Skill check (Rung 2 · Project): In a Diebold–Mariano test of squared error loss differential \(d_t = e_{\text{model}, t}^2 - e_{\text{RW}, t}^2\), the sample mean loss differential is \(\bar{d} = 0.00050\) and the estimated long-run standard error of the mean is \(\sqrt{\hat{V}(\bar{d})} = 0.00020\). What is the Diebold–Mariano test statistic \(DM = \bar{d} / \sqrt{\hat{V}(\bar{d})}\)?

    \(DM = \frac{0.00050}{0.00020} = 2.50\). Because \(DM = 2.50 > 1.96\), the random walk significantly outperforms the model at the 5% significance level.

Project ladder

The module culminates in the Meese–Rogoff Re-Run project (18 hours), where you implement an econometric forecasting engine comparing random-walk forecasts against macroeconomic fundamentals and UIP models across 50 seeded instances.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example: Replay an out-of-sample forecasting horse race on sample currency and macroeconomic series, tracing rolling OLS estimations, forecast errors, Theil's U, and Diebold–Mariano statistics.
  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem: Complete the core forecast evaluation formulas for RMSE, MAE, Theil's U, and the Diebold–Mariano loss differential test.
  3. Rung 3 · Independent project: Code the complete forecasting module meese_rogoff.py satisfying the 50-seed autograder suite and author the research memo report.md evaluated by rubric.
  4. Rung 4 · Stretch: Extend the horse race to nonlinear threshold autoregressions (TAR) or machine-learning ensemble forecasts.
