Microstructure Theory

Financial markets do not clear frictionlessly through an invisible auctioneer. Instead, transactions occur at discrete prices quoted by intermediaries or submitted by competing participants who possess asymmetric information, face inventory financing limits, and incur order-handling expenses. This module establishes the structural foundations of market microstructure theory: the serial covariance properties of transaction prices under bid–ask bounce (Lesson 1), sequential Bayesian quote setting under adverse selection (Lesson 2), optimal dealer inventory control via dynamic programming (Lesson 3), econometric decomposition of bid–ask spreads into component costs (Lesson 4), batch auction equilibrium under continuous informed trading (Lesson 5), Kyle's price impact lambda (Lesson 6), maximum-likelihood estimation of the probability of informed trading (PIN, Lesson 7), and high-frequency order flow toxicity via volume-synchronised probability of informed trading (VPIN, Lesson 8). It culminates in a spread-decomposition project: an autograded empirical solver and a rigorous analytical report.

  • Prerequisites: E4 General Equilibrium & Information in Markets and S1 Statistical Inference (and their foundational concepts in E2, M3, M4, and M7).
  • Unlocks: MS2 Limit Order Books & Market Design and MS3 Market Impact & Execution Algorithms.
  • Project: Spread decomposition engine: an autograded solver (decompose.py) estimating Roll spreads and regression-based spread components, accompanied by a rubric-graded analytical report (report.md).
  • Data: No trade-level dataset is embedded for this module. The examples use generated trades labelled SIMULATED. A student may supply a trade file in bring-your-own-data mode.

64 h lessons · 26 h project (29%)

Objectives

By the end of MS1 you can:

  1. Estimate effective bid–ask spreads from transaction price changes using the serial covariance estimator under the Roll model assumptions (Lesson 1).
  2. Compute bid and ask quotes in a sequential trading specialist model conditional on trade signs under asymmetric information (Lesson 2).
  3. Formulate the dealer's dynamic inventory control problem as a Bellman equation with quadratic holding penalty and derive inventory-dependent quote shifts (Lesson 3).
  4. Decompose the effective half-spread into order-processing and adverse-selection components using regression on current and lagged trade signs (Lesson 4).
  5. Solve the single-period Kyle auction equilibrium for the informed trader's order coefficient and the competitive market maker's pricing rule (Lesson 5).
  6. Derive Kyle's lambda as the sensitivity of transaction price to total order flow and estimate price impact from trade flow regressions (Lesson 6).
  7. Formulate the mixture likelihood for trade arrivals across information events and estimate the probability of informed trading (PIN) by maximum likelihood (Lesson 7).
  8. Construct volume-clock buckets, classify trading volume using bulk volume classification (BVC), and compute volume-synchronised probability of informed trading (VPIN) (Lesson 8).

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on the prerequisites, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. For eight items that means seven correct: six of eight is 75 percent, and seven of eight is 87.5 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. A result below the bar means that rungs 1 and 2 provide valuable preparation.

  1. Trade signs \(q_t \in \{-1, +1\}\) are independent and take each value with probability \(1/2\). What is their expected value \(\E[q_t]\)?

    \(\E[q_t] = (+1)(1/2) + (-1)(1/2) = 0\). This expectation is foundational to the Roll model and spread decomposition.

  2. Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be random variables with zero means: \(\E[X] = 0\) and \(\E[Y] = 0\). If \(\E[XY] = -0.04\), what is their covariance \(\Cov(X, Y)\)?

    By definition, \(\Cov(X, Y) = \E[XY] - \E[X]\E[Y] = -0.04 - (0)(0) = -0.04\).

  3. Under Bayes' rule (S1), what is the relationship between posterior probability \(P(H \mid E)\), prior probability \(P(H)\), likelihood \(P(E \mid H)\), and marginal likelihood \(P(E)\)?

    Options

    Bayes' rule states that the posterior equals the likelihood times prior divided by the marginal likelihood of evidence.

  4. In information economics (E2), why does adverse selection arise when a market maker trades against counterparties who may possess private information?

    Options

    Adverse selection arises because informed traders buy when the fundamental value exceeds the ask and sell when it is below the bid, forcing the market maker to lose on informed trades.

  5. In a no-intercept ordinary least squares regression \(Y = \beta X + \epsilon\) (M3), if \(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i Y_i = 12\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 = 24\), what is the estimated slope \(\hat{\beta}\)?

    The normal equation without intercept gives \(\hat{\beta} = \sum X_i Y_i / \sum X_i^2 = 12 / 24 = 0.5\).

  6. In discrete-time dynamic programming (M7), what fundamental property is established by the principle of optimality?

    Options

    Bellman's principle of optimality states that tail subproblems of an optimal policy must themselves be optimal given the intermediate state.

  7. Skill check (rung 1): In the spread decomposition model \(\Delta p_t = (\theta + c) q_t - c q_{t-1} + \eta_t\), if the no-intercept OLS estimates are \(b_0 = 0.25\) and \(b_1 = -0.15\), what is the estimated adverse-selection price impact parameter \(\theta\)?

    Options

    Since \(b_0 = \theta + c\) and \(b_1 = -c\), adding the two coefficients yields \(b_0 + b_1 = (\theta + c) - c = \theta = 0.25 - 0.15 = 0.10\).

  8. Skill check (rung 2): In the spread decomposition regression \(\Delta p_t = b_0 q_t + b_1 q_{t-1} + \eta_t\), how is the order-processing half-spread \(c\) recovered from the lagged trade sign coefficient \(b_1\)?

    Options

    The structural model predicts coefficient \(b_1 = -c\), so the order-processing half-spread is recovered as \(c = -b_1\).

Lessons

Eight lessons of eight study hours each, totaling 64 hours. Lessons 1 to 4 examine transaction prices, sequential trading, inventory control, and empirical spread decomposition. Lessons 5 and 6 develop Kyle's continuous auction equilibrium and price impact lambda. Lessons 7 and 8 address flow-based measures of informed trading: PIN and high-frequency VPIN. Each lesson features rigorous mathematical definitions, analytical proofs, self-contained Python labs, misconceptions, problem sets with complete solutions, key-results tables, and retrieval quizzes.

L01 · 8 hRoll spread estimatorBid–ask bounce, serial covariance of transaction price changes, the Roll estimator formula, and the failure of the estimator under adverse selection. L02 · 8 hGlosten–Milgrom modelSequential Bayesian quote setting with informed and uninformed traders, zero-expected-profit conditions, and exact rational belief updating. L03 · 8 hInventory modelsDynamic inventory control with quadratic penalty, Bellman backward recursion, and inventory-dependent quote shading. L04 · 8 hSpread decompositionSeparating the effective spread into order processing and adverse selection via trade-sign regression identities. L05 · 8 hKyle modelSingle-period continuous auction equilibrium, linear pricing by market makers, optimal insider order submission, and information aggregation. L06 · 8 hKyle's lambdaKyle's lambda as the price sensitivity to order flow, least-squares projection, market depth, and distinction from sign-based impact. L07 · 8 hProbability of informed tradingPoisson arrival models for information events and order flows, the 3-state mixture likelihood, and maximum likelihood estimation of PIN. L08 · 8 hVPINVolume clocks, bulk volume classification (BVC), volume-synchronised probability of informed trading, and the Flash Crash debate.

The project ladder

The module concludes with the spread decomposition project (26 hours). Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the core regression techniques tested in the placement diagnostic. Learners achieving placement mastery may proceed directly to rung 3.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved, step-by-step implementation of the spread decomposition solver on a SIMULATED trade sequence, with annotations for every mathematical operation.

  2. Completion problem

    Complete the structural recovery line for the order-processing half-spread from the lagged sign regression coefficient, verified immediately in the browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the full decompose_trades interface in decompose.py to satisfy the autograder suite across 50 seeds, and compose an analytical research memo (report.md) evaluated by rubric.

  4. Stretch

    Investigate the asymptotic bias of the Roll estimator and regression decomposition under stochastic sign misclassification and persistent trade flows.

Data status

No real trade-level data is embedded for MS1. The examples, tests, and lab simulations operate on synthetically generated order streams labelled SIMULATED under explicit reproducible random seeds. Students conducting empirical extensions may supply their own tick data formatted as time,price,sign in bring-your-own-data mode.
