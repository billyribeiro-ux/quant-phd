Can we estimate the prevalence of insider trading in a stock using only daily counts of buy and sell transactions? David Easley, Nicholas Kiefer, Maureen O'Hara, and Joseph Paperman showed that a structural mixture model identifies the arrival rates of informed and uninformed traders. The resulting metric, the Probability of Informed Trading (PIN), measures the unconditional probability that an incoming order originates from a trader with private information. This lesson derives the PIN arrival likelihood, formulates its maximum likelihood estimation, and evaluates its structural limitations.

1The daily trade arrival process

Definition 7.1 (Poisson arrival parameters and day types)

Trading takes place across discrete days \(d \in \{1, \ldots, D\}\). On each trading day:

  • An information event occurs with probability \(\alpha \in [0, 1]\). With probability \(1 - \alpha\), no event occurs.
  • Conditional on an event occurring, the signal is bad news with probability \(\delta \in [0, 1]\) and good news with probability \(1 - \delta\).
  • Uninformed buy orders arrive according to a Poisson distribution with daily intensity \(\varepsilon_b > 0\). Uninformed sell orders arrive with intensity \(\varepsilon_s > 0\).
  • Informed traders arrive only when an event occurs, following an independent Poisson process with intensity \(\mu > 0\). They submit exclusively buy orders on good news days and sell orders on bad news days.
Arrival processes are mutually independent conditional on the day's information event state.

Assumption A1 (Poisson conditional independence)

For any non-negative integer count \(k \ge 0\) and rate \(\lambda > 0\), the Poisson probability mass function is: \[ f(k \mid \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}. \] Conditional on the unobserved day type, buy arrivals \(B_d\) and sell arrivals \(S_d\) are independent Poisson random variables.

2Mixture likelihood and the PIN formula

Theorem 7.2 (daily joint likelihood)

Under Assumption A1, the unconditional joint probability of observing \(B\) buys and \(S\) sells on a given trading day is the three-state mixture: \[ \begin{aligned} L(B, S \mid \alpha, \delta, \mu, \varepsilon_b, \varepsilon_s) &= (1 - \alpha)\, \frac{e^{-\varepsilon_b} \varepsilon_b^B}{B!}\, \frac{e^{-\varepsilon_s} \varepsilon_s^S}{S!} \\ &\quad + \alpha(1 - \delta)\, \frac{e^{-(\varepsilon_b + \mu)} (\varepsilon_b + \mu)^B}{B!}\, \frac{e^{-\varepsilon_s} \varepsilon_s^S}{S!} \\ &\quad + \alpha \delta\, \frac{e^{-\varepsilon_b} \varepsilon_b^B}{B!}\, \frac{e^{-(\varepsilon_s + \mu)} (\varepsilon_s + \mu)^S}{S!}. \end{aligned} \] The sum of \(L(B, S)\) over all \((B, S) \in \mathbb{N}_0 \times \mathbb{N}_0\) equals exactly 1.

Proof

By the law of total probability, conditioning on the mutually exclusive and exhaustive event states: \[ \begin{aligned} \Prob(B, S) &= \Prob(\text{no event}) \Prob(B, S \mid \text{no event}) \\ &\quad + \Prob(\text{good news}) \Prob(B, S \mid \text{good news}) \\ &\quad + \Prob(\text{bad news}) \Prob(B, S \mid \text{bad news}). \end{aligned} \] Under no event (prob \(1 - \alpha\)), arrivals are uninformed: \(B \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_b)\) and \(S \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_s)\). Under good news (prob \(\alpha(1-\delta)\)), informed traders buy: \(B \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_b + \mu)\) and \(S \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_s)\). Under bad news (prob \(\alpha \delta\)), informed traders sell: \(B \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_b)\) and \(S \sim \text{Poisson}(\varepsilon_s + \mu)\). Conditional independence establishes the product form for each branch. Summing each Poisson product over all non-negative integer pairs \((B, S)\) yields 1, so the weighted mixture sums to \((1 - \alpha)(1) + \alpha(1 - \delta)(1) + \alpha \delta(1) = 1 - \alpha + \alpha = 1\).

Theorem 7.3 (Probability of Informed Trading formula)

The probability of informed trading (PIN), defined as the ratio of expected informed order arrivals to total expected order arrivals on a randomly chosen trading day, satisfies: \[ \operatorname{PIN} = \frac{\alpha\, \mu}{\alpha\, \mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s}. \] PIN is bounded in \([0, 1)\) for any finite \(\varepsilon_b, \varepsilon_s > 0\).

Proof

Evaluate expected daily informed orders: on no-event days (prob \(1-\alpha\)), informed volume is 0. On good news days (prob \(\alpha(1-\delta)\)), expected informed volume is \(\mu\). On bad news days (prob \(\alpha \delta\)), expected informed volume is \(\mu\). Hence: \[ \E[\text{Informed Orders}] = 0 \cdot (1 - \alpha) + \mu\, \alpha(1 - \delta) + \mu\, \alpha \delta = \alpha\, \mu. \] Uninformed orders arrive at constant expected rate \(\varepsilon_b\) buys and \(\varepsilon_s\) sells regardless of event state: \[ \E[\text{Uninformed Orders}] = \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s. \] Total expected orders are \(\E[\text{Total}] = \alpha \mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s\). Taking the ratio yields \(\operatorname{PIN} = \frac{\alpha \mu}{\alpha \mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s}\). Since \(\alpha \in [0, 1]\), \(\mu \ge 0\), and \(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s > 0\), the ratio lies strictly in \([0, 1)\).

Example 7.4 (PIN evaluation from parameter estimates)

Let event probability \(\alpha = 0.30\), informed intensity \(\mu = 15.0\), and uninformed intensities \(\varepsilon_b = \varepsilon_s = 10.0\). Expected daily informed arrivals are \(\alpha \mu = 0.30 \times 15.0 = 4.5\). Expected daily uninformed arrivals are \(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s = 10.0 + 10.0 = 20.0\). The probability of informed trading is: \[ \operatorname{PIN} = \frac{4.5}{4.5 + 20.0} = \frac{4.5}{24.5} = \frac{9}{49} \approx 0.18367. \] Approximately 18.37% of incoming orders originate from informed traders. Lab 1 recovers this value via grid maximum likelihood.

Remark 7.5 (title citation of Easley et al. 1996)

The PIN model was introduced by David Easley, Nicholas Kiefer, Maureen O'Hara, and Joseph Paperman in "Liquidity, Information, and Infrequently Traded Stocks". They used trade counts to demonstrate that infrequently traded stocks face wider spreads primarily due to higher probabilities of informed trading.

3Estimation and computational verification

Lab 1 · PIN mixture likelihood and grid search estimation

SIMULATED: mixture likelihood evaluated over truncated support [0, 59] x [0, 59] sums to 1.00000000. Grid search on 200 simulated days recovers true parameters alpha = 0.30, mu = 15.0, and PIN = 0.1837.

"""Lab MS1-L07-1: Probability of informed trading (PIN) and mixture likelihood.

SIMULATED: generates daily trade arrival counts from the Easley et al. (1996) mixture model.
Verifies that the mixture likelihood sums to unity over a truncated support,
and performs grid maximum-likelihood estimation of the event probability and informed arrival rate.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def poisson_pmf(k: int, lam: float) -> float:
    """Compute Poisson probability mass function at k with arrival rate lam."""
    if k < 0:
        return 0.0
    return math.exp(-lam + k * math.log(lam) - math.lgamma(k + 1))


def pin_day_pmf(b: int, s: int, alpha: float, delta: float, mu: float, eps_b: float, eps_s: float) -> float:
    """Evaluate mixture joint probability mass function of (B, S) arrivals on one trading day."""
    p_no_event = (1.0 - alpha) * poisson_pmf(b, eps_b) * poisson_pmf(s, eps_s)
    p_good_news = alpha * (1.0 - delta) * poisson_pmf(b, eps_b + mu) * poisson_pmf(s, eps_s)
    p_bad_news = alpha * delta * poisson_pmf(b, eps_b) * poisson_pmf(s, eps_s + mu)
    return p_no_event + p_good_news + p_bad_news


def check_truncated_sum(
    alpha: float, delta: float, mu: float, eps_b: float, eps_s: float, max_count: int = 60
) -> float:
    """Verify that the discrete joint probability sums to 1 over truncated support."""
    total = 0.0
    for b in range(max_count):
        for s in range(max_count):
            total += pin_day_pmf(b, s, alpha, delta, mu, eps_b, eps_s)
    return total


def simulate_pin_days(
    n_days: int,
    alpha: float,
    delta: float,
    mu: float,
    eps_b: float,
    eps_s: float,
    seed: int = 42,
) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """Simulate daily buy and sell counts under the PIN arrival structure."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)

    def sample_poisson(lam: float) -> int:
        l_val = math.exp(-lam)
        k = 0
        p = 1.0
        while p > l_val:
            k += 1
            p *= rng.random()
        return k - 1

    days: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    for _ in range(n_days):
        has_event = rng.random() < alpha
        if not has_event:
            rate_b = eps_b
            rate_s = eps_s
        else:
            is_bad = rng.random() < delta
            if is_bad:
                rate_b = eps_b
                rate_s = eps_s + mu
            else:
                rate_b = eps_b + mu
                rate_s = eps_s
        days.append((sample_poisson(rate_b), sample_poisson(rate_s)))
    return days


def grid_search_pin(
    days: list[tuple[int, int]],
    delta_known: float,
    eps_b_known: float,
    eps_s_known: float,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Perform grid search over (alpha, mu) to maximize log-likelihood."""
    best_ll = -1e18
    best_alpha = 0.0
    best_mu = 0.0

    # Grid specifications: alpha in [0.10, 0.50] step 0.02; mu in [8.0, 22.0] step 1.0
    for a_idx in range(21):
        a = 0.10 + a_idx * 0.02
        for m_idx in range(15):
            m = 8.0 + m_idx * 1.0
            ll = 0.0
            for b, s in days:
                prob = pin_day_pmf(b, s, a, delta_known, m, eps_b_known, eps_s_known)
                ll += math.log(max(prob, 1e-30))
            if ll > best_ll:
                best_ll = ll
                best_alpha = a
                best_mu = m

    pin_est = (best_alpha * best_mu) / (best_alpha * best_mu + eps_b_known + eps_s_known)
    return best_alpha, best_mu, pin_est


def main() -> None:
    alpha_true = 0.30
    delta_true = 0.40
    mu_true = 15.0
    eps_b_true = 10.0
    eps_s_true = 10.0
    pin_true = (alpha_true * mu_true) / (alpha_true * mu_true + eps_b_true + eps_s_true)

    # 1. Truncated support check
    max_k = 60
    trunc_sum = check_truncated_sum(alpha_true, delta_true, mu_true, eps_b_true, eps_s_true, max_k)
    print(f"Truncated support sum [0, {max_k - 1}] x [0, {max_k - 1}]: {trunc_sum:.8f}")
    print(f"Truncation deficit from 1.0: {abs(1.0 - trunc_sum):.2e}")

    # 2. Simulate 200 days of trade counts
    n_days = 200
    days = simulate_pin_days(n_days, alpha_true, delta_true, mu_true, eps_b_true, eps_s_true, seed=42)

    # 3. Grid search
    best_alpha, best_mu, pin_est = grid_search_pin(days, delta_true, eps_b_true, eps_s_true)

    print(f"Sample size: {n_days} trading days")
    print(f"True parameters:      alpha = {alpha_true:.2f}, mu = {mu_true:.1f}, PIN = {pin_true:.4f}")
    print(f"Grid search estimate: alpha = {best_alpha:.2f}, mu = {best_mu:.1f}, PIN = {pin_est:.4f}")
    print(f"PIN estimation error: {abs(pin_est - pin_true):.4f}")


main()

Truncation deficit from 1.0: 6.34e-10
Sample size: 200 trading days
True parameters:      alpha = 0.30, mu = 15.0, PIN = 0.1837
Grid search estimate: alpha = 0.30, mu = 15.0, PIN = 0.1837
PIN estimation error: 0.0000

The mixture joint PMF integrates to 1.00000000 over the truncated support with truncation deficit under 1e-10. Grid maximum likelihood on 200 trading days recovers alpha = 0.30, mu = 15.0, and PIN = 0.1837 without estimation error.

4Where the canon breaks

Limit 7.1 (constant Poisson intensity assumption)

The assumption of constant Poisson arrival intensities throughout the trading day ignores well-documented intraday seasonalities, including U-shaped volume curves, opening/closing auctions, and order clustering. When order arrivals are serially dependent, Poisson likelihoods overstate estimation precision.

Limit 7.2 (numerical underflow and parameter identification)

For actively traded securities with thousands of daily transactions, evaluating \(\varepsilon^B / B!\) causes floating-point underflow. Numerical factorization (e.g. log-likelihood scaling as in Lin and Ke, 2011) is required. Furthermore, when \(\alpha \approx 0\) or \(\mu\) is small, the mixture components become collinear, causing identification failure.

5Misconceptions

  • Wrong: A high PIN indicates that market makers are losing money.

    Why it is wrong: Market makers who correctly anticipate high PIN set wider bid–ask spreads as in the Glosten–Milgrom model, protecting themselves from adverse selection and maintaining zero or positive expected profits.

  • Wrong: PIN is equal to the probability of an information event \(\alpha\).

    Why it is wrong: \(\alpha\) is the probability that an information event occurs on a given day. PIN is the probability that an individual transaction is informed, which depends also on the arrival rates \(\mu\) and \(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s\).

  • Wrong: PIN can be estimated accurately from price changes alone without trade counts.

    Why it is wrong: PIN is fundamentally an order-flow mixture model identified from the joint dispersion and skewness of daily buy and sell transaction frequencies, not price increments.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · PIN calculation from arrival rates

A stock has estimated parameters \(\alpha = 0.25\), \(\delta = 0.50\), \(\mu = 40.0\), \(\varepsilon_b = 30.0\), and \(\varepsilon_s = 30.0\). Calculate PIN.

Solution

Expected informed arrivals: \(\alpha \mu = 0.25 \times 40.0 = 10.0\). Uninformed arrivals: \(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s = 30.0 + 30.0 = 60.0\). Total expected arrivals: \(10.0 + 60.0 = 70.0\). Then \(\operatorname{PIN} = \frac{10.0}{70.0} = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.1429\) (14.29%).

M2 · Expected order counts by day type

Using the parameters from M1, compute expected daily buys and sells under each of the three states: no event, good news, and bad news.

Solution

No event: \(\E[B] = \varepsilon_b = 30\), \(\E[S] = \varepsilon_s = 30\). Good news: \(\E[B] = \varepsilon_b + \mu = 30 + 40 = 70\), \(\E[S] = \varepsilon_s = 30\). Bad news: \(\E[B] = \varepsilon_b = 30\), \(\E[S] = \varepsilon_s + \mu = 30 + 40 = 70\). Buy-sell order imbalances signal the underlying information state.

M3 · Sensitivity of PIN to uninformed volume

If uninformed retail volume \(\varepsilon_b = \varepsilon_s\) doubles from 10.0 to 20.0 with \(\alpha = 0.30\) and \(\mu = 15.0\), what is the new PIN?

Solution

Initial PIN was \(4.5 / 24.5 \approx 0.1837\). With doubled uninformed volume, \(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s = 40.0\). The new PIN is \(\frac{4.5}{4.5 + 40.0} = \frac{4.5}{44.5} = \frac{9}{89} \approx 0.1011\). Doubling noise trading cuts PIN by approximately 45%.

Proof

P1 · Monotonicity of PIN in model parameters

Prove that \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \alpha} > 0\), \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \mu} > 0\), and \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \varepsilon_b} < 0\).

Solution

Let \(k = \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s > 0\). Then \(\operatorname{PIN} = \frac{\alpha \mu}{\alpha \mu + k}\). Let \(z = \alpha \mu > 0\). The function \(g(z) = \frac{z}{z + k}\) has derivative \(g'(z) = \frac{k}{(z+k)^2} > 0\). Since \(z\) is strictly increasing in \(\alpha\) and \(\mu\), by the chain rule \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \alpha} = \mu g'(z) > 0\) and \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \mu} = \alpha g'(z) > 0\). Differentiating with respect to \(\varepsilon_b\): \(\frac{\partial \operatorname{PIN}}{\partial \varepsilon_b} = -\frac{\alpha \mu}{(\alpha \mu + k)^2} < 0\).

P2 · Identification from second moments of order flow

Show how the variance of daily order imbalance \(\Var(B_d - S_d)\) identifies the presence of informed trading.

Solution

Under no informed trading (\(\alpha = 0\) or \(\mu = 0\)), \(B\) and \(S\) are independent Poisson with \(\Var(B - S) = \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s\). When informed trading is present, by the law of total variance: \(\Var(B - S) = \E[\Var(B - S \mid \text{state})] + \Var(\E[B - S \mid \text{state}]) = (\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s + \alpha \mu) + \Var(\E[B - S \mid \text{state}])\). The conditional expectation \(\E[B - S]\) is \(0\) (prob \(1-\alpha\)), \(+\mu\) (prob \(\alpha(1-\delta)\)), and \(-\mu\) (prob \(\alpha \delta\)), adding variance of order \(\alpha \mu^2\). Excess dispersion in daily imbalances identifies \(\alpha\) and \(\mu\).

P3 · Maximum likelihood score equations

Derive the first-order condition for log-likelihood maximization with respect to \(\alpha\).

Solution

The sample log-likelihood is \(\ell(\Theta) = \sum_{d=1}^D \ln L(B_d, S_d \mid \Theta)\). Differentiating with respect to \(\alpha\): \(\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \alpha} = \sum_{d=1}^D \frac{1}{L_d} \frac{\partial L_d}{\partial \alpha}\). From Theorem 7.2, \(\frac{\partial L_d}{\partial \alpha} = -f(B_d \mid \varepsilon_b)f(S_d \mid \varepsilon_s) + (1-\delta)f(B_d \mid \varepsilon_b+\mu)f(S_d \mid \varepsilon_s) + \delta f(B_d \mid \varepsilon_b)f(S_d \mid \varepsilon_s+\mu)\). Setting the score sum to zero yields the MLE condition.

E2 · Truncated support precision

What was the printed sum of the mixture joint PMF over the truncated support \([0, 59] \times [0, 59]\) in Lab 1?

Solution

Lab 1 printed a truncated support sum of 1.00000000, with a truncation deficit of under 1e-10. This proves that bounding support at count 60 captures essentially 100% of the discrete probability mass when arrival rates do not exceed 25.

E3 · Effect of sample days D on estimation standard error

Under maximum likelihood estimation regularity conditions, how does the standard error of \(\widehat{\operatorname{PIN}}\) scale with sample length \(D\)?

Solution

By asymptotic normality of maximum likelihood estimators, \(\sqrt{D}(\hat{\Theta} - \Theta_0) \xrightarrow{d} \Normal(0, \mathcal{I}^{-1})\). Applying the delta method to the scalar function \(g(\Theta) = \frac{\alpha \mu}{\alpha \mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s}\), the standard error of \(\widehat{\operatorname{PIN}}\) scales as \(O(1/\sqrt{D})\). Increasing sample length from 200 to 800 days halves standard error.

Research

R1 · Trade classification error in empirical PIN

Empirical PIN requires classifying trades as buys or sells using the Lee-Ready algorithm. How does classification error bias estimated PIN?

Solution

Misclassifying buys as sells and vice versa attenuates order imbalances \((B_d - S_d)\) on informed days toward zero. The likelihood attributes the dampened imbalance to smaller \(\mu\) or lower \(\alpha\), creating downward bias in estimated PIN.

R2 · Multi-day news event clustering

If private information events persist across multiple consecutive trading days, how does this violate Assumption A1?

Solution

Assumption A1 assumes events are i.i.d. Bernoulli draws across days. If events cluster over multiple days, day types follow a Markov switching process, requiring a hidden Markov model (HMM) formulation rather than independent day-by-day mixtures.

R3 · PIN as an asset pricing factor

Easley, Hvidkjaer, and O'Hara (2002) argue that PIN is a priced risk factor commanding an expected return premium. What is the economic rationale?

Solution

Investors holding stocks with high PIN face persistent adverse selection when rebalancing. Because information risk cannot be completely diversified away when informed traders possess systematic private signals, uninformed investors demand higher expected returns to hold high-PIN assets.

8Retrieval quiz

  1. In Example 7.4 (\(\alpha = 0.30, \mu = 15.0, \varepsilon_b = \varepsilon_s = 10.0\)), what is the Probability of Informed Trading (PIN)?

    \(\operatorname{PIN} = \frac{0.30 \times 15.0}{0.30 \times 15.0 + 10.0 + 10.0} = \frac{4.5}{24.5} \approx 0.1837\).

  2. What probability distribution governs trade arrivals within each day type in the PIN model?

    Options

    By Definition 7.1 and Assumption A1, arrivals are modeled as independent Poisson count processes.

  3. How many distinct information event states does the daily PIN mixture likelihood incorporate?

    Three states: no event (prob \(1-\alpha\)), good news (prob \(\alpha(1-\delta)\)), and bad news (prob \(\alpha \delta\)).

  4. What happens to PIN as uninformed noise trading volume (\(\varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s\)) increases toward infinity?

    Options

    \(\operatorname{PIN} = \frac{\alpha \mu}{\alpha \mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s}\). As the denominator grows without bound, PIN approaches 0.

  5. If \(\alpha = 0.30\) and \(\mu = 15.0\), what is the expected number of informed trades arriving on a randomly chosen day?

    \(\E[\text{Informed}] = \alpha \mu = 0.30 \times 15.0 = 4.5\) trades.

  6. On a bad news trading day, what are the expected numbers of buy and sell orders?

    Options

    On bad news days, informed traders sell, adding \(\mu\) to the sell arrival rate while buys remain at uninformed rate \(\varepsilon_b\).

  7. What does the daily mixture joint probability mass function \(L(B, S)\) sum to over all non-negative integer pairs \((B, S)\)?

    By Theorem 7.2, the mixture PMF is a valid probability distribution that sums to exactly 1.0.

  8. Which data inputs are required to estimate the PIN model via maximum likelihood?

    Options

    The PIN likelihood takes daily buy and sell trade counts \((B_d, S_d)\) as its data inputs.
