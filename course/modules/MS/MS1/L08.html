VPIN and Its Dispute

In modern high-frequency electronic markets, trades execute in milliseconds rather than discrete days. To adapt structural informed trading measures to continuous time, Easley, López de Prado, and O'Hara introduced Volume-Synchronized Probability of Informed Trading (VPIN). VPIN samples the market in volume time and measures order flow toxicity. However, whether VPIN reliably forewarns of liquidity crashes ignited a major academic dispute. This lesson formulates VPIN mathematically, implements its volume-bucket classification, and examines both sides of the published dispute surrounding the 2010 Flash Crash.

1Volume clock and bulk volume classification

Definition 8.1 (volume buckets)

Instead of calendar time, trading is partitioned on a volume clock into discrete volume buckets \(\tau \in \{1, \ldots, M\}\), each containing a constant predetermined quantity of shares \(V > 0\). If a transaction exceeds the remaining capacity of bucket \(\tau\), the excess volume spills into bucket \(\tau + 1\).

Definition 8.2 (bulk volume classification)

Because tick-level signed trade classifications are computationally intensive and prone to timestamp reporting errors at microsecond scales, bulk volume classification (BVC) splits the total bucket volume \(V\) into buy volume \(V_\tau^B\) and sell volume \(V_\tau^S\) using the standardized price change across bucket \(\tau\): \[ V_\tau^B = V \cdot \Phi\left(\frac{\Delta p_\tau}{\sigma}\right), \quad V_\tau^S = V - V_\tau^B = V \cdot \left[1 - \Phi\left(\frac{\Delta p_\tau}{\sigma}\right)\right], \] where \(\Delta p_\tau = p_\tau - p_{\tau-1}\) is the price change over bucket \(\tau\), \(\sigma > 0\) is the price change standard deviation, and \(\Phi(\cdot)\) is the standard normal distribution cumulative distribution function.

Assumption A1 (volume conservation and symmetry)

In every volume bucket \(\tau\), total volume is preserved: \(V_\tau^B + V_\tau^S = V\). By symmetry of the normal CDF \(\Phi(-z) = 1 - \Phi(z)\), zero price change (\(\Delta p_\tau = 0\)) assigns equal volumes \(V_\tau^B = V_\tau^S = V/2\).

2VPIN definition and mathematical bounds

Theorem 8.3 (VPIN formula and bounds)

Under Assumption A1, the VPIN (Volume-Synchronized Probability of Informed Trading) over a rolling window of \(N \ge 1\) volume buckets is: \[ \operatorname{VPIN} = \frac{\sum_{\tau=1}^N |V_\tau^B - V_\tau^S|}{N \cdot V} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\tau=1}^N \left|2\, \Phi\left(\frac{\Delta p_\tau}{\sigma}\right) - 1\right|. \] VPIN is bounded strictly in \([0, 1]\). When every bucket has zero price change, \(\operatorname{VPIN} = 0\); when price changes are infinitely large in absolute value, \(\operatorname{VPIN} \to 1\).

Proof

In each bucket \(\tau\), substitute \(V_\tau^B = V \Phi(z_\tau)\) and \(V_\tau^S = V(1 - \Phi(z_\tau))\): \[ |V_\tau^B - V_\tau^S| = |V \Phi(z_\tau) - V(1 - \Phi(z_\tau))| = V |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|. \] Summing over \(N\) buckets and dividing by \(N \cdot V\): \[ \operatorname{VPIN} = \frac{V \sum_{\tau=1}^N |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|}{N \cdot V} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\tau=1}^N |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|. \] Because \(\Phi(z) \in [0, 1]\) for all \(z \in \R\), \(2\Phi(z) - 1 \in [-1, 1]\), so its absolute value satisfies: \[ 0 \le |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1| \le 1. \] Averaging \(N\) terms bounded in \([0, 1]\) preserves the bounds: \[ 0 \le \operatorname{VPIN} \le 1. \] If \(\Delta p_\tau = 0\) for all \(\tau\), \(\Phi(0) = 0.5 \implies |2(0.5) - 1| = 0 \implies \operatorname{VPIN} = 0\). If \(|\Delta p_\tau| \to \infty\), \(\Phi(z_\tau) \to \{0, 1\} \implies |2(1) - 1| = 1 \implies \operatorname{VPIN} \to 1\).

Example 8.4 (evaluating VPIN on simulated buckets)

Let bucket volume \(V = 100\), rolling window \(N = 10\), and BVC scale \(\sigma = 0.30\). During tranquil periods with small price changes (\(\Delta p \approx 0\)), order imbalances are small (\(|2\Phi(z) - 1| \approx 0.18\)), producing a low baseline VPIN around \(0.18\). When a directional price shock arrives (\(\Delta p \approx -0.80\)), \(z = -0.80 / 0.30 \approx -2.67\), so \(\Phi(-2.67) \approx 0.0038\), creating an order imbalance near \(|2(0.0038) - 1| = 0.9924\). As these toxic buckets enter the rolling window, VPIN surges to over \(0.75\), as verified in Lab 1.

3The Flash Crash and the published academic dispute

Remark 8.5 (the case for VPIN as toxicity warning)

David Easley, Marcos López de Prado, and Maureen O'Hara introduced VPIN in "Flow Toxicity and Liquidity in a High-frequency World". They argued that VPIN measures the toxicity of order flow faced by market makers. They reported that VPIN reached historically extreme levels hours prior to the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash in E-mini S&P 500 futures, presenting VPIN as an early warning metric for market fragility.

Remark 8.6 (the critique: volatility confound and false positives)

Torben Andersen and Oleg Bondarenko contested these findings in "VPIN and the flash crash". Analyzing the same Flash Crash period, they showed that:

  • VPIN's predictive power is confounded by contemporaneous return volatility: because BVC maps price changes into volume imbalances through \(\Phi(\Delta p / \sigma)\), high VPIN primarily proxies for elevated return volatility rather than toxic informed order flow per se.
  • Controlling for volatility eliminates VPIN's incremental forecast ability.
  • VPIN generates frequent false positive spikes during normal volatile trading days without subsequent liquidity failures.

4Empirical implementation and bounds check

Lab 1 · Volume-bucket VPIN computation and regime change

SIMULATED: 30 volume buckets with tranquil periods and an order imbalance shock during buckets 12-18. Expected: rolling VPIN is bounded in [0, 1], rises from baseline 0.18 to over 0.70 during the shock, and declines thereafter.

"""Lab MS1-L08-1: Volume-synchronised probability of informed trading (VPIN).

SIMULATED: generates volume buckets and price changes with a regime shift in order imbalance.
Implements bulk volume classification (BVC) with normal CDF and rolling-window VPIN computation,
verifying that VPIN remains bounded in [0, 1].
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random


def normal_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    """Compute standard normal cumulative distribution function."""
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def bulk_volume_classification(price_change: float, sigma: float, bucket_vol: float) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Partition bucket volume into buy and sell volume using normal CDF."""
    z = price_change / sigma if sigma > 0 else 0.0
    v_buy = bucket_vol * normal_cdf(z)
    v_sell = bucket_vol - v_buy
    imbalance = abs(v_buy - v_sell)
    return v_buy, v_sell, imbalance


def compute_vpin_series(
    price_changes: list[float], sigma: float, bucket_vol: float, window_size: int
) -> list[tuple[int, float]]:
    """Compute rolling window VPIN over consecutive volume buckets."""
    imbalances: list[float] = []
    for dp in price_changes:
        _, _, imb = bulk_volume_classification(dp, sigma, bucket_vol)
        imbalances.append(imb)

    vpin_values: list[tuple[int, float]] = []
    for i in range(window_size - 1, len(price_changes)):
        window_imb = sum(imbalances[i - window_size + 1 : i + 1])
        vpin = window_imb / (window_size * bucket_vol)
        vpin_values.append((i + 1, vpin))
    return vpin_values


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(42)
    n_buckets = 30
    bucket_vol = 100.0
    window_size = 10
    sigma = 0.30

    # Simulate price changes: tranquil period with zero mean, then directional shock (buckets 12-18)
    price_changes: list[float] = []
    for i in range(n_buckets):
        if 12 <= i <= 18:
            price_changes.append(rng.gauss(-0.80, 0.40))
        else:
            price_changes.append(rng.gauss(0.0, 0.20))

    vpin_results = compute_vpin_series(price_changes, sigma, bucket_vol, window_size)

    print("VPIN Estimation across 30 Volume Buckets:")
    print(f"  Bucket volume V: {bucket_vol:.1f}")
    print(f"  Rolling window size N: {window_size} buckets")
    print(f"  Bulk volume classification scale sigma: {sigma:.2f}")
    print("Selected Rolling VPIN Values:")
    for b_idx, vpin in vpin_results:
        if b_idx in (10, 15, 19, 22, 26, 30):
            print(f"  End of Bucket {b_idx:2d}: VPIN = {vpin:.4f}")

    min_vpin = min(v for _, v in vpin_results)
    max_vpin = max(v for _, v in vpin_results)
    bounds_satisfied = (min_vpin >= 0.0) and (max_vpin <= 1.0)
    print("Bounds and Toxicity Checks:")
    print(f"  Minimum rolling VPIN: {min_vpin:.4f}")
    print(f"  Maximum rolling VPIN: {max_vpin:.4f}")
    print(f"  Mathematical bounds 0 <= VPIN <= 1 satisfied: {bounds_satisfied}")


main()

5Where the canon breaks

Limit 8.1 (arbitrary bucket size and lookback window)

VPIN requires two subjective tuning parameters: the bucket volume \(V\) (typically 1/50th of average daily volume) and the rolling window length \(N\) (typically 50 buckets). Small changes in \(V\) or \(N\) dramatically alter the timing and magnitude of VPIN spikes, raising concerns of in-sample parameter overfitting.

Limit 8.2 (BVC classification error versus tick data)

Bulk volume classification uses price changes \(\Delta p\) to infer trade signs. When large trades execute at the bid during a rising market or at the ask during a falling market, BVC misclassifies entire volume blocks, confounding trade direction with price trend.

6Misconceptions

  • Wrong: VPIN is an structural estimate of the probability of informed trading like PIN.

    Why it is wrong: While inspired by PIN, VPIN is an empirical volume-weighted order imbalance index, not a maximum likelihood parameter of a Poisson arrival mixture model.

  • Wrong: A high VPIN level guarantees an imminent market crash.

    Why it is wrong: As shown by Andersen and Bondarenko (2014), VPIN spikes regularly during periods of high fundamental volatility without causing market crashes, resulting in high false-alarm rates.

  • Wrong: VPIN requires millisecond order book depth feeds to compute.

    Why it is wrong: VPIN requires only sequential trade prices and trade volumes. It does not require limit order book quotes, queue depths, or order cancellations.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Buy and sell volume from BVC

A volume bucket has capacity \(V = 500\) shares. The price change is \(\Delta p = +0.40\) and volatility scale is \(\sigma = 0.40\). Using standard normal CDF values (\(\Phi(1) \approx 0.8413\)), compute \(V^B\), \(V^S\), and the bucket imbalance.

Solution

Standardized price change is \(z = \Delta p / \sigma = 0.40 / 0.40 = 1.0\). Then \(V^B = 500 \times \Phi(1) = 500 \times 0.8413 = 420.65\) shares. Sell volume is \(V^S = 500 - 420.65 = 79.35\) shares. Bucket imbalance is \(|V^B - V^S| = 420.65 - 79.35 = 341.30\) shares.

M2 · Rolling window VPIN calculation

Over a window of \(N = 5\) buckets of volume \(V = 100\), the absolute imbalances are \(20, 40, 30, 50, 60\). Compute VPIN.

Solution

Sum of imbalances: \(20 + 40 + 30 + 50 + 60 = 200\). Total volume: \(N \times V = 5 \times 100 = 500\). Then \(\operatorname{VPIN} = \frac{200}{500} = 0.40\).

M3 · Maximum possible VPIN

What sequence of price changes would cause VPIN to attain its theoretical upper bound of 1.0?

Solution

VPIN achieves 1.0 if every bucket in the rolling window experiences an extreme price change \(|\Delta p_\tau| \gg \sigma\), such that \(\Phi(z_\tau) \in \{0, 1\}\). In that case, each bucket is classified as 100% buy or 100% sell volume, yielding imbalance \(|V - 0| = V\) and \(\operatorname{VPIN} = N V / (N V) = 1.0\).

Proof

P1 · Invariance of VPIN to volume scaling

Show that multiplying bucket size \(V\) by constant \(k > 0\) while scaling all trade volumes by \(k\) leaves VPIN unchanged.

Solution

Let \(\tilde{V} = k V\). The standardized price changes \(z_\tau\) depend on price changes and \(\sigma\), not volume units. Then \(|\tilde{V}_\tau^B - \tilde{V}_\tau^S| = k V |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|\). Summing over \(N\) buckets: \(\frac{\sum k V |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|}{N (k V)} = \frac{k V \sum |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|}{k N V} = \frac{\sum |2\Phi(z_\tau) - 1|}{N}\). The factor \(k\) cancels identically, proving scale invariance.

P2 · Bound preservation under arbitrary window size

Prove that \(\operatorname{VPIN} \in [0, 1]\) holds for every positive integer \(N \ge 1\) and every finite bucket capacity \(V > 0\).

Solution

For any bucket \(\tau\), \(V_\tau^B \ge 0\) and \(V_\tau^S \ge 0\) with \(V_\tau^B + V_\tau^S = V\). By the triangle inequality, \(|V_\tau^B - V_\tau^S| \le V_\tau^B + V_\tau^S = V\), and non-negativity gives \(|V_\tau^B - V_\tau^S| \ge 0\). Summing over \(N\) terms yields \(0 \le \sum_{\tau=1}^N |V_\tau^B - V_\tau^S| \le N V\). Dividing by \(N V\) yields \(0 \le \operatorname{VPIN} \le 1\).

P3 · Expected VPIN under Gaussian random walk

If bucket price changes \(\Delta p_\tau \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\), prove that \(\E[\operatorname{VPIN}] = \frac{1}{2}\).

Solution

When \(\Delta p_\tau \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\), the standardized variable \(Z = \Delta p_\tau / \sigma\) is standard normal. By probability integral transform, \(U = \Phi(Z)\) is uniformly distributed on \([0, 1]\). Then \(|2\Phi(Z) - 1| = |2U - 1|\). Since \(U \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)\), \(X = 2U - 1 \sim \text{Uniform}(-1, 1)\). The expectation of its absolute value is \(\E[|X|] = \int_{-1}^1 |x| \frac{1}{2} dx = \int_0^1 x\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\). Linearity of expectation gives \(\E[\operatorname{VPIN}] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\tau=1}^N \E[|2U - 1|] = \frac{1}{2}\).

Empirical

E1 · Reading Lab 1 peak VPIN

From Lab 1 output, report the minimum and maximum rolling VPIN values and identify the bucket where VPIN peaked.

Solution

Lab 1 prints: Minimum rolling VPIN = 0.1831 (bucket 10); Maximum rolling VPIN = 0.7529 (bucket 22). The peak occurs at bucket 22, four buckets after the end of the directional order imbalance shock in buckets 12-18, reflecting the rolling window lookback delay.

E2 · Impact of rolling window length on peak lag

Why does the maximum VPIN in Lab 1 occur at bucket 22 rather than bucket 18 when the shock ended?

Solution

VPIN uses a rolling backward window of \(N = 10\) buckets. At bucket 22, the 10-bucket window spans buckets 13 through 22, which captures the maximal density of toxic buckets (buckets 13-18). Rolling lookback windows inherently lag instantaneous event arrivals.

E3 · BVC with Student-t distribution

Why do some authors propose using a Student-t distribution CDF instead of the normal CDF in BVC?

Solution

High-frequency price changes exhibit fat tails and excess kurtosis. Standard normal CDFs assign near-extreme probabilities (\(\Phi \approx 0\) or 1) to moderate price jumps, creating artificial toxicity spikes. A Student-t CDF dampens sensitivity to fat-tailed outliers.

Research

R1 · The May 6, 2010 Flash Crash timeline

Summarize the events of the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash in E-mini futures and the CFTC-SEC findings regarding algorithmic selling.

Solution

Between 2:32 PM and 2:45 PM EDT on May 6, 2010, the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped approximately 1,000 points (9%) before recovering within minutes. The joint CFTC-SEC report found that a large mutual fund sold 75,000 E-mini contracts using an automated execution algorithm, triggering a cascade as HFT market makers withdrew liquidity.

R2 · Volatility confounding in toxicity metrics

Explain Andersen and Bondarenko's regression methodology for testing whether VPIN has incremental predictive power beyond realized volatility.

Solution

Andersen and Bondarenko estimated predictive regressions of future market turmoil on both VPIN and high-frequency realized volatility (RV). They showed that when RV is included as an explanatory regressor, the coefficient on VPIN becomes statistically insignificant, demonstrating that VPIN is primarily a proxy for realized volatility.

R3 · Real-time risk management using order toxicity

How do modern automated market making desks use order imbalance metrics in production risk systems?

Solution

Algorithmic market makers monitor real-time order imbalances across volume buckets to dynamically widen quoted spreads, cancel passive limit orders, or throttle quote sizes when toxicity exceeds risk limits, protecting against adverse selection cascades.

9Retrieval quiz

  1. What clock sampling mechanism does VPIN use to measure trading activity?

    Options

    By Definition 8.1, VPIN slices trading into volume buckets of constant size \(V\).

  2. What is the theoretical upper bound on VPIN?

    By Theorem 8.3, VPIN is bounded in \([0, 1]\), so its maximum is 1.0.

  3. How does Bulk Volume Classification (BVC) partition a bucket's volume into buy and sell orders?

    Options

    By Definition 8.2, BVC assigns \(V^B = V \Phi(\Delta p / \sigma)\) and \(V^S = V - V^B\).

  4. Under a pure Gaussian random walk price process, what is the theoretical expected value of VPIN?

    As proved in Problem P3, \(\E[\operatorname{VPIN}] = 0.50\).

  5. What primary criticism did Andersen and Bondarenko (2014) level against VPIN's predictability during the 2010 Flash Crash?

    Options

    Andersen and Bondarenko showed that VPIN is primarily a proxy for realized volatility, suffering high false alarm rates.

  6. If every volume bucket in the rolling window experiences zero price change (\(\Delta p = 0\)), what is VPIN?

    When \(\Delta p = 0\), \(\Phi(0) = 0.5\), so \(|2(0.5) - 1| = 0\), yielding \(\operatorname{VPIN} = 0\).

  7. Which paper introduced VPIN as a measure of order flow toxicity?

    Options

    Easley, López de Prado, and O'Hara (2012) published "Flow Toxicity and Liquidity in a High-frequency World" in RFS.

  8. If sum of absolute imbalances across \(N=5\) buckets with \(V=100\) is 200, what is VPIN?

    \(\operatorname{VPIN} = 200 / (5 \times 100) = 200 / 500 = 0.40\).
