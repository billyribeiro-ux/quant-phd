Kyle's Model of Informed Trading

How does a trader with private information exploit that edge when trading moves the price against them? Albert S. Kyle formulated this problem as a strategic game between an informed insider, noise traders, and a competitive market maker. In equilibrium, the insider trades cautiously to conceal private information behind noise volume, while the market maker infers value from aggregate order flow. This lesson derives the single-period Kyle equilibrium in closed form and proves that exactly half of the insider's private information is incorporated into the clearing price.

1The batch auction environment

Definition 5.1 (market participants and random variables)

A single risky asset has a liquidation value \(v \sim \Normal(p_0, \Sigma_0)\), with prior mean \(p_0\) and prior variance \(\Sigma_0 > 0\). Three classes of participants interact in a batch auction:

  • Informed insider: observes the realization of \(v\) perfectly and submits order quantity \(x(v)\).
  • Noise traders: submit aggregate order quantity \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\), with \(\sigma_u > 0\), independent of \(v\).
  • Market maker: observes only total order flow \(\omega = x + u\) (unable to distinguish informed orders from noise) and clears the market at price \(p(\omega)\).
This game is the single-period Kyle model.

Assumption A1 (market maker semi-strong efficiency)

Quotes are set by a competitive, risk-neutral market maker who sets the clearing price equal to the conditional expectation of value given total order flow: \[ p(\omega) = \E[v \mid \omega]. \]

Assumption A2 (insider profit maximization)

The insider is risk-neutral and chooses order quantity \(x\) to maximize expected trading profit conditional on private information \(v\): \[ \max_{x} \E[(v - p(\omega))\, x \mid v]. \]

Assumption A3 (linear equilibrium family)

We solve for a linear Bayesian Nash equilibrium where: \[ x(v) = \beta\, (v - p_0), \] \[ p(\omega) = p_0 + \lambda\, \omega, \] with constant parameters \(\beta > 0\) and \(\lambda > 0\).

2Equilibrium derivation

Theorem 5.2 (Kyle linear equilibrium)

Under Assumptions A1, A2, and A3, there exists a unique linear equilibrium in the linear strategy class. The equilibrium insider aggressiveness \(\beta\) and market maker price impact \(\lambda\) are: \[ \beta = \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}, \] \[ \lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u}. \] Both equilibrium residuals evaluate to zero: \[ \text{Residual}_1 = \beta - \frac{1}{2\lambda} = 0, \] \[ \text{Residual}_2 = \lambda - \frac{\beta\, \Sigma_0}{\beta^2\, \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2} = 0. \]

Proof

First, consider the insider's problem. Substituting the pricing rule \(p(\omega) = p_0 + \lambda(x + u)\) into expected profit: \[ \E[(v - p_0 - \lambda(x + u))\, x \mid v] = (v - p_0 - \lambda x)\, x - \lambda\, x\, \E[u \mid v]. \] Since \(u\) is independent of \(v\), \(\E[u \mid v] = 0\), so expected profit is \((v - p_0 - \lambda x) x\). Differentiating with respect to \(x\): \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[(v - p_0)\, x - \lambda x^2\right] = v - p_0 - 2\lambda x = 0 \implies x^* = \frac{v - p_0}{2\lambda}. \] The second-order condition is \(-2\lambda < 0\), which requires \(\lambda > 0\). Comparing \(x^* = \frac{v - p_0}{2\lambda}\) with the conjectured rule \(x(v) = \beta(v - p_0)\) yields: \[ \beta = \frac{1}{2\lambda} \iff \text{Residual}_1 = \beta - \frac{1}{2\lambda} = 0. \] Second, consider the market maker's pricing rule. The pair \((v, \omega)\) is jointly normal with: \[ \E[v] = p_0, \quad \E[\omega] = \E[\beta(v - p_0) + u] = 0, \] \[ \Var(\omega) = \beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2, \] \[ \Cov(v, \omega) = \Cov(v, \beta(v - p_0) + u) = \beta\, \Var(v) = \beta\, \Sigma_0. \] By the linear projection theorem for Gaussian variables: \[ p(\omega) = \E[v \mid \omega] = p_0 + \frac{\Cov(v, \omega)}{\Var(\omega)}\, \omega = p_0 + \frac{\beta\, \Sigma_0}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2}\, \omega. \] Matching coefficients with \(p(\omega) = p_0 + \lambda \omega\) yields: \[ \lambda = \frac{\beta\, \Sigma_0}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2} \iff \text{Residual}_2 = \lambda - \frac{\beta\, \Sigma_0}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2} = 0. \] Now substitute \(\beta = \frac{1}{2\lambda}\) into the pricing equation: \[ \lambda = \frac{\frac{1}{2\lambda} \Sigma_0}{\frac{1}{4\lambda^2} \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2} = \frac{2\lambda\, \Sigma_0}{\Sigma_0 + 4\lambda^2 \sigma_u^2}. \] Since \(\lambda > 0\), divide both sides by \(\lambda\): \[ 1 = \frac{2\Sigma_0}{\Sigma_0 + 4\lambda^2 \sigma_u^2} \implies \Sigma_0 + 4\lambda^2 \sigma_u^2 = 2\Sigma_0 \implies 4\lambda^2 \sigma_u^2 = \Sigma_0. \] Taking the positive square root: \[ 2\lambda \sigma_u = \sqrt{\Sigma_0} \implies \lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u}. \] Then \(\beta = \frac{1}{2\lambda} = \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}\). Both coefficients are uniquely determined.

Proposition 5.3 (price informativeness and welfare)

In the Kyle linear equilibrium of Theorem 5.2:

  1. The posterior variance of the asset value given clearing price \(p\) is: \[ \Sigma_1 = \Var(v \mid p) = \frac{1}{2}\, \Sigma_0. \] Exactly 50% of the insider's private information is incorporated into the price.
  2. The variance of the informed trader's order flow equals the variance of noise trader order flow: \[ \Var(x) = \beta^2\, \Sigma_0 = \sigma_u^2 = \Var(u). \]
  3. The insider's expected trading profit equals noise trader expected losses: \[ \E[\Pi_{\text{insider}}] = \lambda\, \sigma_u^2 = \frac{1}{2}\, \sqrt{\Sigma_0}\, \sigma_u. \] The market maker earns zero expected economic profit: \(\E[\Pi_{\text{MM}}] = 0\).

Proof

For (1), by the standard normal conditional variance formula: \[ \Var(v \mid \omega) = \Sigma_0 - \frac{[\Cov(v, \omega)]^2}{\Var(\omega)} = \Sigma_0 - \frac{(\beta \Sigma_0)^2}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2}. \] Substitute \(\beta^2 \Sigma_0 = \frac{\sigma_u^2}{\Sigma_0} \Sigma_0 = \sigma_u^2\): \[ \Var(v \mid \omega) = \Sigma_0 - \frac{\sigma_u^2 \Sigma_0}{\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_u^2} = \Sigma_0 - \frac{\sigma_u^2 \Sigma_0}{2\sigma_u^2} = \Sigma_0 - \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0 = \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0. \] This proves (1) and (2). For (3), expected insider profit is: \[ \E[(v - p_0 - \lambda \beta(v - p_0) - \lambda u)\beta(v - p_0)] = (1 - \lambda \beta)\beta\, \E[(v - p_0)^2] = (1 - \lambda \beta)\beta\, \Sigma_0. \] Since \(\lambda \beta = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u} \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}} = \frac{1}{2}\): \[ \E[\Pi_{\text{insider}}] = \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) \beta \Sigma_0 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}} \Sigma_0 = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\Sigma_0}\, \sigma_u = \lambda \sigma_u^2. \] Expected noise trader cash flow is \(\E[(p - v) u] = \E[(p_0 + \lambda(x + u) - v)u] = \lambda \E[u^2] = \lambda \sigma_u^2\). The market maker collects total cash flow from all orders, which clears at zero expected profit.

Example 5.4 (curriculum lab parameters)

Let \(p_0 = 100\), \(\Sigma_0 = 4\), and \(\sigma_u^2 = 4\) (so \(\sigma_u = 2\)). The equilibrium coefficients are: \[ \lambda = \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2 \cdot 2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \beta = \frac{2}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{2}{2} = 1. \] The posterior variance is \(\Sigma_1 = 4 / 2 = 2\). Expected insider profit is \(\lambda \sigma_u^2 = (1/2)(4) = 2\), and noise trader losses are 2. Lab 1 implements this exact calculation in rational arithmetic.

Remark 5.5 (title citation of Kyle 1985)

Albert Kyle published this foundation of strategic microstructure in "Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading". The single-period batch auction derived here serves as the analytical core of continuous-time trading with informed flow.

3Curriculum lab: Kyle equilibrium

Lab 1 · Kyle equilibrium exact solution

Exact solution in Fraction arithmetic for p0 = 100, Sigma0 = 4, sigma_u^2 = 4. Expected: equilibrium beta = 1, lambda = 1/2, insider and MM residuals evaluate to exactly 0, posterior variance is 2, and insider expected profit equals noise losses of 2.

"""Lab MS1-L05-1: Kyle single-period equilibrium.

Exact solution in Fraction arithmetic for the linear Kyle equilibrium.
Verifies the insider optimality condition, the competitive market maker pricing condition,
and checks that both equilibrium residuals evaluate to exactly zero.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction


def solve_kyle_equilibrium(
    p0: Fraction, sigma0: Fraction, sigma_u_sq: Fraction, lam: Fraction, beta: Fraction
) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction, Fraction, Fraction, Fraction, Fraction]:
    """Verify Kyle equilibrium conditions and compute posterior variance and welfare."""
    # Residual 1: Insider optimal order slope condition x = beta * (v - p0)
    res_insider = beta - Fraction(1, 1) / (Fraction(2, 1) * lam)

    # Residual 2: Market maker conditional expectation slope p - p0 = lambda * omega
    denom = beta * beta * sigma0 + sigma_u_sq
    numer = beta * sigma0
    res_mm = lam - (numer / denom)

    # Posterior variance of asset liquidation value v given order flow omega
    sigma1 = sigma0 - (numer * numer) / denom

    # Welfare: insider expected profit and noise trader expected loss
    insider_profit = (Fraction(1, 1) - lam * beta) * beta * sigma0
    noise_loss = lam * sigma_u_sq

    return res_insider, res_mm, sigma1, insider_profit, noise_loss, (sigma0 - sigma1) / sigma0


def main() -> None:
    p0 = Fraction(100, 1)
    sigma0 = Fraction(4, 1)
    sigma_u_sq = Fraction(4, 1)

    # Derived equilibrium values: lambda = sqrt(Sigma0)/(2*sigma_u) = 2 / 4 = 1/2
    # beta = 1 / (2*lambda) = 1
    lam = Fraction(1, 2)
    beta = Fraction(1, 1)

    res_insider, res_mm, sigma1, ins_profit, noise_loss, info_share = solve_kyle_equilibrium(
        p0, sigma0, sigma_u_sq, lam, beta
    )

    print("Kyle Equilibrium Parameters:")
    print(f"  Prior price p_0: {p0}")
    print(f"  Prior variance Sigma_0: {sigma0}")
    print(f"  Noise trader variance sigma_u^2: {sigma_u_sq}")
    print(f"  Equilibrium insider aggressiveness beta: {beta}")
    print(f"  Equilibrium price impact lambda: {lam}")
    print("Equilibrium Condition Residuals:")
    print(f"  Insider optimality residual (beta - 1/(2*lambda)): {res_insider}")
    print(f"  Market maker rationality residual (lambda - Cov/Var): {res_mm}")
    print("Information and Welfare:")
    print(f"  Posterior variance Sigma_1 = Var(v | omega): {sigma1}")
    print(f"  Fraction of information incorporated into price: {info_share}")
    print(f"  Expected insider trading profit: {ins_profit}")
    print(f"  Expected noise trader losses: {noise_loss}")
    print(f"  Market maker expected profit: {noise_loss - ins_profit}")


main()

4Where the canon breaks

Limit 5.1 (single period restriction)

In a single-period auction, the insider trades immediately and reveals 50% of the private signal. In continuous time over a finite trading interval \([0, T]\), the insider trades gradually at rate \(dx_t = \beta_t(v - p_t)dt\), revealing information at a constant rate \(\Sigma_t = \Sigma_0 (1 - t/T)\) until full revelation at \(t = T\).

Limit 5.2 (multiple competing insiders)

When \(N > 1\) symmetric insiders observe the same signal, they engage in Cournot-like competition. Total insider aggressiveness increases to \(N \beta\), prices become more informative (\(\Sigma_1 / \Sigma_0 = \frac{1}{N+1}\)), and total insider profits decline.

5Misconceptions

  • Wrong: The informed trader submits an infinitely large order to maximize immediate profit.

    Why it is wrong: Price impact \(\lambda > 0\) penalizes large orders. Because the clearing price increases with order size (\(p = p_0 + \lambda \omega\)), submitting too large an order moves the price past the fundamental value \(v\), destroying trading profits.

  • Wrong: Noise traders earn positive returns on average because they supply liquidity.

    Why it is wrong: Noise traders in Kyle's model are liquidity demanders whose orders are price-inelastic. They suffer systematic losses (\(\E[\text{Loss}] = \lambda \sigma_u^2\)) by trading at prices distorted against them by adverse selection.

  • Wrong: Kyle's lambda is the bid–ask spread.

    Why it is wrong: Kyle's lambda \(\lambda\) is a price impact coefficient (measured in price per unit volume), reflecting how clearing prices respond to continuous aggregate order flow. The bid-ask spread is a transaction cost per trade unit.

6Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Equilibrium coefficients under higher noise volume

Let \(\Sigma_0 = 9\) and \(\sigma_u = 3\). Compute \(\lambda\), \(\beta\), posterior variance \(\Sigma_1\), and expected insider profit.

Solution

We have \(\sqrt{\Sigma_0} = 3\). Then \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u} = \frac{3}{2 \times 3} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.50\). The insider aggressiveness is \(\beta = \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}} = \frac{3}{3} = 1.0\). Posterior variance is \(\Sigma_1 = \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0 = 4.5\). Expected insider profit is \(\lambda \sigma_u^2 = 0.50 \times 3^2 = 4.50\) dollars.

M2 · Sensitivity of Kyle's lambda to prior uncertainty

Holding noise volume \(\sigma_u = 2\) fixed, how does \(\lambda\) change if prior variance \(\Sigma_0\) quadruples from 4 to 16?

Solution

For \(\Sigma_0 = 4\), \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2(2)} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.50\). For \(\Sigma_0 = 16\), \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{16}}{2(2)} = \frac{4}{4} = 1.00\). Quadrupling variance doubles price impact \(\lambda\) because higher fundamental uncertainty increases information asymmetry.

M3 · Numerical order size for observed value

Using Example 5.4 (\(p_0 = 100, \beta = 1\)), what order size \(x\) does the insider submit if the realized value is \(v = 104\)? What is the expected transaction price if noise order is \(u = -1\)?

Solution

Order size is \(x = \beta(v - p_0) = 1(104 - 100) = +4\) shares. Total order flow is \(\omega = x + u = 4 + (-1) = 3\). Transaction price is \(p = p_0 + \lambda \omega = 100 + 0.5(3) = 101.50\). The insider's realized profit is \((v - p)x = (104 - 101.50) \times 4 = 2.50 \times 4 = 10.00\) dollars.

Proof

P1 · Second-order condition strictly requires positive lambda

Show that a linear equilibrium with \(\lambda \le 0\) cannot exist.

Solution

The insider's second-order condition for profit maximization is \(\frac{\partial^2 \Pi}{\partial x^2} = -2\lambda < 0 \implies \lambda > 0\). If \(\lambda = 0\), price is insensitive to orders, so the insider's profit is unbounded for \(x \to \pm \infty\), precluding equilibrium. If \(\lambda < 0\), the objective is strictly convex, and optimal order size is infinite.

P2 · Invariance of information revelation share

Prove that the percentage of private information revealed by price in the single-period Kyle model is always 50%, regardless of parameters \(\Sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_u^2\).

Solution

Information revealed is measured by \(\frac{\Sigma_0 - \Sigma_1}{\Sigma_0}\). From Proposition 5.3, \(\Sigma_1 = \Sigma_0 - \frac{(\beta \Sigma_0)^2}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2}\). In equilibrium, \(\beta = \sigma_u / \sqrt{\Sigma_0}\), so \(\beta^2 \Sigma_0 = \sigma_u^2\). Then \(\frac{(\beta \Sigma_0)^2}{\beta^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2} = \frac{\sigma_u^2 \Sigma_0}{2\sigma_u^2} = \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0\). Hence \(\Sigma_1 = \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0\), and the revealed share is identically \(\frac{\Sigma_0 - 0.5\Sigma_0}{\Sigma_0} = 50\%\).

P3 · Zero expected profit for the market maker

Prove from market clearing that the market maker's expected profit across all orders is identically zero.

Solution

The market maker executes total volume \(\omega = x + u\) at unit price \(p(\omega)\), with cash flow \(\Pi_{\text{MM}} = (p(\omega) - v) \omega\). Taking expectations: \(\E[\Pi_{\text{MM}}] = \E[\E[(p(\omega) - v)\omega \mid \omega]] = \E[(p(\omega) - \E[v \mid \omega]) \omega]\). By Assumption A1, \(p(\omega) = \E[v \mid \omega]\). Therefore \(p(\omega) - \E[v \mid \omega] = 0\) pointwise for every \(\omega\), proving \(\E[\Pi_{\text{MM}}] = 0\).

Empirical

From Lab 1 output, report the equilibrium parameters \(\beta\), \(\lambda\), the two residuals, and the posterior variance \(\Sigma_1\).

Solution

Lab 1 prints: \(\beta = 1\), \(\lambda = 1/2\). Insider optimality residual is 0, market maker rationality residual is 0. Posterior variance is \(\Sigma_1 = 2\), exactly half of the prior variance \(\Sigma_0 = 4\).

E2 · Camouflage from noise trading

Explain how noise trading variance \(\sigma_u^2\) serves as camouflage for the insider in Kyle's model.

Solution

Higher noise variance \(\sigma_u^2\) makes order flow more volatile and noisier, so the market maker attributes less of any order surge to private information (\(\lambda \propto 1/\sigma_u\)). This allows the insider to trade more aggressively (\(\beta \propto \sigma_u\)) without moving prices as sharply.

E3 · Welfare balance

Confirm that the numerical expected profits and losses reported in Lab 1 balance exactly.

Solution

Lab 1 prints: Expected insider trading profit = 2; Expected noise trader losses = 2; Market maker profit = 0. The zero-sum game property \(\text{Profit}_{\text{insider}} + \text{Profit}_{\text{noise}} + \text{Profit}_{\text{MM}} = 2 - 2 + 0 = 0\) is satisfied exactly.

Research

R1 · Multiple competing insiders

If \(N\) identical informed traders independently observe \(v\) and submit orders \(x_i = \beta_N(v - p_0)\), derive the equilibrium coefficients \(\beta_N\) and \(\lambda_N\).

Solution

Insider \(i\) maximizes \(\E[(v - p_0 - \lambda(x_i + \sum_{j \ne i} x_j + u))x_i \mid v]\). First-order condition: \(v - p_0 - \lambda \sum_{j \ne i} x_j - 2\lambda x_i = 0\). In symmetric equilibrium, \(x_j = x_i = x\), so \(v - p_0 - \lambda(N-1)x - 2\lambda x = 0 \implies x = \frac{v - p_0}{\lambda(N+1)}\). Hence \(\beta_N = \frac{1}{\lambda(N+1)}\). Total order flow is \(\omega = N \beta_N (v - p_0) + u\). Matching with \(\lambda = \frac{N \beta_N \Sigma_0}{(N\beta_N)^2 \Sigma_0 + \sigma_u^2}\) yields \(\beta_N = \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\) and \(\lambda_N = \frac{\sqrt{N}}{N+1} \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{\sigma_u}\).

R2 · Risk aversion of the insider

How does risk aversion on the part of the insider alter Kyle's equilibrium order aggressiveness \(\beta\)?

Solution

A risk-averse insider (e.g. with CARA utility) faces execution price variance and fundamental uncertainty, penalizing aggressive positions. The insider trades less aggressively (\(\beta_{\text{risk-averse}} < \beta_{\text{neutral}}\)), reducing market informativeness and leaving more unrevealed variance.

R3 · Endogenous information acquisition

Suppose the insider must pay a fixed cost \(K\) to observe \(v\). Under what condition will information be purchased?

Solution

An agent purchases information if and only if expected gross trading profits exceed the information cost: \(\E[\Pi] = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Sigma_0}\sigma_u \ge K\). If fundamental uncertainty \(\Sigma_0\) is low or noise trading \(\sigma_u\) is scarce, information acquisition is unprofitable, resulting in an uninformative market.

8Retrieval quiz

  1. In the Kyle model with \(\Sigma_0 = 4\) and \(\sigma_u = 2\), what is Kyle's lambda \(\lambda\)?

    \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u} = \frac{2}{2(2)} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.50\).

  2. In the same model (\(\Sigma_0 = 4, \sigma_u = 2\)), what is the insider's order aggressiveness \(\beta\)?

    \(\beta = \frac{\sigma_u}{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}} = \frac{2}{2} = 1.0\).

  3. What fraction of the insider's private information is incorporated into the clearing price in the single-period Kyle equilibrium?

    By Proposition 5.3, \(\Sigma_1 = \frac{1}{2}\Sigma_0\), so exactly \(0.50\) (50%) of information is incorporated.

  4. How does an increase in noise trading volatility \(\sigma_u\) affect market liquidity (Kyle's lambda)?

    Options

    \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{2\sigma_u}\). As \(\sigma_u\) increases, \(\lambda\) falls, so orders have smaller price impact.

  5. In Example 5.4 (\(\Sigma_0 = 4, \sigma_u = 2, \lambda = 0.5\)), what is the insider's expected trading profit?

    \(\E[\Pi] = \lambda \sigma_u^2 = 0.5 \times 2^2 = 0.5 \times 4 = 2.0\) dollars.

  6. What is the relationship between the variance of informed orders \(\Var(x)\) and noise orders \(\Var(u)\) in equilibrium?

    Options

    By Proposition 5.3, \(\Var(x) = \beta^2 \Sigma_0 = \sigma_u^2 = \Var(u)\). The insider trades with exactly the same dispersion as noise traders.

  7. What is the market maker's expected economic profit in Kyle's equilibrium?

    Bertrand competition and risk neutrality ensure the market maker earns exactly 0 expected profit.

  8. What prevents the informed insider from trading an arbitrarily large quantity when observing \(v > p_0\)?

    Options

    Price impact \(p = p_0 + \lambda \omega\) penalizes order size quadratically, creating an optimal interior trade size.
