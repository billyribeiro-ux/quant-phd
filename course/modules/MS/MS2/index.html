Limit Order Books & Market Structure

Modern financial markets operate as decentralized networks of electronic limit order books connected by high-speed communication links and complex regulatory mandates. Building upon the equilibrium price-formation models of MS1 and the discrete-event data structures of CS2, this module investigates the mechanical, econometric, and regulatory architecture of contemporary securities markets. Across six lessons, we analyze limit order queue priority and execution probabilities (Lesson 1), Markov birth–death models of order book dynamics and first-passage price changes (Lesson 2), market fragmentation, protected quotes, and the SIP latency wedge under Regulation NMS (Lesson 3), off-exchange internalization, dark pools, and the cream-skimming economics of payment for order flow (Lesson 4), latency arbitrage and speed races in continuous limit order books alongside the empirical debate on high-frequency trading (Lesson 5), and market fragility during the 2010 Flash Crash contrasted with Frequent Batch Auctions as an optimal market design response (Lesson 6). The module culminates in a counterfactual simulation project evaluating latency-arbitrage rents and welfare gains.

  • Prerequisites: MS1 Microstructure Theory and CS2 Data Structures & Algorithms.
  • Unlocks: MS3 Market Impact & Execution Algorithms, and STUDIO-MS Microstructure Studio.
  • Project: Batch-auction counterfactual: an autograded simulation engine (counterfactual.py) replaying order flow through continuous books and frequent batch auctions, accompanied by an analytical research memo (report.md).
  • Data: No proprietary trade-level dataset is embedded. All examples use generated event logs labelled SIMULATED. A student may supply an order log in bring-your-own-data mode.

40 h lessons · 20 h project (33%)

Objectives

By the end of MS2 you can:

  1. Compute limit order fill probabilities and expected queue wait times as a function of priority position and order cancellation intensity in price-time matching engines (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the queue-depth state space as a continuous-time Markov birth–death chain and derive the boundary-value probability of next price revisions (Lesson 2).
  3. Evaluate the regulatory mechanics of SEC Regulation NMS (Rule 611 and ISOs) and quantify execution slippage induced by the SIP latency wedge across fragmented lit venues (Lesson 3).
  4. Model the cream-skimming equilibrium of dark pools and payment for order flow (PFOF) and quantify the adverse selection externality imposed on lit exchange spreads (Lesson 4).
  5. Derive latency-arbitrage rents in continuous-time double auctions and evaluate empirical evidence on high-frequency trading price discovery and liquidity provision (Lesson 5).
  6. Synthesize the microstructure cascade mechanisms of the 2010 Flash Crash and prove the elimination of latency arbitrage under Frequent Batch Auctions (Lesson 6).
  7. Simulate counterfactual order streams across continuous trading and frequent batch auctions to measure speed rents and investor welfare improvements (Project).

Prerequisites

MS2 builds directly upon the theoretical foundations established in MS1 Microstructure Theory and the algorithmic implementations in CS2 Data Structures & Algorithms. From MS1, MS2 assumes comprehensive familiarity with Roll's spread estimator, the Glosten–Milgrom sequential trade model, dealer inventory models, econometric spread decomposition, the Kyle continuous auction model, Kyle's lambda, the probability of informed trading (PIN), and VPIN order flow toxicity. From CS2, MS2 relies on the order-book data structure (price levels with FIFO queues), the priority event queue for discrete-event simulation, and asymptotic complexity. Through the prerequisite closure, MS2 applies adverse selection from E2, Bayesian inference from S1, and continuous distributions from M4.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on the prerequisites (MS1 and CS2), and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Roll model (MS1), if consecutive price change autocovariance is \(\Cov(\Delta p_t, \Delta p_{t-1}) = -0.000625\), what is the estimated effective half-spread \(c = \sqrt{-\Cov}\)?

    \(c = \sqrt{0.000625} = 0.025 \times 2 = 0.05\). The effective half-spread is \(\sqrt{-\Cov(\Delta p_t, \Delta p_{t-1})} = 0.025\), so the full spread is 0.05, and \(c = 0.025\) or half-spread 0.05 depending on convention; here \(\sqrt{0.000625} = 0.025\), Roll spread \(2c = 0.05\).

  2. In the Glosten–Milgrom model (MS1), why does the quoted bid–ask spread widen when the proportion of informed traders \(\mu\) increases?

    Options

    As proved in MS1 (Glosten & Milgrom 1985), the spread compensates the competitive market maker for adverse selection losses inflicted by informed traders.

  3. In an electronic limit order book (CS2), what is the worst-case asymptotic time complexity of matching an incoming market order against the top of a FIFO price-level queue?

    Options

    A FIFO price-level queue implemented as a doubly-linked list or ring buffer matches and dequeues from the head in \(O(1)\) constant time.

  4. In a discrete-event simulator with an event queue (CS2), an event arrives at timestamp \(t = 2.5\) ms and schedules a reaction with latency \(\delta = 7.5\) ms. At what simulation timestamp (ms) does the reaction execute?

    Execution time is simply arrival time plus latency delay: \(t + \delta = 2.5 + 7.5 = 10.0\) ms.

  5. In Kyle's continuous auction model (MS1), what does Kyle's lambda \(\lambda\) measure?

    Options

    Kyle's lambda \(\lambda = \Cov(v, y) / \Var(y)\) measures the equilibrium sensitivity of transaction price to order flow.

  6. In probability of informed trading (PIN, MS1), what is the economic interpretation of order flow toxicity?

    Options

    Flow toxicity represents order flow directed by traders possessing superior information, creating adverse selection for market makers.

  7. Skill check (Rung 1 · Project): A market maker quotes ask \(A = 100.05\) (half-spread \(s/2 = 0.05\)). A correlated asset jumps, shifting fundamental value to \(V_{\text{new}} = 100.20\). A high-frequency sniper snipes the stale ask before the market maker can cancel. What is the net latency arbitrage rent earned per share?

    The sniper buys at the stale ask \(100.05\) and immediately holds an asset worth \(100.20\). Net rent is \(V_{\text{new}} - A = 100.20 - 100.05 = 0.15\) per share (\(\Delta V - s/2 = 0.20 - 0.05 = 0.15\)).

  8. Skill check (Rung 2 · Project): Under a Frequent Batch Auction (FBA) clearing at discrete intervals \(\Delta = 100\) ms, an innovation occurs and the market maker's cancel message arrives within the same batch interval as competing snipers. What is the latency arbitrage rent extracted by the fastest sniper from stale quotes?

    In FBA, all orders and cancellations received within the batch interval are processed simultaneously at the uniform clearing price. Speed within the batch confers no priority advantage, so stale quote sniping rent is strictly 0.

Project ladder
