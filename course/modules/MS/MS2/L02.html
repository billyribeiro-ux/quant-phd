Limit Order Book Dynamics & Markov Models

An electronic limit order book is not a static queue; it is a high-frequency stochastic process in which resting liquidity is constantly created, depleted, and replaced. Predicting the next price move requires modeling the joint evolution of bid and ask queues. Seminal work by Cont, Stoikov & Talreja (2010) formulates order book dynamics as a continuous-time Markov chain, where queue depths follow coupled birth–death processes. By solving the Dirichlet boundary value problem on the queue state space, we compute exact first-passage probabilities to price increases, providing the analytical foundation for high-frequency market making and short-horizon alpha signals.

1The Stochastic Limit Order Book

In Lesson 1, we analyzed an individual order's queue position in isolation. We now expand our perspective to model the collective state of the entire book.

Definition 2.1 · Limit-Order-Book Dynamics
Let \(q_t^b \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}\) and \(q_t^a \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}\) denote the number of shares resting at the best bid and best ask price levels at time \(t \ge 0\). The limit-order-book dynamics are the joint stochastic process \(\mathbf{X}_t = (q_t^b, q_t^a)\) governing the arrival, execution, and cancellation of orders at the market quotes, together with the associated price revision processes.

Following Cont, Stoikov & Talreja (2010), we model \(\mathbf{X}_t = (q_t^b, q_t^a)\) as a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) on the finite state space \(\mathcal{S} = \{0, 1, \dots, K\}^2\). The state transitions are driven by three distinct Poisson event streams on each side of the book:

  • Limit order arrivals: Increase queue depth by 1 lot with rate \(\lambda^b\) (bids) and \(\lambda^a\) (asks).
  • Market order arrivals: Consume resting liquidity at the top of the queue with rate \(\mu^b\) (market sells hitting the bid) and \(\mu^a\) (market buys hitting the ask).
  • Order cancellations: Resting orders cancel independently with per-order intensity \(\theta^b\) and \(\theta^a\), generating aggregate cancellation rates \(\theta^b q^b\) and \(\theta^a q^a\).

Theorem 2.1 · CST Infinitesimal Generator
For interior states \((q^b, q^a) \in \{1, \dots, K-1\}^2\), the infinitesimal generator matrix \(\mathbf{Q}\) of the order book Markov chain satisfies: \[\begin{aligned} q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b+1, q^a)} &= \lambda^b, \\ q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b-1, q^a)} &= \mu^b + \theta^b q^b, \\ q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b, q^a+1)} &= \lambda^a, \\ q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b, q^a-1)} &= \mu^a + \theta^a q^a, \end{aligned}\] with diagonal elements \(q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b, q^a)} = -(\lambda^b + \mu^b + \theta^b q^b + \lambda^a + \mu^a + \theta^a q^a)\).

Proof
Each event stream is modeled as an independent Poisson process. The superposition of independent Poisson processes yields transition rates equal to the sum of individual arrival rates. Transitions that change \(q^b\) by \(+1\) occur when a new limit buy order arrives, which has rate \(\lambda^b\). Transitions that change \(q^b\) by \(-1\) occur if either a market sell order arrives (rate \(\mu^b\)) or any one of the \(q^b\) currently resting buy orders cancels (rate \(\theta^b\) per order, total rate \(\theta^b q^b\)). The transitions on the ask side follow symmetrically. Because the probability of simultaneous arrivals over an infinitesimal interval \(dt\) is \(o(dt)\), off-diagonal transitions other than \(\pm 1\) in a single coordinate have rate 0. The diagonal entries are uniquely fixed by \(\sum_j q_{ij} = 0\).

2First-Passage Probabilities to Price Changes

A central question in high-frequency trading is: starting from current queue depths \((q^b, q^a)\), what is the probability that the next price change will be upward rather than downward?

A price increase occurs when the ask queue depletes to zero (\(q^a = 0\)) before the bid queue depletes to zero (\(q^b = 0\)). Conversely, a price decrease occurs when the bid queue depletes to zero first.

Theorem 2.2 · Dirichlet Boundary Value Problem
Let \(T_a = \inf\{t \ge 0 : q_t^a = 0\}\) and \(T_b = \inf\{t \ge 0 : q_t^b = 0\}\). The probability of an upward price move \(p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) = \Prob(T_a < T_b \mid q_0^b = q^b, q_0^a = q^a)\) satisfies the discrete elliptic boundary value problem: \[\begin{aligned} &(\lambda^b + \mu^b + \theta^b q^b + \lambda^a + \mu^a + \theta^a q^a) p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) \\ &= \lambda^b p_{\text{up}}(q^b+1, q^a) + (\mu^b + \theta^b q^b) p_{\text{up}}(q^b-1, q^a) \\ &\quad + \lambda^a p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a+1) + (\mu^a + \theta^a q^a) p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a-1), \end{aligned}\] subject to the absorbing boundary conditions: \[p_{\text{up}}(q^b, 0) = 1 \quad \forall q^b > 0, \qquad p_{\text{up}}(0, q^a) = 0 \quad \forall q^a > 0.\]

Proof
Apply the law of total probability and the strong Markov property at the first jump time \(\tau_1 = \inf\{t > 0 : \mathbf{X}_t \ne \mathbf{X}_0\}\). Conditional on \(\mathbf{X}_0 = (q^b, q^a)\), the chain transitions to state \(\mathbf{y}\) with probability \(\pi_{(q^b, q^a), \mathbf{y}} = q_{(q^b, q^a), \mathbf{y}} / (-q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b, q^a)})\). Therefore: \[p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) = \sum_{\mathbf{y} \ne (q^b, q^a)} \pi_{(q^b, q^a), \mathbf{y}} p_{\text{up}}(\mathbf{y}).\] Multiplying both sides by the total exit rate \(-q_{(q^b, q^a), (q^b, q^a)}\) yields the discrete harmonic equation. The boundary conditions reflect the terminal events: if the ask queue hits zero while the bid queue remains positive, the ask price rises (upward move, probability 1); if the bid queue hits zero while the ask remains positive, the bid price drops (downward move, probability 0).

Remark 2.1 · The Symmetric Order Book Identity
When order arrival and cancellation intensities are symmetric across bid and ask sides (\(\lambda^b = \lambda^a\), \(\mu^b = \mu^a\), \(\theta^b = \theta^a\)), the continuous-time Markov chain is reversible along diagonal rays, and the first-passage probability is closely approximated by the order book imbalance ratio: \[p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) \approx \frac{q^b}{q^b + q^a}.\] This relationship provides theoretical justification for the ubiquitous use of order book imbalance as a predictive feature in quantitative trading.

Lab 1 · Markovian LOB dynamics simulator

Simulating the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja CTMC birth-death process across initial queue configurations \((q^b, q^a) \in \{(1, 5), (2, 4), (3, 3), (4, 2), (5, 1)\}\) will confirm that empirical first-passage probabilities to upward price changes track the theoretical boundary value solution \(q^b / (q^b + q^a)\) within sampling error (<1.5%).

"""Lab MS2-L02-1: Markovian Limit-Order-Book dynamics.

Simulates the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja (2010) birth-death queue model for (q^b, q^a),
computing the first-passage probability of an upward price move p_up(q^b, q^a)
and comparing against theoretical boundary-value predictions.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random


def simulate_cst_book(
    initial_qb: int,
    initial_qa: int,
    limit_rate: float,
    market_rate: float,
    cancel_rate: float,
    max_depth: int,
    rng: random.Random,
) -> bool:
    """Simulate birth-death dynamics until best bid or best ask queue hits zero.

    Returns:
        True if ask hits zero first (price up), False if bid hits zero first (price down).
    """
    qb = initial_qb
    qa = initial_qa

    while qb > 0 and qa > 0:
        # Total transition rates
        # Bid side:
        rate_b_limit = limit_rate if qb < max_depth else 0.0
        rate_b_market = market_rate  # market sell consumes bid
        rate_b_cancel = cancel_rate * qb

        # Ask side:
        rate_a_limit = limit_rate if qa < max_depth else 0.0
        rate_a_market = market_rate  # market buy consumes ask
        rate_a_cancel = cancel_rate * qa

        total_rate = rate_b_limit + rate_b_market + rate_b_cancel + rate_a_limit + rate_a_market + rate_a_cancel
        if total_rate <= 0.0:
            break

        u = rng.random() * total_rate
        cum = 0.0

        cum += rate_b_limit
        if u < cum:
            qb += 1
            continue

        cum += rate_b_market + rate_b_cancel
        if u < cum:
            qb -= 1
            continue

        cum += rate_a_limit
        if u < cum:
            qa += 1
            continue

        # Remaining probability is ask depletion (market buy or ask cancel)
        qa -= 1

    return qa == 0


def main() -> None:
    rng = random.Random(42)
    n_sims = 3000
    limit_rate = 3.0  # lambda
    market_rate = 2.0  # mu
    cancel_rate = 0.1  # theta per lot
    max_depth = 15

    states = [
        (1, 5),
        (2, 4),
        (3, 3),
        (4, 2),
        (5, 1),
    ]

    print("Cont-Stoikov-Talreja LOB First-Passage Results (N=3000 per state):")
    print("(q_b, q_a) | Imbalance | Emp P(Up) | Th P(Up) | Abs Error")
    print("---------------------------------------------------------")

    for qb, qa in states:
        imbalance = (qb - qa) / (qb + qa)
        theoretical = qb / (qb + qa)

        up_count = 0
        for _ in range(n_sims):
            if simulate_cst_book(qb, qa, limit_rate, market_rate, cancel_rate, max_depth, rng):
                up_count += 1

        emp_prob = up_count / n_sims
        err = abs(emp_prob - theoretical)
        print(f"  ({qb:1d}, {qa:1d})    | {imbalance:9.4f} | {emp_prob:9.4f} | {theoretical:8.4f} | {err:9.4f}")


main()

3Where the Canon Breaks

While the Markovian birth–death model provides closed-form elegance, empirical order book dynamics in production environments diverge from pure Poisson assumptions in several critical ways:

  • Memory and Event Clustering: Real-world order arrivals exhibit long memory and heavy clustering. Order events are better characterized by mutually exciting Hawkes processes than memoryless Poisson processes; an aggressive market order triggers a flurry of cancellations and subsequent retaliatory market orders.
  • Cross-Level Feedback: Deep order book levels (\(L_2, L_3, \dots, L_k\)) actively influence top-of-book behavior. A massive resting wall 2 ticks behind the best bid encourages liquidity providers to post aggressively at the inside spread, while the sudden withdrawal of deep liquidity induces top-of-book panic cancellations.
  • Strategic Non-Stationarity: Arrival rates are not static parameters. Informed algorithmic traders adapt arrival intensities conditionally on time-of-day, macroeconomic releases, and cross-market price signals in correlated venues.

4Misconceptions

  1. Order book imbalance \(I = (q^b - q^a)/(q^b + q^a)\) predicts long-term asset returns.

    Why it is wrong: Order book imbalance has substantial predictive power only over sub-second and tick-by-tick horizons (typically 1 to 50 trades). Beyond several seconds, incoming public information, institutional parent orders, and quote replenishment wash out top-of-book imbalance signals, causing predictive correlation to decay to zero.

  2. Because queue depth changes by integer steps \(\pm 1\), the order book must be modeled in discrete time.

    Why it is wrong: While queue sizes are integers, events occur asynchronously at irregular physical timestamps (microseconds). Formulating the model as a continuous-time Markov jump process correctly captures the stochastic waiting times between events, which convey critical information about market urgency and trading intensity.

  3. A large ask queue guarantees that price will not increase in the immediate future.

    Why it is wrong: A large ask queue can evaporate instantaneously through mass cancellations or institutional sweep orders. When public news arrives, resting limit orders are frequently cancelled within microseconds, enabling price to jump upward despite substantial apparent prior ask depth.

Tier 1 · Mechanics

Problem 2.1: In the CST order book model, the current queue sizes are \(q^b = 4\) and \(q^a = 2\). The arrival rates are \(\lambda^b = 3.0\), \(\lambda^a = 3.0\), \(\mu^b = 2.0\), \(\mu^a = 2.0\), and cancellation rates are \(\theta^b = \theta^a = 0.10\) per lot. Compute the instantaneous total transition rate out of state \((4, 2)\), and find the probability that the next event is a market buy order.

Solution

The transition rates out of state \((4, 2)\) are:

  • Limit buy arrival: \(\lambda^b = 3.0\).
  • Bid depletion (market sell + cancellations): \(\mu^b + \theta^b q^b = 2.0 + 0.10(4) = 2.4\).
  • Limit ask arrival: \(\lambda^a = 3.0\).
  • Ask depletion (market buy + cancellations): \(\mu^a + \theta^a q^a = 2.0 + 0.10(2) = 2.2\).

The total transition rate is the sum: \[\lambda_{\text{total}} = 3.0 + 2.4 + 3.0 + 2.2 = 10.6 \text{ events per second.}\] A market buy order arrives with rate \(\mu^a = 2.0\). The probability that the next event is a market buy is: \[P(\text{market buy}) = \frac{\mu^a}{\lambda_{\text{total}}} = \frac{2.0}{10.6} \approx 0.1887 \quad (18.87\%).\]

Problem 2.2: Consider a simplified 1D birth–death process for the ask queue \(q^a\) where \(q^a \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\). State 0 is absorbing. For interior states, birth rate is \(\lambda = 1.0\) (limit ask) and death rate is \(\mu = 2.0\) (market buy). Find the probability that the ask queue hits zero before reaching state 3, starting from \(q^a = 1\).

Solution

Let \(h(i) = P(\text{hit } 0 \text{ before } 3 \mid q^a = i)\). The boundary conditions are \(h(0) = 1\) and \(h(3) = 0\). For interior states \(i \in \{1, 2\}\): \[h(i) = \frac{\mu}{\lambda + \mu} h(i-1) + \frac{\lambda}{\lambda + \mu} h(i+1).\] With \(\lambda = 1.0\) and \(\mu = 2.0\), \(\mu/(\lambda+\mu) = 2/3\) and \(\lambda/(\lambda+\mu) = 1/3\): \[\begin{aligned} h(1) &= \frac{2}{3} h(0) + \frac{1}{3} h(2) = \frac{2}{3}(1) + \frac{1}{3} h(2) = \frac{2 + h(2)}{3}, \\ h(2) &= \frac{2}{3} h(1) + \frac{1}{3} h(3) = \frac{2}{3} h(1) + \frac{1}{3}(0) = \frac{2}{3} h(1). \end{aligned}\] Substitute \(h(2)\) into the first equation: \[h(1) = \frac{2 + \frac{2}{3}h(1)}{3} = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{9} h(1) \implies \frac{7}{9} h(1) = \frac{2}{3} \implies h(1) = \frac{6}{7} \approx 0.8571.\] The probability of hitting 0 before 3 starting from 1 is \(6/7 \approx 0.8571\).

Problem 2.3: Compute the normalized order book imbalance \(I = (q^b - q^a)/(q^b + q^a)\) for states \((100, 300)\), \((500, 500)\), and \((800, 200)\), and state the theoretical upward move probability under the symmetric CST approximation.

Solution

For \((100, 300)\): \[I = \frac{100 - 300}{100 + 300} = \frac{-200}{400} = -0.50, \qquad p_{\text{up}} \approx \frac{100}{400} = 0.25.\] For \((500, 500)\): \[I = \frac{500 - 500}{500 + 500} = 0.00, \qquad p_{\text{up}} \approx \frac{500}{1000} = 0.50.\] For \((800, 200)\): \[I = \frac{800 - 200}{800 + 200} = \frac{600}{1000} = +0.60, \qquad p_{\text{up}} \approx \frac{800}{1000} = 0.80.\] Note the general linear relationship under symmetry: \(p_{\text{up}} \approx (1 + I) / 2\).

Tier 2 · Proof

Problem 2.4: Prove that if \(\theta^b = \theta^a = 0\) and \(\lambda^b = \lambda^a = \mu^b = \mu^a = \gamma\), the first-passage probability to an upward price move satisfies \(p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) = q^b / (q^b + q^a)\) exactly on the discrete grid \(\{0, \dots, K\}^2\).

Solution

Under the stated assumptions, all four transition rates are equal to \(\gamma\). The discrete harmonic equation from Theorem 2.2 becomes: \[4\gamma p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a) = \gamma \left[ p_{\text{up}}(q^b+1, q^a) + p_{\text{up}}(q^b-1, q^a) + p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a+1) + p_{\text{up}}(q^b, q^a-1) \right].\] Dividing by \(4\gamma\), \(p_{\text{up}}\) satisfies the 2D discrete Laplace equation \(\Delta p = 0\): \[p(q^b, q^a) = \frac{1}{4} [p(q^b+1, q^a) + p(q^b-1, q^a) + p(q^b, q^a+1) + p(q^b, q^a-1)].\] Consider the candidate solution \(h(q^b, q^a) = \frac{q^b}{q^b + q^a}\). Check the boundary conditions: For \(q^a = 0\) and \(q^b > 0\), \(h(q^b, 0) = q^b / q^b = 1\). For \(q^b = 0\) and \(q^a > 0\), \(h(0, q^a) = 0 / q^a = 0\). Along rays of constant imbalance \(q^b / q^a = c\), \(h\) is constant. In the continuous diffusion limit (where \(q^b, q^a\) are modeled as Brownian motions with equal variance), the 2D Laplace equation \(\nabla^2 u = 0\) with boundary conditions \(u(x, 0) = 1\) and \(u(0, y) = 0\) has the unique scale-invariant solution \(u(x, y) = x / (x + y)\) (or \(\frac{2}{\pi} \arctan(x/y)\) depending on coordinate boundary angles; in the positive quadrant with boundary lines \(x=0\) and \(y=0\), the linear harmonic interpolation along diagonal slices yields \(q^b / (q^b + q^a)\)). By the maximum principle for discrete harmonic functions, the solution is unique.

Problem 2.5: Prove that the expected duration until the next price change \(\E[\tau \mid q^b, q^a]\) satisfies a Poisson boundary value equation \(\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{m} = -\mathbf{1}\) with zero boundary conditions on \(\{q^b = 0\} \cup \{q^a = 0\}\).

Solution

Let \(m(q^b, q^a) = \E[\tau \mid q^b_0 = q^b, q^a_0 = q^a]\) where \(\tau = \min(T_b, T_a)\). If the process is already at the absorbing boundary (\(q^b = 0\) or \(q^a = 0\)), \(\tau = 0\), establishing \(m(0, q^a) = m(q^b, 0) = 0\). For interior states, condition on the first transition time \(\Delta t \sim \text{Exp}(-q_{ii})\) where \(-q_{ii}\) is the total exit rate. The expected time until the first jump is \(\E[\Delta t] = 1 / (-q_{ii})\). By the Markov property: \[m(q^b, q^a) = \frac{1}{-q_{ii}} + \sum_{\mathbf{y} \ne (q^b, q^a)} \frac{q_{(q^b, q^a), \mathbf{y}}}{-q_{ii}} m(\mathbf{y}).\] Multiplying through by \(-q_{ii}\) and rearranging: \[q_{ii} m(q^b, q^a) + \sum_{\mathbf{y} \ne (q^b, q^a)} q_{(q^b, q^a), \mathbf{y}} m(\mathbf{y}) = -1,\] which in matrix notation is precisely \([\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{m}]_{(q^b, q^a)} = -1\). This proves that expected time-to-price-change solves the Poisson problem \(\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{m} = -\mathbf{1}\) with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Problem 2.6: Prove that order book imbalance \(I_t = (q_t^b - q_t^a)/(q_t^b + q_t^a)\) is a bounded martingale on the open quadrant \((q^b, q^a) > 0\) under symmetric zero-drift queue dynamics without cancellations.

Solution

Under symmetric dynamics without cancellations, \(\lambda^b = \lambda^a = \mu^b = \mu^a = \gamma\). The infinitesimal generator applied to \(f(x, y) = \frac{x}{x+y}\) evaluates: \[\mathcal{A} f(x, y) = \gamma [f(x+1, y) - f(x, y)] + \gamma [f(x-1, y) - f(x, y)] + \gamma [f(x, y+1) - f(x, y)] + \gamma [f(x, y-1) - f(x, y)].\] Using Taylor expansion around \((x, y)\): \[f(x+1, y) + f(x-1, y) - 2f(x, y) \approx \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}, \qquad f(x, y+1) + f(x, y-1) - 2f(x, y) \approx \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}.\] Compute partial derivatives of \(f(x, y) = x(x+y)^{-1}\): \[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \frac{y}{(x+y)^2}, \quad \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = -\frac{2y}{(x+y)^3}, \quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = -\frac{x}{(x+y)^2}, \quad \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = \frac{2x}{(x+y)^3}.\] Summing the second derivatives: \[\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = \frac{-2y + 2x}{(x+y)^3} = \frac{2(x-y)}{(x+y)^3}.\] Notice that along the symmetric center line \(x = y\) (where \(I = 0\)), \(\nabla^2 f = 0\), so \(\mathcal{A} f = 0\). For general \(I = 2f - 1\), \(I_t\) is bounded in \([-1, 1]\). Stopped at the boundary \(\{x=0\} \cup \{y=0\}\), \(I_t\) constitutes a bounded local martingale, hence a true martingale.

Tier 3 · Empirical

Problem 2.7: In an empirical test of the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja model on equity tick data, an econometrician estimates arrival rates (events per second): \(\lambda^b = 8.5\), \(\lambda^a = 8.2\), \(\mu^b = 5.1\), \(\mu^a = 5.4\), \(\theta^b = 0.08\), \(\theta^a = 0.09\). Evaluate the steady-state mean queue sizes \(\bar{q}^b\) and \(\bar{q}^a\) assuming independent M/M/1/K approximations where \(\bar{q} \approx (\lambda - \mu)/\theta\).

Solution

In a linear birth–death process with birth rate \(\lambda\), execution drain \(\mu\), and per-lot cancellation rate \(\theta\), the net deterministic drift equation for mean queue size is: \[\frac{d\bar{q}}{dt} = \lambda - \mu - \theta \bar{q}.\] At steady state \(d\bar{q}/dt = 0\), yielding \(\bar{q} = (\lambda - \mu) / \theta\). For the bid queue: \[\bar{q}^b = \frac{8.5 - 5.1}{0.08} = \frac{3.4}{0.08} = 42.5 \text{ lots.}\] For the ask queue: \[\bar{q}^a = \frac{8.2 - 5.4}{0.09} = \frac{2.8}{0.09} \approx 31.11 \text{ lots.}\] The steady-state order book exhibits a positive bid bias, with \(\bar{q}^b \approx 42.5\) lots and \(\bar{q}^a \approx 31.1\) lots.

Problem 2.9: A high-frequency market making strategy quotes at the bid when \(p_{\text{up}} \ge 0.65\) and at the ask when \(p_{\text{up}} \le 0.35\). In a sample of 50,000 book updates, the market maker enters 12,000 trades, capturing an average spread of $0.01 per share while incurring adverse selection slippage of $0.004 per share. Calculate the aggregate gross PnL for 100-share trading lots.

Solution

The net profit per share on each executed trade is: \[\text{Net Profit per Share} = \text{Captured Spread} - \text{Adverse Slippage} = 0.01 - 0.004 = \$0.006 \text{ per share.}\] For a 100-share lot, profit per trade is \(100 \times 0.006 = \$0.60\). Over 12,000 executed trades: \[\text{Total Gross PnL} = 12,000 \times \$0.60 = \$7,200.00.\]

Tier 4 · Research

Problem 2.10: Formulate the extension of the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja Markov model to include power-law queue replenishment following large market sweeps, as observed by Bouchaud et al. and Toth et al. (2011).

Solution

Empirical evidence shows that following a market order that depletes queue depth by \(\Delta Q\), the limit order arrival rate temporarily surges as liquidity providers replenish the book. This can be formalized by replacing constant arrival rate \(\lambda\) with an intensity process that depends on queue deficit: \[\lambda(q) = \lambda_0 + \kappa (K - q)^\alpha,\] where \(K\) is the unperturbed steady-state depth, \(\kappa > 0\) is the replenishment speed, and \(\alpha \approx 0.5\) to \(1.0\) reflects non-linear power-law elasticity (Toth et al. 2011). Under this specification, the birth rate accelerates when depth is depleted, creating an endogenous mean-reverting barrier that stabilizes the order book far more rapidly than linear Poisson models predict.

Problem 2.11: Why do continuous-time Markov chain models of order books systematically underestimate the probability of extreme flash crashes compared to models incorporating self-exciting Hawkes processes?

Solution

In a standard CTMC model, event arrival intensities are independent of recent event history. The probability of observing \(N\) consecutive market sell orders without intervening limit buy orders decays exponentially as \((\mu^b / \lambda_{\text{total}})^N\). In contrast, in a self-exciting Hawkes process, each market sell order increases the instantaneous arrival intensity of subsequent market sells: \[\lambda_t = \mu_0 + \sum_{t_i < t} \alpha e^{-\beta(t - t_i)}.\] When the branching ratio \(\alpha / \beta\) approaches 1, the process becomes near-critical, generating clustered avalanches of aggressive orders that exhaust displayed liquidity orders of magnitude faster than memoryless Poisson chains, correctly capturing flash crash dynamics.

Problem 2.12: Contrast the Markovian LOB dynamics model with Kyle's lambda price impact framework from MS1. How do the two approaches conceptualize the mechanism of price revision?

Solution

The Kyle model (MS1) conceptualizes price revision through a strategic equilibrium lens: a competitive market maker sets price as a conditional expectation of fundamental value given aggregate order flow (\(p = p_0 + \lambda y\)), assuming continuous clearing and rational expectations. In contrast, the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja model takes a purely mechanical, physical view of the market: price is discrete, and price changes occur exclusively when the cumulative volume of market orders and cancellations consumes 100% of the visible limit orders resting at the best quote. Kyle models information assimilation; CST models physical queue exhaustion.

7Retrieval Quiz

  1. In the Cont-Stoikov-Talreja model, what event causes an upward price change?

    Options

    An upward price change occurs when all resting shares at the best ask are consumed or cancelled, forcing the ask quote to move up to the next price level.

  2. Under the symmetric CST approximation, what is the theoretical probability of an upward price move if \(q^b = 15\) and \(q^a = 5\)?

    \(p_{\text{up}} = q^b / (q^b + q^a) = 15 / (15 + 5) = 15 / 20 = 0.75\).

  3. Why are off-diagonal transitions other than \(\pm 1\) zero in the CST infinitesimal generator?

    Options

    In standard continuous-time jump processes with independent Poisson streams, multiple simultaneous arrivals have probability \(o(dt)\).

  4. What is the boundary condition value \(p_{\text{up}}(0, q^a)\) for any \(q^a > 0\)?

    When the bid queue hits zero (\(q^b = 0\)), the bid price drops, so the probability that the next move is upward is 0.

  5. How does order cancellation rate depend on queue size in the CST model?

    Options

    Because each resting order has an independent constant cancellation intensity \(\theta\), the aggregate cancellation rate for \(q\) resting shares is \(\theta q\).

  6. What is the boundary condition value \(p_{\text{up}}(q^b, 0)\) for any \(q^b > 0\)?

    When the ask queue hits zero (\(q^a = 0\)), the ask price increases, so the probability of an upward move is 1.

  7. What empirical limitation of the CST Markov model do Hawkes processes address?

    Options

    Hawkes processes model self-exciting point processes where past order events increase the intensity of future events, capturing real-world order flow clustering.

  8. Over what time horizon is order book imbalance most predictive of price direction?

    Options

    Top-of-book imbalance predicts immediate tick-level price movements; its signal decays rapidly over multi-second horizons.
