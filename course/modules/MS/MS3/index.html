Market Impact & Optimal Execution

Large institutional transactions cannot be executed instantaneously without inflicting severe price concessions upon the investor. Grounded in the microstructure foundations of MS1 and the continuous-time stochastic control methods of SC6, this module develops the theoretical, econometric, and algorithmic principles governing modern trade scheduling. Across six rigorous lessons, we analyze the empirical square-root law of market impact (Lesson 1), transient price impact and power-law propagator models that prevent round-trip price manipulation (Lesson 2), the calculus of variations in the Almgren–Chriss framework balancing market impact against inventory risk (Lesson 3), the geometry and convexity of the Pareto-efficient execution frontier (Lesson 4), the Obizhaeva–Wang model of limit order book resilience and discrete boundary block trading (Lesson 5), and the Perold implementation shortfall attribution methodology (Lesson 6). The module culminates in delivering milestone A10 of Atlas: an institutional-grade execution and cost engine (atlas.execution).

  • Prerequisites: MS1 Microstructure Theory and SC6 Stochastic Optimal Control.
  • Unlocks: MS4 Costs, Capacity & Implementation Shortfall, and STUDIO-MS Microstructure Studio.
  • Project: Atlas A10: execution and cost models (atlas/execution.py), providing square-root impact, the Almgren–Chriss optimal scheduler, and Perold implementation shortfall accounting.
  • Hours: 56 lesson study hours across 6 lessons + 24 project hours = 80 total hours.

Objectives

By the end of MS3 you can:

  1. Calibrate the empirical square-root law of market impact \(I(Q) \approx Y \sigma \sqrt{Q/V}\) and estimate the dimensionless prefactor \(Y\) across institutional execution records (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate transient market impact via the propagator model and prove the absence of price-manipulation arbitrage under slowly decaying power-law response kernels (Lesson 2).
  3. Derive the Euler–Lagrange optimality equations in the Almgren–Chriss model and solve for closed-form hyperbolic liquidation trajectories balancing price impact against volatility exposure (Lesson 3).
  4. Trace the efficient execution frontier in expected cost–variance space, verify its strict convexity, and demonstrate that linear TWAP is the unique cost-minimizing pacing for risk-neutral traders (Lesson 4).
  5. Solve the Obizhaeva–Wang model of limit order book resilience, proving the optimality of discrete initial and terminal block trades separated by steady continuous trading (Lesson 5).
  6. Decompose execution slippage using implementation shortfall into delay cost, realized price appreciation, opportunity cost of unexecuted shares, and explicit commissions (Lesson 6).
  7. Build milestone A10 of Atlas (atlas.execution), verified by autograder across 50 seeded market environments (Project).

Prerequisites

MS3 assumes mastery of both market microstructure theory (MS1) and continuous-time stochastic control (SC6). From MS1, you should be fully conversant with Kyle's auction model and Kyle's lambda (\(\lambda = \Cov(v, p) / \Var(p)\)), Glosten–Milgrom adverse selection, dealer inventory risk, Roll's effective spread estimator, econometric spread decomposition, the probability of informed trading (PIN), and VPIN toxicity. From SC6, you should understand the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation, stochastic optimal control, the verification theorem, viscosity solutions, finite-difference schemes, and policy iteration. Through the prerequisite closure, MS3 also applies mean-variance optimization, the Bellman equation, and the principle of optimality from M7.

Placement diagnostic

Eight questions: six on prerequisite concepts (MS1 and SC6), and two skill checks marked with data-skill testing skills taught in rungs 1 and 2 of the project. The mastery bar is 80 percent (at least 7 correct of 8). To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the mastery bar and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. In Kyle's single-period auction model (MS1), how does Kyle's lambda \(\lambda\) scale with the variance of noise trader volume \(\sigma_u^2\) and the prior uncertainty of asset value \(\sigma_v^2\)?

    Options

    In Kyle (1985), \(\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\sigma_v^2}}{2 \sqrt{\sigma_u^2}} = \frac{1}{2}\frac{\sigma_v}{\sigma_u}\). Higher noise trader volume provides camouflage for informed orders, reducing price impact.

  2. In the Roll model (MS1), if consecutive price change autocovariance is \(\Cov(\Delta p_t, \Delta p_{t-1}) = -0.0025\), what is the estimated effective half-spread \(c = \sqrt{-\Cov}\) in cents?

    \(c = \sqrt{0.0025} = 0.05\) dollars, which is 5.0 cents; half-spread \(c = 0.05\), but if asking in cents: 5.0 cents. Wait, if \(\sqrt{0.0025} = 0.05\) dollars = 5.0 cents. Let's make the answer 5 cents.

  3. In stochastic optimal control (SC6), what is the role of the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation in execution problems?

    Options

    The HJB PDE is the continuous-time analog of the Bellman equation, expressing the infinitesimal optimality condition for the value function.

  4. Under Itô's lemma (M7), if \(S_t\) follows geometric Brownian motion \(dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t\) with \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the quadratic variation coefficient \((\sigma S_t)^2 / S_t^2\)?

    \(\sigma^2 = 0.20^2 = 0.04\).

  5. In Glosten & Milgrom (1985), why is sequential trade price impact permanent rather than temporary?

    Options

    Order flow contains information: Bayesian market makers update their posterior expectations of fundamental value, leading to permanent quote revisions.

  6. Why does a finite-difference discretization of an execution HJB PDE require an upwind or monotone scheme (SC6)?

    Options

    Monotone finite difference schemes ensure convergence to the unique viscosity solution per the Barles–Souganidis theorem (SC6).

  7. Skill check · Square-root impact: A stock has daily trading volume \(V = 4{,}000{,}000\) shares and daily price volatility \(\sigma = 2.0\%\). Using the standard square-root law \(I = Y \sigma \sqrt{Q / V}\) with \(Y = 0.50\), compute the expected market impact (in basis points) of liquidating \(Q = 40{,}000\) shares.

    \(Q / V = 40{,}000 / 4{,}000{,}000 = 0.01\). \(\sqrt{Q / V} = \sqrt{0.01} = 0.10\). Impact \(I = 0.50 \times 0.02 \times 0.10 = 0.0010 = 10.0\) basis points.

  8. Skill check · Implementation shortfall: A fund manager decides to purchase \(100{,}000\) shares when the decision price is \(P_0 = \$50.00\). When the order reaches the market, the arrival price has risen to \(P_{\text{arr}} = \$50.02\). The entire order of \(100{,}000\) shares is executed at an average price \(P_{\text{arr}}\). What is the total delay cost (\$) incurred before trading commenced?

    \(\text{Delay Cost} = Q_{\text{exec}} \times (P_{\text{arr}} - P_0) = 100{,}000 \times (\$50.02 - \$50.00) = 100{,}000 \times \$0.02 = \$2{,}000.00\).

Lesson Map

The 56 lesson hours are distributed across six focused units:

  1. Lesson 01 · The Square-Root Impact Law 9 hours

    Empirical universality of sub-linear square-root market impact, latent order book dynamics, metaorder duration independence, and collapse of naive linear cost models.

  2. Lesson 02 · Propagator Models of Impact 9 hours

    Transient market impact formulation, power-law response kernels, resolution of the Gatheral price-manipulation arbitrage paradox, and the subtle cancellation maintaining market efficiency.

  3. Lesson 03 · Almgren–Chriss Optimal Execution 10 hours

    Calculus of variations and discrete Euler–Lagrange equations for optimal liquidation, balancing temporary price impact against inventory price risk under quadratic penalties.

  4. Lesson 04 · The Efficient Execution Frontier 9 hours

    Pareto efficiency in cost–variance space, strict convexity of the execution frontier, utility indifference curves, and proof of uniform TWAP optimality in the risk-neutral limit.

  5. Lesson 05 · The Obizhaeva–Wang Model 10 hours

    Limit order book depth density and exponential liquidity resilience, optimal discrete boundary block executions at trade onset and termination, and continuous steady rate pacing.

  6. Lesson 06 · Implementation Shortfall & TCA 9 hours

    Perold transaction cost analysis, rigorous 4-way decomposition into delay cost, price appreciation, opportunity cost of unexecuted shares, and explicit commissions.
