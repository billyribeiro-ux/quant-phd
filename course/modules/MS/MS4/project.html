Factor Strategy Capacity Study

Build an institutional capacity and execution cost engine under the empirical square-root law of market impact. You will derive trade lists and turnover, compute portfolio-level friction coefficients, trace net alpha decay, solve for break-even and profit-maximizing assets under management (AUM), implement buy–hold buffer zones with hysteresis to mitigate trading drag, and author an analytical research memo evaluating strategy viability across market regimes.

Four-rung project ladder

  1. Worked example

    Walk through an end-to-end worked solution on a calibrated 5-asset portfolio (Lab 1), inspecting trade allocation, square-root execution drag, analytical capacity limits, and buffer-zone weights.

  2. Completion

    Complete the core algebraic relations for the portfolio friction coefficient, capacity extrema, and buffer filtering in an instant-feedback browser lab (Lab 2).

  3. Independent

    Implement the complete capacity engine (capacity.py) evaluated across 50 seeded market universes by autograder, and author the analytical research memo (report.md) evaluated by rubric.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the capacity engine to dynamic multi-period rebalancing under multi-strategy trade netting and state-dependent liquidity regimes.

"""Lab MS4-project-1: Rung 1 worked example · Factor strategy capacity calibration.

Provides an end-to-end worked solution computing trade lists, square-root market impact,
portfolio friction kappa, capacity limits, and buffer mitigation on a calibrated universe.
SIMULATED: 5-asset portfolio with known ground-truth parameters.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def run_worked_example() -> None:
    prices = [25.0, 50.0, 75.0, 100.0, 125.0]
    adv = [10000000.0, 20000000.0, 15000000.0, 30000000.0, 25000000.0]
    sigma = [0.020, 0.018, 0.025, 0.015, 0.022]
    w_initial = [0.20, 0.20, 0.20, 0.20, 0.20]
    w_target = [0.35, 0.10, 0.05, 0.30, 0.20]
    aum = 10000000.0
    gross_alpha = 0.10
    prefactor_y = 0.50

    # Step 1: Compute trade lists
    n = len(w_initial)
    dollar_trades = [aum * (w_target[i] - w_initial[i]) for i in range(n)]
    share_trades = [dollar_trades[i] / prices[i] for i in range(n)]
    turnover_dollars = 0.5 * sum(abs(d) for d in dollar_trades)
    turnover_frac = turnover_dollars / aum

    print("Step 1 · Trade Allocation and Turnover:")
    print(f"  AUM:                   {aum:.2f}")
    for i in range(n):
        print(f"  Asset {i}: trade dollars = {dollar_trades[i]:.2f}, shares = {share_trades[i]:.2f}")
    print(f"  Turnover Dollars:      {turnover_dollars:.2f}")
    print(f"  Turnover Fraction:     {turnover_frac:.4f} ({turnover_frac * 100.0:.2f}%)")

    # Step 2: Estimate market impact costs
    total_cost = 0.0
    total_traded = sum(abs(d) for d in dollar_trades)
    for i in range(n):
        abs_d = abs(dollar_trades[i])
        if abs_d > 1e-9:
            impact = prefactor_y * sigma[i] * math.sqrt(abs_d / adv[i])
            cost_i = abs_d * impact
            total_cost += cost_i
    eff_bps = (total_cost / total_traded) * 10000.0 if total_traded > 0 else 0.0

    print("Step 2 · Square-Root Market Impact:")
    print(f"  Total Impact Cost:     {total_cost:.2f}")
    print(f"  Effective Spread:      {eff_bps:.2f} bps")

    # Step 3: Find capacity limits
    kappa = prefactor_y * sum(sigma[i] * (abs(w_target[i] - w_initial[i]) ** 1.5) / math.sqrt(adv[i]) for i in range(n))
    a_star = (gross_alpha / kappa) ** 2
    a_opt = (4.0 / 9.0) * a_star
    max_d_alpha = (4.0 / 27.0) * (gross_alpha**3) / (kappa**2)
    net_opt = gross_alpha / 3.0

    print("Step 3 · Strategy Capacity Limits:")
    print(f"  Portfolio Friction:    {kappa:.8e}")
    print(f"  Break-even AUM A*:     {a_star:.2f}")
    print(f"  Optimal AUM A_opt:     {a_opt:.2f}")
    print(f"  A_opt / A* Ratio:      {a_opt / a_star:.4f}")
    print(f"  Net Alpha at A_opt:    {net_opt * 100.0:.2f}% ({net_opt * 10000.0:.1f} bps)")
    print(f"  Max Dollar Alpha:      {max_d_alpha:.2f}")

    # Step 4: Cost mitigation via buffer zones
    ranks = [1.0, 5.0, 4.0, 2.0, 3.0]
    target_k = 3
    buffer_ratio = 1.5
    k_hold = math.floor(target_k * buffer_ratio)  # 4

    # Existing holdings: assets where w_initial > 1e-6 (all 5 held)
    is_held = [w > 1e-6 for w in w_initial]
    retained = [i for i in range(n) if is_held[i] and ranks[i] <= k_hold]
    retained.sort(key=lambda idx: ranks[idx])
    selected = retained[:target_k]

    w_mitigated = [0.0] * n
    for idx in selected:
        w_mitigated[idx] = 1.0 / target_k

    print("Step 4 · Cost Mitigation Weights:")
    for i in range(n):
        print(f"  Asset {i}: weight = {w_mitigated[i]:.4f}")
    print(f"  Weights Sum:           {sum(w_mitigated):.6f}")


def main() -> None:
    run_worked_example()

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A quantitative asset management firm operates an equity factor strategy. As assets under management expand, portfolio rebalancing incurs severe execution drag via the square-root law of market impact. You will build a production-grade capacity modeling module (capacity.py) that computes trade lists, models market impact, traces net alpha degradation, derives analytical break-even and profit-maximizing capacity thresholds, and implements buy–hold buffer zones with hysteresis to mitigate turnover. In addition, you will author an analytical research memo (report.md) advising the investment committee on optimal scaling and execution policy.
Inputs

Market impact and portfolio scaling

Under the empirical square-root law of market impact (Almgren et al. 2001; Bouchaud et al. 2018), executing a dollar trade \(D_i = A |\Delta w_i|\) incurs execution price concession \(I_i = Y \sigma_i \sqrt{D_i / \text{ADV}_i}\). Aggregating dollar costs across all \(N\) portfolio constituents yields \(\mathcal{C}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^N D_i I_i = Y A^{3/2} \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i \frac{|\Delta w_i|^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\text{ADV}_i}} = \kappa A^{3/2}\), where \(\kappa \equiv Y \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i \frac{|\Delta w_i|^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\text{ADV}_i}}\). Consequently, unit transaction costs expressed as a fraction of fund AUM scale sub-linearly with the square root of assets: \(\mathcal{C}(A) / A = \kappa \sqrt{A}\). This fundamentally refutes naive linear transaction cost assumptions, establishing that unit costs increase continuously with scale.

  • Meets C1 level 4: completely derives the \(A^{3/2}\) total cost scaling and \(\kappa \sqrt{A}\) fractional cost scaling, providing exact definitions for all structural parameters.
Capacity closed forms and optimality

Net annualized alpha follows \(\alpha^{\text{net}}(A) = \alpha_0 - \kappa \sqrt{A}\). Setting net alpha to zero yields the break-even capacity \(A^* = (\alpha_0 / \kappa)^2\). However, an institutional asset manager seeking to maximize total firm value optimizes total dollar alpha: \(\mathcal{P}(A) \equiv A \cdot \alpha^{\text{net}}(A) = \alpha_0 A - \kappa A^{3/2}\). Differentiating with respect to \(A\) gives \(\frac{d\mathcal{P}}{dA} = \alpha_0 - \frac{3}{2}\kappa A^{1/2} = 0\), establishing the unique global maximum at \(A_{\text{opt}} = \left(\frac{2\alpha_0}{3\kappa}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9} A^* \approx 0.4444 A^*\). At this optimal operating scale, net alpha is exactly \(\alpha^{\text{net}}(A_{\text{opt}}) = \alpha_0 - \kappa \left(\frac{2\alpha_0}{3\kappa}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\alpha_0\), and total dollar alpha reaches \(\mathcal{P}(A_{\text{opt}}) = \frac{4}{27} \frac{\alpha_0^3}{\kappa^2}\). Expanding AUM beyond \(A_{\text{opt}}\) toward \(A^*\) degrades total economic profit despite generating positive net return.

  • Meets C2 level 4: provides rigorous proofs of both break-even and profit-maximizing capacities, establishing the exact \(\frac{4}{9}\) ratio and \(\frac{1}{3}\alpha_0\) net alpha property.

Rung 4 · Stretch extensions

The standard model evaluates capacity for a static single-period rebalance. For portfolio extension and advanced research, explore:

  1. Multi-strategy cross-netting: Implement an integrated rebalancing engine that combines Value, Momentum, and Quality sleeves, netting opposing trades internally before routing to market. Measure the portfolio-level netting ratio \(1 - \frac{\sum_i |\sum_s D_{i,s}|}{\sum_i \sum_s |D_{i,s}|}\) and trace the resulting capacity expansion.
  2. Dynamic multi-period rebalancing: Model order execution across multi-day trading horizons using the Garleanu–Pedersen framework, balancing tracking error against transient and permanent market impact.
