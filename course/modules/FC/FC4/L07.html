The Residual-Income Valuation Model

  • Uses: clean-surplus-relation, dividend-discount-model
  • Introduces: residual-income-valuation-model
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled

Traditional accounting net income ignores the opportunity cost of equity capital provided by shareholders. A firm can report substantial positive accounting profits while destroying shareholder wealth if those earnings fail to cover the cost of equity. The residual-income valuation model bridges financial accounting and modern asset pricing by valuing equity as current book value plus the present value of expected economic profit, reconciling fundamental accounting data directly with intrinsic value.

1Definition and Economic Profit Mechanics

Economic profit differs fundamentally from accounting net income. Accounting standards deduct interest expenses paid to debt holders but treat equity capital as free. Residual income deducts an explicit capital charge for the opportunity cost of equity.

Definition 1.1 (Residual Income)

Residual income (\(RI_t\)), also termed abnormal earnings or economic profit to equity, is net income generated in period \(t\) minus the required equity charge on the capital employed at the beginning of the period:

\[ \begin{aligned} RI_t = NI_t - r \cdot B_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

where \(NI_t\) is comprehensive net income, \(r\) is the cost of equity capital, and \(B_{t-1}\) is the book value of common equity at the beginning of period \(t\).

Equivalently, expressing return on beginning equity as \(\mathrm{ROE}_t = NI_t / B_{t-1}\), residual income can be written as the economic spread earned over the cost of capital scaled by the book equity base:

\[ \begin{aligned} RI_t = (\mathrm{ROE}_t - r) \cdot B_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

When a corporation earns exactly its cost of capital (\(\mathrm{ROE}_t = r\)), residual income is zero, and operations generate no abnormal shareholder wealth beyond fair compensation for risk.

Definition 1.2 (Residual-Income Valuation Model)

The residual-income valuation model values intrinsic equity \(V_0\) as the sum of current balance sheet book equity \(B_0\) and the discounted present value of all future expected residual income streams:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_0 = B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^\infty \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} \end{aligned} \]

Over a finite explicit forecast horizon \(N\), the model is specified as:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_0 = B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{PV_N^{\text{terminal}}}{(1 + r)^N} \end{aligned} \]

where \(PV_N^{\text{terminal}}\) represents the capitalized present value at date \(N\) of residual income generated beyond year \(N\).

2The Preinreich-Lücke-Wallis Equivalence Theorem

A foundational theoretical insight of modern corporate finance is that valuation models based on discounted dividends and valuation models based on discounted residual income yield mathematically identical results whenever accounting satisfies the clean-surplus relation.

Theorem 2.1 (Preinreich-Lücke-Wallis Equivalence)

Assume the clean-surplus relation holds: \(B_t = B_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t\) for all \(t \ge 1\), with discount rate \(r > -1\). For any finite planning horizon \(N \ge 1\), the residual income valuation with terminal book value equals the dividend discount model value with terminal value \(B_N\):

\[ \begin{aligned} B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} = \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{B_N}{(1 + r)^N} \end{aligned} \]
Proof

Under clean surplus, express net income as \(NI_t = B_t - B_{t-1} + D_t\). Substitute this into the definition of residual income:

\[ \begin{aligned} RI_t &= NI_t - r B_{t-1} \\ &= (B_t - B_{t-1} + D_t) - r B_{t-1} \\ &= D_t + B_t - (1 + r) B_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

Discounting both sides by \((1 + r)^t\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} &= \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{B_t}{(1 + r)^t} - \frac{(1 + r) B_{t-1}}{(1 + r)^t} \\ &= \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{B_t}{(1 + r)^t} - \frac{B_{t-1}}{(1 + r)^{t-1}} \end{aligned} \]

Summing from \(t = 1\) to \(N\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} = \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \sum_{t=1}^N \left(\frac{B_t}{(1 + r)^t} - \frac{B_{t-1}}{(1 + r)^{t-1}}\right) \end{aligned} \]

The second sum on the right-hand side is a telescoping series:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=1}^N \left(\frac{B_t}{(1 + r)^t} - \frac{B_{t-1}}{(1 + r)^{t-1}}\right) = \frac{B_N}{(1 + r)^N} - \frac{B_0}{(1 + r)^0} = \frac{B_N}{(1 + r)^N} - B_0 \end{aligned} \]

Substituting back:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} = \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{B_N}{(1 + r)^N} - B_0 \end{aligned} \]

Adding \(B_0\) to both sides establishes the exact identity:

\[ \begin{aligned} B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t} = \sum_{t=1}^N \frac{D_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{B_N}{(1 + r)^N} \end{aligned} \]

Thus the residual income model and DDM produce identical valuations. \(\blacksquare\)

Lab 1 · Residual-income valuation vs DDM equivalence

Verifies the Preinreich-Lücke-Wallis equivalence between 5-year residual income valuation and DDM on a SIMULATED accounting path with cost of equity r = 10%.

"""Lab FC4-L07-1: Residual-income valuation vs DDM equivalence."""

from fractions import Fraction


def main() -> None:
    # Illustrative path from L06 (SIMULATED)
    b0 = Fraction(500)
    net_income = [Fraction(100), Fraction(115), Fraction(130), Fraction(145), Fraction(160)]
    dividends = [Fraction(40), Fraction(45), Fraction(50), Fraction(55), Fraction(65)]
    r = Fraction(10, 100)  # 10% cost of equity

    factor = Fraction(1) + r

    # 1. DDM Value with terminal book value B_N
    # V_0^DDM = sum_{t=1}^N D_t / (1+r)^t + B_N / (1+r)^N
    b = b0
    pv_divs = Fraction(0)
    disc = Fraction(1)
    for ni, d in zip(net_income, dividends, strict=True):
        disc *= factor
        pv_divs += d / disc
        b = b + ni - d
    b_n = b
    pv_bn = b_n / (factor ** len(dividends))
    v0_ddm = pv_divs + pv_bn

    # 2. Residual Income Valuation
    # RI_t = NetIncome_t - r * B_{t-1}
    # V_0^RI = B_0 + sum_{t=1}^N RI_t / (1+r)^t
    b_track = b0
    pv_ri = Fraction(0)
    disc = Fraction(1)
    ri_list: list[Fraction] = []
    charge_list: list[Fraction] = []

    for ni, d in zip(net_income, dividends, strict=True):
        charge = r * b_track
        ri = ni - charge
        disc *= factor
        pv_ri += ri / disc
        charge_list.append(charge)
        ri_list.append(ri)
        b_track = b_track + ni - d

    v0_ri = b0 + pv_ri

    print("SIMULATED Residual Income vs DDM Equivalence Under Clean Surplus")
    print(f"Cost of Equity r = {float(r):.2%}, Initial Book Value B_0 = ${float(b0):.2f}M")
    print("-" * 72)
    print(f"{'Year t':>8} {'Net Income':>12} {'Beg Book B_{t-1}':>18} {'Equity Charge':>16} {'Residual Income':>16}")
    print("-" * 72)

    b_disp = b0
    for t in range(1, len(net_income) + 1):
        ni = net_income[t - 1]
        chg = charge_list[t - 1]
        ri = ri_list[t - 1]
        print(f"{t:>8d} {float(ni):12.2f} {float(b_disp):18.2f} {float(chg):16.2f} {float(ri):16.2f}")
        b_disp = b_disp + ni - dividends[t - 1]

    print("-" * 72)
    print(f"DDM Sum of Discounted Dividends:     ${float(pv_divs):.4f}M ({pv_divs})")
    print(f"Discounted Terminal Book Value B_5:   ${float(pv_bn):.4f}M ({pv_bn})")
    print(f"Total DDM Equity Value V_0:          ${float(v0_ddm):.4f}M ({v0_ddm})")
    print(f"Initial Book Value B_0:              ${float(b0):.4f}M ({b0})")
    print(f"Sum of Discounted Residual Income:   ${float(pv_ri):.4f}M ({pv_ri})")
    print(f"Total Residual Income Value V_0:     ${float(v0_ri):.4f}M ({v0_ri})")
    print(f"Exact Difference (V_0^RI - V_0^DDM): {v0_ri - v0_ddm}")
    assert v0_ri == v0_ddm, "Preinreich-Lücke-Wallis identity must hold exactly"


main()

3Where the canon breaks

While the residual income model is theoretically equivalent to the DDM, practical application encounters major friction points:

  • Accounting Conservatism: When GAAP requires immediate expensing of investments in intangible assets (e.g., research and development under ASC 730, brand advertising), beginning book value \(B_0\) is depressed below true economic replacement cost. In subsequent years, ROE appears artificially inflated, shifting value into future residual income without changing total intrinsic value under clean surplus.
  • Terminal Value Speculation: In a competitive market economy, long-run abnormal earnings decay to zero as industry competition and new entry erode excess returns (\(RI_{N+k} \to 0\)). Assuming perpetual growth in residual income without competitive fade leads to severe overvaluation.
  • Dirty Surplus Leaks: As established in L06, items bypassing net income through AOCI directly alter \(B_{t-1}\) without entering \(NI_t\), breaking the telescoping cancellation in the proof of Theorem 2.1.

4Misconceptions

  • A company with positive net income is creating economic value for equity holders.

    Correction: Value creation requires earning a return in excess of the cost of equity capital (\(NI_t > r B_{t-1}\)). If a firm with $1,000M book equity earns $60M net income while its cost of equity is 9% ($90M equity charge), the company suffers a residual income loss of -$30M, actively destroying shareholder wealth.

  • Conservative accounting rules reduce the intrinsic value calculated by a residual income model.

    Correction: By Theorem 2.1, intrinsic value is invariant to accounting choices under clean surplus. Conservative accounting reduces initial book value \(B_0\) but increases future residual income by an equal present value amount through lower asset depreciation charges.

  • The equity capital charge is calculated on ending book equity B_t.

    Correction: The capital charge compensates investors for the capital committed throughout the fiscal year, which is the capital in place at the start of the period: \(r \cdot B_{t-1}\). Charging on ending book equity mistakenly charges investors for retained earnings generated during that same year.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Single-period residual income calculation

A corporation has beginning book equity \(B_0 = \$500\text{M}\), generates net income \(NI_1 = \$75\text{M}\), and has a cost of equity of \(r = 10\%\). Compute the equity charge and the residual income \(RI_1\) for year 1.

Solution

The equity charge is \(r \cdot B_0 = 0.10 \times 500 = \$50\text{M}\). Residual income is \(RI_1 = NI_1 - r B_0 = 75 - 50 = \$25\text{M}\). The company created $25M of economic profit above its required return on equity.

M2 · Economic spread and ROE representation

A firm has beginning book value of \$800M and reports return on equity of \(\mathrm{ROE} = 14\%\). If the cost of equity is \(k_e = 9\%\), compute the residual income using the spread formula.

Solution

The economic spread is \(\mathrm{ROE} - k_e = 0.14 - 0.09 = 0.05\) (500 basis points). Residual income is \(RI = (\mathrm{ROE} - k_e) \cdot B_{t-1} = 0.05 \times 800 = \$40\text{M}\).

M3 · Two-year residual income intrinsic value

A firm has \(B_0 = \$200\text{M}\). Projected residual income is \(RI_1 = \$22\text{M}\) and \(RI_2 = \$33\text{M}\), with zero residual income thereafter. If the cost of equity is \(r = 10\%\), calculate intrinsic equity value \(V_0\).

Solution

Discounted residual income is \(\frac{22}{1.10} + \frac{33}{1.10^2} = 20 + \frac{33}{1.21} = 20 + 27.2727 = \$47.2727\text{M}\). Total intrinsic value is \(V_0 = B_0 + PV(RI) = 200 + 47.2727 = \$247.2727\text{M}\).

Proof

P1 · Invariance of intrinsic value to constant accounting capitalization

Suppose an accounting rule shift immediately capitalizes an asset of value \(A\) on the balance sheet at date 0, increasing \(B_0\) by \(A\), while amortizing \(A\) over 2 years at \(A/2\) per year. Prove that intrinsic value \(V_0\) remains identical under clean surplus.

Solution

Initial book value rises to \(B_0 + A\). In year 1, amortization of \(A/2\) reduces \(NI_1\) by \(A/2\), and the equity charge increases by \(r A\), so \(RI_1\) changes by \(-(A/2 + r A)\). In year 2, amortization reduces \(NI_2\) by \(A/2\), and the equity charge increases by \(r(A - A/2) = r A / 2\). The change in discounted residual income is \(-\frac{A/2 + r A}{1+r} - \frac{A/2 + r A / 2}{(1+r)^2} = -A\). Adding this change to the +\$A increase in \(B_0\) yields a net change of zero, proving accounting invariance.

P2 · Zero economic profit benchmark

Prove that if a firm is expected to earn exactly its cost of equity on beginning book value in perpetuity (\(\mathrm{ROE}_t = r\) for all \(t \ge 1\)), intrinsic equity value equals current book equity \(V_0 = B_0\) regardless of growth rate or payout policy.

Solution

If \(\mathrm{ROE}_t = r\), then \(RI_t = (\mathrm{ROE}_t - r)B_{t-1} = 0 \cdot B_{t-1} = 0\) for every period \(t \ge 1\). Substituting into the residual income model: \(V_0 = B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^\infty \frac{0}{(1+r)^t} = B_0 + 0 = B_0\). When economic profit is zero, equity is fairly valued at book value, and growth neither creates nor destroys shareholder wealth.

P3 · Market-to-book ratio and perpetual growth

Assume residual income grows at constant perpetual rate \(g\) where \(r > g\). Prove that the market-to-book ratio satisfies \(\frac{V_0}{B_0} = 1 + \frac{\mathrm{ROE} - r}{r - g}\), where \(\mathrm{ROE}\) is the constant return on equity.

Solution

First period residual income is \(RI_1 = (\mathrm{ROE} - r)B_0\). Growing at perpetual rate \(g\), the discounted perpetuity evaluates to \(\frac{RI_1}{r - g} = \frac{(\mathrm{ROE} - r)B_0}{r - g}\). Adding \(B_0\) gives \(V_0 = B_0 + \frac{(\mathrm{ROE} - r)B_0}{r - g} = B_0 \left(1 + \frac{\mathrm{ROE} - r}{r - g}\right)\). Dividing by \(B_0\) establishes the market-to-book formula.

Empirical

E1 · Residual income schedule verification (Lab 1)

Using the data from Lab 1 (\(B_0 = \$500\text{M}\), \(r = 10\%\)), calculate the year 1 and year 2 residual incomes by hand and verify they match the lab output.

Solution

Year 1: \(NI_1 = 100\), equity charge \(= 0.10 \times 500 = 50\), so \(RI_1 = 100 - 50 = \$50.00\text{M}\). Ending book value \(B_1 = 500 + 100 - 40 = \$560.00\text{M}\). Year 2: \(NI_2 = 115\), equity charge \(= 0.10 \times 560 = 56\), so \(RI_2 = 115 - 56 = \$59.00\text{M}\). Both match Lab 1 exactly.

E2 · Total equity valuation reconciliation

In Lab 1, the sum of discounted residual income over 5 years is \$280.97M, and initial book value is \$500.00M. Compute total equity value and verify that it matches the DDM value.

Solution

Total residual income valuation is \(V_0^{\text{RI}} = 500.00 + 280.97 = \$780.97\text{M}\). In the DDM calculation, discounted dividends equal \$197.80M and discounted terminal book value equals \$583.17M, summing to \(197.80 + 583.17 = \$780.97\text{M}\), confirming zero discrepancy.

E3 · Impact of cost of capital misestimation

If an analyst underestimates the cost of equity as \(r = 8\%\) instead of the true \(r = 10\%\) for the firm in Lab 1, compute the error in the year 1 residual income.

Solution

With \(r = 8\%\), the equity charge is \(0.08 \times 500 = \$40\text{M}\), yielding \(RI_1^{\text{est}} = 100 - 40 = \$60\text{M}\). True residual income is \$50M. The 200 bps underestimation of cost of equity inflates year 1 economic profit by \(\$10\text{M}\) (a 20% overestimate).

Research

R1 · Competitive fade and residual income persistence

In empirical corporate valuation, residual income is modeled as following an AR(1) process: \(RI_{t+1} = \omega RI_t\), where \(\omega \in [0, 1)\) is the persistence parameter. If \(\omega = 0.60\), \(r = 10\%\), and \(RI_1 = \$50\text{M}\), compute the capitalized present value of the entire residual income stream.

Solution

The discounted stream is \(\sum_{t=1}^\infty \frac{RI_1 \omega^{t-1}}{(1+r)^t} = \frac{RI_1}{1+r} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \left(\frac{\omega}{1+r}\right)^k = \frac{RI_1}{1+r} \frac{1}{1 - \frac{\omega}{1+r}} = \frac{RI_1}{1 + r - \omega}\). For \(r = 0.10\) and \(\omega = 0.60\), \(1 + 0.10 - 0.60 = 0.50\). The present value is \(50 / 0.50 = \$100.00\text{M}\).

R2 · Negative book equity and residual income breakdown

A distressed firm has negative book equity \(B_0 = -\$100\text{M}\) due to past share buybacks. If net income is \(NI_1 = \$10\text{M}\) and cost of equity is \(r = 10\%\), evaluate the standard equity charge \(r \cdot B_0\) and explain the economic breakdown.

Solution

Applying the formula blindly yields an equity charge of \(0.10 \times (-100) = -\$10\text{M}\), so \(RI_1 = 10 - (-10) = +\$20\text{M}\). The negative equity creates a negative capital charge (a subsidy), artificially generating high positive residual income for a distressed firm. In practice, the equity base must be adjusted to market value or replacement cost to restore economic meaning.

R3 · R&D capitalization vs immediate expensing

Explain why expensing research and development immediately under US GAAP increases the proportion of firm valuation attributed to future residual income relative to balance sheet book value.

Solution

Expensing R&D prevents valuable intellectual property from appearing as an asset on the balance sheet, depressing current book value \(B_0\). Because \(B_0\) is depressed, future net income from product commercialization faces a much smaller capital charge \(r B_{t-1}\), inflating future residual income. Intrinsic value is preserved, but the valuation weight shifts entirely from balance sheet capital into speculative future economic profits.

  1. What is the algebraic definition of residual income \(RI_t\)?

    Options

    Residual income is net income minus the equity capital charge on beginning book value \(r \cdot B_{t-1}\).

  2. If a firm has beginning book value of $400M, generates net income of $60M, and has a cost of equity of 10 percent, what is residual income in million dollars?

    \(RI = 60 - 0.10 \times 400 = 60 - 40 = 20\) million dollars.

  3. What theorem establishes that the residual income model and the dividend discount model yield identical intrinsic valuations under clean surplus?

    Options

    The Preinreich-Lücke-Wallis theorem proves the mathematical equivalence of DDM and residual income under clean surplus.

  4. A company has book value of $500M and is expected to earn exactly its cost of equity (\(\mathrm{ROE} = r\)) in all future years. What is its intrinsic equity value in million dollars?

    When \(\mathrm{ROE} = r\), all future residual income is zero, so \(V_0 = B_0 = 500\) million dollars.

  5. Why must the equity charge be computed on beginning book equity \(B_{t-1}\) rather than ending book equity \(B_t\)?

    Options

    The capital charge compensates for the capital in place over the year that was deployed to produce that year's earnings.

  6. If return on equity is 15 percent, cost of equity is 9 percent, and beginning book value is $500M, what is residual income in million dollars?

    \(RI = (0.15 - 0.09) \times 500 = 0.06 \times 500 = 30\) million dollars.

  7. How does immediate expensing of R&D affect intrinsic valuation under the residual income model assuming clean surplus holds?

    Options

    Under clean surplus, accounting conservatism shifts the timing and composition of value but preserves total intrinsic value.

  8. A firm has current book value of $200M. The present value of all expected future residual income is $40M. What is intrinsic equity value in million dollars?

    \(V_0 = B_0 + PV(RI) = 200 + 40 = 240\) million dollars.

  9. What happens to residual income in the long run in a perfectly competitive product market?

    Options

    Competitive forces erode abnormal returns, driving \(\mathrm{ROE} \to r\) and economic profit \(RI \to 0\).

  10. If a firm has negative book equity, what anomaly occurs when evaluating the equity capital charge?

    Options

    A negative book value creates a negative capital charge, distorting the economic meaning of residual income.
