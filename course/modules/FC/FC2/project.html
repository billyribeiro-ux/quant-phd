Arbitrage scanner

A desk wants a daily check of one expiry's European option quotes for violations of the no-arbitrage rules, net of the cost it pays to trade them. Each finding is classed as model-free or model-dependent, as Conventions and assumptions, below, defines the two classes. You write scanner.py to the specification below. It finds each put–call parity gap and each violation of convexity in strike, keeps the findings that survive the cost, and states why each one is a finding. The autograder runs your module on a SIMULATED chain generated from your workspace seed.

Rungs 1 and 2 are on this page. Rung 3 is graded and cannot be skipped. Rungs 1 and 2 are optional when you reach the placement bar on the module hub and answer both skill checks.

Rungs 1 and 2

Lab 1 · Worked example: scanning one chain, strike by strike

The gross amount of a finding is the size of its gap or violation before costs, and its net amount is the gross less the cost. The chain is SIMULATED: one expiry, six strikes, quotes in integer cents, a discount factor of 0.990000 to expiry, and a cost of 5 cents of fee and 10 cents of half-spread per contract (both terms are defined under P6 in Conventions and assumptions, below). Each finding trades three legs, so a finding costs 45 cents. Before running the code, predict how many findings the cost model keeps. Then check each step: the parity gap at each strike, the chord test at each interior strike, and the net amount of each violation. The chord (the straight line through the two neighbouring calls) and the synthetic forward S − K df are defined in Conventions and assumptions, below.

"""Rung 1 worked example: scan one SIMULATED six-strike chain for parity and convexity findings, net of cost.

Standard library only. The chain is SIMULATED and is not a market quote. Money is integer cents. Every money value
is computed from exact Fractions and rounded once to the nearest cent, a tie going toward plus infinity. A finding
trades three legs, and each leg pays the fee and the half-spread, so a finding costs 3 * (fee + half-spread) cents.
The printed numbers are the output of this file and nothing else.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction
from typing import Any

# SIMULATED chain: one expiry, six strikes, quotes in integer cents (not a market quote).
CHAIN: dict[str, Any] = {
    "spot_cents": 10_000,
    "df_ppm": 990_000,  # the discount factor to expiry, 0.990000, in parts per million
    "strikes_cents": [9_000, 9_500, 10_000, 10_500, 11_000, 11_500],
    "call_cents": [1_500, 1_100, 800, 700, 450, 350],
    "put_cents": [410, 385, 700, 1_095, 1_360, 1_735],
    "cost": {"fee_cents_per_contract": 5, "half_spread_cents": 10},
}
LEGS = 3  # every finding trades three legs


def round_cents(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of an exact amount, a tie going toward plus infinity: floor(x + 1/2)."""
    return (2 * x.numerator + x.denominator) // (2 * x.denominator)


def parity_gap(call: int, put: int, spot: int, strike: int, df_ppm: int) -> Fraction:
    """The exact parity gap C - P - (S - K df) in cents, with df = df_ppm / 1e6. The caller rounds it."""
    return Fraction(call) - Fraction(put) - (Fraction(spot) - Fraction(strike * df_ppm, 1_000_000))


def chord_less_call(strikes: list[int], calls: list[int], j: int) -> Fraction:
    """The chord through the two neighbours of strike j, evaluated at strike j, less the call at j, in cents.

    A negative value is a convexity violation: the call sits above the chord. The weights are the distances to the
    other neighbour, divided by the distance between the two neighbours, so unequal spacing is handled exactly.
    """
    low, mid, high = strikes[j - 1], strikes[j], strikes[j + 1]
    w_low = Fraction(high - mid, high - low)
    w_high = Fraction(mid - low, high - low)
    return w_low * calls[j - 1] + w_high * calls[j + 1] - calls[j]


def main() -> None:
    strikes = CHAIN["strikes_cents"]
    calls = CHAIN["call_cents"]
    puts = CHAIN["put_cents"]
    spot = CHAIN["spot_cents"]
    df_ppm = CHAIN["df_ppm"]
    cost = LEGS * (CHAIN["cost"]["fee_cents_per_contract"] + CHAIN["cost"]["half_spread_cents"])
    print(f"cost per finding: {LEGS} legs x (fee + half-spread) = {cost} cents")

    print("step 1: parity gap C - P - (S - K df) at each strike")
    findings: list[tuple[str, int, int, int]] = []
    for k, strike in enumerate(strikes):
        gap = parity_gap(calls[k], puts[k], spot, strike, df_ppm)
        gross = round_cents(abs(gap))
        net = gross - cost
        verdict = "kept" if net > 0 else "dropped, net not positive"
        print(f"  strike {strike}: gap {round_cents(gap)}, gross {gross}, net {net}: {verdict}")
        if net > 0:
            findings.append(("parity", strike, gross, net))

    print("step 2: convexity in strike, the call against the chord of its neighbours")
    for j in range(1, len(strikes) - 1):
        diff = chord_less_call(strikes, calls, j)
        if diff >= 0:
            print(f"  strike {strikes[j]}: chord less call {diff}, no violation")
            continue
        gross = round_cents(-diff)
        net = gross - cost
        verdict = "kept" if net > 0 else "dropped, net not positive"
        print(f"  strike {strikes[j]}: chord less call {diff}, gross {gross}, net {net}: {verdict}")
        if net > 0:
            findings.append(("convexity", strikes[j], gross, net))

    print(f"findings kept: {len(findings)}")
    for kind, strike, gross, net in findings:
        why = (
            "parity: the call less the put differs from the synthetic forward"
            if kind == "parity"
            else ("convexity in strike: the call sits above the chord of its neighbours")
        )
        print(f"  {kind} at strike {strike}: gross {gross}, net {net}; {why}")

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A desk wants a daily check of one expiry's European option quotes for violations of the no-arbitrage rules, net of the cost it actually pays to trade them. Some findings use only the no-arbitrage rules (model-free) and others also use the supplied discount factor (model-dependent), as Conventions and assumptions, below, defines. Each finding must be explained, not only listed. The desk asks two questions about each finding: does it survive the cost, and is the quote a data error or a true violation. The chain is labelled SIMULATED on this page and in the autograder.
Inputs

No market data is embedded. tools/data_sources.yaml lists no option-quote source, so every chain on this page and in the autograder is a simulation, labelled SIMULATED. A chain of your own, from a source whose terms you have read, uses the same dictionary, and your code must check it under the same rules. Nothing on this page uploads a file. The autograder panel can load a .py file from your device into the editor, and the file is read in this browser.

A chain is a Python dictionary with these keys:

  • spot_cents: the spot price of the underlying, an integer number of cents.
  • df_ppm: the discount factor to expiry in parts per million, an integer from 1 to 1000000. It is supplied, not computed, so the parity check is exact (assumption P4, defined under Conventions and assumptions, below).
  • strikes_cents: the strikes, integers in cents, strictly increasing and positive.
  • call_cents and put_cents: the call and put quotes, one integer per strike, in cents, mid prices.
  • cost: a dictionary with fee_cents_per_contract and half_spread_cents, both integers that are zero or more.
Required outputs

A file scanner.py that defines these three functions, with these names and parameters. Start from the starter file: each function raises NotImplementedError until you replace it, and the suite fails on the starter.

  • parity_gap_cents(call_cents, put_cents, spot_cents, strike_cents, df_ppm) returns the parity gap C − P − (S − K df) of one strike, as an integer number of cents.
  • convexity_violations(strikes_cents, calls_cents, df_ppm) returns the list of interior indices, in increasing order, at which the call lies strictly above the chord (defined under Conventions and assumptions, below) through its two neighbours. Each index is a zero-based position in strikes_cents.
  • scan(chain) returns one record for each finding whose net amount is strictly positive. A record is a dictionary with the keys kind (the string "parity" or "convexity"), strike_cents, gross_cents, net_cents and explanation. Parity findings come first, in strike order, then convexity findings, in strike order. The explanation is a non-empty string that names the rule and the assumptions the finding depends on, by the labels in the conventions. A parity finding names P1, P2, P3, P4 and P6, and a convexity finding names P1, P2, P5 and P6.

A file findings.md, the findings note described in the report section.

Constraints

The standard library only. The module must not import any file from the grader, so it runs on its own. The three functions never print, and none of them changes its arguments.

Money is integer cents. Each money value is computed from exact fractions and rounded once to the nearest cent. A tie goes toward plus infinity for a signed gap. A gross amount is the magnitude of the gap, rounded once, half up in magnitude. The cost of a finding is three legs, each paying fee_cents_per_contract plus half_spread_cents, so the net amount is the gross less 3 × (fee + half-spread). A finding is kept only if its net amount is strictly greater than zero.

The named error conditions raise ValueError, and the grader checks the exception type. Each message names its condition. Each condition is checked in every function that receives the input it names, and the grader checks it in each of those functions. The conditions are these:

  • the strikes are not strictly increasing, or a strike is zero or less: in convexity_violations and scan, the message says that the strikes must be strictly increasing and positive; in parity_gap_cents, a strike that is zero or less raises the same error;
  • df_ppm is not in 1 to 1000000: in each of the three functions, the message says that df_ppm must lie in 1 to 1000000;
  • calls_cents or put_cents does not have one quote per strike: in convexity_violations the check applies to calls_cents, and in scan it applies to both lists; the message names the lengths;
  • fee_cents_per_contract or half_spread_cents is negative: in scan, the message says that the cost terms must be non-negative.

Any other invalid input may raise any exception, or return a value. The grader does not require a type for those cases.

Grading

Both modes apply. The code is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which collects 90 checks, counted with pytest --collect-only on the reference solution, and the count is 90 for each of the seeds 0 to 49. It runs with warnings as errors. It checks the parity gap against an exact computation for every strike of two seeded chains, the convexity test against an exhaustive check of every interior strike, including a chain with unequal spacing, the net-of-cost rule at its boundary in both directions, the rounding of a half-cent tie, the named error conditions at their boundaries, the output types, the purity of the module, and a clean chain with no findings. The suite is not secret: the chain changes with the seed, and the expected findings change with it.

The findings note is graded by rubric. It is scored on the five criteria of the rubric, each with four observable levels. You score it yourself with the form in the self-assessment section, and you calibrate that scoring on two annotated exemplars. The note meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Conventions and assumptions

The findings and the explanations use these labels. Each finding names the labels it depends on. These are the project's own labels: the lessons state their assumptions under their own labels, which are not these.

  • P1 · European exercise. An option can be exercised only at expiry.
  • P2 · One expiry. Every quote in the chain has the same expiry.
  • P3 · No income. The underlying pays no dividend and no other income before expiry.
  • P4 · Supplied discount factor. df_ppm is the factor to expiry. The project does not model the interest rate and does not compute the factor. The parity gap uses it, and the convexity test does not.
  • P5 · Present-value quotes. Every quote is a present value at the valuation date and is taken at one moment. So the convexity test compares the quotes directly, without discounting. The sign of the test does not depend on df_ppm.
  • P6 · Cost model. Quotes are mid prices. The fee is the charge per contract that a leg pays to trade, in cents (the input fee_cents_per_contract). The half-spread is half of a leg's bid–ask spread, the cost of crossing from the mid to the market on that leg. A finding trades three legs, each leg pays the fee and the half-spread, and so a finding costs 3 × (fee + half-spread) cents. A parity finding trades the call, the put and the underlying. A convexity finding trades three calls.

In the formulas, C, P and S are the call price, the put price and the spot price, K is the strike, and df is the discount factor df_ppm / 1,000,000, which the lessons write as D. The labels P1 to P6 are not the put price, which this page writes as P in each of its formulas. A finding is model-free when its test uses only P1, P2 and P5, the no-arbitrage rules of this page (the cost in P6 is not a model input), and model-dependent when it uses the supplied factor P4.

The parity gap is C − P − (S − K df). The term S − K df is the synthetic forward: the value of owning the underlying and borrowing the discounted strike. A positive gap means the call is rich against the synthetic forward: the trade sells the call, buys the put and the underlying, and borrows the discounted strike. A negative gap means the reverse trade. The chord is the straight line through the calls at the two neighbouring strikes, read at the strike between them. The convexity test compares the call at a strike with its chord. The weight on each neighbour is the distance from the strike to the other neighbour, divided by the distance between the two neighbours, so unequal spacing is handled exactly. The lessons define parity in Lesson 5 and the bounds in Lesson 5.

Copy test_project.py and instance.py next to your scanner.py, then run, with the seed shown in the autograder panel:

Lab 3 · Reference findings for a chain with uneven strikes

The chain is SIMULATED: six strikes from 4000 to 7000 cents on an uneven grid (spacing 500, 500, 1000, 500 and 500 cents), a spot of 5000 cents, a discount factor of 0.950000, and a cost of 8 cents of fee and 12 cents of half-spread per contract, so each finding costs 60 cents.             if kind == "parity"

        )
        print(f"  {kind} at strike {strike}: gross {gross}, net {net}; {why}")

Summary

The Lab 3 chain has six strikes. Each finding trades three legs at a fee of 8 and a half-spread of 12 cents, a cost of 60 cents. Three findings survive with a positive net: a parity finding at 6500 (gross 226 cents, net 166), a parity finding at 7000 (gross 540, net 480), and a convexity finding at 6000 (gross 63, net 3).

  • Meets C2 level 4: states the net for each finding and its gap from zero, and the cost of 60 cents is three legs at 8 plus 12, and each net is its gross less that cost.
  • Meets C1 level 3: each finding has a strike, a kind, a gross and a net, all in whole cents.
Findings

Parity at 6500: the call is 151 cents, the put 1100 and the underlying 5000 at a strike of 6500 with the discount factor 0.950000, which makes the discounted strike 6175. The gap is 151 minus 1100 minus (5000 minus 6175), which is 226 cents, so the gross is 226 and the net is 166. Parity at 7000: the call is 90, the put 1200, and the discounted strike 6650, so the gap is 90 minus 1200 minus (5000 minus 6650), which is 540 cents, a gross of 540 and a net of 480. Convexity at 6000: the chord through the calls at 5000 (500 cents) and 6500 (151 cents) has weights one third and two thirds, and it equals (500 + 2 × 151) / 3 = 802/3 cents, while the call is 330. The chord less the call is minus 188/3, so the gross is 63 cents after one half-up rounding, and the net is 3.

  • Meets C1 level 4: gives the three legs for the parity findings (the call, the put and the underlying) and the three calls for the convexity finding.
  • Meets C3 level 3: names P1 to P4 and P6 for the two parity findings, and P1, P2, P5 and P6 for the convexity finding.
Model-free and model-dependent

The convexity finding uses P1, P2 and P5 for its test and P6 for its cost: it needs no discount factor, because the quotes are present values. The two parity findings use the supplied discount factor, which is the one model input the project admits (P4), and P6 for their cost. Before rounding, each parity gap would change by the strike times the change in the factor, and the note does not compute the size of that change for any particular factor.

  • Meets C4 level 3: separates the model-free convexity finding from the two findings that use the supplied factor, and says which input each uses.
  • Misses C4 level 4: does not state the size of the factor's effect on the parity grosses, which level 4 requires.
Explanation and settlement

The parity findings at 6500 and 7000 are large against the 60-cent cost, and each would be settled by re-quoting the call, the put and the underlying at the same moment. If the gap survives the re-quote, it is a true violation of parity at P1 to P4, and the direction is that the call is rich. The convexity finding at 6000 has a net of 3 cents, which is small against the half-spread of 12 cents per leg. A re-quote could remove it, and a re-quote is the check that settles it: if the re-quoted call at 6000 sits at or below the chord, it is a data error.

  • Meets C5 level 3: says for each finding whether it is a data error or a true violation, and names the re-quote as the check that settles each one.
  • Misses C5 level 4: says the re-quote could remove the 3-cent finding, but does not say which way the net would move, or by how many cents, if the re-quoted call were 10 cents higher or lower.
Limitations

The note assumes one expiry, European exercise and no income, which are P1 to P3. If the underlying paid a dividend before expiry, the parity relation would change, and both parity findings would need a second look. A change in the discount factor moves both parity grosses. The chain is SIMULATED, so the note makes no claim about any market.

  • Misses C3 level 4: says what a failed dividend assumption (P3) would change for the parity findings, but gives no failure consequence for P1, P2 or P5 on the convexity finding, and level 4 needs one for each assumption a finding depends on.
