Derivatives Fundamentals

A forward price is not a forecast. Under stated assumptions it is the one price at which no riskless profit is possible. This module develops that argument from the definition of arbitrage and applies it to forwards, futures, swaps and options. You will replicate a forward with a loan, check put–call parity and its bounds on a price set, price an option on a binomial tree, and invert the Black–Scholes formula for implied volatility. The module ends in an arbitrage scanner that finds put–call parity violations and violations of convexity in strike net of costs, graded by an autograder and by a findings note that you write.

  • Prerequisites: M4 Probability, CF1 Time Value & Capital Budgeting, and FC1 The Financial System, Markets & Instruments. Their closure adds M1, M2, AC1, E1 and P0.1.
  • Project: Arbitrage scanner (autograded, with a rubric-graded findings note)

60 h lessons · 30 h project (33.3% of the 90 module hours)

Objectives

By the end of FC2 you can:

  1. Derive the no-arbitrage forward price of an asset from a replicating portfolio of the asset and a loan, under stated assumptions.
  2. Construct the payoff and profit at expiry of a call, a put, a forward and a futures position, and state what the futures margin flows add up to.
  3. Apply put–call parity and the model-free bounds to a price set, and identify a price set that violates them.
  4. Compute a one-period and a two-period binomial option price by replication and by risk-neutral discounting, and check that the two agree.
  5. State the Black–Scholes formula under its assumptions, and check it numerically against a binomial price for a stated number of steps.
  6. Invert a Black–Scholes price for implied volatility by bisection to a stated tolerance, and reject a price outside the no-arbitrage bounds.

Prerequisites

FC2 assumes M4 (Probability), CF1 (Time Value & Capital Budgeting) and FC1 (The Financial System, Markets & Instruments), and everything they rest on. FC1 is a prerequisite because its exchange and clearing concepts are used in Lesson 3, and its cost concepts in the project's cost model. The following concepts are used without redefinition:

  • From M4: normal distribution, expectation and variance.
  • From M1 and CF1: present value, discount factor, continuous compounding, geometric series, annuity and net present value.
  • From FC1: exchange-traded and over-the-counter contracts and clearing and settlement.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: six on M4, CF1 and M1, one on FC1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rung 1 of the project ladder below teaches and on Lesson 2. The mastery bar is 80 percent. For nine items that means eight correct, since seven of nine is 78 percent, below the bar. Skip rungs 1 and 2 when you reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, revisit M4, CF1 and FC1 first.

  1. A fair six-sided die shows the number \(X\). What is \(\E[X]\)?

    \(\E[X] = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6)/6 = 3.5\) (M4, expectation).

  2. A coin pays 1 with probability one half and 0 otherwise. What is its variance?

    The variance is \(p(1 - p) = (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4 = 0.25\) (M4, variance).

  3. For a standard normal variable \(Z\), what is \(\Prob(Z \le 0)\)?

    Options

    The normal distribution is symmetric about zero (M4, normal distribution).

  4. If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, what is \(\Var(X + Y)\)?

    Options

    For independent variables the covariance is zero, so the variances add (M4, independence and variance).

  5. A project costs 100 dollars today and returns 60 dollars at the end of each of the next two years. At a rate of 10 percent, what is its net present value, to the cent?

    \(-100 + 60/1.1 + 60/1.21 = 4.1322\), which is 4.13 to the cent (CF1, net present value).

  6. With an annual rate of 5 percent, what is the discount factor for two years, to four places?

    \(1/1.05^2 = 1/1.1025 = 0.9070\) (M1, discount factor).

  7. In an exchange-traded futures trade, which institution stands between the two parties?

    Options

    The clearing house is the counterparty to each side of an exchange trade (FC1, clearing and settlement).

  8. Skill check (rung 1). On a SIMULATED chain with spot 100, strike 105, a discount factor of 0.95 to expiry, a call at 10 and a put at 4, what is the parity gap \(C - P - (S - K\,\mathrm{df})\)?

    \(10 - 4 - (100 - 105 \times 0.95) = 6 - (100 - 99.75) = 5.75\). Rung 1 of the project works this gap line by line (Lesson 5).

  9. Skill check (rung 2). An asset costs 100 dollars today. The rate is 5 percent, continuously compounded, and the expiry is one year. What is the no-arbitrage forward price, to the cent?

    \(F = 100 e^{0.05} = 105.1271\), which is 105.13 to the cent (Lesson 2, Proposition 2.2). Rung 2 does not ask for this line; this check tests Lesson 2 directly.

Lessons

Eight lessons of six to nine study hours each, totalling 60 hours. Every lesson has graded claims, a runnable lab, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.

L01 · 6 hArbitrage and the No-Arbitrage PrincipleThe definition of an arbitrage opportunity, the no-arbitrage principle as a hypothesis, the law of one price, payoff dominance, and positive state prices that exclude arbitrage. L02 · 8 hForward Contracts and the Cost of CarryThe forward contract and its payoff, the no-arbitrage forward price by replication with a loan, and the value of a forward during its life. L03 · 9 hFutures, Margining and SwapsDaily margining and its telescoping identity, a SIMULATED futures path in cents, and the interest-rate swap with its par rate from discount factors. L04 · 7 hOption Payoffs and American versus European ExerciseCall and put payoffs and profits at expiry, and the exercise decision for one contract with and without early exercise. L05 · 8 hPut–Call Parity and Model-Free BoundsPut–call parity for European options, the model-free bounds on option prices, and convexity in strike on a grid. L06 · 8 hOne- and Multi-Period Binomial PricingReplication in one period, the risk-neutral probabilities of the binomial model, and backward induction on a recombining tree. L07 · 8 hThe Black–Scholes Formula and the Binomial LimitThe Black–Scholes formula stated under its model assumptions, and a numerical comparison with binomial prices for stated step counts. L08 · 6 hImplied VolatilityBisection inversion of the Black–Scholes price to a stated tolerance, a round trip to the input volatility, and rejection of prices outside the no-arbitrage bounds.

The project ladder

The module ends in the arbitrage scanner. Rung 1 scaffolds skill check 1, and rung 2 practises the scan with blanks; skill check 2 tests Lesson 2 directly. You may skip them when you reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    Lab 1 on the project page: one fully solved scan of a six-contract SIMULATED chain, with the parity gap, the convexity test, the net-of-cost decision and an explanation for each step.

  2. Completion problem

    Lab 2 on the project page: the same scan with the parity-gap line and the cost-netting lines replaced by blanks (three blanks in all). Its output must match the expected output when the blanks are right.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the scanner to the project specification: parity gaps, convexity violations and the net-of-cost rule, on SIMULATED chains generated for each seed. The autograder checks the scanner, and the rubric grades the findings note. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    Extend the scanner to a chain with a known discrete dividend, with the parity adjustment stated as a hypothesis before it is run.

Data status

No market dataset is used. No FC2 page embeds an options quote or reads a market data file. The option chain in the project is SIMULATED and generated for each seed, and the futures path in Lab 3, Part A, is SIMULATED. The project specifies a bring-your-own-chain schema for a student's own quotes, which never leaves the browser. A redistributable options-quote source has not been cleared for embedding, so no real chain is shown. The other labs use typed-in or formula-generated example inputs, which their pages state, and none of them is market data.
