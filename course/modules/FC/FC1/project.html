Instrument Cash-Flow Engine

A back office receives dated cash-flow records for two cash instruments, a fixed-rate bond and a trade in a cash instrument, and must book them through one interface before reconciliation. You will write the module that turns each into dated amounts in integer cents, price the bond from its cash flows, and test the module against hand-computed fixtures and against the rule the page states.

Rungs 1 and 2

Both labs use one illustrative bond and one illustrative trade, both SIMULATED. The bond has a face of 100000 cents (1,000.00), the amount repaid at maturity; a coupon of 500 basis points of face (one basis point is 1/10000 of face) in each of its three periods, which is the payment a holder receives in each period; and a per-period yield of 400 basis points. The trade buys 10 units at 9850 cents per unit, settling in period 2. No file is read, and no value is market data.

Lab 1 · Worked example: the bond and the trade, step by step

The coupon is the face times 500 over 10000, which is 5000 cents, exact. The events are (1, 5000), (2, 5000) and (3, 105000), since the face is paid with the last coupon. The present value at \(y = 400/10000\) is the sum of each event times \((1+y)^{-t}\), which is 102775.091033 cents before rounding, so the price is 102775 cents. The trade has one event, (2, -98500): the buyer pays 98500 cents in period 2.

"""Rung 1 worked example: one SIMULATED bond and one cash trade, every step annotated, in exact arithmetic."""

from fractions import Fraction


def half_away(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of an exact amount; an exact half goes away from zero."""
    magnitude = abs(x)
    whole = (2 * magnitude.numerator + magnitude.denominator) // (2 * magnitude.denominator)
    return whole if x >= 0 else -whole


def main() -> None:
    face_cents = 100_000  # SIMULATED face of 1,000.00, not market data
    coupon_bps = 500  # the coupon in each period, in basis points of face: 5.00 % of face
    periods = 3
    yield_bps = 400  # the per-period yield, in basis points: 4.00 % per period
    # Step 1. The coupon is face * coupon_bps / 10000, rounded once to the nearest cent, a half going away from zero.
    coupon = half_away(Fraction(face_cents * coupon_bps, 10_000))
    # Step 2. Periods run from 1 to periods. Every period pays the coupon; the last period also pays the face.
    events = [(t, coupon) for t in range(1, periods + 1)]
    events[-1] = (periods, events[-1][1] + face_cents)
    print("bond events (period, cents):", events)
    # Step 3. Each event is discounted by (1 + y) ** -t, where y = yield_bps / 10000 is the per-period yield.
    # The sum is kept exact as a Fraction; nothing is rounded yet.
    y = Fraction(yield_bps, 10_000)
    exact_price = sum((Fraction(amount) / (1 + y) ** period for period, amount in events), Fraction(0))
    print("exact present value (cents):", f"{float(exact_price):.6f}")
    # Step 4. The price is rounded once, at the end, to the nearest cent. Rounding each term first is not the rule.
    print("bond price (cents):", half_away(exact_price))
    # Step 5. A trade: buy 10 units at 9850 cents per unit, settling in period 2. The buyer pays cash, so the amount is
    # negative in the holder's terms; the trade has one settlement event and no other cash flow.
    quantity, price_cents, side, settlement_day = 10, 9_850, 1, 2
    trade_event = (settlement_day, -side * quantity * price_cents)
    print("trade event (period, cents):", trade_event)

exact present value (cents): 102775.091033
bond price (cents): 102775
trade event (period, cents): (2, -98500)

Each comment names one step, and the printed lines are those steps. The exact present value, 102775.091033 cents, is shown with six decimals only so that its rounding is visible: the price is that value rounded once to the nearest cent, which is 102775, because the fraction .091033 is below one half. Rounding each discounted term first can give a different answer: Lab 3 prints one such bond. The trade line is the holder's cash flow: negative, because a buyer pays. The lab does not show that either object is correct in general; the autograder does.

Lab 2 · Completion problem: the discount exponent and the trade's cash flow

Replace each ___ with the expression the conventions below give. In the price line, the exponent of each event is its period, so that the event in period \(t\) is discounted by \((1+y)^{-t}\). In the trade line, the cash flow is the holder's: minus side times quantity times price, which is negative for a buyer. When both blanks are right, the printed lines are the same as those of Lab 1, and the bond price is 102775 cents.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A back office receives dated cash-flow records for two cash instruments: a fixed-rate bond and a trade that settles later. Before reconciliation, each record must become a list of dated amounts in whole cents, with the sign convention stated below, through one interface, and the bond must be priced from its cash flows. You write the module to the interface below and test it against hand-computed fixtures and the rules on this page. Futures, swaps and options are not part of this project; they are the subject of the course's later Derivatives unit (FC2), and this project uses none of them.
Inputs
No data file. The autograder's case comes from grader/instance.py, seeded by your workspace ID. It holds one bond (a face in cents, a coupon in basis points, a number of periods and a per-period yield in basis points) and one trade (a quantity, a price in cents per unit, a side and a settlement period). Every value is SIMULATED; none is market data.
Required outputs
instruments.py at the root of your repository, with these names. Bond(face_cents, coupon_bps, periods) with a method events(), which returns a list of (period, amount_cents) pairs sorted by period. bond_price_cents(bond, yield_bps), which returns the present value of the bond's events as an int. CashTrade(quantity, price_cents, side, settlement_day) with a method events(), which returns the one settlement event of the trade. Start from the starter file, whose names and signatures are complete. The module docstring holds the conventions block: the sign convention, the period indexing and the rounding rule, in your own words. Not checked by the autograder; a reviewer reads it.
Constraints
Standard library only (not checked by the autograder; a reviewer reads it). Money is an int in cents at every output, computed from exact fractions and rounded once. Each named condition below raises ValueError. Any other invalid input may raise any exception or return. On a valid input and on each named refused input, no function prints or warns.
  • Bond: periods less than 1; face_cents not positive; coupon_bps negative.
  • bond_price_cents: yield_bps at or below -10000, because \(1+y\) must be positive.
  • CashTrade: quantity less than 1; price_cents negative; side not in {+1, -1}; settlement_day less than 1.
Grading mode
Autograder only. grader/test_project.py runs 59 checks with warnings as errors: the events of the bond and the trade, the discounted-sum price on two cases, the hand-computed fixtures, the rounding rule with its exact-half cases, every named condition with its boundary on each side, and the silence and purity of the module. The same file runs in your browser and on your machine. It checks the cent results, not the method that computes them. It is not secret: its integrity comes from your seeded case and from a second case, which a fixed answer for the first would also have to match.

Conventions

  • Money. Every amount is an int number of cents. The exact value of an output is computed from fractions, and it is rounded once, to the nearest cent, with a half going away from zero. No intermediate term is rounded.
  • Periods. Periods are whole numbers from 1. Period 0 is the trade date, and no event falls in it. The bond's coupon is paid in each of periods 1 to periods, and its face in the last of them, in the same event.
  • Rates. A rate is an int in basis points, where 10000 is 100 %. The per-period yield is yield_bps over 10000. An event in period \(t\) is discounted by \((1+y)^{-t}\), the discount factor of period \(t\), and the price is the sum of the discounted events, the present value of the bond at that yield.
  • Sides and signs. Every amount is from the holder's side, and a positive amount is cash received. side is +1 for a long holder, who buys and pays cash, and -1 for a short holder, who sells and receives cash. A trade's event is therefore (settlement_day, -side * quantity * price_cents).
  • Events. Each events() call returns the same list for the same object, sorted by period. A zero amount is an event and is kept.

Hand-computed fixtures

The autograder checks these fixtures, which are computed by hand on this page and do not depend on the seed. Lab 3 prints them.

  • Fixture A. Bond(10000, 500, 1).events() is [(1, 10500)], and its price at 500 basis points is 10000 cents: 10500 divided by 1.05 is exactly 10000.
  • Fixture B. The same bond at -500 basis points: 10500 divided by 0.95 is 210000/19, which is 11052.6316 to four decimals and rounds to 11053 cents.
  • Fixture C. Bond(10001, 5000, 2).events() is [(1, 5001), (2, 15002)]: the coupon of 10001 times 50 % is 5000.5 cents, which rounds up to 5001.
  • Fixture D. Bond(1, 5000, 1).events() is [(1, 2)]: the coupon of 0.5 cents rounds up to 1 cent.
  • Fixture E. CashTrade(10, 9850, 1, 2).events() is [(2, -98500)], and the seller's trade with side -1 is [(2, 98500)].
  • Fixture F. Bond(1, 0, 1) at 10000 bps (yield 100 % per period): the event is [(1, 1)], and the exact price is 1/2 cent, which rounds away from zero to 1. Round-half-to-even gives 0.
  • Fixture G. Bond(10001, 0, 1) at 10000 bps: the event is [(1, 10001)], and the exact price is 5000.5 cents, which rounds to 5001.
  • Rounding fixture. Bond(10000, 500, 3) at 400 basis points has events (1, 500), (2, 500) and (3, 10500). The exact present value is 10277.509103 cents, which rounds once to 10278. Rounding each term first gives 481 + 462 + 9334 = 10277, so the price must be 10278.

Lab 3 · Rung 3 check: the fixtures and the rounding rule

Stated before running: the program prints the events and prices of fixtures A to G and of the rounding fixture, using the functions of this lab in exact arithmetic. Expected: fixture A is [(1, 10500)] with price 10000; fixture B is 11053 with exact value 11052.6316; fixture C is [(1, 5001), (2, 15002)]; fixture D is [(1, 2)]; the rounding fixture prints events [(1, 500), (2, 500), (3, 10500)], price 10278 when rounded once, and 10277 when each term is rounded first; fixture E is (2, -98500); fixture F is [(1, 1)] with price 1; and fixture G is [(1, 10001)] with price 5001.

"""Rung 3 check: the hand-computed fixtures and the rounding case the autograder checks, in exact arithmetic.

Standard library only. The values printed here are the ones the autograder's fixtures expect from your instruments.py.
"""

from fractions import Fraction


def half_away(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent of an exact amount; an exact half goes away from zero."""
    magnitude = abs(x)
    whole = (2 * magnitude.numerator + magnitude.denominator) // (2 * magnitude.denominator)
    return whole if x >= 0 else -whole


def bond_events(face_cents: int, coupon_bps: int, periods: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """Coupon in periods 1..periods, the face added in the last period."""
    coupon = half_away(Fraction(face_cents * coupon_bps, 10_000))
    rows = [(t, coupon) for t in range(1, periods + 1)]
    rows[-1] = (periods, rows[-1][1] + face_cents)
    return rows


def bond_price(events: list[tuple[int, int]], yield_bps: int) -> int:
    """Present value at a per-period yield, summed exactly and rounded once."""
    y = Fraction(yield_bps, 10_000)
    return half_away(sum((Fraction(a) / (1 + y) ** t for t, a in events), Fraction(0)))


def term_by_term_price(events: list[tuple[int, int]], yield_bps: int) -> int:
    """The same present value with each term rounded before the terms are added. Not the rule; shown for contrast."""
    y = Fraction(yield_bps, 10_000)
    return sum(half_away(Fraction(a) / (1 + y) ** t) for t, a in events)


def main() -> None:
    par = bond_events(10_000, 500, 1)
    print("fixture A events (one period, par):", par)
    print("fixture A price at 500 bps (cents):", bond_price(par, 500))
    negative = bond_events(10_000, 500, 1)
    exact_negative = Fraction(10_500) / Fraction(95, 100)
    print(
        "fixture B price at -500 bps (cents):", bond_price(negative, -500), "| exact:", f"{float(exact_negative):.4f}"
    )
    half_cent = bond_events(10_001, 5_000, 2)
    print("fixture C events (half-cent coupon, two periods):", half_cent)
    one_cent = bond_events(1, 5_000, 1)
    print("fixture D events (one-cent face, half coupon):", one_cent)
    rounding = bond_events(10_000, 500, 3)
    print("rounding case events (three periods):", rounding)
    print("rounding case, rounded once (the rule):", bond_price(rounding, 400))
    print("rounding case, each term rounded first (not the rule):", term_by_term_price(rounding, 400))
    buy = (2, -10 * 9_850)
    print("fixture E trade event (buy 10 at 9850, settles in period 2):", buy)
    exact_half = bond_events(1, 0, 1)
    print("fixture F events (one cent of face, no coupon, one period):", exact_half)
    print("fixture F price at 10000 bps (cents), exact value 1/2:", bond_price(exact_half, 10_000))
    half_five_thousand = bond_events(10_001, 0, 1)
    print("fixture G events (10001 cents of face, one period):", half_five_thousand)
    print("fixture G price at 10000 bps (cents), exact value 5000.5:", bond_price(half_five_thousand, 10_000))

Autograder

Paste or load your instruments.py and run the suite on your own case. Your code is saved in this browser as you type.

Running the suite locally

Copy test_project.py and instance.py next to your instruments.py, then run, with the seed shown in the autograder panel:

QF_SEED=<your seed> pytest -q -p no:cacheprovider --assert=plain -W error test_project.py

--assert=plain turns off pytest's comparison details, so a failure shows the test's own message and no values, and -W error turns warnings into failures, both as in the browser. The browser runs the same file.

Rung 4 · Stretch

Not graded. Each extension needs a stated convention and a check against a direct computation.

  • A limit order book. Match limit orders on one book by price and then by time, as in Lesson 4, and turn each fill into a CashTrade that settles in the period the book assigns. State the matching rule and check that the total quantity bought equals the total quantity sold.
