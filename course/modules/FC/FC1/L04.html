Order Types and the Limit Order Book

  • Uses: bid–ask spread (Lesson 3), and the algebra of sums and integer products
  • Introduces: order types (market, limit, stop); limit order book
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study

An order states what a trader wants: a quantity, a side and, for most orders, a price limit or a trigger. The rules that match orders decide what a trader actually gets, and a market order can fill at prices other than the one on the screen. This lesson defines three order types and the limit order book they meet, under price-time priority (Definition 2.1). It proves two invariants of that book: it never rests crossed, and the shares entered into matching equal the resting shares, plus twice the traded shares, plus the cancelled shares (Proposition 2.3). Lab 1 checks both invariants after each of 300 simulated events.

1Order types

Definition 1.1 (market, limit and stop orders)

An order states a side, buy or sell, and a whole number of shares. There are three types in this lesson.

  • Market order. It trades at the best prices available on the opposite side now, best price first. Any shares it cannot fill are cancelled. A market order never rests in the book.
  • Limit order. It has a price limit \(p\). A buy limit trades only at prices at most \(p\), and a sell limit only at prices at least \(p\). Shares it does not fill stay in the book at \(p\) as a resting order (defined in Remark 1.2).
  • Stop order. It has a stop price \(s\) and is inactive until the last trade price (defined in Remark 1.2) reaches \(s\). A buy stop activates when the last trade price is at or above \(s\), and a sell stop when the last trade price is at or below \(s\). On activation it becomes a market order for its full quantity.
Remark 1.2 (the rules of this model)

A resting order is the unfilled part of a limit order, waiting in the book at its price. The best bid is the highest resting bid price, and the best ask is the lowest resting ask price. A fill is at the resting order's price, not the incoming order's. The last trade price is the price of the most recent fill. Before the first fill there is no last trade price, so no stop is active. Stops are checked only after the arriving order that caused the trades has finished matching, not after each fill of that order. Only the last trade price at the check counts: a buy stop at 1005 is not triggered by an earlier fill at 1010 when the arriving order's last fill is at 1000. Each stop that triggers is then activated, in arrival order, one at a time. Its fills can move the last trade price, so the check repeats until no pending stop triggers.

Example 1.3 (a market buy walks the book)

The ask side holds 60 shares at 1000 cents and 100 shares at 1100 cents. A market buy of 150 shares fills 60 shares at 1000 and 90 shares at 1100, in that order. The cost is \(60 \cdot 1000 + 90 \cdot 1100 = 159000\) cents. The average price is \(159000/150 = 1060\) cents, which is neither the best ask nor the second. Ten shares remain at 1100 on the ask side. Lab 1 Fixture A prints these numbers.

Example 1.4 (a stop sell fills below its stop)

The ask side holds 100 shares at 990 cents, and the bid side holds 100 shares at 985 cents and 100 shares at 980 cents. A sell stop for 100 shares has stop price 990. A market buy of 100 shares fills at 990, so the last trade price is 990. The stop's condition holds, since 990 is at most 990, so the stop activates as a market sell. It fills 100 shares at the best bid, 985 cents, which is below its stop. Lab 1 Fixture B prints both fills.

2The limit order book

Definition 2.1 (limit order book, price-time priority)

The limit order book is the set of resting limit orders on each side. The bid side is ordered by price from highest to lowest, and the ask side from lowest to highest. The best bid is the highest bid price, and the best ask is the lowest ask price. Under price-time priority, an incoming order meets the best price on the opposite side first. Among resting orders at that price it meets the earliest arrival first. Share size does not affect priority. The book is crossed when the best bid is at least the best ask.

Assumption A1 (the start)

The book starts empty, so it is not crossed before the first event.

Proposition 2.2 (the book is never crossed)

An event is an arrival or an activation of a stop. Under A1 and the rules of Section 1, the book is not crossed after any event.

Proof

Induct on events. Assume the book is not crossed before an event, so the best bid \(\beta\) is below the best ask \(\alpha\), where a side that is empty is treated as having no price. Consider an arriving buy order. If it is a market order, it removes asks and leaves the bids unchanged, so the best bid stays \(\beta\) and the best ask can only rise, to \(\alpha' \ge \alpha\). Then \(\beta < \alpha \le \alpha'\). If it is a buy limit at \(p\), it fills asks priced at most \(p\), in price-time order, until it is filled or no such ask remains. Suppose its residual rests. Then no ask at most \(p\) remains, so every remaining ask is above \(p\), and the new best bid is \(\max(\beta, p)\). The new best ask \(\alpha'\) is at least \(\alpha\), because the book only lost asks, so \(\beta < \alpha \le \alpha'\), and \(p < \alpha'\). Hence \(\max(\beta, p) < \alpha'\). If the residual is filled, nothing rests, and the argument for a market order applies. Sell orders and sell-side activations follow by the same argument with the roles of bids and asks exchanged. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.3 (share conservation)

Let \(G\) be the total shares of all orders that have entered matching, counting a market order at its arrival, a limit order at its arrival, and a stop order at its activation. Let \(R\) be the shares resting in the book, \(X\) the shares traded, and \(C\) the shares cancelled from market orders. Then \[ G = R + 2X + C. \] Stops that have not activated are not counted in \(G\).

Proof

Each order's entered quantity is split into three parts: the shares it filled, the shares cancelled, and the shares it left resting. For an order that fully fills, the last two parts are zero. Summing over all orders gives \(G = \sum_o \text{filled}_o + C + R\). Each fill of \(q\) shares between a buy and a sell adds \(q\) to the filled amount of both orders, so \(\sum_o \text{filled}_o = 2X\). Substituting gives \(G = R + 2X + C\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.4 (why twice)

A trade of \(q\) shares is one event with two sides, and it reduces the quantity of an incoming order and of a resting order by \(q\) each. Counting \(X\) once would undercount the shares that have left the book. The factor 2 counts the two orders that each traded share belongs to.

Lab 1 · Two fixtures and a seeded order flow on one book

The lab first prints two fixtures worked in Examples 1.3 and 1.4. Fixture A should fill 60 shares at 1000 and 90 at 1100, with average 1060 cents and 10 shares left at 1100. Fixture B should fill the market buy at 990 and then the activated stop at 985, below its stop of 990. Then it generates 300 SIMULATED events from seed 2026, which are limit, market and stop orders with prices within 20 cents of 10000. By Proposition 2.2 the book should never be crossed, and by Proposition 2.3 the identity \(G = R + 2X + C\) should hold after every event. The lab counts failures of each, and both counts should be zero.

"""FC1 lab L04: market, limit and stop orders on one limit order book, in integer cents.

Standard library only; no network. Prices are whole cents. The order flow is generated from a fixed seed, so it is
SIMULATED and every run prints the same numbers. Two fixtures are worked by hand in the lesson and are printed first.
The matching follows the lesson's rules: a fill is at the resting order's price, the best price is taken first, and
ties go to the earlier arrival. A market order keeps no residual, so its unfilled shares are cancelled. A stop order
waits until the last trade price reaches its stop price, checked once the arriving order has finished matching, and
then it becomes a market order. Every event is checked for an uncrossed book and for share conservation,
submitted = resting + 2 * traded + cancelled, which is exact in integers.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from collections import Counter
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 2026
MID = 10000
OFFSET_CENTS = 20
EVENTS = 300
TRACE = 8


@dataclass
class Order:
    """An order. side is +1 for a buy and -1 for a sell. kind is limit, market or stop; a stop becomes market."""

    seq: int
    side: int
    kind: str
    qty: int
    price: int | None = None
    stop: int | None = None


class Book:
    """A limit order book with price-time priority, plus a list of stop orders that have not yet activated."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.bids: list[Order] = []
        self.asks: list[Order] = []
        self.pending: list[Order] = []
        self.last: int | None = None
        self.fills: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
        self.submitted = 0
        self.traded = 0
        self.cancelled = 0

    def resting(self) -> int:
        """Shares resting in the book on both sides."""
        return sum(o.qty for o in self.bids) + sum(o.qty for o in self.asks)

    def best_bid(self) -> int | None:
        """The highest resting bid price, or None when the bid side is empty."""
        return max((o.price for o in self.bids if o.price is not None), default=None)

    def best_ask(self) -> int | None:
        """The lowest resting ask price, or None when the ask side is empty."""
        return min((o.price for o in self.asks if o.price is not None), default=None)

    def best_resting(self, side: int) -> Order | None:
        """The order an incoming order on side meets first: best price, then earliest arrival."""
        book = self.asks if side > 0 else self.bids
        if not book:
            return None
        if side > 0:
            return min(book, key=lambda o: (o.price, o.seq))
        return min(book, key=lambda o: (-(o.price or 0), o.seq))

    def match(self, order: Order) -> None:
        """Match an arriving market or limit order, then rest any limit residual. A stop is sent here once active."""
        self.submitted += order.qty
        while order.qty > 0:
            best = self.best_resting(order.side)
            if best is None or best.price is None:
                break
            if order.kind == "limit" and order.price is not None:
                crosses = best.price <= order.price if order.side > 0 else best.price >= order.price
                if not crosses:
                    break
            fill = min(order.qty, best.qty)
            best.qty -= fill
            order.qty -= fill
            self.traded += fill
            self.last = best.price
            self.fills.append((best.price, fill))
            if best.qty == 0:
                (self.asks if order.side > 0 else self.bids).remove(best)
        if order.qty > 0 and order.kind == "limit":
            (self.bids if order.side > 0 else self.asks).append(order)
        elif order.qty > 0:
            self.cancelled += order.qty
            order.qty = 0

    def activate(self) -> int:
        """Send every pending stop whose trigger the last trade price has reached, earliest arrival first.

        A buy stop triggers when the last trade price is at or above its stop price, and a sell stop when the last
        trade price is at or below it. Each activation can trade and move the last price, so the check repeats until
        none triggers.
        """
        count = 0
        while self.last is not None:
            index = next(
                (
                    i
                    for i, o in enumerate(self.pending)
                    if o.stop is not None
                    and ((o.side > 0 and self.last >= o.stop) or (o.side < 0 and self.last <= o.stop))
                ),
                None,
            )
            if index is None:
                break
            order = self.pending.pop(index)
            order.kind = "market"
            self.match(order)
            count += 1
        return count


def fixture_walk() -> None:
    """Fixture A: a market buy of 150 shares walks two ask levels. Fixture B: a stop sell fills below its stop."""
    book = Book()
    book.match(Order(0, -1, "limit", 60, price=1000))
    book.match(Order(1, -1, "limit", 100, price=1100))
    book.match(Order(2, 1, "market", 150))
    cost = sum(price * qty for price, qty in book.fills)
    filled = sum(qty for _, qty in book.fills)
    print("Fixture A, market buy of 150 shares against asks 1000 x 60 and 1100 x 100")
    print(f"fills (price, shares): {book.fills}")
    print(f"total cost {cost} cents for {filled} shares, average {cost // filled} cents")
    print(f"shares still resting on the ask side at 1100: {sum(o.qty for o in book.asks)}")

    book = Book()
    book.match(Order(0, -1, "limit", 100, price=990))
    book.match(Order(1, 1, "limit", 100, price=985))
    book.match(Order(2, 1, "limit", 100, price=980))
    book.pending.append(Order(3, -1, "stop", 100, stop=990))
    book.activate()
    book.match(Order(4, 1, "market", 100))
    activated = book.activate()
    print("Fixture B, asks 990 x 100, bids 985 x 100 and 980 x 100, a sell stop of 100 at 990")
    print(f"market buy fills, then the stop activates: {activated} stop(s) activated")
    print(f"all fills (price, shares): {book.fills}")
    print(f"the stop sell filled at {book.fills[-1][0]} cents, below its stop of 990 cents")


def random_order(rng: random.Random, seq: int) -> Order:
    """A SIMULATED order: a limit, market or stop order on a random side, for a whole number of lots of 10 shares."""
    side = rng.choice((1, -1))
    qty = 10 * rng.randint(1, 10)
    roll = rng.random()
    if roll < 0.6:
        return Order(seq, side, "limit", qty, price=MID - side * rng.randint(0, OFFSET_CENTS))
    if roll < 0.85:
        return Order(seq, side, "market", qty)
    return Order(seq, side, "stop", qty, stop=MID + side * rng.randint(0, OFFSET_CENTS))


def label(value: int | None) -> str:
    """The price in cents, or none when the side is empty."""
    return "none" if value is None else f"{value}"


def main() -> None:
    fixture_walk()

    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    book = Book()
    kinds: Counter[str] = Counter()
    uncrossed_failures = 0
    conservation_failures = 0
    stops_activated = 0
    for seq in range(EVENTS):
        order = random_order(rng, seq)
        kinds[order.kind] += 1
        if order.kind == "stop":
            book.pending.append(order)
        else:
            book.match(order)
        stops_activated += book.activate()
        bid, ask = book.best_bid(), book.best_ask()
        if bid is not None and ask is not None and bid >= ask:
            uncrossed_failures += 1
        if book.submitted != book.resting() + 2 * book.traded + book.cancelled:
            conservation_failures += 1
        if seq < TRACE:
            print(
                f"event {seq}: {order.kind} {'buy' if order.side > 0 else 'sell'}, "
                f"best bid {label(bid)}, best ask {label(ask)}, last trade {label(book.last)}"
            )

    print(
        f"SIMULATED flow of {EVENTS} events, seed {SEED}: {kinds['limit']} limit, "
        f"{kinds['market']} market, {kinds['stop']} stop arrivals"
    )
    print(
        f"trades {len(book.fills)}, shares traded {book.traded}, stops activated {stops_activated}, "
        f"stops still pending {len(book.pending)}"
    )
    print(f"market shares cancelled {book.cancelled}, shares resting {book.resting()}")
    print(f"events with a crossed book: {uncrossed_failures}")
    print(f"events where submitted != resting + 2 * traded + cancelled: {conservation_failures}")
    print(f"best bid and ask at the end: {label(book.best_bid())} and {label(book.best_ask())}")

3Where the canon breaks

  • The order types are this lesson's definitions. The lesson states no venue's rules for market, limit or stop orders. Stop-limit orders and other variants are outside this lesson, and Definition 1.1 applies only to the three types it states.
  • The model has no cancellations or modifications of resting orders. Proposition 2.3 has no term for shares removed from the book by withdrawal. A resting order that is withdrawn would add a term to the identity, and this lesson does not analyse withdrawals.
  • Price-time priority is one rule. The lesson uses it because Definition 2.1 states it. Its consequences, such as which order fills first, follow from the rule, and this lesson does not compare the rule with any other.
  • The invariants depend on A1. Proposition 2.2 needs the empty start in A1. Problem R2 shows what fails from a crossed start.
  • The simulated flow is not evidence about markets. Lab 1 uses generated orders from a fixed seed. Its counts describe the model's events under this seed only.

4Misconceptions

  1. “A market order fills at the price on the screen.”

    A market order fills at the resting prices it meets, in order. Example 1.3 has a best ask of 1000 cents, but a market buy of 150 shares fills at 1000 and at 1100, and its average is 1060 cents. Only the first 60 shares fill at the screen price.

  2. “A stop order sells at its stop price.”

    A stop becomes a market order when it activates, so it fills at the prices on the opposite side. Example 1.4 has a stop of 990 cents that fills at 985 cents, the best bid at the time of activation.

  3. “Price-time priority fills the largest order at the best price first.”

    Definition 2.1 gives priority by arrival time at a price, and share size plays no part. If two sells at 1000 cents arrive with 10 shares first and 100 shares later, a market buy of 10 shares fills the 10-share order, which arrived first, and leaves the 100-share order untouched.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · A market sell walks two bid levels

The bid side holds 60 shares at 995 cents and 100 shares at 990 cents. A market sell of 100 shares arrives. Find the fills, the average price in cents over the 100 shares, and the shares left resting.

Solution

The sell meets the highest bid first. It fills 60 shares at 995 and then 40 shares at 990. The cost is \(60 \cdot 995 + 40 \cdot 990 = 59700 + 39600 = 99300\) cents. The average is \(99300/100 = 993\) cents, on the basis of the 100 shares sold. The bid at 990 keeps \(100 - 40 = 60\) shares.

M2 · A limit buy that partly rests

The ask side holds 30 shares at 1000, 30 at 1001 and 40 at 1003. A buy limit of 50 shares at 1002 arrives. Find the fills, and say what is left on each side.

Solution

The buy meets the asks at or below 1002 in price-time order. It fills 30 shares at 1000 and 20 shares at 1001. It is fully filled, so nothing rests. The asks left are 10 shares at 1001 and 40 shares at 1003, and the bid side is empty. The book is not crossed, as Proposition 2.2 requires.

M3 · When a sell stop activates

A sell stop has stop price 1000 cents. The last trade price is 1005 cents, and then a trade occurs at 1001 cents. Does the stop activate at either price? Then a trade occurs at 1000 cents. Does it activate now?

Solution

A sell stop activates when the last trade price is at or below its stop. The last trade price 1005 is above 1000, so no activation. After a trade at 1001 the last trade price is 1001, which is above 1000, so no activation. After a trade at 1000 the last trade price is at the stop, so the stop triggers and activates.

Proof

P1 · A market order never crosses the book

Under A1, prove that the book is not crossed after an arriving market buy order, using only the facts that a market buy removes asks and leaves bids unchanged.

Solution

Before the arrival, the best bid \(\beta\) is below the best ask \(\alpha\). The market buy removes asks, so the best ask after it is \(\alpha' \ge \alpha\), since the lowest remaining ask cannot be lower than the lowest ask before. The bids are unchanged, so the best bid is still \(\beta\). Then \(\beta < \alpha \le \alpha'\). If the ask side is now empty, the book is trivially not crossed.

P2 · Conservation with a cancelled remainder

The ask side holds 100 shares at 1000 cents and the book has no other orders. A market buy of 150 shares arrives. Check Proposition 2.3 by counting \(G\), \(R\), \(X\) and \(C\).

Solution

The market buy enters with 150 shares, and the 100 resting asks entered earlier, so \(G = 250\). The fills are 100 shares, so \(X = 100\). The market buy's 50 unfilled shares are cancelled, so \(C = 50\). Nothing rests, so \(R = 0\). Then \(R + 2X + C = 0 + 200 + 50 = 250 = G\).

P3 · Activation at the stop price

A buy stop has stop price \(s\). Show that a last trade price \(t\) with \(t > s\) activates it, and that \(t < s\) does not.

Solution

A buy stop activates when the last trade price is at or above its stop, so \(t \ge s\) is the condition. For \(t > s\) the condition holds, so the stop activates. For \(t < s\) it fails, so the stop stays pending. The case \(t = s\) also activates, because the condition includes equality.

Empirical

E1 · Stops in Lab 1

The lab prints 24 stops activated and 18 pending, so 24 + 18 = 42. The lab prints 42 stop arrivals in its count line. Every stop either activates once or is still in the pending list, so the two totals agree.

E2 · Reading the trace

Lab 1 prints the best bid, best ask and last trade price after the first eight events. Identify the first event after which a best ask is printed, and explain why the best bid is printed as none after event 7.

Solution

Events 0 and 1 leave the ask side empty, so the best ask is none. Event 2, a limit sell, is the first to leave a resting ask, and the trace prints a best ask of 10015. After event 7, a market sell has consumed the last resting bid, so no bid rests and the best bid is none.

E3 · Shares per trade in the flow

Solution

The average is \(5600/173\) shares per trade. It is an exact fraction, with no approximation, and it is a property of this simulated run.

Research

R1 · A cascade of two stops

Construct a book and two pending sell stops so that the first activation moves the last trade price to the stop price of the second. Give the fills.

Solution

Let the ask side hold 100 shares at 990, and the bid side hold 100 shares at 985 and 100 shares at 980. Let sell stop A be 100 shares with stop 990, and sell stop B be 100 shares with stop 985. A market buy of 100 shares fills at 990, so the last trade price is 990 and A activates. A sells 100 shares at the best bid, 985, so the last trade price becomes 985. That is at most B's stop of 985, so B activates and sells 100 shares at 980. The fills are (990, 100), (985, 100) and (980, 100), in that order. The rule of Remark 1.2 gives exactly this sequence for this book.

R2 · What fails from a crossed start

Suppose a book begins with a best bid of 1005 cents and a best ask of 1000 cents, which A1 excludes. Show that a later limit buy of 5 shares at 999 cents, which does not trade, leaves the book crossed, so the conclusion of Proposition 2.2 fails.

Solution

The limit buy at 999 does not trade, because the best ask 1000 is above 999. It rests as a bid, and the best bid stays 1005 since 1005 is above 999. The best bid 1005 is still at least the best ask 1000 after the event, so the book is crossed. The proof of Proposition 2.2 uses the assumption \(\beta < \alpha\) at the start, and that assumption is what fails here.

R3 · Why the traded term has a factor of two

Explain in one paragraph why Proposition 2.3 uses \(2X\), not \(X\), and check the factor on a single fill of 60 shares.

Solution

A fill is one event between two orders, a buyer and a seller. Each order's quantity falls by the fill, so the fill appears in the filled part of both orders. Summing the filled parts over all orders counts each fill twice. For a single fill of 60 shares, the buyer's and the seller's filled parts are each 60, so the sum is 120 = \(2 \cdot 60\).

    Definition 2.1 gives priority by arrival at a price. Share size plays no part, so the earlier 100-share sell fills 10 shares.

  7. Lab 1 prints 24 stops activated and 18 pending at the end. How many stop arrivals are there in total?

    Each stop arrival is either activated or pending, so \(24 + 18 = 42\), which matches the lab's printed count of stop arrivals.

  8. In Proposition 2.3, why is the traded term \(2X\) and not \(X\)?

    Options

    A fill of \(q\) shares adds \(q\) to the filled part of the buyer and \(q\) to the filled part of the seller, so the sum over orders is \(2X\). Problem R3.

  9. Which assumption makes the book uncrossed before the first event in Proposition 2.2?

    Options

    Assumption A1 starts the book empty, so it is not crossed. Problem R2 shows the conclusion fails from a crossed start.
