Carr–Madan FFT Option Pricing

In advanced asset pricing models—such as Heston stochastic volatility, Bates jump-diffusion, and Variance Gamma—the risk-neutral probability density of the terminal stock price is rarely known in closed form, but its characteristic function is readily available. The landmark Carr–Madan Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) method bypasses density integration by damping the non-integrable call payoff and inverting the modified characteristic function directly. A single call to an \(N\)-point FFT algorithm simultaneously evaluates option prices across an entire grid of strikes in \(O(N \log N)\) operations.

  • Introduces: Carr–Madan FFT
  • Prerequisites: characteristic function, Black–Scholes–Merton PDE

1. Characteristic Functions and Payoff Damping

Let \(s_T = \ln(S_T)\) be the log-price of the underlying asset at maturity \(T\). The risk-neutral characteristic function of \(s_T\) is defined for complex argument \(u\) by:

\[ \phi_T(u) = \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q} \left[ e^{i u s_T} \right] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i u s} q_T(s) \mathrm{d}s \]

where \(q_T(s)\) is the risk-neutral density. For Black–Scholes dynamics with drift \(\nu = r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\), \(\phi_T(u) = \exp\left(i u (s_0 + \nu T) - \frac{1}{2} u^2 \sigma^2 T\right)\).

The price of a European call option with strike \(K = e^k\) is given by \(C_T(k) = e^{-rT} \int_k^\infty (e^s - e^k) q_T(s) \mathrm{d}s\). As \(k \to -\infty\), \(C_T(k) \to S_0 - K e^{-rT} \approx S_0 > 0\). Consequently, \(C_T(k)\) is not square-integrable on \(\mathbb{R}\) and does not possess a direct Fourier transform.

Definition 4.1 · Damped Call Price and Carr–Madan FFT

The damped call price is defined for a strictly positive damping parameter \(\alpha > 0\) by:

\[ c_T(k) = e^{\alpha k} C_T(k) \]

Carr–Madan FFT pricing is the numerical inversion of the Fourier transform of the damped call price \(c_T(k)\) using the discrete Fast Fourier Transform, producing an entire cross-section of European option prices across a log-strike grid in \(O(N \log N)\) computational operations.

2. The Carr–Madan Fourier Inversion Formula

Following Carr & Madan (1999), let \(\psi_T(v)\) denote the Fourier transform of the damped option price \(c_T(k)\):

\[ \psi_T(v) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i v k} c_T(k) \mathrm{d}k = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i v k} e^{\alpha k} C_T(k) \mathrm{d}k \]
Theorem 4.1 · Carr–Madan Transform

If the \((\alpha + 1)\)-th moment of the underlying asset \(\mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[S_T^{\alpha + 1}] < \infty\), the Fourier transform of the damped call price is given analytically by:

\[ \psi_T(v) = \frac{e^{-rT} \phi_T(v - (\alpha + 1)i)}{\alpha^2 + \alpha - v^2 + i (2\alpha + 1) v} \]

and the original call price is recovered via the inverse Fourier integral:

\[ C_T(k) = \frac{e^{-\alpha k}}{\pi} \int_0^\infty e^{-i v k} \psi_T(v) \mathrm{d}v \]

Proof

Substitute the definition of \(C_T(k)\) into \(\psi_T(v)\) and interchange the order of integration via Fubini's theorem:

\[ \psi_T(v) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i v k} e^{\alpha k} \left[ e^{-rT} \int_k^\infty (e^s - e^k) q_T(s) \mathrm{d}s \right] \mathrm{d}k = e^{-rT} \int_{-\infty}^\infty q_T(s) \left[ \int_{-\infty}^s (e^s e^{\alpha k} - e^{(\alpha + 1)k}) e^{i v k} \mathrm{d}k \right] \mathrm{d}s \]

Evaluating the inner integral over \(k \in (-\infty, s)\):

\[ \int_{-\infty}^s e^s e^{(\alpha + iv)k} \mathrm{d}k = \frac{e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)s}}{\alpha + iv}, \quad \int_{-\infty}^s e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)k} \mathrm{d}k = \frac{e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)s}}{\alpha + 1 + iv} \]

Subtracting the two fractions yields:

\[ \frac{e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)s}}{\alpha + iv} - \frac{e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)s}}{\alpha + 1 + iv} = \frac{e^{(\alpha + 1 + iv)s}}{(\alpha + iv)(\alpha + 1 + iv)} = \frac{e^{i(v - (\alpha + 1)i)s}}{\alpha^2 + \alpha - v^2 + i (2\alpha + 1) v} \]

Integrating against \(q_T(s) \mathrm{d}s\) produces the characteristic function \(\phi_T(v - (\alpha + 1)i)\), proving the theorem.

3. Discrete FFT Mapping and Grid Reciprocity

To evaluate the continuous inversion integral numerically, we discretize the frequency domain over \(N\) points with step size \(\Delta v\): \(v_j = j \Delta v\) for \(j = 0, \dots, N-1\). The integral is approximated via Simpson's rule quadrature:

\[ C_T(k) \approx \frac{e^{-\alpha k}}{\pi} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} e^{-i v_j k} \psi_T(v_j) w_j \Delta v \]

where \(w_j = \frac{1}{3} [3 + (-1)^j - \delta_{j, 0}]\) are the Simpson quadrature weights.

To apply the discrete Fast Fourier Transform, we define a uniform log-strike grid with \(N\) points and step size \(\Delta k\):

\[ k_u = -b + u \Delta k, \quad u = 0, \dots, N-1, \quad b = \frac{N \Delta k}{2} \]

Substituting \(k_u\) and \(v_j\) into the exponent yields \(v_j k_u = j \Delta v (-b + u \Delta k) = -j b \Delta v + j u (\Delta v \Delta k)\).

Theorem 4.2 · FFT Reciprocal Grid Constraint

The discrete quadrature sum reduces exactly to a standard Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) if and only if the spatial and frequency mesh step sizes satisfy the reciprocal relationship:

\[ \Delta k \cdot \Delta v = \frac{2\pi}{N} \]

Proof

Under this reciprocal relationship, the term \(j u (\Delta v \Delta k)\) becomes \(\frac{2\pi}{N} j u\). The sum can be factored as:

\[ C_T(k_u) \approx \frac{e^{-\alpha k_u}}{\pi} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} e^{-i \frac{2\pi}{N} j u} \left[ e^{i b v_j} \psi_T(v_j) w_j \Delta v \right] \]

The bracketed quantity forms an \(N\)-dimensional complex vector \(\mathbf{x}\). The summation is the exact definition of the inverse Discrete Fourier Transform \(\operatorname{IDFT}(\mathbf{x})_u\), which is computed via the Cooley–Tukey FFT algorithm in \(O(N \log_2 N)\) operations.

Lab 1 · Carr–Madan Fourier inversion across damping parameter alpha

We implement Carr–Madan Fourier inversion option pricing on SIMULATED market parameters, inverting the Black–Scholes characteristic function via discrete Simpson quadrature across damping parameter alpha in {0.75, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5}, and compare against analytical call prices at strikes K in {90, 100, 110}.

"""Lab 1 (L04): Carr-Madan FFT characteristic function inversion and damping parameter sweep.

SIMULATED market parameters under geometric Brownian motion.
Prices European call options across strikes using Carr-Madan FFT, sweeps damping parameter
alpha, and investigates strike-frequency grid spacing reciprocity.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math

import numpy as np


def bsm_call_analytic(spot: float, strike: float, rate: float, vol: float, maturity: float) -> float:
    sqrt_t = math.sqrt(maturity)
    d1 = (math.log(spot / strike) + (rate + 0.5 * vol * vol) * maturity) / (vol * sqrt_t)
    d2 = d1 - vol * sqrt_t
    cdf_d1 = 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(d1 / math.sqrt(2.0)))
    cdf_d2 = 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(d2 / math.sqrt(2.0)))
    return float(spot * cdf_d1 - strike * math.exp(-rate * maturity) * cdf_d2)


def carr_madan_fft_prices(
    spot: float,
    rate: float,
    vol: float,
    maturity: float,
    alpha: float = 1.5,
    n_points: int = 1024,
    eta: float = 0.25,
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Evaluate European call option prices across strike grid using Carr-Madan FFT."""
    lambda_k = 2.0 * math.pi / (n_points * eta)
    b = 0.5 * n_points * lambda_k
    k_grid = -b + np.arange(n_points) * lambda_k  # Log-strikes k = ln(K)
    strikes = np.exp(k_grid)

    v = np.arange(n_points) * eta
    simpson_weights = 3.0 + (-1.0) ** (np.arange(n_points) + 1)
    simpson_weights[0] = 1.0
    simpson_weights[-1] = 1.0
    simpson_weights = simpson_weights / 3.0

    # Characteristic function of log(S_T) under Q
    u = v - (alpha + 1.0) * 1j
    # phi(u) = exp(i * u * (ln(S0) + (r - 0.5*sigma^2)*T) - 0.5 * sigma^2 * u^2 * T)
    drift = math.log(spot) + (rate - 0.5 * vol * vol) * maturity
    phi_u = np.exp(1j * u * drift - 0.5 * (vol * vol) * (u * u) * maturity)

    denom = alpha * alpha + alpha - v * v + 1j * (2.0 * alpha + 1.0) * v
    psi_v = math.exp(-rate * maturity) * phi_u / denom

    x = np.exp(1j * b * v) * psi_v * eta * simpson_weights
    fft_vals = np.fft.fft(x)

    c_damped = np.real(fft_vals) / math.pi
    call_prices = np.exp(-alpha * k_grid) * c_damped

    return strikes, call_prices


def main() -> None:
    spot = 100.0
    rate = 0.05
    vol = 0.20
    maturity = 1.0

    alphas = [0.75, 1.50, 2.50, 3.50]
    test_strikes = [90.0, 100.0, 110.0]

    print(f"Target strikes: {test_strikes}")
    print("Analytical BSM benchmarks:")
    for k in test_strikes:
        print(f"  K = {k:5.1f} | Price = {bsm_call_analytic(spot, k, rate, vol, maturity):.6f}")

    print("\nCarr-Madan FFT prices across damping alpha:")
    for a in alphas:
        strikes, prices = carr_madan_fft_prices(spot, rate, vol, maturity, alpha=a, n_points=1024, eta=0.20)
        # Interpolate to test strikes
        atm_price = float(np.interp(100.0, strikes, prices))
        atm_ref = bsm_call_analytic(spot, 100.0, rate, vol, maturity)
        err = abs(atm_price - atm_ref)
        print(f"  alpha = {a:4.2f} | ATM Price = {atm_price:.6f} | Error = {err:.2e}")


main()

Where the canon breaks

While the Carr–Madan algorithm revolutionized quantitative volatility surface calibration, practical implementations face severe constraints:

  1. Reciprocal Grid Rigidity: The fundamental constraint \(\Delta k \Delta v = 2\pi / N\) creates an unyielding tradeoff between strike resolution and integration domain. Obtaining fine strike resolution (\(\Delta k\) small) forces \(\Delta v = 2\pi / (N \Delta k)\) to be large, introducing substantial discretization error unless \(N\) is scaled to massive values (e.g., \(N = 65,536\)). Fractional FFT (Chourdakis 2005) is required to decouple the grids.
  2. Heavy-Tailed Moment Explosions: Under stochastic volatility models (such as Heston), \(\mathbb{E}[S_T^{\alpha + 1}]\) is finite only on a bounded interval \(\alpha \in (\alpha_{\min}, \alpha_{\max})\). Choosing \(\alpha\) outside this strip causes explosive numerical overflow in \(\phi_T(v - (\alpha + 1)i)\).
  3. Strikes Off-Lattice: Exchange-traded strikes are fixed arbitrary numbers (e.g., \(100, 105, 110\)), whereas FFT generates prices on a rigid lattice \(k_u = -b + u \Delta k\). Interpolating off-grid prices using cubic splines introduces interpolation artifacts that contaminate high-order Greeks like Gamma.

Misconceptions

  1. Carr–Madan FFT can price options with arbitrary strike grids directly without interpolation.

    Reality: The standard FFT algorithm produces option prices on a rigid uniform log-strike grid \(k_u = -b + u \Delta k\) determined by the reciprocal constraint \(\Delta k = \frac{2\pi}{N \Delta v}\). Quoted strikes rarely coincide with lattice points, requiring cubic spline interpolation to extract prices at market strikes.

  2. The damping parameter \(\alpha\) can be chosen as any positive real number.

    Reality: The parameter \(\alpha\) must satisfy the integrability condition that the \((\alpha + 1)\)-th moment of the underlying stock exists under the risk-neutral measure. In models with heavy tails (such as Heston or jump diffusions), moments explode above a critical threshold, restricting admissible \(\alpha\) to a finite interval.

  3. Carr–Madan FFT is directly applicable to American-style option pricing.

    Reality: Carr–Madan FFT relies on analytical terminal payoff integration via characteristic functions, which applies strictly to European contingent claims. American options require tracking an unknown free boundary across all intermediate time steps, which single-step Fourier inversion cannot accomplish.

Tier 1 · Mechanics

An options pricing engine uses \(N = 4096\) points and an integration upper bound of \(v_{\max} = 100.0\). Calculate the frequency grid spacing \(\Delta v\), the resulting log-strike grid spacing \(\Delta k\), and the total log-strike span \(2b\).

Solution

The frequency spacing is \(\Delta v = v_{\max} / N = 100.0 / 4096 \approx 0.024414\). Under the Carr–Madan reciprocal relationship, \(\Delta k = \frac{2\pi}{N \Delta v} = \frac{2\pi}{100.0} \approx 0.0628318\). The total log-strike span is \(2b = N \Delta k = 4096 \times 0.0628318 \approx 257.36\). This wide span ensures that boundary truncation errors are negligible.

Evaluate the complex denominator \(D(\alpha, v) = \alpha^2 + \alpha - v^2 + i (2\alpha + 1) v\) for damping parameter \(\alpha = 1.5\) and frequency \(v = 2.0\), and compute its modulus \(|D|\).

Solution

Compute the real part: \(\operatorname{Re}(D) = (1.5)^2 + 1.5 - (2.0)^2 = 2.25 + 1.5 - 4.0 = -0.25\). Compute the imaginary part: \(\operatorname{Im}(D) = (2(1.5) + 1)(2.0) = (4.0)(2.0) = 8.0\). Thus \(D = -0.25 + 8.0i\). The modulus is \(|D| = \sqrt{(-0.25)^2 + (8.0)^2} = \sqrt{0.0625 + 64.0} = \sqrt{64.0625} \approx 8.0039\).

Suppose an FFT inversion evaluates the damped option price at log-strike \(k = \ln(100) \approx 4.60517\) as \(c_T(k) = 10450.58\) with damping parameter \(\alpha = 1.5\). Calculate the true call option price \(C_T(k)\).

Solution

The relationship between damped price and true call price is \(c_T(k) = e^{\alpha k} C_T(k)\). Solving for \(C_T(k)\): \(C_T(k) = e^{-\alpha k} c_T(k)\). The damping factor is \(e^{-\alpha k} = e^{-1.5 \ln(100)} = (100)^{-1.5} = 1 / 1000 = 0.001\). Multiplying by the damped price: \(C_T(k) = 0.001 \times 10450.58 = 10.45058\) currency units.

Tier 2 · Proof

Prove that under the Black–Scholes geometric Brownian motion model, the moment generating function \(\mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[S_T^p]\) is finite for all real exponents \(p \in \mathbb{R}\), implying that any \(\alpha > 0\) is an admissible damping parameter.

Solution

Under Black–Scholes, \(S_T = S_0 \exp\left((r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)T + \sigma \sqrt{T} Z\right)\) where \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\). Raising to the power \(p\) gives \(S_T^p = S_0^p \exp\left(p(r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)T + p \sigma \sqrt{T} Z\right)\). Because \(Z\) is Gaussian, its moment generating function \(\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta Z}] = e^{\theta^2 / 2}\) is finite for all \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}\). Setting \(\theta = p \sigma \sqrt{T}\), \(\mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[S_T^p] = S_0^p \exp\left(p(r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)T + \frac{1}{2} p^2 \sigma^2 T\right) < \infty\) for every \(p \in \mathbb{R}\). Since the \((\alpha + 1)\)-th moment is finite for all \(\alpha > 0\), the integrability condition holds globally on \((0, \infty)\).

Prove that if \(\alpha \le 0\), the damped call price \(c_T(k) = e^{\alpha k} C_T(k)\) is not integrable in \(L^1(\mathbb{R})\), so the Fourier transform \(\psi_T(v)\) does not converge.

Solution

As \(k \to -\infty\), the European call price satisfies \(\lim_{k \to -\infty} C_T(k) = S_0 - K e^{-rT} \to S_0 > 0\). For \(\alpha = 0\), \(c_T(k) = C_T(k) \to S_0\), so \(\int_{-\infty}^0 |c_T(k)| \mathrm{d}k \ge \int_{-\infty}^0 S_0 \mathrm{d}k = \infty\). For \(\alpha < 0\), as \(k \to -\infty\), \(e^{\alpha k} \to +\infty\), causing \(c_T(k)\) to diverge exponentially as \(k \to -\infty\). In both cases, \(c_T(k) \notin L^1(\mathbb{R})\), causing the Fourier integral to diverge.

Prove that the Fourier inversion formula produces an exact zero imaginary component for real option prices when evaluated over symmetric frequency ranges.

Solution

Because the damped price \(c_T(k)\) is real-valued, its Fourier transform satisfies Hermitian symmetry: \(\psi_T(-v) = \overline{\psi_T(v)}\). The inverse Fourier transform is \(c_T(k) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-i v k} \psi_T(v) \mathrm{d}v\). Splitting into real and imaginary parts: the imaginary integrand is \(\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i v k} \psi_T(v)) = -\sin(v k) \operatorname{Re}(\psi_T(v)) + \cos(v k) \operatorname{Im}(\psi_T(v))\). By Hermitian symmetry, \(\operatorname{Re}(\psi_T(v))\) is even and \(\operatorname{Im}(\psi_T(v))\) is odd. Thus both products with \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) are odd functions of \(v\). The integral of an odd function over symmetric bounds \((-\infty, \infty)\) vanishes identically, guaranteeing that the recovered price is strictly real.

A desk calculates option prices using Carr–Madan FFT with grid size \(N = 1024\) and integration limit \(v_{\max} = 50\). If the FFT execution takes \(0.12\) milliseconds in C++, how many full volatility surface calibrations (each requiring 50 strikes) can be completed per second?

Solution

Because an \(N = 1024\) FFT evaluates all 50 strikes simultaneously in a single sweep of \(0.12\) ms, the throughput is \(\text{Throughput} = \frac{1.0}{0.00012} \approx 8,333\) surface sweeps per second. In contrast, evaluating 50 strikes individually via numerical quadrature would require \(50 \times 0.05\text{ ms} = 2.5\text{ ms}\), yielding only 400 calibrations per second.

Suppose cubic spline interpolation across the FFT log-strike grid introduces a maximum error of \(0.004\) on out-of-the-money options. How does doubling \(N\) from \(2048\) to \(4096\) affect the interpolation error under cubic spline convergence?

Solution

Cubic spline interpolation error on smooth functions scales as \(O(\Delta k^4)\). When \(N\) is doubled with fixed \(v_{\max}\), \(\Delta k = \frac{2\pi}{N \Delta v}\) is halved: \(\Delta k \to \Delta k / 2\). The interpolation error is reduced by a factor of \(2^4 = 16\). The maximum interpolation error declines from \(0.004\) to \(0.004 / 16 = 0.00025\), eliminating spline artifacts.

Tier 4 · Research

Explain how the Fractional Fast Fourier Transform (Chourdakis 2005) decouples the reciprocal grid constraint \(\Delta k \Delta v = 2\pi / N\), and describe the Bluestein chirp-z transform algorithm used to compute it.

Solution

The Fractional FFT replaces the fixed Fourier kernel \(\exp(-i \frac{2\pi}{N} j u)\) with \(\exp(-i \gamma j u)\) for an arbitrary parameter \(\gamma = \Delta k \Delta v\). Bluestein's identity decomposes the product into squares: \(2 j u = j^2 + u^2 - (u - j)^2\). Substituting this into the kernel converts the discrete sum into a circular convolution of two sequences: \(y_j = x_j e^{-i \frac{\gamma}{2} j^2}\) and \(h_m = e^{i \frac{\gamma}{2} m^2}\). The convolution is evaluated using two standard power-of-two FFTs in \(O(N \log N)\) operations, allowing quants to select \(\Delta k\) and \(\Delta v\) independently.

In Heston stochastic volatility models, explain the 'Little Heston Trap' identified by Albrecher et al. (2007) regarding complex square root branch cuts in the characteristic function, and state the numerically stable representation.

Solution

In Heston's original formulation, the characteristic function contains terms \(d = \sqrt{(\kappa - i \rho \sigma v)^2 + \sigma^2 (v^2 - i v)}\) and \(g = \frac{\kappa - i \rho \sigma v - d}{\kappa - i \rho \sigma v + d}\). As maturity \(T\) or frequency \(v\) increases, the complex argument crosses the principal branch cut of the complex logarithm, introducing spurious \(2\pi i\) phase jumps that produce negative or erratic option prices. Albrecher et al. (2007) show that redefining \(d\) and using \(g^* = 1/g\) ensures that the argument remains strictly on the principal sheet of the complex plane, completely stabilizing Fourier inversion.

How does the Carr–Madan Fourier framework extend to pricing discrete barrier options using Hilbert transform operators and sequential convolution?

Solution

For a discrete monitoring barrier option with dates \(t_1, \dots, t_K\), the option value between monitoring dates satisfies the free-space diffusion governed by convolution with the transitional characteristic function. At each monitoring date, the barrier condition truncates the density in state space: \(V(t_k^-, s) = V(t_k^+, s) \mathbf{1}_{s > b}\). In the Fourier domain, spatial multiplication by an indicator corresponds to convolution with a Cauchy kernel (the Hilbert transform). The discrete Fourier-Hilbert sequence alternates between FFT and projection, pricing discrete barriers in \(O(K N \log N)\) time.

Retrieval quiz

  1. Why is a damping factor \(e^{\alpha k}\) necessary when taking the Fourier transform of a European call option price?

    Options

    As \(k \to -\infty\), \(C_T(k) \to S_0 > 0\). The un-damped integral diverges; damping by \(e^{\alpha k}\) forces exponential decay to zero as \(k \to -\infty\).

  2. What reciprocal mesh relationship connects the log-strike spacing \(\Delta k\) and frequency integration spacing \(\Delta v\) in the standard Carr–Madan FFT algorithm?

    Options

    The discrete DFT kernel \(\exp(-i \frac{2\pi}{N} j u)\) requires \(\Delta k \Delta v = 2\pi / N\).

  3. What computational complexity is required to evaluate European option prices across \(N\) strikes using Carr–Madan FFT?

    Options

    The Cooley–Tukey Fast Fourier Transform evaluates the entire discrete matrix-vector product in \(O(N \log N)\) operations.

  4. If the frequency spacing is \(\Delta v = 0.05\) and the desired log-strike spacing is \(\Delta k = 0.0306796\), what power-of-two FFT grid size \(N\) satisfies the reciprocal condition?

    \(N = \frac{2\pi}{\Delta k \Delta v} = \frac{2\pi}{0.05 \times 0.0306796} = \frac{6.283185}{0.00153398} = 4096\).

  5. What condition restricts the choice of the damping parameter \(\alpha\) in heavy-tailed asset pricing models?

    Options

    Evaluating the damped payoff transform requires the moment generating function to exist at \(\alpha + 1\), restricting \(\alpha\) to the model's finite moment strip.

  6. Why does the Carr–Madan method require off-grid interpolation for market option strikes?

    Options

    FFT outputs are locked to uniform steps \(\Delta k = \frac{2\pi}{N \Delta v}\); exact market strikes must be extracted via spline interpolation.

  7. If the damped call price is \(c_T(k) = 100\) at \(k = 0\) (\(S = K\)) with \(\alpha = 1.0\), and the risk-free rate is zero, what is the call option price \(C_T(0)\)?

    \(C_T(k) = e^{-\alpha k} c_T(k)\). At \(k = 0\), \(e^0 = 1\), so \(C_T(0) = 100\).

  8. How does the Fractional FFT (FRFT) overcome the limitation of the Carr–Madan reciprocal grid constraint?

    Options

    FRFT uses Bluestein's identity to express the discrete transform as a circular convolution, decoupling \(\Delta k\) and \(\Delta v\).

  9. What quadrature rule is typically applied to weight the discrete frequency nodes in the Carr–Madan inversion integral?

    Options

    Simpson's rule weights \(w_j = \frac{1}{3}[3 + (-1)^j - \delta_{j, 0}]\) provide third-order accuracy for the discrete Fourier integral.
