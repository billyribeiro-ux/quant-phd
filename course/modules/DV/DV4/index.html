Numerical Pricing (C++)

Analytical Black–Scholes formulas break down when contingent claims permit early exercise, depend on multiple state variables, or trade under non-Gaussian asset dynamics. This module develops the theoretical foundations and high-performance computational architectures of quantitative derivative pricing: 3-branch discrete recombining trinomial lattices and explicit finite difference equivalence; the Snell envelope and free-boundary PDE formulations for American early exercise; Longstaff–Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo (LSMC) regression on orthogonal polynomials; characteristic function inversion via Carr–Madan FFT and the exponential-convergence Fourier-cosine (COS) series expansion; adjoint algorithmic differentiation (AAD) for constant-time Greek vector evaluation; and systematic cross-method consistency architectures reconciling analytical, PDE, and Monte Carlo engines.

  • Prerequisites: DV1 Continuous-Time Arbitrage Pricing, M8 Scientific Computing, SC4 PDEs & Feynman–Kac, and CS5 C++ & Systems for Finance.
  • Unlocks: DV5 Variance Risk Premium & Volatility Trading, DV6 Exotic Derivatives, and Capstone Studios.
  • Project: Atlas milestone A6: Pricing library delivering BSM, binomial, trinomial, PDE, Monte Carlo, and LSMC engines with Greeks, a C++ kernel behind a Python API, and cross-method consistency suites. 52 hours.
  • Data: SIMULATED market parameters and continuous price trajectories under geometric Brownian motion labeled SIMULATED.

78 h lessons · 52 h project (40% of the 130 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV4 you can:

  1. Construct recombining trinomial lattices matching risk-neutral drift and variance, and prove their algebraic equivalence to explicit finite difference schemes under spatial mesh spacing \(\Delta x = \sigma \sqrt{3 \Delta t}\) (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the American option early-exercise boundary as a free-boundary variational inequality over the Snell envelope, and solve it numerically via Brennan–Schwartz and projected SOR algorithms (Lesson 2).
  3. Implement the Longstaff–Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo (LSMC) algorithm using weighted Laguerre and Hermite orthogonal polynomials to regress conditional continuation values on in-the-money paths (Lesson 3).
  4. Invert generalized characteristic functions via the Carr–Madan damped Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), analyzing the reciprocal grid spacing constraint \(\Delta k \cdot \Delta v = 2\pi / N\) and optimal damping parameter \(\alpha\) (Lesson 4).
  5. Expand transitional density functions into Fourier-cosine series via the Fang–Oosterlee COS method, achieving exponential convergence rates \(O(e^{-c N})\) for vanilla European contingent claims (Lesson 5).
  6. Implement an adjoint algorithmic differentiation (AAD) reverse-mode tape, evaluating complete option Greek sensitivity vectors \((\Delta, \Gamma, \mathcal{V}, \rho, \Theta)\) in bounded constant computational time relative to forward pricing (Lesson 6).
  7. Reconcile analytical, lattice, PDE, and Monte Carlo pricer implementations within an automated cross-method testing architecture with formal numerical error bounds (Lesson 7).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite readiness and core numerical engineering capabilities. Questions 1 through 8 test foundations from DV1, M8, SC4, and CS5. Questions 9 and 10 test skill competencies taught in project rungs 1 and 2 (trinomial lattice moment matching and Thomas tridiagonal obstacle solving). Bypassing rungs 1 and 2 requires achieving an 80% score (at least 8 of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Black–Scholes–Merton PDE for a derivative \(V(t, S)\), what algebraic relation connects the Theta (\(\Theta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial t}\)), Delta (\(\Delta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\)), and Gamma (\(\Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}\))?

    Options

    By DV1, the no-arbitrage Black–Scholes–Merton PDE is \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + r S \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - r V = 0\), establishing the fundamental gamma–theta–delta trade-off.

  2. Why does the explicit finite difference scheme for the diffusion equation \(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\) suffer from conditional stability?

    Options

    By SC4, von Neumann stability analysis demonstrates that the amplification factor for the forward-time central-space explicit scheme exceeds unity in modulus unless \(\Delta t \le \frac{\Delta x^2}{\sigma^2}\).

  3. What convergence rate does the Crank–Nicolson scheme achieve for smooth parabolic PDEs?

    Options

    By SC4, the Crank–Nicolson scheme averages explicit and implicit spatial operators at half-steps, canceling first-order temporal truncation errors to yield \(O(\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^2)\) unconditional stability.

  4. In Monte Carlo numerical integration with \(N\) independent paths, how does the standard error of the estimator scale with sample size \(N\)?

    Options

    By M8, the Central Limit Theorem dictates that the sample variance scales as \(\sigma^2 / N\), producing standard error \(\operatorname{SE} = \sigma / \sqrt{N} = O(N^{-1/2})\) regardless of dimension.

  5. How does the antithetic variates variance reduction technique reduce estimator variance in geometric Brownian motion simulation?

    Options

    By M8, the variance of \(\frac{1}{2}(f(Z) + f(-Z))\) equals \(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}(f(Z)) + \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Cov}(f(Z), f(-Z))\). Because option payoffs are monotonic in \(Z\), the covariance is strictly negative.

  6. In modern C++ systems engineering, what is the primary computational benefit of adhering to RAII (Resource Acquisition Is Initialization)?

    Options

    By CS5, RAII guarantees that heap allocations, file handles, and threading locks are released predictably at scope exit, avoiding memory leaks and latency jitter.

  7. What does the Feynman–Kac theorem establish for the linear parabolic partial differential equation \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \mu(t, x) \frac{\partial V}{\partial x} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(t, x)\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} - r(t, x)V = 0\)?

    Options

    By SC4, Feynman–Kac proves the rigorous equivalence between classical solutions of parabolic PDEs and discounted conditional expectations of diffusions.

  8. Under the Cox–Ross–Rubinstein binomial model, what is the product of the upward factor \(u = e^{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}\) and downward factor \(d\): \(u \cdot d\)?

    In the standard recombining CRR tree, \(d = 1 / u = e^{-\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}\), ensuring \(u \cdot d = 1.0\).

  9. Skill check (rung 1): In a 3-branch recombining trinomial lattice matching the first two moments of log-returns, what spatial log-mesh spacing \(\Delta x\) eliminates spurious oscillations and guarantees positive middle branch probability \(p_m\)?

    Options

    Following Boyle (1977) and Hull–White (1990), choosing \(\Delta x = \sigma \sqrt{3 \Delta t}\) sets the baseline central probability \(p_m \approx 2/3\) and matches fourth-order kurtosis moments while guaranteeing numerical stability.

  10. Skill check (rung 2): In implicit finite difference PDE solvers, how does the Thomas algorithm solve a tridiagonal linear system \(A x = d\) of dimension \(M\)?

    Options

    The Thomas algorithm specializes LU decomposition to tridiagonal matrices, executing in exactly \(O(M)\) operations without storing non-zero off-band matrix fill-in.
