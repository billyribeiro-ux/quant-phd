Exotics, Structured Products & Dealer Hedging

When derivative contracts depart from terminal European vanilla payoffs, their path-dependent cash flows generate complex higher-order Greeks, model-dependent valuation discrepancies, and non-linear dealer hedging requirements. This doctoral module investigates the pricing, risk management, and market impact of exotic derivatives and structured retail products. Students derive closed-form symmetries for barrier and lookback options, implement variance-reduced Monte Carlo schemes for Asian options, price multi-period autocallable Phoenix notes under Dupire local volatility surfaces, dissect dealer gamma exposure (GEX) profiles, examine expiration strike pinning mechanics, and quantify the endogenous market footprint of systematic delta-hedging flows near the market close.

  • Prerequisites: DV2 The Volatility Surface, DV4 Numerical Pricing (C++). Prerequisite closure includes DV2, DV4, DV1, FC2, M4, M5, and SC1–SC3.
  • Unlocks: Doctoral Capstone Studios, Quantitative Portfolio Management, and Professional Exotic Desk Trading.
  • Project: Autocallable pricer and risk report: price a Phoenix autocallable note by Monte Carlo under Dupire local volatility skew, compute its Greeks, map the dealer's negative gamma exposure cliff across spot levels, and author an institutional risk memorandum. Graded by both autograder and rubric.
  • Data: Simulated option surfaces, exotic asset paths, and high-frequency order flow data labeled SIMULATED.

45 h lessons · 25 h project (35.7% of the 70 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV6 you can:

  1. Derive closed-form solutions and analytical reflection symmetries for single and double barrier options, and quantify the Broadie, Glasserman & Kou (1997) discrete monitoring continuity correction (Lesson 1).
  2. Price arithmetic and geometric Asian options, implement the Kemna & Vorst (1990) control variate achieving >99% simulation variance reduction, and value Goldman, Sosin & Gatto (1979) floating-strike lookbacks (Lesson 2).
  3. Deconstruct the architecture of structured retail products (autocallables, Phoenix notes, reverse convertibles), and evaluate how local volatility skew shifts barrier breach probabilities and expected product durations (Lesson 3).
  4. Formulate aggregate Dealer Gamma Exposure (\(\mathrm{GEX}\)), map the sharp sign inversion from positive to negative gamma near structured product knock-in barriers, and replicate the Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005) expiration strike pinning dynamics (Lesson 4).
  5. Model the market footprint of systematic option selling and delta rebalancing flows, and reproduce the Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021) intraday momentum effect near the market close (Lesson 5).
  6. Engineer a production-grade Monte Carlo pricer for multi-period autocallable notes under parametric Dupire local volatility, compute finite-difference Greeks with common random numbers, and defend an institutional hedging risk report against an adversarial risk committee (Project).

Placement Diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite derivatives mastery and advanced exotic pricing concepts. Questions 1 through 8 test prerequisites from DV2, DV4, DV1, and stochastic calculus. Questions 9 and 10 are skill checks testing local volatility evaluation and autocallable payoff mechanics taught in project rungs 1 and 2. Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold (at least 8 correct out of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Breeden–Litzenberger (1978) theorem, how is the risk-neutral probability density \(q(K)\) extracted from European call prices?

    Options

    Differentiating the discounted risk-neutral expectation \(C(K) = e^{-r T} \int_K^\infty (S_T - K) q(S_T)\,dS_T\) twice with respect to strike \(K\) yields \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} = e^{-r T} q(K)\), so \(q(K) = e^{r T} \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\).

  2. What is the Dupire (1994) equation expressing local variance \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(K, T)\) in terms of market European call option prices?

    Options

    The Dupire forward PDE relates the maturity derivative \(\partial C / \partial T\) to the strike derivative and strike convexity \(\partial^2 C / \partial K^2\), uniquely determining the continuous local variance function consistent with the market volatility surface.

  3. Under the reflection principle for standard Brownian motion \(W_t\), what is the probability that the running maximum \(M_T = \max_{0 \le t \le T} W_t\) exceeds level \(m > 0\)?

    Options

    By the strong Markov property and reflection at the stopping time \(\tau_m = \inf\{t : W_t = m\}\), paths that reach \(m\) are equally likely to finish above \(m\) or below \(m\), yielding \(\mathbb{P}(M_T \ge m) = 2 \mathbb{P}(W_T \ge m)\).

  4. What is the discrete hedging P&L identity relating an option's gamma, theta, and realized underlying price return over period \(\Delta t\)?

    Options

    From the Black–Scholes–Merton PDE, \(\Theta = -\frac{1}{2} \sigma_{\text{impl}}^2 S_t^2 \Gamma + r(V - \Delta S_t)\). Combining this with Itô's Taylor expansion shows that delta-hedged P&L earns the spread between realized variance and implied variance weighted by dollar gamma.

  5. Why is Common Random Numbers (CRN) essential when computing finite-difference Greeks in Monte Carlo simulation?

    Options

    Without CRN, the variance of \((V(S+h) - V(S))/h\) is \(\mathcal{O}(1/h^2)\) as \(h \to 0\). Synchronizing random numbers makes the paths highly correlated, reducing the finite-difference variance by orders of magnitude.

  6. In-out barrier option parity establishes that:

    Options

    On every sample path \(\omega\), the barrier is either hit or not hit. If hit, the knock-in option pays the vanilla payoff and the knock-out pays 0. If not hit, the knock-out pays the vanilla payoff and the knock-in pays 0. Thus \(V_{\text{in}} + V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{vanilla}}\) pathwise.

  7. Why does an arithmetic Asian option have lower variance than the terminal spot price under geometric Brownian motion?

    Options

    The integral or sum of positive stock prices over \([0, T]\) averages out high-frequency Brownian fluctuations, producing a terminal distribution with substantially lower variance than \(S_T\).

  8. What is the cross Greek 'vanna' (\(\partial^2 V / \partial S \partial \sigma\)) and how does it affect dealer delta hedging?

    Options

    By Schwarz's theorem, \(\partial (\partial V / \partial S) / \partial \sigma = \partial (\partial V / \partial \sigma) / \partial S\). When volatility shifts (e.g. during market selloffs), vanna forces dealers to adjust their delta hedge even before the underlying price moves.

  9. In a multi-period Phoenix autocallable note with a memory coupon feature, what happens if spot breaches the coupon barrier on date 1 but recovers on date 2?

    Options

    The memory feature stores unpaid coupons from prior observation periods when the underlying was below the barrier; upon recovery above the barrier, the full accumulated backlog of coupons is paid out.

  10. Why does dealer gamma exposure (\(\mathrm{GEX}\)) flip from positive to sharply negative as underlying spot falls towards an autocallable knock-in barrier?

    Options

    The autocallable note embeds a short knock-in put position for the client. When spot is well above the barrier, the put has low delta. Near the barrier, knock-in risk causes delta to plunge from 0 to 1/S0, creating huge client gamma. Because the dealer is short the note, the dealer inherits violent negative gamma.

Lessons

  1. L01 Path-Dependent Options: Barriers, Rebates & Analytical Symmetries 9 hours

    Derivation of closed-form barrier options under Black–Scholes via the reflection principle, Merton (1973) down-and-out call, in-out parity, analytical put-call symmetries, Broadie, Glasserman & Kou (1997) discrete monitoring continuity correction, and barrier pin risk.

  2. L02 Averaging & Extremum Payoffs: Asians & Lookbacks 9 hours

    Arithmetic vs. geometric Asian options, Kemna & Vorst (1990) closed-form geometric Asian control variates for high-precision Monte Carlo, Goldman, Sosin & Gatto (1979) floating-strike lookbacks, running extremum distributions, and volatility dampening.

  3. L03 Structured Products: Autocallables, Phoenix Notes & Knock-Out Boundaries 9 hours

    Architecture of structured notes (autocallables, Phoenix memory coupons, reverse convertibles), payoff decomposition into zero-coupon bond, digital coupon strip, and short down-and-in put, and local volatility pricing under downside skew (Gatheral 2006).

  4. L04 Dealer Gamma Positioning, Vanna/Volga & Pinning Mechanics 9 hours

    Market maker positioning aggregation, Dealer Gamma Exposure (\(\mathrm{GEX}\)) curves, the negative gamma cliff near structured product knock-in barriers, vanna/volga cross Greeks, and Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005) option expiration strike pinning.

  5. L05 Hedging Flow Footprint & Systematic Option Selling Dynamics 9 hours

    Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021) market intraday momentum driven by mechanical dealer delta-hedging flows near the close, systematic option selling cascades, 0DTE option footprints, and Whalley & Wilmott (1997) rebalancing under transaction costs.
