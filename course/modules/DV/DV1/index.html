Continuous-Time Arbitrage Pricing

Arbitrage pricing in continuous time establishes that any sufficiently smooth contingent claim can be replicated dynamically by continuous trading in the underlying asset and a risk-free cash account. This module develops the analytical and operational foundation of derivative pricing: formulating the continuous-time self-financing condition, deriving the Black–Scholes–Merton partial differential equation by eliminating diffusion risk, computing the full spectrum of option Greeks, simulating dynamic delta-hedged portfolios, characterizing the asymptotic distribution of discrete rebalancing error, and proving the fundamental gamma–theta P&L identity linking option trading returns directly to realized versus implied volatility.

  • Prerequisites: FC2 Derivatives Foundations and SC3 Change of Measure. Prerequisite closure includes FC2, SC3, SC2, SC1, FC1, M6, M5, M4, M2, M1, E1, CF1, AC1, and P0.1.
  • Unlocks: DV2 The Volatility Surface, DV3 Stochastic Volatility, DV4 Numerical Pricing, and Capstone Studios.
  • Project: Hedging-error study: Measuring tracking error versus rebalancing frequency and transaction costs, verifying theoretical \(O(N^{-1/2})\) error scaling. Graded by both autograder and rubric.
  • Data: Simulated continuous-time price paths under geometric Brownian motion labeled SIMULATED.

80 h lessons · 20 h project (20% of the 100 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV1 you can:

  1. Formulate the continuous-time self-financing portfolio condition in Itô calculus, and prove the dynamic replication theorem for European contingent claims (Lesson 1).
  2. Derive the Black–Scholes–Merton partial differential equation by constructing an instantaneously risk-free hedged portfolio that eliminates Brownian increments (Lesson 2).
  3. Solve the BSM PDE via heat equation transformation and risk-neutral expectation under \(\mathbb{Q}\), and verify analytical put–call parity (Lesson 3).
  4. Calculate analytical first- and second-order option Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, Rho), and prove the algebraic identity connecting Gamma and Vega (Lesson 4).
  5. Implement an operational discrete delta-hedging engine, tracking stock inventory, borrowing costs, and terminal replication discrepancies (Lesson 5).
  6. Quantify the asymptotic distribution of discrete-rebalancing hedging error, verifying the theoretical \(O(N^{-1/2})\) root-mean-square error scaling (Lesson 6).
  7. Prove the hedging P&L identity decomposing hedged option returns into gamma, theta, and realized variance spreads (Lesson 7).
  8. Incorporate proportional transaction costs using Leland's modified volatility framework, and determine the optimal rebalancing frequency trading off tracking error and execution friction (Lesson 8).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite knowledge and core project skills. Questions 1 through 8 test prerequisites from FC2, SC3, SC2, and SC1. Questions 9 and 10 are skill checks testing discrete delta-rebalancing mechanics and transaction-cost scaling taught in project rungs 1 and 2. Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold (at least 8 correct out of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under Itô's formula for \(f(t, S_t)\) where \(\mathrm{d}S_t = \mu S_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma S_t \mathrm{d}W_t\), what is the diffusion term of \(\mathrm{d}f\)?

    Options

    By SC2 (Itô's lemma), the first-order differential is \(\frac{\partial f}{\partial S}\mathrm{d}S_t\). The Brownian increment comes entirely from \(\mathrm{d}S_t = \mu S_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma S_t \mathrm{d}W_t\), giving \(\sigma S_t \frac{\partial f}{\partial S} \mathrm{d}W_t\).

  2. What eliminates the Brownian noise \(\mathrm{d}W_t\) in the Black–Scholes–Merton portfolio \(\Pi_t = V_t - \Delta_t S_t\)?

    Options

    By choosing \(\Delta_t = \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\), the diffusion increment \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} \sigma S \mathrm{d}W_t\) is exactly cancelled by \(-\Delta_t \sigma S \mathrm{d}W_t\), making the hedged portfolio instantaneously risk-free.

  3. Under standard put–call parity for non-dividend paying stock, what is the difference between call delta and put delta: \(\Delta_{\mathrm{call}} - \Delta_{\mathrm{put}}\)?

    By differentiating put–call parity \(C(S) - P(S) = S - K e^{-r(T-t)}\) with respect to \(S\), we obtain \(\frac{\partial C}{\partial S} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial S} = 1\), so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{call}} - \Delta_{\mathrm{put}} = 1\).

  4. Under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), what is the drift of a non-dividend paying stock price \(S_t\)?

    Options

    Under \(\mathbb{Q}\), the discounted stock price \(e^{-rt} S_t\) is a martingale, which implies \(\mathrm{d}S_t = r S_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma S_t \mathrm{d}W_t^\mathbb{Q}\). The undiscounted drift is \(r S_t\).

  5. For a European call option at maturity (\(t = T\)), what is the option gamma \(\Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2}\) when the stock price is out-of-the-money (\(S_T < K\))?

    For \(S_T < K\), the payoff is flat at 0, so the first derivative \(\Delta = 0\) and the second derivative \(\Gamma = 0\).

  6. By Girsanov's theorem (SC3), what happens to the volatility \(\sigma\) when transitioning from the physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) to the equivalent martingale measure \(\mathbb{Q}\)?

    Options

    Equivalent probability measures share the exact same null sets; therefore pathwise quadratic variation \([S, S]_t = \int_0^t \sigma^2 S_u^2 \mathrm{d}u\) is identical under \(\mathbb{P}\) and \(\mathbb{Q}\).

  7. What does the martingale representation theorem (SC3) establish for a complete Brownian filtration \(\mathbb{F}^W\)?

    Options

    The martingale representation theorem guarantees that any \(\mathbb{Q}\)-martingale can be integrated against the Brownian motion, enabling exact self-financing replication of any contingent claim.

  8. What is the expected cumulative terminal P&L of a continuous, frictionless delta-hedged option book priced at the true volatility?

    In frictionless continuous time, delta hedging provides an exact replication of the option payoff, so the net tracking error is zero.

  9. Skill check (rung 1): In discrete delta-hedging with \(N\) rebalancing intervals \(\Delta t = T/N\), how does the standard deviation of terminal hedging error \(\operatorname{Std}(\epsilon_N)\) scale asymptotically as \(N \to \infty\)?

    Options

    By Boyle & Emanuel (1980), discrete hedging errors accumulate as independent increments of order \(\Delta t\), whose standard deviation scales as \(\sqrt{N \cdot (\Delta t)^2} = \sqrt{N \cdot (T/N)^2} \propto N^{-1/2}\).

  10. Skill check (rung 2): Under Leland's (1985) transaction-cost framework with proportional round-trip cost \(k = 2c\), what is the modified volatility \(\hat{\sigma}^2\) that compensates for expected hedging costs?

    Options

    Leland (1985) proved that expected transaction costs scale as \(\sqrt{2/\pi} \frac{k}{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}\) times gamma, requiring an upward adjustment of variance rate by \(\sqrt{2/\pi} \frac{2c \sigma}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\).
