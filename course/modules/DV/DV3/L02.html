The SABR Model and Asymptotic Expansions

In fixed income and foreign exchange derivatives markets, options are quoted across strikes using Hagan et al.'s SABR model. By coupling a Constant Elasticity of Variance backbone with lognormal volatility dynamics, SABR generates realistic smiles and skews without requiring numerical PDE solves. However, its celebrated closed-form implied volatility formula is an asymptotic approximation that produces severe arbitrage violations—including negative probability densities—in the deep wings under high volatility of volatility.

  • Prerequisites: volatility smile, risk-neutral density, butterfly arbitrage, Heston model

1. The SABR SDE System and the CEV Backbone

The SABR (Stochastic Alpha, Beta, Rho) model describes the joint continuous-time evolution of a forward price \(F_t\) and its stochastic volatility state variable \(\sigma_t\) under the forward measure \(\mathbb{Q}^T\):

\[ \begin{aligned} dF_t &= \sigma_t F_t^\beta\,dW_t^1 \\ d\sigma_t &= \nu \sigma_t\,dW_t^2 \\ d\langle W^1, W^2\rangle_t &= \rho\,dt \end{aligned} \]

with initial conditions \(F_0 > 0\) and \(\sigma_0 = \alpha > 0\). The four structural parameters are:

  • \(\alpha > 0\): Initial volatility level.
  • \(\beta \in [0, 1]\): Constant elasticity of variance (CEV) exponent. When \(\beta = 1\), the model is lognormal; when \(\beta = 0\), the forward follows a normal (Bachelier) diffusion.
  • \(\nu \ge 0\): Volatility of volatility ('vol-of-vol').
  • \(\rho \in [-1, 1]\): Correlation between asset forward returns and volatility innovations.

Definition 2.1 (SABR Model). The SABR model is a two-dimensional stochastic volatility diffusion for forward contracts where the local volatility backbone is power-law \(F_t^\beta\) and volatility follows a geometric Brownian motion correlated with the forward price.

The CEV backbone \(F^\beta\) determines the relationship between price movements and volatility changes (the backbone skew). For interest rate options (swaptions and caps), \(\beta\) is typically fixed by market convention (e.g., \(\beta = 0.5\) for CIR-like dynamics or \(\beta = 0\) in negative-rate environments), while \((\alpha, \rho, \nu)\) are calibrated to cross-sectional smile quotes.

2. The Hagan et al. Perturbation Expansion

Because the SABR system has no exact closed-form transition density, Hagan et al. (2002) applied singular perturbation techniques and hyperbolic heat kernel expansions to derive an asymptotic formula for Black equivalent implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(K, F)\).

Theorem 2.1 (Hagan et al. Asymptotic Implied Volatility). Let \(\tau = T - t\) be the time to maturity. For small \(\nu^2 \tau \ll 1\), the Black–76 implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(K, F)\) of a European option with strike \(K\) and forward \(F\) satisfies the asymptotic expansion:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(K, F) \approx \frac{\alpha}{(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}} \left[1 + \frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\ln^2\left(\frac{F}{K}\right) + \frac{(1-\beta)^4}{1920}\ln^4\left(\frac{F}{K}\right)\right]} \cdot \frac{z}{\chi(z)} \cdot \Phi(\tau) \]

where \(z = \frac{\nu}{\alpha}(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\ln\left(\frac{F}{K}\right)\), the hyperbolic transformation is:

\[ \chi(z) = \ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1 - 2\rho z + z^2} + z - \rho}{1 - \rho}\right) \]

and the tenor correction factor is:

\[ \Phi(\tau) = 1 + \left[\frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\frac{\alpha^2}{(FK)^{1-\beta}} + \frac{\rho\beta\nu\alpha}{4(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}} + \frac{2 - 3\rho^2}{24}\nu^2\right]\tau \]

In the at-the-money limit \(K = F\), the logarithmic moneyness vanishes (\(z \to 0\), \(\frac{z}{\chi(z)} \to 1\)), yielding the exact ATM implied volatility:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(F, F) \approx \frac{\alpha}{F^{1-\beta}} \left[1 + \left(\frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\frac{\alpha^2}{F^{2(1-\beta)}} + \frac{\rho\beta\nu\alpha}{4 F^{1-\beta}} + \frac{2 - 3\rho^2}{24}\nu^2\right)\tau\right] \]

3. At-The-Money Implied Volatility Skew

Differentiating the Hagan formula with respect to log-moneyness \(k = \ln(K/F)\) at \(k = 0\) decomposes the ATMI skew into two distinct economic mechanisms:

\[ \left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0} \approx \frac{\beta - 1}{2}\frac{\alpha}{F^{1-\beta}} + \frac{1}{2}\rho\nu \]

The first term represents the CEV backbone effect: when \(\beta < 1\), higher prices reduce volatility, creating negative skew even with \(\rho = 0\). The second term represents the pure stochastic volatility leverage effect: negative correlation \(\rho < 0\) between spot returns and volatility innovations generates downward sloping skew.

"""Lab 2 · SABR Implied Volatility Expansion & Arbitrage Pathologies.

This lab implements the Hagan et al. asymptotic implied volatility formula for the
SABR model, evaluates the implied smile across moneyness, computes the risk-neutral
density via the Breeden-Litzenberger identity, and detects butterfly arbitrage
(negative probability density) in the deep left wing under high vol-of-vol stress on seed 0 (SIMULATED).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def sabr_implied_vol(
    f: float,
    k: float,
    t: float,
    alpha: float,
    beta: float,
    rho: float,
    nu: float,
) -> float:
    """Hagan et al. asymptotic formula for Black implied volatility."""
    if f <= 0.0 or k <= 0.0 or t <= 0.0:
        return 0.0

    eps = 1e-7
    if abs(f - k) < eps:
        fk_mid = f ** (1.0 - beta)
        term1 = (1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0 * (alpha * alpha) / (fk_mid * fk_mid)
        term2 = 0.25 * rho * beta * nu * alpha / fk_mid
        term3 = (2.0 - 3.0 * rho * rho) / 24.0 * nu * nu
        val = (alpha / fk_mid) * (1.0 + (term1 + term2 + term3) * t)
        return float(val)

    log_fk = math.log(f / k)
    fk = f * k
    fk_beta = fk ** ((1.0 - beta) / 2.0)

    denom = fk_beta * (1.0 + ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (log_fk**2) + ((1.0 - beta) ** 4 / 1920.0) * (log_fk**4))

    z = (nu / alpha) * fk_beta * log_fk
    arg = math.sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - 2.0 * rho * z + z * z)) + z - rho
    chi_z = z if arg <= 0.0 or abs(1.0 - rho) < 1e-12 else math.log(arg / (1.0 - rho))
    z_over_chi = 1.0 if abs(z) < 1e-6 else z / chi_z

    factor = (
        1.0
        + (
            ((1.0 - beta) ** 2 / 24.0) * (alpha * alpha) / (fk ** (1.0 - beta))
            + 0.25 * (rho * beta * nu * alpha) / fk_beta
            + ((2.0 - 3.0 * rho * rho) / 24.0) * (nu * nu)
        )
        * t
    )

    ans = (alpha / denom) * z_over_chi * factor
    return float(ans)


def black76_call_price(f: float, k: float, t: float, r: float, sigma: float) -> float:
    """Black-76 call price on forward."""
    if t <= 0.0 or sigma <= 0.0:
        return math.exp(-r * t) * max(0.0, f - k)
    std = sigma * math.sqrt(t)
    d1 = (math.log(f / k) + 0.5 * std * std) / std
    d2 = d1 - std

    def n_cdf(x: float) -> float:
        return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))

    disc = math.exp(-r * t)
    return disc * (f * n_cdf(d1) - k * n_cdf(d2))


def breeden_litzenberger_density(
    f: float,
    k: float,
    t: float,
    r: float,
    alpha: float,
    beta: float,
    rho: float,
    nu: float,
    dk: float = 0.5,
) -> float:
    """Compute risk-neutral state-price density q(K) = exp(r*T) * d^2 C / dK^2."""
    vol_c = sabr_implied_vol(f, k, t, alpha, beta, rho, nu)
    vol_up = sabr_implied_vol(f, k + dk, t, alpha, beta, rho, nu)
    vol_dn = sabr_implied_vol(f, k - dk, t, alpha, beta, rho, nu)

    c_center = black76_call_price(f, k, t, r, vol_c)
    c_up = black76_call_price(f, k + dk, t, r, vol_up)
    c_dn = black76_call_price(f, k - dk, t, r, vol_dn)

    second_deriv = (c_up - 2.0 * c_center + c_dn) / (dk * dk)
    return math.exp(r * t) * second_deriv


def main() -> None:
    print("=== SABR Model Asymptotics & Density Analysis ===")
    f0 = 100.0
    r = 0.02

    # Case A: Well-behaved baseline (T=1.0, beta=0.5, nu=0.40)
    alpha_base = 2.0
    beta_base = 0.50
    rho_base = -0.30
    nu_base = 0.40
    t_base = 1.0

    # Case B: Stressed long-maturity regime (T=3.0, beta=0.2, nu=1.20, rho=-0.60)
    alpha_stress = 2.0
    beta_stress = 0.20
    rho_stress = -0.60
    nu_stress = 1.20
    t_stress = 3.0

    print(f"Baseline: F0={f0}, T={t_base}, alpha={alpha_base}, beta={beta_base}, rho={rho_base}, nu={nu_base}")
    print(
        f"Stressed: F0={f0}, T={t_stress}, alpha={alpha_stress}, beta={beta_stress}, rho={rho_stress}, nu={nu_stress}"
    )

    strikes = [15.0, 30.0, 50.0, 75.0, 100.0, 125.0, 150.0]

    print("\nImplied Volatility Smile & Density Comparison (SIMULATED):")
    print(" Strike | IV (Base) | Density (Base) | IV (Stress) | Density (Stress) ")
    print("-------------------------------------------------------------------")
    negative_density_found = False
    for k in strikes:
        iv_b = sabr_implied_vol(f0, k, t_base, alpha_base, beta_base, rho_base, nu_base)
        dens_b = breeden_litzenberger_density(f0, k, t_base, r, alpha_base, beta_base, rho_base, nu_base)
        iv_s = sabr_implied_vol(f0, k, t_stress, alpha_stress, beta_stress, rho_stress, nu_stress)
        dens_s = breeden_litzenberger_density(f0, k, t_stress, r, alpha_stress, beta_stress, rho_stress, nu_stress)
        if dens_s < 0.0:
            negative_density_found = True
        print(f" {k:6.1f} |   {iv_b:7.4f} |        {dens_b:7.5f} |     {iv_s:7.4f} |          {dens_s:7.5f} ")

    print(f"\nButterfly Arbitrage Detected Under Stress (Density < 0): {negative_density_found}")
    print("Interpretation: Under large vol-of-vol nu*sqrt(T), the Hagan expansion violates")
    print("no-arbitrage in the deep wing, proving the need for arbitrage-free SABR formulations.")


main()

Under moderate vol-of-vol, the Hagan formula generates a smooth implied volatility smile with strictly positive risk-neutral density. Under high vol-of-vol stress (\(\nu = 1.20\), \(T = 3.0\), \(\beta = 0.20\)), the second derivative of call prices becomes negative in the left wing (\(K \le 50\)), proving the presence of butterfly arbitrage in the asymptotic expansion.

Where the canon breaks

The primary breakdown of the classical SABR model lies in the arbitrage pathologies of the Hagan asymptotic expansion. The formula is derived from a leading-order perturbation expansion assuming \(\nu^2 \tau \ll 1\). In practice, when applied to long maturities (\(\tau > 2\) years), high vol-of-vol (\(\nu > 0.80\)), or low strikes (\(K \to 0\) when \(\beta < 1\)), the formula breaks down severely:

  • Butterfly Arbitrage: The implied call price curve \(C(K)\) becomes concave (\(\partial^2 C / \partial K^2 < 0\)), yielding negative state-price densities. An option trader can construct butterfly spreads with negative cost and non-negative payoff, creating arbitrage.
  • Zero-Strike Boundary Absorption: For \(\beta < 1\), the boundary \(F = 0\) is attainable. Hagan's expansion does not properly enforce absorbing or reflecting boundary conditions at zero, leading to probability leaks where \(\int_0^\infty q(K)\,dK < 1\).
  • Diffusive Short-Time Skew: Like Heston, SABR variance is driven by Brownian motion. Consequently, the short-dated ATMI skew remains bounded (\(O(1)\)) as \(T \to 0\), failing to capture the market's explosive power-law skew without setting \(\nu \to \infty\).

Misconceptions

  • The Hagan et al. formula is an exact closed-form analytical solution of the SABR SDE.

    The Hagan formula is a leading-order singular perturbation expansion in powers of \(\nu^2 \tau\). The true SABR pricing problem requires solving a two-dimensional PDE without closed-form solution. For large \(\nu^2 \tau\), the expansion diverges from true SABR option prices, producing negative density wings.

  • Setting \(\beta = 0\) eliminates probability mass at zero and prevents negative interest rates.

    Setting \(\beta = 0\) creates normal (Bachelier) dynamics \(dF_t = \sigma_t\,dW_t\). Under normal dynamics, forward rates are Gaussian and can freely become negative, which is why normal SABR became the market standard after 2014 when EUR and JPY interest rates went negative.

  • A single set of SABR parameters \((\alpha, \beta, \rho, \nu)\) can fit an entire multi-tenor volatility surface.

    In market practice, SABR is an interpolation tool calibrated independently slice-by-slice across each expiration tenor. Calibrating across maturities simultaneously fails because SABR has no term-structure mean reversion mechanism for volatility, forcing desks to re-calibrate parameters for every maturity.

Problem set

Mechanics

In a lognormal SABR model (\(\beta = 1.0\)), parameters are \(\alpha = 0.20\), \(\rho = -0.30\), and \(\nu = 0.40\). Calculate the at-the-money implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(F, F)\) for maturity \(\tau = 1.0\) year.

Worked solution

For \(\beta = 1.0\), \(F^{1-\beta} = F^0 = 1\). The ATM formula simplifies to:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(F, F) = \alpha \left[1 + \left(\frac{1}{4}\rho\nu\alpha + \frac{2 - 3\rho^2}{24}\nu^2\right)\tau\right] \]

Compute the inner terms:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1}{4}\rho\nu\alpha &= \frac{1}{4}(-0.30)(0.40)(0.20) = -0.0060 \\ \frac{2 - 3(-0.30)^2}{24}(0.40)^2 &= \frac{2 - 0.27}{24} \times 0.16 = \frac{1.73}{24} \times 0.16 = 0.011533 \end{aligned} \]

Summing the correction: \(-0.0060 + 0.011533 = 0.005533\). Then:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{B}} = 0.20 \times (1 + 0.005533) = 0.201107 \]

The ATM implied volatility is \(20.11\%\).

Evaluate the leading-order ATMI skew \(\left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0}\) for forward \(F = 100\), \(\beta = 0.50\), \(\alpha = 2.0\), \(\rho = -0.40\), and \(\nu = 0.50\).

Worked solution

Apply the SABR ATMI skew decomposition:

\[ \left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0} \approx \frac{\beta - 1}{2}\frac{\alpha}{F^{1-\beta}} + \frac{1}{2}\rho\nu \]

Substitute the parameters with \(F^{1-\beta} = 100^{0.5} = 10\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Backbone skew} &= \frac{0.50 - 1}{2} \times \frac{2.0}{10} = -0.25 \times 0.20 = -0.050 \\ \text{Leverage skew} &= \frac{1}{2}(-0.40)(0.50) = -0.100 \\ \text{Total ATMI skew} &= -0.050 + (-0.100) = -0.150 \end{aligned} \]

The total ATMI skew is \(-0.150\).

For forward \(F = 100\) and strike \(K = 80\), compute the hyperbolic parameter \(z\) in the Hagan formula when \(\alpha = 2.0\), \(\beta = 0.5\), and \(\nu = 0.40\).

Worked solution

Compute \(FK = 100 \times 80 = 8000\), so \((FK)^{(1-\beta)/2} = 8000^{0.25} \approx 9.4574\). Log-moneyness is \(\ln(F/K) = \ln(100/80) \approx 0.22314\). Then:

\[ z = \frac{\nu}{\alpha}(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\ln\left(\frac{F}{K}\right) = \frac{0.40}{2.0} \times 9.4574 \times 0.22314 \approx 0.20 \times 2.1103 \approx 0.4221 \]

The parameter \(z\) is \(0.4221\).

Proof

Prove that when vol-of-vol \(\nu \to 0\), the SABR model degenerates to the Constant Elasticity of Variance (CEV) model.

Worked solution

In the SABR SDE system, \(d\sigma_t = \nu \sigma_t\,dW_t^2\). As \(\nu \to 0\), the diffusion term vanishes, so \(d\sigma_t \equiv 0\), implying \(\sigma_t = \sigma_0 = \alpha\) for all \(t \ge 0\) almost surely. Substituting this constant into the forward price SDE yields:

\[ dF_t = \alpha F_t^\beta\,dW_t^1 \]

which is the classical local volatility Constant Elasticity of Variance diffusion of Cox (1975). Examining the Hagan formula as \(\nu \to 0\): \(z \to 0\), so \(\frac{z}{\chi(z)} \to 1\), and all terms with \(\nu\) in \(\Phi(\tau)\) vanish, recovering the leading-order CEV implied volatility expansion. \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that for \(\beta < 1\), the origin \(F = 0\) is an attainable boundary for the forward process under the SABR model if no boundary condition is imposed.

Worked solution

Condition on a frozen volatility path \(\sigma_t = \alpha\). The scale function \(p(x)\) of \(dF_t = \alpha F_t^\beta\,dW_t\) is linear: \(p(x) = x\), because the drift is zero. The speed measure density is \(m'(x) = \frac{2}{\alpha^2 x^{2\beta}}\). The boundary behavior at \(x = 0\) is determined by the convergence of the integral:

\[ \int_0^\epsilon (x - 0) m'(x)\,dx = \frac{2}{\alpha^2}\int_0^\epsilon x^{1 - 2\beta}\,dx \]

The integrand \(x^{1 - 2\beta}\) is integrable near zero if and only if \(1 - 2\beta > -1\), which simplifies to \(2\beta < 2 \iff \beta < 1\). Thus, for all \(\beta < 1\), the boundary \(F = 0\) is accessible in finite time with positive probability, requiring an explicit specification of absorbing or reflecting boundary conditions to preserve martingale pricing. \(\blacksquare\)

Prove that the function \(\chi(z) = \ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1 - 2\rho z + z^2} + z - \rho}{1 - \rho}\right)\) satisfies \(\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z}{\chi(z)} = 1\) for all \(\rho \in (-1, 1)\).

Worked solution

Consider the Taylor expansion of \(f(z) = \sqrt{1 - 2\rho z + z^2} + z - \rho\) around \(z = 0\):

\[ \sqrt{1 - 2\rho z + z^2} = 1 - \rho z + \frac{1 - \rho^2}{2}z^2 + O(z^3) \]

Adding \(z - \rho\):

\[ f(z) = (1 - \rho) + (1 - \rho)z + \frac{1 - \rho^2}{2}z^2 + O(z^3) = (1 - \rho)\left[1 + z + \frac{1 + \rho}{2}z^2 + O(z^3)\right] \]

Dividing by \(1 - \rho\) and taking the logarithm:

\[ \chi(z) = \ln\left(1 + z + \frac{1+\rho}{2}z^2 + O(z^3)\right) = z + \frac{1+\rho}{2}z^2 - \frac{1}{2}z^2 + O(z^3) = z + \frac{\rho}{2}z^2 + O(z^3) \]

Therefore, as \(z \to 0\):

\[ \frac{z}{\chi(z)} = \frac{z}{z + \frac{\rho}{2}z^2 + O(z^3)} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\rho}{2}z + O(z^2)} \to 1 \]

establishing that the ratio is well-behaved and non-singular at the money. \(\blacksquare\)

Empirical

Why did interest rate options trading desks shift from lognormal SABR (\(\beta = 1\)) to normal SABR (\(\beta = 0\)) following the European sovereign debt crisis?

Worked solution

Under lognormal SABR (\(\beta = 1\)), the forward SDE is \(dF_t = \sigma_t F_t\,dW_t\), which strictly requires \(F_t > 0\). When EUR and CHF benchmark rates (Euribor, swap rates) turned negative after 2014, lognormal formulas broke down completely (\(\ln(F/K)\) is undefined for non-positive numbers). Desks transitioned to normal (Bachelier) SABR (\(\beta = 0\)), where \(dF_t = \sigma_t\,dW_t\), naturally accommodating negative swap rates. Swaptions and caps began quoting in normal basis point volatility (\(\sigma_N\), bp/year) rather than lognormal percentage volatility.

Describe how a swaptions desk calibrates SABR parameters \((\alpha, \rho, \nu)\) to market quotes for a fixed expiry and tenor, given an externally chosen \(\beta = 0.5\).

Worked solution

With \(\beta\) fixed at 0.5 by market convention, the desk calibrates the three remaining parameters to the market smile (typically quoted at ATM, \(\pm 50\), \(\pm 100\), and \(\pm 200\) basis points). First, the ATM quote pins \(\alpha\) via the analytical ATM inversion formula. Second, the slope of the smile (the risk reversal) determines the correlation \(\rho\). Third, the curvature (the butterfly spread) determines the vol-of-vol \(\nu\). The three parameters are fitted using non-linear least squares minimizing weighted sum of squared pricing errors.

Explain how arbitrage in the Hagan formula manifests in market pricing systems when pricing deep out-of-the-money put swaptions.

Worked solution

In deep out-of-the-money puts (\(K \ll F\)), the Hagan formula overestimates implied volatility in the left wing under large \(\nu\). When evaluating call prices \(C(K)\) across strikes, the curve becomes concave (\(\partial^2 C / \partial K^2 < 0\)), violating the Breeden–Litzenberger non-negativity requirement for risk-neutral densities. In automated trading engines, this manifests as negative digital option prices, butterfly arbitrage violations where an out-of-the-money butterfly spread trades at a negative premium, or Monte Carlo pricers sampling impossible negative weights.

Research

Evaluate the PDE and normal-integration methods developed to cure the Hagan negative density pathology (e.g., Ballotta & Sen (2014) or Doust (2012) No-Arbitrage SABR).

Worked solution

To eliminate the butterfly arbitrage inherent in the Hagan expansion, Doust (2012) and Ballotta & Sen (2014) solve the forward Kolmogorov PDE numerically on a dense spatial grid with absorbing boundary conditions at zero. Because the PDE directly governs the Fokker–Planck probability density, the computed state prices are guaranteed to be positive and arbitrage-free by construction. While PDE solving is computationally more expensive than evaluating Hagan's algebraic formula, modern ADI (alternating direction implicit) solvers compute the slice in under 2 milliseconds, making PDE-based SABR practical for live electronic risk management.

Discuss the 'Free Boundary SABR' framework of Lions & Musiela (2007) and how boundary conditions at \(F = 0\) impact the martingale property of the forward price.

Worked solution

Lions & Musiela (2007) proved that for CEV exponent \(\beta < 1\), the forward price process \(F_t\) can hit the origin \(F=0\). If zero is treated as an absorbing boundary, probability mass accumulates at zero: \(\mathbb{P}(F_T = 0) > 0\). If zero is reflecting, the process bounces back into \((0, \infty)\). Crucially, under absorption at zero, \(F_t\) is a true martingale, whereas under reflection, \(F_t\) develops a positive drift (submartingale), violating no-arbitrage. Thus, any arbitrage-free implementation of SABR with \(\beta < 1\) must enforce strict absorption at the origin.

Compare the smile dynamics of SABR versus Heston under forward price shocks: does the SABR smile move with the forward price (sticky moneyness) or stay fixed (sticky strike)?

Worked solution

Under a spot shock \(\Delta F\), the SABR model generates smile dynamics that depend heavily on \(\beta\). When \(\beta = 1\), the smile moves horizontally with the forward price, maintaining the same smile shape as a function of log-moneyness \(\ln(K/F)\)—a 'sticky moneyness' behavior. When \(\beta < 1\), the backbone causes the ATM volatility level to shift with price, moving the smile along the CEV curve. In contrast, standard Heston with fixed parameters displays a more rigid smile structure that behaves closer to 'sticky strike' over short horizons unless re-calibrated. Empirical market smiles typically lie between sticky strike and sticky moneyness.

  1. In the SABR SDE system \(dF_t = \sigma_t F_t^\beta\,dW_t^1\), what does the parameter \(\beta = 1\) correspond to?

    Options

    When \(\beta = 1\), \(F_t^\beta = F_t\), which yields geometric Brownian motion for the forward contract with stochastic percentage volatility.

  2. What are the two distinct economic drivers of the at-the-money implied volatility skew in the SABR model?

    Options

    The ATMI skew decomposes into \(\frac{\beta - 1}{2}\frac{\alpha}{F^{1-\beta}}\) (local volatility backbone) plus \(\frac{1}{2}\rho\nu\) (stochastic volatility correlation).

  3. Why does the Hagan et al. asymptotic formula fail in the deep left wing under high vol-of-vol \(\nu\)?

    Options

    The formula is a perturbation expansion around \(\nu^2 \tau = 0\). For large \(\nu^2 \tau\), higher-order terms dominate, making \(C(K)\) concave and producing negative state prices.

  4. What market condition led derivatives desks to standardize on Normal SABR (\(\beta = 0\)) rather than Lognormal SABR (\(\beta = 1\))?

    Options

    When interest rates went negative, lognormal models broke down because \(\ln(F/K)\) is undefined for non-positive numbers. Normal SABR handles negative forward rates naturally.

  5. Under the SABR model with \(\beta < 1\), what boundary condition must be enforced at \(F = 0\) to prevent arbitrage?

    Options

    If the origin is reflecting, the forward price becomes a submartingale (develops upward drift), creating arbitrage. Absorption at zero is required to preserve martingale pricing.

  6. In the limit where vol-of-vol \(\nu \to 0\), what celebrated option pricing model does SABR reduce to?

    Options

    When \(\nu = 0\), \(\sigma_t \equiv \alpha\) is constant, reducing the forward dynamics to \(dF_t = \alpha F_t^\beta\,dW_t\), the pure CEV local volatility diffusion.

  7. How does the parameter \(\nu\) in SABR affect the geometry of the volatility smile?

    Options

    \(\nu\) is the volatility of volatility. Higher \(\nu\) increases the variance of implied volatility, elevating the wings and deepening the smile curvature.

  8. What is the primary practical limitation of the SABR model when managing options across multiple maturities?

    Options

    Because volatility follows geometric Brownian motion without mean reversion, SABR cannot fit a full term structure with a single parameter set; each tenor slice requires independent calibration.

  9. What does the Breeden–Litzenberger identity calculate from a slice of European option prices?

    Options

    Differentiating call prices twice with respect to strike isolates the terminal risk-neutral state-price density: \(q(K) = e^{r T}\partial^2 C / \partial K^2\).

  10. How does the short-time ATMI skew in the SABR model behave as expiration \(T \to 0\)?

    Options

    Because the driving processes are standard diffusions, the short-time ATMI skew converges to the finite constant \(\frac{\beta - 1}{2}\frac{\alpha}{F^{1-\beta}} + \frac{1}{2}\rho\nu\).
