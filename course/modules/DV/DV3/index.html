Stochastic & Rough Volatility

Continuous-time option markets present two persistent empirical puzzles that standard diffusion models cannot resolve: the term structure of implied volatility skew blows up like \(T^{-\alpha}\) (\(\alpha \approx 0.4\)) as expiration approaches zero, and high-frequency realized volatility exhibits heavy clustering with rough sample paths. This doctoral module constructs the modern theory of volatility beyond the semimartingale paradigm. Beginning with the affine Heston model and Fourier inversion, we examine the asymptotic singular perturbation expansion of SABR, formulate Bergomi-type forward variance dynamics calibrated to variance swaps, and transition to fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter \(H < 1/2\). Students analyze the mathematical mechanics of rough volatility (rBergomi), develop noise-robust variogram estimators of \(H\), and rigorously engage with the central empirical debate: is volatility genuinely rough, or is roughness an artifact of microstructure noise and discrete jumps?

  • Prerequisites: DV2 The Volatility Surface, SC5 Jumps & Lévy Processes. Prerequisite closure includes DV2, SC5, DV1, SC3, SC2, SC1, FC2, FC1, M6, M5, M4, M2, M1, E1, CF1, AC1, and P0.1.
  • Unlocks: DV4 Numerical Pricing (C++), DV5 Variance Risk Premium & Volatility Trading, DV6 Exotics & Structured Products, and Doctoral Capstone Studios.
  • Project: Rough-vol estimation: Estimating the Hurst parameter of log realized variance on open data, with honest treatment of measurement error, and assessing the empirical evidence for rough volatility. Graded by both autograder and rubric.
  • Data: Simulated high-frequency realized variance series and option chains labeled SIMULATED.

60 h lessons · 30 h project (33% of the 90 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV3 you can:

  1. Derive the Heston (1993) log-price characteristic function by solving the 2D PDE via separation of variables, and implement the Carr-Madan Fourier inversion formula with the Albrecher et al. (2007) stable branch cut ('Little Heston Trap' fix) (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the SABR stochastic volatility model, derive the Hagan et al. leading-order perturbation expansion for implied volatility, and diagnose butterfly arbitrage violations and negative probability densities in the extreme wings (Lesson 2).
  3. Construct continuous forward-variance curves \(\xi_0(t)\) from discrete market variance swaps, and simulate multi-factor Bergomi forward-variance dynamics, proving that smooth diffusion kernels yield bounded short-dated ATMI skew limits (Lesson 3).
  4. Characterize fractional Brownian motion \(B_t^H\) with Hurst parameter \(H < 1/2\), prove that rough fractional paths are not semimartingales, and demonstrate that the rough Bergomi (rBergomi) model matches the power-law explosion of short-dated equity skew \(T^{H - 1/2}\) without price jumps (Lesson 4).
  5. Estimate the Hurst parameter \(H\) using \(q\)-th order structure functions and variograms on high-frequency realized volatility time series, and correct for microstructure measurement noise bias using lag truncation (Lesson 5).
  6. Evaluate and defend both sides of the doctoral debate: 'Is volatility rough?', assessing empirical evidence across hundreds of assets against alternative explanations including microstructure noise, discrete sampling, and jump activity (Lesson 5 & Project).

Prerequisites

This module builds directly upon the mathematical foundations established in DV2 The Volatility Surface and SC5 Jumps & Lévy Processes:

  • From DV2: Total implied variance \(w(k, \tau) = \sigma_{\text{BS}}^2 \tau\), static arbitrage bounds, Gatheral-Jacquier density factor \(g(k) \ge 0\), Dupire local volatility equation \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2 = \partial_\tau w / g(k)\), and Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density extraction \(q(K) = e^{r T} \partial^2 C / \partial K^2\).
  • From SC5: Infinite divisibility, Lévy-Khintchine representation, characteristic function pricing for jump-diffusions (Merton) and pure-jump processes (Variance Gamma, CGMY).
  • From SC1–SC3: Continuous-time martingales, Itô calculus in multiple dimensions, quadratic variation, Girsanov change of measure, and risk-neutral pricing under equivalent martingale measures.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite derivatives mastery and core stochastic volatility skills. Questions 1 through 8 test prerequisites from DV2, SC5, SC3, and DV1. Questions 9 and 10 are skill checks testing the Heston characteristic function stable branch and variogram structure function scaling taught in project rungs 1 and 2. Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold (at least 8 correct out of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under Breeden–Litzenberger (1978), how is the risk-neutral state-price density \(q(K)\) extracted from a continuum of European call prices?

    Options

    Differentiating the discounted risk-neutral expectation \(C(K) = e^{-r T} \int_K^\infty (S_T - K) q(S_T)\,dS_T\) twice with respect to strike \(K\) yields \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} = e^{-r T} q(K)\), so \(q(K) = e^{r T} \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\).

  2. In Gatheral's SVI parameterization, what condition guarantees the absence of butterfly (strike) arbitrage?

    Options

    By Gatheral & Jacquier (2014), the Breeden–Litzenberger density in total variance coordinates is strictly non-negative if and only if the density factor \(g(k) = (1 - \frac{k w'}{2w})^2 - \frac{(w')^2}{4}(\frac{1}{w} + \frac{1}{4}) + \frac{w''}{2} \ge 0\).

  3. Under Dupire's formula expressed in total variance \(w(k, \tau)\), what is the analytical expression for local variance \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(k, \tau)\)?

    Options

    Dupire's equation transforms into total variance coordinates as \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(k, \tau) = \frac{\partial_\tau w}{g(k, \tau)}\), showing that calendar arbitrage freedom (\(\partial_\tau w \ge 0\)) and butterfly arbitrage freedom (\(g(k) > 0\)) guarantee positive local variance.

  4. In Merton's (1976) jump-diffusion model, what is the economic source of the steep short-dated volatility smile?

    Options

    Merton (1976) introduces compound Poisson jumps into geometric Brownian motion. Because jump probabilities scale as \(O(\Delta t)\) rather than \(O(\sqrt{\Delta t})\), jumps dominate short-dated out-of-the-money option prices.

  5. Under the Lévy–Khintchine formula (SC5), what three components completely characterize any continuous-time Lévy process?

    Options

    By the Lévy–Khintchine theorem, every Lévy process is uniquely specified by its characteristic triplet \((\gamma, \sigma^2, \nu)\), describing linear drift, continuous diffusion, and jump arrival intensity.

  6. When applying Girsanov's theorem (SC3) to transform an SDE from physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) to risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), which parameter is strictly invariant?

    Options

    An equivalent change of measure alters only the drift by translating Brownian increments \(\mathrm{d}W_t^{\mathbb{P}} = \mathrm{d}W_t^{\mathbb{Q}} - \lambda_t \mathrm{d}t\). Pathwise quadratic variation and diffusion coefficients remain identical almost surely.

  7. What is the multidimensional Itô formula correction term for two correlated Brownian motions with \(\mathrm{d}\langle W^1, W^2\rangle_t = \rho\,\mathrm{d}t\)?

    Options

    By Itô's lemma in \(\R^2\), the cross-term in Taylor's expansion expands as \(\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2}\,\mathrm{d}X_1 \mathrm{d}X_2 = \rho \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2}\,\mathrm{d}t\).

  8. What does the pathwise Gamma–Theta identity (DV1 Lesson 7) prove about the P&L of a delta-hedged option?

    Options

    Delta hedging neutralizes directional delta risk. Instantaneous hedging P&L is driven by dollar gamma multiplied by the spread between realized and implied variance: \(\frac{1}{2} S_t^2 \Gamma_t (\sigma_R^2 - \sigma_I^2)\,\mathrm{d}t\).

  9. Skill check 1: In the Heston (1993) model \(dv_t = \kappa(\theta - v_t)\,dt + \xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^v\), what condition ensures that the variance process \(v_t\) stays strictly positive almost surely?

    Options

    By Cox, Ingersoll & Ross (1985), the origin is an inaccessible boundary for the square-root diffusion if and only if the inward drift dominates diffusion: \(2\kappa\theta \ge \xi^2\).

  10. Skill check 2: When realized variance contains additive measurement error \(\log \widehat{\mathrm{RV}}_t = \log \mathrm{RV}_t + \epsilon_t\) with \(\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_t) = \sigma_\epsilon^2\), what is the behavior of the sample variogram at lag \(\Delta = 1\)?

    Options

    Because \(\mathbb{E}[(\epsilon_{t+\Delta} - \epsilon_t)^2] = 2\sigma_\epsilon^2\) for uncorrelated noise, the observed variogram is \(C \Delta^{2H} + 2\sigma_\epsilon^2\). For small \(\Delta\), the constant noise floor dominates, flattening the log-log slope and distorting naive OLS.

Lessons

  1. Lesson 1 · The Heston Stochastic Volatility Model

    12 hours · Introduces concept heston-model

    The Cox–Ingersoll–Ross square-root variance diffusion, Feller boundary classification, risk-neutral transformation, PDE derivation, characteristic function formulation, the Albrecher et al. 'Little Heston Trap' stable branch cut, Carr–Madan Fourier inversion pricing, and the flat \(O(1)\) short-dated ATMI skew limitation.

  2. Lesson 2 · The SABR Model and Asymptotic Expansions

    12 hours

    The CEV backbone, stochastic vol dynamics, singular perturbation analysis, the Hagan et al. asymptotic formula for implied volatility, normal vs lognormal quoting conventions, and arbitrage pathologies (butterfly arbitrage and negative densities in the deep wings).

  3. Lesson 3 · Forward-Variance and Bergomi-Type Models

    12 hours · Introduces concept forward-variance-model

    Direct modeling of the forward-variance curve \(\xi_t(T)\), variance swap term structure calibration, the \(n\)-factor Bergomi model with exponential kernels, joint calibration puzzles of SPX and VIX options, and the structural inability of continuous diffusion kernels to generate steep short-time skew.

  4. Lesson 4 · Rough Volatility: Foundations and Pricing

    12 hours · Introduces concept rough-volatility

    Fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter \(H < 1/2\), failure of the semimartingale property, singular Volterra integral kernels, the rough Bergomi (rBergomi) model, and the derivation of the power-law ATMI skew explosion \(T^{H - 1/2}\) as \(T \to 0\).

  5. Lesson 5 · Estimating Rough Volatility and the Empirical Debate

    12 hours · Introduces concept hurst-exponent-estimation

    Realized variance time series, \(q\)-th order structure functions and variograms, microstructure noise contamination, noise-robust lag-truncation estimation of \(H\), and the doctoral debate: 'Is volatility rough?'.

Project ladder

The 30-hour independent project, Rough-vol estimation, challenges you to build a complete stochastic and rough volatility engine, estimating the Hurst parameter \(H\) on high-frequency data with rigorous treatment of microstructure noise.

  1. Rung 1 · Worked example

    A fully solved instance implementing Heston Fourier inversion, SABR smile evaluation, Bergomi forward variance, variogram estimation on seed 0 SIMULATED log-RV series, and noise variance correction.

  2. Rung 2 · Completion problem

    Complete the missing variogram structure function calculation and noise-corrected regression slope in the interactive verification script, graded instantly in the browser.

  3. Rung 3 · Independent project

    Author the full rough volatility engine rough_volatility.py, passing the 9-test invariant suite across 50 independent seeds, and submit an analytical research note defending your findings. Graded by both autograder and rubric.

  4. Rung 4 · Stretch

    Extend the framework to multi-factor rough Bergomi simulation and joint calibration against simultaneous SPX and VIX option chains.
