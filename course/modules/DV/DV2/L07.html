Discrete Stripping, Forward Smirks & Implied Trees

Options trading desks do not operate in continuous mathematical abstractions; they trade across discrete expiration tenors and discrete strike grids. Stripping forward implied volatilities between sequential expirations reveals the forward smile implied by the market. Crucially, pure local volatility models predict that forward smiles flatten dramatically over time due to diffusive mixing, whereas empirical forward smiles remain stubbornly steep. Implied trees discretize local volatility onto recombining lattices, providing intuitive numerical engines for pricing Bermudan and barrier options while laying bare the fundamental dynamical flaws of one-dimensional Markovian pricing models.

  • Prerequisites: volatility smile and skew, calendar arbitrage, local volatility

1. Discrete Forward Stripping of Total Variance

In market practice, exchange-traded options are quoted for a discrete set of expiration dates \(\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots < \tau_M\). Forward stripping computes the implied volatility of a forward-starting option spanning the forward interval \([\tau_1, \tau_2]\) Gatheral (2006).

Definition 7.1 (Forward Implied Total Variance). The forward implied total variance between two tenors \(\tau_1 < \tau_2\) at forward log-moneyness \(k\) is the total variance difference:

\[ w_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2) = w(k, \tau_2) - w(k, \tau_1) \]

The corresponding annualized forward implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2)\) is given by:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2) = \sqrt{\frac{w(k, \tau_2) - w(k, \tau_1)}{\tau_2 - \tau_1}} \]

Theorem 7.1 (Forward Variance Non-Negativity). The absence of calendar arbitrage between expiries \(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) is mathematically equivalent to the non-negativity of forward implied total variance across all strikes:

\[ w_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2) \ge 0 \quad \forall k \in \mathbb{R} \]

Proof. From Theorem 3.1, absence of calendar arbitrage requires \(C(K, \tau_2) \ge C(K, \tau_1)\) for any fixed strike \(K\). In total variance coordinates, this necessitates \(w(k, \tau_2) \ge w(k, \tau_1)\). Subtracting \(w(k, \tau_1)\) yields \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2) = w(k, \tau_2) - w(k, \tau_1) \ge 0\). Conversely, if \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}} < 0\) for some \(k^*\), the near-dated call has higher total variance than the far-dated call, enabling the riskless calendar arbitrage trade described in Lesson 3. ■

2. The Local Volatility Forward Skew Flattening Trap

A crucial limitation of one-dimensional local volatility models \(\mathrm{d}S_t = \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_t, t) S_t \mathrm{d}W_t\) is their inability to generate realistic forward smile dynamics. Consider a forward-starting option with strike determined at future time \(\tau_1\) and expiring at \(\tau_2\). Its payoff is \((S_{\tau_2} - \alpha S_{\tau_1})^+\).

Theorem 7.2 (Forward Smile Decay under Local Volatility). In a pure local volatility diffusion, the forward implied volatility skew slope \(\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}}{\partial k}\) over the forward horizon \(\Delta \tau = \tau_2 - \tau_1\) decays as \(\tau_1\) increases, satisfying:

\[ \left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0} = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_1}}\right) \quad \text{as } \tau_1 \to \infty \]

Proof Sketch. Under a local volatility diffusion, the log return \(\ln(S_{\tau_2} / S_{\tau_1})\) conditional on \(\mathcal{F}_{\tau_1}\) is an Itô integral \(\int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2} \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_u, u)\mathrm{d}W_u\). As \(\tau_1\) grows, the distribution of \(S_{\tau_1}\) spreads out over a wide range. When integrating over the marginal distribution of \(S_{\tau_1}\), the non-linear curvature of \(\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(S)\) averages out across paths. By the central limit theorem for diffusions with bounded coefficients, the conditional distribution converges toward Gaussianity, forcing higher standardized cumulants (skewness) to vanish at the rate \(\tau_1^{-1/2}\). ■

In real equity markets, forward-starting smiles do not flatten; empirical market quotes for forward-starting options and forward variance swaps maintain steep skews even for distant forward horizons. Calibrating an exotic derivative (such as a cliquet or reverse knockout) with pure local volatility systematically underprices the forward skew risk, creating massive hedging losses when market skews persist.

3. Implied Trees: Derman–Kani and Rubinstein Lattices

To implement local volatility numerically for Bermudan and American options, Emanuel Derman and Iraj Kani (1994), and Mark Rubinstein (1994) developed implied binomial and trinomial trees Hull (2021).

An implied tree constructs a recombining lattice where nodes \((i, j)\) at discrete time step \(t_i\) represent asset prices \(S_{i,j}\). At each step, forward Arrow–Debreu state prices \(\lambda_{i,j}\) are propagated inductively using risk-neutral transition probabilities \(p_{i,j}\):

\[ \lambda_{i+1, j} = \sum_k \lambda_{i, k} \, p(k \to j) \, e^{-r \Delta t} \]

The transition probabilities \(p_{i,j}\) are solved recursively to match the market prices of vanilla calls and puts maturing at \(t_{i+1}\):

\[ C(K_m, t_{i+1}) = \sum_j \lambda_{i+1, j} (S_{i+1, j} - K_m)^+ \]

Remark 7.1 (Negative Transition Probabilities). If the market option prices contain discrete calendar arbitrage (\(w(k, \tau_{i+1}) < w(k, \tau_i)\)) or butterfly arbitrage, the solved transition probabilities \(p_{i,j}\) fall outside \([0, 1]\). When \(p_{i,j} < 0\), the lattice violates probability axioms and generates unstable or negative option prices.

"""Lab 1 · Discrete Stripping, Forward Smirks & Implied Trees.

Implements forward implied volatility stripping across discrete maturities T1 < T2:
sigma_fwd^2(k) = (w(k, T2) - w(k, T1)) / (T2 - T1).
Demonstrates the flattening of forward skew under local volatility models,
a classic limitation compared to stochastic volatility dynamics.
All data is SIMULATED (seed 42).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def svi_val(k: float, a: float, b: float, rho: float, m: float, sigma: float) -> float:
    diff = k - m
    disc = math.sqrt(diff**2 + sigma**2)
    return a + b * (rho * diff + disc)


def main() -> None:
    t1 = 0.25
    t2 = 0.50
    t3 = 1.00

    # Calibrated term structure slices (SIMULATED)
    p1 = {"a": 0.015, "b": 0.080, "rho": -0.40, "m": 0.005, "sigma": 0.060}
    p2 = {"a": 0.030, "b": 0.115, "rho": -0.38, "m": 0.010, "sigma": 0.080}
    p3 = {"a": 0.060, "b": 0.165, "rho": -0.35, "m": 0.020, "sigma": 0.110}

    k_grid = [-0.30, -0.20, -0.10, 0.0, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30]

    print("Log-Moneyness (k) | Spot Vol T1 (0.25) | Spot Vol T2 (0.50) | Fwd Vol [0.25, 0.50] | Fwd Vol [0.50, 1.00]")
    print("-" * 105)

    spot_vols_t1: list[float] = []
    spot_vols_t2: list[float] = []
    fwd_vols_12: list[float] = []
    fwd_vols_23: list[float] = []

    for k in k_grid:
        w1 = svi_val(k, **p1)
        w2 = svi_val(k, **p2)
        w3 = svi_val(k, **p3)

        vol_t1 = math.sqrt(w1 / t1)
        vol_t2 = math.sqrt(w2 / t2)

        # Discrete forward variance: w_fwd = (w2 - w1) / (t2 - t1)
        var_fwd_12 = (w2 - w1) / (t2 - t1)
        var_fwd_23 = (w3 - w2) / (t3 - t2)

        vol_fwd_12 = math.sqrt(max(0.0, var_fwd_12))
        vol_fwd_23 = math.sqrt(max(0.0, var_fwd_23))

        spot_vols_t1.append(vol_t1)
        spot_vols_t2.append(vol_t2)
        fwd_vols_12.append(vol_fwd_12)
        fwd_vols_23.append(vol_fwd_23)

        print(f"{k:17.2f} | {vol_t1:18.4f} | {vol_t2:18.4f} | {vol_fwd_12:21.4f} | {vol_fwd_23:21.4f}")

    # Measure skew slope d(sigma) / dk around ATM (k in [-0.10, 0.10])
    idx_m = k_grid.index(-0.10)
    idx_p = k_grid.index(0.10)
    dk = k_grid[idx_p] - k_grid[idx_m]

    slope_spot_t1 = (spot_vols_t1[idx_p] - spot_vols_t1[idx_m]) / dk
    slope_fwd_12 = (fwd_vols_12[idx_p] - fwd_vols_12[idx_m]) / dk
    slope_fwd_23 = (fwd_vols_23[idx_p] - fwd_vols_23[idx_m]) / dk

    print("-" * 105)
    print(f"Spot ATM Skew Slope at T1=0.25:            {slope_spot_t1:.4f}")
    print(f"Forward ATM Skew Slope for [0.25, 0.50]:    {slope_fwd_12:.4f}")
    print(f"Forward ATM Skew Slope for [0.50, 1.00]:    {slope_fwd_23:.4f}")
    print("Forward Skew Flattens Across Distant Horizons:   ", abs(slope_fwd_23) < abs(slope_spot_t1))
    print("Forward Variances Are Strictly Positive (No Arb):", all(v > 0.0 for v in fwd_vols_12 + fwd_vols_23))


main()

Where the canon breaks

Discrete surface stripping and implied lattices expose fundamental practical pitfalls:

  • Microstructure Spikes in Forward Volatility: Stripping forward variance across closely spaced tenors (e.g. 1 week apart) divides small differences in total variance \(\Delta w\) by a tiny \(\Delta \tau\). A small pricing error of \$0.05 in market quotes can cause forward volatility to collapse to zero or spike to 100%.
  • Cliquet Pricing Failure: A cliquet option with periodic local caps and floors depends directly on forward skew. Pricing cliquets with pure local volatility models understates the likelihood of consecutive extreme returns, mispricing the contract by up to 40% relative to stochastic volatility models.
  • Tree Recombination Instability: In markets with high skewness, standard implied tree algorithms can encounter node inversion, where the forward price falls outside the upper and lower branches, breaking tree recombination.

Misconceptions

  • A model that perfectly prices European vanilla options will automatically produce fair prices for forward-starting options.

    Vanilla options determine only the one-dimensional marginal distributions. Forward-starting options depend on the joint distribution \((S_{\tau_1}, S_{\tau_2})\), which local volatility mis-specifies through excessive forward smile flattening.

  • Forward implied volatility must always lie between the short-term and long-term spot implied volatilities.

    When the implied volatility term structure is upward-sloping, forward volatility is higher than both spot volatilities: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2 = \frac{\sigma_2^2 \tau_2 - \sigma_1^2 \tau_1}{\tau_2 - \tau_1} > \sigma_2^2\).

  • Implied binomial trees always guarantee non-negative transition probabilities across all nodes.

    If market quotes contain even subtle localized calendar or butterfly arbitrage, the recursive equations force transition probabilities to become negative (\(p_{i,j} < 0\)) or exceed 1, corrupting the tree.

Practice problems

An option has 3-month total variance \(w(k, 0.25) = 0.0100\) and 6-month total variance \(w(k, 0.50) = 0.0225\) at the money. Calculate the forward total variance \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}}\) and the annualized forward implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}\).

Worked Solution

Forward total variance is \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}} = w(k, 0.50) - w(k, 0.25) = 0.0225 - 0.0100 = 0.0125\). The forward time horizon is \(\Delta \tau = 0.50 - 0.25 = 0.25\). Annualized forward implied volatility is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}} = \sqrt{w_{\mathrm{fwd}} / \Delta \tau} = \sqrt{0.0125 / 0.25} = \sqrt{0.0500} \approx 0.22361\) or 22.36%.

Given 1-month spot volatility \(\sigma_1 = 0.20\) (\(\tau_1 = 1/12\)) and 2-month spot volatility \(\sigma_2 = 0.22\) (\(\tau_2 = 2/12\)), compute the 1-month forward implied volatility spanning month 1 to month 2.

Worked Solution

Compute total variances: \(w_1 = 0.20^2 \times (1/12) = 0.04 \times (1/12) = 0.003333\), and \(w_2 = 0.22^2 \times (2/12) = 0.0484 \times (2/12) = 0.008067\). Forward variance is \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}} = w_2 - w_1 = 0.008067 - 0.003333 = 0.004733\). Forward horizon is \(\Delta \tau = 1/12\). Annualized forward volatility is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}} = \sqrt{0.004733 / (1/12)} = \sqrt{0.056800} \approx 0.23833\) (23.83%). Notice that \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}} > \sigma_2 > \sigma_1\).

In an implied tree, a node at time step \(i\) has forward price \(F = 100\). The tree branches to an up state \(S_u = 108\) and a down state \(S_d = 94\). Compute the risk-neutral transition probability \(p_u\).

Worked Solution

Under the risk-neutral measure, the expectation matches the forward price: \(p_u S_u + (1 - p_u) S_d = F\). Rearranging: \(p_u(S_u - S_d) = F - S_d\), so \(p_u = \frac{F - S_d}{S_u - S_d} = \frac{100 - 94}{108 - 94} = \frac{6}{14} \approx 0.42857\). Because \(0 \le p_u \le 1\), the transition probability is valid.

Prove that when the spot implied volatility term structure is strictly increasing with maturity (\(\sigma_2 > \sigma_1\)), the forward implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}\) must be strictly greater than \(\sigma_2\).

Worked Solution

Express forward variance: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2(\tau_2 - \tau_1) = \sigma_2^2 \tau_2 - \sigma_1^2 \tau_1\). Subtract \(\sigma_2^2(\tau_2 - \tau_1)\) from both sides: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2(\tau_2 - \tau_1) - \sigma_2^2(\tau_2 - \tau_1) = \sigma_2^2 \tau_2 - \sigma_1^2 \tau_1 - \sigma_2^2 \tau_2 + \sigma_2^2 \tau_1 = (\sigma_2^2 - \sigma_1^2)\tau_1\). Dividing by \(\tau_2 - \tau_1 > 0\): \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2 - \sigma_2^2 = (\sigma_2^2 - \sigma_1^2)\frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2 - \tau_1}\). Since \(\sigma_2 > \sigma_1 > 0\), the right side is strictly positive. Therefore, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2 > \sigma_2^2\), which implies \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}} > \sigma_2\).

Prove that if forward implied variance \(w_{\mathrm{fwd}}(k; \tau_1, \tau_2) < 0\), a long calendar spread position constructed from European calls has a strictly negative initial value.

Worked Solution

A long calendar spread buys the call at \(\tau_2\) and sells the call at \(\tau_1\). Its market price is \(\Pi = C(K, \tau_2) - C(K, \tau_1)\). Since Black–Scholes call price is strictly monotonic in total variance \(w\) (\(\frac{\partial C}{\partial w} = \frac{\mathcal{V}}{2\sqrt{w}} > 0\)), if \(w(k, \tau_2) < w(k, \tau_1)\), then \(C(K, \tau_2) < C(K, \tau_1)\). Therefore, \(\Pi = C(K, \tau_2) - C(K, \tau_1) < 0\), meaning the spread cost is strictly negative, delivering immediate cash to the buyer.

Show that in Derman–Kani implied trees, if the forward price \(F_{i,j}\) at node \((i,j)\) falls outside the interval \([S_{i+1, j}, S_{i+1, j+1}]\), the implied transition probability \(p_{i,j}\) is not a valid probability.

Worked Solution

In a binomial branch, risk-neutral pricing requires \(p S_{u} + (1 - p) S_{d} = F\), yielding \(p = \frac{F - S_d}{S_u - S_d}\). For \(p\) to satisfy \(0 \le p \le 1\), we must have \(0 \le F - S_d \le S_u - S_d\), which is equivalent to \(S_d \le F \le S_u\). If \(F > S_u\), then \(F - S_d > S_u - S_d\), so \(p > 1\). If \(F < S_d\), then \(F - S_d < 0\), so \(p < 0\). In either case, \(p\) violates the Kolmogorov probability axiom \(p \in [0, 1]\).

An investment bank prices a 1-year cliquet option with monthly reset caps of +2% and floors of -1% on the S&P 500 index. When priced under a local volatility model, the price is \$3.80; when priced under a Heston stochastic volatility model calibrated to the exact same vanilla options, the price is \$5.10. Explain the economic source of this 34% discrepancy.

Worked Solution

The monthly cliquet consists of 12 forward-starting options. Under local volatility, the forward smile flattens rapidly over time, so forward months 4 through 12 have artificially low skew and kurtosis. Under Heston stochastic volatility, the forward smile remains steep because volatility continues to fluctuate stochastically across future periods. The higher probability of extreme monthly returns under stochastic volatility increases the value of the option's asymmetry, resulting in a significantly higher fair price (\$5.10 vs \$3.80).

A trader strips forward implied volatilities from an S&P 500 chain across sequential weekly expiries. At week 3, the stripped forward volatility is 12%, but at week 4, it drops to 1.5% before rebounding to 15% at week 5. What market artifact is responsible for this erratic path?

Worked Solution

Erratic weekly forward volatilities are classic symptoms of microstructure noise and small quote misalignments. When \(\Delta \tau = 1/52 \approx 0.0192\), the denominator in \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2 = \frac{\Delta w}{\Delta \tau}\) is very small. A tiny \$0.05 difference in option bid-ask midpoints distorts \(\Delta w\) significantly, resulting in unstable, spurious swings in stripped forward volatilities unless regularized by cross-tenor smoothing.

How does forward volatility stripping enable a trader to execute a forward-starting volatility trade using standard vanilla options?

Worked Solution

A trader synthesizes forward volatility exposure over \([\tau_1, \tau_2]\) by taking a long position in variance swaps or options expiring at \(\tau_2\) and a short position in options expiring at \(\tau_1\), weighted such that vega exposure over the initial period \([0, \tau_1]\) cancels out. The net position captures the difference in total variance \(w(\tau_2) - w(\tau_1)\), isolating pure forward volatility performance.

Formulate the Bergomi (2005) forward variance model \(\xi_t^T = \mathbb{E}[v_T \mid \mathcal{F}_t]\) and explain how it resolves the forward smile flattening pathology of local volatility.

Worked Solution

Bergomi models forward instantaneous variance curves directly: \(\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_t^T}{\xi_t^T} = \sum_{k=1}^n \omega_k e^{-\kappa_k(T - t)}\mathrm{d}W_t^{(k)}\). By specifying stochastic dynamics directly on the forward variance curve, the model decouples vanilla smile calibration from forward smile decay. The exponential kernels preserve persistent forward skew and kurtosis across all forward horizons, accurately pricing cliquets and forward-starting options.

Describe the Derman–Kani implied tree construction algorithm and detail the condition for preventing tree recombination breakdown in the presence of steep skews.

Worked Solution

Derman & Kani define a recombining tree with node spacing \(\Delta S = S \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\). To calibrate node \((i+1, j)\), the algorithm equates tree-discounted expectations of call options to market call prices, solving for local transition probabilities \(p_{i,j}\). In steep skew regimes, tree spacing must be adapted non-linearly (or transformed into log coordinates \(x = \ln S\)) to ensure that the forward price at each parent node always lies strictly between its child nodes, preserving \(p_{i,j} \in (0, 1)\).

How do Bermudan swaption pricing models in fixed income (such as SABR-LMM or Cheyette models) avoid the forward smile decay problem of standard Black–Karasinski short-rate trees?

Worked Solution

One-factor short-rate trees suffer from identical forward smile decay as equity local volatility. In contrast, the SABR Libor Market Model (SABR-LMM) assigns an independent stochastic volatility process to each forward swap rate. Because each forward rate maintains its own stochastic variance and correlation with forward rate innovations, forward smiles remain non-normal and heavily skewed across all swaption expiries, correctly pricing early exercise boundaries.

    Options

    Because \(\sigma_{\mathrm{fwd}}^2(\tau_2 - \tau_1) = \sigma_2^2 \tau_2 - \sigma_1^2 \tau_1\), an upward-sloping term structure forces forward volatility to be higher than both.

  6. What causes Derman–Kani implied tree algorithms to produce negative transition probabilities at certain nodes?

  7. Why are cliquet options particularly sensitive to the model chosen for the forward volatility surface?
