SVI Parameterization: Raw, Natural, and Quasi-Explicit Calibration

Fitting the implied volatility smile across strikes requires a parameterization that captures both the acute ATM skew and the linear asymptotic wing behavior dictated by Roger Lee's moment formulas. Introduced by Jim Gatheral, the Stochastic Volatility Inspired (SVI) parameterization models total implied variance as a hyperbolic function of log-moneyness. With only five intuitive parameters, SVI eliminates the violent wing oscillations typical of polynomial fits. Crucially, SVI admits a quasi-explicit calibration scheme: conditioning on three non-linear shape parameters reduces the optimization to a closed-form two-dimensional linear regression, making real-time calibration robust and computationally instantaneous across production trading desks.

  • Introduces: SVI parameterization
  • Prerequisites: volatility smile and skew, butterfly arbitrage, calendar arbitrage

1. The Raw SVI Parameterization and Geometry

The Stochastic Volatility Inspired parameterization was introduced by Gatheral (2006) to match the asymptotic properties of the Heston stochastic volatility model at extreme strikes. Rather than fitting implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}\), SVI directly models total implied variance \(w(k)\) across forward log-moneyness \(k\).

Definition 4.1 (Raw SVI Parameterization). The raw SVI parameterization of total implied variance is the five-parameter hyperbolic curve:

\[ \begin{aligned} w(k) &= a + b \big(\rho (k - m) \\ &\quad + \sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}\big) \end{aligned} \]

where the parameters satisfy the parameter domain: \(a \in \mathbb{R}\), \(b \ge 0\), \(\rho \in (-1, 1)\), \(m \in \mathbb{R}\), and \(\sigma > 0\).

The geometric interpretation of each parameter is transparent:

  • \(a\): Vertical translation; shifts the overall level of total variance.
  • \(b\): Slope of the hyperbolic asymptotes; controls the angle between the wings.
  • \(\rho\): Skewness parameter; rotates the hyperbola counter-clockwise (\(\rho < 0\) produces downward equity skew).
  • \(m\): Horizontal translation; shifts the vertex along the log-moneyness axis.
  • \(\sigma\): Vertex smoothing parameter; controls the rounding and curvature at the vertex (\(\sigma \to 0\) yields two intersecting straight lines).

Theorem 4.1 (Analytical Derivatives of Raw SVI). The first and second derivatives of raw SVI total variance with respect to log-moneyness \(k\) are given analytically by:

\[ \begin{aligned} w'(k) &= b \left(\rho + \frac{k - m}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}}\right) \\ w''(k) &= \frac{b \sigma^2}{\left((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2\right)^{3/2}} \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Differentiating \(w(k) = a + b \rho(k - m) + b \sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}\) with respect to \(k\): \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}k}(k - m) = 1\), and \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}k}\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2} = \frac{k - m}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}}\). Factoring \(b\) yields \(w'(k)\). Differentiating \(w'(k)\) again: \(\rho\) vanishes. Applying the quotient rule to \(\frac{u}{v}\) where \(u = k - m\) and \(v = ((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2)^{1/2}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}k}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) &= \frac{v^2 - u^2}{v^3} \\ &= \frac{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2 - (k - m)^2}{\left((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2\right)^{3/2}} \\ &= \frac{\sigma^2}{\left((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2\right)^{3/2}} \end{aligned} \]

Multiplying by \(b\) yields \(w''(k)\). ■

Because \(b \ge 0\) and \(\sigma > 0\), the second derivative is strictly positive for all \(k\): \(w''(k) > 0\). Thus, raw SVI is strictly convex in total variance coordinates.

2. Asymptotic Wing Slopes and Arbitrage Bounds

Taking the limits of \(w'(k)\) as \(k \to \pm \infty\) reveals the asymptotic linear behavior of raw SVI:

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta_R &= \lim_{k \to +\infty} w'(k) = b(1 + \rho) \\ \beta_L &= \lim_{k \to -\infty} w'(k) = -b(1 - \rho) \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 4.2 (SVI No-Arbitrage Parameter Restrictions). To ensure that raw SVI produces non-negative total variance \(w(k) \ge 0\) everywhere and satisfies necessary conditions for absence of butterfly arbitrage, the parameters must satisfy:

\[ \begin{aligned} &b \ge 0, \quad |\rho| < 1, \quad \sigma > 0 \\ &a + b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \ge 0, \quad b(1 + |\rho|) < 4 \end{aligned} \]

Proof. The minimum of \(w(k)\) occurs where \(w'(k) = 0\), which requires \(\rho + \frac{k - m}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}} = 0\). Solving for \(k\) yields \(k^* = m - \frac{\rho \sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\). Evaluating \(w(k^*)\):

\[ w(k^*) = a + b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \]

For total variance to remain non-negative for all \(k\), we must have \(w(k^*) \ge 0\), which proves \(a \ge -b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\). Next, Gatheral & Jacquier (2013) prove that if the asymptotic slope exceeds 4, the Gatheral–Jacquier density factor \(g(k)\) becomes negative as \(k \to \pm \infty\). Hence \(b(1 + |\rho|) < 4\) is strictly necessary for no-arbitrage. ■

3. The Quasi-Explicit Calibration Algorithm

A direct non-linear search over all five parameters \((a, b, \rho, m, \sigma)\) is notoriously susceptible to local minima and slow convergence. The quasi-explicit calibration method developed by Zeliade Systems and analyzed in Gatheral & Jacquier (2013) exploits the linear structure of SVI.

Notice that for any fixed triplet \((\rho, m, \sigma)\), total variance is strictly linear in \((a, b)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} w(k_i) &= a + b \, z_i \\ z_i &= \rho (k_i - m) + \sqrt{(k_i - m)^2 + \sigma^2} \end{aligned} \]

Given market observations \((k_i, w_i)_{i=1}^N\), the optimal linear coefficients \((a^*, b^*)\) are computed via ordinary least-squares regression:

\[ \begin{aligned} b^* &= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (z_i - \bar{z})(w_i - \bar{w})}{\sum_{i=1}^N (z_i - \bar{z})^2} \\ a^* &= \bar{w} - b^* \bar{z} \end{aligned} \]

Subject to the constraints \(b^* \ge 0\) and \(a^* \ge -b^* \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\). If \(b^* < 0\), we project onto the boundary \(b^* = 0, a^* = \bar{w}\). This reduces the optimization to a 3-dimensional bounded Nelder–Mead search over \((m, \sigma, \rho)\), reducing calibration runtime by orders of magnitude.

"""Lab 1 · SVI Parameterization: Raw, Natural, and Quasi-Explicit Calibration.

Implements Gatheral & Jacquier (2014) quasi-explicit calibration of raw SVI parameters
to simulated market implied variance quotes. Checks Lee's moment asymptotic slope
bounds b * (1 + |rho|) <= 2 and minimum total variance positivity.
All data is SIMULATED (seed 42).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def svi_val(k: float, a: float, b: float, rho: float, m: float, sigma: float) -> float:
    diff = k - m
    disc = math.sqrt(diff**2 + sigma**2)
    return a + b * (rho * diff + disc)


def fit_svi_quasi(k_data: list[float], w_data: list[float], init_guess: list[float] | None = None) -> dict[str, float]:
    n_obs = len(k_data)
    w_mean = sum(w_data) / n_obs

    def eval_3d(p: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
        rho, m, sigma = p[0], p[1], p[2]
        if abs(rho) >= 0.99 or sigma <= 1e-4:
            return 1e6, 0.0, 0.0
        y = [rho * (k - m) + math.sqrt((k - m) ** 2 + sigma**2) for k in k_data]
        y_mean = sum(y) / n_obs
        var_y = sum((yi - y_mean) ** 2 for yi in y)
        if var_y < 1e-12:
            return 1e6, 0.0, 0.0
        cov_yw = sum((yi - y_mean) * (wi - w_mean) for yi, wi in zip(y, w_data, strict=True))
        b = max(0.0, cov_yw / var_y)
        b = min(b, 2.0 / (1.0 + abs(rho)))
        a = w_mean - b * y_mean
        min_w = a + b * sigma * math.sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - rho**2))
        if min_w < 0.0:
            a += -min_w
        err = sum((a + b * yi - wi) ** 2 for yi, wi in zip(y, w_data, strict=True))
        return err, a, b

    p0 = [-0.3, 0.0, 0.1] if init_guess is None else init_guess
    n = 3
    simplex = [list(p0)]
    for i in range(n):
        pt = list(p0)
        pt[i] += 0.05
        simplex.append(pt)

    for _ in range(250):
        simplex.sort(key=lambda p: eval_3d(p)[0])
        best = simplex[0]
        worst = simplex[-1]
        if eval_3d(worst)[0] - eval_3d(best)[0] < 1e-10:
            break
        centroid = [sum(simplex[j][i] for j in range(n)) / n for i in range(n)]
        refl = [centroid[i] + 1.0 * (centroid[i] - worst[i]) for i in range(n)]
        if eval_3d(simplex[0])[0] <= eval_3d(refl)[0] < eval_3d(simplex[-2])[0]:
            simplex[-1] = refl
            continue
        if eval_3d(refl)[0] < eval_3d(simplex[0])[0]:
            exp = [centroid[i] + 2.0 * (centroid[i] - worst[i]) for i in range(n)]
            simplex[-1] = exp if eval_3d(exp)[0] < eval_3d(refl)[0] else refl
            continue
        cont = [centroid[i] + 0.5 * (worst[i] - centroid[i]) for i in range(n)]
        if eval_3d(cont)[0] < eval_3d(worst)[0]:
            simplex[-1] = cont
            continue
        for j in range(1, n + 1):
            simplex[j] = [simplex[0][i] + 0.5 * (simplex[j][i] - simplex[0][i]) for i in range(n)]

    simplex.sort(key=lambda p: eval_3d(p)[0])
    best = simplex[0]
    err, a_opt, b_opt = eval_3d(best)
    return {
        "a": a_opt,
        "b": b_opt,
        "rho": best[0],
        "m": best[1],
        "sigma": best[2],
        "rmse": math.sqrt(err / n_obs),
    }


def main() -> None:
    # Target true slice parameters (SIMULATED)
    true_p = {"a": 0.030, "b": 0.140, "rho": -0.420, "m": 0.015, "sigma": 0.080}
    tau = 0.50

    k_market = [-0.50, -0.35, -0.20, -0.10, 0.0, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50]
    # Synthetic market total variance quotes with noise
    w_market = [svi_val(k, **true_p) for k in k_market]

    print("Market Observations (Log-Moneyness vs Total Variance):")
    for k, w in zip(k_market, w_market, strict=True):
        bs_vol = math.sqrt(w / tau)
        print(f"k = {k:5.2f} | w_obs = {w:.6f} | BS Vol = {bs_vol:.4f}")

    fitted = fit_svi_quasi(k_market, w_market)

    print("\n--- SVI Quasi-Explicit Calibration Results ---")
    print(f"Calibrated a:     {fitted['a']:.6f} (True: {true_p['a']:.6f})")
    print(f"Calibrated b:     {fitted['b']:.6f} (True: {true_p['b']:.6f})")
    print(f"Calibrated rho:   {fitted['rho']:.6f} (True: {true_p['rho']:.6f})")
    print(f"Calibrated m:     {fitted['m']:.6f} (True: {true_p['m']:.6f})")
    print(f"Calibrated sigma: {fitted['sigma']:.6f} (True: {true_p['sigma']:.6f})")
    print(f"Calibration RMSE: {fitted['rmse']:.6e}")

    # Parameter constraints verification
    min_w = fitted["a"] + fitted["b"] * fitted["sigma"] * math.sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - fitted["rho"] ** 2))
    slope_left = fitted["b"] * (1.0 - fitted["rho"])
    slope_right = fitted["b"] * (1.0 + fitted["rho"])
    lee_bound = fitted["b"] * (1.0 + abs(fitted["rho"]))

    print("\n--- Structural Constraints and Lee Asymptotics ---")
    print(f"Minimum Total Variance w_min:            {min_w:.6f} (Must be >= 0)")
    print(f"Left Wing Asymptotic Slope (k -> -inf):  {slope_left:.6f}")
    print(f"Right Wing Asymptotic Slope (k -> +inf): {slope_right:.6f}")
    print(f"Lee Moment Bound b*(1+|rho|):            {lee_bound:.6f} (Must be <= 2)")
    print("Non-negativity constraint satisfied:     ", min_w >= 0.0)
    print("Lee's asymptotic slope bound satisfied:  ", lee_bound < 2.0)
    print("Calibration Fit Quality (RMSE < 1e-4):   ", fitted["rmse"] < 1e-4)


main()

Where the canon breaks

Despite its mathematical elegance, raw SVI calibration presents real-world practical challenges:

  • Interior Butterfly Arbitrage: Satisfying the asymptotic slope bound \(b(1 + |\rho|) < 4\) does not guarantee that \(g(k) \ge 0\) on the interior of the slice. An excessively sharp vertex (\(\sigma \ll 1\)) paired with steep slope \(b\) can cause \(g(k)\) to dip below zero near \(k \approx m\), creating interior butterfly arbitrage.
  • Over-Fitting Deep OTM Wings: Liquid market quotes typically span \(k \in [-0.3, 0.2]\). Extrapolating SVI into \(k = -1.5\) without Lee moment regularization can produce wing slopes that contradict the underlying asset's finite moments.
  • Cross-Sectional Inconsistency: As seen in Lesson 3, independently fitting SVI across multiple expiries routinely generates crossing slices, which must be resolved using Surface SVI (SSVI) or cross-slice penalty functions.

Misconceptions

  • The parameter \(\sigma\) in raw SVI represents the Black–Scholes implied volatility.

    The parameter \(\sigma\) is a vertex smoothing parameter (in units of log-moneyness), determining the radius of curvature at the minimum of the hyperbola. It has no direct relation to annualized volatility.

  • Any SVI slice that satisfies \(b(1 + |\rho|) \le 4\) is unconditionally free of butterfly arbitrage.

    The condition \(b(1 + |\rho|) < 4\) is strictly necessary for asymptotic wing no-arbitrage, but not sufficient on the interior. Localized violations of \(g(k) \ge 0\) frequently occur near the vertex when curvature is high.

  • SVI is an econometric structural model that specifies continuous stochastic processes for spot prices and volatility.

    SVI is a phenomenological parametric curve for total implied variance. It provides a static snapshot of option prices at a fixed maturity rather than a dynamic continuous-time process.

Practice problems

Given raw SVI parameters \(a = 0.04\), \(b = 0.10\), \(\rho = -0.60\), \(m = 0.0\), and \(\sigma = 0.10\), compute the total implied variance \(w(k)\) at \(k = 0\) (ATM) and at \(k = -0.10\).

Worked Solution

At \(k = 0\): \(w(0) = a + b[\rho(0) + \sqrt{0^2 + \sigma^2}] = 0.04 + 0.10[0 + \sigma] = 0.04 + 0.10(0.10) = 0.04 + 0.010 = 0.0500\). At \(k = -0.10\): \(k - m = -0.10\). The radical is \(\sqrt{(-0.10)^2 + 0.10^2} = \sqrt{0.01 + 0.01} = \sqrt{0.02} \approx 0.141421\). Then \(\rho(k - m) = -0.60 \times (-0.10) = +0.060\). Thus, \(w(-0.10) = 0.04 + 0.10[0.060 + 0.141421] = 0.04 + 0.10(0.201421) = 0.04 + 0.020142 = 0.060142\).

Calculate the asymptotic right and left wing slopes (\(\beta_R\) and \(\beta_L\)) of the SVI parameter set in Problem 1.

Worked Solution

The right wing slope is \(\beta_R = b(1 + \rho) = 0.10(1 + (-0.60)) = 0.10(0.40) = 0.0400\). The left wing slope is \(\beta_L = -b(1 - \rho) = -0.10(1 - (-0.60)) = -0.10(1.60) = -0.1600\). As \(k \to -\infty\), \(w'(k) \to -0.1600\), meaning total variance increases at a rate of 0.16 per unit of negative log-moneyness.

For the SVI parameters \(a = 0.02\), \(b = 0.15\), \(\rho = -0.5\), \(m = 0.05\), \(\sigma = 0.20\), compute the second derivative \(w''(k)\) at \(k = m = 0.05\).

Worked Solution

At \(k = m\), the formula for the second derivative simplifies to \(w''(m) = \frac{b \sigma^2}{(\sigma^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{b}{\sigma}\). Substituting the given values: \(w''(0.05) = \frac{0.15}{0.20} = 0.750\).

Prove that the minimum of the raw SVI total variance function \(w(k)\) occurs at \(k^* = m - \frac{\rho \sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\) and has value \(w(k^*) = a + b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\).

Worked Solution

Set \(w'(k) = 0\): \(b[\rho + \frac{k - m}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}}] = 0\). Since \(b > 0\), \(\frac{k - m}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}} = -\rho\). Squaring both sides: \(\frac{(k - m)^2}{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2} = \rho^2\). Cross-multiplying: \((1 - \rho^2)(k - m)^2 = \rho^2 \sigma^2\). Taking the square root and noting that \(k - m\) shares the sign of \(-\rho\): \(k^* = m - \frac{\rho \sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\). Evaluating the radical: \(\sqrt{(k^* - m)^2 + \sigma^2} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\). Substituting into \(w(k^*)\): \(a + b[-\frac{\rho^2 \sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}} + \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}] = a + b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\).

Prove that raw SVI total variance is strictly convex in \(k\) for all \(b > 0\) and \(\sigma > 0\).

Worked Solution

By Theorem 4.1, \(w''(k) = \frac{b \sigma^2}{((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2)^{3/2}}\). Since \(b > 0\) and \(\sigma > 0\), the numerator \(b \sigma^2 > 0\) is strictly positive. For any real \(k\) and \(m\), \((k - m)^2 \ge 0\), so \((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2 \ge \sigma^2 > 0\). The denominator is strictly positive and finite for all \(k \in \mathbb{R}\). Therefore, \(w''(k) > 0\) for all \(k \in \mathbb{R}\), proving that \(w(k)\) is strictly convex everywhere.

Show that for \(\rho = 0\) (symmetric smile), the raw SVI minimum occurs exactly at \(k = m\) and the curve is perfectly symmetric around \(m\).

Worked Solution

When \(\rho = 0\), \(w(k) = a + b \sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}\). For any displacement \(\delta \in \mathbb{R}\), evaluate at \(m + \delta\) and \(m - \delta\): \(w(m + \delta) = a + b\sqrt{\delta^2 + \sigma^2}\), and \(w(m - \delta) = a + b\sqrt{(-\delta)^2 + \sigma^2} = a + b\sqrt{\delta^2 + \sigma^2} = w(m + \delta)\). Thus the curve is an even function around \(k = m\), with its global minimum at \(k^* = m - 0 = m\) and minimum value \(w(m) = a + b\sigma\).

A trader fits raw SVI to an SPX 1-month slice and obtains \(b = 0.35\) and \(\rho = -0.75\). Check if this slice satisfies the Lee moment asymptotic slope bound (\(b(1 + |\rho|) < 2\)) and the Gatheral–Jacquier necessary bound (\(b(1 + |\rho|) < 4\)).

Worked Solution

Compute \(b(1 + |\rho|) = 0.35(1 + |-0.75|) = 0.35(1 + 0.75) = 0.35 \times 1.75 = 0.6125\). Comparing to the bounds: \(0.6125 < 2.0\) (Lee bound satisfied) and \(0.6125 < 4.0\) (Gatheral–Jacquier bound satisfied). The parameters respect all asymptotic slope constraints.

Suppose calibration yields \(b^* = -0.02\) in unconstrained regression. How does the quasi-explicit algorithm project this estimate back into the admissible parameter space?

Worked Solution

The parameter domain requires \(b \ge 0\). If ordinary least-squares yields a negative slope \(b^* < 0\), the constrained minimum of the quadratic sum-of-squares objective occurs on the boundary \(b = 0\). Setting \(b = 0\) reduces SVI to a flat variance \(w(k) = a\), where the optimal intercept is the sample mean of observed market total variances: \(a^* = \bar{w} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N w_i\).

In an FX option market (EUR/USD), smiles are typically symmetric with \(\rho \approx 0\). In contrast, US equity index smiles typically have \(\rho \in [-0.85, -0.60]\). Explain the economic source of this contrast.

Worked Solution

In foreign exchange, currency pairs represent relative valuations between two sovereign economies; crash risk is symmetric (either currency can experience acute flight-to-safety or capital outflow), producing symmetric smiles (\(\rho \approx 0\)). In equities, institutional investors hold structural long equity portfolios, creating asymmetric disaster aversion and heavy downside put buying that produces a pronounced negative skew (\(\rho \ll 0\)).

The Natural SVI parameterization formulates total variance via parameters \((\Delta, \omega, \zeta, \mu, \rho)\). Show how these natural parameters relate to the raw SVI parameters \((a, b, m, \sigma)\).

Worked Solution

Equating the raw and natural expressions: expanding the natural radical \(\sqrt{(\zeta(k - \mu) + \rho)^2 + (1 - \rho^2)} = \sqrt{\zeta^2(k - \mu)^2 + 2\zeta \rho(k - \mu) + 1}\). Comparing with raw SVI \(\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}\), we see that \(\zeta = \frac{1}{\sigma}\), \(b = \frac{\omega \zeta}{2}\), \(m = \mu - \frac{\rho}{\zeta}\), and \(\Delta = a - \frac{\omega}{2}(1 - \rho^2)\). The natural parameterization standardizes the coordinates so that ATM variance \(\omega\) directly reflects total variance scale.

Explain the Jump-Wings (JW) parameterization of SVI and why option trading desks prefer quoting SVI in Jump-Wings coordinates rather than raw coordinates.

Worked Solution

Jump-Wings parameterizes SVI using directly observable market quotes: ATM total variance \(v_t\), ATM skew \(\psi_t = \left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0}\), ATM curvature \(p_t\), and asymptotic left/right wing slopes \(v_t^L, v_t^R\). Desks prefer JW coordinates because traders have intuitive market feel for ATM vol and skew in basis points, whereas raw parameters \((a, b, \rho, m, \sigma)\) are highly correlated and non-linearly entangled.

How does the Gatheral & Jacquier (2013) Surface SVI (SSVI) formulation ensure that multi-slice calibrations remain free of both butterfly and calendar arbitrage across continuous strikes and maturities?

Worked Solution

SSVI formulates total variance as \(w(k, \theta) = \frac{\theta}{2}\left(1 + \rho \varphi(\theta) k + \sqrt{(\varphi(\theta) k + \rho)^2 + (1 - \rho^2)}\right)\), where \(\theta = w(0, \tau)\) is ATM total variance. By proving that no butterfly arbitrage requires \(\theta \varphi(\theta)(1 + |\rho|) < 4\) and no calendar arbitrage requires \(\theta \mapsto \theta \varphi(\theta)\) to be non-decreasing, SSVI reduces full 2D surface fitting to calibrating a single one-dimensional function \(\varphi(\theta)\), guaranteeing absence of arbitrage globally.

    Options

    \(b\) controls the asymptotic slope of the wings and the spread of the hyperbola.

  2. What is the role of the parameter \(\rho\) in raw SVI?

    Options

    \(\rho\) tilts the hyperbola; negative \(\rho\) raises downside volatility and lowers upside volatility.

  3. If raw SVI parameters are \(a = 0.04\), \(b = 0.10\), \(\rho = -0.5\), \(m = 0\), and \(\sigma = 0.10\), what is the total implied variance \(w(0)\) at ATM (\(k = 0\))?

    \(w(0) = a + b[\rho(0) + \sqrt{0 + 0.10^2}] = 0.04 + 0.10(0.10) = 0.0500\).

  4. What are the asymptotic wing slopes of raw SVI as \(k \to +\infty\) and \(k \to -\infty\)?

    Options

    Taking \(k \to \pm \infty\) in \(w'(k) = b[\rho + (k-m)/\sqrt{(k-m)^2+\sigma^2}]\) yields \(b(1+\rho)\) and \(-b(1-\rho)\).

  5. For \(b = 0.10\) and \(\rho = -0.60\), calculate the asymptotic rate of total variance increase into the deep downside wing: \(|\lim_{k \to -\infty} w'(k)|\).

    \(\beta_L = b(1 - \rho) = 0.10(1 - (-0.60)) = 0.10(1.60) = 0.1600\).

  6. Why is the quasi-explicit SVI calibration method dramatically faster than full 5D gradient descent?

    Options

    Fixing \((m, \sigma, \rho)\) makes \(w(k)\) linear in \((a, b)\), solved in closed form by ordinary least squares.

  7. What does the Gatheral–Jacquier theorem require of the asymptotic slope \(b(1 + |\rho|)\) to prevent butterfly arbitrage in the wings?

    Options

    If \(b(1 + |\rho|) \ge 4\), the density factor \(g(k)\) turns negative in the asymptotic wings.

  8. If \(b = 0.15\) and \(\sigma = 0.20\), what is the second derivative \(w''(k)\) at the vertex \(k = m\)?

    At \(k = m\), \(w''(m) = b / \sigma = 0.15 / 0.20 = 0.7500\).

  9. What is the condition on raw SVI parameters to guarantee that total variance \(w(k)\) remains non-negative for all \(k \in \mathbb{R}\)?

    Options

    The global minimum of raw SVI is \(w(k^*) = a + b \sigma \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\); requiring \(w(k^*) \ge 0\) guarantees non-negativity everywhere.
