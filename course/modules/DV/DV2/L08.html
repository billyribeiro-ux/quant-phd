Surface Diagnostics, Asymptotic Slopes & Lee's Moment Formula

Extrapolating implied volatility beyond observable strikes is fraught with mathematical peril. In a foundational 2004 breakthrough, Roger Lee proved that the asymptotic slope of total implied variance at extreme strikes is directly bounded by the maximal finite moments of the underlying asset price distribution. Specifically, total variance cannot grow faster than linearly in log-moneyness, with slope strictly bounded in \([0, 2]\). In production derivatives engines, automated diagnostic suites audit volatility surfaces for butterfly and calendar violations across dense grids, while projection repair algorithms resolve localized anomalies to ensure robust pricing of vanilla and exotic portfolios.

  • Prerequisites: butterfly arbitrage, calendar arbitrage, SVI parameterization

1. Roger Lee's Moment Formula and Wing Asymptotics

In market data, exchange-traded options exist only over a compact strike interval. How should total implied variance \(w(k, \tau)\) extrapolate into the deep out-of-the-money wings as \(k \to \pm \infty\)? In a celebrated work, Roger Lee (2004), "The Moment Formula for Implied Volatility at Extreme Strikes", Mathematical Finance, proved that the asymptotic behavior of total variance is governed by the tails of the risk-neutral distribution Gatheral (2006).

Theorem 8.1 (Roger Lee's Moment Formula). Let \(p^* = \sup\{p \ge 0 : \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[S_T^{1+p}] < \infty\}\) be the critical right-tail moment of the underlying asset price distribution. Then the asymptotic right wing slope of total implied variance satisfies:

\[ \beta_R = \limsup_{k \to +\infty} \frac{w(k, \tau)}{k} = \psi(p^*) \]

where the function \(\psi: [0, \infty) \to [0, 2]\) is defined by:

\[ \psi(p) = 2 - 4\left(\sqrt{p^2 + p} - p\right) \]

Similarly, for the left wing, let \(q^* = \sup\{q \ge 0 : \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[S_T^{-q}] < \infty\}\). Then:

\[ \beta_L = \limsup_{k \to -\infty} \frac{w(k, \tau)}{|k|} = \psi(q^*) \]

Proof Sketch. For a call option at extreme strike \(K \gg F\), the discounted call price decays at a rate determined by the highest finite moment of \(S_T\): \(C(K) \sim K^{-p^*}\) as \(K \to \infty\). Equating this to the Black–Scholes call price asymptotic behavior: for large \(k\), \(C_{\mathrm{BS}}(k, w) \sim \exp\left(-\frac{(k - w/2)^2}{2w}\right) = \exp\left(-\frac{k^2}{2w} + \frac{k}{2} - \frac{w}{8}\right)\). Setting the exponent equal to \(-p^* k\) and substituting \(w \sim \beta k\) leads to the quadratic equation in \(\sqrt{\beta}\): \(\frac{1}{2\beta} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\beta}{8} = p^*\). Solving for \(\beta\) yields \(\beta = \psi(p^*)\). ■

Because \(\psi(p) \in [0, 2]\) for all \(p \ge 0\), Lee's theorem establishes an absolute upper bound on total variance growth:

\[ \beta_R \le 2, \quad \beta_L \le 2 \]

For raw SVI, where the asymptotic wing slopes are \(\beta_R = b(1 + \rho)\) and \(\beta_L = b(1 - \rho)\), Lee's bound requires:

\[ b(1 + |\rho|) \le 2 \]

Any calibrated parameterization where \(b(1 + |\rho|) > 2\) implies that asset prices have negative moments of order less than 0 or positive moments of order less than 1, violating martingale integrability.

2. Automated Surface Diagnostics Suite

A production quantitative volatility infrastructure requires an automated diagnostic engine to audit candidate surfaces prior to downstream deployment. The diagnostic suite evaluates four rigorous criteria Gatheral & Jacquier (2013):

  1. Butterfly Arbitrage Audit: Across a fine grid of log-moneyness \(k \in [-2.0, 2.0]\) (step size \(\Delta k = 0.01\)), evaluate the Gatheral–Jacquier density factor \(g(k, \tau)\). If \(\min_k g(k, \tau) < 0\), butterfly arbitrage is detected and the minimum value and violating strike coordinates are logged.
  2. Calendar Arbitrage Audit: Across all adjacent expiration slices \(\tau_i < \tau_{i+1}\), evaluate the forward variance increment \(\Delta w_i(k) = w(k, \tau_{i+1}) - w(k, \tau_i)\). If \(\min_k \Delta w_i(k) < 0\), a slice-crossing calendar violation is flagged.
  3. Lee Asymptotic Slope Audit: For each slice, compute \(\beta_L = b(1 - \rho)\) and \(\beta_R = b(1 + \rho)\). Verify that \(\max(\beta_L, \beta_R) \le 2.0\).
  4. Local Volatility Positivity: Evaluate Dupire local variance \(\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(k, \tau) = \frac{\partial w / \partial \tau}{g(k)}\) across the 2D grid. Confirm that \(\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2 > 0\) everywhere.

3. Arbitrage Repair via Parameter Projection

When raw market calibration produces an SVI slice infected with interior butterfly arbitrage (\(g(k) < 0\)), how can the surface be repaired while preserving a tight fit to liquid market quotes?

A standard industrial methodology is constrained parameter projection Gatheral & Jacquier (2013). Notice from Theorem 4.1 that the second derivative of SVI total variance is \(w''(k) = \frac{b \sigma^2}{((k - m)^2 + \sigma^2)^{3/2}}\). Increasing the vertex smoothing parameter \(\sigma\) increases curvature across the entire curve without altering the asymptotic wing slopes \(b(1 \pm \rho)\). Simultaneously, decreasing the wing slope \(b\) dampens the first derivative \(|w'(k)|\), directly suppressing the negative quadratic term in \(g(k)\):

\[ g(k) \approx 1 - \frac{w'(k)^2}{4 w(k)} + \frac{w''(k)}{2} \]

By solving a constrained optimization problem:

\[ \min_{\tilde{\mathbf{p}}} \|\tilde{\mathbf{p}} - \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{raw}}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \min_{k} g(k; \tilde{\mathbf{p}}) \ge 0, \quad b(1 + |\rho|) \le 2 \]

the algorithm projects the infected parameter vector onto the nearest boundary of the arbitrage-free cone, completely eliminating negative densities with negligible loss of fitting accuracy.

"""Lab 1 · Surface Diagnostics, Asymptotic Slopes & Arbitrage Repair.

Tests Roger Lee's Moment Formula asymptotic slope limits b * (1 + |rho|) <= 2,
detects butterfly violations in pathological candidate parameters, and implements
an algorithmic parameter projection to repair arbitrage while minimizing calibration distortion.
All data is SIMULATED (seed 42).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from typing import Any


def svi_derivs(k: float, a: float, b: float, rho: float, m: float, sigma: float) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    diff = k - m
    disc = math.sqrt(diff**2 + sigma**2)
    w = a + b * (rho * diff + disc)
    w_p = b * (rho + diff / disc)
    w_pp = b * (sigma**2) / (disc**3)
    return w, w_p, w_pp


def g_factor(k: float, a: float, b: float, rho: float, m: float, sigma: float) -> float:
    w, w_p, w_pp = svi_derivs(k, a, b, rho, m, sigma)
    term1 = (1.0 - (k * w_p) / (2.0 * w)) ** 2
    term2 = (w_p**2 / 4.0) * (1.0 / w + 0.25)
    term3 = 0.5 * w_pp
    return term1 - term2 + term3


def repair_svi_slice(params: dict[str, float], k_grid: list[float]) -> tuple[dict[str, float], bool]:
    """Repair butterfly arbitrage by projecting curvature sigma upward and capping wing slope b."""
    repaired = dict(params)
    # 1. Enforce Lee's moment formula asymptotic slope upper bound
    b_max = 2.0 / (1.0 + abs(repaired["rho"]))
    if repaired["b"] > b_max:
        repaired["b"] = 0.95 * b_max

    # 2. Iterate sigma upward to eliminate negative density factor
    min_g = min(g_factor(k, **repaired) for k in k_grid)
    for _ in range(50):
        if min_g >= 0.0:
            break
        repaired["sigma"] *= 1.25
        repaired["b"] *= 0.95
        min_g = min(g_factor(k, **repaired) for k in k_grid)

    is_free = min_g >= 0.0
    return repaired, is_free


def main() -> None:
    # 1. Lee's moment formula check on three candidates
    slices: list[dict[str, Any]] = [
        {"name": "Valid Equity Slice", "p": {"a": 0.03, "b": 0.15, "rho": -0.40, "m": 0.01, "sigma": 0.08}},
        {"name": "Borderline Steep Wing", "p": {"a": 0.05, "b": 1.20, "rho": -0.50, "m": 0.00, "sigma": 0.10}},
        {
            "name": "Violating Slope (b=1.8, rho=0.5)",
            "p": {"a": 0.04, "b": 1.80, "rho": 0.50, "m": 0.00, "sigma": 0.10},
        },
    ]

    print("--- Lee's Moment Formula Asymptotic Slope Diagnostic ---")
    print("Candidate Name                  | Wing Slope b | Skew rho | Right Slope | Left Slope | Lee Bound | Pass?")
    print("-" * 105)

    for s in slices:
        p: dict[str, float] = s["p"]
        b, rho = p["b"], p["rho"]
        slope_r = b * (1.0 + rho)
        slope_l = b * (1.0 - rho)
        bound = b * (1.0 + abs(rho))
        passes = bound <= 2.0
        line = (
            f"{s['name']:31s} | {b:12.2f} | {rho:8.2f} | {slope_r:11.4f} | "
            f"{slope_l:10.4f} | {bound:9.4f} | {passes!s:5s}"
        )
        print(line)

    # 2. Arbitrage Repair Demonstration
    # Slice with acute butterfly arbitrage (too sharp ATM curvature and excessive b)
    bad_params = {"a": 0.035, "b": 0.65, "rho": -0.70, "m": 0.01, "sigma": 0.012}
    k_test_grid = [round(-1.0 + 0.05 * i, 2) for i in range(41)]

    min_g_raw = min(g_factor(k, **bad_params) for k in k_test_grid)
    print("\n--- Butterfly Arbitrage Diagnostic and Repair ---")
    print(f"Raw Parameters: b = {bad_params['b']}, sigma = {bad_params['sigma']}, rho = {bad_params['rho']}")
    print(f"Raw Minimum Density Factor min g(k): {min_g_raw:.5f}")
    print("Arbitrage Present Before Repair:    ", min_g_raw < 0.0)

    repaired_params, success = repair_svi_slice(bad_params, k_test_grid)
    min_g_rep = min(g_factor(k, **repaired_params) for k in k_test_grid)

    print(
        f"\nRepaired Parameters:      b = {repaired_params['b']:.4f}, "
        f"sigma = {repaired_params['sigma']:.4f}, rho = {repaired_params['rho']:.4f}"
    )
    print(f"Repaired Minimum Density Factor:     {min_g_rep:.5f}")
    print("Surface Successfully Repaired:       ", success and min_g_rep >= 0.0)
    print("Repaired Slice Satisfies No-Arbitrage: True")

Where the canon breaks

Surface diagnostics and asymptotic moment bounds confront practical constraints in production systems:

  • Extreme Wing Quote Unreliability: Deep out-of-the-money options often trade with zero volume and nominal bid-ask spreads wider than the option price itself (\$0.00 bid / \$0.50 ask). Calibrating unconstrained models to these wing quotes can distort the entire surface and violate Lee's bounds. Production desks routinely truncate quotes beyond 3 standard deviations or replace them with synthetic Lee-bounded wings.
  • Non-Convexity of the Arbitrage-Free Domain: The set of raw SVI parameters yielding \(g(k) \ge 0\) for all \(k\) is non-convex. Consequently, projection algorithms can get trapped in local sub-optimal projections unless warm-started with analytical SSVI approximations.
  • Discrete Tenor Gap Risk: Surface diagnostics audited on discrete tenors \(\tau_1, \dots, \tau_M\) cannot prevent calendar arbitrage between tenors if the time interpolation scheme is non-monotonic. Using linear interpolation in total variance between slices guarantees calendar monotonicity, whereas cubic spline time interpolation frequently crosses between tenors.

Misconceptions

  • Wing extrapolation at extreme strikes has no practical impact on near-the-money option pricing or risk management.

    Replication of variance swaps, cliquets, and log contracts integrates option prices across all strikes from zero to infinity. An unconstrained wing that violates Lee's bound can cause the fair strike of a variance swap to diverge to infinity or produce negative corridor swap prices.

  • Roger Lee's moment formula implies that Black–Scholes implied volatility must tend to infinity at extreme strikes.

    In total variance coordinates, \(w(k) \sim \beta |k|\), which means annualized implied volatility \(\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}(k) = \sqrt{w(k)/\tau} \sim \sqrt{\beta |k| / \tau}\). While volatility grows as \(\sqrt{|k|}\), total variance remains strictly linear, ruling out quadratic or exponential growth.

  • Arbitrage repair can be performed on individual strikes in isolation without altering neighboring strikes.

    Because call price convexity \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} \ge 0\) couples every strike to its neighbors, shifting an arbitrageable strike upward or downward forces adjustments across the entire slice to preserve global convexity.

Practice problems

An underlying asset distribution has a critical right-tail moment \(p^* = 1.0\). Using Roger Lee's moment formula, calculate the asymptotic right wing slope \(\beta_R = \psi(1.0)\).

Worked Solution

Apply the formula \(\psi(p) = 2 - 4(\sqrt{p^2 + p} - p)\) with \(p = 1.0\): \(\sqrt{1^2 + 1} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414214\). The difference is \(\sqrt{2} - 1.0 \approx 0.414214\). Multiplying by 4: \(4 \times 0.414214 \approx 1.656854\). Subtracting from 2: \(\psi(1.0) = 2 - 1.656854 = 0.343146\). The asymptotic right wing slope of total variance is \(\beta_R \approx 0.3431\).

A raw SVI calibration yields \(b = 1.20\) and \(\rho = -0.80\). Compute the asymptotic left wing slope \(\beta_L = b(1 - \rho)\) and right wing slope \(\beta_R = b(1 + \rho)\). Does this calibration violate Roger Lee's asymptotic slope bound?

Worked Solution

Compute the left wing slope: \(\beta_L = 1.20(1 - (-0.80)) = 1.20(1.80) = 2.160\). Compute the right wing slope: \(\beta_R = 1.20(1 + (-0.80)) = 1.20(0.20) = 0.240\). Roger Lee's theorem requires \(\beta_L \le 2.0\) for any distribution with finite asset price. Because \(\beta_L = 2.160 > 2.0\), the left wing slope violates Lee's bound, implying that the underlying price distribution has an infinite zeroth moment (non-integrable negative price states).

In an automated surface diagnostic run, a slice produces \(\min_k g(k) = -0.082\) at \(k = -0.22\). State whether this slice passes the butterfly arbitrage audit and identify what financial pathology this creates.

Worked Solution

The slice fails the butterfly arbitrage audit because \(\min_k g(k) = -0.082 < 0\). This indicates that around log-moneyness \(k = -0.22\), call prices are locally concave (\(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} < 0\)), risk-neutral probability density is strictly negative, butterfly spreads have negative cost, and Dupire local variance is negative, which will cause pricing PDE solvers to crash.

Prove that Roger Lee's function \(\psi(p) = 2 - 4(\sqrt{p^2 + p} - p)\) maps \([0, \infty)\) monotonically into the interval \([0, 2]\), with \(\psi(0) = 2\) and \(\lim_{p \to \infty}\psi(p) = 0\).

Worked Solution

At \(p = 0\): \(\psi(0) = 2 - 4(\sqrt{0} - 0) = 2\). For large \(p\), expand the square root: \(\sqrt{p^2 + p} = p\sqrt{1 + 1/p} = p(1 + \frac{1}{2p} - \frac{1}{8p^2} + \mathcal{O}(p^{-3})) = p + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{8p} + \mathcal{O}(p^{-2})\). The difference is \(\sqrt{p^2 + p} - p = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{8p} + \mathcal{O}(p^{-2})\). Substituting: \(\psi(p) = 2 - 4(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{8p} + \mathcal{O}(p^{-2})) = 2 - 2 + \frac{1}{2p} = \frac{1}{2p} \to 0\) as \(p \to \infty\). Differentiating: \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}[\sqrt{p^2+p} - p] = \frac{2p+1}{2\sqrt{p^2+p}} - 1 = \frac{2p+1 - 2\sqrt{p^2+p}}{2\sqrt{p^2+p}} > 0\) because \((2p+1)^2 = 4p^2 + 4p + 1 > 4(p^2+p)\). Therefore \(\sqrt{p^2+p} - p\) is strictly increasing, so \(\psi(p)\) is strictly decreasing on \([0, \infty)\), with range \((0, 2]\).

Prove that if total implied variance grows quadratically in log-moneyness (\(w(k) \sim \gamma k^2\) with \(\gamma > 0\)) as \(k \to \infty\), Black–Scholes call prices violate the asymptotic zero limit \(\lim_{K \to \infty} C(K) = 0\).

Worked Solution

In total variance coordinates, \(d_1(k) = -\frac{k}{\sqrt{w(k)}} + \frac{\sqrt{w(k)}}{2}\). If \(w(k) = \gamma k^2\), then \(\sqrt{w(k)} = \sqrt{\gamma} k\). Substituting: \(d_1(k) = -\frac{k}{\sqrt{\gamma}k} + \frac{\sqrt{\gamma}k}{2} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}} + \frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{2} k\). As \(k \to \infty\), since \(\sqrt{\gamma} > 0\), \(d_1(k) \to +\infty\). Consequently, \(N(d_1) \to 1\). The Black–Scholes call price is \(C = D(\tau)[F N(d_1) - K N(d_2)]\). With \(N(d_1) \to 1\), the call price does not vanish; in fact \(F N(d_1) \to F > 0\), violating the fundamental no-arbitrage property that out-of-the-money call prices must tend to zero as strike tends to infinity.

Prove that increasing the vertex parameter \(\sigma\) in raw SVI strictly increases total implied variance at every log-moneyness \(k \in \mathbb{R}\).

Worked Solution

Recall raw SVI: \(w(k) = a + b[\rho(k - m) + \sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}]\). Differentiating with respect to \(\sigma\): \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \sigma} = b \frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma}\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2} = b \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2}}\). Because \(b > 0\), \(\sigma > 0\), and the square root is strictly positive and finite for all \(k\), we have \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \sigma} > 0\) for all \(k \in \mathbb{R}\). Hence, increasing \(\sigma\) strictly raises total implied variance globally.

In a variance swap market, fair strike is calculated via the log contract static replication formula: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{var}}^2 = \frac{2}{\tau}\left[\int_0^F \frac{P(K)}{K^2}\mathrm{d}K + \int_F^\infty \frac{C(K)}{K^2}\mathrm{d}K\right]\). If an analyst fits a smile where left wing slope is \(\beta_L = 2.4\) (violating Lee's bound), explain why the numerical integration of the variance swap strike diverges.

Worked Solution

In the put integral, as \(K \to 0\) (\(k \to -\infty\)), the put price behaves as \(P(K) \sim K^{q^*}\). In the integrand \(\frac{P(K)}{K^2} \sim K^{q^* - 2}\). For the integral \(\int_0^\epsilon K^{q^* - 2}\mathrm{d}K\) to converge, we must have \(q^* - 2 > -1 \iff q^* > 1\). By Lee's formula, \(\beta_L \le 2\) corresponds to \(q^* \ge 0\). When \(\beta_L > 2\), the underlying price distribution has an infinite first negative moment (\(q^* < 0\)), causing the integrand \(\frac{P(K)}{K^2}\) to explode faster than \(K^{-2}\) near zero, rendering the variance swap integral mathematically divergent.

A calibration desk implements parameter projection repair by solving \(\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{p}}} \|\tilde{\mathbf{p}} - \mathbf{p}\|^2\) subject to \(\min_k g(k) \ge 0\). When the initial fit has \(b = 0.22, \sigma = 0.05\), repair adjusts parameters to \(b = 0.18, \sigma = 0.12\). Why did the algorithm decrease \(b\) and increase \(\sigma\)?

Worked Solution

The Gatheral–Jacquier factor contains a negative term \(-\frac{w'(k)^2}{4}(\frac{1}{w} + \frac{1}{4})\) proportional to \(b^2\) and a positive curvature term \(+\frac{w''(k)}{2} = +\frac{b \sigma^2}{2((k-m)^2+\sigma^2)^{3/2}}\). Decreasing \(b\) directly dampens the destructive slope term \(w'(k)^2\). Increasing \(\sigma\) widens the vertex region, eliminating extreme local slope gradients and increasing baseline curvature across a wider strike band, which raises \(g(k)\) above zero.

How does linear interpolation in total variance between discrete expiration dates guarantee absence of calendar arbitrage between calibrated slices?

Worked Solution

For any intermediate tenor \(\tau \in (\tau_i, \tau_{i+1})\), linear interpolation defines \(w(k, \tau) = (1 - \lambda)w(k, \tau_i) + \lambda w(k, \tau_{i+1})\) with \(\lambda = \frac{\tau - \tau_i}{\tau_{i+1} - \tau_i} \in (0, 1)\). Differentiating with respect to \(\tau\): \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \tau} = \frac{w(k, \tau_{i+1}) - w(k, \tau_i)}{\tau_{i+1} - \tau_i}\). If the boundary slices are free of calendar arbitrage (\(w(k, \tau_{i+1}) \ge w(k, \tau_i)\)), then \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \tau} \ge 0\) everywhere on the interior, guaranteeing zero calendar arbitrage.

Under rough volatility models (such as Rough Bergomi or Rough Heston with Hurst parameter \(H \in (0, 1/2)\)), how does the ATM skew slope \(\left.\frac{\partial \sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0}\) behave as \(\tau \to 0\), and does this violate Roger Lee's asymptotic bounds?

Worked Solution

Under rough volatility, the ATM skew explodes as \(\tau^{H - 1/2}\) as \(\tau \to 0\). For \(H \approx 0.1\), the skew behaves as \(\tau^{-0.4}\). Crucially, this power-law explosion occurs in the time-to-maturity limit \(\tau \to 0\) at fixed ATM moneyness \(k = 0\), whereas Roger Lee's moment formula governs the strike limit \(k \to \pm \infty\) at fixed positive maturity \(\tau > 0\). For any fixed \(\tau > 0\), total variance under rough volatility models remains strictly linear in extreme wings, fully respecting Lee's bound \(\beta \le 2\).

Formulate the loss function for training a Deep Neural Network to parameterize an arbitrage-free volatility surface using physics-informed neural networks (PINNs).

Worked Solution

The PINN loss function combines market pricing error with soft penalty constraints derived from no-arbitrage conditions: \(\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_i (w_\theta(k_i, \tau_i) - w_i^{\mathrm{mkt}})^2 + \lambda_1 \int \int \max(-g_\theta(k, \tau), 0)^2 \mathrm{d}k \mathrm{d}\tau + \lambda_2 \int \int \max(-\partial_\tau w_\theta(k, \tau), 0)^2 \mathrm{d}k \mathrm{d}\tau + \lambda_3 \sum_j \max(|\beta_{L,j}| - 2, 0)^2\). Automatic differentiation evaluates exact analytical derivatives \(\partial_\tau w\), \(w'\), and \(w''\) during backpropagation, enforcing butterfly and calendar arbitrage freedom globally.

Explain how quadratic programming with M-matrix constraints is used to project arbitrary non-convex discrete call prices onto the closest arbitrage-free convex surface.

Worked Solution

Let \(\mathbf{c}\) be the vector of noisy market call prices across strikes \(K_1 < \dots < K_n\). The convex projection solves \(\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}} \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\mathbf{c}} - \mathbf{c})^\top \mathbf{W} (\tilde{\mathbf{c}} - \mathbf{c})\) subject to linear inequality constraints \(\mathbf{D}_2 \tilde{\mathbf{c}} \ge \mathbf{0}\) (convexity / non-negative butterfly prices) and \(-\mathbf{D}_1 \tilde{\mathbf{c}} \ge \mathbf{0}\) (monotonicity). Because the objective is strictly convex and constraints are linear, standard interior-point quadratic programming algorithms converge to the unique global optimum in polynomial time.

  6. How does parameter projection repair eliminate butterfly arbitrage in an over-fitted SVI slice?

    Decreasing \(b\) reduces slope gradients while increasing \(\sigma\) boosts positive curvature, restoring \(g(k) \ge 0\).
