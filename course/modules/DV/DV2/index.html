The Volatility Surface

The Black–Scholes assumption of constant volatility across strikes and maturities was shattered by the 1987 crash. In contemporary derivative trading desks, market quotes form a two-dimensional volatility surface across strike and expiration. This module develops the analytical, geometric, and numerical theory of arbitrage-free implied and local volatility surfaces: formalizing strike (butterfly) and calendar static arbitrage conditions, calibrating Gatheral's Stochastic Volatility Inspired (SVI) parameterization, inverting Dupire's partial differential equation to extract unique local volatility surfaces, reconstructing risk-neutral state-price densities via the Breeden–Litzenberger identity, and enforcing Roger Lee's asymptotic moment limits.

  • Prerequisites: DV1 Continuous-Time Arbitrage Pricing. Prerequisite closure includes DV1, FC2, SC3, SC2, SC1, M6, M5, M4, M2, M1, E1, CF1, AC1, and P0.1.
  • Unlocks: DV3 Stochastic & Rough Volatility, DV5 Variance Risk Premium & Volatility Trading, DV6 Exotics & Structured Products, and Capstone Studios.
  • Project: Arbitrage-free surface builder: Calibrating SVI slices with no-butterfly and no-calendar constraints, extracting Dupire local volatility, and verifying absence of static arbitrage on a dense grid. Graded by autograder (with rubric and exemplars).
  • Data: Simulated option chains across multiple expiries and strikes labeled SIMULATED.

56 h lessons · 24 h project (30% of the 80 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV2 you can:

  1. Diagnose the breakdown of the Black–Scholes constant volatility model across global asset classes, and transform market quotes into total implied variance coordinates \(w(k, \tau) = \sigma_{\text{BS}}^2 \tau\) (Lesson 1).
  2. Formulate the non-negativity and convexity conditions preventing butterfly (strike) arbitrage, and calculate the Gatheral-Jacquier density factor \(g(k)\) (Lesson 2).
  3. Characterize the monotonicity requirement \(\partial_\tau w \ge 0\) preventing calendar arbitrage, and construct arbitrage trades exploiting calendar spread inversions (Lesson 3).
  4. Calibrate Gatheral's raw and natural SVI parameterizations to empirical slices using quasi-explicit optimization while strictly enforcing admissibility constraints (Lesson 4).
  5. Derive and implement Bruno Dupire's continuous local volatility equation, proving the exact connection between local variance, calendar spread derivatives, and the butterfly density factor (Lesson 5).
  6. Extract risk-neutral state-price densities via the Breeden–Litzenberger formula, and verify risk-neutral moment identities under \(\mathbb{Q}\) (Lesson 6).
  7. Strip discrete forward implied volatilities across adjacent maturities, and explain why local volatility models predict unphysical forward skew flattening (Lesson 7).
  8. Apply Roger Lee's Moment Formula to bound asymptotic wing slopes \(b(1+|\rho|) \le 2\), and repair butterfly violations via parameter projection onto the non-negative density set (Lesson 8).

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite derivatives mastery and core surface builder skills. Questions 1 through 8 test prerequisites from DV1, FC2, SC3, and SC2. Questions 9 and 10 are skill checks testing Gatheral-Jacquier density factor evaluation and Dupire local volatility extraction taught in project rungs 1 and 2. Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold (at least 8 correct out of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Black–Scholes model, what is the theoretical dependence of implied volatility \(\sigma_{\text{BS}}\) on the strike price \(K\)?

    Options

    By definition, the Black–Scholes model assumes geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility \(\sigma\). Hence, under the null hypothesis of the model, implied volatility is strictly constant across all strikes.

  2. What does put–call parity require regarding the implied volatilities of a European call and European put with identical strike \(K\) and maturity \(\tau\)?

    Options

    Since put–call parity \(C - P = S - K e^{-r\tau}\) holds identically for the Black–Scholes pricing formula with any volatility parameter \(\sigma\), the unique implied volatility that prices the call must equal the implied volatility that prices the put.

  3. If the European call price function \(C(K)\) is concave on an interval (\(\partial^2 C / \partial K^2 < 0\)), what arbitrage trade can be constructed?

    Options

    A butterfly spread has non-negative terminal payoff \(\max(0, S_T - K_1) - 2\max(0, S_T - K_2) + \max(0, S_T - K_3) \ge 0\). Concavity implies \(C(K_1) - 2C(K_2) + C(K_3) < 0\), meaning buying the butterfly generates a cash credit upfront with zero downside.

  4. In total implied variance coordinates \(w(k, \tau) = \sigma^2 \tau\), what condition guarantees the absence of calendar arbitrage?

    Options

    Calendar arbitrage is ruled out if and only if total implied variance is non-decreasing in maturity for all log-moneyness strikes: \(\partial_\tau w(k, \tau) \ge 0\).

  5. What is the Breeden–Litzenberger relationship connecting the risk-neutral probability density \(q(S_T)\) to European call prices?

    Options

    Differentiating \(C(K) = e^{-r\tau} \int_K^\infty (S_T - K) q(S_T) dS_T\) twice with respect to strike \(K\) gives \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} = e^{-r\tau} q(K)\), so \(q(K) = e^{r\tau} \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\).

  6. Under Itô's formula, if \(S_t\) follows a diffusion \(\mathrm{d}S_t = \mu_t S_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma(S_t, t) S_t \mathrm{d}W_t\), what is the quadratic variation \(\mathrm{d}\langle S \rangle_t\)?

    Options

    By SC2 (Itô's lemma), \((\mathrm{d}W_t)^2 = \mathrm{d}t\) and \((\mathrm{d}t)^2 = \mathrm{d}t \mathrm{d}W_t = 0\), yielding \(\mathrm{d}\langle S \rangle_t = \sigma^2(S_t, t) S_t^2 \mathrm{d}t\).

  7. How does Girsanov's theorem change the drift of an asset price process when transforming from the physical measure \(\mathbb{P}\) to the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\)?

    Options

    By SC3 (Change of Measure), an equivalent measure transformation alters only the drift of Brownian motion by translating \(\mathrm{d}W_t^{\mathbb{P}} = \mathrm{d}W_t^{\mathbb{Q}} - \lambda_t \mathrm{d}t\), leaving diffusion volatility \(\sigma\) invariant.

  8. What is the pathwise Gamma–Theta P&L identity proven in DV1 Lesson 7?

    Options

    Delta hedging eliminates directional market delta, leaving instantaneous P&L driven by the spread between realized and implied variance scaled by dollar gamma: \(\frac{1}{2} \Gamma_t S_t^2 (\sigma_R^2 - \sigma_I^2) \mathrm{d}t\).

  9. Skill check 1: In Gatheral's SVI parameterization, what is the Gatheral-Jacquier density factor \(g(k)\) that guarantees absence of butterfly arbitrage when \(g(k) \ge 0\)?

    Options

    By Gatheral & Jacquier (2014), the Breeden–Litzenberger density expressed in total variance coordinates \(w(k)\) contains the multiplicative factor \(g(k) = (1 - \frac{k w'}{2w})^2 - \frac{(w')^2}{4}(\frac{1}{w} + \frac{1}{4}) + \frac{w''}{2}\). Absence of butterfly arbitrage requires \(g(k) \ge 0\).

  10. Skill check 2: Under Dupire's formula expressed in total implied variance \(w(k, \tau)\) and density factor \(g(k)\), what is the analytical expression for local variance \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(k, \tau)\)?

    Options

    Dupire's equation transforms into total variance coordinates as \(\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(k, \tau) = \frac{\partial_\tau w}{g(k, \tau)}\), demonstrating that local variance is strictly positive if and only if there is neither calendar arbitrage (\(\partial_\tau w \ge 0\)) nor butterfly arbitrage (\(g(k) > 0\)).
