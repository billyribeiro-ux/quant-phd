Variance Swaps & Log-Contract Replication

Standard options tie volatility exposure to underlying price path direction through path-dependent Greeks. A variance swap isolates pure quadratic variation without requiring continuous dynamic delta rebalancing. By synthesizing a synthetic log contract through a static continuum of out-of-the-money options weighted inversely by strike squared, traders can statically replicate and price forward variance under continuous diffusions, while exposing unhedged residual risks under price jumps.

  • Introduces: variance-swap, variance-static-replication
  • Prerequisites: volatility-smile, risk-neutral-density, delta-hedging

1. Variance Swap Contract Mechanics & Payoff Definition

A standard option position provides non-linear volatility exposure (\(\mathcal{V} = \partial V / \partial \sigma\)), but this exposure is highly path-dependent and decays as spot moves away from the strike. In contrast, variance derivatives deliver linear exposure to realized quadratic variation over the contract tenor.

Definition 1.1 (Variance Swap). A variance swap is an over-the-counter forward contract on the annualized realized variance of an underlying asset over horizon \([0, T]\). The holder receives floating realized variance \(RV(0, T)\) in exchange for paying a fixed strike \(K_{\text{var}}\), with settlement payoff at expiration \(T\) given by:

\[ V_T = N_{\text{var}} \left(RV(0, T) - K_{\text{var}}\right) \]

where \(N_{\text{var}}\) is the variance notional expressed in currency units per variance point (\((\Delta \sigma^2 = 0.0001)\) or percentage point squared).

In market quoting conventions documented by Demeterfi, Derman, Kamal & Zou (1999), realized variance is calculated from discrete daily closing log-returns with annualization factor \(A = 252\):

\[ RV(0, T) = \frac{A}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left(\ln\frac{S_i}{S_{i-1}}\right)^2 \]

where \(N\) is the number of observation trading days and \(S_i\) is the official closing price on trading day \(i\). Institutional participants typically trade in terms of vega notional \(N_{\text{vega}}\), defined as the contract P&L sensitivity to a 1% shift in volatility when realized volatility equals the strike volatility \(\sigma_0 = \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}\):

\[ N_{\text{var}} = \frac{N_{\text{vega}}}{2 \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}} = \frac{N_{\text{vega}}}{2 \sigma_0} \]

Example 1.1 (Variance vs. Vega Notional). Consider a 3-month variance swap on the S&P 500 with vega notional \(N_{\text{vega}} = \$100{,}000\) and volatility strike \(\sigma_0 = 20\%\) (\(K_{\text{var}} = 0.0400\)). The variance notional is \(N_{\text{var}} = \frac{\$100{,}000}{2 \times 0.20} = \$250{,}000\) per unit of variance (or \(\$25\) per variance point squared). If realized volatility over the quarter settles at \(25\%\) (\(RV = 0.0625\)), the payoff to the long side is:

\[ V_T = \$250{,}000 \times (0.0625 - 0.0400) = \$250{,}000 \times 0.0225 = \$5{,}625 \]

Notice that while the linear volatility increase is \(+5\%\), the quadratic payoff reflects \((25^2 - 20^2) = 225\) variance points, producing a convexity gain over a linear volatility contract.

2. The Log Contract & Static Replication Theory

The foundation of model-free volatility replication is Neuberger's (1994) log contract, formalized for continuous diffusions by Demeterfi et al. (1999).

Theorem 1.1 (Itô Log-Contract Variance Identity). Let the underlying asset price follow a continuous semimartingale diffusion \(dS_t / S_t = \mu_t dt + \sigma_t dW_t\) with continuous sample paths. Then the total accumulated quadratic variation of log returns \(\int_0^T \sigma_t^2 dt\) is exactly synthesized by holding a log contract and dynamically delta-hedging with \(1/S_t\) shares:

\[ \int_0^T \sigma_t^2\,dt = 2 \left[ \int_0^T \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - \ln\left(\frac{S_T}{S_0}\right) \right] \]

Proof. Applying Itô's formula to the twice-differentiable function \(f(S) = \ln S\) with \(f'(S) = 1/S\) and \(f''(S) = -1/S^2\):

\[ d\ln S_t = \frac{1}{S_t} dS_t + \frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{S_t^2}\right) d\langle S\rangle_t = \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_t^2\,dt \]

Rearranging terms to isolate the continuous variance increment \(\sigma_t^2 dt\):

\[ \sigma_t^2\,dt = 2\left(\frac{dS_t}{S_t} - d\ln S_t\right) \]

Integrating both sides from \(t = 0\) to \(t = T\) yields the exact identity:

\[ \int_0^T \sigma_t^2\,dt = 2 \int_0^T \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - 2\left(\ln S_T - \ln S_0\right) = 2 \int_0^T \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - 2 \ln\left(\frac{S_T}{S_0}\right) \]

Taking conditional expectations under the risk-neutral pricing measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), the stochastic integral \(\int_0^T \frac{dS_t}{S_t}\) has expectation \(r T\), establishing that the risk-neutral expected variance is determined entirely by the valuation of the terminal log payoff \(-\ln(S_T / S_0)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Definition 1.2 (Static Replication of Variance). The static replication of variance (log contract) is the synthesis of the terminal log payoff \(-\ln(S_T / S_*)\) via a forward contract and a static continuum of out-of-the-money European put and call options weighted inversely by strike squared (\(1/K^2\)), eliminating underlying model assumptions under continuous price paths.

Theorem 1.2 (Carr–Madan Static Option Decomposition). For any arbitrary reference strike cutoff \(S_* > 0\), the terminal log payoff decomposes into linear forward exposure and a weighted portfolio of European puts and calls:

\[ -\ln\left(\frac{S_T}{S_*}\right) = -\frac{S_T - S_*}{S_*} + \int_0^{S_*} \frac{1}{K^2}(K - S_T)^+\,dK + \int_{S_*}^\infty \frac{1}{K^2}(S_T - K)^+\,dK \]

Proof. By Taylor's theorem with second-order integral remainder, any twice continuously differentiable payoff \(g(S_T)\) evaluated at \(S_*\) satisfies:

\[ g(S_T) = g(S_*) + g'(S_*)(S_T - S_*) + \int_0^{S_*} g''(K)(K - S_T)^+\,dK + \int_{S_*}^\infty g''(K)(S_T - K)^+\,dK \]

Setting \(g(S) = -\ln(S / S_*)\), we calculate the derivatives: \(g(S_*) = -\ln(1) = 0\), \(g'(S) = -1/S \implies g'(S_*) = -1/S_*\), and \(g''(K) = 1/K^2\). Substituting these directly into Taylor's formula completes the proof. \(\blacksquare\)

Combining Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), the theoretical fair variance swap strike \(K_{\text{var}} = \frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\int_0^T \sigma_t^2 dt\right]\) is given by:

\[ K_{\text{var}} = \frac{2}{T}\left[ r T - \left(\frac{S_0 e^{r T}}{S_*} - 1\right) - \ln\left(\frac{S_*}{S_0}\right) \right] + \frac{2}{T} e^{r T} \left[ \int_0^{S_*} \frac{1}{K^2} P(K, T)\,dK + \int_{S_*}^\infty \frac{1}{K^2} C(K, T)\,dK \right] \]

When the reference strike is set to the forward price \(S_* = F_0 = S_0 e^{r T}\), the linear and log drift terms simplify, leaving:

\[ K_{\text{var}} = \frac{2}{T} e^{r T} \left[ \int_0^{F_0} \frac{1}{K^2} P(K, T)\,dK + \int_{F_0}^\infty \frac{1}{K^2} C(K, T)\,dK \right] \]

3. Inline Lab: Discrete Grid Error & Strike Truncation

In market reality, options trade at discrete strike increments \(\Delta K\) and over a finite strike range \([K_{\min}, K_{\max}]\). In this lab, we measure the replication error between the continuous theoretical variance and the discrete option portfolio.

Lab 1 · Variance swap static replication & discrete grid error

Simulating static log-contract replication across strike spacings dK in [0.5, 10.0] and truncation windows demonstrates that discrete trapezoidal error is negligible (<1 bps) for dK <= 2.5, but strike truncation within [80, 120] creates a significant negative variance under-replication of -4.69 bps due to missing tail wings.

"""Lab 1 · Variance Swap Static Replication & Discrete Grid Error.

This lab implements the Demeterfi, Derman, Kamal & Zou (1999) static replication
of the log contract using a portfolio of out-of-the-money European puts and calls
weighted inversely by strike squared (1/K^2). Evaluates continuous theoretical
variance against discrete option portfolios across varying strike spacings dK
and strike cutoffs [K_min, K_max] on seed 42 (SIMULATED).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math


def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    """Standard normal cumulative distribution function."""
    return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))


def bs_call_price(s: float, k: float, r: float, sigma: float, t: float) -> float:
    """Analytical Black-Scholes call price."""
    if t <= 1e-12 or sigma <= 1e-12:
        return max(0.0, s - k * math.exp(-r * t))
    d1 = (math.log(s / k) + (r + 0.5 * sigma * sigma) * t) / (sigma * math.sqrt(t))
    d2 = d1 - sigma * math.sqrt(t)
    return s * norm_cdf(d1) - k * math.exp(-r * t) * norm_cdf(d2)


def bs_put_price(s: float, k: float, r: float, sigma: float, t: float) -> float:
    """Analytical Black-Scholes put price."""
    if t <= 1e-12 or sigma <= 1e-12:
        return max(0.0, k * math.exp(-r * t) - s)
    d1 = (math.log(s / k) + (r + 0.5 * sigma * sigma) * t) / (sigma * math.sqrt(t))
    d2 = d1 - sigma * math.sqrt(t)
    return k * math.exp(-r * t) * norm_cdf(-d2) - s * norm_cdf(-d1)


def replicate_variance(
    s0: float,
    r: float,
    sigma: float,
    t: float,
    k_min: float,
    k_max: float,
    dk: float,
) -> float:
    """Discrete static replication of annualized variance using 1/K^2 weighted options."""
    forward = s0 * math.exp(r * t)
    s_star = forward  # ATM cutoff strike

    # Discrete strike grid
    n_strikes = round((k_max - k_min) / dk) + 1
    strikes = [k_min + i * dk for i in range(n_strikes)]

    integral_puts = 0.0
    integral_calls = 0.0

    for k in strikes:
        if k < s_star:
            # Out-of-the-money put
            p = bs_put_price(s0, k, r, sigma, t)
            integral_puts += (dk / (k * k)) * p
        elif k > s_star:
            # Out-of-the-money call
            c = bs_call_price(s0, k, r, sigma, t)
            integral_calls += (dk / (k * k)) * c

    # Demeterfi et al. (1999) fair variance swap strike formula:
    # K_var = (2 / T) * exp(r * T) * [int_0^S* (1/K^2) P(K) dK + int_S*^inf (1/K^2) C(K) dK]
    k_var = (2.0 / t) * math.exp(r * t) * (integral_puts + integral_calls)
    return k_var


def main() -> None:
    print("=== Demeterfi et al. (1999) Variance Swap Static Replication ===")
    s0 = 100.0
    r = 0.03
    sigma = 0.20
    t = 0.25  # 3-month variance swap
    true_var = sigma * sigma  # 0.040000

    print(f"Underlying S0={s0:.1f}, r={r:.4f}, True Flat Vol={sigma:.4f}, True Variance={true_var:.6f}, T={t:.2f} yr")

    print("\n1. Impact of Strike Spacing dK (K_min=20, K_max=250):")
    print("   dK | Replicated Var | Error (bps) | Replicated Vol ")
    print("-----------------------------------------------------")
    for dk in [0.5, 1.0, 2.5, 5.0, 10.0]:
        rep_var = replicate_variance(s0, r, sigma, t, k_min=20.0, k_max=250.0, dk=dk)
        err_bps = (rep_var - true_var) * 10000.0
        rep_vol = math.sqrt(max(0.0, rep_var))
        print(f" {dk:4.1f} |    {rep_var:10.6f} |  {err_bps:10.4f} |    {rep_vol:10.4f} ")

    print("\n2. Impact of Strike Truncation Bounds (dK=1.0):")
    print(" [K_min, K_max] | Replicated Var | Truncation Error (bps) ")
    print("----------------------------------------------------------")
    truncations = [
        (80.0, 120.0),
        (60.0, 150.0),
        (40.0, 180.0),
        (20.0, 220.0),
        (10.0, 300.0),
    ]
    for k_min, k_max in truncations:
        rep_var = replicate_variance(s0, r, sigma, t, k_min=k_min, k_max=k_max, dk=1.0)
        err_bps = (rep_var - true_var) * 10000.0
        print(f" [{k_min:5.1f}, {k_max:5.1f}] |    {rep_var:10.6f} |        {err_bps:12.4f} ")

4. Where the Canon Breaks: Jumps and Price Discontinuities

The static replication of variance rests decisively on the assumption of continuous price paths. In the presence of discrete jumps, the canon breaks down completely.

Theorem 1.3 (Jump-Diffusion Replication Leakage). Suppose the underlying asset price follows a jump diffusion with jump multiplier \(J = S_t / S_{t^-}\). Then the difference between realized quadratic variation \([x, x]_T\) and the log contract static replication payoff is:

\[ \Delta \Pi_{\text{jump}} = 2 \sum_{0 < t \le T} \left[ (J - 1) - \ln J - \frac{1}{2}(\ln J)^2 \right] \]

For downward market crashes (\(J < 1\)), \(\Delta \Pi_{\text{jump}} > 0\), meaning true realized variance strictly exceeds the option replication portfolio payoff.

Proof sketch. For a jump of size \(J\), the discrete price return is \(\Delta S / S_{t^-} = J - 1\), while the log return is \(\Delta \ln S = \ln J\). Quadratic variation measures the sum of squared log jumps: \(\sum (\ln J)^2\). Meanwhile, the dynamically hedged log contract generates payoff \(2 \sum [(J - 1) - \ln J]\). Subtracting the quadratic jump variation from the log payoff yields the residual discrepancy:

\[ \mathcal{R}(J) = 2(J - 1 - \ln J) - (\ln J)^2 \]

Performing a Taylor expansion of \(\mathcal{R}(J)\) around \(J = 1\) (where \(x = \ln J\)):

\[ 2(e^x - 1 - x) - x^2 = 2\left(1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^3}{6} + \mathcal{O}(x^4) - 1 - x\right) - x^2 = \frac{1}{3}x^3 + \mathcal{O}(x^4) = \frac{1}{3}(\ln J)^3 + \mathcal{O}((\ln J)^4) \]

When the asset crashes (\(J < 1 \implies \ln J < 0\)), the term \((\ln J)^2\) (which variance swaps pay) is strictly positive, but the dynamic hedge \(2(J - 1 - \ln J)\) fails to keep pace, leaving an unhedged shortfall. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 1.1 (Jump Premium & Crash Asymmetry). Because index crashes are discontinuous down-jumps, a short-variance desk hedging with the static log portfolio is short unhedged jump risk. Market makers demand an additional jump risk premium, forcing the market variance swap strike \(K_{\text{var}}\) substantially higher than expected continuous diffusive variance.

5. Misconceptions

  • A variance swap and a volatility swap have the same payoff up to taking a square root of the final P&L.

    Why it is wrong: Jensen's inequality implies \(\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{RV}] < \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[RV]}\). A volatility swap pays \(\sqrt{RV} - K_{\text{vol}}\) and cannot be statically replicated using vanilla options; its valuation requires full dynamic modeling of volatility-of-volatility. In contrast, a variance swap pays linear realized variance \(RV - K_{\text{var}}\) and admits exact model-free static replication via the log contract.

  • Static replication of variance works for any asset price process, regardless of whether jumps or halts occur.

    Why it is wrong: As proved in Theorem 1.3, static replication via European options exactly equals realized quadratic variation only when sample paths are continuous. Price gaps, earnings jumps, or overnight market halts introduce higher-order residual terms \(\frac{1}{3}(\ln J)^3\) that leave the static portfolio unhedged.

  • The 1/K^2 weighting in the log contract means out-of-the-money call options contribute the most to the fair variance strike.

    Why it is wrong: Because the weighting kernel is \(1/K^2\), out-of-the-money put options with small strikes \(K \ll S_*\) receive vastly larger weights than out-of-the-money calls with large strikes \(K \gg S_*\). For example, a strike at \(K = 50\) has four times the weight of a strike at \(K = 100\), making the fair variance strike exquisitely sensitive to the pricing of deep out-of-the-money put options.

6. Problem Set

Mechanics

A desk enters a 6-month variance swap with vega notional \(N_{\text{vega}} = \$250{,}000\) and volatility strike \(\sigma_0 = 18\%\). Over the 126 trading days, the sum of daily squared log returns is \(\sum_{i=1}^{126} (\ln(S_i / S_{i-1}))^2 = 0.0250\). Calculate: (a) the variance notional \(N_{\text{var}}\), (b) the annualized realized variance \(RV\), and (c) the net dollar settlement P&L to the long variance position.

Solution

(a) The variance strike is \(K_{\text{var}} = 0.18^2 = 0.0324\). The variance notional is \(N_{\text{var}} = \frac{N_{\text{vega}}}{2 \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}} = \frac{\$250{,}000}{2 \times 0.18} = \$694{,}444.44\) per unit of variance.

(b) Annualized realized variance is \(RV = \frac{252}{126} \times 0.0250 = 2.0 \times 0.0250 = 0.0500\) (realized volatility \(\sqrt{0.0500} \approx 22.36\%\)).

(c) The settlement P&L is \(V_T = N_{\text{var}} \times (RV - K_{\text{var}}) = \$694{,}444.44 \times (0.0500 - 0.0324) = \$694{,}444.44 \times 0.0176 = +\$12{,}222.22\).

Calculate the static replication weights \(w(K)\) for three European options with strikes \(K = 80\), \(K = 100\), and \(K = 125\) under a reference forward \(F_0 = 100\). Express the weights as numerical multipliers of \(dK\).

Solution

Under the Carr–Madan static replication theorem, the weight on each option is \(w(K) = \frac{1}{K^2}\). For \(K = 80\), \(w(80) = \frac{1}{6400} = 0.00015625\). For \(K = 100\), \(w(100) = \frac{1}{10000} = 0.00010000\). For \(K = 125\), \(w(125) = \frac{1}{15625} = 0.00006400\). The 80-strike put receives 2.44 times the weight of the 125-strike call.

An underlying asset trades at \(S_0 = 100\). Over a single trading period, it experiences a discontinuous drop to \(S_T = 80\). Compute the exact percentage difference between the quadratic variation of the log return \((\ln(S_T / S_0))^2\) and the log contract static payoff \(2\left(\frac{S_T - S_0}{S_0} - \ln\frac{S_T}{S_0}\right)\).

Solution

Here \(J = 0.80\). The log return is \(\ln(0.80) \approx -0.223144\). Quadratic variation is \((\ln 0.80)^2 \approx (-0.223144)^2 = 0.049793\). The log payoff is \(2\left((0.80 - 1) - \ln 0.80\right) = 2(-0.20 - (-0.223144)) = 2(0.023144) = 0.046287\). The quadratic variation exceeds the static portfolio payoff by \(0.049793 - 0.046287 = 0.003506\) (approximately \(7.1\%\) unhedged shortfall), illustrating jump leakage.

Proof

Prove that for any continuous diffusion \(dS_t / S_t = \mu_t dt + \sigma_t dW_t\), the dynamic hedging position in the underlying stock required to replicate the log contract is exactly \(\Delta_t = \frac{1}{S_t}\) shares at all times \(t \in [0, T]\).

Solution

Consider the portfolio \(V_t = -\ln(S_t / S_0) + \int_0^t \Delta_u dS_u\). By Itô's lemma, \(d(-\ln S_t) = -\frac{1}{S_t} dS_t + \frac{1}{2 S_t^2} d\langle S\rangle_t = -\frac{1}{S_t} dS_t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_t^2 dt\). For the portfolio increment to be purely exposed to variance without directional stock risk, we must have \(dV_t = d(-\ln S_t) + \Delta_t dS_t = (\Delta_t - \frac{1}{S_t}) dS_t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_t^2 dt\). Setting the coefficient of \(dS_t\) to zero requires \(\Delta_t = \frac{1}{S_t}\). Hence, holding \(1/S_t\) shares of stock exactly neutralizes delta, leaving pure quadratic variation.

Prove that Jensen's inequality implies that the fair strike of a volatility swap \(K_{\text{vol}}\) is strictly less than the square root of the fair strike of a variance swap \(K_{\text{var}}\), and derive the leading-order Taylor correction in terms of the variance-of-variance.

Solution

Under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb{Q}\), \(K_{\text{var}} = \mathbb{E}[RV]\) and \(K_{\text{vol}} = \mathbb{E}[\sqrt{RV}]\). Because \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\) is strictly concave for \(x > 0\), Jensen's inequality guarantees \(\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{RV}] < \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[RV]}\). Expanding \(\sqrt{RV}\) in a second-order Taylor series around \(\mathbb{E}[RV] = K_{\text{var}}\): \(\sqrt{RV} \approx \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}}(RV - K_{\text{var}}) - \frac{1}{8 K_{\text{var}}^{3/2}}(RV - K_{\text{var}})^2\). Taking expectations yields \(K_{\text{vol}} \approx \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}} - \frac{\mathrm{Var}(RV)}{8 K_{\text{var}}^{3/2}}\). The convexity adjustment is proportional to the variance of realized variance.

Prove that if the volatility smile is flat (\(\sigma(K) = \sigma\) for all \(K\)), the Carr–Madan option static replication integral reproduces the Black–Scholes variance \(K_{\text{var}} = \sigma^2\) exactly.

Solution

Under Black–Scholes with constant volatility \(\sigma\), European call and put prices solve the Black–Scholes PDE. Integrating the Carr–Madan static replication formula: \(\frac{2}{T} e^{r T} \left[ \int_0^{F_0} \frac{P(K)}{K^2} dK + \int_{F_0}^\infty \frac{C(K)}{K^2} dK \right]\). Inverting the order of integration and using Breeden–Litzenberger \(\partial^2 C / \partial K^2 = e^{-r T} q(K)\), integration by parts twice yields the expectation \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[ -2\ln(S_T / F_0) \right]\). Under Black–Scholes, \(\ln(S_T / F_0) = -\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 T + \sigma W_T^{\mathbb{Q}}\). Taking the expectation, \(\mathbb{E}[-2\ln(S_T / F_0)] = -2(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 T) = \sigma^2 T\). Dividing by \(T\) gives exactly \(K_{\text{var}} = \sigma^2\).

Empirical

In a study of historical S&P 500 variance swaps, an analyst compares the fair variance strike \(K_{\text{var}}\) derived from option prices with the realized variance \(RV\) measured 30 days later. Across 20 years, \(\mathbb{E}[K_{\text{var}}] = 0.0450\) (\(21.2\%\) vol) while \(\mathbb{E}[RV] = 0.0280\) (\(16.7\%\) vol). State the empirical implications of this spread for the profitability of selling variance swaps, and identify the primary risk factor that prevents arbitrage.

Solution

The persistent positive spread \(\mathbb{E}[K_{\text{var}} - RV] = 0.0170\) (170 variance points, or \(\sim 4.5\) volatility points) indicates that systematically selling variance swaps generates positive average returns. However, this is not an arbitrage: variance sellers face catastrophic left-tail risk during market crashes (such as October 2008, February 2018, or March 2020), where realized variance explodes by several hundred percent, wiping out years of accumulated carry.

Using the results from Lab 1, calculate the percentage error in replicated variance caused by truncating the strike grid to \([K_{\min}, K_{\max}] = [80, 120]\) versus \([20, 220]\) when the true underlying volatility is \(20\%\). Explain why institutional desks require strike ranges down to \(K = 0.20 \times F_0\).

Solution

From Lab 1, true variance is \(0.040000\). The \([80, 120]\) truncated grid replicates variance as \(0.039531\), an underestimation of \(4.69\) bps (\(1.17\%\) relative error). The \([20, 220]\) grid achieves \(0.039992\), reducing the error to \(0.08\) bps. Institutional desks require deep out-of-the-money puts because the \(1/K^2\) kernel assigns immense weight to downside strikes; omitting them leads to substantial mispricing and unhedged crash exposure.

Analyze how the bid-ask spread of out-of-the-money put options affects the cost of assembling the static replication portfolio. If the half-spread on OTM puts averages \(\$0.15\) across 40 strikes with average \(K = 3500\) and \(\Delta K = 25\), estimate the total round-trip friction in variance points.

Solution

The transaction cost contribution from the half-spread is \(\Delta K_{\text{cost}} = \frac{2}{T} e^{r T} \sum_i \frac{\Delta K_i}{K_i^2} (\text{spread}_i)\). With \(\Delta K = 25\), \(K \approx 3500\), and spread \(= 0.15\) across 40 strikes, the total sum is approximately \(40 \times \frac{25}{(3500)^2} \times 0.15 \approx 40 \times (2.04 \times 10^{-6}) \times 0.15 \approx 1.22 \times 10^{-5}\). Over a 30-day tenor (\(T = 0.0822\)), the friction is \(\frac{2}{0.0822} \times 1.22 \times 10^{-5} \approx 0.00030\) (or \(3.0\) variance points, approximately \(0.35\%\) in volatility terms), representing a meaningful hurdle for high-turnover static rebalancing.

How does the choice between standard return variance \(RV = \frac{252}{N}\sum (\ln(S_i / S_{i-1}))^2\) and zero-mean variance \(RV_0 = \frac{252}{N}\sum (\ln(S_i / S_{i-1}) - \bar{r})^2\) affect contract valuation? Cite Demeterfi et al. (1999) and evaluate the drift bias.

Solution

As analyzed by Demeterfi et al. (1999, p. 11), standard OTC variance swaps specify uncentered returns (\(\bar{r} = 0\)). Subtracting the sample mean \(\bar{r}\) introduces a small downward bias equal to \(\frac{252}{N} \bar{r}^2 \approx \mu^2 / 252\), which is typically less than \(0.1\) variance points for index returns. More importantly, uncentered variance matches quadratic variation \([x, x]_T\) directly, whereas centered variance introduces cross-product terms that distort model-free static replication.

Propose a corridor variance swap contract that pays quadratic variation only while the underlying spot remains inside a range \([S_{\text{lower}}, S_{\text{upper}}]\). What option portfolio replicates a corridor variance swap?

Solution

A corridor variance swap pays \(\int_0^T \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{1}_{\{S_{\text{lower}} \le S_t \le S_{\text{upper}}\}} dt\). Replicating it requires a modified log contract truncated at the corridor boundaries, supplemented by binary options and double-barrier options to manage boundary discontinuities. The static option weights remain \(1/K^2\) inside \((S_{\text{lower}}, S_{\text{upper}})\), but concentrated gamma positions are required at the barriers, creating substantial gamma risk if spot hovers near the boundary.

Evaluate the impact of market close auction price discovery on daily realized variance. Why do institutional variance swap contracts explicitly specify the official exchange closing price rather than a 4:00 PM consolidated tape midpoint?

Solution

Official exchange closing prices (such as the NYSE Closing Auction or Nasdaq Closing Cross) concentrate deep terminal liquidity and reflect centralized, executable clearing prices. Specifying consolidated tape midpoints would expose the settlement calculation to bid-ask bounce, odd-lot microstructure noise, and timing misalignments between cash equities and index derivative markets. Using the closing auction eliminates microstructure bounce bias in daily realized variance calculations.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What is the exact weighting applied to an out-of-the-money European option of strike \(K\) in the static replication of the continuous log contract?

    Options

    Taylor's expansion of the log payoff \(-\ln(S / S_*)\) has second derivative \(g''(K) = 1 / K^2\). Hence each option is weighted by \(1 / K^2\).

  2. How does vega notional \(N_{\text{vega}}\) relate to variance notional \(N_{\text{var}}\) for a contract with volatility strike \(\sigma_0\)?

    Options

    Because \(d(\sigma^2) = 2 \sigma d\sigma\), a 1-point change in volatility produces P&L \(N_{\text{var}} \times 2 \sigma = N_{\text{vega}}\), so \(N_{\text{var}} = N_{\text{vega}} / (2\sigma_0)\).

  3. Under Itô's lemma, what dynamic position in the underlying asset is required to delta-hedge a continuous log contract?

    Options

    Differentiating \(-\ln S_t\) yields \(-dS_t / S_t\). To neutralize delta, the trader must hold \(+1/S_t\) shares dynamically.

  4. If the underlying asset experiences a sudden downward crash jump (\(J < 1\)), what happens to the static replication hedge?

    Options

    By Theorem 1.3, the residual jump error \(\mathcal{R}(J) \approx \frac{1}{3}(\ln J)^3\) leaves realized return variance higher than the synthesized log contract payoff during market crashes.

  5. Why does truncating the option strike grid to a narrow range \([0.80 F_0, 1.20 F_0]\) underestimate fair variance?

    Options

    Because \(1/K^2\) grows rapidly as \(K \to 0\), omitting deep out-of-the-money puts truncates substantial positive variance contributions from the left tail.

  6. What annualization factor is standardly specified in institutional equity index variance swaps?

    Options

    Standard OTC equity variance swaps use \(A = 252\) trading days per year for daily return sampling.

  7. How does Jensen's inequality relate the fair strike of a volatility swap \(K_{\text{vol}}\) to that of a variance swap \(K_{\text{var}}\)?

    Options

    Because the square-root function is strictly concave, \(\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{RV}] < \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[RV]}\), so \(K_{\text{vol}} < \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}\).

  8. In Demeterfi et al. (1999), why are uncentered returns \((\ln(S_i / S_{i-1}))^2\) preferred over centered returns \((\ln(S_i / S_{i-1}) - \bar{r})^2\)?

    Options

    Quadratic variation of a continuous semimartingale is the limit of uncentered squared log increments. Centering introduces sample mean cross-terms that destroy static replication.
