Variance Risk Premium & Volatility Trading

Across equity index derivative markets, implied volatility systematically exceeds realized volatility over the same tenor. This empirical wedge—the variance risk premium (VRP)—reflects the premium investors pay to insure against catastrophic market crashes and sudden volatility surges. This doctoral module develops the structural mathematics and empirical reality of volatility trading. Students analyze the continuous static replication of variance via portfolios of out-of-the-money options, examine the Cboe VIX index discretization methodology, study return predictability driven by aggregate risk aversion, evaluate the cross-section of delta-hedged option returns, dissect the negative roll economics of protective put tail hedges, and perform a structural post-mortem of the February 2018 short-volatility liquidation cascade ('Volmageddon').

  • Prerequisites: DV2 The Volatility Surface, F2 Factor Models & Cross-Section of Returns. Prerequisite closure includes DV2, F2, DV1, F1, M4, M2, M1, and SC1–SC3.
  • Unlocks: DV6 Exotics & Structured Products, Doctoral Capstone Studios, and Professional Portfolio Defense.
  • Project: Systematic short-volatility strategy audit: backtesting variance swaps and model-free volatility strategies under realistic leverage and margin constraints, stress testing crash drawdowns (2008, 2018, 2020), and authoring an institutional risk audit memorandum. Graded by both autograder and rubric.
  • Data: Simulated option chains, variance swap paths, and high-frequency volatility market data labeled SIMULATED.

42 h lessons · 28 h project (40% of the 70 h module)

Objectives

By the end of DV5 you can:

  1. Derive the continuous static replication formula for variance swaps using the Carr–Madan / Demeterfi et al. (1999) log contract, and quantify the replication error induced by discrete strike spacing and price jumps (Lesson 1).
  2. Implement the official Cboe VIX methodology, calculate model-free implied volatility from discrete out-of-the-money option chains, and diagnose zero-bid truncation biases and expiration interpolation distortions (Lesson 2).
  3. Estimate the empirical variance risk premium \(\mathrm{VRP}_t = \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}}[RV] - \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{P}}[RV]\), test its statistical significance on index vs. single-stock options (Carr & Wu 2009), and replicate Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009) multi-horizon stock return predictive regressions (Lesson 3).
  4. Decompose the P&L of continuously delta-hedged option portfolios via the Itô gamma-theta-variance identity, and evaluate the empirical cross-section of delta-hedged returns across strike moneyness (Coval & Shumway 2001) (Lesson 4).
  5. Demonstrate the economic drag ('negative roll carry') of rolling protective put options on long-term wealth accumulation (Israelov 2018), proving why static tail hedging fails to deliver cost-effective crash insurance (Lesson 5).
  6. Model the mechanical rebalancing feedback loops of inverse and leveraged VIX exchange-traded products, and reconstruct the structural collapse of February 5, 2018 ('Volmageddon') (Lesson 5).
  7. Audit a systematic short-volatility investment strategy, enforce broker margin constraints, stress test historical crash shocks, and defend an institutional risk policy against an adversarial investment committee (Project).

Placement Diagnostic

Ten questions evaluate prerequisite derivatives mastery and core volatility trading skills. Questions 1 through 8 test prerequisites from DV2, DV1, F2, and stochastic calculus. Questions 9 and 10 are skill checks testing variance swap notional scaling and model-free VIX strike weighting taught in project rungs 1 and 2. Skipping rungs 1 and 2 requires meeting the 80% mastery threshold (at least 8 correct out of 10) and answering both skill checks correctly.

  1. Under the Breeden–Litzenberger (1978) theorem, how is the risk-neutral probability density \(q(K)\) extracted from European call prices?

    Options

    Differentiating the discounted risk-neutral expectation \(C(K) = e^{-r T} \int_K^\infty (S_T - K) q(S_T)\,dS_T\) twice with respect to strike \(K\) yields \(\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2} = e^{-r T} q(K)\), so \(q(K) = e^{r T} \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}\).

  2. Under Itô's lemma for a continuous geometric Brownian diffusion \(dS_t / S_t = \mu dt + \sigma dW_t\), what is the quadratic variation of the log price \(\langle \ln S\rangle_t\)?

    Options

    By Itô's lemma, \(d\ln S_t = (\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)dt + \sigma dW_t\). The quadratic variation of \(\ln S_t\) comes exclusively from the diffusion term, yielding \(d\langle \ln S\rangle_t = \sigma_t^2 dt\), which integrates to \(\int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du\).

  3. What is the discrete hedging P&L identity relating an option's gamma, theta, and realized underlying price return over period \(\Delta t\)?

    Options

    Substituting the Black–Scholes PDE identity \(\Theta + r S \Delta + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\text{implied}}^2 S^2 \Gamma = r V\) into the discrete delta-hedged portfolio change yields the gamma-theta-variance attribution \(d\Pi_t \approx \frac{1}{2} S_t^2 \Gamma_t [(\Delta S_t / S_t)^2 - \sigma_{\text{implied}}^2 \Delta t]\).

  4. Why does the Black–Scholes implied volatility of equity index options exhibit a persistent negative slope (skew) with respect to strike?

    Options

    Equity index implied volatility skew reflects the negative correlation between market returns and volatility spikes (the leverage and volatility feedback effects) combined with high investor risk aversion toward left-tail crash events.

  5. In continuous time without jumps, which exotic contract's terminal payoff allows exact static replication of continuous realized variance \(\int_0^T \sigma_t^2 dt\)?

    Options

    By Itô's formula, \(d\ln S_t = \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_t^2 dt\). Integrating yields \(\int_0^T \sigma_t^2 dt = 2 \int_0^T \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - 2\ln(S_T / S_0)\). Hence, holding a log contract and dynamically hedging with \(1/S_t\) shares replicates continuous variance.

  6. What is the Carr–Madan (1999) static replication weighting across out-of-the-money European option strikes \(K\) for any twice-differentiable payoff \(g(S_T)\)?

    Options

    Taylor's theorem with integral remainder implies \(g(S_T) = g(S_*) + g'(S_*)(S_T - S_*) + \int_0^{S_*} g''(K)(K - S_T)^+\,dK + \int_{S_*}^\infty g''(K)(S_T - K)^+\,dK\). For the log contract \(g(S) = -\ln(S / S_*)\), \(g''(K) = 1 / K^2\).

  7. How does the presence of discontinuous downward asset price jumps (\(J < 1\)) affect the log-contract static replication of realized variance?

    Options

    For jump size \(J = S_t / S_{t^-}\), the difference between quadratic variation and the log contract is \(2(J - 1 - \ln J) - (\ln J)^2\). For crash jumps (\(J < 1\)), this residual is strictly positive and scales as \(\frac{1}{3}|\ln J|^3\), leaving variance sellers exposed to crash losses.

  8. What empirical finding did Coval & Shumway (2001) document regarding the cross-section of delta-hedged option returns?

    Options

    Coval & Shumway (2001) established that delta-hedged option returns are deeply negative on average, with the steepest losses occurring in out-of-the-money puts (-30% to -40% per month), proving that market jump risk commands a massive negative risk premium.

  9. Skill Check 1 (Variance Swap Notional Scaling): A variance swap contract specifies a vega notional \(N_{\text{vega}} = \$100{,}000\) and a volatility strike of \(\sigma_0 = 20\%\) (variance strike \(K_{\text{var}} = 0.0400\)). What is the contract's variance notional \(N_{\text{var}}\)?

    Options

    By definition of vega notional, a 1% shift in volatility \(\Delta \sigma\) near \(\sigma_0\) produces P&L \(\Delta \Pi \approx N_{\text{var}} \times 2 \sigma_0 \Delta \sigma = N_{\text{vega}} \Delta \sigma\). Hence, \(N_{\text{var}} = \frac{N_{\text{vega}}}{2 \sigma_0} = \frac{100{,}000}{0.40} = \$250{,}000\).

  10. Skill Check 2 (Model-Free VIX Discretization): In the Cboe VIX formula \(\sigma^2 = \frac{2}{T} e^{r T} \sum_i \frac{\Delta K_i}{K_i^2} Q(K_i) - \frac{1}{T}\left(\frac{F}{K_0} - 1\right)^2\), how is the strike interval \(\Delta K_i\) computed for an intermediate strike \(K_i\)?

    Options

    The Cboe VIX methodology uses a midpoint trapezoidal rule for strike integration: for intermediate strikes \(K_i\), \(\Delta K_i = \frac{K_{i+1} - K_{i-1}}{2}\). For the lowest strike, \(\Delta K_1 = K_2 - K_1\); for the highest, \(\Delta K_m = K_m - K_{m-1}\).
