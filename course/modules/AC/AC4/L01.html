Cost Behaviour and Contribution Margin

  • Uses: the income statement
  • Introduces: fixed, variable and mixed costs; the contribution margin; the high-low estimate of a mixed cost
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or lab
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

The pricing, make-or-buy and volume questions in this module start from one question: how does the cost of an activity move when the activity moves? This lesson fixes the vocabulary for that question. It writes a cost as a function of volume, derives the contribution margin (defined in Section 2) that a unit adds toward fixed cost, and shows how to split a mixed cost (defined in Section 1) into its two parts from a history of costs. It also marks where the split fails, because a number from the split inherits the limits of the method that produced it.

1Three kinds of cost

Money in this lesson is in US dollars, USD, per period. Volume is measured in one activity unit, such as units produced, units sold or machine hours. The lab in this lesson uses integer cents and integer hours.

Definition 1.1 (cost behaviour)

Cost behaviour is the way a cost's total changes with the volume of activity \(Q\) within a stated relevant range. A fixed cost does not change in total as \(Q\) changes. A variable cost changes in total in proportion to \(Q\). A mixed cost has both parts. The relevant range is the span of volumes over which the analyst believes these descriptions hold, and it is stated with the cost estimate.

Definition 1.2 (linear cost function)

Let \(F \ge 0\) be the fixed cost per period, in USD, and \(v \ge 0\) the variable cost per unit of activity, in USD per unit. The linear cost function is

\[ TC(Q) = F + vQ, \qquad Q \ge 0. \]

A cost that follows this function is a mixed cost with a fixed part \(F\) and a variable part \(vQ\). A purely fixed cost has \(v = 0\), and a purely variable cost has \(F = 0\).

Proposition 1.3 (the two per-unit views of a linear cost)

Under Definition 1.2, with \(Q > 0\): (a) the variable cost per unit is \(vQ/Q = v\), the same at every volume; (b) the fixed cost per unit is \(F/Q\), which is strictly decreasing in \(Q\) when \(F > 0\); (c) the total cost per unit is \(TC(Q)/Q = F/Q + v\).

Proof

(a) \(vQ/Q = v\) for every \(Q > 0\), by cancellation. (b) For \(0 < Q_1 < Q_2\), \(F/Q_2 - F/Q_1 = F(Q_1 - Q_2)/(Q_1 Q_2)\). This is negative when \(F > 0\) because the numerator is negative and the denominator is positive. (c) \(TC(Q)/Q = (F + vQ)/Q = F/Q + v\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.4 (the same fixed cost at two volumes)

A fixed cost of 6,000 USD a month is 12 USD a unit at 500 units and 6 USD a unit at 1,000 units. The total did not change; the per-unit figure halved. A per-unit cost that is quoted without its volume therefore says nothing about how the cost will move. Problem M3 uses the same arithmetic.

2Contribution margin

Definition 2.1 (contribution margin)

Let \(p\) be the selling price per unit, in USD, and \(v\) the variable cost per unit. The contribution margin per unit is \(m = p - v\). At volume \(Q\) the total contribution margin is \(mQ\). The contribution margin ratio is \(m/p\), the share of each sales dollar that is left to cover fixed cost and profit.

Proposition 2.2 (operating income from the contribution margin)

If the only costs are fixed cost \(F\) and variable cost \(v\) per unit, then operating income at volume \(Q\) is \(OI(Q) = mQ - F\). The contribution margin ratio satisfies \(m/p = 1 - v/p\).

Proof

Revenue is \(pQ\) and total cost is \(F + vQ\). Operating income is their difference, \(OI(Q) = pQ - (F + vQ) = (p - v)Q - F = mQ - F\). The ratio follows from \(m/p = (p - v)/p = 1 - v/p\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (a contribution margin is not a profit)

Proposition 2.2 shows that \(mQ\) is larger than \(OI(Q)\) by exactly \(F\). A product that earns a positive contribution margin can still lose money once its share of fixed cost is subtracted. Lesson 2 uses this identity to find the volume at which operating income is zero.

3Splitting a mixed cost from a history

The model's \(F\) and \(v\) are not observed directly. Total cost is observed at several volumes, and a split of the cost into its two parts is estimated from those observations. The simplest split uses two of them.

Definition 3.1 (high-low estimate)

Let the months be indexed by \(i\), with activity \(Q_i\) and total cost \(C_i\). Let \(H\) be a month with the highest activity and \(L\) a month with the lowest activity, with \(Q_H \ne Q_L\). The high-low estimates are

\[ \hat v = \frac{C_H - C_L}{Q_H - Q_L}, \qquad \hat F = C_H - \hat v\,Q_H. \]

The method uses only the two extreme months. A tie on the highest activity takes the earliest such month, and the same rule applies to the lowest.

Proposition 3.2 (the fitted line passes through the two extreme months)

With \(Q_H \ne Q_L\), as Definition 3.1 requires, the line \(\hat F + \hat v Q\) passes through \((Q_H, C_H)\) and \((Q_L, C_L)\). Its residual, the observed cost less the fitted cost \(\hat F + \hat v Q\), is zero at those two months, and \(\hat v\) is the only slope that does so.

Proof

At \(Q = Q_H\), \(\hat F + \hat v Q_H = C_H - \hat v Q_H + \hat v Q_H = C_H\). At \(Q = Q_L\), \(\hat F + \hat v Q_L = C_H - \hat v (Q_H - Q_L) = C_H - (C_H - C_L) = C_L\). The two equations \(C_H = F + vQ_H\) and \(C_L = F + vQ_L\) have the unique solution \(v = (C_H - C_L)/(Q_H - Q_L)\) and \(F = C_H - vQ_H\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.3 (what the two-point method cannot see)

By Proposition 3.2, every number in the estimate comes from two months. The residual of any other month, \(C_i - (\hat F + \hat v Q_i)\), is free to be positive or negative, and its size depends on how far that month sits from the line through the two extremes. An error in either extreme month moves \(\hat v\) and \(\hat F\), which moves the fitted line and so changes the residual of the other months. The method therefore carries no measure of its own error. Lab 1 prints the residual of every month, and because the history is a simulation it also prints the generator's true parameters for comparison.

Lab 1 · High-low on a SIMULATED twelve-month cost history

The lab generates twelve months of machine hours and total costs from a seeded random number generator, with a fixed part of 18,000 USD, a variable rate of 42.50 USD an hour and noise of at most 400 USD either way. The generator's true values are printed only for comparison. Predicted from Proposition 3.2, not from the output: the two months printed as on the fitted line are the lowest-activity month and the highest-activity month, and their residuals are exactly 0; the fitted fixed cost and variable rate differ from the generator's values by the noise in the two extreme months; and a month between the extremes has a nonzero residual unless its cost happens to lie exactly on the line.

"""AC4 lab L01: fit a mixed cost with the high-low method on a SIMULATED monthly history.

SIMULATED: the twelve months, the machine hours and the costs are generated from a seeded
random number generator. They are not any firm's figures. Money is integer cents. The fitted
parameters are exact rationals (fractions.Fraction); a value is converted to the nearest cent
only when it is printed. Standard library only; no network; every number printed is computed here.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261027
MONTHS = 12
GEN_FIXED_CENTS = 1_800_000  # generator's fixed part, 18000.00 USD a month; printed only for comparison
GEN_VARIABLE_CENTS = 4_250  # generator's variable rate, 42.50 USD an hour; printed only for comparison
NOISE_CENTS = 40_000  # the noise is uniform on [-40000, 40000] cents, 400.00 USD either way


def simulate(seed: int, months: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """Return (machine_hours, total_cost_cents) per month, from a seeded generator.

    Hours are integers in [400, 1200]. Cost is GEN_FIXED + GEN_VARIABLE * hours + noise, in integer cents.
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    rows: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    for _ in range(months):
        hours = rng.randint(400, 1200)
        noise = rng.randint(-NOISE_CENTS, NOISE_CENTS)
        rows.append((hours, GEN_FIXED_CENTS + GEN_VARIABLE_CENTS * hours + noise))
    return rows


def cents(x: Fraction) -> int:
    """Nearest integer number of cents; a half goes up. Exact on a Fraction."""
    return (2 * x.numerator + x.denominator) // (2 * x.denominator)


def high_low(rows: list[tuple[int, int]]) -> tuple[Fraction, Fraction]:
    """Fit cost = fixed + variable * hours through the highest- and lowest-activity months.

    Returns (fixed_cents, variable_cents_per_hour) as exact Fractions. A tie on activity takes the
    earliest month. Raises ValueError when there are fewer than two months, or when the highest and
    lowest activity are equal, because the slope is then undefined.
    """
    if len(rows) < 2:
        raise ValueError("high-low needs at least two months of data")
    high = max(range(len(rows)), key=lambda i: (rows[i][0], -i))
    low = min(range(len(rows)), key=lambda i: (rows[i][0], i))
    high_hours, high_cost = rows[high]
    low_hours, low_cost = rows[low]
    if high_hours == low_hours:
        raise ValueError("high-low needs different highest and lowest activity; the slope is undefined")
    variable = Fraction(high_cost - low_cost, high_hours - low_hours)
    fixed = Fraction(high_cost) - variable * high_hours
    return fixed, variable


def main() -> None:
    """Fit the history, print the parameters, the residual of each month, and the loud failures."""
    rows = simulate(SEED, MONTHS)
    fixed, variable = high_low(rows)
    print(f"seed {SEED} months {len(rows)}")
    print(f"generator fixed_cents {GEN_FIXED_CENTS} variable_cents_per_hour {GEN_VARIABLE_CENTS}")
    print(f"high-low fixed_cents {cents(fixed)} variable_cents_per_hour {float(variable):.2f}")
    residuals: list[int] = []
    for month, (hours, cost) in enumerate(rows, start=1):
        residual = Fraction(cost) - (fixed + variable * hours)
        residuals.append(cents(residual))
        print(f"month {month:2d} hours {hours:5d} cost_cents {cost:9d} residual_cents {cents(residual):7d}")
    high_hours = max(hours for hours, _ in rows)
    low_hours = min(hours for hours, _ in rows)
    on_line = [m for m, (h, _) in enumerate(rows, start=1) if h in (high_hours, low_hours)]
    print(f"months on the fitted line exactly {on_line}")
    print(f"largest absolute residual_cents {max(abs(r) for r in residuals)}")
    print(f"months with nonzero residual {sum(1 for r in residuals if r != 0)} of {len(rows)}")
    for label, data in (("one month", rows[:1]), ("equal activity", [(500, 100), (500, 200)])):
        try:
            high_low(data)
        except ValueError as exc:
            print(f"ValueError for {label}: {exc}")
        else:
            print(f"no error for {label}")


main()

4Estimating a mixed cost from a history

Example 4.1 applies Definition 3.1 to two months. Lab 1 applies the same definition to twelve.

Example 4.1 (a worked split)

Over two months a unit's activity and costs are: month A, 1,000 units and 30,000 USD; month B, 400 units and 18,000 USD. The highest-activity month is A and the lowest is B. Then \(\hat v = (30000 - 18000)/(1000 - 400) = 20\) USD a unit and \(\hat F = 30000 - 20 \times 1000 = 10000\) USD a month. The check at month B is \(10000 + 20 \times 400 = 18000\), which equals its cost, as Proposition 3.2 requires.

5Where the canon breaks

The linear cost function is the starting point used on this page, and four conditions can limit it.

  • Relevant range. The function describes costs over a range of volumes. Outside that range the fixed part can change (a second shift, a larger lease), so \(F\) is a constant only inside the range. Lesson 2 shows what a step in fixed cost does to the break-even point.
  • The split is a convention of the method. A different method applied to the same history can give a different \(F\) and \(v\). Proposition 3.2 proves what the two-point method does, and nothing in the lesson proves that the two-point split is the right one for a decision.
  • Extreme months carry the estimate. A month of unusual activity (a plant shutdown, a promotion) can become one of the two extremes and set the estimate. Remark 3.3 gives the reason.
  • Accounting costs can mix behaviours. A cost recorded as one line can mix a fixed commitment with discretionary spending. The label does not tell the analyst which, so the split has to be estimated and checked.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A fixed cost is a fixed amount per unit.”

    A fixed cost is fixed in total. Its per-unit amount is \(F/Q\), which falls as volume rises (Proposition 1.3(b)). Example 1.4 shows the same 6,000 USD fixed cost as 12 USD a unit at 500 units and 6 USD at 1,000 units.

  2. “The contribution margin is the operating income.”

    The contribution margin is \(mQ\), and operating income is \(mQ - F\) (Proposition 2.2). The gap is the fixed cost, which the contribution margin has not yet covered. Remark 2.3 gives the consequence.

  3. “The high-low line is the best line through the data.”

    The high-low line is exact at two months and is chosen by two observations only (Proposition 3.2), so the other months do not enter the estimate. Lab 1 shows nonzero residuals in ten of the twelve months, and the method states no error measure.

  4. “Once a mixed cost has been split, the split is exact.”

    The split describes the two-point line. Lab 1 prints the residual at every month, and the largest is 514.73 USD. A parameter that is stated to the cent still carries the error of the method that produced it.

7Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Every problem has a full worked solution. Problems marked Lab use the Lab 1 output.

Mechanics

M1 · Contribution and operating income

A product sells for 40 USD a unit, has a variable cost of 26 USD a unit and a fixed cost of 7,000 USD a month. Find the contribution margin per unit, the contribution margin ratio, and operating income at 1,500 units.

Solution

The contribution margin per unit is \(m = 40 - 26 = 14\) USD. The ratio is \(m/p = 14/40 = 0.35\). The total contribution at 1,500 units is \(14 \times 1500 = 21000\) USD. By Proposition 2.2, operating income is \(21000 - 7000 = 14000\) USD a month.

M2 · Split a mixed cost

Over two months a plant's machine hours and costs are: March, 800 hours and 52,000 USD; July, 1,400 hours and 73,000 USD. Estimate the variable rate and the fixed cost by the high-low method, and check the fitted cost at 1,100 hours.

Solution

July is the highest-activity month and March the lowest. The variable rate is \(\hat v = (73000 - 52000)/(1400 - 800) = 21000/600 = 35\) USD an hour. The fixed cost is \(\hat F = 73000 - 35 \times 1400 = 73000 - 49000 = 24000\) USD a month. The fitted cost at 1,100 hours is \(24000 + 35 \times 1100 = 24000 + 38500 = 62500\) USD. Because 1,100 hours lies between the two extremes, the fitted cost there is an estimate and not an observed cost, so the method gives no error for it.

M3 · Per-unit fixed cost

A fixed cost of 6,000 USD a month is spread over 500 units and then over 1,000 units. What is the fixed cost per unit in each case, and what is the total fixed cost in each case?

Solution

The total fixed cost is 6,000 USD in both cases, because \(F\) does not depend on \(Q\). Per unit, \(6000/500 = 12\) USD and \(6000/1000 = 6\) USD. The per-unit figure halves when volume doubles, which is Proposition 1.3(b) applied with \(Q_2 = 2Q_1\).

Proof

P1 · Operating income is linear in volume

Prove that, with fixed cost \(F\) and variable cost \(v\) per unit, the function \(OI(Q)\) has constant slope and find the slope.

Solution

By Proposition 2.2, \(OI(Q) = mQ - F\) with \(m = p - v\). For \(Q_2 > Q_1\), \(OI(Q_2) - OI(Q_1) = m(Q_2 - Q_1)\), so the slope between any two volumes is \(m\), independent of the volumes chosen. The slope is the contribution margin per unit, \(m = p - v\), and the intercept is \(-F\).

P2 · Limit of the per-unit fixed cost

For \(F > 0\), show that \(\lim_{Q \to \infty} F/Q = 0\) and that the total cost per unit tends to \(v\).

Solution

For any \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(F/Q < \varepsilon\) whenever \(Q > F/\varepsilon\), so \(F/Q \to 0\). By Proposition 1.3(c), \(TC(Q)/Q = F/Q + v\), and so \(TC(Q)/Q \to v\). The per-unit total cost approaches the variable cost, and the fixed part of the per-unit cost disappears at high volume.

P3 · The high-low slope is the slope of the chord

Show that \(\hat v\) equals the slope of the straight line through the points \((Q_L, C_L)\) and \((Q_H, C_H)\), and that the estimate is undefined when \(Q_H = Q_L\).

Solution

The slope of the line through two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) with \(x_1 \ne x_2\) is \((y_2 - y_1)/(x_2 - x_1)\). With \((x_1, y_1) = (Q_L, C_L)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (Q_H, C_H)\), this is \((C_H - C_L)/(Q_H - Q_L) = \hat v\). When \(Q_H = Q_L\) the denominator is zero, the two points share an abscissa, and no line of the form \(F + vQ\) passes through two different costs at one volume. The high-low estimate is therefore undefined, which is the error that Lab 1 raises for equal activity.

Empirical

E1 · Lab · Which months fix the line?

From the Lab 1 output, which two months are on the fitted line exactly, and what are their hours and costs?

Solution

The output prints months 6 and 11. Month 6 has 428 hours and a cost of 3,643,546 cents (36,435.46 USD). Month 11 has 1,161 hours and a cost of 6,721,387 cents (67,213.87 USD). These are the lowest and highest activity in the history, as Proposition 3.2 predicts: 428 is the minimum of the hours column and 1,161 is the maximum.

E2 · Lab · How far is the worst month from the line?

The Lab 1 output reports the largest absolute residual. Give it in cents and in USD, and name the month where it occurs.

Solution

The largest absolute residual is 51,473 cents, which is 514.73 USD. It occurs in month 1, whose residual is printed as −51,473 cents. The cost in month 1 is therefore 514.73 USD below the estimate at that month's 556 hours. The generator's noise is at most 400 USD either way, so noise alone cannot produce a residual of 514.73 USD. The rest comes from the gap between the fitted line and the generator's line at that month.

E3 · Lab · Count the months off the line

How many of the twelve months have a nonzero residual, and what does that count say about the two-point method?

Solution

The output reports 10 of 12 months with a nonzero residual. Proposition 3.2 makes the two extreme months exact, and Lab 1 prints no other month exactly on the line. The count says that, on this history, the fitted line is an exact description of two months and an approximation of the other ten. It does not say how large the error of the approximation is at a volume outside the history, which the method cannot report.

Research

R1 · Stability of the split

Specify a procedure that tests whether the high-low split in Lab 1 would change if one extreme month were removed. State what the procedure reports, and what it cannot report.

Solution

Remove the highest-activity month and re-run the high-low method on the remaining eleven months, which makes the new highest month the old second-highest. Do the same for the lowest month. Compare the full-history pair \((\hat F, \hat v)\) with the pair after removing the highest-activity month and the pair after removing the lowest-activity month. The procedure reports how sensitive the split is to each extreme month. It cannot report an error for a volume outside the history, and it cannot say which of the three pairs is the true cost behaviour, because the removal changes the sample and no truth is observed on a real history.

R2 · Which estimate is the decision input?

A plant manager asks for the cost of an extra 300 hours. Explain which of the three quantities (the fixed cost, the variable rate, or the fitted cost at the new total) answers the question, and state the assumption the answer depends on.

Solution

The cost of an extra 300 hours is the change in total cost, which under the linear function is \(\hat v \times 300\). The fixed cost does not change with hours inside the relevant range, so it does not enter the difference. The fitted total at the new level answers a different question, the total cost, and it equals the fixed cost plus \(\hat v\) times the new total hours. The answer depends on the assumption that the variable rate holds for the next 300 hours, which is a statement about the relevant range and is not proved by the history.

R3 · Step or mixed?

A supervisor is paid 4,000 USD a month for each shift of 400 machine hours or part thereof. Is this cost fixed, variable, mixed, or a step cost (one that jumps by a set amount at each threshold), over the range 0 to 1,200 hours? Give the reason and say which assumption about the relevant range the answer depends on.

Solution

Over 0 to 1,200 hours the cost takes the value 4,000 USD for 1 to 400 hours, 8,000 USD for 401 to 800 hours and 12,000 USD for 801 to 1,200 hours. It is not a linear function of hours, so it is neither purely fixed nor purely variable, and it does not have the form \(F + vQ\). A straight-line split fits the three steps badly. Apply the high-low rule of Definition 3.1 to the two ends of the range, 0 hours at 0 USD (no shift runs) and 1,200 hours at 12,000 USD (three shifts). Then \(\hat v = 12000/1200 = 10\) USD an hour and \(\hat F = 0\). Computed at each of the 1,201 whole hours from 0 to 1,200, the largest absolute error is 3,990 USD, at 1, 401 and 801 hours, where the cost is 4,000, 8,000 and 12,000 USD and the line gives 10, 4,010 and 8,010 USD. That error is 99.75% of the 4,000 USD step (3,990 / 4,000 = 0.9975). The answer depends on treating the relevant range as the 0 to 1,200 hour span; if the range is restricted to 1 to 400 hours, the cost is a fixed 4,000 USD.

9Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Which cost is fixed in total within the relevant range?

    Options

    Factory rent does not change with volume inside the range (Definition 1.1). Materials and commission both change in proportion to volume.

  2. A product sells for 25 USD and has a variable cost of 15 USD a unit. What is the contribution margin per unit, in USD?

    \(m = p - v = 25 - 15 = 10\) USD (Definition 2.1).

  3. With the product above, fixed cost of 4,000 USD a month and 500 units sold, what is operating income in USD?

    \(OI = mQ - F = 10 \times 500 - 4000 = 1000\) USD (Proposition 2.2).

  4. Which statement about the contribution margin ratio of a product selling at 40 USD with a variable cost of 10 USD is correct?

    Options

    \(m/p = (40 - 10)/40 = 0.75\) (Proposition 2.2). The ratio is a number between 0 and 1 when \(0 \le v \le p\).

  5. Two months: 1,000 hours costing 30,000 USD, and 400 hours costing 18,000 USD. What is the high-low variable rate, in USD an hour?

    \(\hat v = (30000 - 18000)/(1000 - 400) = 20\) (Definition 3.1, Example 4.1).

  6. For the same two months, what is the high-low fixed cost, in USD a month?

    \(\hat F = C_H - \hat v Q_H = 30000 - 20 \times 1000 = 10000\) USD (Definition 3.1).

  7. Which statement about a high-low estimate is correct?

    Options

    Proposition 3.2 proves the zero residuals at the two extremes. Other months carry residuals (Remark 3.3), because the method does not use them to set the line.

  8. A fixed cost of 7,000 USD a month is spread over 1,000 units. What is the fixed cost per unit, in USD?

    \(F/Q = 7000/1000 = 7\) USD a unit (Proposition 1.3(b)).

  9. A cost of 4,000 USD a month for each block of 400 machine hours or part thereof is best described, over 0 to 1,200 hours, as:

    Options

    The cost rises when hours pass 400 and when they pass 800 (Problem R3: 4,000 USD at exactly 400 hours, 8,000 USD at 401 hours). No single fixed part and variable rate reproduce the cost at every hour from 1 to 1,200: over 1 to 400 hours the cost is constant, so the slope must be zero, and then the line cannot reach 8,000 USD.

  10. Why must a cost estimate state its relevant range?

    Options

    Section 5: the fixed part can change outside the relevant range, so an estimate describes the range it was made in.
