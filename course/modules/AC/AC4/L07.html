Transfer pricing

  • Uses: contribution margin
  • Introduces: transfer pricing (defined in §1)
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or simulation
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

When one division of a firm sells to another, the price charged between them moves profit from one division to the other, and it can also decide whether the sale happens. A supplier with spare capacity and a supplier at full capacity face different costs of making the sale. This lesson derives the lowest price a supplier will accept and the highest price a buyer will pay, under a capacity limit. The two bounds follow from one identity: the firm's gain from the sale does not depend on the price. Every example here is SIMULATED, and the one lab checks the identities on generated division pairs.

1Setting and definitions

A firm has a supplier division S and a buyer division B. S makes an intermediate good that B needs. The model is one period, with the following inputs, all stated as given. Money is in SCU, a simulated currency unit used for every example on this page; no real currency is represented.

  • \(v\), the variable cost of one unit of the intermediate good, in SCU per unit;
  • \(K\), the capacity of S, in units;
  • \(p_m\), the price at which S can sell the good to outside customers, in SCU per unit, with \(p_m \ge v\);
  • \(E\), the units S sells outside at \(p_m\), with \(E \le K\), so outside demand fits in capacity by itself;
  • \(q\), the units B asks S to supply, with \(q > 0\);
  • \(r\), B's net contribution per unit of its own final product, before the transfer price, in SCU per unit.

The fixed costs of both divisions are the same whether or not the sale happens. The labs store money in integer cents, with 1 SCU = 100 cents.

Definition 7.1 (transfer pricing)

Transfer pricing is the choice of the price \(t\), in SCU per unit, at which one division of a firm supplies another division of the same firm. The transfer price is an internal amount. It moves money between the two divisions and leaves the firm's total cash flow from the sale unchanged, a fact §3 proves.

Two terms need a precise meaning here. The displaced units are the outside sales that the internal order forces out. Each displaced unit gives up the contribution margin \(p_m - v\) per unit. The opportunity cost of the internal order is the total contribution of the displaced units, \(D(p_m - v)\). Of the \(q\) internal units, \(q - D\) are absorbed by spare capacity and give up nothing. Every quantity here is computed from the inputs, not taken from any source.

2The supplier's and the buyer's gains

Assume \(q \le K\). If it is not, no transfer is possible, because S cannot make \(q\) units. With \(q \le K\), S can serve B and still sell \(\min(E, K-q)\) units outside. The number of outside sales lost is

\[ D = E - \min(E, K-q) = \max(0,\; q + E - K). \]

The supplier's gain from accepting the order at price \(t\), compared with refusing it, is the contribution on the internal units minus the contribution lost on the displaced outside units:

\[ \Delta_S(t) = q\,(t - v) - D\,(p_m - v). \]

The buyer's gain from buying the \(q\) units at \(t\) is

\[ \Delta_B(t) = q\,(r - t). \]
Lemma 7.2 (the two bounds)

Let \(q > 0\) and \(q \le K\). Define

\[ t_{\min} = v + \frac{D\,(p_m - v)}{q}, \qquad t_{\max} = r. \]

Then \(\Delta_S(t) \ge 0\) if and only if \(t \ge t_{\min}\), and \(\Delta_B(t) \ge 0\) if and only if \(t \le t_{\max}\).

Proof. Since \(q > 0\), \(\Delta_S(t) \ge 0\) is equivalent to \(q(t-v) \ge D(p_m - v)\), that is \(t \ge v + D(p_m - v)/q = t_{\min}\). Likewise \(\Delta_B(t) \ge 0\) is equivalent to \(q(r-t) \ge 0\), that is \(t \le r = t_{\max}\). \(\square\)

The term \(D(p_m - v)/q\) is the opportunity cost per internal unit, averaged over the \(q\) internal units. It is the outside contribution of the displaced units, spread over the internal units. When spare capacity absorbs the order (\(D = 0\)), the opportunity cost is zero and \(t_{\min} = v\). When every internal unit displaces one outside unit (\(D = q\)), the opportunity cost is \(p_m - v\) and \(t_{\min} = p_m\). Since \(E \le K\) gives \(D \le q\), and \(p_m \ge v\), the lowest price never exceeds \(p_m\).

3The feasible range and the firm-wide surplus

Adding the two gains removes the price:

\[ \Delta_S(t) + \Delta_B(t) = q\,(t-v) - D\,(p_m-v) + q\,(r-t) = q\,(r-v) - D\,(p_m-v). \]

The right side does not contain \(t\). Call it the firm-wide surplus \(\Sigma = q(r-v) - D(p_m - v)\). The transfer price divides \(\Sigma\) between the divisions and does not change its size.

Theorem 7.3 (the feasible range)

Let \(q > 0\), \(q \le K\), \(E \le K\), \(p_m \ge v\), and let \(\Sigma\) be the firm-wide surplus. Then:

  1. the price range that leaves both divisions no worse off is \([t_{\min}, t_{\max}]\);
  2. the range is nonempty if and only if \(\Sigma \ge 0\);
  3. for every \(t\) in the range, \(\Delta_S(t) \ge 0\), \(\Delta_B(t) \ge 0\) and \(\Delta_S(t) + \Delta_B(t) = \Sigma\).

Proof. Part 1 follows from Lemma 7.2: both gains are nonnegative exactly for \(t \in [t_{\min}, t_{\max}]\). For part 2, the interval is nonempty if and only if \(t_{\min} \le t_{\max}\). Multiplying by \(q > 0\) gives \(qv + D(p_m-v) \le qr\), that is \(q(r-v) - D(p_m-v) \ge 0\), which is \(\Sigma \ge 0\). For part 3, both gains are nonnegative on the range by part 1, and their sum is \(\Sigma\) by the identity above. \(\square\)

Part 2 is the decision rule. A transfer that the firm as a whole should make is one that leaves a range of prices both divisions accept. If \(\Sigma < 0\), no price can make both divisions agree, and the firm loses on the sale. The price cannot make a sale that loses money overall pay. Inside a nonempty range, neither division loses on the sale, and the firm keeps \(\Sigma\) whatever the price.

4A worked case

The following case is a hand-built SIMULATED pair of divisions, not a firm. Inputs: \(v = 40\), \(p_m = 60\) (SCU per unit); \(K = 10{,}000\), \(E = 8{,}000\), \(q = 5{,}000\) (units); \(r = 80\) (SCU per unit).

  • Displaced units: \(D = \max(0,\; 5{,}000 + 8{,}000 - 10{,}000) = 3{,}000\).
  • Lowest price: \(t_{\min} = 40 + 3{,}000 \times (60-40)/5{,}000 = 40 + 12 = 52\) SCU per unit.
  • Highest price: \(t_{\max} = 80\) SCU per unit. The range is \([52, 80]\), nonempty.
  • Firm-wide surplus: \(\Sigma = 5{,}000 \times (80-40) - 3{,}000 \times 20 = 200{,}000 - 60{,}000 = 140{,}000\) SCU. Theorem 7.3 says the range is nonempty, and it is, because \(\Sigma \ge 0\).
  • At \(t = 52\): \(\Delta_S = 5{,}000 \times 12 - 60{,}000 = 0\) and \(\Delta_B = 5{,}000 \times 28 = 140{,}000\). At \(t = 46\), the supplier's gain is \(5{,}000 \times 6 - 60{,}000 = -30{,}000\), so S refuses.

Changing one input shows which bound moves. With capacity \(K = 12{,}000\), the displaced units fall to \(D = 1{,}000\), so \(t_{\min} = 40 + 1{,}000 \times 20/5{,}000 = 44\) and \(\Sigma = 180{,}000\) SCU. When capacity binds (\(D > 0\)) and \(p_m > v\), extra capacity lowers the supplier's floor and raises the surplus; when \(D = 0\), it changes neither. The buyer's ceiling \(r = 80\) does not move.

Lab 1 repeats the derivation for generated pairs and checks the identities exactly.

Lab 1 · The transfer-price range on SIMULATED division pairs

Stated before running: for every pair with \(q \le K\), the supplier's gain is exactly zero at \(t_{\min}\), the buyer's gain is exactly zero at \(t_{\max}\), the range is nonempty exactly when \(\Sigma \ge 0\), and the two gains sum to \(\Sigma\) at the ends and the midpoint of the range. The lab does not predict how often a range is empty. That count describes the generator's settings. All divisions are SIMULATED. Money is in integer cents, and every test uses exact fractions, not floating-point tolerances.

"""Lab AC4-L07-1: the transfer-price range under a capacity constraint, on SIMULATED divisions.

Every division here is SIMULATED: the cases come from a seeded generator, no firm is represented,
and no figure estimates any real company. Money is in integer cents (1 SCU = 100 cents); quantities
are integer units. Transfer prices are exact Fractions of cents. They are shown to two decimal places only when printed,
and every check uses the exact value.

Model (stated on the page): supplier S makes one intermediate good at variable cost v per unit with
capacity K units. S also sells E units externally at market price p_m (E <= K). Buyer B asks for q
units. B's net contribution per unit before the transfer price is r_net. Fixed costs are unaffected.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261027
N_CASES = 200


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Case:
    """One SIMULATED division pair. Money in integer cents; quantities in units."""

    case_id: int
    v: int
    p_m: int
    capacity: int
    external: int
    q: int
    r_net: int

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if min(self.v, self.p_m, self.capacity, self.external, self.q) < 0:
            raise ValueError("costs, prices, capacity and quantities must be non-negative")
        if self.external > self.capacity:
            raise ValueError("external demand must fit within capacity (E <= K)")


def displaced(c: Case) -> int:
    """External units lost to the internal order: max(0, q + E - K). Requires q <= K."""
    if c.q > c.capacity:
        raise ValueError("q exceeds capacity: no transfer is feasible")
    return max(0, c.q + c.external - c.capacity)


def t_min(c: Case) -> Fraction:
    """Lowest transfer price at which the supplier is no worse off: v + D(p_m - v)/q."""
    if c.q <= 0:
        raise ValueError("q must be positive")
    return Fraction(c.v) + Fraction(displaced(c) * (c.p_m - c.v), c.q)


def t_max(c: Case) -> Fraction:
    """Highest transfer price at which the buyer is no worse off: r_net."""
    return Fraction(c.r_net)


def supplier_increment(c: Case, t: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Supplier's gain from accepting the order at price t, against refusing it."""
    return Fraction(c.q) * (t - c.v) - displaced(c) * (c.p_m - c.v)


def buyer_increment(c: Case, t: Fraction) -> Fraction:
    """Buyer's gain from buying q units at price t."""
    return c.q * (Fraction(c.r_net) - t)


def firm_surplus(c: Case) -> int:
    """Firm-wide gain from the transfer, independent of t: q(r_net - v) - D(p_m - v)."""
    return c.q * (c.r_net - c.v) - displaced(c) * (c.p_m - c.v)


def generate(seed: int, n: int) -> list[Case]:
    """Generate n SIMULATED cases from a seeded generator."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    cases: list[Case] = []
    for case_id in range(1, n + 1):
        v = rng.randrange(3000, 5001, 100)
        p_m = v + rng.randrange(500, 3001, 100)
        capacity = rng.randrange(6000, 12001, 500)
        external = rng.randint(4000, min(9000, capacity))
        q = rng.randint(1000, 8000)
        r_net = v + rng.randint(-1500, 4000)
        cases.append(Case(case_id, v, p_m, capacity, external, q, r_net))
    return cases


def show(x: Fraction) -> str:
    """Print an exact cents amount to two decimals (display only)."""
    value = (Decimal(x.numerator) / Decimal(x.denominator)).quantize(Decimal("0.01"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)
    return f"{value}"


def main() -> None:
    cases = generate(SEED, N_CASES)
    print(f"SIMULATED divisions (seed {SEED}): {N_CASES} cases; money in cents, quantities in units")
    print("case  v      p_m    K      E      q      D     t_min     t_max     status")
    for c in cases[:6]:
        status = "no transfer (q > K)"
        tmin_text, tmax_text = "-", show(t_max(c))
        if c.q <= c.capacity:
            lo = t_min(c)
            tmin_text = show(lo)
            status = "feasible" if lo <= t_max(c) else "empty range"
        d_text = str(displaced(c)) if c.q <= c.capacity else "-"
        print(
            f"{c.case_id:>4}  {c.v:>5}  {c.p_m:>5}  {c.capacity:>5}  {c.external:>5}  {c.q:>5}  "
            f"{d_text:>5}  {tmin_text:>8}  {tmax_text:>8}  {status}"
        )

    over = [c for c in cases if c.q > c.capacity]
    fits = [c for c in cases if c.q <= c.capacity]
    empty = [c for c in fits if t_min(c) > t_max(c)]
    feasible = [c for c in fits if t_min(c) <= t_max(c)]
    binding = [c for c in feasible if displaced(c) > 0]
    print(f"cases with q > K (no transfer): {len(over)}")
    print(f"cases with q <= K and an empty range: {len(empty)}")
    print(f"cases with a feasible range: {len(feasible)}")
    print(f"  of which capacity binds (D > 0): {len(binding)}")

    checks = 0
    for c in fits:
        lo, hi = t_min(c), t_max(c)
        assert supplier_increment(c, lo) == 0
        assert buyer_increment(c, hi) == 0
        assert (lo <= hi) == (firm_surplus(c) >= 0)
        if lo <= hi:
            for t in (lo, hi, (lo + hi) / 2):
                assert supplier_increment(c, t) + buyer_increment(c, t) == firm_surplus(c)
        checks += 1
    print(f"exact checks passed: {checks}/{len(fits)} cases with q <= K")

6Where the model stops

  • One period, known costs. The bounds are computed from \(v\), \(p_m\), \(K\), \(E\), \(q\) and \(r\) as given. Real divisions learn these values with error and over time, and the lesson does not model that error.
  • The external price is a real option. The floor \(t_{\min}\) assumes that S can actually sell \(D\) units outside at \(p_m\). If the outside sale is not available, the opportunity cost is zero and \(t_{\min} = v\). The model takes the availability as an input.
  • No tax term. The model has no tax, so it says nothing about a transfer price chosen for tax reasons.
  • Capacity is a hard limit. A unit beyond \(K\) is not sold at any price. The model has no overtime or outsourcing option, which would change the supplier's cost of an internal unit.

7Misconceptions

  1. “A transfer price at full cost is fair, so the supplier should accept it.”

    Full cost includes fixed overhead, which does not change with the sale. The supplier's gain in §2 uses only the variable cost and the displaced contribution. In the worked case, a transfer at a full cost of 46 SCU per unit, which is 40 variable plus 6 of fixed overhead, gives \(\Delta_S = 5{,}000 \times 6 - 60{,}000 = -30{,}000\) SCU. The supplier loses on the sale and refuses, although the full-cost price is above \(v\). The full-cost price is below \(t_{\min} = 52\).

  2. “The transfer price should equal the market price, because the market price is the true value of the good.”

    The market price is the supplier's outside option, not the buyer's willingness to pay. Take \(v = 40\), \(p_m = 60\), \(K = 10{,}000\), \(E = 8{,}000\), \(q = 2{,}000\), \(r = 55\). There is spare capacity, so \(D = 0\) and \(t_{\min} = 40\). A transfer at 60 gives the buyer \(\Delta_B = 2{,}000 \times (55-60) = -10{,}000\) SCU, so the buyer refuses. The firm-wide surplus is \(\Sigma = 2{,}000 \times (55-40) = 30{,}000\) SCU, which is positive. A price of 40 to 55 would make the sale, and the market price does not.

  3. “The transfer price changes the firm's profit.”

    Theorem 7.3 part 3 shows that \(\Delta_S + \Delta_B = \Sigma\) for every \(t\). The price moves profit between the two divisions. The firm's total from the sale is \(\Sigma\) whichever price is set, so a price change that raises one division's profit lowers the other's by the same amount.

8Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The bounds for the worked case

For \(v = 40\), \(p_m = 60\), \(K = 10{,}000\), \(E = 8{,}000\), \(q = 5{,}000\), \(r = 80\), compute \(D\), \(t_{\min}\), \(t_{\max}\) and \(\Sigma\).

Solution

\(D = 5{,}000 + 8{,}000 - 10{,}000 = 3{,}000\). \(t_{\min} = 40 + 3{,}000 \times 20 / 5{,}000 = 52\) SCU. \(t_{\max} = 80\) SCU. \(\Sigma = 5{,}000 \times 40 - 3{,}000 \times 20 = 140{,}000\) SCU. The range \([52, 80]\) is nonempty, as \(\Sigma > 0\) requires.

M2 · Extra capacity

Keep M1 but set \(K = 12{,}000\). Find the new \(D\), \(t_{\min}\) and \(\Sigma\), and say which bound moved.

Solution

\(D = \max(0,\, 5{,}000 + 8{,}000 - 12{,}000) = 1{,}000\). \(t_{\min} = 40 + 1{,}000 \times 20/5{,}000 = 44\) SCU. \(\Sigma = 5{,}000 \times 40 - 1{,}000 \times 20 = 180{,}000\) SCU. The floor fell from 52 to 44 and the ceiling stayed at 80, because \(t_{\max} = r\) does not depend on capacity.

M3 · An empty range

Keep M1 but set \(r = 50\). Show that the range is empty and find \(\Sigma\).

Solution

\(t_{\min} = 52\) is unchanged, and \(t_{\max} = r = 50\). Since \(52 > 50\), the range is empty. \(\Sigma = 5{,}000 \times (50-40) - 3{,}000 \times 20 = 50{,}000 - 60{,}000 = -10{,}000\) SCU, which is negative, as Theorem 7.3 part 2 requires.

Proof

P1 · The sum does not depend on the price

Show that \(\Delta_S(t) + \Delta_B(t)\) is the same for every \(t\), and state its value.

Solution

\(\Delta_S(t) + \Delta_B(t) = q(t-v) - D(p_m-v) + q(r-t)\). The terms in \(t\) are \(qt - qt = 0\). What remains is \(-qv + qr - D(p_m-v) = q(r-v) - D(p_m-v) = \Sigma\). The sum is therefore \(\Sigma\) for every \(t\), which is Theorem 7.3 part 3.

P2 · The lowest price never exceeds \(p_m\)

Using \(E \le K\), \(q \le K\) and \(p_m \ge v\), prove that \(t_{\min} \le p_m\).

Solution

\(E \le K\) gives \(q + E - K \le q\), so \(D = \max(0, q+E-K) \le q\), because \(q \ge 0\). Since \(p_m - v \ge 0\), \(D(p_m - v) \le q(p_m - v)\). Dividing by \(q > 0\) gives \(D(p_m-v)/q \le p_m - v\), so \(t_{\min} = v + D(p_m-v)/q \le p_m\).

P3 · A zero-cost case

Suppose \(v = 0\). Show that \(t_{\min} = D p_m / q\) and that the range is nonempty if and only if \(q\,r \ge D\,p_m\).

Solution

Setting \(v = 0\) in Lemma 7.2 gives \(t_{\min} = D p_m / q\). Since \(t_{\min} \le r\) is \(Dp_m/q \le r\), multiplying by \(q > 0\) gives \(D p_m \le q r\). This is the condition \(\Sigma \ge 0\) with \(v = 0\), because \(\Sigma = q r - D p_m\).

E3 · Which bound a case hits

The output shows \(D = 0\) for cases 1, 2 and 5. Their lowest prices are 3400.00, 3600.00 and 3600.00 cents, which are 34, 36 and 36 SCU. Each equals the variable cost in its row, since \(t_{\min} = v + 0\). The output lists the variable cost in cents, so a \(D = 0\) row has \(t_{\min} = v\).

Research

R1 · Add an outside option with a cost

Extend the model so that each outside unit costs \(c\) to deliver, and derive the new \(t_{\min}\). State which term changes and why \(t_{\max}\) does not.

Solution

The outside contribution per displaced unit becomes \(p_m - v - c\), because delivery is a cost that the outside sale carries and the internal unit does not. Replacing \(p_m - v\) by \(p_m - v - c\) in Lemma 7.2 gives \(t_{\min} = v + D(p_m - v - c)/q\). \(t_{\max} = r\) does not change, because the buyer's gain \(q(r-t)\) does not involve the supplier's outside sales. The change is a hypothesis about the case and is not supported by any source here.

R2 · Two buyers

Suppose two buyers, with ceilings \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), ask for \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) units from the same capacity. State which constraint binds and how the range changes. Leave the answer as a model statement.

Solution

Capacity is shared, so \(q_1 + q_2 \le K\) is required, and the displaced units become \(D = \max(0,\, q_1 + q_2 + E - K)\). The lowest price for each buyer depends on the total displaced units, not only on its own order, so each buyer's floor changes with the other's order. The model in §1 does not cover this case, and the range in Theorem 7.3 does not apply to either buyer alone.

R3 · Test the market-price rule

Design a test of whether a firm's divisions reach profitable sales more often under a market-price rule than under a cost-based rule. State the outcome that would count against the market-price rule.

Solution

Use the feasibility rule of Theorem 7.3 as the benchmark: a sale is profitable for the firm when \(\Sigma \ge 0\). Compare the share of sales that the market-price rule clears (a price inside the range) with the share that the cost-based rule clears. The market-price rule counts against itself if it clears a smaller share of sales with \(\Sigma \ge 0\), as in the misconception example in §7. The test needs real division data, which this lesson does not have.
