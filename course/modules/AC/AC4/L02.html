Cost-Volume-Profit Analysis and Break-Even

  • Uses: the contribution margin, fixed, variable and mixed costs, the income statement
  • Introduces: cost-volume-profit analysis; the break-even point
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or lab
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A manager who proposes a price, a new lease or a second shift needs to know how much volume the change requires before it pays. This lesson relates operating income (revenue less total cost in this model) to volume, finds the volume at which operating income crosses zero, and derives the revenue at that volume. Section 3 then turns to the planning measures built on it, and the lesson shows where a single break-even formula stops being correct, using a cost structure with a step (a fixed cost that jumps at a threshold volume).

1The CVP relation

Definition 1.1 (cost-volume-profit analysis)

Cost-volume-profit analysis (CVP) relates operating income to the selling price \(p\), the variable cost per unit \(v\), the fixed cost \(F\) and the volume \(Q\), through the relation \(OI(Q) = pQ - vQ - F = mQ - F\), with \(m = p - v\) (Lesson 1, Proposition 2.2). Its assumptions are: one product, or a fixed mix of products; a price and a variable cost per unit that do not change over the range of volumes; a fixed cost that does not change over that range; and units produced equal to units sold.

The assumptions in Definition 1.1 are conventions of the model. Section 6 lists the places where they fail, and the lab in Section 4 breaks one of them on purpose.

2The break-even point

Definition 2.1 (break-even point)

The break-even point is a volume \(Q^\ast\) at which operating income is zero, \(OI(Q^\ast) = 0\). Its revenue counterpart is \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = pQ^\ast\).

Proposition 2.2 (the single break-even volume and its revenue)

Let \(m > 0\). Under Definition 1.1 with constant \(F\), the unique volume with \(OI(Q) = 0\) is \(Q^\ast = F/m\). Operating income is negative for \(Q < Q^\ast\) and positive for \(Q > Q^\ast\). The revenue at break-even is \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = pQ^\ast = F/(m/p)\), where \(m/p\) is the contribution margin ratio.

Proof

Solve \(mQ - F = 0\) for \(Q\), which gives \(Q^\ast = F/m\) because \(m \ne 0\). Since \(OI(Q) = mQ - F\) has slope \(m > 0\), \(OI\) is strictly increasing, so it is negative below \(Q^\ast\), positive above it, and zero only at \(Q^\ast\). Then \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = pF/m = F/(m/p)\) by multiplying numerator and denominator by \(1/p\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (whole units)

Volume is a whole number of units. When \(F\) and \(m\) are whole numbers of cents, \(Q^\ast\) is a whole number exactly when \(m\) divides \(F\). Because \(OI\) is strictly increasing, the smallest whole volume with \(OI \ge 0\) is \(\lceil Q^\ast \rceil\), the least integer that is at least \(Q^\ast\). The argument is one step: \(OI(Q^\ast) = 0\) and \(OI\) is strictly increasing, so \(OI(n) < 0\) for every integer \(n < Q^\ast\) and \(OI(n) \ge 0\) for every integer \(n \ge Q^\ast\). A break-even of 88.89 units therefore becomes 89 units, and 88 units still lose money. Lab 1 prints both values.

Example 2.4 (a worked break-even)

A product sells for 50 USD a unit, has a variable cost of 30 USD a unit and a fixed cost of 60,000 USD a month. Then \(m = 20\) USD, \(Q^\ast = 60000/20 = 3000\) units, the contribution margin ratio is \(20/50 = 0.4\), and \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = 60000/0.4 = 150000\) USD. At 3,000 units revenue is \(50 \times 3000 = 150000\) USD and total cost is \(60000 + 30 \times 3000 = 150000\) USD, so operating income is zero.

3Margin of safety and sensitivity

Definition 3.1 (margin of safety)

For a planned volume \(Q_{\text{plan}} > Q^\ast\), the margin of safety in units is \(Q_{\text{plan}} - Q^\ast\), and as a share of plan it is \((Q_{\text{plan}} - Q^\ast)/Q_{\text{plan}}\). It is the amount by which volume can fall before operating income reaches zero.

Proposition 3.2 (operating leverage as an exact elasticity)

Let \(OI(Q) \ne 0\). Define the degree of operating leverage at \(Q\) as \(DOL(Q) = mQ/OI(Q)\). For any change in volume \(h\) with \(Q\) and \(Q + h\) both in a range where the price, the variable cost and the fixed cost of Definition 1.1 do not change (no step, meaning no jump in fixed cost at a threshold volume), the percentage change in operating income equals \(DOL(Q)\) times the percentage change in volume, exactly: \(\dfrac{OI(Q+h) - OI(Q)}{OI(Q)} = DOL(Q)\cdot\dfrac{h}{Q}\).

Proof

\(OI(Q + h) - OI(Q) = m(Q+h) - F - (mQ - F) = mh\). Divide by \(OI(Q)\) and multiply the right side by \(Q/Q\): \(mh/OI(Q) = (mQ/OI(Q))(h/Q)\). The left side is the percentage change in income and the right side is \(DOL(Q)\) times the percentage change in volume. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3.3 (a fixed-cost change moves the break-even volume by a known amount)

In the single-segment model of Proposition 2.2, if fixed cost rises by \(\Delta F\) and the margin \(m\) is unchanged, the break-even volume rises by exactly \(\Delta F/m\) units. The derivative of \(Q^\ast\) with respect to \(F\) is \(1/m\).

Proof

\((F + \Delta F)/m - F/m = \Delta F/m\) by Proposition 2.2. The derivative of \(F/m\) with respect to \(F\) is \(1/m\). \(\blacksquare\)

4Break-even under a mixed cost structure

A cost structure can mix fixed and variable parts (Lesson 1), and a fixed cost can step up at a volume. A step cost is a fixed cost that jumps by a set amount when volume passes a threshold.

Proposition 4.1 (a step cost creates one break-even point per segment)

Let operating income be \(OI(Q) = mQ - F - S\,\mathbf{1}\{Q > Q_{\mathrm{s}}\}\), with a step of size \(S \ge 0\) above the threshold \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\), and \(m > 0\). Then \(OI\) is strictly increasing on \([0, Q_{\mathrm{s}}]\) and on \((Q_{\mathrm{s}}, \infty)\), it jumps down by \(S\) at \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\), and it has at most one zero in each segment. Each zero in a segment is given by the formula of Proposition 2.2 for that segment's fixed cost.

Proof

On \([0, Q_{\mathrm{s}}]\) the function is \(mQ - F\), and on \((Q_{\mathrm{s}}, \infty)\) it is \(mQ - F - S\). Each piece has slope \(m > 0\), so each is strictly increasing and has at most one zero. The zero on \([0, Q_{\mathrm{s}}]\) is \(F/m\) if \(F/m \le Q_{\mathrm{s}}\). The zero on \((Q_{\mathrm{s}}, \infty)\) is \((F + S)/m\) if that value exceeds \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\). Both formulas are those of Proposition 2.2 with the fixed cost of the segment. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 4.1 has a consequence that a single formula hides. Applying \(F/m\) to the total fixed cost, including the step, finds the second zero and misses the first. Applying it to the fixed cost before the step finds the first and misses the second. The lab below prints both formulas and the runs of loss-making whole volumes from 0 to 5,000 units, each run by its first and last volume.

Lab 1 · Break-even under a step cost, one SIMULATED product

The lab uses a SIMULATED product with a price of 25.00 USD, a variable cost of 11.50 USD a unit (materials and labour 10.00 USD, the variable part of utilities 1.50 USD), a fixed cost of 1,200.00 USD a month, and a step of 27,000.00 USD a month for a second shift above 2,000 units. Predicted from Propositions 2.2 and 4.1, before running: the margin is 13.50 USD a unit; the formula with the fixed cost before the step gives 88.89 units, so the first profitable whole volume is 89; the loss-making volumes are 0 to 88 and 2001 to 2088; the formula with the step included gives 2,088.89 units; at a planned 2,500 units operating income is 5,550.00 USD; and an increase of 100.00 USD in fixed cost raises the first break-even by 7.41 units.

"""AC4 lab L02: break-even under a mixed cost structure with a step cost.

SIMULATED: a single product in a single month. Every figure is invented for this lab and is not
any firm's. Money is integer cents and volume is integer units. Break-even points are exact
Fractions; a value is shown with two decimals only when it is printed. Standard library only;
no network; every number printed is computed below.

Cost structure, all in cents:
  price per unit                      2500
  variable cost per unit              1150 (materials and labour 1000; variable part of utilities 150)
  fixed cost per month                120000 (rent and the fixed part of utilities)
  step cost per month, above 2000 units  2700000 (a second shift)
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from fractions import Fraction

PRICE_CENTS = 2500
VARIABLE_CENTS = 1150
FIXED_CENTS = 120_000
STEP_CENTS = 2_700_000
STEP_ABOVE_UNITS = 2000
SCAN_LIMIT_UNITS = 5000
PLANNED_UNITS = 2500


def contribution_margin(price: int, variable: int) -> int:
    """Contribution margin per unit in cents: price minus variable cost. Raises ValueError if it is not positive."""
    margin = price - variable
    if margin <= 0:
        raise ValueError("contribution margin per unit must be positive for a break-even point to exist")
    return margin


def operating_income(units: int) -> int:
    """Operating income in cents at an integer volume of units. Raises ValueError for negative units.

    The step cost applies to every unit above STEP_ABOVE_UNITS, so the function jumps down at 2001 units.
    """
    if units < 0:
        raise ValueError("units must be a non-negative integer")
    step = STEP_CENTS if units > STEP_ABOVE_UNITS else 0
    return (PRICE_CENTS - VARIABLE_CENTS) * units - FIXED_CENTS - step


def break_even(fixed_cents: int, margin_cents: int) -> Fraction:
    """The single-segment break-even volume F / m, as an exact Fraction, in units."""
    return Fraction(fixed_cents, margin_cents)


def loss_runs(limit: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """Maximal inclusive runs of integer volumes in [0, limit] at which operating income is negative."""
    runs: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    start: int | None = None
    for units in range(limit + 1):
        loss = operating_income(units) < 0
        if loss and start is None:
            start = units
        elif not loss and start is not None:
            runs.append((start, units - 1))
            start = None
    if start is not None:
        runs.append((start, limit))
    return runs


def dollars(cents: int | Fraction) -> str:
    """Format cents as USD with two decimals, half up, for printing only."""
    value = Fraction(cents)
    scaled = (2 * value.numerator + value.denominator) // (2 * value.denominator)
    sign = "-" if scaled < 0 else ""
    whole, part = divmod(abs(scaled), 100)
    return f"{sign}{whole}.{part:02d}"


def main() -> None:
    """Print the margin, the single-formula break-even, the exact loss runs, and the sensitivities."""
    margin = contribution_margin(PRICE_CENTS, VARIABLE_CENTS)
    print(f"margin_cents_per_unit {margin} ratio {Fraction(margin, PRICE_CENTS)}")
    first = break_even(FIXED_CENTS, margin)
    first_whole = -(-first.numerator // first.denominator)
    print(f"single_formula_break_even_units {float(first):.2f} first_whole_unit {first_whole}")
    print(f"check operating_income(88) {dollars(operating_income(88))}", end=" ")
    print(f"operating_income(89) {dollars(operating_income(89))}")
    revenue = PRICE_CENTS * first
    print(f"revenue_break_even_usd {dollars(revenue)}")
    wrong = break_even(FIXED_CENTS + STEP_CENTS, margin)
    print(f"formula_with_step_cost_units {float(wrong):.2f}")
    for lo, hi in loss_runs(SCAN_LIMIT_UNITS):
        print(f"loss run units {lo} to {hi}")
    planned = operating_income(PLANNED_UNITS)
    print(f"planned_units {PLANNED_UNITS} operating_income_usd {dollars(planned)}")
    upper = break_even(FIXED_CENTS + STEP_CENTS, margin)
    safety = PLANNED_UNITS - upper
    print(f"margin_of_safety_units {float(safety):.2f} share_of_planned {float(safety / PLANNED_UNITS * 100):.2f}")
    contribution = margin * PLANNED_UNITS
    leverage = Fraction(contribution, planned)
    up_one = operating_income(PLANNED_UNITS + PLANNED_UNITS // 100)
    growth = Fraction(up_one - planned, planned) * 100
    print(f"operating_leverage {float(leverage):.2f} volume_up_1pct_income_change_pct {float(growth):.2f}")
    raised = break_even(FIXED_CENTS + 10_000, margin)
    print(f"fixed_plus_10000_cents_moves_break_even_units {float(raised - first):.2f}")
    for label, args in (("zero margin", (2500, 2500)), ("negative units", None)):
        try:
            if args is None:
                operating_income(-1)
            else:
                break_even(FIXED_CENTS, contribution_margin(*args))
        except ValueError as exc:
            print(f"ValueError for {label}: {exc}")
        else:
            print(f"no error for {label}")

The lab shows what Proposition 4.1 implies: in this SIMULATED product, operating income is negative from 0 to 88 units, positive from 89 to 2,000 units, negative again from 2,001 to 2,088 units and positive from 2,089 units upward. 5Multiple products and the other assumptions

Proposition 5.1 (a fixed mix collapses to one weighted margin)

Let products \(k = 1, \dots, K\) have contribution margins \(m_k\) and a constant share \(w_k\) of total units, with \(w_k \ge 0\) and \(\sum_k w_k = 1\). Then total contribution at \(Q\) units is \(Q\bar m\), where \(\bar m = \sum_k w_k m_k\), and the break-even volume in total units is \(Q^\ast = F/\bar m\) when \(\bar m > 0\).

Proof

Product \(k\) sells \(w_k Q\) units, so its contribution is \(m_k w_k Q\). Summing over \(k\) gives \(Q\sum_k w_k m_k = Q\bar m\). Operating income is \(Q\bar m - F\), which is Proposition 2.2 with \(m\) replaced by \(\bar m\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 5.2 (where the mix is not fixed)

Proposition 5.1 requires the shares \(w_k\) to stay constant as \(Q\) changes. When a product's share depends on volume, \(\bar m\) depends on \(Q\), the relation need not be linear, the break-even can be found by scanning operating income over the volumes (the scan that Lab 1 runs on its step cost; Lab 1 does not model a changing mix), and Proposition 5.1, which assumes constant shares, does not apply across such a change; each segment needs its own weighted margin, as Research problem R3 shows. This lesson does not model a changing mix.

6Where the canon breaks

The CVP relation is an exact description of a model, and four features of the model can fail when it is used.

  • Steps and the relevant range. Lab 1 shows that a single fixed cost, applied across a step, gives the upper zero and misses the first break-even. A CVP statement applies inside the range in which its costs hold, as Definition 1.1 requires.
  • The inputs are estimates. When the variable cost and fixed cost in a CVP analysis come from a cost split of the kind in Lesson 1, that split carries its own error (Lesson 1, Remark 3.3). The break-even inherits the error. Proposition 2.2 is exact given the inputs and says nothing about the inputs.
  • Units produced and units sold. The model sets production equal to sales and charges the fixed cost \(F\) to the period, with no charge that depends on production. This lesson makes no claim about reported income in a period when production and sales differ.
  • The mix. Proposition 5.1 gives the break-even for a fixed mix. A mix that shifts with volume, or a price that changes with volume, can make operating income non-linear in \(Q\), and then Propositions 2.2 and 3.2 do not apply as stated.

7Misconceptions

  1. “Break-even is the volume at which profit is largest.”

    In the single-segment model operating income is a straight line (Proposition 2.2). Break-even is its zero, not its maximum. A line with positive slope has no maximum on \(Q \ge 0\), so the claim does not describe the model.

  2. “The formula F/m gives the break-even volume for any cost structure.”

    The formula gives the zero of one segment (Proposition 4.1). Lab 1 applies it to the fixed cost before the step and gets 88.89 units, which is correct, and to the fixed cost including the step and gets 2,088.89 units, which is a second zero, not the first. A cost structure with a step needs one formula per segment.

  3. “A plan above break-even is safe.”

    The margin of safety is the volume by which the plan can fall before income reaches zero (Definition 3.1). At 2,001 units in Lab 1, the plan is above the first break-even and loses money. For a plan whose volume lies in a segment that has a zero, operating income is non-negative exactly when that volume is at or above the segment's zero (Proposition 4.1); a segment with no zero has operating income of one sign throughout.

  4. “A product with a higher break-even is a worse product.”

    The break-even depends on the margin and the fixed cost together. Proposition 2.2 gives \(Q^\ast = F/m\), so a product with a larger fixed cost and a larger margin can have a lower break-even than one with a smaller fixed cost. The comparison needs both inputs.

8Problem set

Attempt each problem before opening its solution. Every problem has a full worked solution. Problems marked Lab use the Lab 1 output.

Mechanics

M1 · Break-even volume and revenue

A product sells for 80 USD, has a variable cost of 50 USD a unit and fixed cost of 45,000 USD a month. Find the break-even volume and the break-even revenue.

Solution

The margin is \(m = 80 - 50 = 30\) USD. By Proposition 2.2, \(Q^\ast = 45000/30 = 1500\) units. The margin ratio is \(30/80 = 0.375\), so \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = 45000/0.375 = 120000\) USD. Check: \(80 \times 1500 = 120000\) USD of revenue, and the total cost is \(45000 + 50 \times 1500 = 120000\) USD, so operating income is zero.

M2 · Whole units

A product's margin is 7 USD a unit and its fixed cost is 10,000 USD a month. What is the break-even volume in whole units, and what is operating income at that volume and one unit below it?

Solution

\(Q^\ast = 10000/7 = 1428.57\ldots\) units. By Remark 2.3 the smallest whole volume with non-negative income is \(\lceil 1428.57\ldots \rceil = 1429\). At 1,429 units, operating income is \(7 \times 1429 - 10000 = 10003 - 10000 = 3\) USD. At 1,428 units it is \(7 \times 1428 - 10000 = 9996 - 10000 = -4\) USD.

M3 · Margin of safety

A plant plans 1,000 units and its break-even volume is 800 units. Find the margin of safety in units and as a share of plan. Then the fixed cost rises and the break-even moves to 900 units; find the margin of safety at the same plan.

Solution

Definition 3.1 gives \(1000 - 800 = 200\) units, and \(200/1000 = 20\%\) of plan. If the break-even moves to 900 units, the margin of safety is \(1000 - 900 = 100\) units, or 10% of plan. The move of 100 units in the break-even is the fixed-cost increase divided by the margin, as Proposition 3.3 requires.

Proof

P1 · Uniqueness of the zero

Prove that, for \(m > 0\), \(OI(Q) = mQ - F\) has exactly one zero, and that the zero is \(F/m\), without using the formula for \(Q^\ast\) to find it.

Solution

Suppose \(OI(Q_1) = OI(Q_2) = 0\) with \(Q_1 < Q_2\). Then \(mQ_2 - mQ_1 = OI(Q_2) - OI(Q_1) = 0\), so \(m(Q_2 - Q_1) = 0\). Since \(m > 0\) and \(Q_2 - Q_1 > 0\), this is impossible, so there is at most one zero. For existence, \(OI(F/m) = m(F/m) - F = 0\). Hence the zero is \(F/m\).

P2 · Elasticity and finite change for a linear income function

Using Proposition 3.2, show that the elasticity of \(OI\) with respect to \(Q\) equals \(DOL(Q)\), and that the finite percentage change over a volume change \(h\) equals \(DOL(Q) \cdot h/Q\) with no limit needed.

Solution

The elasticity of \(OI\) with respect to \(Q\) is \(\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(OI(Q+h) - OI(Q))/OI(Q)}{h/Q} = \frac{Q}{OI(Q)} \cdot OI'(Q) = \frac{mQ}{OI(Q)} = DOL(Q)\). For the linear function, Proposition 3.2 gives the same ratio for every finite \(h\), with no limit needed, because \(OI(Q+h) - OI(Q) = mh\) exactly. The two statements agree because \(OI\) is linear: for a curved function the finite-\(h\) change would differ from the elasticity.

P3 · Weighted margin

Two products have margins 10 USD and 30 USD, and each makes half of the units. Show that the weighted margin is 20 USD and that the break-even for 60,000 USD of fixed cost is 3,000 total units.

Solution

By Proposition 5.1, \(\bar m = 0.5 \times 10 + 0.5 \times 30 = 20\) USD. The break-even volume in total units is \(60000/20 = 3000\). At that total, each product sells 1,500 units, its contribution is \(10 \times 1500 = 15000\) USD for the first and \(30 \times 1500 = 45000\) USD for the second, which sum to 60,000 USD, exactly the fixed cost.

E1 · Lab · The loss-making volumes

From the Lab 1 output, list the volumes at which the SIMULATED product loses money, and state how many whole units each run contains.

Solution

The output prints two runs: 0 to 88 units, which is 89 whole volumes, and 2,001 to 2,088 units, which is 88 whole volumes. The first run ends one whole unit below 89, the first whole volume with non-negative income, as Remark 2.3 predicts. The second run starts one unit above the step threshold of 2,000 units, because the step applies to every volume above 2,000.

E2 · Lab · Margin of safety at the plan

The plan is 2,500 units. From the Lab 1 output, what is the margin of safety, in units and as a share of plan, and which break-even is it measured from?

Solution

It is measured from the upper break-even, 2,088.89 units, because 2,500 units lies in the upper segment. The margin is \(2500 - 2088.89 = 411.11\) units, which the output prints as 411.11, and \(411.11/2500 = 16.44\%\). Measured from the first break-even of 88.89 units, the margin would be 2,411.11 units, a figure that does not describe the plan's exposure, because the second shift is in force at 2,500 units.

E3 · Lab · Which formula is right?

Using only the Lab 1 output, decide which of the printed values 88.89 and 2,088.89 bounds the loss run that contains 2,001 units, and explain how the output shows it.

Solution

The output lists 2,001 to 2,088 as a loss run, so 2,001 units lose money. The run 2,001 to 2,088 ends one whole unit below 2,089, the first whole volume with non-negative income in the upper segment, and 2,088.89 is the zero of that segment. The value 88.89 is the zero of the lower segment and does not bound this run, because the step has applied at 2,001 units. Neither number is the break-even for every volume, which is the content of Proposition 4.1.

Research

R1 · Fixed or step?

A firm asks whether a second shift should be counted in its fixed cost or handled as a step. Specify the test the decision needs, the data it requires, and what the break-even analysis can and cannot say before that test is run.

Solution

The decision depends on whether the second shift's cost is incurred for every volume above a threshold. A step is the right treatment if the cost changes when volume crosses the threshold and stays fixed on each side of it, and a fixed cost is right if the cost is incurred at every volume in the relevant range. The test needs the cost of the shift over a history that includes volumes on both sides of the threshold, and the threshold itself with its uncertainty. The break-even analysis can compute both segments once those inputs are given. It cannot say whether the threshold will be crossed, which is a question about demand.

R2 · Sensitivity to price

Derive the rate of change of the single-segment break-even with respect to price, \(dQ^\ast/dp\), for \(Q^\ast = F/(p - v)\). Evaluate it at the Lab 1 inputs, where the price is 25.00 USD and the variable cost 11.50 USD. Then compare the derivative with the change in break-even for a 1.00 USD rise in price.

Solution

Differentiate: \(dQ^\ast/dp = -F/(p - v)^2\). With \(F = 120000\) cents and \(p - v = 1350\) cents, the derivative is \(-120000/1350^2 \approx -0.0658\) units per cent, or \(-6.58\) units per USD. The exact change for a 1.00 USD rise (100 cents) is \(120000/1450 - 120000/1350 = 82.76 - 88.89 = -6.13\) units, which differs from the derivative because the function is curved in \(p\). The derivative is the rate at a point and the difference is the change over an interval.

R3 · Two products with a shifting mix

Two products have margins 10 USD and 30 USD. Product 2 is sold only when total volume is 1,000 units or more, and at and above that volume it makes up half the units. Explain why Proposition 5.1 does not apply to the whole volume range, and find the break-even for 60,000 USD of fixed cost in the range below 1,000 units.

Solution

Proposition 5.1 requires constant shares. Here the share of product 2 is zero below 1,000 units and one half at or above it, so the weighted margin is 10 USD below 1,000 units and 20 USD above. Below 1,000 units the break-even is \(60000/10 = 6000\) units, which is outside the range, so there is no break-even below 1,000 units. Above 1,000 units the weighted margin is 20 USD and the break-even is 3,000 units, which lies inside the range. The break-even is therefore the upper segment's zero, and the analysis has the same structure as Proposition 4.1.

  1. Which expression is operating income under cost-volume-profit analysis?

    Options

    Revenue is \(pQ\) and the variable cost is \(vQ\), so \(OI = (p - v)Q - F\) (Definition 1.1).

  2. Fixed cost is 60,000 USD a month and the margin is 20 USD a unit. What is the break-even volume, in units?

    \(Q^\ast = F/m = 60000/20 = 3000\) (Proposition 2.2).

  3. With a price of 50 USD, a variable cost of 30 USD and fixed cost of 60,000 USD a month, what is the break-even revenue, in USD?

    \(R^{\mathrm{BE}} = F/(m/p) = 60000/0.4 = 150000\) USD (Example 2.4).

  4. The single-segment break-even is 88.89 units. What is the smallest whole volume with non-negative operating income?

    The ceiling of 88.89 is 89 (Remark 2.3). At 88 units income is negative.

  5. A plan of 2,500 units against a break-even of 2,000 units gives what margin of safety, in units?

    The margin of safety is \(Q_{\text{plan}} - Q^\ast = 2500 - 2000 = 500\) units (Definition 3.1).

  6. A margin of 10 USD a unit, a volume of 1,000 units and fixed cost of 5,000 USD give what degree of operating leverage?

    \(OI = 10000 - 5000 = 5000\) USD, so \(DOL = mQ/OI = 10000/5000 = 2\) (Proposition 3.2).

  7. Fixed cost rises by 10,000 USD and the margin stays at 13.50 USD. By how much does the break-even volume rise, if the single segment still applies?

    Options

    Proposition 3.3: the break-even rises by \(\Delta F/m\) units, here \(10000/13.50 \approx 740.7\) units. Lab 1 gives 7.41 units for a 100.00 USD rise, which is 100.00 / 13.50 = 7.41 units.

  8. Under a step cost that applies above a threshold, what is the correct use of \(F/m\)?

    Options

    Proposition 4.1: each segment has its own fixed cost and its own zero, and a zero outside its segment is not a break-even of the function.

  9. A fixed mix of two products, with margins 10 USD and 30 USD and equal shares of units, has what weighted margin?

    Options

    \(\bar m = 0.5 \times 10 + 0.5 \times 30 = 20\) USD (Proposition 5.1).

  10. Which statement about break-even is correct?

    Options

    Definition 2.1. Operating income is a straight line in the single-segment model, so break-even is its zero (Misconceptions, first item). Remark 2.3 covers the integer question.
