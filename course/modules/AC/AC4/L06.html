Budget variance analysis

A profit variance is one number, and a manager needs to know which decision produced it. The analysis names the parts. Four are of interest here: a sales price effect, a sales volume effect, a sales mix effect, and an efficiency effect on material. This lesson defines each one, proves that the four sum exactly to the change in contribution, and states the conditions under which a part is zero. Lab 1 reconciles the four parts for the SIMULATED case of Lesson L05, with no residual.

1Definitions and notation

The sign convention is that of Lesson L05, Definition 1.3, applied to the contribution line. The case has products \(i \in \{A, B\}\) and one material. Let \(q_{b,i}\) and \(q_{a,i}\) be the budgeted and actual units of product \(i\), and let \(Q_b = \sum_i q_{b,i}\) and \(Q_a = \sum_i q_{a,i}\) be the totals. Let \(p_{b,i}\) and \(p_{a,i}\) be the budgeted and actual selling prices per unit. Let \(s_i\) be the standard kilograms of material per unit of product \(i\), and \(\pi\) the standard material price per kilogram, in cents. Let \(\text{kg}_{a,i}\) be the kilograms of material actually used for product \(i\), so that the actual material price is taken to equal \(\pi\) (see Section 4).

Definition 1.1 (standard contribution, budget mix and the standard allowed)

The budgeted contribution per unit of product \(i\) at standard material cost is \(\text{CM}_i = p_{b,i} - s_i \pi\). The budget mix share of product \(i\) is \(m_i = q_{b,i} / Q_b\), and the budget average contribution per unit is \(\overline{\text{CM}}_b = \sum_i m_i \text{CM}_i\). The standard kilograms allowed for the actual output of product \(i\) are \(s_i\, q_{a,i}\).

Definition 1.2 (the four variances and the contribution variance)

Budget variance analysis assigns the contribution variance to four named effects by the following formulas. The contribution is revenue less the cost of the material used.

  • Sales price variance. \(\text{SP} = \sum_i (p_{a,i} - p_{b,i})\, q_{a,i}\).
  • Sales volume variance. \(V = (Q_a - Q_b)\, \overline{\text{CM}}_b\).
  • Sales mix variance. \(M = \sum_i \text{CM}_i \big(q_{a,i} - Q_a m_i\big)\).
  • Efficiency variance. \(E = -\pi \sum_i \big(\text{kg}_{a,i} - s_i\, q_{a,i}\big)\).

The contribution variance is \(T = C_a - C_b\), where \(C_a = \sum_i \big(p_{a,i}\, q_{a,i} - \pi\, \text{kg}_{a,i}\big)\) is actual contribution and \(C_b = \sum_i \text{CM}_i\, q_{b,i}\) is budget contribution.

2The four effects sum exactly to the contribution variance

Proposition 2.1 (exact reconciliation)

Under Definition 1.2, \(T = \text{SP} + V + M + E\) with no residual, for any actual units, actual prices and actual kilograms.

Proof

Step 1. Write \(C_a\) with the sales price variance. Since \(\sum_i p_{a,i} q_{a,i} = \text{SP} + \sum_i p_{b,i} q_{a,i}\), we get \(C_a = \text{SP} + \sum_i p_{b,i}\, q_{a,i} - \pi \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\).

Step 2. Write the efficiency variance as \(E = -\pi \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i} + \pi \sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\), so that \(-\pi \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i} = E - \pi \sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\). Substituting into step 1 gives \(C_a = \text{SP} + E + \sum_i (p_{b,i} - s_i \pi) q_{a,i} = \text{SP} + E + \sum_i \text{CM}_i\, q_{a,i}\).

Step 3. Subtract \(C_b = \sum_i \text{CM}_i\, q_{b,i}\): \(T = \text{SP} + E + \sum_i \text{CM}_i (q_{a,i} - q_{b,i})\).

Step 4. Split the last sum. Since \(q_{a,i} = (q_{a,i} - Q_a m_i) + Q_a m_i\) and \(\sum_i m_i \text{CM}_i = \overline{\text{CM}}_b\), we have \(\sum_i \text{CM}_i\, q_{a,i} = M + Q_a \overline{\text{CM}}_b\). Also \(\sum_i \text{CM}_i\, q_{b,i} = Q_b \overline{\text{CM}}_b\), because \(q_{b,i} = Q_b m_i\). Therefore \(\sum_i \text{CM}_i (q_{a,i} - q_{b,i}) = M + (Q_a - Q_b)\overline{\text{CM}}_b = M + V\).

Combining steps 3 and 4 gives \(T = \text{SP} + V + M + E\). Every step is an identity in the inputs, so the residual is zero for every set of inputs. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.2 (when mix and efficiency are zero)

(a) If \(q_{a,i} = Q_a m_i\) for every product \(i\), then \(M = 0\). (b) If \(\text{kg}_{a,i} = s_i q_{a,i}\) for every product \(i\), then \(E = 0\). (c) If the total kilograms used exceed the total standard allowed, \(\sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i} > \sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\), then \(E\) is negative. Only the direction stated is proved; the converses are not claimed.

Proof

Part (a). If \(q_{a,i} = Q_a m_i\) for every \(i\), each term \(\text{CM}_i(q_{a,i} - Q_a m_i)\) is zero, so \(M = 0\). Part (b). If \(\text{kg}_{a,i} = s_i q_{a,i}\) for every \(i\), the sum in Definition 1.2 is zero, so \(E = 0\). Part (c) follows because \(E = -\pi \sum_i (\text{kg}_{a,i} - s_i q_{a,i})\) with \(\pi > 0\), which is negative when \(\sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i} > \sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (the reference point of the split)

The split of the contribution variance into \(V\) and \(M\) depends on the reference. Both use the budget mix shares \(m_i\) and the budget average \(\overline{\text{CM}}_b\) of Definition 1.1. This lesson uses that budget reference throughout and does not evaluate any other reference point.

3Reconciling the L05 case

SIMULATED. The case is the case of Lesson L05. Budget units are 600 of A and 400 of B. Budget prices are 2,500 and 3,400 cents. Standard material is 2 kg and 3 kg per unit at 500 cents per kg. Actual units are 650 of A and 500 of B, at actual prices of 2,400 and 3,450 cents. Actual material is 1,350 kg for A and 1,600 kg for B. Fixed overhead is outside the four variances. It enters only at operating profit, where it adds a fixed-cost variance of −10,000 cents.

Lab 1 · The four variances reconcile to the contribution variance

Before running: the four variances should sum to the contribution variance, with zero residual. The lab predicts a sales price variance of −40,000 cents (A sells 100 cents below budget on 650 units, and B sells 50 cents above budget on 500 units), a volume variance of +249,000 (150 more units at the budget average of 1,660 cents), a mix variance of +16,000 (A is below its budget share and B above it, and B has the larger contribution), and an efficiency variance of −75,000 (150 kg used above the standard allowed, at 500 cents per kg). The contribution variance should be +150,000, and the operating variance, after the −10,000 fixed-cost variance, +140,000, which matches the static variance of Lesson L05.

"""AC4 L06 lab 1: the four-way variance decomposition, reconciled exactly. SIMULATED company.

SIMULATED: every input below is invented by the author for this lab. No company's accounts are read and no published
figure is reproduced. The lab shows that four named variances sum to the total contribution variance with zero
residual. It does not show how real firms classify their variances.

All money is integer cents. Fractions are used for the intermediate mix shares, so the reconciliation is exact, and the
lab fails loudly if any reported variance is not a whole number of cents.

Case (the same case as L05, lab 1):
    budget units        A 600, B 400
    budget price        A 2,500 cents, B 3,400 cents per unit
    standard material   A 2 kg, B 3 kg per unit, at 500 cents per kg (the actual price equals it)
    actual units        A 650, B 500
    actual price        A 2,400 cents, B 3,450 cents per unit
    actual material     A 1,350 kg, B 1,600 kg (2,950 kg in total)
    fixed overhead      budget 500,000 cents, actual 510,000 cents (outside the four variances)

Definitions used by the code. Let q_b,i and q_a,i be budget and actual units of product i, Q_b and Q_a the totals,
and m_i = q_b,i / Q_b the budget mix share. Let CM_i = p_b,i - s_i * pi be the budget contribution per unit at standard
cost, where s_i is standard kilograms per unit and pi the material price.

    sales price variance   SP = sum_i (p_a,i - p_b,i) * q_a,i
    sales volume variance  V  = (Q_a - Q_b) * CMbar_b, with CMbar_b = sum_i m_i * CM_i
    sales mix variance     M  = sum_i CM_i * (q_a,i - Q_a * m_i)
    efficiency variance    E  = -pi * sum_i (kg_a,i - s_i * q_a,i)

The four sum to the total contribution variance T = C_a - C_b. The code asserts this identity and
raises ValueError if the residual is not zero.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

MATERIAL_PRICE = 500  # cents per kg, standard and actual
FIXED_BUDGET = 500_000
FIXED_ACTUAL = 510_000


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Product:
    """Budget and actual inputs for one product. Money in cents; quantities in units or kilograms."""

    name: str
    price_budget: int  # cents per unit
    std_kg: int  # standard kilograms of material per unit
    units_budget: int
    price_actual: int  # cents per unit
    units_actual: int
    kg_actual: int  # kilograms of material used


PRODUCTS: tuple[Product, ...] = (
    Product("A", 2_500, 2, 600, 2_400, 650, 1_350),
    Product("B", 3_400, 3, 400, 3_450, 500, 1_600),
)


def whole_cents(value: Fraction, label: str) -> int:
    """Return value as an integer number of cents, or fail loudly if it is a fraction of a cent."""
    if value.denominator != 1:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} is {value}, which is not a whole number of cents")
    return value.numerator


def contribution_per_unit(p: Product) -> int:
    """Budget contribution per unit at standard material cost, in cents: CM_i = p_b,i - s_i * pi."""
    return p.price_budget - p.std_kg * MATERIAL_PRICE


def decompose(products: tuple[Product, ...]) -> dict[str, int]:
    """The four variances and the total contribution variance, in cents. Raises ValueError if they do not reconcile."""
    q_b = sum(p.units_budget for p in products)
    q_a = sum(p.units_actual for p in products)
    cm_bar = Fraction(sum(contribution_per_unit(p) * p.units_budget for p in products), q_b)

    sales_price = sum((p.price_actual - p.price_budget) * p.units_actual for p in products)
    volume = Fraction(q_a - q_b) * cm_bar
    mix = sum(
        (Fraction(contribution_per_unit(p)) * (p.units_actual - Fraction(q_a * p.units_budget, q_b)) for p in products),
        Fraction(0),
    )
    efficiency = -MATERIAL_PRICE * sum(p.kg_actual - p.std_kg * p.units_actual for p in products)

    budget_contribution = sum(contribution_per_unit(p) * p.units_budget for p in products)
    actual_contribution = sum(p.price_actual * p.units_actual - MATERIAL_PRICE * p.kg_actual for p in products)
    total = actual_contribution - budget_contribution

    out = {
        "sales_price": sales_price,
        "sales_volume": whole_cents(volume, "sales volume variance"),
        "sales_mix": whole_cents(mix, "sales mix variance"),
        "efficiency": efficiency,
    }
    residual = total - sum(out.values())
    if residual != 0:
        raise ValueError(f"the four variances leave a residual of {residual} cents")
    out["total"] = total
    out["budget_contribution"] = budget_contribution
    out["actual_contribution"] = actual_contribution
    return out


def main() -> None:
    result = decompose(PRODUCTS)
    print(f"budget_contribution {result['budget_contribution']}")
    print(f"actual_contribution {result['actual_contribution']}")
    print(f"total_contribution_variance {result['total']}")
    print(f"sales_price_variance {result['sales_price']}")
    print(f"sales_volume_variance {result['sales_volume']}")
    print(f"sales_mix_variance {result['sales_mix']}")
    print(f"efficiency_variance {result['efficiency']}")
    print(f"sum_of_four {result['sales_price'] + result['sales_volume'] + result['sales_mix'] + result['efficiency']}")
    print(f"fixed_cost_variance {FIXED_BUDGET - FIXED_ACTUAL}")
    print(f"operating_profit_variance {result['total'] + (FIXED_BUDGET - FIXED_ACTUAL)}")

    # Check 1: actual units in the budget mix (690 A and 460 B, 1,150 in total) at standard material use and budget
    # prices leave the mix and efficiency variances at zero. Only the volume variance remains.
    on_mix = tuple(
        Product(p.name, p.price_budget, p.std_kg, p.units_budget, p.price_budget, units, units * p.std_kg)
        for p, units in zip(PRODUCTS, (690, 460), strict=True)
    )
    check = decompose(on_mix)
    print(f"check_on_mix_sales_mix_variance {check['sales_mix']}")
    print(f"check_on_mix_efficiency_variance {check['efficiency']}")
    print(f"check_on_mix_sales_volume_variance {check['sales_volume']}")
    print(f"check_on_mix_total {check['total']}")

4Where the canon breaks

The identity in Proposition 2.1 is exact, but it holds for a specific set of definitions. Four limits follow from them.

  • The actual material price is taken to equal the standard. If the actual price \(a\) differs from \(\pi\), the cost of the material used is \(a \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\), and the identity needs a fifth term, \(-(a - \pi)\sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\). Problem R1 derives it. The case in Lab 1 has no price difference, so the four terms are the whole contribution variance.
  • One material. The efficiency variance sums over products at one standard price. With several inputs per unit, each input has its own quantity and price terms, and this lesson does not split them.
  • The standard sets both sides of the comparison. The standard kilograms enter the budget contribution and the efficiency variance. Changing the standard changes the budget as well as the variance (Problem R3). A variance is only as good as the standard it is measured against.
  • The reference point. The split between volume and mix depends on the budget reference of Remark 2.3, and the lesson does not compare it with other references.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A mix variance is zero whenever the total units equal the budget total.”

    Mix depends on the shares, not on the total. Take 500 units of A and 500 of B, a total of 1,000 equal to the budget total, at budget prices and standard kilograms. Then \(M = 1{,}500 \times (500 - 600) + 1{,}900 \times (500 - 400) = -150{,}000 + 190{,}000 = +40{,}000\) cents, while \(V = 0\). The contribution variance is also +40,000, which is the whole of it, and it is all mix.

  2. “The efficiency variance is the budgeted kilograms minus the actual kilograms.”

    The budgeted kilograms are \(600 \times 2 + 400 \times 3 = 2{,}400\) kg. The actual kilograms are 2,950 kg. Their difference is 550 kg, which at 500 cents is 275,000 cents. That difference has two parts: 400 kg for the 150 extra units (2 × 50 + 3 × 100, at the standard kilograms) and 150 kg of excess use. Definition 1.2 compares actual kilograms with the standard allowed for the actual output, \(2 \times 650 + 3 \times 500 = 2{,}800\) kg, which gives the efficiency variance of −75,000 cents.

  3. “An unfavourable efficiency variance means the material price rose.”

    In Lab 1 the material price is the standard, and the efficiency variance is −75,000 cents. It is unfavourable because 150 kg more were used than the standard allowed (Proposition 2.2, part c). A price change would enter through a separate term, which Section 4 describes.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · Sales price variance by product

From the inputs of Lab 1, compute the sales price variance for product A and for product B, and their sum.

Solution

For A, \((2{,}400 - 2{,}500) \times 650 = -65{,}000\) cents. For B, \((3{,}450 - 3{,}400) \times 500 = +25{,}000\) cents. The sum is \(-65{,}000 + 25{,}000 = -40{,}000\) cents, which is the printed sales_price_variance.

M2 · Efficiency variance for product B

Product B uses 1,600 kg against a standard allowance of 3 kg per unit at 500 units. The standard price is 500 cents per kg. Compute the efficiency variance for B.

Solution

The standard allowed is \(3 \times 500 = 1{,}500\) kg, so the excess is \(1{,}600 - 1{,}500 = 100\) kg. The efficiency variance is \(-500 \times 100 = -50{,}000\) cents, unfavourable.

M3 · Volume variance at a different total

The budget average contribution is 1,660 cents per unit. If actual total units were 1,300 against a budget of 1,000, compute the sales volume variance.

Solution

\(V = (1{,}300 - 1{,}000) \times 1{,}660 = 300 \times 1{,}660 = 498{,}000\) cents, favourable. The volume variance uses the budget average, so it is the same for any mix of those 1,300 units.

Proof

P1 · The exact reconciliation

Reproduce the four steps of the proof of Proposition 2.1, and state in one sentence which step uses the definition of the efficiency variance.

Solution

Step 1 writes the actual revenue as the sales price variance plus budget price times actual units. Step 2 writes the material cost using \(E\), since \(-\pi\sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i} = E - \pi\sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\). Step 3 subtracts budget contribution. Step 4 splits \(\sum_i \text{CM}_i(q_{a,i} - q_{b,i})\) into \(M + V\) using \(q_{b,i} = Q_b m_i\). Step 2 is where the efficiency definition is used, because it is the only step that depends on the kilograms.

P2 · Mix is zero at the budget shares

Show that if the actual units are split in the budget shares, \(q_{a,i} = Q_a m_i\), then \(M = 0\). Give a numerical instance from the case.

Solution

Each term of \(M = \sum_i \text{CM}_i(q_{a,i} - Q_a m_i)\) is \(\text{CM}_i \times 0 = 0\), so \(M = 0\). In the case, \(Q_a = 1{,}150\), so the budget shares give 690 units of A and 460 units of B. Lab 1's check line uses exactly these units and prints a mix variance of zero.

P3 · One product has no mix

For a single product, show that \(M = 0\) and \(V = (Q_a - Q_b)\,\text{CM}\).

Solution

With one product, \(m = 1\) and \(q_a = Q_a\), so \(M = \text{CM}(q_a - Q_a \cdot 1) = 0\). The budget average is \(\overline{\text{CM}}_b = \text{CM}\), so \(V = (Q_a - Q_b)\text{CM}\). A single product has volume and price and efficiency effects only, and a mix effect needs at least two products.

Empirical

E1 · Check the reconciliation from the printed lines

Using only the values printed by Lab 1, check that the four variances sum to the total contribution variance and that adding the fixed-cost variance gives the operating variance of Lesson L05.

Solution

The four printed values are \(-40{,}000 + 249{,}000 + 16{,}000 - 75{,}000 = 150{,}000\), which matches total_contribution_variance and sum_of_four. The fixed-cost variance is \(500{,}000 - 510{,}000 = -10{,}000\), so the operating variance is \(150{,}000 - 10{,}000 = 140{,}000\), which equals the static variance printed in Lesson L05.

E2 · Standard allowed for the actual output

Compute the standard kilograms allowed for the actual output of Lab 1 and the excess kilograms that give the efficiency variance.

Solution

The standard allowed is \(2 \times 650 + 3 \times 500 = 2{,}800\) kg. The actual is \(1{,}350 + 1{,}600 = 2{,}950\) kg, an excess of 150 kg. At 500 cents per kg the efficiency variance is \(-500 \times 150 = -75{,}000\) cents, as the lab prints.

E3 · Mix for another actual split

At budget prices and standard kilograms, actual units of 600 A and 550 B give a total of 1,150. Compute the mix variance and check the contribution variance against the direct difference in contribution.

Solution

The budget shares give \(Q_a m_A = 690\) and \(Q_a m_B = 460\). So \(M = 1{,}500 \times (600 - 690) + 1{,}900 \times (550 - 460) = -135{,}000 + 171{,}000 = +36{,}000\) cents. At budget prices and standard kilograms the contribution is \(1{,}500 \times 600 + 1{,}900 \times 550 = 1{,}945{,}000\) cents, against a budget of 1,660,000, a difference of 285,000. The volume variance is \(150 \times 1{,}660 = 249{,}000\), and \(249{,}000 + 36{,}000 = 285{,}000\), as Proposition 2.1 requires with zero price and efficiency terms.

Research

R1 · The material price term

Let the actual material price be \(a\) cents per kilogram, with \(a \neq \pi\). Derive the fifth term that the reconciliation needs, and state how the efficiency variance changes if it is computed at the actual price.

Solution

The actual contribution becomes \(C_a = \sum_i p_{a,i} q_{a,i} - a \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\). Writing \(a = \pi + (a - \pi)\) gives \(C_a = \text{SP} + E + \sum_i \text{CM}_i q_{a,i} - (a - \pi) \sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\), so the reconciliation holds with a fifth term, the material price variance \(P = -(a - \pi)\sum_i \text{kg}_{a,i}\). This lesson keeps the standard price in \(E\) and adds the fifth term. If the efficiency variance is computed at the actual price instead, \(E_a = -a\sum_i(\text{kg}_{a,i} - s_i q_{a,i})\), which differs from \(E\) by \(-(a - \pi)\sum_i(\text{kg}_{a,i} - s_i q_{a,i})\). The reconciliation then holds with the fifth term \(P_a = -(a - \pi)\sum_i s_i q_{a,i}\) in place of \(P\), because \(E_a + P_a = E + P\). The two placements split the same total differently.

R2 · A fixed-cost underspend

Suppose actual fixed overhead had been 490,000 cents in Lab 1. Compute the operating-profit variance and state how it compares with the contribution variance.

Solution

The fixed-cost variance is \(500{,}000 - 490{,}000 = +10{,}000\), favourable. The operating-profit variance is \(150{,}000 + 10{,}000 = 160{,}000\) cents, which is 10,000 cents more than the contribution variance. The four contribution effects are unchanged, because fixed overhead is outside them.

R3 · Changing the standard changes the budget

Restate the case with the standard for A changed to 2.2 kg per unit, and the actual kilograms unchanged. Compute the four variances and the contribution variance, and explain why the budget itself moves.

Solution

The budget contribution per unit of A becomes \(2{,}500 - 2.2 \times 500 = 1{,}400\) cents, so the budget average is \(\overline{\text{CM}}_b = 0.6 \times 1{,}400 + 0.4 \times 1{,}900 = 1{,}600\) cents, and budget contribution is \(1{,}600{,}000\) cents. The sales price variance stays at −40,000. The volume variance is \(150 \times 1{,}600 = 240{,}000\). The mix variance is \(1{,}400 \times (-40) + 1{,}900 \times 40 = 20{,}000\). The efficiency variance uses the standard allowance \(2.2 \times 650 + 3 \times 500 = 2{,}930\) kg, so \(E = -500 \times (2{,}950 - 2{,}930) = -10{,}000\). The sum is \(-40{,}000 + 240{,}000 + 20{,}000 - 10{,}000 = 210{,}000\), which equals the new contribution variance \(1{,}810{,}000 - 1{,}600{,}000\). The contribution variance rises from 150,000 to 210,000 because the standard moved, not because any actual result changed. The budget and the variance both depend on the standard.
