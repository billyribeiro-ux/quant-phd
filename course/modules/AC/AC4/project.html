Budget and variance engine

A plant manager receives a master budget (the plan at the budgeted units and mix) and a report of actual profit each month. Your engine splits the gap between them into sales volume, sales mix, sales price and input efficiency, so that the four parts add to the gap exactly. You write budget_engine.py, which the autograder tests on a SIMULATED plant generated from your workspace seed, and a memo to the plant manager, which is graded against a rubric.

Rungs 1 and 2

Lab 1 · Worked example: the master budget to the four variances

The plant below makes a Standard and a Deluxe product from one input. Before running it, predict the sign of the sales mix variance. In the actual period Standard is a larger share of units than budgeted, and Standard earns the smaller margin per unit (its budgeted price less the standard input cost of one Standard unit, 3 inputs at 200 cents), so the mix effect should be unfavourable. The code computes the master budget (the plan at the budgeted units and mix), the flexed budget (the same budgeted mix at the actual total of 1,500 units), the price, volume, mix and efficiency variances, and the total, and it checks the reconciliation. Every money figure is in cents, and the case is SIMULATED.

from fractions import Fraction
from typing import Any

# SIMULATED plant, not a real company. Money is whole cents; quantities are whole units.
INPUT_PRICE = 200  # cents per input unit: the standard price and the price actually paid
FIXED = 5_000_000  # fixed overhead in cents, the same in the budget and in the actual period
ACTUAL_INPUTS = 5_300  # input units actually used in the period

PRODUCTS: list[dict[str, Any]] = [
    {
        "name": "Standard",
        "budget_units": 1000,
        "budget_price": 5000,
        "standard": 3,
        "actual_units": 1200,
        "actual_price": 5200,
    },
    {
        "name": "Deluxe",
        "budget_units": 400,
        "budget_price": 9000,
        "standard": 5,
        "actual_units": 300,
        "actual_price": 8800,
    },
]


def nearest_cent(amount: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest whole cent, halves up. The amounts in this case are never negative."""
    return (2 * amount.numerator + amount.denominator) // (2 * amount.denominator)


def main() -> None:
    # Step 1. Master budget. Why: it is the plan the actual period is compared with, at the budgeted volume and mix.
    budget_units = sum(p["budget_units"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    budget_revenue = sum(p["budget_units"] * p["budget_price"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    budget_variable = sum(p["budget_units"] * p["standard"] * INPUT_PRICE for p in PRODUCTS)
    master = budget_revenue - budget_variable - FIXED
    print("step 1 master budget profit (cents):", master)

    # Step 2. Flexed budget. Why: the same budgeted mix at the volume actually sold, so volume is separated from mix.
    actual_total = sum(p["actual_units"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    flex_revenue = nearest_cent(Fraction(actual_total * budget_revenue, budget_units))
    flex_variable = nearest_cent(Fraction(actual_total * budget_variable, budget_units))
    flexed = flex_revenue - flex_variable - FIXED
    print("step 2 flexed budget at", actual_total, "units, profit (cents):", flexed)

    # Step 3. Sales price variance. Why: each unit sold is compared with its budgeted price, favourable when positive.
    price = sum((p["actual_price"] - p["budget_price"]) * p["actual_units"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    print("step 3 sales price variance (cents):", price)

    # Step 4. Sales volume variance. Why: the change in total units, valued at the budgeted mix.
    volume = flexed - master
    print("step 4 sales volume variance (cents):", volume)

    # Step 5. Efficiency variance. Why: the inputs the actual output should have used, against the inputs used.
    standard_inputs = sum(p["standard"] * p["actual_units"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    efficiency = INPUT_PRICE * (standard_inputs - ACTUAL_INPUTS)
    print("step 5 efficiency variance (cents):", efficiency)

    # Step 6. Sales mix variance. Why: the actual mix priced at budgeted margins, less the flexed profit.
    actual_mix_margin = sum(p["actual_units"] * (p["budget_price"] - p["standard"] * INPUT_PRICE) for p in PRODUCTS)
    actual_mix_contribution = actual_mix_margin - FIXED
    mix = actual_mix_contribution - flexed
    print("step 6 sales mix variance (cents):", mix)
    exact_flexed = Fraction(actual_total * budget_revenue, budget_units)
    exact_flexed -= Fraction(actual_total * budget_variable, budget_units) + FIXED
    print("step 6 exact flexed profit minus whole-cent flexed profit (cents):", exact_flexed - flexed)

    # Step 7. Total profit variance. Why: the reported profit of the period less the master budget profit.
    actual_profit = sum(p["actual_units"] * p["actual_price"] for p in PRODUCTS)
    actual_profit -= INPUT_PRICE * ACTUAL_INPUTS + FIXED
    total = actual_profit - master
    print("step 7 total profit variance (cents):", total)

    # Step 8. Reconciliation. Why: the four variances must add to the total with no residual.
    print("step 8 four variances sum to total:", price + volume + mix + efficiency == total)


main()

Read the steps in order. The master budget profit is 2,600,000 cents. Flexing it to 1,500 units at the budgeted mix gives 3,142,857 cents, rounded to the whole cent by the step 2 rule, so the sales volume variance is +542,857: 1,500 units were sold against 1,400 budgeted, and the 100 extra units earn the budgeted average margin of 5,428.57 cents each (7,600,000 cents of budgeted margin over 1,400 units, rounded to two decimals). The mix variance is −462,857. Standard rose from 71.4% of budgeted total units (1,000 of 1,400) to 80.0% of actual total units (1,200 of 1,500), and Standard earns 4,400 cents of margin per unit against Deluxe's 8,000, so the shift cost profit. The price variance is +180,000 and the efficiency variance is −40,000, because 5,300 input units were used where 5,100 were the standard for the output. The four variances add to the total of +220,000 exactly. The step 6 line shows the effect of the rounding in step 2, where the flexed revenue and the flexed variable cost are each taken to the nearest cent: the exact flexed profit is 1/7 of a cent away from the whole-cent figure. The lab does not compute what a different input price would do, because the engine has no input price variance; the stretch section asks you to add one.

Rung 3 · Specification

Context
A SIMULATED plant makes three products from one input. Each month the plant manager compares the actual profit with the master budget, and the question is which part of the gap came from volume, from the mix of products, from the price of each product and from the number of inputs used for the output. You write the engine that computes the master budget, the flexed budget and the four variances, and the memo that tells the plant manager what caused the gap and what to do next.
Inputs
One case, a mapping with these fields. Money is in whole cents and quantities are whole units. input_price_cents: the standard price of one input unit, which is also the price paid in the actual period. fixed_overhead_cents: fixed overhead, the same in the budget and in the actual period. actual_inputs_used: the input units used in the actual period. products: a list of mappings, one per product, each with the whole-number fields budget_units, budget_price_cents, standard_inputs_per_unit, actual_units and actual_price_cents, and a non-empty name that no other product shares. The autograder's case is generated by grader/instance.py, with make_instance(seed), from your workspace seed. Its payload is a JSON string, and decoding it gives the case; the case carries a label that says it is SIMULATED. The grader decodes it, and you do not call make_instance from budget_engine.py. No real plant data is embedded in this course: the course's data manifest holds no plant-level dataset, so every case is generated.
Required outputs
A file budget_engine.py at the root of your repository, with three functions of these names and signatures. master_budget(case) returns a dictionary with the keys units, revenue_cents and variable_cost_cents (each a mapping from product name to its value), total_units, total_revenue_cents, total_variable_cost_cents, fixed_cost_cents and profit_cents. flexible_budget(case, actual_units) returns a dictionary with the keys units, revenue_cents, variable_cost_cents, fixed_cost_cents and profit_cents, the budget flexed to actual_units in total at the budgeted mix. variance_decomposition(case) returns a dictionary of whole cents with the keys sales_price, sales_volume, sales_mix, efficiency and total. The starter file is the starter; every function raises NotImplementedError until you replace it, and the suite fails on the starter.
Conventions

Write s for the input price, F for the fixed overhead, AQ for the input units used, and for each product i: BU and AU for its budgeted and actual units, bp and ap for its budgeted and actual prices, and q for its standard input units per unit. The contribution margin per budgeted unit is bp minus q times s. The budgeted profit is the sum of BU times the margin, less F. The actual profit is the sum of ap times AU, less s times AQ, less F. The total profit variance is the actual profit less the budgeted profit.

The flexed budget at AT, the actual total of AU, uses the budgeted mix. Its revenue is AT times the budgeted revenue per budgeted unit; its variable cost is AT times the budgeted variable cost per budgeted unit. Each of these two amounts is taken to the nearest whole cent, halves up. Its profit is the rounded revenue less the rounded variable cost less F.

The variances are these. The sales price variance is the sum of (ap minus bp) times AU. The sales volume variance is the flexed profit less the budgeted profit. The sales mix variance is the sum of AU times the budgeted margin, less F, minus the flexed profit. The efficiency variance is s times the sum of q times AU, less AQ. A favourable variance is positive. With these definitions the four variances sum to the total exactly, with no residual.

The case holds the input price and the fixed overhead at budget, so the engine has no input price variance and no fixed overhead variance; the stretch section asks you to add them. The sales mix variance uses the whole-cent flexed profit, so it differs from the mix variance computed with the exact, unrounded flexed profit by less than one cent. The engine converts no currency and no units. It rounds only where the flexed budget rule says to, and every number it returns is an int.

Constraints
Standard library only. Every function validates its input and raises ValueError for each of these: a case that is not a mapping, or whose products are not a list of mappings; a case without a required field; a value that is not a whole number (text, a float and a boolean are all refused); a negative count or amount; an empty product list; a product name that is not text, is empty or appears twice; a budget with no budgeted units; and a negative or non-integer actual unit count passed to flexible_budget. The functions are pure: they never print, never warn and never change an argument; master_budget returns a fresh object on each call.
Grading mode
Both. The autograder is grader/test_project.py, which collects 563 checks on every seed, run with warnings as errors. It covers the master budget by product and in total; the flexed budget at actual units and at budgeted units; each of the four variances and the total, exactly, against values computed from the case's own numbers; the reconciliation on seeds 0 to 49; the sign of each variance, with the price, volume, mix and efficiency each tested in both directions; the zero-variance cases; the single product; the zero-unit case; each named ValueError, with a test for each refused condition in the constraints above, and sixteen product conditions, each put in the middle product and again in the last product: a negative budgeted, actual or standard count, or a negative budget price; a boolean, float, text or nothing (none) in a number field; a missing field (actual price, name or budgeted units); an empty or non-text name; or a product that is not a mapping; purity and silence. The memo is rubric-graded against the five criteria in the memo section, with four observable levels each. The memo meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Rung 3 · The management memo

Write memo/variance_memo.md to the plant manager, about the plant your seed generates. Its body is 300 to 600 words, tables excluded. Its four headings are Result, Causes, Assumptions and Recommendation, in that order. Every money figure is in whole cents and carries its sign; a unit count or a percentage carries its unit. For criterion C4 (in the table below), an observation is a stated value of a figure in the case (a unit count, a price or an input count) at which the recommendation would change, and a threshold is that value.

The rubric

Scoring rule. Score each criterion on its own. The score is the highest level whose descriptor is fully met, and a descriptor is fully met only when every condition in it is met; a memo that meets no level above 1 scores 1. The overall level of a memo is the lowest of its five criterion scores. The following terms are fixed here. A variance has a correct sign only when the memo gives it the sign it has in the case: favourable positive, unfavourable negative. A variance is tied to a case figure when the paragraph that states it contains a number of the case, or a number computed from the case figures and shown in the memo. A variance is tied to a named product when the paragraph that states it names each product whose price, units or inputs enter that variance. An input count is a number of input units stated in the memo. An action for a variance is a step the memo recommends now that addresses that variance; a review that waits for a future condition is not an action. A memo that recommends no action for any unfavourable variance scores 1 on C4, whatever else it says.

The management memo: five criteria, four levels each, scored by the rule above. The memo meets the bar at level 3 or above on every criterion.

Two exemplars

Two memos written to this brief, with overall levels 1 and 3 under the scoring rule. Both use the case figures that Lab 1 computes. Every figure they state is either derived from those figures or labelled as a what-if, except the sign of Exemplar A's sales mix figure, which its annotation marks as wrong. Each passage carries an annotation that names the descriptors it meets or misses. With JavaScript on, the annotations and the official scores stay hidden until you reveal them with the form below, so score both exemplars first.

Exemplar A

Official scoring: C1 2 · C2 2 · C3 2 · C4 1 · C5 2. The memo gives the total and three of the four variances, two of them with correct signs, reads the mix sign wrongly, leaves out the efficiency variance, never states the input price, recommends a volume target and gives no action for either unfavourable variance, and has three of the four headings.

Result

The plant beat its budget by 220,000 cents this month. That is a good result for a month in which the plant sold more than it planned, and the volume variance, +542,857 cents, is the largest of the four variances. The plant should aim to keep this pattern going, because the gain shows that the plant can sell more than the budget assumed. The gain is large enough that the plan for next month should be reset to match it, so the budget does not hold the plant back.

Causes

Sales volume is +542,857 cents. The plant sold 1,500 units, and the budget had 1,400. This is the largest of the four variances, and the two unfavourable variances take part of it back.

Sales price is +180,000 cents. Standard sold above its budgeted price of 5,000 cents, and that added to profit. Deluxe sold below its budgeted price of 9,000 cents, which took some of that gain back, but the net effect on the price line was still favourable.

The mix is positive as well. Standard sold a larger share of the units than the budget planned, and the plant earned more on the units it sold more of. So the mix helped the result, and the mix variance of +462,857 cents is the second reason for the gain.

Fixed overhead was the same as budgeted, so it did not change the result. The plant's cost structure held through the month, and the variable cost of each product stayed in line with the plan. Those two points are this memo's account of costs for the month.

Recommendation

Keep the current plan and set a higher sales target for next month. The volume gain is the reason for the result, and a higher target should repeat it. Review the product mix if volume falls back to the budgeted level, because a higher target should be the first thing the plant tries.

  • Meets C1 level 2: gives the total and three variances, two of them with correct signs.
  • Misses C1 level 3: the efficiency variance is absent, so the four variances cannot be shown to add to the total. The mix sign is also wrong, so the sum the reader would check does not hold.
  • Meets C2 level 2: the volume variance is tied to the 1,500 and 1,400 units of the case, the price variance is tied to the budgeted prices of 5,000 and 9,000 cents, and the mix variance is tied to the magnitude of the computed mix shift, 462,857 cents, to which the memo gives the wrong sign, so three variances are tied to case figures and efficiency is the one untied.
  • Misses C2 level 3: the mix variance is explained by a wrong statement (the memo says the plant earned more on the units it sold more of; Standard took the larger share and earns the smaller margin, so the shift is unfavourable), and the efficiency variance is not tied to anything. The Result and Recommendation also infer that the plant can sell more and that a higher target will repeat the gain; no figure in the case tests either inference.
  • Meets C3 level 2: states the fixed overhead as fixed, which is one of the two items.
  • Misses C3 level 3: the input price is never stated, and the claim that variable cost stayed in line with the plan is contradicted by the efficiency variance of −40,000 cents, which the memo omits.
  • Misses C4 level 2: it recommends no action for either unfavourable variance. The review of the product mix waits for volume to fall back, so it is not an action now, and the efficiency variance gets none. Under the scoring rule, C4 scores 1.
  • Misses C4 level 3: the recommendation gives no action for either unfavourable variance (mix and efficiency), so the level 3 condition of an action for each is not met.
  • Meets C5 level 2 by its headings: it has Result, Causes and Recommendation, and not Assumptions. Its body is inside 300 to 600 words.

Exemplar B

Official scoring: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 3 · C4 4 · C5 4. The memo reports each variance with its sign, ties each variance to the case, states both assumptions and says which costs vary, and gives a reversing threshold for its recommendation. It states the input price consequence, but it does not test the variable-cost assumption against any figure, which is the level-4 element of C3.

Result

Profit was 2,820,000 cents against a master budget of 2,600,000, a total variance of +220,000 cents, favourable. The four variances add to that figure exactly: sales volume +542,857 cents, favourable; sales price +180,000, favourable; efficiency −40,000, unfavourable; and sales mix −462,857, unfavourable.

Causes

Sales volume, +542,857 cents, favourable. The plant sold 1,500 units against 1,400 budgeted, and the budget had 1,000 Standard and 400 Deluxe units. Valued at that budgeted mix, the flexed profit, rounded to the whole cent, is 3,142,857 cents against 2,600,000 budgeted.

Sales mix, −462,857 cents, unfavourable, a whole-cent figure taken against the whole-cent flexed profit. Standard's share of units rose from 71.4% (1,000 of 1,400) to 80.0% (1,200 of 1,500), and Deluxe's fell from 28.6% (400 of 1,400) to 20.0% (300 of 1,500). Standard earns 4,400 cents of margin per unit and Deluxe 8,000, so the shift toward Standard cost profit.

Sales price, +180,000 cents, favourable. Standard sold at 5,200 cents against 5,000 budgeted, +240,000 on 1,200 units. Deluxe sold at 8,800 against 9,000, −60,000 on 300 units.

Efficiency, −40,000 cents, unfavourable. The plant used 5,300 input units. The standard for the units sold is 5,100 (3 per Standard, 5 per Deluxe). The 200 extra units, at 200 cents each, cost 40,000.

Assumptions

Fixed overhead is 5,000,000 cents in both periods, so the fixed cost is not a variance here. Costs vary with units in proportion to the standard input per unit. The input price is held at 200 cents in the actual period, so the case has no input price variance. If the actual input price were 210 cents, the actual input cost would rise by 53,000 cents, and the total variance would fall from 220,000 to 167,000.

Recommendation

Restore the budgeted mix in the sales plan: the mix variance, −462,857 cents, is the largest unfavourable figure, and the efficiency variance of −40,000 cents is the second. Keep the Standard price increase, which earned +240,000 cents. Investigate the 200 extra input units.

The mix recommendation reverses if the budgeted Deluxe price falls below 5,400 cents. At 5,400 cents its budgeted margin is 5,400 less 1,000 for its five inputs, or 4,400 cents per unit, which equals Standard's budgeted margin of 4,400, so the shift adds no margin and the mix variance is zero. Below 5,400 cents Deluxe earns less per unit than Standard, so the shift toward Standard adds margin, the mix variance turns favourable, and the recommendation reverses: the plant should keep the mix it has.

  • Meets C2 level 4: each variance tied to a named product and a case figure (volume names Standard and Deluxe and the budgeted units of each); volume and mix are separated, and the mix shift is a change in each product's share of units, Standard from 71.4% to 80.0% and Deluxe from 28.6% to 20.0%.
  • Meets C3 level 3: states the variable and fixed split and the input price assumption.
  • Misses C3 level 4, in part: the input price consequence is stated (53,000 cents, and a total of 167,000), but the variable-cost assumption is not tested against any figure in the memo.
  • Meets C4 level 4: an action for each unfavourable variance (the mix and the efficiency), with the Deluxe price of 5,400 cents as the threshold that reverses the mix recommendation.
