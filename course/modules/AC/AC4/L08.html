Residual income and EVA

  • Uses: the income statement, the balance sheet
  • Introduces: residual income and economic value added (EVA), both defined in §1
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or simulation
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

A division's profit is an amount, and its operating income per unit of capital is a ratio. Two measures, defined in §1, subtract a charge for the capital a division uses, so an amount can be judged against what that capital costs. Both depend on the operating income, the capital base and the capital-charge rate (defined in §1), and the second also depends on the tax rate. A choice of base or an adjustment to operating income can move both measures, and the two measures differ by a term in the tax rate and the operating income. This lesson derives the identities, shows the effect of an accounting choice, and checks them on SIMULATED divisions.

1Inputs and definitions

For one division and one period, the inputs are below. SCU is a simulated currency unit used for every example here.

  • \(\mathrm{OI}\), the operating income for the period, in SCU;
  • \(C\), the invested capital, a stated base in SCU at the same date;
  • \(r\), the capital-charge rate, a number per period, stated as an input;
  • \(\tau\), the tax rate applied to operating income, stated as an input.

The lab stores money in integer cents, with 1 SCU = 100 cents, and states \(r\) and \(\tau\) in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 percent). The rates are inputs. This lesson derives neither a cost of capital nor a tax rate, and it defines no concept for either. Operating income is read from the income statement, and the capital base is read from the balance sheet, on the base the reader chooses.

Definition 8.1 (residual income and EVA)

Residual income for a division is

\[ \mathrm{RI} = \mathrm{OI} - r\,C. \]

Its economic value added, written EVA, is

\[ \mathrm{EVA} = \mathrm{OI}\,(1-\tau) - r\,C. \]

The term \(\mathrm{OI}\,(1-\tau)\) is net operating profit after tax (NOPAT), and \(r\,C\) is the capital charge. Both measures are amounts in SCU. This lesson uses the two definitions as stated, and does not refer to any other definition of EVA.

The ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) is the operating income earned per unit of capital, on the stated base. It is a plain ratio, not a named measure. Lemma 8.3 and §5 use it.

2Two identities

Lemma 8.2 (the gap between the two measures)

For every division, \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\).

Proof. \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = \mathrm{OI}(1-\tau) - rC - (\mathrm{OI} - rC) = \mathrm{OI} - \tau\,\mathrm{OI} - \mathrm{OI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\). \(\square\)

Lemma 8.3 (the sign of residual income)

If \(C > 0\), then \(\mathrm{RI} \ge 0\) if and only if \(\mathrm{OI}/C \ge r\).

Proof. \(\mathrm{RI} \ge 0\) is \(\mathrm{OI} \ge rC\). Dividing by \(C > 0\) gives \(\mathrm{OI}/C \ge r\). \(\square\)

Lemma 8.2 says the two measures differ only by a term that is proportional to operating income. Lemma 8.3 says that, for \(C > 0\), residual income is nonnegative exactly when the ratio on the base reaches the rate. Neither lemma says anything about whether a rate or a base is correct, because both are inputs.

3How an accounting choice moves both measures

Suppose a choice changes the operating income by \(\Delta\mathrm{OI}\) and the capital base by \(\Delta C\), with the rate and the tax rate unchanged. Expensing an outlay in the period (charging all of it to operating income now), or capitalizing it (recording it as an asset whose cost is charged to operating income over the asset's life, starting with the first-year charge in the period of the outlay, as in §4), is one such choice.

Proposition 8.4 (the change in each measure)

Let the change be \(\Delta\mathrm{OI}\) and \(\Delta C\). Then

\[ \Delta\mathrm{RI} = \Delta\mathrm{OI} - r\,\Delta C, \qquad \Delta\mathrm{EVA} = (1-\tau)\,\Delta\mathrm{OI} - r\,\Delta C. \]

Proof. Subtract the definitions before and after the change: \((\mathrm{OI}+\Delta\mathrm{OI}) - r(C+\Delta C) - (\mathrm{OI} - rC) = \Delta\mathrm{OI} - r\Delta C\), and \((\mathrm{OI}+\Delta\mathrm{OI})(1-\tau) - r(C+\Delta C) - (\mathrm{OI}(1-\tau) - rC) = (1-\tau)\Delta\mathrm{OI} - r\Delta C\). \(\square\)

So a choice that raises both operating income and the base can raise both measures. The two changes differ by \(\Delta\mathrm{RI} - \Delta\mathrm{EVA} = \tau\,\Delta\mathrm{OI}\), so they are equal only when the tax rate is zero or the operating income does not change. With \(r > 0\), \(\tau \le 1\) and \(\Delta C > 0\), a choice that raises the base without raising operating income lowers both.

4Worked cases

The cases below are hand-built SIMULATED divisions. Money is in SCU, the rate is \(r = 9\%\), and the tax rate is \(\tau = 20\%\) where it is used.

A division. Let \(C = 1{,}000\) and \(\mathrm{OI} = 120\). Then \(\mathrm{RI} = 120 - 0.09 \times 1{,}000 = 30\) SCU. Net operating profit is \(120 \times 0.8 = 96\), so \(\mathrm{EVA} = 96 - 90 = 6\) SCU. Check Lemma 8.2: \(6 - 30 = -24 = -0.20 \times 120\). Since \(\mathrm{OI}/C = 0.12 \ge 0.09\), Lemma 8.3 says \(\mathrm{RI} \ge 0\), and \(\mathrm{RI} = 30\) agrees.

A capitalized outlay. The reported operating income of 120 already charges an outlay of 100 SCU in full. Suppose the outlay is instead treated as an asset with a life of five years, amortized straight-line, so the first year's charge is 20 SCU. Operating income then rises by \(100 - 20 = 80\), to 200, and the base rises by the unamortized balance \(100 - 20 = 80\), to 1,080. Proposition 8.4 gives the change directly:

  • \(\Delta\mathrm{RI} = 80 - 0.09 \times 80 = 72.8\) SCU, so RI moves from 30 to 102.8.
  • \(\Delta\mathrm{EVA} = 0.8 \times 80 - 0.09 \times 80 = 56.8\) SCU, so EVA moves from 6 to 62.8.

By construction, the cash outlay is 100 SCU in both treatments. The reported figures differ because the choice changes operating income and the base (§3). A reader who compares divisions must know which treatment each report used.

5Amounts and ratios can rank divisions differently

Residual income is an amount, so a large division can have a large residual income with a low return. The ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) is a return per unit of capital, so it ignores scale. The two can order a pair of divisions in opposite ways.

Take two hand-built SIMULATED divisions with \(r = 9\%\). Division X has \(C = 500\) and \(\mathrm{OI} = 60\). Division Y has \(C = 2{,}000\) and \(\mathrm{OI} = 200\). Then \(\mathrm{RI}_X = 60 - 45 = 15\) and \(\mathrm{RI}_Y = 200 - 180 = 20\), so residual income ranks Y above X. The ratios are \(\mathrm{OI}_X/C_X = 0.12\) and \(\mathrm{OI}_Y/C_Y = 0.10\), so the ratio ranks X above Y. Neither ranking is wrong. Each answers a different question, and a choice between them is a choice about what the comparison should measure.

When two divisions have the same base, the orders agree: if \(C_a = C_b = C\), then \(\mathrm{RI}_a - \mathrm{RI}_b = \mathrm{OI}_a - \mathrm{OI}_b\), and \(\mathrm{OI}_a/C - \mathrm{OI}_b/C\) has the same sign. Lab 1 counts how often the two orders disagree across all 28 pairs of its eight generated divisions, and a tie in either measure is not counted as a disagreement. A pair with a common base cannot disagree, and in this seed no two of the eight divisions share a base: the C column of the lab output lists eight different values.

Lab 1 · Residual income and EVA on SIMULATED divisions

Stated before running: the identity \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\) holds exactly for each division the lab generates; the sign identity of Lemma 8.3 holds exactly for each generated division and for one hand-built division with \(\mathrm{OI}/C = r\), where \(\mathrm{RI} = 0\), so the boundary case is tested at equality; and the orders by residual income and by the ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) disagree for some pairs. Same-base pairs cannot disagree (§5). The lab does not predict how many pairs disagree. That count describes the generator's settings, not real divisions. Tax is 2,000 bp and the capital-charge rate is 900 bp, both set in the code. Money is in integer cents, and every test uses exact fractions.

"""Lab AC4-L08-1: residual income and EVA on SIMULATED divisions.

Every division here is SIMULATED: the figures come from a seeded generator, no firm is represented,
and no figure estimates any real company. Money is in integer cents (1 SCU = 100 cents). The tax rate
and the capital-charge rate are stated inputs, in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 percent). Their values are
set here by the lab, not derived; the lesson does not derive a cost of capital.

Definitions (stated on the page):
  residual income  RI  = OI - r * C
  NOPAT                 = OI * (1 - tax)
  EVA              = NOPAT - r * C
where OI is operating income for the period, C is invested capital (a stated base), r is the
capital-charge rate and tax is the tax rate applied to OI.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass, replace
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal
from fractions import Fraction

SEED = 20261027
N_DIVISIONS = 8
TAX_BP = 2000
RATE_BP = 900
SPEND = 10000
LIFE_YEARS = 5


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Division:
    """One SIMULATED division for one period. Money in integer cents."""

    name: str
    invested: int
    oi: int

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if self.invested <= 0:
            raise ValueError("invested capital must be positive")


def residual_income(d: Division) -> Fraction:
    """RI = OI - r * C, in cents."""
    return Fraction(d.oi) - Fraction(RATE_BP, 10_000) * d.invested


def eva(d: Division) -> Fraction:
    """EVA = OI * (1 - tax) - r * C, in cents."""
    nopat = Fraction(d.oi) * (10_000 - TAX_BP) / 10_000
    return nopat - Fraction(RATE_BP, 10_000) * d.invested


def return_ratio(d: Division) -> Fraction:
    """OI / C, a dimensionless ratio on the stated capital base."""
    return Fraction(d.oi, d.invested)


def generate(seed: int, n: int) -> list[Division]:
    """n SIMULATED divisions. C is a multiple of 1000 cents; OI/C lies in [4%, 5/31], so at most 16.13%."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    out: list[Division] = []
    for i in range(n):
        invested = rng.randrange(20_000, 200_001, 1000)
        oi = 5 * rng.randint(invested // 125, invested // 31)
        out.append(Division(f"SIM-{chr(ord('A') + i)}", invested, oi))
    return out


# Hand-built boundary division: OI / C = 90 / 1000 = 9% = r exactly, so RI = 0 exactly.
BOUNDARY = Division("BOUNDARY", 1000, 90)


def capitalized(d: Division) -> Division:
    """Treat a SPEND outlay as an asset amortized straight-line over LIFE_YEARS, first year only.

    Reported OI already charges the outlay in full. Capitalizing adds it back to OI net of the first
    year's amortization, and adds the unamortized balance to C. The tax rate applies to the changed operating
    income. The model does not represent a difference between tax and book treatment of the outlay.
    """
    first_year = SPEND // LIFE_YEARS
    return replace(d, invested=d.invested + SPEND - first_year, oi=d.oi + SPEND - first_year)


def show(x: Fraction) -> str:
    """Print an exact cents amount to two decimals (display only)."""
    value = (Decimal(x.numerator) / Decimal(x.denominator)).quantize(Decimal("0.01"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)
    return f"{value}"


def main() -> None:
    divisions = generate(SEED, N_DIVISIONS)
    print(f"SIMULATED divisions (seed {SEED}); tax {TAX_BP} bp, capital charge {RATE_BP} bp; money in cents")
    print("name      C         OI      OI/C %   RI          EVA")
    for d in divisions:
        print(
            f"{d.name:<8} {d.invested:>8} {d.oi:>9} {show(100 * return_ratio(d)):>8}"
            f" {show(residual_income(d)):>11} {show(eva(d)):>11}"
        )

    gap_ok = [eva(d) - residual_income(d) == -Fraction(TAX_BP, 10_000) * d.oi for d in divisions]
    rate = Fraction(RATE_BP, 10_000)
    sign_ok = [(residual_income(d) >= 0) == (return_ratio(d) >= rate) for d in [*divisions, BOUNDARY]]
    print(f"identity EVA - RI = -tax * OI holds for {sum(gap_ok)}/{N_DIVISIONS} divisions")
    print(f"RI >= 0 exactly when OI/C >= r holds for {sum(sign_ok)}/{N_DIVISIONS + 1} divisions, boundary included")
    b = BOUNDARY
    print(
        f"boundary division (hand-built, OI/C = r): RI {show(residual_income(b))} cents, "
        f"RI >= 0 {residual_income(b) >= 0}, OI/C >= r {return_ratio(b) >= rate}"
    )

    discordant: list[tuple[str, str]] = []
    for a in divisions:
        for b in divisions:
            if a.name < b.name:
                by_ri = residual_income(a) - residual_income(b)
                by_ratio = return_ratio(a) - return_ratio(b)
                if (by_ri > 0) != (by_ratio > 0) and by_ri != 0 and by_ratio != 0:
                    discordant.append((a.name, b.name))
    print(f"pairs ranked differently by RI and by OI/C: {len(discordant)} of {N_DIVISIONS * (N_DIVISIONS - 1) // 2}")
    if discordant:
        first, second = discordant[0]
        print(f"first such pair: {first} and {second}")

    base = divisions[0]
    adj = capitalized(base)
    print(f"capitalization example on {base.name}: outlay {SPEND} cents, life {LIFE_YEARS} years")
    print(f"  reported:    C {base.invested}  OI {base.oi}  RI {show(residual_income(base))}  EVA {show(eva(base))}")
    print(f"  capitalized: C {adj.invested}  OI {adj.oi}  RI {show(residual_income(adj))}  EVA {show(eva(adj))}")
    print(
        f"  change in RI {show(residual_income(adj) - residual_income(base))}; "
        f"change in EVA {show(eva(adj) - eva(base))}"
    )

6Reading the lab

The lab's table shows the two measures can change sign differently. SIM-G has residual income of 2250.00 cents (22.50 SCU) and EVA of -486.00 cents. The gap is \(\tau\,\mathrm{OI} = 0.20 \times 13{,}680 = 2{,}736\) cents, and \(2{,}250 - 2{,}736 = -486\), as Lemma 8.2 requires. SIM-G earns a positive residual income and a negative EVA on the same inputs. By Lemma 8.2, EVA is residual income less the tax term, and here the tax term (2,736 cents) is larger than the residual income (2,250 cents).

The capitalization lines for SIM-A show a change in residual income of 7280.00 cents and in EVA of 5680.00 cents. Proposition 8.4 gives \(\Delta\mathrm{RI} = \Delta\mathrm{OI} - r\,\Delta C\). For SIM-A the outlay is 10,000 cents with first-year charge 2,000, so \(\Delta\mathrm{OI} = \Delta C = 8{,}000\), and \(8{,}000 - 0.09 \times 8{,}000 = 7{,}280\). The same method gives \(0.8 \times 8{,}000 - 720 = 5{,}680\) for EVA.

7Where the measures stop

  • The base decides the level. Lemma 8.3 is exact for the base chosen. A different base can give a different sign for the same operating income: at \(\mathrm{OI} = 120\) and \(r = 9\%\), a base of 1,000 gives \(\mathrm{RI} = 30\), and a base of 1,400 gives \(\mathrm{RI} = -6\). The lesson takes \(C\) as given, so it cannot say which base is right.
  • The rate is an input. The residual income of a division is zero at \(\mathrm{OI}/C = r\), and changing \(r\) moves that point. This lesson states \(r\) and derives no value for it.
  • Single period. The measures are computed for one period. The lesson does not model how an outlay that is capitalized in one year is charged over later years, beyond the first-year charge in §4.
  • Tax and book treatment. The capitalization example applies the tax rate to the changed operating income. The model does not represent a difference between tax and book treatment of the outlay.

8Misconceptions

  1. “A positive residual income proves that a division creates value.”

    With \(C > 0\) on the stated base, residual income is nonnegative exactly when \(\mathrm{OI}/C \ge r\) (Lemma 8.3). The base and the rate are inputs. The capitalization case in §4 moves residual income from 30 to 102.8 SCU on the same cash outlay, because the base and the operating income change. A positive figure is a statement about the stated base and rate, not a finding that holds for every base.

  2. “Residual income and EVA always agree in sign, so either one gives the same verdict.”

    Lemma 8.2 gives \(\mathrm{EVA} = \mathrm{RI} - \tau\,\mathrm{OI}\). The two differ by a term that depends on operating income, so a division with a large operating income and a small margin over the charge can have a positive residual income and a negative EVA. Lab 1's SIM-G is such a case: 2250.00 cents and -486.00 cents.

  3. “The division with the largest residual income is the best performer.”

    Residual income is an amount, so it depends on the size of the division. For two divisions with the same return on capital above the rate \(r\), the larger one has the larger residual income, because \(\mathrm{RI} = (\mathrm{OI}/C - r)\,C\). In Lab 1, SIM-D has the larger base, 124,000 cents against 59,000 for SIM-A, and the smaller residual income, −4130.00 against −2440.00 cents, while SIM-D has the higher return on its base, OI/C (5.67% against 4.86%). Both returns are below \(r\), so both residual incomes are negative. In the §5 example, Y has the larger residual income (20 SCU against 15), while X has the higher return per unit of capital (0.12 against 0.10). Which division is better depends on the question asked, and residual income alone does not settle it.

9Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Both measures for one division

For \(C = 1{,}200\), \(\mathrm{OI} = 150\), \(\tau = 25\%\) and \(r = 10\%\), compute RI, NOPAT and EVA, and check Lemma 8.2.

Solution

\(\mathrm{RI} = 150 - 0.10 \times 1{,}200 = 150 - 120 = 30\) SCU. NOPAT is \(150 \times 0.75 = 112.5\) SCU. \(\mathrm{EVA} = 112.5 - 120 = -7.5\) SCU. Check: \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = -37.5 = -0.25 \times 150\), as Lemma 8.2 requires. RI is positive and EVA is negative for this division.

M2 · The sign test

For \(C = 800\), \(\mathrm{OI} = 64\) and \(r = 9\%\), compute RI and the ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\), and state the sign of RI.

Solution

\(\mathrm{RI} = 64 - 0.09 \times 800 = 64 - 72 = -8\) SCU. The ratio is \(64/800 = 0.08\), which is below \(0.09\). Lemma 8.3 says RI is negative exactly in that case, and it is.

M3 · A capitalized outlay of 50

Start from \(C = 1{,}000\), \(\mathrm{OI} = 120\), \(\tau = 20\%\), \(r = 9\%\). Capitalize an outlay of 50 SCU over five years, with a first-year charge of 10. Find the new RI and EVA, and check Proposition 8.4.

Solution

\(\Delta\mathrm{OI} = 50 - 10 = 40\) and \(\Delta C = 40\). New \(\mathrm{OI} = 160\) and new \(C = 1{,}040\). \(\mathrm{RI} = 160 - 0.09 \times 1{,}040 = 160 - 93.6 = 66.4\) SCU. \(\mathrm{EVA} = 0.8 \times 160 - 93.6 = 34.4\) SCU. Proposition 8.4: \(\Delta\mathrm{RI} = 40 - 3.6 = 36.4\), and \(66.4 - 30 = 36.4\). \(\Delta\mathrm{EVA} = 0.8 \times 40 - 3.6 = 28.4\), and \(34.4 - 6 = 28.4\).

Proof

P1 · Prove Lemma 8.2

Prove \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\) from the two definitions, showing every step.

Solution

Write the two definitions: \(\mathrm{EVA} = \mathrm{OI}(1-\tau) - rC\) and \(\mathrm{RI} = \mathrm{OI} - rC\). Subtract the second from the first. The \(rC\) terms cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{OI}(1-\tau) - \mathrm{OI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\). No assumption beyond the two definitions is used.

P2 · Prove Lemma 8.3

Prove that for \(C > 0\), \(\mathrm{RI} \ge 0\) if and only if \(\mathrm{OI}/C \ge r\).

Solution

\(\mathrm{RI} \ge 0\) is \(\mathrm{OI} - rC \ge 0\), that is \(\mathrm{OI} \ge rC\). Since \(C > 0\), dividing both sides by \(C\) preserves the inequality and gives \(\mathrm{OI}/C \ge r\). Each step is an equivalence, so the two conditions are equivalent.

P3 · Same base, same order

Show that if two divisions have the same base \(C > 0\), then residual income and \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) order them in the same way.

Solution

For divisions a and b with \(C_a = C_b = C\), \(\mathrm{RI}_a - \mathrm{RI}_b = \mathrm{OI}_a - \mathrm{OI}_b\), because the \(rC\) terms cancel. Also \(\mathrm{OI}_a/C - \mathrm{OI}_b/C = (\mathrm{OI}_a - \mathrm{OI}_b)/C\), which has the same sign as \(\mathrm{OI}_a - \mathrm{OI}_b\) since \(C > 0\). Both differences have the same sign, so the two orders agree.

Empirical

E1 · Read the pair count

Lab 1 reports 4 discordant pairs out of 28. What share of pairs is that, and what does the share describe?

Solution

\(4/28 = 1/7 \approx 0.1429\). The share describes the eight SIMULATED divisions generated with the seed and ranges in the code. It is not a statistic about real divisions. A different seed could change it. Seeds 14, 20, 26 and 28 of seeds 1 to 40 also give 4 discordant pairs, and the other 36 give other counts, so the count shows that the two orders can disagree, not how often.

E2 · Find the sign disagreement

In Lab 1's table, SIM-G has residual income of 2250.00 cents and EVA of -486.00 cents. Compute the gap from the tax term and check it against the two printed values.

Solution

From the printed operating income of 13,680 cents, the tax term is \(0.20 \times 13{,}680 = 2{,}736\) cents. Then \(\mathrm{RI} - \tau\,\mathrm{OI} = 2{,}250 - 2{,}736 = -486\), which matches the printed EVA. The two measures disagree in sign because the tax term exceeds the residual income.

E3 · What the identity checks cover

The lab prints that the identity \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI} = -\tau\,\mathrm{OI}\) holds for 8 of 8 divisions. State what this check does and does not establish about the lesson's measures.

Solution

The check confirms that the arithmetic of the two definitions gives the identity on these eight inputs, with exact fractions. It does not establish that the base or the rate is right, since both are inputs, and it does not test any division outside the generator. The identity is a theorem (Lemma 8.2), so the lab adds a numeric check of the algebra, not evidence about divisions.

Research

R1 · Gross versus net capital

Suppose a division's capital base can be taken gross of accumulated depreciation or net of it. Using Proposition 8.4, state how the choice changes residual income for a given operating income, and what a comparison across divisions needs in order to be fair.

Solution

Taking the base gross instead of net raises \(C\) by the accumulated depreciation, \(D_{\mathrm{acc}}\), with \(\mathrm{OI}\) unchanged. By Proposition 8.4 with \(\Delta\mathrm{OI} = 0\) and \(\Delta C = D_{\mathrm{acc}}\), residual income falls by \(r\,D_{\mathrm{acc}}\). A fair comparison needs one base rule for every division, stated before the figures are read, and an explicit note of which rule each figure uses.

R2 · Choose the rate from evidence

The lesson takes \(r\) as an input. State what a study would need to show before a value of \(r\) could be justified for a division, and say what the lesson does not claim.

Solution

A justification would need a stated definition of the return the firm requires, its sources, and a demonstration that the same rate applies to the division's risk. The lesson does not claim any value for \(r\), does not derive one, and gives no source for one. The 9% in the worked cases and the 900 bp in the lab are illustrative inputs.

R3 · A pre-stated ranking rule

A manager compares divisions of different sizes on residual income. Design a pre-stated rule that uses both residual income and the ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\), and say which outcome would show the rule is no better than residual income alone.

Solution

Rank first by residual income, the amount, and break ties or near-ties by the ratio, the return per unit of capital. The rule fails to add anything if, on a holdout set of divisions, the ratio-based tie-breaks never change a decision the amount-based order would make. The test uses the same bases for every division, stated before any figure is read.

11Retrieval quiz

  1. For any division, \(\mathrm{EVA} - \mathrm{RI}\) equals:

    Options

    Lemma 8.2: the capital charges cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{OI}(1-\tau) - \mathrm{OI}\).

  2. A division has \(C = 1{,}000\) SCU, \(\mathrm{OI} = 120\) SCU and \(r = 9\%\). What is its residual income, in SCU?

    \(120 - 0.09 \times 1{,}000 = 30\).

  3. For the same division, with tax \(\tau = 20\%\), what is EVA, in SCU?

    \(120 \times 0.8 - 90 = 6\).

  4. For \(C > 0\), residual income is nonnegative exactly when:

    Options

    Lemma 8.3: divide \(\mathrm{OI} \ge rC\) by \(C > 0\).

  5. Residual income and the ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) can order two divisions differently because:

    Options

    §5 and P3: with a common base the orders agree. With different bases they can disagree, as 4 of the 28 pairs in Lab 1 do.

  6. Capitalizing an outlay of 100 SCU, with a first-year charge of 20 SCU, on the worked division of §4, changes residual income by how much, in SCU?

    Proposition 8.4: \(\Delta\mathrm{OI} = \Delta C = 80\), so \(\Delta\mathrm{RI} = 80 - 0.09 \times 80 = 72.8\).

  7. For \(C = 1{,}200\), \(\mathrm{OI} = 150\), \(\tau = 25\%\) and \(r = 10\%\), what is EVA, in SCU? (M1)

    NOPAT is \(112.5\); the capital charge is \(120\); \(112.5 - 120 = -7.5\).

  8. In Lab 1, SIM-G has positive residual income and negative EVA. The reason is:

    Options

    §6 and E2: \(2{,}250 - 2{,}736 = -486\).

  9. Where do the capital-charge rate and the tax rate come from in this lesson?

    Options

    §1: both are inputs, and no cost-of-capital concept is defined here.

  10. The ratio \(\mathrm{OI}/C\) for division X in §5 is 0.12 and for Y is 0.10. Residual income ranks Y above X. This shows:
