Managerial & Cost Accounting for Decisions

A financial statement reports what happened. A manager needs to know what will happen if a price changes, a product is dropped, a plant runs a second shift or a division is charged a different transfer price. This module gives the cost-accounting tools for those questions: how costs move with volume, which costs a decision can change, how overhead is assigned, how a budget is set and then compared with results, and how divisions are measured. Its cases are SIMULATED, generated for the module. The module ends in a budget and variance engine, tested by an autograder, and a management memo, graded against a rubric.

  • Prerequisites: AC1 Financial Accounting, and the program foundations that AC1 rests on (P0.1)
  • Unlocks: no module in the curriculum lists AC4 as a prerequisite
  • Project: Budget and variance engine, with a management memo

50 h lessons · 20 h project (28.6%)

Objectives

By the end of AC4 you can:

  1. Classify a cost as fixed, variable or mixed, split a mixed cost with the high-low method, and state what that method does and does not guarantee.
  2. Compute the contribution margin, the break-even volume and the break-even revenue, and show where a single break-even formula fails under a step cost.
  3. Decide between two courses of action by comparing relevant costs, and explain why sunk costs and allocated overhead are excluded from that comparison.
  4. Assign overhead by activity-based costing, and compare the resulting unit costs with volume-based costs on the same case.
  5. Construct a master budget and a flexible budget, and decompose a profit variance into price, volume, mix and efficiency parts that sum exactly to the total.
  6. Measure a division by residual income and economic value added with the capital charge stated as an input, and argue which measure a manager should be judged on.

Prerequisites

AC4 assumes AC1 (Financial Accounting) and the foundations AC1 rests on. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From AC1: the income statement, the balance sheet, accrual accounting and depreciation and amortization.
  • From P0.1: the evidence grade that every claim on these pages carries, and the citation registry that the reading list draws on.

Lesson 1 estimates a mixed cost from two observations and states the limits of that method.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on the prerequisites, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80%, which for nine items means eight correct, since seven is 78%. Skip rungs 1 and 2 only if you reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, revisit AC1 first.

  1. Which identity holds for every balance sheet?

    Options

    Total assets equal total liabilities plus equity (AC1, balance sheet).

  2. Revenue is 500 and cost of revenue is 300. What is the gross margin, as a fraction of revenue?

    \((500 - 300)/500 = 0.4\) (AC1, income statement).

  3. Why is depreciation added back to net income in operating cash flow?

    Options

    Depreciation is a non-cash charge in the income statement (AC1, depreciation and amortization).

  4. What is the median of 1, 2 and 100?

    The median is the middle value of the sorted sample, 2.

  5. Under the program's evidence grades, which grade is given to a result proved on the page?

    Options

    A result proved on the page is graded T (P0.1, evidence grade).

  6. Which statement about the citation registry is correct?

    Options

    Only registry keys can be cited (P0.1, citation registry).

  7. Under accrual accounting, when is revenue recorded?

    Options

    Accrual accounting separates recognition from the cash flow (AC1, accrual accounting).

  8. Skill check (rung 1). A cost has a fixed part of 2,000 USD a month and a variable part of 4 USD a unit. What is the cost at 1,200 units, in USD? This is the flexed cost at actual volume.

    \(2000 + 4 \times 1200 = 6800\) USD (AC4, Lesson 1, Definition 1.2).

  9. Skill check (rung 2). The sales price variance is the actual price less the budget price, times the actual units. The budget price is 20 USD, the actual price is 22 USD and 1,000 units were sold. What is the sales price variance, in USD? A positive value is favourable.

    \((22 - 20) \times 1000 = 2000\) USD, favourable, because the price received exceeded the budget price.

Lessons

Eight lessons of six or seven study hours each; the hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Lessons 1 and 2 cover cost behaviour and break-even. Lessons 3 and 4 cover the decision and costing tools. Lessons 5 and 6 cover budgets and variances, and Lessons 7 and 8 cover transfer prices and divisional measures.

L01 · 6 hCost Behaviour and Contribution MarginFixed, variable and mixed costs, the linear cost function, the contribution margin, and the high-low split of a mixed cost and the limits of that method. L02 · 7 hCost-Volume-Profit Analysis and Break-EvenThe break-even volume and revenue, the margin of safety, operating leverage, and break-even under a step cost in a SIMULATED product. L03 · 6 hRelevant Costs for DecisionsDifferential future cost, sunk costs and allocated overhead, and make-or-buy and keep-or-drop decisions under a capacity limit. L04 · 6 hActivity-Based CostingCost pools, cost drivers and the two-stage assignment, with volume-based and activity-based unit costs compared on two SIMULATED products. L05 · 7 hMaster and Flexible BudgetsThe master budget as the operating plan, the flexible budget at actual volume, and why a master budget cannot be compared with actuals directly. L06 · 6 hBudget Variance AnalysisSales price, sales volume, sales mix and efficiency variances, the sign convention, and the exact reconciliation to total profit variance. L07 · 6 hTransfer PricingThe feasible transfer-price range under a capacity constraint, the opportunity-cost argument, and the conditions under which the range is empty. L08 · 6 hResidual Income and Economic Value AddedResidual income and EVA with a stated capital charge, the accounting choices that change both measures, and their use in divisional review.

The project ladder

The module ends in the budget and variance engine and management memo. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. A student skips them only by reaching the placement bar and answering both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated SIMULATED case: a master budget, a flexed budget, four variances, and a reconciliation line by line, with the reason for every step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same case with the mix and efficiency lines removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    An autograded budget engine, tested on a generated SIMULATED case for the seed shown in the autograder panel of the project page, and a management memo graded against a rubric with annotated exemplars. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended extension of the engine, such as a rolling forecast or a two-stage transfer price, with a stated hypothesis and no ceiling.

Data status

SIMULATED cases only. The curriculum names no data source for this module, and company cost data is internal. The cases, figures and labs of AC4 are generated from a seeded random number generator and labelled SIMULATED, and no number on any page is a real company's. Open items for the module, including the open references and data sources, are recorded in plans/AC4-open-items.md.
