Earnings Management and Discretionary Accruals

Estimates such as receivables, depreciation and reserves, and the timing of revenue, involve judgement. Accrual-based tests try to find the part of accruals that discretion explains, after removing the part that normal growth explains. The modified Jones model is one such split, and it is also easy to misuse. This lesson sets out the model as the A15 contract fits it, proves what its no-intercept regression does to the residuals, and shows in a SIMULATED cross-section with a planted manipulation how well the residuals recover it. The limits are the lesson's main result, and they are stated with the numbers.

1What earnings management is

Definition 1.1 (earnings management)

Earnings management is the use of managerial discretion over accounting estimates, classifications and the timing of recognition to change the reported earnings of a period. Discretion within the accounting rules is one case. Outright misstatement is a separate category, which the accrual tests of this lesson do not establish.

The A15 contract fits the model defined in Section 2 and cites it to the accrual-model literature; see registry key dechow1995detecting, Detecting Earnings Management, The Accounting Review 70(2), 1995. The paper evaluates alternative accrual-based models for detecting earnings management and compares the specification and power of commonly used test statistics. No power figure from the paper is stated here. The results that follow come from Lab 1, which is SIMULATED.

Definition 1.2 (nondiscretionary and discretionary accruals)

Scaled total accruals are split into two parts: \(TA_i/A_{i,t-1} = NDA_i + DA_i\), where \(A_{i,t-1}\) is lagged total assets, \(NDA_i\) is the part that normal growth and the asset mix explain, and \(DA_i\) is the remainder. The remainder is the discretionary accrual. It is the quantity an accrual test reads as evidence of management.

2The modified Jones model

Definition 2.1 (discretionary accruals, modified Jones model)

Discretionary accruals (modified Jones model) are the residuals of the regression \[ \frac{TA_i}{A_{i,t-1}} = a_1\frac{1}{A_{i,t-1}} + a_2\frac{\Delta REV_i - \Delta REC_i}{A_{i,t-1}} + a_3\frac{PPE_i}{A_{i,t-1}} + \eta_i, \] estimated by ordinary least squares without an intercept. Here \(TA_i\) is total accruals, \(\Delta REV_i\) and \(\Delta REC_i\) are the changes in revenue and in receivables, and \(PPE_i\) is net PP&E, all in CU. The fitted values \(\widehat{NDA}_i\) are the nondiscretionary accruals, and \(\widehat{DA}_i = TA_i/A_{i,t-1} - \widehat{NDA}_i\) are the discretionary accruals.

The model adds a receivables adjustment to the revenue term: the regressor is the change in revenue less the change in receivables, divided by lagged assets. The A15 contract fits these same three regressors, and the lesson uses the contract's definition exactly. The contract fits no intercept. The passage locator for the published specification's intercept is not confirmed, and Section 4 shows why the choice matters. The rationale for the receivables term is not sourced in this lesson, and the lesson does not test it on data.

3What the no-intercept regression does to its residuals

Proposition 3.1 (residual sums under the normal equations)

Let \(X\) be the \(n \times 3\) design with columns \(1/A\), \((\Delta REV - \Delta REC)/A\) and \(PPE/A\), let \(y = TA/A\), and let \(b\) be the least-squares coefficient vector with residuals \(e = y - Xb\). Then \(X^\top e = 0\). For every response vector \(y\), the residuals sum to zero if and only if the ones vector \(\mathbf{1}\) lies in the column space of \(X\).

Proof

The least-squares solution satisfies the normal equations \(X^\top(y - Xb) = 0\), which is \(X^\top e = 0\). Let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto the column space of \(X\), so that \(\hat y = Py\) and \(e = (I - P)y\). The projection is symmetric and idempotent. The sum of the residuals is \(\mathbf{1}^\top e = \mathbf{1}^\top (I - P) y = \big((I - P)\mathbf{1}\big)^\top y\). This is zero for every \(y\) if and only if \((I - P)\mathbf{1} = 0\), that is, \(\mathbf{1} = P\mathbf{1}\), which says exactly that \(\mathbf{1}\) lies in the column space of \(X\). If \(\mathbf{1} = Xc\) for some \(c\), then \(\mathbf{1}^\top e = c^\top X^\top e = 0\) directly.

Remark 3.2 (a two-firm counterexample)

Take two firms with \(A = 1\) and \(A = 2\), and a single regressor \(1/A\), so that \(X = (1, 0.5)^\top\). Let \(y = (1, 1)^\top\). Then \(b = (x^\top y)/(x^\top x) = 1.5/1.25 = 1.2\), the fitted values are \((1.2, 0.6)\), and the residuals are \((-0.2, 0.4)\). They sum to \(0.2\), not zero. The ones vector \((1, 1)^\top\) is not a multiple of \((1, 0.5)^\top\), so it is outside the column space, as Proposition 3.1 requires for a nonzero sum.

Proposition 3.3 (the coefficient bias is the projection's coefficient vector)

Suppose \(y = Xb^\ast + d + u\), where \(d\) is a function of \(X\) (or is fixed given \(X\)), and \(\E[u \mid X] = 0\). Then \(\E[\hat b \mid X] = b^\ast + (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\). The vector \((X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\) is the coefficient bias: it is the coefficient vector of the least-squares projection of \(d\) on the regressors. The projection itself is \(X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\), an \(n\)-vector.

Proof

Substitute \(y = Xb^\ast + d + u\) into \(\hat b = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y\). This gives \(\hat b = b^\ast + (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d + (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top u\). Because \(d\) is a function of \(X\), the first correction term is a function of \(X\) and passes through the conditional expectation unchanged. Because \(\E[u \mid X] = 0\), the last term has conditional mean zero. Hence \(\E[\hat b \mid X] = b^\ast + (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\). Writing \(c = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\), the fitted projection of \(d\) is \(Xc = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\).

Proposition 3.3 is the reason the estimation sample matters. The coefficient bias \((X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\) is zero exactly when \(X^\top d = 0\), that is, when the omitted term is orthogonal to every regressor. Otherwise the estimate absorbs part of \(d\), and the residuals then understate it. The regressor \(1/A\) is largest for the smallest firms, so a planted term concentrated among small firms projects onto it most strongly. Lesson L06 does not claim that every real sample has this problem. It identifies the condition under which the problem arises.

The units of the coefficients follow from Definition 2.1. Because \(TA/A\) is a ratio and \(1/A\) carries units of \(1/\text{CU}\), the coefficient \(a_1\) is measured in CU. A coefficient such as 0.05 is 0.05 CU, and its size depends on the currency unit of the filer. The other two coefficients are unitless, since \((\Delta REV - \Delta REC)/A\) and \(PPE/A\) are ratios of CU to CU. Under a common scaling by \(k\), \(a_1\) is multiplied by \(k\), while \(a_2\), \(a_3\) and the residuals are unchanged. This is the wording of the A15 contract's Scale row.

Lab 1 · Recovering a planted manipulation with the modified Jones regression

The lab generates 2,000 SIMULATED firm-years. Ten per cent are manipulators, and each manipulator has a planted discretionary accrual of 0.05 times lagged assets in the strong scenario, or 0.01 in the weak one. Non-manipulators have a discretionary accrual with standard deviation 0.01. Stated before running: the regression estimated on the non-manipulators (an oracle, not available in practice) should recover the planted \(a_2\) and \(a_3\) to within about two classical standard errors. The coefficient \(a_1\) on \(1/A\) is weakly identified, so its standard error should be large relative to its planted value. Estimated on all firms, the coefficient on \(1/A\) should shift by the projection of the planted term plus the difference in sampling noise between the all-firm and non-manipulator estimates, and the lab decomposes the shift into those components. Flagging the top 10% of discretionary accruals should separate manipulators well when the planted shift is 0.05, and less well when it is 0.01. All flag scores come from one draw at seed 606.

"""AC2 L06 lab 1: the modified Jones model on a SIMULATED cross-section with a planted manipulation.

SIMULATED: every firm-year is generated here from the seeded model below. No filing is read.
The manipulation is planted by the author: a known share of firms receives a positive discretionary
accrual of SHIFT times lagged assets. The lab asks whether the regression recovers it, and reports
the limits of that recovery. The generator records which firm-years manipulate, so every flag can be scored.
The flag scores quoted in the lesson are one simulated draw (seed 606) and are not averages over seeds.
In real data, a large discretionary accrual is evidence of a candidate, not of intent.

Model, per firm i (all money in currency units, CU; ratios are per unit of lagged assets):
    lag_i     lagged total assets, lognormal around 1000 CU
    drev_i    change in revenue, drec_i change in receivables
    ppe_i     net PP&E
    total_i   total accruals = C1 / lag + C2 * (drev - drec) / lag + C3 * ppe / lag + DA_i
    DA_i      = SHIFT * manipulator_i + NOISE_SD * v_i,  v_i iid N(0, 1)

The estimator is the A15 contract's modified_jones: regress total/lag on
[1/lag, (drev - drec)/lag, ppe/lag] WITHOUT an intercept, by least squares (numpy lstsq, rcond=None).
Its fitted values are the nondiscretionary accruals and its residuals the discretionary accruals.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import math
from dataclasses import dataclass

import numpy as np
from numpy.typing import NDArray

F64 = NDArray[np.float64]

N_FIRMS = 2000
MANIP_SHARE = 0.10  # share of firm-years that manipulate, planted
C_TRUE = (0.05, 0.10, -0.08)  # planted coefficients of the nondiscretionary part, on 1/lag, (drev-drec)/lag, ppe/lag
NOISE_SD = 0.01  # normal discretionary variation, ratio to lagged assets
SEED = 606


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Sample:
    """A cross-section of firm-years: money in CU, manipulator flags are the planted truth."""

    total: F64
    lag: F64
    drev: F64
    drec: F64
    ppe: F64
    manipulator: NDArray[np.bool_]
    planted: F64  # SHIFT times the manipulator flag: the omitted term d of the regression, in ratio units
    noise: F64  # NOISE_SD times the normal draw: the sampling error of the discretionary term


def make_sample(seed: int, shift: float, n: int = N_FIRMS) -> Sample:
    """Generate n firm-years; shift is the planted discretionary accrual of a manipulator, in ratio units."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    lag = np.exp(rng.normal(np.log(1000.0), 0.6, n))
    drev = lag * rng.normal(0.10, 0.20, n)
    drec = drev * rng.uniform(0.10, 0.30, n) + lag * rng.normal(0.0, 0.02, n)
    ppe = lag * rng.uniform(0.20, 0.60, n)
    manipulator = np.zeros(n, dtype=np.bool_)
    manipulator[rng.permutation(n)[: round(MANIP_SHARE * n)]] = True
    noise = rng.standard_normal(n)
    da = shift * manipulator + NOISE_SD * noise
    nondiscretionary = C_TRUE[0] / lag + C_TRUE[1] * (drev - drec) / lag + C_TRUE[2] * ppe / lag
    total = lag * (nondiscretionary + da)
    return Sample(
        total=total,
        lag=lag,
        drev=drev,
        drec=drec,
        ppe=ppe,
        manipulator=manipulator,
        planted=shift * manipulator.astype(np.float64),
        noise=NOISE_SD * noise,
    )


def design(s: Sample) -> tuple[F64, F64]:
    """Regressors [1/lag, (drev - drec)/lag, ppe/lag] and the dependent variable total/lag, one row per firm-year."""
    x = np.column_stack([1.0 / s.lag, (s.drev - s.drec) / s.lag, s.ppe / s.lag])
    return x, s.total / s.lag


def modified_jones(s: Sample, mask: NDArray[np.bool_] | None = None) -> tuple[tuple[float, float, float], F64, F64]:
    """A15 contract's modified_jones on the rows selected by mask (all rows if None).

    Returns the coefficients (a1, a2, a3), the fitted nondiscretionary accruals and the discretionary accruals
    (total/lag minus fitted) for every row of the sample, all in ratio units of lagged assets.
    """
    keep = np.ones(s.lag.size, dtype=np.bool_) if mask is None else mask
    x, y = design(s)
    coef, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(x[keep], y[keep], rcond=None)
    fitted = x @ coef
    return (float(coef[0]), float(coef[1]), float(coef[2])), fitted, y - fitted


def classical_se(s: Sample, mask: NDArray[np.bool_]) -> F64:
    """Classical OLS standard errors of (a1, a2, a3) on the rows in mask, with iid errors assumed."""
    x, y = design(s)
    xk, yk = x[mask], y[mask]
    coef, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(xk, yk, rcond=None)
    resid = yk - xk @ coef
    s2 = float(resid @ resid) / (yk.size - xk.shape[1])
    return np.sqrt(np.diag(s2 * np.linalg.inv(xk.T @ xk)))


def report(label: str, s: Sample) -> None:
    """Estimate on all firm-years (the practical case) and on the non-manipulators (an oracle, not available)."""
    (a1, a2, a3), _, da = modified_jones(s)
    print(f"{label} coefficients_all_firms {a1:.4f} {a2:.4f} {a3:.4f}")
    (o1, o2, o3), _, da_oracle = modified_jones(s, ~s.manipulator)
    print(f"{label} coefficients_non_manipulators {o1:.4f} {o2:.4f} {o3:.4f}")
    print(f"{label} rows_non_manipulators {int(np.sum(~s.manipulator))} of {s.lag.size}")
    se = classical_se(s, ~s.manipulator)
    print(f"{label} se_non_manipulators {se[0]:.4f} {se[1]:.4f} {se[2]:.4f}")
    decompose_a1(label, s, a1, o1)
    z2 = (o2 - C_TRUE[1]) / se[1]
    tail2 = 2.0 * (1.0 - 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(abs(z2) / math.sqrt(2.0))))
    print(f"{label} a2_nm_6dp {o2:.6f} se2_6dp {se[1]:.6f} z_a2_fp {z2:.3f} two_sided_tail_a2_fp {tail2:.3f}")
    print(f"{label} planted_coefficients {C_TRUE[0]:.4f} {C_TRUE[1]:.4f} {C_TRUE[2]:.4f}")

    m = s.manipulator
    print(f"{label} mean_da_manipulators {float(da[m].mean()):.4f} mean_da_others {float(da[~m].mean()):.4f}")

    # Flag the 10% of firm-years with the largest discretionary accruals, the same share as the planted one.
    cut = float(np.quantile(da, 1.0 - MANIP_SHARE))
    flagged = da >= cut
    tpr = float(np.mean(flagged[m]))
    fpr = float(np.mean(flagged[~m]))
    print(f"{label} all_firms_top10pct true_positive_rate {tpr:.4f} false_positive_rate {fpr:.4f}")

    cut_o = float(np.quantile(da_oracle, 1.0 - MANIP_SHARE))
    flagged_o = da_oracle >= cut_o
    print(
        f"{label} oracle_top10pct true_positive_rate {float(np.mean(flagged_o[m])):.4f} "
        f"false_positive_rate {float(np.mean(flagged_o[~m])):.4f}"
    )
    print(f"{label} flags_only_all_firms {int(np.sum(flagged & ~flagged_o))}")
    print(f"{label} flags_only_oracle {int(np.sum(flagged_o & ~flagged))}")
    print(f"{label} flags_differing_all_vs_oracle {int(np.sum(flagged != flagged_o))}")


def decompose_a1(label: str, s: Sample, a1_all: float, a1_nm: float) -> None:
    """Split the all-firm minus non-manipulator difference in a1 into its exact components.

    With y = X C + planted + noise (C the planted coefficients), b_all - C = P_all planted + P_all noise, and
    b_nm - C = (X_nm'X_nm)^-1 X_nm' noise_nm, because the planted term is zero on non-manipulators. The
    conditional-mean prediction uses E[planted | X] = SHIFT * MANIP_SHARE, a constant, which is a function of X.
    """
    x, _ = design(s)
    xtx_inv = np.linalg.inv(x.T @ x)
    nm = ~s.manipulator
    x_nm = x[nm]
    dev_nm = np.linalg.inv(x_nm.T @ x_nm) @ (x_nm.T @ s.noise[nm])
    proj_planted = (xtx_inv @ (x.T @ s.planted))[0]
    proj_noise = (xtx_inv @ (x.T @ s.noise))[0]
    diff = a1_all - a1_nm
    residual = diff - (proj_planted + proj_noise - dev_nm[0])
    mean_d = float(np.mean(s.planted))
    pred = (xtx_inv @ (x.T @ np.full(s.lag.size, mean_d)))[0]
    print(f"{label} a1_all_minus_nm {diff:.4f}")
    print(f"{label} a1_projection_of_planted {proj_planted:.4f}")
    print(f"{label} a1_projection_of_noise_all {proj_noise:.4f}")
    print(f"{label} a1_nm_sampling_deviation {float(dev_nm[0]):.4f}")
    print(f"{label} a1_decomposition_residual {residual:.6f}")
    print(f"{label} a1_conditional_mean_prediction {float(pred):.4f}")


SCALE_SEEDS = (606, 607, 608, 609, 610)


def se_scaling() -> None:
    """Mean classical standard error of a1 on the non-manipulator rows, over SCALE_SEEDS, at two sample sizes."""
    for n in (2000, 64000):
        ses: list[float] = []
        rows = 0
        for seed in SCALE_SEEDS:
            sample = make_sample(seed, 0.05, n)
            rows = int(np.sum(~sample.manipulator))
            ses.append(classical_se(sample, ~sample.manipulator)[0])
        print(f"a1_se_mean firms {n} non_manipulator_rows {rows} seeds {len(SCALE_SEEDS)} {float(np.mean(ses)):.4f}")


def main() -> None:
    print(f"seed {SEED} firms {N_FIRMS} manipulator_share {MANIP_SHARE}")
    report("strong_shift_0.05", make_sample(SEED, 0.05))
    report("weak_shift_0.01", make_sample(SEED, 0.01))
    se_scaling()

4Estimation choices that the lab exposes

Three choices in the fit decide what the residuals mean. The lab bears on the second and third. It fits no intercept, so it says nothing about the first: Remark 3.2 shows that without an intercept the discretionary accruals need not average to zero. The second is the estimation sample. The all-firm and non-manipulator estimates of \(a_1\) differ by 0.2084 at seed 606, and the interpretation of Lab 1 gives the three printed components of that difference. The third is the scale of the regressors. The coefficient on \(1/A\) is measured in CU, and its regressor is about 0.001 for a 1,000 CU firm, so the same residual noise gives a large standard error. In Lab 1 the classical standard error of \(a_1\) on the 1,800 non-manipulator rows of the seed-606 sample of 2,000 firm-years is 0.2824, which is 5.6 times the planted value of 0.05 (\(0.2824/0.05 = 5.6\)). The lab's se_scaling output gives a mean standard error of 0.0489 over seeds 606 to 610, on 57,600 non-manipulator rows at 64,000 firm-years, which is close to the planted value.

5Where the canon breaks

The modified Jones model is a model of normal accruals. The residual measures discretion only if the model is correct about normal accruals. The lab removes that doubt by construction, because the generator's normal accruals are the model's. Real data restore the doubt, and the following limits apply.

  • The residual is not a test of intent. A large discretionary accrual can come from a growing firm whose normal model is misspecified, from an estimate that was honest but wrong, or from deliberate choices. The residual measures a component, and the component is not identified as manipulation. The paper compares the specification and power of commonly used test statistics for detecting earnings management, and no power figure from it is quoted here.
  • The receivables adjustment is not tested against a revenue manipulation in Lab 1. The adjustment is a regressor: the change in revenue less the change in receivables, over lagged assets, enters the fit whether or not the manipulation runs through revenue. Lab 1 plants no manipulation through revenue: the planted manipulation enters the discretionary term directly. The revenue-less-receivables term does enter the generator's true accruals, with coefficient 0.10 on (ΔREV − ΔREC)/A, so the lab cannot test whether the adjustment picks up a revenue manipulation.
  • Accrual tests see only accruals. The model of Definition 2.1 has no term for management that works through real activity, such as the timing of discretionary spending or of production. An accrual test is therefore blind to such management by construction. No registry source for that practice is cited in this lesson, so the lesson makes no claim about its size.
  • Grouping changes the model. The model can be estimated within groups of firm-years, such as industry-years, so that industry-wide accrual patterns are absorbed. The A15 contract estimates one cross-section, and Lab 1 has one cross-section, so the lab cannot show the effect of grouping.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A large discretionary accrual proves earnings management.”

    The residual is a component of accruals that the model does not explain (Definition 2.1). It is a candidate, and its size is evidence of unexplained accrual, not of intent. In Lab 1, at seed 606, the top-decile flag identified 197 manipulators in the strong scenario. That result is clean because the generator makes the planted shift the only systematic term in the residual. In the weak scenario the same rule had a false-positive rate of 0.0717 on 1,800 non-manipulators, about 129 firms, next to 71 true flags.

  2. “When manipulation is rare, estimating on all firms is harmless.”

    The all-firm estimate of \(a_1\) differs from the non-manipulator estimate by 0.2084 at seed 606. That difference is the realised projection of the planted term (0.1425), plus the projection of the all-firm sampling noise (0.1965), minus the non-manipulator estimate's own sampling deviation (0.1306). Only the first term is the effect of including manipulators, and the other two are sampling error. The share of manipulators does not bound the projection, because the projection depends on the regressors as well. The difference is 0.74 of the classical standard error 0.2824, which is measured on 1,800 non-manipulator rows.

  3. “A residual test that flags the top decile identifies the manipulators.”

    Precision is the share of flagged firm-years that are manipulators: 71 of 200 (0.355) in the weak scenario and 197 of 200 (0.985) in the strong one, both at seed 606. In the weak scenario, at seed 606, that precision is 71 of 200, or 0.355 (E1), so most flagged firms are not manipulators. In the strong scenario, at the same seed, the precision is 0.985 (E2). Two shifts at one seed do not establish how the precision depends on the shift.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · Discretionary accrual by hand

Coefficients are \(a_1 = 0.05\), \(a_2 = 0.10\) and \(a_3 = -0.08\), all with the units of Definition 2.1. A firm has \(A = 1000\) CU, \(\Delta REV = 120\), \(\Delta REC = 30\), \(PPE = 400\), and total accruals \(TA = -30\) CU. Compute \(\widehat{NDA}\), \(\widehat{DA}\), and say what the sign means.

Solution

The nondiscretionary ratio is \(0.05/1000 + 0.10 \times (120 - 30)/1000 - 0.08 \times 400/1000 = 0.00005 + 0.009 - 0.032 = -0.02295\). The scaled total accrual is \(-30/1000 = -0.03\). So \(\widehat{DA} = -0.03 - (-0.02295) = -0.00705\). The discretionary accrual is negative: the firm's accruals are more negative than the model's normal level for a firm of this size and mix. A negative residual is not evidence of income-decreasing manipulation on its own, for the reasons of Section 6.

M2 · Detection under the planted shift

Suppose a manipulator's discretionary accrual is \(N(0.05, 0.01^2)\) and a non-manipulator's is \(N(0, 0.01^2)\). A rule flags a firm if its accrual exceeds a threshold \(c\) that gives a 5% one-sided false-positive rate. Find \(c\) and the detection rate.

Solution

A 5% one-sided false-positive rate needs \(c = 0.01 \times 1.6449 = 0.01645\). The detection rate is \(P(N(0.05, 0.01^2) \gt c) = \Phi((0.05 - 0.01645)/0.01) = \Phi(3.355) \approx 0.9996\). The planted shift is five standard deviations of the noise, so under these assumed distributions the rule almost never misses. The lab's 0.985 is the share of the 200 manipulators that the top-decile rule flags in one draw. That rule uses a threshold set by the sample, not the 5% threshold of this calculation, so the two figures are not directly comparable. The gap is a reminder that the distributions are not known in practice.

M3 · The contamination in standard-error units

From Lab 1, the all-firm and non-manipulator estimates of \(a_1\) are 0.3891 and 0.1806, and the classical standard error of the non-manipulator estimate is 0.2824. Express the contamination in standard-error units and say whether it is large. Then say which flags change in the weak scenario, using the lab's printed counts.

Solution

The difference of the printed values is \(0.3891 - 0.1806 = 0.2085\), and the full-precision difference is 0.2084. In standard-error units it is \(0.2084/0.2824 = 0.738\), where 0.2824 is the classical standard error on the 1,800 non-manipulator rows. The contamination is about three quarters of one standard error. It is not large by the usual test standard, but it is comparable to the estimate's own sampling error. The difference splits into the realised projection of the planted term (0.1425) and two sampling terms (0.1965 and \(-0.1306\)), so it is not a clean measure of the bias. Whether it matters depends on the question. In the weak scenario at seed 606 the lab prints flags_only_all_firms 6 and flags_only_oracle 6: six firm-years enter the top decile under the all-firm estimate and six leave it, so 12 firm-years change flag status. The printed true-positive and false-positive rates are the same under both estimates.

Proof

P1 · When the residuals sum to zero

Using Proposition 3.1, prove that the residuals sum to zero for every \(y\) when the ones vector lies in the column space of \(X\). Then show that the design of Remark 3.2 does not satisfy that condition.

Solution

If \(\mathbf{1} = Xc\) for some \(c\), then \(\mathbf{1}^\top e = c^\top X^\top e = 0\) by the normal equations \(X^\top e = 0\), for every \(y\). For the design of Remark 3.2, \(X = (1, 0.5)^\top\), and the ones vector \((1, 1)^\top\) is not a multiple of \((1, 0.5)^\top\), so it lies outside the column space. Consistently, the residuals \((-0.2, 0.4)\) sum to 0.2, not zero.

P2 · Discretionary accruals are scale invariant

Show that replacing each money item of every firm by \(k\) times its value, for a constant \(k \gt 0\), leaves the residuals \(\widehat{DA}_i\) and the coefficients \(a_2\) and \(a_3\) unchanged, though \(a_1\) is multiplied by \(k\).

Solution

Under scaling, \(TA/A\) is unchanged, \((\Delta REV - \Delta REC)/A\) is unchanged, and \(PPE/A\) is unchanged, because each is a ratio of money items. The regressors \(a_2\) and \(a_3\) multiply unchanged regressors and the dependent variable is unchanged, so \(\hat a_2\) and \(\hat a_3\) are unchanged. The regressor \(1/A\) becomes \(1/(kA) = (1/k)(1/A)\). A least-squares fit with a regressor rescaled by \(1/k\) has its coefficient rescaled by \(k\), so \(\hat a_1\) becomes \(k\hat a_1\). The fitted values \(X\hat b\) are unchanged, so \(\widehat{NDA}_i\) and \(\widehat{DA}_i = TA_i/A_i - \widehat{NDA}_i\) are unchanged. This is the wording of the A15 contract's Scale row: residuals, \(a_2\) and \(a_3\) are invariant, and \(a_1\) scales, because it multiplies \(1/A\) and carries CU.

P3 · The coefficient bias

Prove Proposition 3.3 assuming only that \(d\) is a function of \(X\) and that \(\E[u \mid X] = 0\). Then say what the proposition implies when \(X^\top d = 0\).

Solution

Write \(\hat b - b^\ast = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d + (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top u\). The first term is a function of \(X\), because \(d\) is a function of \(X\), so it passes through the conditional expectation unchanged. The second term has conditional mean \((X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top\,\E[u \mid X] = 0\). Hence \(\E[\hat b \mid X] - b^\ast = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\). Both requirements are used: if \(d\) were not fixed given \(X\), the first term would not pass through the conditional expectation. When \(X^\top d = 0\), the coefficient bias is zero, and the projection \(X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top d\) is zero too. The omitted term is then absorbed only in the residuals.

Empirical

E1 · Precision of the flags in the weak scenario

In the weak scenario of Lab 1, at seed 606, the top-decile rule flags 200 firm-years of 2,000, and 71 of the flagged firms are manipulators. What is the precision of the flag, and how does it compare with the 10% base rate of manipulators?

Solution

Precision is \(71/200 = 0.355\). The base rate of manipulators is 10%. The flags therefore raise the share of manipulators among flagged firms from 10% to 35.5%, a factor of 3.55. The rule is better than chance, but most flagged firms are not manipulators. The false-positive rate is 0.0717, which means about 129 of the 1,800 non-manipulators are flagged, and \(129/200 = 0.645\) of the flags are false.

E2 · Precision in the strong scenario

In the strong scenario of Lab 1, at seed 606, 197 of the 200 flagged firm-years are manipulators. Compute the precision and the number of false flags, and say what those numbers depend on.

Solution

Precision is \(197/200 = 0.985\). The false-positive rate is 0.0017, which is \(0.0017 \times 1800 \approx 3\) non-manipulators flagged, matching the 3 flags that are not manipulators. Both numbers depend on the planted shift in this generator and on the one draw at seed 606. Neither is a property of real filers.

E3 · How far the planted \(a_2\) is

In the strong scenario, the non-manipulator estimate of \(a_2\) is 0.103076 against a planted 0.10, and its classical standard error is 0.001467 (both printed by the lab at full precision). Express the difference in standard-error units, compute the two-sided tail probability at that distance, and say whether one seed is enough to judge the regression's recovery.

Solution

The lab prints the estimate \(b = 0.103076\) and the classical standard error \(0.001467\) to six decimals. The difference from the planted 0.10 is \(0.003076\), so the quotient of the six-decimal values is \(z = 0.003076/0.001467 = 2.097\) standard errors. At full precision the lab computes \(z = 2.096\) (printed as z_a2_fp 2.096). Under correct specification and iid errors, the two-sided tail probability is \(2(1 - \Phi(2.096)) = 0.036\) (printed as two_sided_tail_a2_fp 0.036). Using the rounded inputs 0.0031 and 0.0015 gives \(z = 2.067\) and a tail of 0.039. That is an artefact of rounding the inputs: rounding the standard error alone gives \(z = 2.051\) (tail 0.040), and rounding the difference alone gives \(z = 2.113\) (tail 0.035). A draw this far out has that two-sided probability. A single seed cannot settle whether the estimator recovers \(a_2\) without bias. The judgement needs many seeds, which is the design of R1 and not a result of this lesson.

Research

R1 · How much data the coefficient on 1/A needs

In Lab 1, at seed 606, the classical standard error of \(a_1\) on the 1,800 non-manipulator rows of a 2,000 firm-year sample is 0.2824. Using the same generator over seeds 606 to 610, find the number of firm-years at which the mean classical standard error of \(a_1\), on the non-manipulator rows, falls to about the planted value 0.05.

Solution

The lab's se_scaling output is the mean classical standard error of \(a_1\) over the non-manipulator rows, averaged over five replications, seeds 606 to 610. It prints 0.2781 on 1,800 non-manipulator rows (2,000 firm-years) and 0.0489 on 57,600 rows (64,000 firm-years). The square-root rule in the number of rows gives \(0.2781\sqrt{1800/57600} = 0.0492\), close to the printed 0.0489. Setting the rule equal to 0.05 gives \(1800 \times (0.2781/0.05)^2 \approx 55{,}700\) non-manipulator rows, which is about 61,900 firm-years at a 10% manipulator share. The answer is a property of this generator, whose 1/A regressor has the scale it has. The classical standard error of \(a_1\) at the 2,000 firm-years of Lab 1 is 5.6 times the planted value; at 64,000 firm-years the lab's mean is 0.0489.

R2 · A real version of the test

Specify the design of a real modified Jones test: the grouping, the estimation sample, the treatment of the intercept, the outlier rule, and the way the discretionary accrual would be linked to a later outcome. Name the input most likely to make the flags meaningless.

Solution

Group firm-years by industry and year, so that the normal accrual model is estimated within a set of firms that face similar accounting and economic conditions. Estimate on all firms in the group, but report the estimates with and without a robust outlier rule, because the coefficient bias of Proposition 3.3 is the projection of the omitted term, so a few firms with large terms can move the coefficients. Settle the intercept choice before estimation, and report the residual sums. Link the residual to an outcome such as a later restatement or an enforcement action, with the outcome defined before the sort. The input most likely to make the flags meaningless is the normal-accrual model itself: if the model misses a real driver of accruals in a group, the residuals measure the miss. The lab cannot test this, because its normal model is exactly right by construction.

R3 · What a SIMULATED result can and cannot show

State what Lab 1 shows about the modified Jones regression, and what a real study would need in addition before its flags could support a claim about a specific firm.

Solution

Lab 1 shows that, when the normal model is correct and the manipulation enters the discretionary term, the regression recovers the planted structure to a degree that depends on the planted shift and the sample size, and that estimating on all firms moves the coefficients by the realised projection of the planted term plus sampling noise, as the decomposition in Lab 1 shows. It does not show that a real manipulation enters the discretionary term, that the normal model is correct for a real industry, or that a flagged firm manipulated. A claim about a specific firm needs the outcome, the normal model's fit to that firm's industry, the alternative explanations for the residual, and an external check of the accounting, none of which the lab supplies.
