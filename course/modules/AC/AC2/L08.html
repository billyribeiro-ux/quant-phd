The Beneish M-score; using the scores together

A screen that flags manipulators also flags firms that are not manipulating. How many of its flags are wrong depends on the false-positive rate and on how common manipulation is (Proposition 4.2). The A15 contract defines a linear score from eight ratios, seven of them year-on-year. The constants are the part a user most needs and the part that could not be confirmed. This lesson defines the score as the A15 contract does, shows the arithmetic in full, and derives the false-positive rates of the screens that the module's two scores make possible. It ends the module with what the scores can and cannot establish.

1The eight indices and the M-score

Beneish (1999) appears in the registry as the paper in the Financial Analysts Journal, volume 55, issue 5, pages 24 to 36, titled "The Detection of Earnings Manipulation" (beneish1999detection). The score below is defined by the A15 contract. The contract says that the intercept and weights are published constants (unconfirmed here) and that the implementation must state them with a locator. The intercept and the eight weights have not been confirmed against the paper or a source that reproduces its table, so §2 treats them as unconfirmed.

Definition 1.1 (Beneish M-score)

For a firm-year \(t\) with prior year \(t-1\), define the eight indices of the table below. The Beneish M-score is the linear combination \(M = \beta_0 + \sum_k \beta_k x_k\), where \(x_k\) is index \(k\) and \(\beta_0\), \(\beta_k\) are the constants of §2, which are unconfirmed. Every index is a ratio, so the index values are dimensionless.

IndexReading when large
DSRIReceivables grow faster than sales
GMIGross margin falls
AQINon-current, non-PPE assets grow as a share of total assets
SGISales growth
DEPIThe depreciation rate falls
SGAISelling, general and administrative costs grow faster than sales
LVGILeverage rises
TATAAccruals are high relative to assets

The index definitions, as the contract's beneish_m_score states them, are:

  • DSRI: \[\mathrm{DSRI}=\frac{\mathrm{rec}_t/\mathrm{rev}_t}{\mathrm{rec}_{t-1}/\mathrm{rev}_{t-1}}\]
  • GMI: \[\mathrm{GMI}=\frac{\mathrm{GM}_{t-1}}{\mathrm{GM}_t}\]
  • AQI: \[\mathrm{AQI}=\frac{a_t}{a_{t-1}}\], where \[a_s=1-\frac{\mathrm{CA}_s+\mathrm{PPE}_s}{\mathrm{TA}_s}\]
  • SGI: \[\mathrm{SGI}=\frac{\mathrm{rev}_t}{\mathrm{rev}_{t-1}}\]
  • DEPI: \[\mathrm{DEPI}=\frac{\mathrm{dep}_{t-1}/(\mathrm{dep}_{t-1}+\mathrm{PPE}_{t-1})}{\mathrm{dep}_t/(\mathrm{dep}_t+\mathrm{PPE}_t)}\]
  • SGAI: \[\mathrm{SGAI}=\frac{\mathrm{sga}_t/\mathrm{rev}_t}{\mathrm{sga}_{t-1}/\mathrm{rev}_{t-1}}\]
  • LVGI: \[\mathrm{LVGI}=\frac{(\mathrm{CL}_t+\mathrm{LTD}_t)/\mathrm{TA}_t}{(\mathrm{CL}_{t-1}+\mathrm{LTD}_{t-1})/\mathrm{TA}_{t-1}}\]
  • TATA: \[\mathrm{TATA}=\frac{\mathrm{NI}_t-\mathrm{CFO}_t}{\mathrm{TA}_t}\]

The "reading when large" column is the usual interpretation of each index. It is the lesson's reading, not a quotation from the paper, which has not been checked against the paper. It is therefore graded D, lore, and no claim in the lesson rests on it.

Four features of the definitions matter for use. Seven indices are ratios of a year-\(t\) quantity to the same quantity at \(t-1\). TATA is a single-year accrual level scaled by year-end total assets. A zero denominator, or a missing item, raises an error in the contract, and nothing is imputed. Every index is scale-free, so a common factor on money cancels, as with the F-score. Finally, the contract's M-score is linear in the indices. The intercept and the eight weights are the numbers that depend on the source, and they cannot yet be confirmed.

Definition 4.1 (screen, sensitivity, specificity, prevalence)

A screen flags a firm-year as positive or negative. Its false-positive rate \(\mathrm{FPR}\) is the probability of a flag among firm-years that are not manipulating. Its specificity is \(1-\mathrm{FPR}\). Its sensitivity \(s\) is the probability of a flag among manipulating firm-years. The prevalence \(p\) is the share of firm-years that manipulate in the population the screen is applied to. Its positive predictive value (PPV) is the probability that a flagged firm-year is a manipulator. For two screens, the AND rule flags a firm-year when both screens flag it, and the OR rule when either does.

Lab 1 · The eight indices and M on SIMULATED firm-years, with base rates

Stated before running: every index is unchanged when money is multiplied by 1,000; the M-values of planted manipulators, whose year-\(t\) receivables, depreciation and accruals carry the footprints the generator plants, have a higher median M than clean firms, because the generator plants the footprints; and the lab's check reproduces the PPV of 0.0917 at prevalence 0.01, specificity 0.95 and sensitivity 0.5 (PPV is defined in Definition 4.1 above; its formula is Proposition 4.2 below). from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 20260528
N_CLEAN = 300
N_PLANTED = 300

# UNCONFIRMED candidate constants (see the module docstring). Do not cite as Beneish's values.
UNCONFIRMED_INTERCEPT = -4.84
UNCONFIRMED_COEFFICIENTS: dict[str, float] = {
    "DSRI": 0.920,
    "GMI": 0.528,
    "AQI": 0.404,
    "SGI": 0.892,
    "DEPI": 0.115,
    "SGAI": -0.172,
    "LVGI": -0.327,
    "TATA": 4.679,
}


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FirmYear:
    """One fiscal year of a SIMULATED firm. Money in SCU."""

    revenue: float
    cogs: float
    receivables: float
    current_assets: float
    ppe: float
    total_assets: float
    depreciation: float
    sga: float
    current_liabilities: float
    long_term_debt: float
    net_income: float
    cfo: float


def gross_margin(f: FirmYear) -> float:
    """Gross margin (revenue - cogs) / revenue, dimensionless; revenue must be positive."""
    if f.revenue <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError("revenue must be positive")
    return (f.revenue - f.cogs) / f.revenue


def _need_positive(value: float, name: str) -> None:
    if not value > 0.0:
        raise ValueError(f"{name} must be positive")


def beneish_indices(t: FirmYear, p: FirmYear) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Return the eight Beneish indices for year t (= t) against year t-1 (= p), per the contract.

    Raises ValueError on any zero or negative denominator the formulas need.
    """
    for name, v in (
        ("revenue_t", t.revenue),
        ("revenue_t-1", p.revenue),
        ("total_assets_t", t.total_assets),
        ("total_assets_t-1", p.total_assets),
        ("gross_margin_t", gross_margin(t)),
        ("gross_margin_t-1", gross_margin(p)),
        ("sga_t", t.sga),
        ("sga_t-1", p.sga),
        ("dep+ppe_t", t.depreciation + t.ppe),
        ("dep+ppe_t-1", p.depreciation + p.ppe),
        ("depreciation_t", t.depreciation),
        ("depreciation_t-1", p.depreciation),
    ):
        _need_positive(v, name)
    dsri = (t.receivables / t.revenue) / (p.receivables / p.revenue)
    gmi = gross_margin(p) / gross_margin(t)
    aqi_t = 1.0 - (t.current_assets + t.ppe) / t.total_assets
    aqi_p = 1.0 - (p.current_assets + p.ppe) / p.total_assets
    _need_positive(aqi_p, "asset-quality denominator t-1")
    aqi = aqi_t / aqi_p
    sgi = t.revenue / p.revenue
    depi = (p.depreciation / (p.depreciation + p.ppe)) / (t.depreciation / (t.depreciation + t.ppe))
    sgai = (t.sga / t.revenue) / (p.sga / p.revenue)
    lvgi = ((t.current_liabilities + t.long_term_debt) / t.total_assets) / (
        (p.current_liabilities + p.long_term_debt) / p.total_assets
    )
    tata = (t.net_income - t.cfo) / t.total_assets
    return {"DSRI": dsri, "GMI": gmi, "AQI": aqi, "SGI": sgi, "DEPI": depi, "SGAI": sgai, "LVGI": lvgi, "TATA": tata}


def m_score(indices: dict[str, float]) -> float:
    """M = intercept + sum of weight * index, with the UNCONFIRMED candidate constants."""
    return UNCONFIRMED_INTERCEPT + sum(UNCONFIRMED_COEFFICIENTS[k] * indices[k] for k in UNCONFIRMED_COEFFICIENTS)


def _clean_pair(rng: random.Random) -> tuple[FirmYear, FirmYear]:
    """A SIMULATED firm with ordinary year-on-year movement."""
    assets = rng.uniform(900.0, 1100.0)
    revenue = assets * rng.uniform(0.9, 1.3)
    gm = rng.uniform(0.3, 0.5)
    years: list[FirmYear] = []
    for _ in range(2):
        assets *= rng.uniform(0.97, 1.05)
        revenue *= rng.uniform(0.97, 1.06)
        ppe = assets * rng.uniform(0.25, 0.35)
        dep = ppe * rng.uniform(0.09, 0.12)
        sga = revenue * rng.uniform(0.15, 0.22)
        cogs = revenue * (1.0 - gm * rng.uniform(0.97, 1.03))
        receivables = revenue * rng.uniform(0.11, 0.14)
        ca = assets * rng.uniform(0.3, 0.4)
        cl = assets * rng.uniform(0.2, 0.3)
        ltd = assets * rng.uniform(0.1, 0.2)
        ni = revenue * rng.uniform(0.02, 0.06)
        cfo = ni + rng.uniform(-0.03, 0.03) * assets
        years.append(
            FirmYear(
                revenue=revenue,
                cogs=cogs,
                receivables=receivables,
                current_assets=ca,
                ppe=ppe,
                total_assets=assets,
                depreciation=dep,
                sga=sga,
                current_liabilities=cl,
                long_term_debt=ltd,
                net_income=ni,
                cfo=cfo,
            )
        )
    return years[0], years[1]


def _planted_pair(rng: random.Random) -> tuple[FirmYear, FirmYear]:
    """A SIMULATED firm whose year t carries the manipulation footprints the generator plants:
    receivables outrun revenue, margins fall, depreciation is slowed, and accruals are high."""
    p, t = _clean_pair(rng)
    t_rec = t.receivables * rng.uniform(1.35, 1.6)
    t_gm_cogs = t.revenue * (1.0 - (gross_margin(p) * rng.uniform(0.7, 0.85)))
    t_dep = p.depreciation * rng.uniform(0.7, 0.85)
    t_cfo = t.net_income - rng.uniform(0.06, 0.1) * t.total_assets
    planted = FirmYear(
        revenue=t.revenue,
        cogs=t_gm_cogs,
        receivables=t_rec,
        current_assets=t.current_assets,
        ppe=t.ppe,
        total_assets=t.total_assets,
        depreciation=t_dep,
        sga=t.sga,
        current_liabilities=t.current_liabilities,
        long_term_debt=t.long_term_debt,
        net_income=t.net_income,
        cfo=t_cfo,
    )
    return p, planted


def scale(f: FirmYear, k: float) -> FirmYear:
    """Multiply every money item by k > 0."""
    return FirmYear(
        revenue=f.revenue * k,
        cogs=f.cogs * k,
        receivables=f.receivables * k,
        current_assets=f.current_assets * k,
        ppe=f.ppe * k,
        total_assets=f.total_assets * k,
        depreciation=f.depreciation * k,
        sga=f.sga * k,
        current_liabilities=f.current_liabilities * k,
        long_term_debt=f.long_term_debt * k,
        net_income=f.net_income * k,
        cfo=f.cfo * k,
    )


def _median(xs: list[float]) -> float:
    s = sorted(xs)
    n = len(s)
    mid = n // 2
    return s[mid] if n % 2 else (s[mid - 1] + s[mid]) / 2.0


def base_rate_table() -> list[tuple[float, float, float, float, float]]:
    """Rows (prevalence, specificity, FPR, PPV, false flags per 1000 firm-years).

    Illustrative sensitivity 0.5 (an assumption, not an estimate). PPV by Bayes' rule:
    PPV = p*s / (p*s + (1-p)*(1-spec)); FPR = 1 - spec; false flags per 1000 = 1000*(1-p)*FPR.
    """
    sensitivity = 0.5
    rows: list[tuple[float, float, float, float, float]] = []
    for prevalence in (0.001, 0.01, 0.05):
        for spec in (0.90, 0.95, 0.99):
            fpr = 1.0 - spec
            ppv = prevalence * sensitivity / (prevalence * sensitivity + (1.0 - prevalence) * fpr)
            false_flags = 1000.0 * (1.0 - prevalence) * fpr
            rows.append((prevalence, spec, fpr, ppv, false_flags))
    return rows


def combined_false_positive_rates(f1: float, f2: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """False-positive rates of the AND and OR rules for two screens with false-positive rates f1, f2.

    Assumes the two screens' false flags are independent among clean firms, which the lesson
    states as an assumption and does not establish. AND = f1*f2; OR = 1-(1-f1)*(1-f2).
    """
    if not (0.0 <= f1 <= 1.0 and 0.0 <= f2 <= 1.0):
        raise ValueError("false-positive rates must lie in [0, 1]")
    return f1 * f2, 1.0 - (1.0 - f1) * (1.0 - f2)


def main() -> None:
    """Print the worked index vector for one planted and one clean firm, the M-score distributions,
    the scale-invariance check, and the base-rate table."""
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    clean_m: list[float] = []
    planted_m: list[float] = []
    clean_pairs = [_clean_pair(rng) for _ in range(N_CLEAN)]
    planted_pairs = [_planted_pair(rng) for _ in range(N_PLANTED)]

    p0, t0 = planted_pairs[0]
    idx0 = beneish_indices(t0, p0)
    print("SIMULATED planted firm, year t against t-1 (index, value):")
    for name in ("DSRI", "GMI", "AQI", "SGI", "DEPI", "SGAI", "LVGI", "TATA"):
        print(f"  {name:4s} {idx0[name]:.4f}")
    print(f"  M (UNCONFIRMED constants) = {m_score(idx0):.4f}")

    for p, t in clean_pairs:
        clean_m.append(m_score(beneish_indices(t, p)))
    for p, t in planted_pairs:
        planted_m.append(m_score(beneish_indices(t, p)))
    print("M-score medians (UNCONFIRMED constants, SIMULATED firms):")
    print(f"  clean firm-years   n={N_CLEAN}: median M = {_median(clean_m):.4f}")
    print(f"  planted firm-years n={N_PLANTED}: median M = {_median(planted_m):.4f}")

    # Each index is a ratio of money items, so every index and M are unchanged when money scales.
    unchanged = 0
    all_pairs = clean_pairs + planted_pairs
    for p, t in all_pairs:
        a = beneish_indices(t, p)
        b = beneish_indices(scale(t, 1000.0), scale(p, 1000.0))

    print(f"indices unchanged under money x1000 (all clean and planted firm-years): {unchanged}/{len(all_pairs)}")

    s4_ppv = next(row[3] for row in base_rate_table() if row[0] == 0.01 and row[1] == 0.95)

    print("  prevalence  specificity   FPR    PPV     false flags per 1000")
    for prevalence, spec, fpr, ppv, flags in base_rate_table():
        print(f"  {prevalence:9.3f}  {spec:11.2f}  {fpr:5.3f}  {ppv:6.4f}  {flags:8.2f}")
    and_fpr, or_fpr = combined_false_positive_rates(0.10, 0.05)
    print("combined rules, illustrative false-positive rates 0.10 (screen 1) and 0.05 (screen 2):")
    print(f"  AND rule FPR = {and_fpr:.4f}")
    print(f"  OR rule  FPR = {or_fpr:.4f}")

3A worked firm

The pair below is a hand-built SIMULATED firm (SCU), not a filer. Year \(t-1\) has revenue 1,000, receivables 100, cost of revenue 600, current assets 400, PPE 300, total assets 1,000, depreciation 30, SG&A 150, current liabilities 200, long-term debt 200, net income 40 and CFO 45. Year \(t\) has revenue 1,200, receivables 180, cost of revenue 780, current assets 480, PPE 330, total assets 1,200, depreciation 30, SG&A 180, current liabilities 250, long-term debt 220, net income 60 and CFO 20.

  • DSRI: \((180/1200)/(100/1000) = 0.150/0.100 = 1.5000\).
  • GMI: gross margin is \(0.40\) at \(t-1\) and \(0.35\) at \(t\), so \(\mathrm{GMI} = 0.40/0.35 = 1.1429\).
  • AQI: \(1 - (480+330)/1200 = 0.3250\) at \(t\) and \(1 - (400+300)/1000 = 0.3000\) at \(t-1\), so \(\mathrm{AQI} = 0.3250/0.3000 = 1.0833\).
  • SGI: \(1200/1000 = 1.2000\).
  • DEPI: \(30/330 = 0.0909\) at \(t-1\) and \(30/360 = 0.0833\) at \(t\), so \(\mathrm{DEPI} = 0.0909/0.0833 = 1.0909\).
  • SGAI: \((180/1200)/(150/1000) = 0.15/0.15 = 1.0000\).
  • LVGI: \((250+220)/1200 = 0.3917\) at \(t\) and \((200+200)/1000 = 0.4000\) at \(t-1\), so \(\mathrm{LVGI} = 0.3917/0.4000 = 0.9792\).
  • TATA: \((60-20)/1200 = 0.0333\).

With the unconfirmed candidate constants, the value is

\[\begin{gathered}M=-4.84+0.920(1.5)\\ +0.528(1.1429)+0.404(1.0833)\\ +0.892(1.2)+0.115(1.0909)\\ -0.172(1.0)-0.327(0.9792)\\ +4.679(0.0333)=-1.5593\end{gathered}\]

The indices in the display are rounded to four decimals. M is computed from the unrounded values, so the displayed terms sum to −1.5594 while M is −1.5593. It is arithmetic under unconfirmed constants. It is not a classification, and the lesson does not rely on it for one.

Two things in this example are worth seeing. The largest index is DSRI, at 1.5000, which compares receivables with revenue across the two years. The TATA term is small because accruals are a small share of assets in this example, which shows that the M-score is a sum of many modest terms and not a single test.

4Using the scores together, and what the false-positive rate becomes

The F-score (L07) and the M-score measure different things. The F-score counts favourable signs in profitability, leverage, liquidity and efficiency: \(s_1\) and \(s_2\) are sign tests at zero, \(s_4\) is a same-year comparison, and \(s_3\) and \(s_5\) to \(s_9\) are change tests, conditioned on distress (L07 §3). The M-score is a screen for an earnings-manipulation pattern. A firm can be strong on one and weak on the other, and the module's two scores are therefore sometimes combined. The question is what a combined rule does to false positives.

Proposition 4.2 (positive predictive value)

For a screen with sensitivity \(s\), specificity \(\sigma = 1-\mathrm{FPR}\) and prevalence \(p\), the probability that a flagged firm-year is a manipulator is \(\mathrm{PPV} = \dfrac{ps}{ps + (1-p)(1-\sigma)}\).

Proof

Two facts are used. (i) Bayes' rule: for events \(A, B\) with \(\Pr(B)>0\), \(\Pr(A\mid B) = \Pr(B\mid A)\Pr(A)/\Pr(B)\). This is the definition \(\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A\cap B)/\Pr(B)\) applied twice: \(\Pr(A\cap B) = \Pr(B\mid A)\Pr(A) = \Pr(A\mid B)\Pr(B)\). (ii) The law of total probability: for \(A\) and its complement, \(\Pr(B) = \Pr(B\mid A)\Pr(A) + \Pr(B\mid A^c)\Pr(A^c)\), because \(B\) is the disjoint union of \(B\cap A\) and \(B\cap A^c\). With \(A\) the manipulation event \(D\) and \(B\) the flag \(F\): \(\Pr(D\mid F) = s\,p/\Pr(F)\), where \(\Pr(F\mid D)=s\), \(\Pr(D)=p\), and \(\Pr(F) = sp + \mathrm{FPR}\,(1-p)\) by (ii), with \(\mathrm{FPR}=1-\sigma\). Dividing gives the formula.

Proposition 4.2 shows that the PPV depends on both prevalence and specificity. It is Bayes' rule, the posterior formula of Bayesian inference, applied to a screen. A screen with 95% specificity has a 5% false-positive rate. At sensitivity 0.5 and prevalence 0.01, the PPV is 0.0917: fewer than one flag in ten is a manipulator, although the screen catches half of them and passes 95% of clean firms. The lab's table prints this for an illustrative sensitivity of 0.5, and it is arithmetic, not an estimate for any screen.

Proposition 4.3 (false-positive rates of combined rules)

Let screens 1 and 2 have false-positive rates \(f_1\) and \(f_2\). If their false flags are independent among non-manipulating firm-years, the AND rule (flag when both flag) has \(\mathrm{FPR}_{\wedge} = f_1 f_2\), and the OR rule (flag when either flags) has \(\mathrm{FPR}_{\vee} = 1-(1-f_1)(1-f_2)\).

Proof

Among non-manipulating firm-years, the AND rule flags when both flag, which under independence has probability \(f_1 f_2\). The OR rule fails to flag only when neither flags, with probability \((1-f_1)(1-f_2)\), so it flags with probability \(1-(1-f_1)(1-f_2)\).

The AND rule lowers false positives and the OR rule raises them, as the arithmetic of Proposition 4.3 shows. The independence in Proposition 4.3 is an assumption, and this lesson does not establish it. The two scores share inputs. The F-score's accrual test \(s_4\) and the M-score's TATA index both compare net income with cash flow, and the F-score's ROA and CFO are scaled by beginning-of-year total assets, whereas TATA, AQI and LVGI are scaled by year-end total assets. Positively correlated false flags make the AND rule's false-positive rate higher than \(f_1 f_2\), and the OR rule's lower. Negative correlation reverses both. Take \(f_1 = 0.10\) and \(f_2 = 0.05\), and a joint law in which the two screens never flag the same non-manipulator: the AND rate is 0, below 0.0050, and the OR rate is 0.1500, above 0.1450. The sign of the error therefore depends on the sign of the dependence, which the lesson cannot establish, and its size is not known.

With illustrative false-positive rates of 0.10 and 0.05, the lab gives an AND rate of 0.0050 and an OR rate of 0.1450 under independence. The AND rule's rate of 0.0050 is below either screen's rate (0.10 and 0.05), and the rule also lowers sensitivity, because a true manipulator must pass both screens. The sensitivity of the AND rule is at most that of either screen, and the lab does not give a sensitivity for either screen.

5Where the canon breaks

  • The constants are unconfirmed. Every M-value in this lesson depends on constants that could not be confirmed (§2). A reader must not use any threshold until the constants and the cut-off are read from the paper or a reproduction.
  • The sample decides the rate. The prevalence in any estimation sample need not equal the prevalence of the population a screen is later applied to. By Proposition 4.2, the PPV for the same sensitivity and specificity falls as prevalence falls. The paper's sample design is not confirmed, so no prevalence from the paper is used.
  • Manipulation is not fraud. The M-score flags an accounting pattern. A flagged firm may have legitimate reasons for the same pattern (growth in receivables after a new contract, for example). The model's false positives are real firms with that pattern, not errors in the arithmetic.
  • Growth is a confound. SGI is the growth factor itself: it equals revenue growth. DSRI and SGAI are ratios of ratios and change only when receivables or SG&A grow faster or slower than revenue. Witness: revenue 1,000 to 1,500, receivables 100 to 150 and SG&A 150 to 225 give DSRI = SGAI = 1.0000 and SGI = 1.5000. Whether the index should be checked within growth groups is the lesson's judgement (grade D), not a finding.
  • Accruals are measured twice. TATA has the numerator of the contract's cash-flow accrual measure, net income less cash flow from operations (atlas/A15.contract.md, accruals_cash_flow), but divides by year-end total assets, not average total assets (atlas/A15.contract.md, TATA definition). An accrual screen and an accrual-based discretionary measure may flag the same firm-years, which breaks the independence in Proposition 4.3.
  • No joint evidence is in the registry. The lesson has the arithmetic of combined rules, but no verified study measures how the F-score and M-score perform together. Studies of the joint use are an open item listed in plans/AC2-open-items.md; none is read here. Until a study is read, the joint use is a design choice, not a result.

6Misconceptions

  1. “An M-score above a threshold proves that a firm manipulated its earnings.”

    A score is a flag, and Proposition 4.2 gives the probability that a flag is correct. At low prevalence even a specific screen produces mostly false flags. The lab's table shows PPVs below 0.1 for prevalence 0.001 and specificity up to 0.95 at sensitivity 0.5. The flag is a reason to read the filing, not a finding.

  2. “The M-score is a probability of manipulation.”

    The contract defines M as a linear combination of ratios with constants. Its value is not a probability. Converting it to one requires a fitted model with a stated distribution, which the contract does not supply. The worked firm's M of −1.5593 under the unconfirmed constants is a number, not a probability.

  3. “Using the F-score and the M-score together always reduces false positives.”

    The AND rule reduces false positives under independence (Proposition 4.3), but it also reduces sensitivity, since a manipulator must pass both screens. The OR rule raises false positives. If the false flags are positively correlated, the AND rule's rate is higher than the product of the single rates, and its OR rate is lower. A combined rule is a trade-off, and the lesson's arithmetic gives its size only under the stated assumptions.

7Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The DSRI and SGI of the worked firm

For the worked firm in §3, compute DSRI and SGI, and say which one reflects the receivables footprint.

Solution

\(\mathrm{DSRI} = (180/1200)/(100/1000) = 0.15/0.10 = 1.50\). \(\mathrm{SGI} = 1200/1000 = 1.20\). DSRI is the receivables footprint: it compares receivables with sales in both years, while SGI compares sales alone.

M2 · The GMI

Compute the GMI of the worked firm from the gross margins, and say what a GMI above 1 means.

Solution

Gross margin at \(t-1\) is \((1000-600)/1000 = 0.40\) and at \(t\) is \((1200-780)/1200 = 0.35\). So \(\mathrm{GMI} = 0.40/0.35 = 1.1429\). A GMI above 1 means the margin fell from \(t-1\) to \(t\), which is the sign the contract's index is built to pick up.

M3 · Scale

Multiply every money item of both years by 1,000 and show that DSRI and TATA are unchanged.

Solution

DSRI is \((\mathrm{rec}_t/\mathrm{rev}_t)/(\mathrm{rec}_{t-1}/\mathrm{rev}_{t-1})\). Each receivables and revenue term carries the factor 1000, which cancels in each ratio and then in the quotient. TATA is \((\mathrm{NI}_t - \mathrm{CFO}_t)/\mathrm{TA}_t\), where numerator and denominator both carry the factor. Both are unchanged, at 1.5000 and 0.0333.

Proof

P1 · The PPV formula

Prove Proposition 4.2 from Bayes' rule, stating each step.

Solution

Let \(D\) be the event that a firm-year manipulates and \(F\) the event that it is flagged. Then \(\Pr(D\mid F) = \Pr(F\mid D)\Pr(D)/\Pr(F)\). By definition \(\Pr(F\mid D) = s\) and \(\Pr(D) = p\). By the law of total probability, \(\Pr(F) = \Pr(F\mid D)\Pr(D) + \Pr(F\mid D^c)\Pr(D^c) = sp + \mathrm{FPR}(1-p)\), and \(\mathrm{FPR} = 1-\sigma\). Substituting gives \(\mathrm{PPV} = ps/(ps + (1-p)(1-\sigma))\).

P2 · The AND and OR rates

Prove Proposition 4.3 under independence of the two screens' false flags among non-manipulators.

Solution

Condition on a non-manipulating firm-year. The AND rule flags when both screens flag, so \(\Pr(\text{AND flag}\mid D^c) = \Pr(F_1\mid D^c)\Pr(F_2\mid D^c) = f_1 f_2\), using independence. The OR rule does not flag only when both screens fail to flag, so \(\Pr(\text{OR no flag}\mid D^c) = (1-f_1)(1-f_2)\), and \(\Pr(\text{OR flag}\mid D^c) = 1-(1-f_1)(1-f_2)\).

P3 · Sensitivity of the AND rule

Show that the sensitivity of the AND rule is at most the smaller of the two single sensitivities, and that the OR rule's is at least the larger.

Solution

Let \(s_1, s_2\) be the single sensitivities. The AND rule flags a manipulator only when both screens flag it, so \(\Pr(\text{AND flag}\mid D) = \Pr(F_1\cap F_2\mid D) \le \min(\Pr(F_1\mid D), \Pr(F_2\mid D)) = \min(s_1, s_2)\). The OR rule flags a manipulator when either screen flags it, so \(\Pr(\text{OR flag}\mid D) = \Pr(F_1\cup F_2\mid D) \ge \max(s_1, s_2)\). Both bounds hold without any independence assumption.

Empirical

E1 · The planted gap in Lab 1

From Lab 1's output, compute the difference between the planted and clean medians of M. State what the difference shows and what it does not show.

Solution

The output gives a clean median M of −2.4747 and a planted median of −1.5067. The difference is \(-1.5067 - (-2.4747) = 0.9680\). The gap is a property of the generator: the generator plants the footprints. It does not show that a real manipulator scores higher, and the constants are unconfirmed, so the gap is in arithmetic units under candidate weights.

E2 · Prevalence and the flag

From Lab 1's base-rate table, for a screen with specificity 0.95 and sensitivity 0.5, compare the PPV at prevalence 0.001 with the PPV at prevalence 0.05. Give the ratio and say what it shows.

Solution

The table gives PPV 0.0099 at prevalence 0.001 and 0.3448 at prevalence 0.05. The ratio is \(0.3448/0.0099 = 34.8\) (to two significant figures, about 35). Both PPVs use the same sensitivity and specificity, so the ratio comes only from prevalence. A flag is about 35 times as likely to be a manipulator in the higher-prevalence population. That is the base-rate effect of Proposition 4.2.

E3 · Flags per thousand

From the table, at prevalence 0.01 and specificity 0.95, how many false flags does the screen produce per 1,000 firm-years of the population? Check the table's value by the formula \(1000(1-p)\,\mathrm{FPR}\).

Solution

The table gives 49.50 at prevalence 0.01 and specificity 0.95. Check: \(1000 \times (1-0.01) \times 0.05 = 1000 \times 0.99 \times 0.05 = 49.5\). The table is consistent with the formula. Per 1,000 clean firm-years the count is \(1000 \times \mathrm{FPR} = 50\), whatever the prevalence. The population count of 49.50 is smaller only because clean firms are \(1-p = 0.99\) of the population.

Research

R1 · Confirm the constants

Specify exactly what must be read from Beneish (1999) to confirm the intercept and weights, and the design of the check that would count as confirmation.

Solution

Read the paper's own statement of the eight-variable model and its constants: the intercept, the eight coefficients, and the table and page of each. Confirmation requires each value to match the table exactly, with the locator recorded in beneish_m_score. Where the paper's table is not available, a reproduction in a source the registry can verify must carry the same numbers. The intercept and all eight weights come from the same single secondary page, so the whole set needs checking against the paper, not only the TATA weight. Until this is done, §2 stands.

R2 · A joint validation design

Design a study that measures the false-positive rate and sensitivity of the AND and OR combinations of the F-score and the M-score on a labelled sample. State the labels, the controls for growth and industry, and the test of the independence assumption in Proposition 4.3.

Solution

Labels: firm-years with a known restatement or enforcement outcome, each paired with a control firm-year matched on size, industry and year, so the prevalence is stated and known. Growth controls: compare the indices within growth tercile, since SGI is the growth term and DSRI and SGAI respond to receivables or SG&A growing relative to revenue (§5). Independence test: estimate the correlation of the two screens' flags among the control firm-years, and compare the observed AND false-positive rate with \(f_1 f_2\). A higher observed rate is consistent with the positive correlation the lesson warns about. Report the PPV at each prevalence the user is likely to face, not only at the sample's prevalence.

R3 · A pre-registered threshold

State how a threshold for M should be chosen and tested without repeating the sample on which the constants were estimated.

Solution

Split the labelled sample before any threshold is chosen. Choose the threshold on the estimation half to attain a stated false-positive rate, which fixes specificity. Report sensitivity and the PPV at the prevalences of §4 on the holdout half. A threshold chosen on the same firm-years used to report its error rates is in-sample and overstates performance. The threshold is a choice the user makes and reports, not a value this lesson supplies.
