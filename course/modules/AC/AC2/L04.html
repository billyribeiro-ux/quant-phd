Earnings and Cash-Flow Quality: Operating Accruals

Net income is an accrual number. It adds estimates of revenue earned but not yet collected and of expenses incurred but not yet paid to the cash the firm generated in the same year, and the non-cash part is where estimates, errors and deliberate choices enter. This lesson defines the two accrual measures of the A15 engine and proves how they differ, under stated assumptions. Lab 1 checks the identity on a SIMULATED panel. The accrual measures feed the accruals anomaly (L05), discretionary accruals (L06), the F-score cash-flow signal (L07) and the M-score TATA index (L08).

1Earnings, cash and the accrual gap

Let \(NI_t\) be net income and \(CFO_t\) cash flow from operations for year \(t\), both in currency units (CU). The accrual gap is the part of earnings that the operating cash flow did not deliver in the year.

Definition 1.1 (operating accruals)

Operating accruals in year \(t\) are \(NI_t - CFO_t\), in CU. A positive value means earnings exceed operating cash flow. Operating accruals are the non-cash components of earnings. They include revenue recognised before collection, expenses recognised before payment, and the non-cash charge for depreciation and amortization, which lowers earnings and leaves cash untouched.

Definition 1.2 (earnings and cash-flow quality)

Earnings and cash-flow quality is the usefulness of a reported earnings figure as a guide to future cash flows and to the firm's economic performance. Quality is a property of a number relative to a use. No single ratio measures it for every use, and this lesson measures one component: how much of earnings is non-cash.

The A15 contract scales the gap by the average of the two year-end total-asset balances, \(\overline{TA}_t = (TA_t + TA_{t-1})/2\), which puts firms of different size on one scale. Two measures follow.

Definition 1.3 (the two contract measures)

Let \(\Delta x_t = x_t - x_{t-1}\) for a balance-sheet item. The cash-flow measure and the balance-sheet measure are \[ AC^{CF}_t = \frac{NI_t - CFO_t}{\overline{TA}_t}, \qquad AC^{BS}_t = \frac{(\Delta CA_t - \Delta Cash_t) - \Delta CL_t - D\&A_t}{\overline{TA}_t}. \] Here \(CA\) is current assets, \(Cash\) is cash and cash equivalents, \(CL\) is current liabilities as published, and \(D\&A\) is depreciation and amortization of the year. Both are unitless, because numerator and denominator are in CU.

The two measures use the same denominator, so any difference between them is a difference of numerators. The next section identifies that difference exactly.

Remark 1.4 (what the contract's balance-sheet measure leaves out)

\(AC^{BS}\) uses current liabilities as published. It contains no term for non-current operating assets or liabilities, and it contains no separate term for short-term debt or taxes payable. Section 2 shows what the first omission costs. Section 5 shows what the second costs when debt sits inside current liabilities. The lesson states the contract's definition only.

2The articulation identity

The two measures are tied together by the three statements. The identity below links two balance sheets, the income statement between them and the cash-flow statement, under explicit assumptions A1 to A4.

Definition 2.1 (the operating partition)

Write total assets as \(TA = Cash + CAo + PPE + ONCA\), where \(CAo\) is current assets other than cash, \(PPE\) is net property, plant and equipment and \(ONCA\) is other non-current operating assets. Write liabilities as \(L = CL + LTD + ONCL\), where \(LTD\) is long-term debt and \(ONCL\) is other non-current operating liabilities. Equity is \(E = TA - L\). Net distributions to shareholders in year \(t\) are \(D_t\), dividends less issuance, and \(capex_t\) is capital expenditure.

Assumptions (A1 to A4)

A1, clean surplus: \(E_t = E_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t\). A2, PP&E roll-forward: \(PPE_t = PPE_{t-1} + capex_t - D\&A_t\), with no disposals, impairments or acquisitions. A3, complete classification: \(CL\), \(LTD\) and \(ONCL\) are the only liabilities, and current liabilities carry no debt. A4, complete cash-flow statement: \(\Delta Cash_t = CFO_t + CFI_t + CFF_t\), with \(CFI_t = -capex_t\) and \(CFF_t = \Delta LTD_t - D_t\).

Proposition 2.2 (articulation of the accrual gap)

Under A1 to A4, \(NI_t - CFO_t = (\Delta CA_t - \Delta Cash_t) - \Delta CL_t - D\&A_t + (\Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)\), in CU.

Proof

By A4, \(CFO_t = \Delta Cash_t - CFI_t - CFF_t = \Delta Cash_t + capex_t - \Delta LTD_t + D_t\). Subtract this from \(NI_t\): \[ NI_t - CFO_t = (NI_t - D_t) - \Delta Cash_t - capex_t + \Delta LTD_t. \] By A1, \(NI_t - D_t = \Delta E_t\). Expand \(E = TA - L\) and use the partition of Definition 2.1: \[ \Delta E_t = \Delta Cash_t + \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta LTD_t - \Delta ONCL_t. \] Substituting, the \(\Delta Cash_t\) and \(\Delta LTD_t\) terms cancel: \[ NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t - capex_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta ONCL_t. \] By A2, \(\Delta PPE_t - capex_t = -D\&A_t\). Finally \(\Delta CA_t - \Delta Cash_t = \Delta CAo_t\), which gives the statement. The distributions \(D_t\) cancel in the first step, and so do the financing flows, because each enters equity and cash in the same amount.

Proposition 2.2 holds for every pair of firm-years that satisfy A1 to A4. It is an accounting identity and a test of the algebra, not an empirical finding. Its content is the gap it names.

Corollary 2.3 (the two measures differ by non-current items)

Under A1 to A4, \(AC^{CF}_t - AC^{BS}_t = (\Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)/\overline{TA}_t\).

Proof

Divide Proposition 2.2 by \(\overline{TA}_t\). The left side becomes \(AC^{CF}_t\). The first three terms on the right become \(AC^{BS}_t\), by Definition 1.3. The remaining term is \((\Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)/\overline{TA}_t\).

Remark 2.4 (the identity holds under A1 to A4)

Two separate changes are involved. Relaxing A3 to A3′, so that current liabilities contain short-term debt, changes the definition of CL: with A4 as written, Proposition 2.2 still holds exactly. The extra term appears only when A4′ also books \(\Delta STD\) in financing, so that CFO excludes it, and the balance-sheet measure, which subtracts all of \(\Delta CL\), does not. Section 5 derives the extra term. If a cash item is classified as operating in one framework and as financing or investing in another, \(CFO\) changes, \(\Delta Cash\) and \(NI\) do not, and \(AC^{CF}\) moves while \(AC^{BS}\) does not. A reclassification of a non-zero amount between \(CFO\) and the other cash-flow sections changes the cash-flow measure and leaves the balance-sheet measure unchanged. A reclassification of zero leaves both measures as they were.

Lab 1 · The two accrual measures on a SIMULATED panel

The lab generates 400 SIMULATED firm-years in two scenarios. In the clean scenario, non-current operating items and long-term debt are unchanged. In the moving scenario, they change. Stated before running: the indirect method reproduces the cash-flow identity to rounding in both scenarios; the measures agree to rounding in the clean scenario; in the moving scenario, the gap equals \((\Delta ONCA - \Delta ONCL)/\overline{TA}\) to rounding, and the measures diverge for a substantial share of firms. Generator inputs are in CU; the non-current items move by standard deviations of 20% and 25% of their lagged value. These are the author's choices, not estimates from filings.

"""AC2 L04 lab 1: the two operating-accrual measures on a SIMULATED two-year panel of firms.

SIMULATED: every firm is generated here from a seeded generator. No filing is read.
Units: currency units (CU) for every money item, and ratios are unitless.

The generator draws cash and the operating items for two year-ends, takes equity as the balance-sheet
residual (assets less liabilities), and derives net income from the change in equity plus distributions
(clean surplus). The cash-flow statement is then built from the balance-sheet changes, so the statements
articulate by construction. Scenario "clean" keeps non-current operating items and long-term debt unchanged;
scenario "moving" lets them move, which is where the two measures part company.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass

import numpy as np
from numpy.typing import NDArray

F64 = NDArray[np.float64]


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Panel:
    """Two year-ends (0 = lagged, 1 = current) for N firms, all money in CU."""

    cash0: F64
    cash1: F64
    ca_other0: F64  # receivables and inventories: current assets excluding cash
    ca_other1: F64
    cl0: F64
    cl1: F64
    ppe0: F64
    ppe1: F64
    onca0: F64  # other non-current operating assets
    onca1: F64
    oncl0: F64  # other non-current operating liabilities
    oncl1: F64
    ltd0: F64  # long-term debt
    ltd1: F64
    ni: F64  # net income of the current year
    dep: F64  # depreciation of the current year
    capex: F64  # capital expenditure of the current year
    payout: F64  # net distributions to shareholders (dividends less issuance)


def total_assets(cash: F64, ca_other: F64, ppe: F64, onca: F64) -> F64:
    """Total assets in CU: cash + other current assets + PP&E + other non-current operating assets."""
    return cash + ca_other + ppe + onca


def build_panel(rng: np.random.Generator, n: int, moving: bool) -> Panel:
    """Generate n SIMULATED firm-years with articulated statements; moving=True lets non-current items move."""
    size = np.exp(rng.normal(np.log(1000.0), 0.6, n))  # scale of the firm in CU
    ca_other0 = size * rng.uniform(0.15, 0.30, n)
    ppe0 = size * rng.uniform(0.25, 0.50, n)
    onca0 = size * rng.uniform(0.05, 0.15, n)
    cl0 = size * rng.uniform(0.10, 0.20, n)
    ltd0 = size * rng.uniform(0.02, 0.12, n)
    oncl0 = size * rng.uniform(0.01, 0.04, n)
    cash0 = size * rng.uniform(0.05, 0.15, n)

    dep = ppe0 * rng.uniform(0.06, 0.12, n)
    capex = ppe0 * rng.uniform(0.05, 0.15, n)
    ppe1 = ppe0 + capex - dep  # PP&E roll-forward
    cash1 = cash0 * np.exp(rng.normal(0.03, 0.15, n))  # positive by construction
    ca_other1 = ca_other0 * (1.0 + rng.normal(0.08, 0.10, n))
    cl1 = cl0 * (1.0 + rng.normal(0.05, 0.10, n))
    if moving:
        onca1 = onca0 * (1.0 + rng.normal(0.0, 0.20, n))
        oncl1 = oncl0 * (1.0 + rng.normal(0.0, 0.25, n))
        ltd1 = ltd0 * (1.0 + rng.normal(0.02, 0.10, n))
    else:
        onca1, oncl1, ltd1 = onca0.copy(), oncl0.copy(), ltd0.copy()
    payout = size * rng.normal(0.01, 0.02, n)  # net distributions to shareholders, CU

    # Balance sheet at year-end 1 fixes equity; clean surplus then gives net income: NI = dE + payout.
    equity0 = total_assets(cash0, ca_other0, ppe0, onca0) - (cl0 + ltd0 + oncl0)
    equity1 = total_assets(cash1, ca_other1, ppe1, onca1) - (cl1 + ltd1 + oncl1)
    if np.any(equity1 <= 0.0):
        raise ValueError("generator produced non-positive equity; check the balance-sheet draws")
    ni = equity1 - equity0 + payout
    return Panel(
        cash0=cash0,
        cash1=cash1,
        ca_other0=ca_other0,
        ca_other1=ca_other1,
        cl0=cl0,
        cl1=cl1,
        ppe0=ppe0,
        ppe1=ppe1,
        onca0=onca0,
        onca1=onca1,
        oncl0=oncl0,
        oncl1=oncl1,
        ltd0=ltd0,
        ltd1=ltd1,
        ni=ni,
        dep=dep,
        capex=capex,
        payout=payout,
    )


def average_total_assets(p: Panel) -> F64:
    """Mean of the two year-end total-asset balances in CU, the scale of both accrual measures."""
    ta0 = total_assets(p.cash0, p.ca_other0, p.ppe0, p.onca0)
    ta1 = total_assets(p.cash1, p.ca_other1, p.ppe1, p.onca1)
    return (ta0 + ta1) / 2.0


def cfo_from_balance_sheet(p: Panel) -> F64:
    """Operating cash flow in CU by the cash-flow identity: CFO = d(cash) + capex - d(LTD) + payout."""
    return (p.cash1 - p.cash0) + p.capex - (p.ltd1 - p.ltd0) + p.payout


def cfo_indirect(p: Panel) -> F64:
    """Operating cash flow in CU by the indirect method: NI + D&A - d(other CA) + d(CL) - d(ONCA) + d(ONCL)."""
    return p.ni + p.dep - (p.ca_other1 - p.ca_other0) + (p.cl1 - p.cl0) - (p.onca1 - p.onca0) + (p.oncl1 - p.oncl0)


def accruals_cash_flow(p: Panel) -> F64:
    """(NI - CFO) / average total assets, the cash-flow measure of the A15 contract."""
    return (p.ni - cfo_from_balance_sheet(p)) / average_total_assets(p)


def accruals_balance_sheet(p: Panel) -> F64:
    """Sloan-style balance-sheet accruals over average total assets, the A15 contract's definition."""
    # current assets less cash: d(CA) - d(cash) = d(other current assets), since CA = cash + other current assets
    d_ca_less_cash = p.ca_other1 - p.ca_other0
    d_cl = p.cl1 - p.cl0
    return (d_ca_less_cash - d_cl - p.dep) / average_total_assets(p)


def describe(name: str, x: F64) -> str:
    """One printed line: mean and standard deviation of a ratio, to four decimals."""
    return f"{name:<28} mean {x.mean():+.4f}  sd {x.std(ddof=1):.4f}"


def main() -> None:
    n = 400
    clean = build_panel(np.random.default_rng(2604), n, moving=False)
    moving = build_panel(np.random.default_rng(2604), n, moving=True)

    # 1. Articulation: the indirect method reproduces the cash-flow identity to floating-point precision.
    art = float(np.max(np.abs(cfo_indirect(clean) - cfo_from_balance_sheet(clean))))
    print(f"articulation_max_abs_residual_clean {art:.6f}")
    art_m = float(np.max(np.abs(cfo_indirect(moving) - cfo_from_balance_sheet(moving))))
    print(f"articulation_max_abs_residual_moving {art_m:.6f}")

    # 2. Clean scenario: the two measures coincide, because the gap is d(ONCA) - d(ONCL) by identity.
    cf_c = accruals_cash_flow(clean)
    bs_c = accruals_balance_sheet(clean)
    avg_c = average_total_assets(clean)
    # Identity: (NI - CFO) - [d(CA) - d(cash) - d(CL) - D&A] = d(ONCA) - d(ONCL), in CU.
    identity_c = (cf_c - bs_c) * avg_c - ((clean.onca1 - clean.onca0) - (clean.oncl1 - clean.oncl0))
    print(f"clean_identity_max_abs_residual {float(np.max(np.abs(identity_c))):.6f}")
    print(f"clean_max_abs_difference {float(np.max(np.abs(cf_c - bs_c))):.6f}")

    # 3. Moving scenario: the gap is exactly the non-current operating items, and the measures diverge.
    cf_m = accruals_cash_flow(moving)
    bs_m = accruals_balance_sheet(moving)
    avg = average_total_assets(moving)
    identity_m = (cf_m - bs_m) * avg - ((moving.onca1 - moving.onca0) - (moving.oncl1 - moving.oncl0))
    print(f"moving_identity_max_abs_residual {float(np.max(np.abs(identity_m))):.6f}")
    print(describe("cash_flow_accruals_moving", cf_m))
    print(describe("balance_sheet_accruals_moving", bs_m))
    print(f"correlation_moving {float(np.corrcoef(cf_m, bs_m)[0, 1]):.4f}")
    print(f"share_of_firms_gap_above_0.01 {float(np.mean(np.abs(cf_m - bs_m) > 0.01)):.4f}")
    # The share also depends on the seed: the same moving scenario, drawn with three seeds.
    for seed_b in (2604, 2605, 2606):
        panel_b = build_panel(np.random.default_rng(seed_b), n, moving=True)
        gap_b = accruals_cash_flow(panel_b) - accruals_balance_sheet(panel_b)
        print(f"share_of_firms_gap_above_0.01_seed_{seed_b} {float(np.mean(np.abs(gap_b) > 0.01)):.4f}")

3Free cash flow and the reversal of accruals

Accruals are the part of earnings that the firm has not yet turned into cash. Write net operating assets as \(NOA_t = CAo_t + PPE_t + ONCA_t - (CL_t - STD_t) - ONCL_t\), where \(STD_t\) is short-term debt, so that operating liabilities exclude debt. Under A1 to A4, a second identity links accruals to investment.

Proposition 3.1 (free cash flow equals earnings less growth in net operating assets)

Under A1 to A4, \(NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta NOA_t - capex_t\). Equivalently, \(CFO_t - capex_t = NI_t - \Delta NOA_t\).

Proof

Proposition 2.2 gives \(NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta ONCL_t - D\&A_t\). By A3, \(STD_t = 0\), so \(\Delta NOA_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta ONCL_t\). Subtracting, \(NI_t - CFO_t - \Delta NOA_t = -D\&A_t - \Delta PPE_t\). By A2, \(\Delta PPE_t = capex_t - D\&A_t\), so the right side is \(-capex_t\).

Proposition 3.1 says that operating cash flow, net of investment, equals earnings net of the growth in the capital the operations use. It is the algebraic link between accruals and net operating assets. Whether the growth an accrual records persists is an empirical question, and Lesson L05 sets out the test that asks it.

Sloan (1996) investigates whether stock prices reflect information about future earnings contained in the accrual and cash-flow components of current earnings. No finding of that paper is stated in this lesson, and no coefficient or return spread is quoted. Lesson L05 sets out the sorted-portfolio test that the question calls for.

4Scale, outliers and the data you cannot yet use

Proposition 4.1 (scale invariance)

Let \(k \gt 0\) and multiply every money item of a firm-year by \(k\). Then \(AC^{CF}_t\) and \(AC^{BS}_t\) are unchanged.

Proof

Every term of the numerator of each measure, and the denominator \(\overline{TA}_t\), is a linear combination of money items. Multiplying all of them by \(k\) multiplies both the numerator and the denominator by \(k\). The ratio is unchanged.

Scale invariance is the Scale convention of the A15 contract (atlas/A15.contract.md, Conventions table): accrual ratios are invariant to a common positive multiplier of money items. It does not make the measures comparable across currencies, because the invariance is for a common multiplier applied to one firm-year. Comparing a firm that reports in thousands with one that reports in millions needs a common unit first, which is the standardization step of Lesson L01.

Scaling by total assets divides a given non-cash item by a smaller total for a small firm, so the same item is a larger ratio. Summaries across firms can use robust statistics (medians, and spreads that are not driven by a few extreme values) and an explicit outlier rule, both taught in S0. The A15 contract does not impute or drop missing items. A firm-year without a required item has no accrual value, and the caller decides how to treat it, which is a missing-data decision and is taught in S0.

5Where the canon breaks

  • Short-term debt inside current liabilities. The contract's balance-sheet measure uses published current liabilities. When those include short-term debt, and the cash-flow statement books its change in financing (A4′), the two measures part by a term the contract does not model. With \(STD\) in current liabilities, \(AC^{CF}_t - AC^{BS}_t = (\Delta STD_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)/\overline{TA}_t\). Derivation under A1 and A2, with two replacements of the stated assumptions: A3′ allows short-term debt \(STD_t\) inside current liabilities, and A4′ replaces A4 by the complete cash-flow statement: \(\Delta Cash_t = CFO_t + CFI_t + CFF_t\), \(CFI_t = -capex_t\), and \(CFF_t = \Delta LTD_t + \Delta STD_t - D_t\), so the financing flow includes the debt change. Then \(CFO_t = \Delta Cash_t + capex_t - \Delta LTD_t - \Delta STD_t + D_t\). By A1, \(NI_t - D_t = \Delta E_t\), and with \(CL_t\) now including \(STD_t\), \(\Delta E_t = \Delta Cash_t + \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta LTD_t - \Delta ONCL_t\). Substituting, \(NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t - capex_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t + \Delta STD_t - \Delta ONCL_t\). By A2, \(\Delta PPE_t - capex_t = -D\&A_t\), so \(NI_t - CFO_t = (\Delta CA_t - \Delta Cash_t) - \Delta CL_t - D\&A_t + (\Delta STD_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)\). Dividing by \(\overline{TA}_t\) gives the claim. The balance-sheet measure subtracts all of \(\Delta CL_t\). The derivation is exact under A1, A2, A3′ and A4′. The A15 contract's accruals_balance_sheet entry states the same consequence: when short-term debt sits in current liabilities, the balance-sheet measure differs from the cash-flow measure by (ΔSTD + ΔONCA − ΔONCL)/average total assets, which is the formula above. The lesson keeps the contract's simplification under that name.
  • Classification choices move the cash-flow measure. Remark 2.4 shows that a reclassification of a non-zero amount between operating and other cash flows changes \(AC^{CF}\) and leaves \(AC^{BS}\) alone. The classification options come from the reporting framework, which AC1 covers under GAAP versus IFRS. The cash-flow measure is therefore partly a property of the reporting framework and not only of the firm's economics.
  • Accruals are not the same as manipulation. A large accrual can come from a growing business whose receivables and inventories rise with sales, from estimates that were honest but wrong, or from deliberate choices. The identity cannot tell these apart. Lesson L06 separates the part of the accrual that a model of normal growth explains from the part it does not, and states what that separation can and cannot show.

6Misconceptions

  1. “Low accruals always mean high-quality earnings.”

    Quality depends on the use (Definition 1.2). A firm with large negative accruals can have received a one-off cash payment that will not recur, so the accrual does not show whether the cash will recur. Negative accruals are a fact about the year's cash, not a guarantee about its persistence. The moving scenario of Lab 1 shows that the two measures are different numbers, so neither one alone is a complete quality test. Whether accruals predict future returns is the question Lesson L05 takes up. No size for that relation is stated in this lesson.

  2. “The two accrual measures are two noisy estimates of one number, so averaging them reduces error.”

    Corollary 2.3 identifies their difference exactly: it is \((\Delta ONCA - \Delta ONCL)/\overline{TA}\) under A1 to A4. Averaging does not remove a known term, and it moves the estimate away from the cash-flow measure in firms where the non-current items move. Lab 1 gives residuals of 0.000000 against the corollary in both scenarios.

  3. “Cash flow from operations is a clean number, so the cash-flow accrual is noise around zero.”

    The cash-flow accrual is \(NI - CFO\), and CFO depends on how the framework classifies each cash item (Remark 2.4). The gap also contains \(-D\&A\), which is a real accounting charge and not noise. Proposition 3.1 shows that the accrual is the growth in net operating assets less capital expenditure, a quantity with economic content. Treating it as noise discards that content.

Mechanics

M1 · The balance-sheet measure by hand

A firm reports: \(CA_0 = 420\), \(Cash_0 = 60\), \(CL_0 = 240\), \(TA_0 = 1500\). In year 1: \(CA_1 = 500\), \(Cash_1 = 80\), \(CL_1 = 260\), \(TA_1 = 1700\), \(D\&A_1 = 30\). All in CU. Compute \(AC^{BS}_1\).

Solution

The average total assets are \((1500 + 1700)/2 = 1600\). The change in current assets less cash is \((500 - 80) - (420 - 60) = 420 - 360 = 60\). The change in current liabilities is \(260 - 240 = 20\). The numerator is \(60 - 20 - 30 = 10\). So \(AC^{BS}_1 = 10/1600 = 0.00625\).

M2 · A negative accrual

A second firm has the same average total assets as M1, \(\overline{TA}_1 = 1600\) CU, with net income of 120 CU and operating cash flow of 150 CU. Compute \(AC^{CF}_1\) and say what its sign means.

Solution

The numerator is \(NI - CFO = 120 - 150 = -30\). The denominator is 1600, so \(AC^{CF}_1 = -30/1600 = -0.01875\). The sign is negative because operating cash flow exceeded earnings. The year's cash was larger than its earnings, which is the reverse of an accrual build-up. Whether that persists is a separate question that the identity does not answer.

M3 · The gap between the two measures

For the firm of M1, suppose \(\Delta ONCA_1 = 30\), \(\Delta ONCL_1 = 10\) (CU) and \(\overline{TA}_1 = 1600\). Using Corollary 2.3, compute \(AC^{CF}_1 - AC^{BS}_1\), then the cash-flow accrual that Proposition 2.2 requires, and the implied \(NI_1 - CFO_1\) in CU.

Solution

Corollary 2.3 gives \(AC^{CF}_1 - AC^{BS}_1 = (30 - 10)/1600 = 0.0125\). The balance-sheet measure is 0.00625 from M1, so \(AC^{CF}_1 = 0.00625 + 0.0125 = 0.01875\). Multiplying by the denominator gives \(NI_1 - CFO_1 = 0.01875 \times 1600 = 30\) CU. The consistency condition is therefore that net income exceeds operating cash flow by 30 CU in this firm.

M4 · Short-term debt inside current liabilities

A firm has the figures of M1 and in addition \(\Delta STD_1 = 15\), \(\Delta ONCA_1 = 30\), \(\Delta ONCL_1 = 10\), with \(\overline{TA}_1 = 1600\) and \(D\&A = 30\). Using the remark in Section 5, compute \(AC^{CF}_1 - AC^{BS}_1\).

Solution

The remark gives the gap under A4′, where the cash-flow statement books the debt change in financing: \((\Delta STD + \Delta ONCA - \Delta ONCL)/\overline{TA}\). The three terms are the debt change, \(15/1600 = 0.009375\); the change in other non-current operating assets, \(30/1600 = 0.01875\); and minus the change in other non-current operating liabilities, \(-10/1600 = -0.00625\). Their sum is \(0.009375 + 0.01875 - 0.00625 = 0.021875\), which is the gap. The debt term alone is 0.009375, so the debt change is not the whole gap. The contract's balance-sheet measure subtracts all of \(\Delta CL\), which includes the 15 CU of short-term debt, and that is why the debt term enters the gap.

Proof

P1 · Distributions cancel

Show that Proposition 2.2 holds when \(D_t \ne 0\), and explain why the distributions do not appear in the gap.

Solution

Under A4, \(CFF_t = \Delta LTD_t - D_t\), so \(CFO_t = \Delta Cash_t + capex_t - \Delta LTD_t + D_t\). Under A1, \(NI_t - D_t = \Delta E_t\). The term \(+D_t\) in CFO and the term \(-D_t\) in equity therefore cancel in \(NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta E_t - \Delta Cash_t - capex_t + \Delta LTD_t\). The distributions enter equity and cash in the same amount, so they never reach the gap. The proof of Proposition 2.2 is valid for any \(D_t\), including zero.

P2 · When the two measures agree

Show that \(AC^{CF}_t = AC^{BS}_t\) if and only if \(\Delta ONCA_t = \Delta ONCL_t\), under A1 to A4 and \(\overline{TA}_t \gt 0\). Then give a firm in which both non-current changes are nonzero and the measures still agree, and say what that shows about the condition.

Solution

By Corollary 2.3, \(AC^{CF}_t - AC^{BS}_t = (\Delta ONCA_t - \Delta ONCL_t)/\overline{TA}_t\). Since \(\overline{TA}_t \gt 0\), the difference is zero if and only if the numerator is zero, which is \(\Delta ONCA_t = \Delta ONCL_t\). For the example, take \(\Delta ONCA_t = \Delta ONCL_t = 25\) CU: both non-current items move, the measures agree, and the condition is met. What matters is the difference of the two changes, not each change on its own.

P3 · Free cash flow from the proof

Prove Proposition 3.1 directly from Proposition 2.2, and state any assumption it uses that Proposition 2.2 does not.

Solution

Proposition 2.2 gives \(NI_t - CFO_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta ONCL_t - D\&A_t\). By A3, \(STD_t = 0\), so the change in net operating assets is \(\Delta NOA_t = \Delta CAo_t + \Delta PPE_t + \Delta ONCA_t - \Delta CL_t - \Delta ONCL_t\). Subtracting the second from the first gives \(NI_t - CFO_t - \Delta NOA_t = -D\&A_t - \Delta PPE_t\). A2 gives \(\Delta PPE_t = capex_t - D\&A_t\), so \(-D\&A_t - \Delta PPE_t = -capex_t\). No assumption beyond Proposition 2.2 is used: A3 already gives \(STD_t = 0\), which keeps debt out of \(NOA\).

P4 · Both measures are ratios of CU

Show that \(AC^{CF}_t\) is invariant to replacing the currency unit by a unit \(k\) times as large, and explain why the invariance does not extend to comparing two firms.

Solution

Expressed in the new unit, every money item is \(x/k\). The numerator and the denominator are each divided by \(k\), so the ratio is unchanged. Comparing two firms is different: if one firm reports in thousands and the other in millions, their numbers are on different scales before any ratio is taken, and the ratio of one firm's accrual to the other's is not meaningful until they share a unit. Scale invariance holds within a firm-year, and the unit must be common across firms.

Empirical

E1 · The mean gap in the moving scenario

Run Lab 1. Report the mean of the cash-flow measure minus the mean of the balance-sheet measure in the moving scenario, to four decimals. Explain why it is not zero.

Solution

The printed means are \(-0.0308\) for the cash-flow measure and \(-0.0298\) for the balance-sheet measure. Their difference is \(-0.0010\). It is not zero because the moving scenario lets \(ONCA\) and \(ONCL\) change, and the gap in Corollary 2.3 is \((\Delta ONCA - \Delta ONCL)/\overline{TA}\). Its sample mean is the mean of \((\Delta ONCA - \Delta ONCL)/\overline{TA}\) over the 400 firms. The generator centres each non-current change at zero, so the mean is small relative to the spread of either measure but not exactly zero in a finite sample.

E2 · How often the measures disagree

In the moving scenario of Lab 1, what share of the 400 firm-years has an absolute gap above 0.01? Report four decimals and say what the number depends on.

Solution

The lab prints 0.6775 for seed 2604. The same moving scenario gives 0.6800 at seed 2605 and 0.7000 at seed 2606 (share_of_firms_gap_above_0.01_seed_2604 to _2606). The share therefore depends on the seed. It also depends on the 0.01 threshold and on the move sizes in the generator (standard deviations of 20% and 25% of lagged values). The number describes the generator, and it does not estimate how often real firms' measures disagree.

E3 · Correlation and shared variance

Lab 1 reports a correlation of 0.8221 between the two measures in the moving scenario. What share of the variance of one measure is linearly explained by the other?

Solution

The squared correlation of the unrounded panel is 0.6759, so \(1 - r^2 = 0.3241\) of the cross-firm variance of the cash-flow measure is not linearly explained by the balance-sheet measure. Separately, \(\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{gap})/\mathrm{var}(cf) = 0.3285\), where the gap is \(cf - bs\), on the same SIMULATED panel. The two figures differ (0.3241 and 0.3285), so the unexplained share is not the same as the variance share of the gap. The identity gives the gap exactly, but it does not decompose the variance. A high correlation does not make the measures interchangeable.

Research

R1 · Testing the identity on a real filer

Describe, in full, a test of Proposition 2.2 on one real filer's statements. Name every input, the sources, the assumptions you would need to check first, and what result would refute the identity. Do not run it; the honest-pending note in Section 4 applies.

Solution

Inputs: the net income, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, depreciation and amortization, and the two balance sheets (current assets, cash, current liabilities, short-term debt, total assets, long-term debt, non-current operating items) for two consecutive year-ends, from the filer's own filings. First check A2: a PP&E roll-forward with disposals and acquisitions disclosed as separate lines, and set those lines aside. Second check A3 and A4: whether short-term debt sits in current liabilities, and whether every cash item is in one of the three sections. Third compute both sides of Proposition 2.2. The identity is refuted if the residual is larger than the rounding of the filing after the disclosed non-current items are included. The data are not yet available to this course.

R2 · Which assumption a real filer breaks first

Name the assumptions a real filing can violate, and for each say how the violation would show in the statements. Then say which violation the contract's balance-sheet measure cannot repair.

Solution

A3 and A4 can fail in a filing as published: the cash-flow statement groups items by a classification rule, and current liabilities may hold current debt. A2 fails when disposals, impairments or acquisitions move PP&E; those items can be separated if the filer discloses them. A1 can fail through a restatement or through an equity item that bypasses net income; the statement of equity shows such items. The contract's balance-sheet measure cannot repair a violation of A3 that puts debt in current liabilities, because the debt change then sits inside \(\Delta CL\) and is not separable from the operating change without the filer's own debt disclosure. This is the Section 5 case, and it is why the lesson keeps the simplification named.

R3 · Accruals as a measurement problem

Argue whether a low accrual measure in a year is evidence of higher quality, evidence of no manipulation, or neither. State which of the identities in this lesson you would use, and what your argument would need to show empirically before a claim about quality could be made.

Solution

Neither, on the identities alone. A low accrual is a low value of a number that is a sum of growth in net operating assets and the reclassification effects of Remark 2.4. Proposition 3.1 shows that the accrual equals the growth in net operating assets less capital expenditure, so a low accrual can come from a shrinking business or from a firm that invests more, not only from better earnings. An argument for quality would have to show, empirically, that the component of the accrual that is not explained by normal growth predicts a future outcome that matters to the use. Lesson L05 gives the predictive test, and Lesson L06 the normal-growth model. Neither proves quality on its own.

9Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Under A1 to A4, what is the difference between the cash-flow accrual and the balance-sheet accrual, both scaled by average total assets?

    Options

    Corollary 2.3. The non-current operating items are the only terms the balance-sheet measure omits.

  2. A firm has \(\Delta CA = 80\), \(\Delta Cash = 20\), \(\Delta CL = 20\), \(D\&A = 30\), and \(\overline{TA} = 1600\), all in CU. What is \(AC^{BS}\)? (Give the decimal.)

    \((80 - 20 - 20 - 30)/1600 = 10/1600 = 0.00625\).

  3. Net income is 120 CU and operating cash flow is 150 CU, with \(\overline{TA} = 1600\). What is the cash-flow accrual? (Give the decimal.)

    \((120 - 150)/1600 = -0.01875\). The sign is negative because cash exceeded earnings.

  4. Why do distributions to shareholders not appear in the accrual gap?

    Options

    Clean surplus adds \(NI - D\) to equity, and the cash-flow statement subtracts \(D\) in financing, so \(D\) cancels in the gap.

  5. Which change makes the contract's balance-sheet measure differ from the cash-flow measure by \(\Delta STD/\overline{TA}\)?

    Options

    The debt change is financing in the cash-flow statement but sits in \(\Delta CL\), so the contract measure subtracts it and the cash-flow measure does not. Section 5 gives the derivation.

  6. If a cash item moves from operating to financing in the cash-flow statement, which measure changes?

    Options

    Remark 2.4. Net income and \(\Delta Cash\) do not change, so only CFO moves and only the cash-flow measure follows it.

  7. In the moving scenario of Lab 1 the correlation of the two measures is 0.8221. What is the squared correlation, to four decimals?

    The squared correlation of the unrounded panel is 0.6759 (E3).

  8. Under A1 to A4, the articulation identity is exact. Which claim about real filings does that exactness establish?

    Options

    Proposition 2.2 is a theorem under A1 to A4. Whether a real filing satisfies A1 to A4 is an empirical question the identity leaves open (R1, R2).

  9. A firm has \(\Delta STD = 15\), \(\Delta ONCA = 30\), \(\Delta ONCL = 10\), and \(\overline{TA} = 1600\), all in CU. What is \(AC^{CF} - AC^{BS}\) under the Section 5 remark? (Give the decimal.)

    \((15 + 30 - 10)/1600 = 0.021875\) (M4).
