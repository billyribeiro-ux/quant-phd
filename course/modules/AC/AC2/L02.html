A return on equity of 20% does not say where it came from. A ratio is a quotient of two statement items, and the choice of numerator, denominator and timing decides what it measures. DuPont splits return on equity into three factors that have three different economic meanings, and the split is exact. This lesson defines the ratios the A15 engine computes, proves the identity behind DuPont, shows how the denominator convention moves the answer, and shows where a ratio reads the balance sheet in a way the firm does not intend.

1A ratio is a function of standardized items

Definition 1.1 (financial ratio)

A financial ratio is a quotient of two standardized items, or of one item and the average of two of its year-end values, with a stated denominator timing. The ratio is dimensionless. The A15 engine reads its inputs from the standardized table of Lesson 1, and every ratio it returns is defined on that table alone.

The A15 contract fixes three conventions that every ratio here obeys. The previous year is the latest period end 350 to 380 days earlier. An average is \((x_t + x_{t-1})/2\) of year-end balances. A missing item, or a missing previous year, raises ValueError and is never imputed. The conventions are the A15 contract's. The module's core reading for the ratio framework is Penman (2013). The passage locator for these conventions is not confirmed.

The ratio framework used here, with average balances for return measures and year-end balances for solvency and liquidity measures, is the one Penman (2013) presents as the standard analysis of reported statements. The grade is C because it is a single textbook's presentation, and the claim has not been checked against the page.

Proposition 1.2 (scale invariance)

Let every money item of every period end be multiplied by a constant \(\kappa \ne 0\), and leave share counts alone. Then every ratio that is a quotient of money items is unchanged. Under the A15 contract's averages, the same holds for the ratios that use them.

Proof

A ratio \(x/y\) of money items becomes \((\kappa x)/(\kappa y) = x/y\), because \(\kappa \ne 0\). An average \((x_t + x_{t-1})/2\) becomes \(\kappa (x_t + x_{t-1})/2\), which is \(\kappa\) times the original average, so a quotient of two averages is again unchanged. Quotients involving share counts are not covered, because the factor does not multiply shares. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 1.2 says that a ratio is free of the currency and scale of the filing. It does not say that two firms' ratios are comparable. That is a separate question, taken up in Section 5.

2Denominator timing

Return measures divide income for a year by a balance. The balance can be the year-end value or the average of the two year ends. The choice is a convention, and it changes the answer whenever the balance moves.

Remark 2.1 (a growing balance and its average)

Let equity grow at a constant rate \(g\), so \(E_{t-1} = E_t / (1+g)\). The average is \(\bar E = E_t (1 + 1/(1+g))/2\), so \(\bar E / E_t = (1 + 1/(1+g))/2\). Return on average equity is therefore \(1 / (\bar E/E_t) = 2(1+g)/(2+g)\) times return on year-end equity. At \(g = 0.2\) the factor is \(1.0909\): the average-based return is 9.1% higher. The gap comes only from timing, not from the firm's performance.

Lab 1 · The DuPont identity and the ratio conventions on a SIMULATED filer

The lab runs the same SIMULATED filer as Lesson 1's lab, passed through the standardizer, then computes the five DuPont measures with average balances for each year that has a previous year, and the other ratios, of which the current ratio, debt to assets and DSO use year-end balances and gross margin uses no balance. Predicted from the rules, before running: the identity holds to relative 1e-12 in every year that has a previous year; the 2020 year end raises ValueError for DuPont, because it has no previous year in the table; multiplying every money item by 1000 leaves every ratio unchanged; and the average-based ROE for 2022 is higher than the year-end-based ROE, because equity grew. The standardizer of Lesson 1 raises on a conflicting tie between the two best observations, and an equal-valued tie does not raise.

"""AC2 lab L02: ratio analysis and the DuPont identity on a SIMULATED generated filer.

SIMULATED: the filer is generated from a seeded RNG (make_filer, as in Lesson 1's lab). Nothing here is a real
company's data. The ratios follow atlas/A15.contract.md (Ratios, Conventions). The lab checks the identity
roe == net_margin * asset_turnover * leverage to relative 1e-12 in every year that has a previous year, and shows
how the choice of denominator (year-end or average) moves ROE. Standard library only; no network.
"""

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Observation:
    """One parsed observation. Dates are datetime.date; val is a finite float in the filing's unit."""

    end: date
    val: float
    filed: date
    form: str
    fp: str
    accn: str
    start: date | None


def _date(value: object, where: str) -> date:
    """Parse an ISO date with date.fromisoformat; anything else raises ValueError."""
    if not isinstance(value, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where} must be an ISO date string, not {type(value).__name__}")
    try:
        return date.fromisoformat(value)
    except ValueError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{where} is not an ISO date: {value!r}") from exc


def _observation(raw: object, tag: str, unit: str) -> Observation:
    """Validate one raw observation; a missing or malformed required key raises ValueError."""
    where = f"{tag} [{unit}]"
    if not isinstance(raw, Mapping):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: an observation must be a mapping")
    for key in ("end", "val", "filed", "form"):
        if key not in raw:
            raise ValueError(f"{where}: observation lacks {key!r}")
    value = raw["val"]
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int | float) or not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: val must be a finite number, not {value!r}")
    form = raw["form"]
    if not isinstance(form, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: form must be a string")
    fp = raw.get("fp", "")
    accn = raw.get("accn", "")
    if not isinstance(fp, str) or not isinstance(accn, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: fp and accn must be strings when present")
    start = _date(raw["start"], f"{where}: start") if "start" in raw else None
    return Observation(
        end=_date(raw["end"], f"{where}: end"),
        val=float(value),
        filed=_date(raw["filed"], f"{where}: filed"),
        form=form,
        fp=fp,
        accn=accn,
        start=start,
    )


def _is_annual(obs: Observation, flow: bool) -> bool:
    """Annual: form 10-K*, fp FY, and a 350-380 day duration (flow) or no start date (instant)."""
    if not obs.form.startswith("10-K") or obs.fp != "FY":
        return False
    if flow:
        return obs.start is not None and 350 <= (obs.end - obs.start).days <= 380
    return obs.start is None


def _us_gaap(facts: Mapping[str, Any]) -> Mapping[str, Any]:
    """Return the us-gaap block; a mapping without 'facts' raises ValueError."""
    if not isinstance(facts, Mapping) or "facts" not in facts:
        raise ValueError("the company-facts mapping lacks 'facts'")
    block = facts["facts"]
    if not isinstance(block, Mapping):
        raise ValueError("'facts' must be a mapping")
    gaap = block.get("us-gaap", {})
    if not isinstance(gaap, Mapping):
        raise ValueError("'facts[us-gaap]' must be a mapping")
    return gaap


def _as_of_pick(
    gaap: Mapping[str, Any], tag: str, unit: str, flow: bool, cutoff: date | None
) -> dict[date, Observation]:
    """For one tag: per period end, the annual observation with the greatest (filed, accn) among filed <= cutoff."""
    block = gaap.get(tag)
    if block is None:
        return {}
    if not isinstance(block, Mapping) or not isinstance(block.get("units", {}), Mapping):
        raise ValueError(f"{tag}: must be a mapping with a 'units' mapping")
    rows = block.get("units", {}).get(unit, [])
    if not isinstance(rows, list):
        raise ValueError(f"{tag} [{unit}]: observations must be a list")
    candidates: dict[date, list[Observation]] = {}
    for raw in rows:
        obs = _observation(raw, tag, unit)
        if not _is_annual(obs, flow):
            continue
        if cutoff is not None and obs.filed > cutoff:
            continue
        candidates.setdefault(obs.end, []).append(obs)
    best: dict[date, Observation] = {}
    for end, group in candidates.items():
        # Rank by (filed, accn or ""), best first. Contract: if the two best tie on that key and carry different
        # val, the facts are inconsistent and standardize raises ValueError. Equal values are not a conflict.
        ranked = sorted(group, key=lambda o: (o.filed, o.accn), reverse=True)
        top = ranked[0]
        if len(ranked) > 1:
            runner = ranked[1]
            if (top.filed, top.accn) == (runner.filed, runner.accn) and top.val != runner.val:
                raise ValueError(
                    f"{tag} at period end {end.isoformat()}: two annual observations tie on "
                    f"(filed {top.filed.isoformat()}, accn {top.accn!r}) with different values "
                    f"{top.val!r} and {runner.val!r}"
                )
        best[end] = top
    return best


def select(facts: Mapping[str, Any], as_of: str | None = None) -> dict[str, dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]]]:
    """Per item and period end, the winning (tag, observation) under the tag priority and the as-of rule."""
    cutoff = None if as_of is None else _date(as_of, "as_of")
    gaap = _us_gaap(facts)
    out: dict[str, dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]]] = {}
    for item in ITEMS:
        flow = item in FLOW_ITEMS
        unit = "shares" if item == "shares_outstanding" else "USD"
        per_tag = {tag: _as_of_pick(gaap, tag, unit, flow, cutoff) for tag in TAG_PRIORITY[item]}
        ends: set[date] = set()
        for picks in per_tag.values():
            ends.update(picks)
        winners: dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]] = {}
        for end in ends:
            for tag in TAG_PRIORITY[item]:
                if end in per_tag[tag]:
                    winners[end] = (tag, per_tag[tag][end])
                    break
        out[item] = winners
    return out


def standardize(facts: Mapping[str, Any], as_of: str | None = None) -> dict[str, dict[str, float]]:
    """{period_end_iso: {item: value}}, period ends ascending; absent items are absent (nothing imputed)."""
    picked = select(facts, as_of)
    table: dict[date, dict[str, float]] = {}
    for item in ITEMS:
        for end, (_tag, obs) in picked[item].items():
            table.setdefault(end, {})[item] = obs.val
    return {end.isoformat(): table[end] for end in sorted(table)}


def make_filer(seed: int = SEED) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """A SIMULATED company-facts mapping: five fiscal years, 10-K comparatives, a restatement, a 10-Q, a Q4 row."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    years = (2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024)
    rev = {2020: 1_000_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        rev[y] = round(rev[y - 1] * (1 + rng.uniform(0.04, 0.09)))
    cogs = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(0.58, 0.64)) for y in years}
    sga = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(0.18, 0.22)) for y in years}
    noise = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(-0.03, 0.03)) for y in years}
    dep: dict[int, int] = {2020: 38_000_000}
    ppe: dict[int, int] = {2020: 400_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        dep[y] = round(ppe[y - 1] * 0.10)
        ppe[y] = ppe[y - 1] + round(rev[y] * 0.06) - dep[y]
    oi = {y: rev[y] - cogs[y] - sga[y] - dep[y] for y in years}
    ni = {y: round(oi[y] * 0.75) for y in years}
    cfo = {y: ni[y] + dep[y] + noise[y] for y in years}
    eq: dict[int, int] = {2020: 600_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        eq[y] = eq[y - 1] + ni[y] - round(0.4 * ni[y])
    ltd = {y: 300_000_000 + 10_000_000 * (y - 2020) for y in years}
    cash = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.08) for y in years}
    recv = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.12) for y in years}
    inv = {y: round(cogs[y] * 0.15) for y in years}
    other_ca = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.03) for y in years}
    ca = {y: cash[y] + recv[y] + inv[y] + other_ca[y] for y in years}
    cl = {y: round(cogs[y] * 0.25) for y in years}
    ol = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.05) for y in years}
    tl = {y: cl[y] + ltd[y] + ol[y] for y in years}
    ta = {y: tl[y] + eq[y] for y in years}
    shares = {y: 50_000_000 if y < 2024 else 52_000_000 for y in years}

    def fy_tag(item: str, fy: int) -> str:
        if item == "revenue":
            return "Revenues" if fy <= 2021 else "RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax"
        if item == "cogs":
            return "CostOfRevenue" if fy <= 2022 else "CostOfGoodsAndServicesSold"
        return TAG_PRIORITY[item][0]

    truth: dict[str, dict[int, int]] = {
        "revenue": rev,
        "cogs": cogs,
        "sga": sga,
        "depreciation": dep,
        "operating_income": oi,
        "net_income": ni,
        "cfo": cfo,
        "total_assets": ta,
        "current_assets": ca,
        "cash": cash,
        "receivables": recv,
        "ppe": ppe,
        "total_liabilities": tl,
        "current_liabilities": cl,
        "long_term_debt": ltd,
        "equity": eq,
        "shares_outstanding": shares,
    }
    flows = {"revenue", "cogs", "sga", "depreciation", "operating_income", "net_income", "cfo"}
    gaap: dict[str, dict[str, dict[str, list[dict[str, Any]]]]] = {}

    def add(item: str, fy: int, end_year: int, val: int, form: str, fp: str, accn: str, filed: str) -> None:
        tag = fy_tag(item, fy)
        unit = "shares" if item == "shares_outstanding" else "USD"
        obs: dict[str, Any] = {"end": f"{end_year}-12-31", "val": val, "filed": filed, "form": form, "fp": fp}
        if item in flows:
            obs["start"] = f"{end_year}-01-01"  # a full fiscal year, 365 days
            if fp == "Q1":
                obs["end"] = f"{end_year}-03-31"
            if fp == "Q4":
                obs["start"] = f"{end_year}-10-01"
        obs["accn"] = accn
        units: dict[str, list[dict[str, Any]]] = gaap.setdefault(tag, {"units": {}})["units"]
        units.setdefault(unit, []).append(obs)

    for fy in years:
        filed = f"{fy + 1}-02-15"
        accn = f"SIM-{fy}-K"
        for end_year in (fy, fy - 1):
            if end_year < 2020:
                continue
            for item in truth:
                if item == "long_term_debt" and end_year == 2020:
                    continue  # never published: the 2020 long-term debt is absent from the file
                add(item, fy, end_year, truth[item][end_year], "10-K", "FY", accn, filed)
        if fy == 2023:
            add("revenue", fy, 2023, rev[2023] // 4, "10-K", "Q4", accn, filed)
    # Restatement of FY2022 revenue, filed later as a 10-K/A.
    add("revenue", 2023, 2022, round(rev[2022] * 1.012), "10-K/A", "FY", "SIM-2023-KA", "2024-03-01")
    # Quarterly revenue from 10-Q filings: never annual, so the rules ignore them.
    for fy in years[1:]:
        add("revenue", fy, fy, rev[fy] // 4, "10-Q", "Q1", f"SIM-{fy}-Q1", f"{fy}-05-10")
    return {
        "cik": 1,
        "entityName": "SIMULATED FILER 1 (generated; not a real company)",
        "facts": {"us-gaap": gaap},
    }


Statements = Mapping[str, Mapping[str, float]]


def _previous_year(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> str:
    """The latest period end 350 to 380 days before period_end; none raises ValueError."""
    end = _date(period_end, "period_end")
    earlier = [e for e in statements if 350 <= (end - date.fromisoformat(e)).days <= 380]
    if not earlier:
        raise ValueError(f"no previous year for {period_end}: no period end 350 to 380 days earlier")
    return max(earlier, key=date.fromisoformat)


def _item(statements: Statements, period_end: str, item: str) -> float:
    row = statements.get(period_end)
    if row is None:
        raise ValueError(f"no statement for period end {period_end}")
    if item not in row:
        raise ValueError(f"{item} is missing for period end {period_end}")
    return row[item]


def _average(statements: Statements, period_end: str, item: str) -> float:
    """(x_t + x_{t-1}) / 2 of year-end balances."""
    previous = _previous_year(statements, period_end)
    return (_item(statements, period_end, item) + _item(statements, previous, item)) / 2


def _ratio(numerator: float, denominator: float, name: str) -> float:
    """A zero denominator or a non-finite result raises ValueError."""
    if denominator == 0:
        raise ValueError(f"{name}: zero denominator")
    value = numerator / denominator
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f"{name}: non-finite result")
    return value


def dupont(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> dict[str, float]:
    """net_margin, asset_turnover, leverage, roe, roa on average balances (A15 contract, Ratios)."""
    net_income = _item(statements, period_end, "net_income")
    revenue = _item(statements, period_end, "revenue")
    avg_assets = _average(statements, period_end, "total_assets")
    avg_equity = _average(statements, period_end, "equity")
    net_margin = _ratio(net_income, revenue, "net_margin")
    asset_turnover = _ratio(revenue, avg_assets, "asset_turnover")
    leverage = _ratio(avg_assets, avg_equity, "leverage")
    return {
        "net_margin": net_margin,
        "asset_turnover": asset_turnover,
        "leverage": leverage,
        "roe": _ratio(net_income, avg_equity, "roe"),
        "roa": _ratio(net_income, avg_assets, "roa"),
    }


def current_ratio(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> float:
    return _ratio(
        _item(statements, period_end, "current_assets"),
        _item(statements, period_end, "current_liabilities"),
        "current_ratio",
    )


def gross_margin(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> float:
    revenue = _item(statements, period_end, "revenue")
    return _ratio(revenue - _item(statements, period_end, "cogs"), revenue, "gross_margin")


def debt_to_assets(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> float:
    return _ratio(
        _item(statements, period_end, "long_term_debt"),
        _item(statements, period_end, "total_assets"),
        "debt_to_assets",
    )


def days_sales_outstanding(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> float:
    revenue = _item(statements, period_end, "revenue")
    return _ratio(_item(statements, period_end, "receivables"), revenue, "dso") * 365


def scaled(statements: Statements, factor: float) -> dict[str, dict[str, float]]:
    """Every money item times factor; shares are left alone. Ratios must not change."""
    return {
        end: {k: (v if k == "shares_outstanding" else v * factor) for k, v in row.items()}
        for end, row in statements.items()
    }


def tie_filer(first: float, second: float) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """A SIMULATED one-tag mapping with two annual observations filed the same day under the same accession."""
    row = {"end": "2023-12-31", "filed": "2024-02-15", "form": "10-K", "fp": "FY", "accn": "SIM-2023-K"}
    rows = [{**row, "val": first}, {**row, "val": second}]
    return {"facts": {"us-gaap": {"Assets": {"units": {"USD": rows}}}}}


def millions(value: float) -> str:
    return f"{value / 1e6:,.1f}"


def main() -> None:
    filer = make_filer()
    statements = standardize(filer)
    ends = list(statements)
    print("DuPont decomposition, average balances (SIMULATED filer 1)")
    print("period_end  net_margin  asset_turn  leverage     roe      roa  identity_ok")
    for end in ends[1:]:
        d = dupont(statements, end)
        product = d["net_margin"] * d["asset_turnover"] * d["leverage"]
        identity_ok = abs(d["roe"] - product) <= 1e-12 * abs(d["roe"])
        print(
            f"{end}  {d['net_margin']:10.4f}  {d['asset_turnover']:10.4f}  {d['leverage']:8.4f}"
            f"  {d['roe']:7.4f}  {d['roa']:7.4f}  {identity_ok}"
        )

    print("other ratios (SIMULATED): year-end balances, except gross margin, which uses only the year's flows")
    print("period_end  current_ratio  gross_margin  debt_to_assets  dso_days")
    for end in ends:
        ltd = "absent" if "long_term_debt" not in statements[end] else f"{debt_to_assets(statements, end):.4f}"
        print(
            f"{end}  {current_ratio(statements, end):13.4f}  {gross_margin(statements, end):12.4f}"
            f"  {ltd:>14}  {days_sales_outstanding(statements, end):8.2f}"
        )

    y2022 = "2022-12-31"
    avg_eq = _average(statements, y2022, "equity")
    year_end_roe = _item(statements, y2022, "net_income") / _item(statements, y2022, "equity")
    print(
        f"FY2022 ROE: average equity {avg_eq / 1e6:,.1f} USD m gives {dupont(statements, y2022)['roe']:.4f};"
        f" year-end equity gives {year_end_roe:.4f}"
    )

    restated = dupont(standardize(filer), y2022)
    original = dupont(standardize(filer, as_of="2023-06-30"), y2022)
    print(f"FY2022 net margin: as filed {original['net_margin']:.4f}, restated {restated['net_margin']:.4f}")

    end = "2022-12-31"
    d = dupont(statements, end)
    year_end_assets = _item(statements, end, "total_assets")
    mismatched = d["net_margin"] * (_item(statements, end, "revenue") / year_end_assets) * d["leverage"]
    print(
        f"identity with year-end assets in turnover but average in leverage: {mismatched:.4f}"
        f" against roe {d['roe']:.4f}"
    )

    earlier, later = dupont(statements, "2023-12-31"), dupont(statements, "2024-12-31")
    log_total = math.log(later["roe"] / earlier["roe"])
    print(f"log change in ROE, 2023 to 2024: {log_total:.4f}")
    for factor in ("net_margin", "asset_turnover", "leverage"):
        share = math.log(later[factor] / earlier[factor])

3The DuPont decomposition

Definition 3.1 (DuPont decomposition)

The DuPont decomposition writes return on equity as the product of three factors, with \(\bar A\) and \(\bar E\) the averages of total assets and equity over the two year ends: \[ \mathrm{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{NI}}{\mathrm{Rev}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{Rev}}{\bar A}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\bar A}{\bar E}}_{\text{leverage}}. \] Return on assets is the first two factors multiplied, \(\mathrm{NI}/\bar A\).

Theorem 3.2 (the DuPont identity)

If the three factors of Definition 3.1 are defined on the same averages, then \(\mathrm{ROE} = \text{net margin} \times \text{asset turnover} \times \text{leverage}\) exactly, with no residual. In the A15 engine the identity holds to relative \(10^{-12}\) by construction.

Proof

Multiply the three factors and cancel. The revenue in the margin cancels the revenue in the turnover, leaving \(\mathrm{NI}/\bar A\). The average assets in the turnover cancel the average assets in the leverage, leaving \(\mathrm{NI}/\bar E\). That is ROE by definition. Every cancellation needs both factors to use the same \(\bar A\), and the same revenue, so the identity fails when the denominators differ. \(\blacksquare\)

Example 3.3 (FY2023 of the SIMULATED filer)

Lab 1 prints, for 2023, net margin 0.1179, asset turnover 0.8701, leverage 1.6936 and ROE 0.1738. The three rounded factors multiply to 0.1737 in the last printed digit, because each factor is rounded before the product is taken. The identity holds on the unrounded values, and the lab's identity_ok column is true for every year.

Remark 3.4 (what the three factors say)

Net margin measures what the firm keeps from each unit of sales. Asset turnover measures how much sales each unit of assets supports. Leverage measures how many units of assets each unit of equity carries. A rise in ROE from leverage alone leaves the operating economics unchanged. The identity says nothing about which factor is the cause. It only allocates the change.

The allocation is most useful as a decomposition of a change. Taking logarithms turns the product into a sum, and the sum is exact: \[ \ln \frac{\mathrm{ROE}_b}{\mathrm{ROE}_a} = \sum_{f} \ln \frac{f_b}{f_a}, \] where \(f\) runs over the three factors. Each term is the share of the change that the factor explains (Problem P3). Lab 1 prints the decomposition for the change from 2023 to 2024: the log change in ROE is \(-0.3401\), and net margin accounts for 88.1% of it, asset turnover for 6.5% and leverage for 5.4%. The shares are exact, and they sum to 100%.

4Other ratios the engine computes

Definition 4.1 (solvency, liquidity and margin ratios)

Three of these ratios use year-end balances: the current ratio is current assets over current liabilities, debt to assets is long-term debt over total assets, and days sales outstanding is the year-end receivables over the fiscal year's revenue, times 365. Gross margin is \((\mathrm{Rev} - \mathrm{COGS})/\mathrm{Rev}\) for the fiscal year and uses no balance. The 365 is a convention of day count. A 364- or 371-day fiscal year does not change it.

Example 4.2 (the DSO exposes a restatement)

In Lab 1, days sales outstanding is 43.80 in every year except 2022, where it is 43.28. The receivables figure is the one the file reports for 2022, and it carries no restatement. The 2022 revenue used in the ratio is the restated 1,145.5 USD m rather than the 1,131.9 USD m originally filed. The ratio therefore differs from the 43.80 that the originally filed revenue gives. The lab shows the same effect on FY2022 net margin: 0.1228 as filed, 0.1214 restated. Section 5 returns to this as a canon break.

5Where the canon breaks

The standard analysis reads a ratio as a measure of performance or risk. Four features of the definitions in this lesson make that reading unsafe without checks.

  • Leverage counts every liability. Total assets equal total liabilities plus equity, by articulation. So assets over equity equals one plus liabilities over equity, and every liability raises the leverage factor. Payables and deferred revenue raise it as much as borrowings do. A rise in the leverage factor therefore does not mean the firm borrowed more. The A15 engine's leverage factor is total assets over equity by contract, so the lesson draws no financing conclusion from it alone.
  • Denominators must match. Theorem 3.2 needs one average in every factor. Lab 1 shows the mismatch: with year-end assets in turnover and average assets in leverage, the 2022 product is 0.1838 against an ROE of 0.1925. The mismatch is a bug, not a view.
  • A restatement can split a ratio across two states of the filing. Example 4.2 shows the 2022 DSO built from a restated revenue and the receivables the file reports, which carry no restatement. A ratio should be computed from one as-of state of the table, which the standardizer's cut-off makes possible.
  • Scale invariance is within a filer. Proposition 1.2 says a ratio does not depend on the currency unit of one filer. It says nothing about two filers that report under different accounting policies. Penman (2013) treats adjustment for accounting differences as part of the analysis of reported statements.

6Misconceptions

  1. “A higher ROE means the firm runs its operations better.”

    By Theorem 3.2, ROE can rise with no change in margin or turnover, if the leverage factor rises. The factor is an accounting ratio of total assets to equity. The identity alone lets ROE rise while margin and turnover stay unchanged. Lab 1's three factors all fall together in each year pair, so the lab does not show that they move independently. The operating reading requires the margin and turnover factors, not ROE alone.

  2. “Average and year-end balances give the same return up to rounding.”

    For a balance that moves, they do not. Lab 1's FY2022 ROE is 0.1925 on average equity and 0.1820 on year-end equity, a gap of 5.8%. Remark 2.1 gives the formula, and the gap grows with the growth rate of the balance.

  3. “The DuPont identity holds whichever average is used in each factor.”

    Theorem 3.2 needs the same average in the turnover and the leverage. Lab 1's mismatched product is 0.1838, not the ROE of 0.1925, so the identity fails when the denominators differ.

  4. “Because ratios are unit-free, any two firms' ratios can be compared directly.”

    Proposition 1.2 makes a ratio unit-free within one filer. Comparing two filers also needs their accounting to be comparable, and the scale of a filing does not guarantee that. This is the C-graded claim of Section 1; its passage locator is not confirmed.

Mechanics

M1 · DuPont by hand

A firm has net income 50, revenue 500, total assets 800 this year and 700 last year, and equity 300 this year and 260 last year (USD m). Compute net margin, asset turnover, leverage and ROE on average balances, and check the identity.

Solution

Average assets are \((800 + 700)/2 = 750\) and average equity is \((300 + 260)/2 = 280\). Net margin is \(50/500 = 0.1\). Asset turnover is \(500/750 = 0.6667\). Leverage is \(750/280 = 2.6786\). ROE is \(50/280 = 0.1786\). The product of the three factors is \(0.1 \times 0.6667 \times 2.6786 = 0.1786\), which equals ROE to the four digits shown. ROA is \(50/750 = 0.0667\).

M2 · Day count

Receivables are 120 and revenue is 900 (USD m). Compute days sales outstanding with the 365-day convention and with a 360-day convention, and say which one the A15 engine uses.

Solution

With 365 days, DSO is \(120/900 \times 365 = 48.67\) days. With 360 days it is \(120/900 \times 360 = 48.00\) days. The A15 engine uses 365 (Definition 4.1). The difference is the ratio of the two day counts, \(365/360 = 1.0139\), so the convention moves the answer by 1.4%, which matters when two firms are compared on different conventions.

M3 · Liquidity and margin

Current assets are 400 and current liabilities are 250. Revenue is 1,000 and cost of revenue is 640 (USD m). Compute the current ratio and the gross margin.

Solution

The current ratio is \(400/250 = 1.60\). Gross profit is \(1{,}000 - 640 = 360\), so the gross margin is \(360/1{,}000 = 0.36\), or 36%. The current ratio is a year-end ratio, from the year-end balances of current assets and current liabilities. The gross margin uses the year's revenue and cost of revenue, so it takes no balance and no average.

Proof

P1 · ROA is the product of two factors

Show that \(\mathrm{ROA} = \text{net margin} \times \text{asset turnover}\) for average balances, and that no average of equity appears in it.

Solution

Net margin times asset turnover is \((\mathrm{NI}/\mathrm{Rev}) \times (\mathrm{Rev}/\bar A)\). The revenue cancels, leaving \(\mathrm{NI}/\bar A\), which is ROA by Definition 3.1. The product uses only net income, revenue and average total assets, so no equity average enters.

P2 · ROE equals ROA times leverage

Using the definitions, show that \(\mathrm{ROE} = \mathrm{ROA} \times \text{leverage}\), and identify the step where the common average is needed.

Solution

ROA times leverage is \((\mathrm{NI}/\bar A) \times (\bar A/\bar E)\). The average assets cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{NI}/\bar E\), which is ROE. The cancellation needs the \(\bar A\) in ROA to be the \(\bar A\) in leverage. If ROA used year-end assets \(A_t\), the product would be \(\mathrm{NI}\,\bar A/(A_t\,\bar E)\), which is not ROE. That is the mismatch of Section 5.

P3 · The log decomposition is exact

Let ROE take the value \(\mathrm{ROE}_a\) in year \(a\) and \(\mathrm{ROE}_b\) in year \(b\), with factors \(f_a\) and \(f_b\). Show that \(\ln(\mathrm{ROE}_b/\mathrm{ROE}_a) = \sum_f \ln(f_b/f_a)\) exactly, so that each term is a share of the change.

Solution

By Theorem 3.2, \(\mathrm{ROE}_b = \prod_f f_b\) and \(\mathrm{ROE}_a = \prod_f f_a\). Dividing gives \(\mathrm{ROE}_b/\mathrm{ROE}_a = \prod_f (f_b/f_a)\). The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms, so \(\ln(\mathrm{ROE}_b/\mathrm{ROE}_a) = \sum_f \ln(f_b/f_a)\). Dividing each term by the left side gives shares that sum to one. Nothing was approximated, so the shares are exact, and a term can be negative when its factor falls.

Research

R1 · A DuPont screen across filers

You want to know whether a set of firms' ROE differences come from margins, from turnover or from leverage. Specify the data, the denominators, the cut-off and the test. State one result that would make the leverage conclusion wrong.

Solution

Data: for each firm, the standardized table of Lesson 1 with a cut-off at each decision date, so that every ratio uses one as-of state. Denominators: averages in all three factors, so Theorem 3.2 holds. Test: for each pair of firms, compute the log difference of ROE and its three log shares, then test whether the leverage share is systematically different from zero across the sample. Caveat: the leverage factor counts every liability (Section 5), so a result that leverage explains the spread may reflect non-debt liabilities. A result that would overturn the conclusion is a split of the leverage factor into interest-bearing and non-interest-bearing liabilities that removes the leverage share.

R2 · Splitting leverage

Propose a decomposition of the leverage factor that separates financing leverage from operating liabilities, and state the balance-sheet identity it relies on. Say what data it needs that the A15 table does not contain.

Solution

Write total assets as operating assets plus financial assets, and total liabilities as operating liabilities plus financial obligations. Articulation gives equity equal to net operating assets minus net financial obligations. Dividing total assets by equity then splits leverage into an operating part, which rises with operating liabilities, and a financing part, which rises with net financial obligations. The A15 table has total liabilities, current liabilities, long-term debt and cash, but no split of current liabilities into operating and financial, and no short-term debt item (the contract says so). The missing data are short-term borrowings and the operating or financial classification of each current liability.

R3 · Average or year-end for a growing firm

A firm grows equity at 20% a year. Which denominator should a study use for ROE, and what is the cost of the other choice? Give the size of the bias.

Solution

By Remark 2.1, with \(g = 0.2\) the average equity is \(0.9167\) of year-end equity, so ROE on average equity is \(1/0.9167 = 1.0909\) times ROE on year-end equity, a bias of 9.1% in favour of the average. The average is the right denominator when the numerator is income earned over the year, because the income was earned on the capital that existed through the year. The year-end denominator is right only for a balance that a decision reads at year end. A study that mixes the two conventions across firms with different growth rates compares a bias with a performance difference, and should report both.
