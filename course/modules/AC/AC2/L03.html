ROIC asks how much operating profit the firm earns on the capital its investors and lenders have put in, net of cash that does nothing. Its numerator is operating income after tax, using the A15 operating-income item (Definition 2.1; an A15 contract rule). Its answer depends on three conventions: the tax rate applied to operating income, the definition of invested capital, and the denominator timing. This lesson fixes the A15 conventions, proves how each one moves the answer, and says where the measure breaks.

1Definition and the A15 invested capital

Definition 1.1 (return on invested capital)

Return on invested capital is \[ \mathrm{ROIC}_t = \frac{\mathrm{NOPAT}_t}{\overline{IC}_t}, \qquad \overline{IC}_t = \tfrac12\big(IC_t + IC_{t-1}\big), \] where \(\mathrm{NOPAT}\) is net operating profit after tax (Definition 2.1) and \(IC\) is invested capital at a year end (Definition 3.1).

The A15 engine defines both sides on its standardized table of Lesson 1. The numerator is operating income after tax. The denominator is an average of two year-end values of a financing-side measure, equity plus long-term debt minus cash. The engine's definition is therefore a convention, not the only one in use, and Section 4 lists what it leaves out.

Remark 1.2 (what the A15 measure omits)

The A15 item list contains no short-term debt, no operating lease liability and no minority interest. Those items are absent from the standardized table, so the engine's invested capital cannot include them. The contract records the short-term-debt omission as a deliberate simplification: invested capital is equity plus long-term debt minus cash, and the item list has no short-term debt. The contract keeps the omission, because adding the item would change every ROIC the lessons print. Its size on the generated filer is a SIMULATED hypothetical in Lab 1: adding 50 USD m of short-term borrowing to the debt in every year lowers FY2023 ROIC at 25% from 0.1349 to 0.1287. A firm that carries its borrowing in current liabilities has an invested capital that is understated by exactly that borrowing. The omission is a fact about the contract's inputs, and the lesson treats it as a canon break.

2NOPAT and the tax-rate convention

Definition 2.1 (NOPAT at tax rate \(\tau\))

For a tax rate \(\tau\) with \(0 \le \tau < 1\), \(\mathrm{NOPAT}_t(\tau) = \mathrm{OI}_t\,(1 - \tau)\), where \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) is operating income. The A15 engine raises ValueError for any \(\tau\) outside this range, and for a non-finite \(\tau\).

The rate \(\tau\) is an input, and the engine does not read it from the filing. A reader can choose a statutory rate, an effective rate (tax expense over pre-tax income), a cash tax rate, or a rate implied by the other lines. The choice changes the answer. The next proposition shows exactly how much.

Proposition 2.2 (ROIC is linear in \(1 - \tau\))

For any \(\tau_1, \tau_2\) in \([0, 1)\), \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau_2) = \dfrac{1 - \tau_2}{1 - \tau_1}\,\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau_1)\). In particular \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau) = (1 - \tau)\,\mathrm{ROIC}_t(0)\), and the relative difference between two conventions does not depend on the firm.

Proof

The average invested capital \(\overline{IC}_t\) does not depend on \(\tau\). Therefore \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau) = \mathrm{OI}_t (1-\tau)/\overline{IC}_t\). Taking the ratio at two rates, \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau_2)/\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau_1) = (1-\tau_2)/(1-\tau_1)\), and the ratio is the same for every firm because \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) and \(\overline{IC}_t\) cancel. Setting \(\tau_1 = 0\) gives the second form. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (an implied rate is not a tax rate)

A reader might recover a tax rate as \(1 - \mathrm{NI}/\mathrm{OI}\). That quantity equals \(\tau\) only when net income is operating income after tax and nothing else. If interest expense \(I\) is paid and net income is \((\mathrm{OI} - I)(1 - \tau)\), then \(1 - \mathrm{NI}/\mathrm{OI} = 1 - (1 - I/\mathrm{OI})(1 - \tau)\), which is not \(\tau\). The implied rate mixes the tax rate with the financing cost. Lab 1 prints it, and gets 0.2500 for every year, because the generator sets net income to 75% of operating income with no interest. That is a property of the simulation.

Lab 1 · ROIC, invested capital and the tax-rate convention on a SIMULATED filer

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Observation:
    """One parsed observation. Dates are datetime.date; val is a finite float in the filing's unit."""

    end: date
    val: float
    filed: date
    form: str
    fp: str
    accn: str
    start: date | None


def _date(value: object, where: str) -> date:
    """Parse an ISO date with date.fromisoformat; anything else raises ValueError."""
    if not isinstance(value, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where} must be an ISO date string, not {type(value).__name__}")
    try:
        return date.fromisoformat(value)
    except ValueError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{where} is not an ISO date: {value!r}") from exc


def _observation(raw: object, tag: str, unit: str) -> Observation:
    """Validate one raw observation; a missing or malformed required key raises ValueError."""
    where = f"{tag} [{unit}]"
    if not isinstance(raw, Mapping):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: an observation must be a mapping")
    for key in ("end", "val", "filed", "form"):
        if key not in raw:
            raise ValueError(f"{where}: observation lacks {key!r}")
    value = raw["val"]
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int | float) or not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: val must be a finite number, not {value!r}")
    form = raw["form"]
    if not isinstance(form, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: form must be a string")
    fp = raw.get("fp", "")
    accn = raw.get("accn", "")
    if not isinstance(fp, str) or not isinstance(accn, str):
        raise ValueError(f"{where}: fp and accn must be strings when present")
    start = _date(raw["start"], f"{where}: start") if "start" in raw else None
    return Observation(
        end=_date(raw["end"], f"{where}: end"),
        val=float(value),
        filed=_date(raw["filed"], f"{where}: filed"),
        form=form,
        fp=fp,
        accn=accn,
        start=start,
    )


def _is_annual(obs: Observation, flow: bool) -> bool:
    """Annual: form 10-K*, fp FY, and a 350-380 day duration (flow) or no start date (instant)."""
    if not obs.form.startswith("10-K") or obs.fp != "FY":
        return False
    if flow:
        return obs.start is not None and 350 <= (obs.end - obs.start).days <= 380
    return obs.start is None


def _us_gaap(facts: Mapping[str, Any]) -> Mapping[str, Any]:
    """Return the us-gaap block; a mapping without 'facts' raises ValueError."""
    if not isinstance(facts, Mapping) or "facts" not in facts:
        raise ValueError("the company-facts mapping lacks 'facts'")
    block = facts["facts"]
    if not isinstance(block, Mapping):
        raise ValueError("'facts' must be a mapping")
    gaap = block.get("us-gaap", {})
    if not isinstance(gaap, Mapping):
        raise ValueError("'facts[us-gaap]' must be a mapping")
    return gaap


def _as_of_pick(
    gaap: Mapping[str, Any], tag: str, unit: str, flow: bool, cutoff: date | None
) -> dict[date, Observation]:
    """For one tag: per period end, the annual observation with the greatest (filed, accn) among filed <= cutoff."""
    block = gaap.get(tag)
    if block is None:
        return {}
    if not isinstance(block, Mapping) or not isinstance(block.get("units", {}), Mapping):
        raise ValueError(f"{tag}: must be a mapping with a 'units' mapping")
    rows = block.get("units", {}).get(unit, [])
    if not isinstance(rows, list):
        raise ValueError(f"{tag} [{unit}]: observations must be a list")
    candidates: dict[date, list[Observation]] = {}
    for raw in rows:
        obs = _observation(raw, tag, unit)
        if not _is_annual(obs, flow):
            continue
        if cutoff is not None and obs.filed > cutoff:
            continue
        candidates.setdefault(obs.end, []).append(obs)
    best: dict[date, Observation] = {}
    for end, group in candidates.items():
        # Rank by (filed, accn or ""), best first. Contract: if the two best tie on that key and carry different
        # val, the facts are inconsistent and standardize raises ValueError. Equal values are not a conflict.
        ranked = sorted(group, key=lambda o: (o.filed, o.accn), reverse=True)
        top = ranked[0]
        if len(ranked) > 1:
            runner = ranked[1]
            if (top.filed, top.accn) == (runner.filed, runner.accn) and top.val != runner.val:
                raise ValueError(
                    f"{tag} at period end {end.isoformat()}: two annual observations tie on "
                    f"(filed {top.filed.isoformat()}, accn {top.accn!r}) with different values "
                    f"{top.val!r} and {runner.val!r}"
                )
        best[end] = top
    return best


def select(facts: Mapping[str, Any], as_of: str | None = None) -> dict[str, dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]]]:
    """Per item and period end, the winning (tag, observation) under the tag priority and the as-of rule."""
    cutoff = None if as_of is None else _date(as_of, "as_of")
    gaap = _us_gaap(facts)
    out: dict[str, dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]]] = {}
    for item in ITEMS:
        flow = item in FLOW_ITEMS
        unit = "shares" if item == "shares_outstanding" else "USD"
        per_tag = {tag: _as_of_pick(gaap, tag, unit, flow, cutoff) for tag in TAG_PRIORITY[item]}
        ends: set[date] = set()
        for picks in per_tag.values():
            ends.update(picks)
        winners: dict[date, tuple[str, Observation]] = {}
        for end in ends:
            for tag in TAG_PRIORITY[item]:
                if end in per_tag[tag]:
                    winners[end] = (tag, per_tag[tag][end])
                    break
        out[item] = winners
    return out


def standardize(facts: Mapping[str, Any], as_of: str | None = None) -> dict[str, dict[str, float]]:
    """{period_end_iso: {item: value}}, period ends ascending; absent items are absent (nothing imputed)."""
    picked = select(facts, as_of)
    table: dict[date, dict[str, float]] = {}
    for item in ITEMS:
        for end, (_tag, obs) in picked[item].items():
            table.setdefault(end, {})[item] = obs.val
    return {end.isoformat(): table[end] for end in sorted(table)}


def make_filer(seed: int = SEED) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """A SIMULATED company-facts mapping: five fiscal years, 10-K comparatives, a restatement, a 10-Q, a Q4 row."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    years = (2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024)
    rev = {2020: 1_000_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        rev[y] = round(rev[y - 1] * (1 + rng.uniform(0.04, 0.09)))
    cogs = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(0.58, 0.64)) for y in years}
    sga = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(0.18, 0.22)) for y in years}
    noise = {y: round(rev[y] * rng.uniform(-0.03, 0.03)) for y in years}
    dep: dict[int, int] = {2020: 38_000_000}
    ppe: dict[int, int] = {2020: 400_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        dep[y] = round(ppe[y - 1] * 0.10)
        ppe[y] = ppe[y - 1] + round(rev[y] * 0.06) - dep[y]
    oi = {y: rev[y] - cogs[y] - sga[y] - dep[y] for y in years}
    ni = {y: round(oi[y] * 0.75) for y in years}
    cfo = {y: ni[y] + dep[y] + noise[y] for y in years}
    eq: dict[int, int] = {2020: 600_000_000}
    for y in years[1:]:
        eq[y] = eq[y - 1] + ni[y] - round(0.4 * ni[y])
    ltd = {y: 300_000_000 + 10_000_000 * (y - 2020) for y in years}
    cash = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.08) for y in years}
    recv = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.12) for y in years}
    inv = {y: round(cogs[y] * 0.15) for y in years}
    other_ca = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.03) for y in years}
    ca = {y: cash[y] + recv[y] + inv[y] + other_ca[y] for y in years}
    cl = {y: round(cogs[y] * 0.25) for y in years}
    ol = {y: round(rev[y] * 0.05) for y in years}
    tl = {y: cl[y] + ltd[y] + ol[y] for y in years}
    ta = {y: tl[y] + eq[y] for y in years}
    shares = {y: 50_000_000 if y < 2024 else 52_000_000 for y in years}

    def fy_tag(item: str, fy: int) -> str:
        if item == "revenue":
            return "Revenues" if fy <= 2021 else "RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax"
        if item == "cogs":
            return "CostOfRevenue" if fy <= 2022 else "CostOfGoodsAndServicesSold"
        return TAG_PRIORITY[item][0]

    truth: dict[str, dict[int, int]] = {
        "revenue": rev,
        "cogs": cogs,
        "sga": sga,
        "depreciation": dep,
        "operating_income": oi,
        "net_income": ni,
        "cfo": cfo,
        "total_assets": ta,
        "current_assets": ca,
        "cash": cash,
        "receivables": recv,
        "ppe": ppe,
        "total_liabilities": tl,
        "current_liabilities": cl,
        "long_term_debt": ltd,
        "equity": eq,
        "shares_outstanding": shares,
    }
    flows = {"revenue", "cogs", "sga", "depreciation", "operating_income", "net_income", "cfo"}
    gaap: dict[str, dict[str, dict[str, list[dict[str, Any]]]]] = {}

    def add(item: str, fy: int, end_year: int, val: int, form: str, fp: str, accn: str, filed: str) -> None:
        tag = fy_tag(item, fy)
        unit = "shares" if item == "shares_outstanding" else "USD"
        obs: dict[str, Any] = {"end": f"{end_year}-12-31", "val": val, "filed": filed, "form": form, "fp": fp}
        if item in flows:
            obs["start"] = f"{end_year}-01-01"  # a full fiscal year, 365 days
            if fp == "Q1":
                obs["end"] = f"{end_year}-03-31"
            if fp == "Q4":
                obs["start"] = f"{end_year}-10-01"
        obs["accn"] = accn
        units: dict[str, list[dict[str, Any]]] = gaap.setdefault(tag, {"units": {}})["units"]
        units.setdefault(unit, []).append(obs)

    for fy in years:
        filed = f"{fy + 1}-02-15"
        accn = f"SIM-{fy}-K"
        for end_year in (fy, fy - 1):
            if end_year < 2020:
                continue
            for item in truth:
                if item == "long_term_debt" and end_year == 2020:
                    continue  # never published: the 2020 long-term debt is absent from the file
                add(item, fy, end_year, truth[item][end_year], "10-K", "FY", accn, filed)
        if fy == 2023:
            add("revenue", fy, 2023, rev[2023] // 4, "10-K", "Q4", accn, filed)
    # Restatement of FY2022 revenue, filed later as a 10-K/A.
    add("revenue", 2023, 2022, round(rev[2022] * 1.012), "10-K/A", "FY", "SIM-2023-KA", "2024-03-01")
    # Quarterly revenue from 10-Q filings: never annual, so the rules ignore them.
    for fy in years[1:]:
        add("revenue", fy, fy, rev[fy] // 4, "10-Q", "Q1", f"SIM-{fy}-Q1", f"{fy}-05-10")
    return {
        "cik": 1,
        "entityName": "SIMULATED FILER 1 (generated; not a real company)",
        "facts": {"us-gaap": gaap},
    }


Statements = Mapping[str, Mapping[str, float]]


def _previous_year(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> str:
    """The latest period end 350 to 380 days before period_end; none raises ValueError."""
    end = _date(period_end, "period_end")
    earlier = [e for e in statements if 350 <= (end - date.fromisoformat(e)).days <= 380]
    if not earlier:
        raise ValueError(f"no previous year for {period_end}: no period end 350 to 380 days earlier")
    return max(earlier, key=date.fromisoformat)


def _item(statements: Statements, period_end: str, item: str) -> float:
    row = statements.get(period_end)
    if row is None:
        raise ValueError(f"no statement for period end {period_end}")
    if item not in row:
        raise ValueError(f"{item} is missing for period end {period_end}")
    return row[item]


def invested_capital(statements: Statements, period_end: str) -> float:
    """Year-end invested capital under the A15 convention: equity + long_term_debt - cash."""
    return (
        _item(statements, period_end, "equity")
        + _item(statements, period_end, "long_term_debt")
        - _item(statements, period_end, "cash")
    )


def nopat(statements: Statements, period_end: str, tax_rate: float) -> float:
    """Operating income after tax at the stated rate; 0 <= tax_rate < 1 or ValueError."""
    if not math.isfinite(tax_rate) or not 0 <= tax_rate < 1:
        raise ValueError(f"tax_rate must satisfy 0 <= tax_rate < 1, got {tax_rate!r}")
    return _item(statements, period_end, "operating_income") * (1 - tax_rate)


def roic(statements: Statements, period_end: str, tax_rate: float) -> float:
    """NOPAT over the average of the two year-end invested-capital balances (A15 contract, roic)."""
    operating = nopat(statements, period_end, tax_rate)
    previous = _previous_year(statements, period_end)
    average_ic = (invested_capital(statements, period_end) + invested_capital(statements, previous)) / 2
    if average_ic <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"average invested capital at {period_end} is not positive ({average_ic:,.0f})")
    value = operating / average_ic
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError("roic: non-finite result")
    return value


def tie_filer(first: float, second: float) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """A SIMULATED one-tag mapping with two annual observations filed the same day under the same accession."""
    row = {"end": "2023-12-31", "filed": "2024-02-15", "form": "10-K", "fp": "FY", "accn": "SIM-2023-K"}
    rows = [{**row, "val": first}, {**row, "val": second}]
    return {"facts": {"us-gaap": {"Assets": {"units": {"USD": rows}}}}}


def millions(value: float) -> str:
    return f"{value / 1e6:,.1f}"


def main() -> None:
    filer = make_filer()
    statements = standardize(filer)
    ends = list(statements)
    print("invested capital at year ends, USD millions (equity + long-term debt - cash)")
    print("period_end  equity  long_term_debt     cash  invested_capital  operating_income  net_income")
    for end in ends:
        row = statements[end]
        if "long_term_debt" not in row:
            print(f"{end}  {millions(row['equity']):>6}  absent: long_term_debt not published")
            continue
        ic = invested_capital(statements, end)
        print(
            f"{end}  {millions(row['equity']):>6}  {millions(row['long_term_debt']):>14}  {millions(row['cash']):>7}"
            f"  {millions(ic):>16}  {millions(row['operating_income']):>16}  {millions(row['net_income']):>10}"
        )

    implied = {end: 1 - statements[end]["net_income"] / statements[end]["operating_income"] for end in ends}
    print("tax rate implied by net income (1 - NI/OI):", ", ".join(f"{implied[e]:.4f}" for e in ends))

    rates = (0.0, 0.15, 0.21, 0.25, 0.30)
    print("ROIC, average invested capital, by tax rate (rates are grid values, not statutory claims)")
    print("period_end  " + "  ".join(f"t={r:.2f}" for r in rates))
    worst = 0.0
    for end in ends[2:]:
        values = [roic(statements, end, r) for r in rates]
        base = values[0]
        for r, value in zip(rates, values, strict=True):
            worst = max(worst, abs(value - base * (1 - r)) / abs(base))
        print(f"{end}  " + "  ".join(f"{v:6.4f}" for v in values))
    print("largest relative deviation of ROIC(t) from ROIC(0) * (1 - t):", f"{worst:.1e}")

    end = "2023-12-31"
    no_cash = {e: {**row, "cash": 0.0} for e, row in statements.items()}
    print(
        f"FY2023 ROIC at 25%: with cash netted {roic(statements, end, 0.25):.4f};"
        f" cash not netted {roic(no_cash, end, 0.25):.4f}"
    )
    average_ic = (invested_capital(statements, end) + invested_capital(statements, "2022-12-31")) / 2
    year_end_roic = nopat(statements, end, 0.25) / invested_capital(statements, end)
    print(
        f"FY2023 ROIC at 25%: on average invested capital {roic(statements, end, 0.25):.4f}"
        f" ({average_ic / 1e6:,.1f} USD m); on year-end invested capital {year_end_roic:.4f}"
    )

    for label, payload in (
        ("conflicting tie", tie_filer(1.0, 2.0)),
        ("equal-valued tie", tie_filer(1.0, 1.0)),
    ):
        try:
            standardize(payload)
        except ValueError as exc:
            print(f"ValueError ({label}): {exc}")
        else:

3Invested capital, averaging and the cash convention

Definition 3.1 (invested capital, A15 convention)

At a year end, \(IC_t = E_t + D_t - C_t\), where \(E_t\) is equity, \(D_t\) is long-term debt, and \(C_t\) is cash and cash equivalents, all as standardized in Lesson 1. The average \(\overline{IC}_t\) is the mean of \(IC_t\) and \(IC_{t-1}\), as in Definition 1.1. A non-positive average raises ValueError.

Proposition 3.2 (average of a sum)

\(\overline{IC}_t = \overline E_t + \overline D_t - \overline C_t\), where each bar denotes the average of the two year ends. Averaging is linear, so the average of a sum is the sum of the averages.

Proof

\(\overline{IC}_t = \tfrac12(IC_t + IC_{t-1}) = \tfrac12\big((E_t + D_t - C_t) + (E_{t-1} + D_{t-1} - C_{t-1})\big)\). Regroup the four terms by item: \(\tfrac12(E_t + E_{t-1}) + \tfrac12(D_t + D_{t-1}) - \tfrac12(C_t + C_{t-1})\), which is the claimed sum. \(\blacksquare\)

Example 3.3 (FY2023 of the SIMULATED filer)

Lab 1 prints operating income of 186.8 USD m for 2023 and invested capital of 1,083.1 for 2023 and 993.5 for 2022. The average is 1,038.3. At \(\tau = 0.25\), NOPAT is \(186.8 \times 0.75 = 140.1\) and ROIC on average capital is \(140.1/1038.3 = 0.1349\). On year-end capital it would be \(140.1/1083.1 = 0.1294\). The lab prints both, and the average-based figure is the one the engine returns.

Proposition 3.4 (timing of the denominator)

Suppose \(IC_{t-1} > 0\), \(IC_t > 0\) and \(\mathrm{NOPAT}_t > 0\). If \(IC_t > IC_{t-1}\), then \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t\) on average capital exceeds \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t\) on year-end capital. If \(IC_t < IC_{t-1}\), the inequality reverses.

Proof

Both year ends are positive, so the average is positive and both ratios are defined. If \(IC_{t-1} < IC_t\), the average lies strictly below \(IC_t\); if \(IC_{t-1} > IC_t\), it lies strictly above \(IC_t\). With a positive numerator, dividing by a smaller denominator gives a larger ratio, and dividing by a larger one gives a smaller ratio. The positivity of \(IC_t\) is needed. With \(IC_{t-1} = 10\), \(IC_t = -5\) and \(\mathrm{NOPAT}_t = 1\), the average-based ROIC is \(2/5\) and the year-end ROIC is \(-1/5\), so the ordering of the two ratios is not the ordering of the denominators. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.5 (the cash convention)

Definition 3.1 subtracts all of the firm's cash. Cash that the business needs to operate is then treated as if it were a financial asset, which lowers invested capital and raises ROIC. Lab 1 shows the effect for FY2023 at 25%: ROIC is 0.1349 with cash netted and 0.1239 without. The netted figure is 8.9% higher. The convention is a choice, and a study that nets only excess cash needs a rule for what counts as excess.

4Where the canon breaks

The A15 measure is built from the financing side of the balance sheet: equity, long-term debt and cash. Operating assets less operating liabilities equal equity plus debt less cash whenever every financing item sits in those three lines. Proof: the balance-sheet identity gives assets equal liabilities plus equity; separating operating items from financing items and regrouping gives operating assets minus operating liabilities equal equity plus financing liabilities minus financing assets, which are debt and cash in the A15 definition. Where some financing items are outside those lines, the two sides differ, and four features of the A15 definition need a check.

  • Omitted financing lines. Remark 1.2 shows that short-term debt and operating lease liabilities are absent from the A15 item list, and the contract calls the short-term-debt omission a deliberate simplification. The A15 denominator leaves out the omitted borrowing. If that borrowing funds the operations whose income is in NOPAT, the A15 ROIC is overstated. On the SIMULATED hypothetical of Lab 1, 50 USD m of short-term borrowing lowers FY2023 ROIC at 25% from 0.1349 to 0.1287. The size for a real filer depends on its use of those lines, and the standardized table cannot reveal it.
  • Equity includes what was paid for acquisitions. Equity is the residual of assets over liabilities. If a firm acquires another, goodwill enters the assets, and the price enters equity or debt, or, if paid from cash, lowers the cash balance. In each case invested capital rises by the price, because equity or debt rises or cash falls. The identity fixes only the change in the denominator. If the acquired operating income enters the filing's operating income from the acquisition date, it also enters NOPAT, and the direction of ROIC then depends on the acquired earnings relative to the price. The equity-based measure includes goodwill by construction, and a study that excludes goodwill has a different denominator.
  • Negative equity removes the denominator. A firm that has repurchased shares until its equity is negative can have a negative invested capital, and the A15 engine raises ValueError when the average is not positive. Lab 1 prints this case. A ratio with a negative denominator is not a return, and the error is the correct response.
  • The engine's measure is one convention among several. The engine's invested capital is the financing side of the balance sheet, and the operating side gives another measure (the Penman claim below). Penman (2013) measures the operating side of the balance sheet as net operating assets.

5Misconceptions

  1. “The tax rate in NOPAT is a fact read off the filing.”

    It is an input chosen by the analyst. Proposition 2.2 shows that changing the rate multiplies ROIC by \((1-\tau_2)/(1-\tau_1)\). Lab 1 shows the FY2023 figure moving from 0.1349 at 25% to 0.1259 at 30%. The filing supplies operating income, not the rate.

  2. “ROIC on year-end capital is the same as ROIC on average capital, up to rounding.”

    Proposition 3.4 gives the direction of the difference. In Lab 1 the FY2023 figures are 0.1349 on average capital and 0.1294 on year-end capital, a gap of 4.31%. The printed 0.1349 and 0.1294 give 4.3%; the exact gap, from the unrounded ratios 0.134941 and 0.129360, is 4.31%. The gap exists because capital grew during the year.

  3. “Cash is always a non-operating asset, so netting all of it is correct.”

    Netting all cash is the A15 convention, and it is one choice. Remark 3.5 shows that the choice moves FY2023 ROIC from 0.1239 to 0.1349. A business that needs cash to trade should not net that cash, and the convention cannot tell the two apart.

  4. “An equity-based invested capital excludes goodwill.”

    Equity is net assets, so goodwill recognized in an acquisition is in invested capital: the price raises equity or debt, or lowers cash, and each of those raises invested capital by the price. If the acquired operating income enters NOPAT from the acquisition date, ROIC after an acquisition depends on the earnings bought as well as the price paid.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it.

Mechanics

M1 · NOPAT and ROIC

Operating income is 200, the tax rate is 25% and average invested capital is 1,000 (USD m). Compute NOPAT and ROIC.

Solution

NOPAT is \(200 \times (1 - 0.25) = 150\). ROIC is \(150/1{,}000 = 0.15\), or 15%.

M2 · Invested capital from the balance sheet

In the current year equity is 600, long-term debt 250 and cash 80. In the prior year invested capital was 730. Operating income is 160 and the rate is 25% (USD m). Compute invested capital for the current year, the average, NOPAT and ROIC.

Solution

Current invested capital is \(600 + 250 - 80 = 770\). The average with the prior 730 is \((770 + 730)/2 = 750\). NOPAT is \(160 \times 0.75 = 120\). ROIC is \(120/750 = 0.16\), or 16%.

M3 · The rate that gives a target return

ROIC at zero tax is 0.13. At what tax rate does ROIC equal 10%?

Solution

By Proposition 2.2, \(\mathrm{ROIC}(\tau) = (1-\tau)\,\mathrm{ROIC}(0)\). Setting this to 0.10 gives \(1 - \tau = 0.10/0.13 = 0.7692\), so \(\tau = 0.2308\), or 23.1%.

Proof

P1 · The linearity of ROIC in the tax rate

Prove Proposition 2.2 without setting \(\tau_1 = 0\), and state which quantities must be the same for both rates.

Solution

Let \(X = \mathrm{OI}_t/\overline{IC}_t\). Then \(\mathrm{ROIC}_t(\tau) = X(1-\tau)\). The two values are \(X(1-\tau_1)\) and \(X(1-\tau_2)\), and their ratio is \((1-\tau_2)/(1-\tau_1)\) whenever \(\tau_1 < 1\). The same operating income and the same average invested capital must enter both, so the denominator and the numerator's operating part must not depend on \(\tau\). That holds because the A15 average is defined from the balance sheet alone.

P2 · The denominator timing

Prove Proposition 3.4 for a firm whose invested capital rose from 900 to 1,000 with NOPAT of 100. Compute both ROICs and check the direction.

Solution

The average is \((900 + 1000)/2 = 950\). ROIC on average capital is \(100/950 = 0.1053\), and on year-end capital it is \(100/1000 = 0.1000\). The average-based figure is larger, as the proposition requires. The general argument, with both year ends positive, is that \(\tfrac12(IC_t + IC_{t-1}) < IC_t\) whenever \(IC_{t-1} < IC_t\), and dividing a positive numerator by a smaller denominator gives a larger ratio.

P3 · Averaging is linear

Show that \(\overline{IC}_t\) equals the average equity plus the average long-term debt minus the average cash, and that the identity needs no assumption about the sign of any item.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 3.2 regroups four terms that appear in \(\tfrac12(IC_t + IC_{t-1})\). Each item appears once per year end with a fixed coefficient of \(\tfrac12\) and a sign that comes from Definition 3.1. Addition is commutative and associative, so the grouping is valid for any real values, and the identity holds for negative equity or negative cash as well.

Empirical

E1 · What moved ROIC from 2023 to 2024? (SIMULATED)

Using Lab 1 at 25%, ROIC fell from 0.1349 to 0.0974. Decompose the change into the change in NOPAT and the change in average invested capital. Use the operating income of 186.8 and 145.3 and the average invested capital for 2024 of 1,118.5 USD m.

Solution

NOPAT falls from 140.1 to 109.0, a change of \(109.0/140.1 - 1 = -22.2\%\), using operating income of 145.3 at 25%. Average invested capital rises from 1,038.3 to 1,118.5, a change of \(1118.5/1038.3 - 1 = +7.7\%\). The ROIC ratio is \((1 - 0.222)/(1 + 0.077) = 0.722\), a fall of 27.8%, which equals \(0.0974/0.1349 - 1\). Most of the fall is in operating income. The capital base grew through the period, which lowered ROIC further. The lesson reports the generator's shape, not a finding about a firm.

E2 · The tax rate that would bring 2024 to 10% (SIMULATED)

At zero tax Lab 1 gives 0.1299 for 2024. At what tax rate would the 2024 ROIC fall to 10%, and what does that say about the sensitivity of the conclusion?

Solution

By Proposition 2.2, \(\tau = 1 - 0.10/0.12989 = 0.2301\), or 23.0%, using the unrounded 2024 zero-tax ROIC of 0.12989 (the lab prints 0.1299; the printed value would give 0.2302). The 2024 ROIC is above 10% for every rate below 23%, and the lab's grid shows 0.0974 at 25% and 0.0909 at 30%. The conclusion that the 2024 return is below 10% therefore depends on the rate: it holds for the 25% and 30% rows and fails for the 15% and 21% rows, which show 0.1104 and 0.1026. A claim about the level of ROIC needs the rate stated.

E3 · Invested-capital growth (SIMULATED)

From Lab 1's invested capital, compute the year-on-year growth of invested capital for 2022, 2023 and 2024. Say whether the capital base is growing faster or slower over the period.

Solution

From 906.9 to 993.5 is growth of \(993.5/906.9 - 1 = 9.5\%\). From 993.5 to 1,083.1 is \(9.0\%\). From 1,083.1 to 1,153.8 is \(6.5\%\). Growth slows each year, so the capital base grows more slowly over the period. With NOPAT fixed, slower capital growth raises ROIC, so the slowdown pushes the other way. The fall in ROIC in 2024 is driven by the operating-income decline (Problem E1), and the slower capital growth offsets part of it. The generator sets equity by retained earnings, so this path is an artefact of the simulation's rules.

Research

R1 · Persistence of ROIC

Specify a test of whether ROIC persists from one year to the next across many firms, using the A15 definition. Name the controls that the test needs, and state what result would show that the persistence comes from the denominator convention rather than from operations.

Solution

For each firm and year with a previous year, take ROIC at a fixed rate and the rank of that ROIC in its cross-section. Estimate the regression of this year's rank on last year's rank, with the rate and the cash convention fixed in every firm, and cluster by firm. Controls: the firm's invested-capital growth, because Proposition 3.4 shows that the denominator timing moves ROIC when capital grows, and the share of invested capital in goodwill, because Section 4 shows acquisitions enter the denominator. The denominator explains the persistence if the rank correlation falls once growth and goodwill are controlled. If the correlation survives both controls and also appears when the denominator is replaced by a fixed base, the denominator does not explain it, and the result supports an operating interpretation.

R2 · Adding operating leases

Describe the adjustment that adds operating lease liabilities to invested capital, state the NOPAT adjustment it requires, and list the data that the A15 table does not contain.

Solution

If lease liabilities \(L_t\) are treated as debt, invested capital becomes \(IC_t + L_t\). The operating profit must then add back the lease interest component, \(\mathrm{OI}_t + i\,L_t\), where \(i\) is the implied interest rate on leases, because the interest is now financing and should not reduce operating profit. NOPAT becomes \((\mathrm{OI}_t + i L_t)(1-\tau)\). The A15 table has none of \(L_t\), the lease interest or the implied rate \(i\), so the adjustment needs new tagged items, which are a data request and not a calculation on the current table. Until those items exist, the A15 ROIC cannot be adjusted for leases.

R3 · An operating-cash rule

Suppose a fraction \(s\) of cash is operating cash and must stay in invested capital, while the rest is excess cash and is netted. Write invested capital and ROIC as functions of \(s\), and say what value of \(s\) would reproduce the A15 convention.

Solution

With operating cash \(sC_t\) kept and excess cash \((1-s)C_t\) netted, invested capital is \(IC_t(s) = E_t + D_t - (1-s)C_t\). Its average and ROIC follow from Proposition 3.2 with \(C\) replaced by \((1-s)C\). The A15 convention nets all cash, so it corresponds to \(s = 0\). Because \(IC_t(s)\) rises linearly in \(s\), ROIC falls as \(s\) rises, and the whole range of \(s\) in \([0,1]\) gives a band of ROIC values. A study that reports ROIC over that band rather than at one value states its dependence on the cash rule explicitly.
