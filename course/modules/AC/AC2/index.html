Financial Statement Analysis & Earnings Quality

A reported profit is a number produced by accounting choices, and the choices leave traces in the statements. This module turns filed statements into standardized data, computes the ratios and returns an analyst reads, and measures how much of a reported figure is cash, how much is accrual, and how much may be managed. You leave able to standardize a filer's statements under explicit rules, to say which part of a ratio is operations and which part is financing, and to defend a quality score against its critics. The module ends in Atlas milestone A15, the fundamentals engine.

  • Prerequisites: AC1 Financial Accounting, S0 Exploratory Data Analysis & Visualization, S2 Linear Regression
  • Unlocks: the valuation engine (Atlas A16, CF4) and the modules that read reported statements
  • Project: Atlas A15: fundamentals engine, autograded

76 h lessons · 34 h project (30.9%)

Objectives

By the end of AC2 you can:

  1. Standardize a company's tagged facts into one row per fiscal year, applying the annual-observation rule, the as-of rule and tag priority per period end, and state which values a point-in-time analysis may use.
  2. Compute the ratio set on average and year-end balances, prove the DuPont identity, and decompose a change in return on equity into its three factors.
  3. Compute NOPAT, invested capital and return on invested capital under an explicit tax-rate and cash convention, and show how each convention moves the result.
  4. Separate cash-flow quality from accrual-based earnings, define operating accruals under two measures, and test whether the difference matters.
  5. Evaluate the accruals anomaly and discretionary-accrual models against their published evidence, and state where each one's assumptions fail.
  6. Implement the Piotroski F-score and the Beneish M-score from standardized statements, and argue what a score can and cannot tell an investment committee.

Prerequisites

AC2 assumes AC1 (Financial Accounting), S0 (Exploratory Data Analysis & Visualization) and S2 (Linear Regression), and everything they rest on; S2 carries S1 and M3. It uses these concepts without redefining them:

  • From AC1: double-entry bookkeeping, accrual accounting, the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash-flow statement, articulation of the three statements, depreciation and amortization, revenue recognition, lease accounting, stock-based compensation and deferred taxes.
  • From S2, because Lessons 5 and 6 estimate ordinary least squares regressions and read their t-statistics: the OLS estimator, and the t-statistic (defined in S1, which S2 carries).
  • From S0: robust summary statistics, outlier detection, missing-data mechanisms and stale prices.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: six on the prerequisites, four on AC1 and two on S0, and three skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. No placement item tests S2, which the prerequisites above also name: if the regressions in Lessons 5 and 6 are unfamiliar, review S2 directly. The mastery bar is 80%, which for nine items means eight correct, since seven is 78%. Skip rungs 1 and 2 only if you reach the bar and answer all three skill checks. Below the bar, revisit AC1 and S0 first.

  1. Which identity holds for every balance sheet?

    Options

    The balance sheet identity: total assets equal total liabilities plus equity (AC1).

  2. Revenue is 500 and cost of revenue is 300. What is the gross margin? (one decimal place is enough)

    \((500 - 300)/500 = 0.4\).

  3. Where does a year's net income appear on the balance sheet at the year end?

    Options

    Articulation: net income closes into retained earnings, which is part of equity.

  4. Why is depreciation added back to net income in operating cash flow?

    Options

    Depreciation is a non-cash charge in the income statement (AC1, depreciation and amortization).

  5. What is the median of 1, 2 and 100?

    The median is the middle value of the sorted sample, 2. The mean is 34.3, which the outlier moves (S0).

  6. When the chance of a value being missing depends on the value itself, the mechanism is called…

    Options

    MNAR: the missingness depends on the unobserved value (S0).

  7. Skill check (rung 1). A filer's FY2024 10-K is filed on 2025-02-15. A standardized table is built with a cut-off of 2025-01-31. Is the 2024 year end in the table?

    Options

    The as-of rule admits only filings with filed at or before the cut-off, and nothing is imputed (AC2, Lesson 1).

  8. Skill check (rung 2). A firm has net margin 0.10, asset turnover 0.50 and ROE 0.20, all on average balances. What is its leverage factor?

    By the DuPont identity, ROE = net margin × asset turnover × leverage, so leverage is \(0.20/(0.10 \times 0.50) = 4.0\) (AC2, Lesson 2).

  9. Skill check (rung 2). NOPAT is 150 and average invested capital is 1,000, in the same units. What is ROIC?

    \(150/1{,}000 = 0.15\) (AC2, Lesson 3).

Lessons

Eight lessons of six to twelve study hours each; the hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares. Every lesson has graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck. Lessons 1 to 3 cover standardization and the ratio core, and the rest cover earnings quality and the scores.

L01 · 9 hFilings as Data: XBRL Facts and StandardizationThe annual-observation rule, the as-of rule for point-in-time analysis, tag priority per period end, and loud failure on malformed input. L02 · 10 hRatio Analysis and the DuPont DecompositionScale invariance, average versus year-end denominators, the exact DuPont identity, the log decomposition of a change in ROE, and where ratios break. L03 · 8 hReturn on Invested CapitalNOPAT, invested capital under the A15 convention, the linear dependence of ROIC on the tax rate, and the cash and denominator conventions. L04 · 9 hEarnings and Cash-Flow Quality; Operating AccrualsWhat earnings quality means, two measures of operating accruals, and a generated panel on which both are computed. L05 · 8 hThe Accruals Anomaly (Sloan 1996)Accrual-sorted portfolios, the question Sloan (1996) asks, and a SIMULATED portfolio study with the honest-pending note for real data. L06 · 10 hEarnings Management and Discretionary AccrualsThe modified Jones model and the accrual-based models that Detecting Earnings Management (key dechow1995detecting) evaluates, and its estimation, and a planted manipulation that the model is tested to recover. L07 · 10 hThe Piotroski F-ScoreThe nine binary signals of Piotroski (2000), their construction from standardized statements, and their behaviour on generated firm-years. L08 · 12 hThe Beneish M-Score; Using the Scores TogetherThe eight indices and the M-score of Beneish (1999), the status of its published constants, and how the scores agree and disagree.

The project ladder

The module ends in Atlas A15, the fundamentals engine. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. A student skips them only by reaching the placement bar and answering all three skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated worked example of the as-of rule and tag priority for revenue on a small SIMULATED filer, with the reason for every rule at every step.

  2. Completion problem

    dupont, roic and an accrual measure with their key lines removed. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement the A15 library to the contract: the standardizer and the ratio and accrual functions, modified_jones, piotroski_f_score and beneish_m_score. Instances are generated from your workspace seed, and the suite grades your module against the filer's generated numbers for that instance. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended industry-adjusted quality screen with a stated hypothesis, built on the A15 library and tested on your own generated panel.
