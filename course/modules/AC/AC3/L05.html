Corporate executives do not remain passive observers during the fiscal quarter. By issuing voluntary earnings guidance, managers steer market expectations, mitigate securities class action litigation risk, and shape valuation multiples. In modern quantitative strategies, guidance surprises provide higher information density than backward-looking earnings releases.

1. Voluntary Forward-Looking Disclosure

While mandatory quarterly reports (Form 10-Q) and annual reports (Form 10-K) document historical accounting performance, corporate executives possess substantial private information regarding contemporaneous operations and future demand. Managers frequently communicate this information through voluntary forecasts.

Definition 5.1: Management Earnings Guidance

Management guidance is the voluntary, public disclosure by corporate executives conveying forward-looking expectations regarding upcoming financial performance (such as quarterly or annual revenue, operating margins, or earnings per share) in advance of mandatory SEC filings.

Guidance is typically issued as a point estimate (\(E_{i,t}^{\text{guide}} = \$1.25\)), a closed range (\(E_{i,t}^{\text{guide}} \in [\$1.20, \$1.30]\)), or an open-ended directional qualitative statement.

2. Asymmetric Timeliness and the Guidance Response Coefficient

A central empirical regularity in corporate disclosure is asymmetric timeliness: managers pre-announce negative earnings surprises ("bad news") substantially earlier and more frequently than positive surprises ("good news").

This asymmetry arises from structural litigation incentives under US securities law (SEC Rule 10b-5). If a company reports a catastrophic earnings shortfall on announcement day without prior warning, shareholders face large discrete losses, exposing directors and officers to securities fraud class actions. Pre-announcing adverse news diffuses the negative shock over time, establishing that management disclosed material events promptly.

Theorem 5.1: Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC) and Asymmetric Timeliness

Let total fundamental quarterly news be \(\Delta \equiv E_t - \E_{0}[E_t]\). Suppose managers face expected shareholder litigation penalties \(L(\Delta) = C_{\text{lit}} \cdot \max(0, -\Delta)\) if adverse news is withheld until official filing, but zero penalty for withholding favorable news. Then:

  1. The probability of voluntary guidance issuance is strictly higher for adverse innovations than for favorable innovations:
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(\text{Issue Guidance} \mid \Delta < 0) > \mathbb{P}(\text{Issue Guidance} \mid \Delta \ge 0) \end{aligned} \]
  1. The valuation sensitivity of stock prices to guidance innovations—the Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC)—strictly exceeds the conventional Earnings Response Coefficient (ERC):
\[ \begin{aligned} \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} \equiv \frac{\partial P}{\partial \Delta^{\text{guide}}} > \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} \equiv \frac{\partial P}{\partial \Delta^{\text{earnings}}} \end{aligned} \]

because guidance innovations alter long-run expected trajectory (\(\omega_{\text{guide}} > \omega_{\text{quarter}}\)) rather than reflecting transitory quarterly accrual noise.

Proof of Theorem 5.1

For Part 1, the manager's utility from withholding news \(\Delta\) is \(U(\text{silent}) = -L(\Delta) = -C_{\text{lit}} \max(0, -\Delta)\). When \(\Delta < 0\), withholding news incurs a strictly positive expected litigation penalty \(C_{\text{lit}} |\Delta| > 0\). Disclosing guidance eliminates this liability. Conversely, when \(\Delta \ge 0\), \(L(\Delta) = 0\), so litigation provides no incentive to disclose early. With independent idiosyncratic proprietary costs \(c \sim F(c)\), the manager discloses if and only if benefits exceed cost: for \(\Delta < 0\), disclosure occurs if \(C_{\text{lit}} |\Delta| > c\); for \(\Delta \ge 0\), disclosure occurs only if personal reputational benefits \(B > c\). When \(C_{\text{lit}} |\Delta| > B\), \(\mathbb{P}(\text{disclose} \mid \Delta < 0) > \mathbb{P}(\text{disclose} \mid \Delta \ge 0)\).

For Part 2, apply the Ohlson (1995) linear valuation formula from Theorem 3.1: \(P_t = y_t + \alpha_1 x_t^a + \alpha_2 v_t\), where \(\alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{1 + r - \omega}\). An earnings release provides a realization of historical net income \(x_t\), which contains both permanent operational cash flow and transitory accrual noise, yielding persistence \(\omega_{\text{earnings}}\). Management guidance provides an update to forward-looking operational expectations \(v_t\), which shifts multi-period future earnings streams. Under Theorem 3.1, \(\alpha_2 = \frac{1+r}{(1+r-\omega)(1+r-\gamma)} > \alpha_1\) whenever \(\gamma > 0\). Hence \(\beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} > \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}}\).

Remark 5.1: The Strategic Beat-and-Walk-Down Game

Management guidance interacts strategically with sell-side analyst consensus. Corporate executives face severe market penalties for missing quarterly analyst consensus. Consequently, managers engage in a sequential expectations management game:

  1. Early in the fiscal period, managers issue conservative guidance to damp down over-optimistic analyst expectations.
  2. Analysts systematically revise forecasts downward toward the guided target (the "walk-down").
  3. At official reporting date, reported EPS slightly beats the walked-down consensus (the "positive surprise beat"), triggering immediate positive announcement returns.

Example 5.1: GRC vs ERC in Event Studies

A firm has consensus expectations of $2.00 per share. Mid-quarter, management issues guidance of $1.80 per share (\(\Delta^{\text{guide}} = -\$0.20\)). At the guidance date, the stock experiences an abnormal return of \(-1.20\%\), implying \(\beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} = \frac{-0.0120}{-0.20} = 0.0600\).

Analysts revise consensus to $1.80. At the subsequent earnings announcement, reported EPS is $1.82 (\(\Delta^{\text{announcement}} = +\$0.02\)). The announcement abnormal return is \(+0.06\%\), implying \(\beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} = \frac{+0.0006}{+0.02} = 0.0300\).

The guidance response coefficient (\(0.0600\)) is exactly twice the earnings response coefficient (\(0.0300\)), reflecting the higher information persistence conveyed by forward-looking management guidance.

Lab 1: Guidance Response Coefficient & Asymmetric Timeliness

Hypothesis: Management guidance displays asymmetric timeliness with higher disclosure probability for bad news than good news, and the empirical Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC) strictly exceeds the official Earnings Response Coefficient (ERC).

"""AC3 lab L05: management earnings guidance and information conveyance.

SIMULATED: the management forecasts, consensus analyst expectations, actual earnings realizations,
and abnormal announcement returns are generated under an asymmetric voluntary disclosure model.
All calculations use standard library float and math. Deterministic seed; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random

SEED = 20261110
N_FIRMS = 60


def simulate_guidance_and_earnings(
    seed: int,
) -> tuple[list[float], list[float], list[float], list[float], list[bool]]:
    """Simulate guidance surprises, earnings surprises, abnormal returns, and guidance issuance indicators.

    Returns (guidance_surprises, earnings_surprises, car_guidance, car_announcement, issued_guidance).
    """
    rng = random.Random(seed)

    guidance_surprises: list[float] = []
    earnings_surprises: list[float] = []
    car_guidance: list[float] = []
    car_announcement: list[float] = []
    issued_guidance: list[bool] = []

    # GRC (guidance response coefficient) is typically larger than ERC (earnings response coefficient)
    # because guidance conveys forward-looking, persistent operational news.
    true_grc = 0.045
    true_erc = 0.025

    for _ in range(N_FIRMS):
        # Initial consensus expectation E_0 ~ N(2.0, 0.5)
        # Fundamental innovation total_news ~ N(0, 0.4)
        total_news = rng.gauss(0.0, 0.4)

        # Asymmetric guidance probability:
        # Bad news (total_news < -0.1) has 85% probability of early guidance (pre-announcement)
        # Good news (total_news > +0.1) has 50% probability of early guidance
        # Modest news has 30% probability
        if total_news < -0.1:
            p_issue = 0.85
        elif total_news > 0.1:
            p_issue = 0.50
        else:
            p_issue = 0.30

        issues = rng.random() < p_issue
        issued_guidance.append(issues)

        if issues:
            # Management guides ~ 75% of total news in mid-quarter guidance
            guide_news = 0.75 * total_news + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.05)
            rem_news = total_news - guide_news

            car_g = true_grc * guide_news + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.008)
            car_e = true_erc * rem_news + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.008)

            guidance_surprises.append(guide_news)
            earnings_surprises.append(rem_news)
            car_guidance.append(car_g)
            car_announcement.append(car_e)
        else:
            # No guidance: entire total_news lands at official announcement
            guidance_surprises.append(0.0)
            earnings_surprises.append(total_news)
            car_guidance.append(0.0)
            car_e = true_erc * total_news + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.010)
            car_announcement.append(car_e)

    return (
        guidance_surprises,
        earnings_surprises,
        car_guidance,
        car_announcement,
        issued_guidance,
    )


def ols_slope(x: list[float], y: list[float]) -> float:
    """Compute univariate OLS slope coefficient cov(x, y) / var(x)."""
    n = len(x)
    x_bar = sum(x) / n
    y_bar = sum(y) / n
    cov = sum((x[i] - x_bar) * (y[i] - y_bar) for i in range(n))
    var = sum((x[i] - x_bar) ** 2 for i in range(n))
    return cov / var if var > 0 else 0.0


def main() -> None:
    g_surp, e_surp, car_g, car_e, issued = simulate_guidance_and_earnings(SEED)

    # Filter to firms that issued guidance
    g_idxs = [i for i in range(N_FIRMS) if issued[i]]
    no_g_idxs = [i for i in range(N_FIRMS) if not issued[i]]

    # Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC)
    x_g = [g_surp[i] for i in g_idxs]
    y_g = [car_g[i] for i in g_idxs]
    grc = ols_slope(x_g, y_g)

    # Earnings Response Coefficient for guidance firms at official announcement
    x_eg = [e_surp[i] for i in g_idxs]
    y_eg = [car_e[i] for i in g_idxs]
    erc_g = ols_slope(x_eg, y_eg)

    # Earnings Response Coefficient for no-guidance firms
    x_no = [e_surp[i] for i in no_g_idxs]
    y_no = [car_e[i] for i in no_g_idxs]
    erc_no = ols_slope(x_no, y_no)

    print(f"seed {SEED} total_firms {N_FIRMS} firms_guiding {len(g_idxs)} firms_silent {len(no_g_idxs)}")
    print(f"guidance_response_coefficient_GRC: {grc:.4f}")
    print(f"earnings_response_coefficient_guided_firms: {erc_g:.4f}")
    print(f"earnings_response_coefficient_unguided_firms: {erc_no:.4f}")

    # Check that guidance response coefficient exceeds earnings response coefficient
    print(f"grc_exceeds_guided_erc: {grc > erc_g}")
    print(f"grc_exceeds_unguided_erc: {grc > erc_no}")

    # Check guidance asymmetry: fraction of bad-news firms guiding vs good-news firms guiding
    bad_news = [i for i in range(N_FIRMS) if (g_surp[i] + e_surp[i]) < -0.1]
    good_news = [i for i in range(N_FIRMS) if (g_surp[i] + e_surp[i]) > 0.1]

    pct_bad_guided = sum(1 for i in bad_news if issued[i]) / len(bad_news) if bad_news else 0.0
    pct_good_guided = sum(1 for i in good_news if issued[i]) / len(good_news) if good_news else 0.0

    print(f"bad_news_firms {len(bad_news)} pct_guiding {pct_bad_guided:.3f}")
    print(f"good_news_firms {len(good_news)} pct_guiding {pct_good_guided:.3f}")
    print(f"asymmetric_timeliness_observed: {pct_bad_guided > pct_good_guided}")


main()

4. Where the Canon Breaks

While management guidance provides powerful real-time signals, several institutional realities disrupt textbook disclosure models:

  • Regulation Fair Disclosure (Reg FD, 2000): Prior to Reg FD, corporate managers conducted private one-on-one conference calls with favored sell-side analysts to leak selective guidance. Reg FD mandated that all material non-public guidance must be disclosed simultaneously to the public, shifting guidance from private phone calls to Form 8-K filings and public press releases.
  • Guidance cessation and quarterly myopia: In response to criticisms that quarterly guidance encourages short-term managerial myopia (cutting R&D and maintenance to meet quarterly numbers), many large corporations (including Berkshire Hathaway and Unilever) completely eliminated quarterly earnings guidance.
  • Range and qualitative guidance ambiguity: Most modern guidance is issued as ranges rather than point forecasts. When managers issue wide ranges or adjust qualitative language (e.g. changing "robust demand" to "stable demand"), quantitative text-parsing models face classification ambiguity that simple linear surprise metrics miss.

5. Misconceptions

  • Managers issue guidance equally often regardless of whether upcoming earnings will exceed or miss market expectations.

    Asymmetric litigation risk creates powerful incentives to pre-announce negative surprises early to avoid shareholder fraud lawsuits. Empirical evidence demonstrates that bad news is pre-announced nearly twice as often as good news.

  • A firm that beats analyst consensus by $0.01 per share must have experienced an unexpected operational surge.

    Firms systematically manage expectations downward prior to earnings releases through conservative guidance (the beat-and-walk-down game). A 1-cent beat frequently represents successful expectations manipulation rather than operational outperformance.

  • Market reactions to management guidance are weaker than reactions to audited earnings releases because guidance is unaudited.

    Guidance Response Coefficients (GRC) are consistently higher than Earnings Response Coefficients (ERC). Forward-looking guidance provides information regarding persistent multi-period trajectory, whereas quarterly earnings contain substantial transitory accrual noise.

6. Problem Set

Mechanics Tier

Problem M.1. A firm with consensus EPS expectations of $2.50 issues pre-announcement guidance of $2.10. At guidance release, the stock experiences an abnormal return of \(-3.20\%\). Compute the empirical Guidance Response Coefficient \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{GRC}}\) with respect to dollar guidance surprise.

Worked Solution

Guidance surprise: \(\Delta^{\text{guide}} = 2.10 - 2.50 = -\$0.40\). The response coefficient is \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{GRC}} = \frac{AR}{\Delta^{\text{guide}}} = \frac{-0.0320}{-0.40} = 0.0800\) (or 8.00% per dollar of guidance surprise).

Problem M.2. Following Problem M.1, analysts revise their consensus forecast from $2.50 to $2.10. At the subsequent earnings announcement date, reported EPS is $2.12. If the empirical ERC is \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ERC}} = 0.0400\), compute the expected announcement abnormal return.

Worked Solution

Announcement surprise relative to walked-down consensus is \(\Delta = 2.12 - 2.10 = +\$0.02\). Expected announcement abnormal return is \(AR = \hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ERC}} \times \Delta = 0.0400 \times (+0.02) = +0.0008 = +0.08\%\).

Problem M.3. In a cross-sectional sample of 200 firms, 80 experience negative fundamental news (\(\Delta < 0\)) and 120 experience positive fundamental news (\(\Delta > 0\)). If 64 negative-news firms and 48 positive-news firms issue pre-announcement guidance, calculate the guidance issuance probability for each cohort.

Worked Solution

Negative news guidance rate: \(P(\text{guide} \mid \Delta < 0) = \frac{64}{80} = 0.800\) (80.0%). Positive news guidance rate: \(P(\text{guide} \mid \Delta > 0) = \frac{48}{120} = 0.400\) (40.0%). Bad news is pre-announced at twice the frequency of good news.

Proof Tier

Problem P.1. Let a manager face quadratic disclosure costs \(C(g) = \frac{c}{2} g^2\) and derive utility from stock price \(U = P - C(g)\), where price is set rationally by investors as \(P = \E[\theta \mid g]\). If the manager has private signal \(\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) and announces guidance \(g = \theta\), show that the optimal guidance policy is fully revealing when verification costs are negligible.

Worked Solution

When investors believe guidance is truthful, \(\E[\theta \mid g] = g\). The manager chooses announced message \(m\) given true signal \(\theta\) to maximize \(U(m \mid \theta) = m - \frac{c}{2} (m - \theta)^2\). The first-order condition with respect to \(m\) is \(1 - c(m - \theta) = 0 \implies m^* = \theta + \frac{1}{c}\). In equilibrium, rational investors anticipate this constant bias, adjusting their pricing rule to \(P(m) = m - \frac{1}{c} = \theta\). The resulting pricing incorporates full information revelation, matching the unravelling property.

Problem P.2. Prove that if managers successfully walk down analyst consensus from \(E_0\) to \(E_{\text{walk}}\) such that \(E_{\text{actual}} > E_{\text{walk}}\) but \(E_{\text{actual}} < E_0\), the cumulative abnormal return across the guidance window and announcement window \(\mathrm{CAR}[\text{guidance}] + \mathrm{CAR}[\text{announcement}]\) remains negative whenever \(\beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} \ge \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}}\).

Worked Solution

Let \(\Delta_g = E_{\text{walk}} - E_0 < 0\) and \(\Delta_e = E_{\text{actual}} - E_{\text{walk}} > 0\). Total fundamental news is \(\Delta_g + \Delta_e = E_{\text{actual}} - E_0 < 0\). Cumulative abnormal return is \(\mathrm{CAR}_{\text{total}} = \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} \Delta_g + \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} \Delta_e\). Since \(\beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} \ge \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} > 0\) and \(\Delta_g = (E_{\text{actual}} - E_0) - \Delta_e\), we have \(\mathrm{CAR}_{\text{total}} = \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}}(E_{\text{actual}} - E_0) + (\beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} - \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}})\Delta_e\). Because \(E_{\text{actual}} - E_0 < 0\), the first term is strictly negative; because \(\beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} \le \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}}\) and \(\Delta_e > 0\), the second term is non-positive (\(\le 0\)). Thus \(\mathrm{CAR}_{\text{total}} < 0\), proving that the positive announcement beat cannot offset the adverse guidance decline.

Problem P.3. Show that under SEC Rule 10b-5 litigation risk, a manager who pre-announces bad news reduces total damages by truncating the duration of the investor class period.

Worked Solution

In securities class action litigation under the fraud-on-the-market doctrine, damages are calculated as the artificial price inflation multiplied by the number of shares purchased by class members during the class period \([t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]\). If management stays silent until the scheduled quarterly report at \(t_{\text{scheduled}}\), the class period extends to \(t_{\text{scheduled}}\), accumulating daily share purchases \(V \times (t_{\text{scheduled}} - t_{\text{start}})\). By releasing guidance at earlier date \(t_{\text{guide}} < t_{\text{scheduled}}\), management immediately terminates the class period at \(t_{\text{guide}}\), eliminating liability for all shares traded between \(t_{\text{guide}}\) and \(t_{\text{scheduled}}\) and strictly truncating expected damages.

Empirical Tier

Problem E.1. In an event study of 120 management guidance releases, the regression of 3-day abnormal returns on standardized guidance surprises yields slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{GRC}} = 0.0520\) with standard error \(\mathrm{se} = 0.0080\). Test the null hypothesis \(H_0: \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} = 0\) against \(H_1: \beta_{\mathrm{GRC}} > 0\) at the 1% significance level.

Worked Solution

The t-statistic is \(t = \frac{\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{GRC}} - 0}{\mathrm{se}} = \frac{0.0520}{0.0080} = 6.50\). With \(n - 2 = 118\) degrees of freedom, the one-tailed critical value at the 1% level is \(z_{0.01} \approx 2.33\). Because \(t = 6.50 > 2.33\), the null hypothesis is rejected with \(p < 10^{-9}\), confirming statistically significant guidance response.

Problem E.2. A researcher examines quarterly consensus forecast revisions following management guidance. In 85% of cases, analysts revise their forecast in the exact direction of management guidance within 48 hours. What does this high revision frequency indicate about the informational dominance of management forecasts over analyst time-series models?

Worked Solution

The immediate 85% concordance confirms that sell-side analysts treat management guidance as private insider information with near-absolute credibility. Rather than maintaining independent forecasts based on external macroeconomic or channel-check indicators, analysts immediately update their priors to align with the guided range, confirming the information conveyance role of management guidance.

Problem E.3. Contrast the market reaction to a point guidance release (e.g. "$1.50 EPS") versus a range guidance release (e.g. "$1.45 to $1.55 EPS"). Why do range guidance releases exhibit lower abnormal return volatility at the guidance timestamp?

Worked Solution

A point guidance forecast conveys high precision and low uncertainty regarding the distribution of outcomes, concentrating price revision immediately at the point estimate. A range forecast reflects managerial parameter uncertainty, widening the posterior variance of expectations. Investors price range guidance using the midpoint while applying a higher discount for information ambiguity, moderating the immediate price jump.

Research Tier

Problem R.1. How did the implementation of Regulation Fair Disclosure (Reg FD) in 2000 alter the transmission channel of management earnings guidance to capital markets?

Worked Solution

Prior to Reg FD, corporate managers engaged in selective disclosure, tipping favored sell-side analysts and institutional clients during private conference calls. This generated substantial private information advantages and asymmetric pre-announcement trading drift. Reg FD legally mandated simultaneous public dissemination via Form 8-K filings and open webcasts, eliminating private insider tipping, leveling the informational playing field, and concentrating market price adjustment directly at public disclosure timestamps.

Problem R.2. Discuss the managerial trade-offs of guidance cessation. Why do corporate governance advocates often support discontinuing quarterly guidance?

Worked Solution

Issuing quarterly guidance incentivizes managerial myopia: executives face immense pressure to meet short-term 90-day targets, frequently leading them to postpone valuable long-term R&D investments, cut maintenance capital expenditures, or offer aggressive quarter-end customer discounts. Discontinuing quarterly guidance frees executive management to focus on multi-year strategic capital allocation and fundamental value creation rather than quarterly expectations management.

Problem R.3. How can quantitative natural language processing (NLP) models detect tone shifts and evasion in management earnings guidance calls that are not captured by quantitative EPS numbers?

Worked Solution

Managers constrained by legal safe harbor rules cannot falsify numerical guidance, but often adjust linguistic registers during guidance conference calls. Quantitative NLP measures such as sentiment polarity, lexical diversity, modal verb frequency ("might", "could" indicating hesitation), and evasion metrics (failure to answer direct analyst questions during Q&A) provide orthogonal predictive power for future operational deterioration before formal guidance cuts occur.

Retrieval Quiz

  1. What primary institutional mechanism drives the asymmetric timeliness of voluntary management earnings guidance?

    Options

    Managers face severe shareholder litigation risk if large negative surprises are reported without warning. Pre-announcing bad news early diffuses the negative shock and terminates the class period.

  2. If a $0.50 negative guidance surprise produces a -4.00% abnormal return, what is the empirical Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC)? (e.g. 0.08)

    \(\mathrm{GRC} = \frac{-0.04}{-0.50} = 0.08\).

  3. Why does the Guidance Response Coefficient (GRC) typically exceed the Earnings Response Coefficient (ERC)?

    Options

    Management guidance updates the market's assessment of multi-period future operational profitability, whereas quarterly historical reports contain temporary accrual fluctuations with lower persistence.

  4. What was the major regulatory effect of SEC Regulation Fair Disclosure (Reg FD, 2000)?

    Options

    Reg FD eliminated selective disclosure calls between corporate executives and favored Wall Street analysts, mandating that material guidance be disclosed publicly and simultaneously.

  5. What characterizes the strategic "beat-and-walk-down" game in corporate financial reporting?

    Options

    Managers manage analyst expectations downward to achievable levels so the firm can report a positive earnings surprise on announcement day.

  6. In a cohort of 50 bad-news firms, 40 issue early guidance. What is the bad-news guidance percentage? (e.g. 80.00)

    \(40 / 50 \times 100\% = 80.00\%\).

  7. Why do corporate governance reformers criticize quarterly earnings guidance?

    Options

    Quarterly guidance pressures executives to cut R&D, reduce maintenance, and manipulate accruals to meet 90-day targets at the expense of long-term corporate health.

  8. How does range guidance differ from point guidance in market price impact?

    Options

    Range guidance acknowledges uncertainty about future outcomes, dampening the immediate point revision compared to an exact point estimate.
