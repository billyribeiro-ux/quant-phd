Not all earnings surprises move stock prices equally. A one-dollar unexpected earnings shock generates price revisions ranging from zero for transitory items to over ten dollars for durable operational innovations. The Earnings Response Coefficient formalizes this valuation multiplier, bridging financial reporting quality with quantitative cross-sectional asset pricing.

1. The Earnings Response Coefficient Paradigm

In L01 and L02, we established that unexpected earnings innovations convey incremental information content. However, the magnitude of the market's price reaction per unit of unexpected earnings varies dramatically across firms and through time. The standard cross-sectional event-study regression models abnormal return as a linear function of scaled unexpected earnings:

\[ \begin{aligned} AR_{i,t} = \alpha + \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} \cdot UE_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t} \end{aligned} \]

The regression slope \(\beta_{\mathrm{ERC}}\) is termed the earnings response coefficient (ERC). It quantifies the dollar revision in equity value generated by a one-dollar innovation in unexpected accounting income.

Definition 3.1: Earnings Response Coefficient (ERC)

Let \(P_{i,t}\) denote the equity market capitalization of firm \(i\) at announcement time \(t\), and let \(UE_{i,t} = E_{i,t} - \E[E_{i,t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\) denote unexpected net income. The earnings response coefficient \(\beta_{\mathrm{ERC}}\) is the marginal valuation multiplier:

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta_{\mathrm{ERC}} \equiv \frac{\partial P_{i,t}}{\partial UE_{i,t}} = \frac{\Cov(AR_{i,t}, UE_{i,t})}{\Var(UE_{i,t})} \end{aligned} \]

where unexpected earnings \(UE_{i,t}\) are deflated by beginning-of-period market value or standard deviation.

2. The Ohlson (1995) Linear Information Dynamics

Prior to Ohlson (1995), empirical accounting studies lacked a rigorous micro-founded model connecting accounting accruals and earnings persistence directly to equity valuation multiples. Ohlson integrated the clean surplus relation with linear autoregressive information dynamics.

Let \(y_t\) denote the book value of equity at date \(t\), \(x_t\) denote net income for period \(t\), and \(d_t\) denote net dividends paid at date \(t\). The clean surplus relation requires that changes in book value equal net income minus net distributions to shareholders: \(y_t = y_{t-1} + x_t - d_t\). Define abnormal earnings (also termed residual income) as earnings in excess of the cost of equity capital applied to opening book value:

\[ \begin{aligned} x_t^a \equiv x_t - r \cdot y_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

where \(r > 0\) is the constant risk-adjusted discount rate.

Theorem 3.1: Ohlson (1995) Valuation and ERC Multiplier

Assume that clean surplus accounting holds and that the firm's abnormal earnings \(x_t^a\) and non-accounting information \(v_t\) satisfy the linear information dynamics (LID):

\[ \begin{aligned} x_{t+1}^a &= \omega x_t^a + v_t + \varepsilon_{1,t+1} \\ v_{t+1} &= \gamma v_t + \varepsilon_{2,t+1} \end{aligned} \]

where \(0 \le \omega < 1 + r\), \(0 \le \gamma < 1 + r\), and innovations \(\varepsilon_{1,t+1}, \varepsilon_{2,t+1}\) are mean-zero white noise orthogonal to history \(\mathcal{F}_t\). If the transversality condition \(\lim_{\tau \to \infty} (1+r)^{-\tau} \E_t[y_{t+\tau}] = 0\) holds, then the equity market value \(P_t\) admits the unique closed-form representation:

\[ \begin{aligned} P_t = y_t + \alpha_1 x_t^a + \alpha_2 v_t \end{aligned} \]

where the abnormal earnings valuation multiplier (ERC) \(\alpha_1\) and non-accounting information multiplier \(\alpha_2\) are given by:

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{1 + r - \omega}, \qquad \alpha_2 = \frac{1 + r}{(1 + r - \omega)(1 + r - \gamma)} \end{aligned} \]

Proof of Theorem 3.1

Under the clean surplus relation and absence of speculative bubbles, the market value of equity equals book value plus the present value of expected future abnormal earnings:

\[ \begin{aligned} P_t = y_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} (1 + r)^{-\tau} \E_t[x_{t+\tau}^a] \end{aligned} \]

From the linear information dynamics, the conditional expectation of \(v_{t+\tau}\) is \(\E_t[v_{t+\tau}] = \gamma^\tau v_t\). Iterating the abnormal earnings dynamics forward yields:

\[ \begin{aligned} \E_t[x_{t+\tau}^a] = \omega^\tau x_t^a + \sum_{k=0}^{\tau-1} \omega^{\tau-1-k} \gamma^k v_t \end{aligned} \]

Discounting the first component involving \(x_t^a\) across all \(\tau \ge 1\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} (1 + r)^{-\tau} \omega^\tau x_t^a = \frac{\frac{\omega}{1+r}}{1 - \frac{\omega}{1+r}} x_t^a = \frac{\omega}{1 + r - \omega} x_t^a = \alpha_1 x_t^a \end{aligned} \]

Discounting the double sum for the second component involving \(v_t\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{\tau=1}^\infty (1+r)^{-\tau} \sum_{k=0}^{\tau-1} \omega^{\tau-1-k} \gamma^k &= \frac{1}{\omega - \gamma} \sum_{\tau=1}^\infty (1+r)^{-\tau} (\omega^\tau - \gamma^\tau) \\ &= \frac{1}{\omega - \gamma} \left( \frac{\omega}{1+r-\omega} - \frac{\gamma}{1+r-\gamma} \right) \\ &= \frac{1+r}{(1+r-\omega)(1+r-\gamma)} = \alpha_2 \end{aligned} \]

Summing both terms produces \(P_t = y_t + \alpha_1 x_t^a + \alpha_2 v_t\), completing the proof.

Remark 3.1: Comparative Statics of ERC

Theorem 3.1 provides immediate analytical comparative statics for the determinants of the earnings response coefficient \(\alpha_1\):

  • Earnings persistence (\(\omega\)): \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial \omega} = \frac{1 + r}{(1 + r - \omega)^2} > 0\). Highly persistent earnings generate substantially larger valuation multiples. Transitory earnings (\(\omega = 0\)) imply \(\alpha_1 = 0\), meaning abnormal earnings add zero capitalized future value.
  • Cost of capital (\(r\)): \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial r} = -\frac{\omega}{(1 + r - \omega)^2} < 0\). Higher discount rates suppress valuation multiples.
  • Accrual quality: As shown by Sloan (1996), earnings driven by high operating accruals exhibit lower persistence \(\omega_{\text{accruals}} < \omega_{\text{cash}}\), resulting in fundamentally lower ERCs.

Example 3.1: Computing Valuation Multipliers

Consider a firm with book value of equity \(y_t = 100\), abnormal earnings \(x_t^a = 15\), other information \(v_t = 5\), cost of capital \(r = 0.10\), other information persistence \(\gamma = 0.40\), and earnings persistence \(\omega = 0.60\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha_1 &= \frac{0.60}{1 + 0.10 - 0.60} = \frac{0.60}{0.50} = 1.2000 \\ \alpha_2 &= \frac{1.10}{(1.10 - 0.60)(1.10 - 0.40)} = \frac{1.10}{0.50 \times 0.70} = \frac{1.10}{0.35} \approx 3.1429 \\ P_t &= 100 + 1.2000 \times 15 + 3.1429 \times 5 = 100 + 18.00 + 15.714 = 133.714 \end{aligned} \]

A $1.00 shock to current abnormal earnings revisions equity value by \(\alpha_1 = \$1.20\).

"""AC3 lab L03: Ohlson (1995) residual income valuation and earnings response coefficient.

SIMULATED: the valuation parameters, linear information dynamics coefficients, and firm
abnormal earnings paths are generated under the Ohlson (1995) framework. All calculations
use standard library float and math. No network; deterministic seed.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

SEED = 20261110


def ohlson_closed_form(
    book_value: float,
    abnormal_earnings: float,
    other_info: float,
    omega: float,
    gamma: float,
    cost_of_capital: float,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Compute Ohlson (1995) closed-form price and valuation multipliers alpha_1, alpha_2.

    Returns (price, alpha_1, alpha_2).
    """
    r = cost_of_capital
    denom_1 = 1.0 + r - omega
    alpha_1 = omega / denom_1
    alpha_2 = (1.0 + r) / (denom_1 * (1.0 + r - gamma))
    price = book_value + alpha_1 * abnormal_earnings + alpha_2 * other_info
    return price, alpha_1, alpha_2


def ohlson_discounted_sum(
    book_value: float,
    abnormal_earnings: float,
    other_info: float,
    omega: float,
    gamma: float,
    cost_of_capital: float,
    horizon: int = 500,
) -> float:
    """Compute price by summing expected discounted abnormal earnings over horizon H.

    E_t[x_{t+tau}^a] is derived from the linear information dynamics:
    v_{t+k} = gamma^k * v_t
    x_{t+tau}^a = omega^tau * x_t^a + sum_{k=0}^{tau-1} omega^{tau-1-k} * gamma^k * v_t.
    """
    r = cost_of_capital
    cum_pv = 0.0

    # Current state expectations
    # E_t[x_{t+k}^a] and E_t[v_{t+k}]
    e_x = abnormal_earnings
    e_v = other_info

    for tau in range(1, horizon + 1):
        # Step forward one period:
        # E_t[x_{t+tau}^a] = omega * E_t[x_{t+tau-1}^a] + E_t[v_{t+tau-1}]
        next_x = omega * e_x + e_v
        next_v = gamma * e_v
        e_x = next_x
        e_v = next_v

        discount = (1.0 + r) ** (-tau)
        cum_pv += discount * e_x

    return book_value + cum_pv

4. Where the Canon Breaks

While the Ohlson (1995) linear information dynamics framework provides an elegant, closed-form foundation, several theoretical and empirical assumptions break down in real-world corporate finance:

  • Conservative accounting bias: Ohlson assumes unbiased accounting where expected long-run market-to-book equals 1.0. In reality, accounting conservatism (such as expensing R&D and advertising immediately) creates persistent unrecorded intangibles, causing market-to-book ratios to remain systematically greater than unity. Feltham & Ohlson (1995) extended the model to accommodate conservative operating assets.
  • Non-linear payoff structures and growth options: Linear information dynamics assume that abnormal earnings innovations are strictly linear. Real options to expand, abandon, or liquidate introduce convex payoff profiles that linear regression specifications under-estimate.
  • Loss asymmetry: Firms reporting operating losses exhibit ERCs close to zero because equity holders possess an abandonment option (the limited liability put option), decoupling current negative earnings from future terminal value.

5. Misconceptions

  • The earnings response coefficient is a constant universal parameter equal across all firms in an industry.

    ERC varies cross-sectionally based on firm-specific earnings persistence \(\omega\), risk-adjusted cost of capital \(r\), growth opportunities, and financial leverage. A tech firm with persistent recurring software revenues has an ERC several times larger than a cyclical commodity producer with transitory windfalls.

  • A transitory earnings shock (\(\omega = 0\)) increases firm value by the full capitalized amount of the shock.

    Under Theorem 3.1, when \(\omega = 0\), the multiplier \(\alpha_1 = 0\). A transitory shock adds only its immediate cash flow realization to current book equity \(y_t\) but adds zero capitalized stream of future abnormal profits.

  • Higher debt leverage increases the earnings response coefficient because debt magnifies returns.

    High financial leverage reduces the equity ERC because debt claims absorb a substantial portion of the firm's cash flow risk. In highly levered firms, unexpected earnings shocks transfer value between debt and equity holders rather than translating directly into equity price appreciation.

6. Problem Set

Mechanics Tier

Problem M.1. A firm has cost of capital \(r = 0.12\) and abnormal earnings persistence parameter \(\omega = 0.70\). Non-accounting information \(v_t\) has persistence \(\gamma = 0.30\). Compute the valuation multipliers \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_2\) under Theorem 3.1.

Worked Solution

Under Theorem 3.1: \(\alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{1 + r - \omega} = \frac{0.70}{1 + 0.12 - 0.70} = \frac{0.70}{0.42} \approx 1.6667\). The second multiplier is \(\alpha_2 = \frac{1 + r}{(1 + r - \omega)(1 + r - \gamma)} = \frac{1.12}{(0.42)(1.12 - 0.30)} = \frac{1.12}{0.42 \times 0.82} = \frac{1.12}{0.3444} \approx 3.2520\).

Problem M.2. A firm has book value of equity \(y_0 = \$50.00\), reported net income \(x_1 = \$8.00\), and cost of equity capital \(r = 10\%\). Compute the firm's abnormal earnings (residual income) \(x_1^a\) for period 1.

Worked Solution

Normal return on opening equity is \(r \cdot y_0 = 0.10 \times 50.00 = \$5.00\). Abnormal earnings is net income in excess of normal return: \(x_1^a = x_1 - r \cdot y_0 = 8.00 - 5.00 = +\$3.00\).

Problem M.3. In an event study regression \(AR_i = \alpha + \beta UE_i + \varepsilon_i\), the sample covariance between abnormal returns and unexpected earnings is \(\Cov(AR, UE) = 0.048\), and the variance of unexpected earnings is \(\Var(UE) = 0.012\). Compute the empirical ERC \(\hat{\beta}\).

Worked Solution

The OLS slope estimator is \(\hat{\beta} = \frac{\Cov(AR, UE)}{\Var(UE)} = \frac{0.048}{0.012} = 4.00\). An unexpected earnings surprise of 1% is associated with a 4.00% abnormal equity return.

Proof Tier

Problem P.1. Prove that \(\alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{1 + r - \omega}\) is strictly increasing in earnings persistence \(\omega\) for all \(\omega \in [0, 1+r)\) and strictly decreasing in the cost of capital \(r > 0\).

Worked Solution

Differentiating \(\alpha_1\) with respect to \(\omega\): \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial \omega} = \frac{1 \cdot (1 + r - \omega) - \omega (-1)}{(1 + r - \omega)^2} = \frac{1 + r}{(1 + r - \omega)^2}\). Since \(r > 0\), the numerator \(1 + r > 1 > 0\) and the denominator is strictly positive, hence \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial \omega} > 0\). Differentiating with respect to \(r\): \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial r} = -\frac{\omega}{(1 + r - \omega)^2}\). For any \(\omega > 0\), this derivative is strictly negative (\(< 0\)), proving monotonicity in both arguments.

Problem P.2. Prove that under the clean surplus relation \(y_t = y_{t-1} + x_t - d_t\) and the transversality condition \(\lim_{\tau \to \infty} (1+r)^{-\tau} \E_t[y_{t+\tau}] = 0\), the dividend discount model \(P_t = \sum_{\tau=1}^\infty (1+r)^{-\tau} \E_t[d_{t+\tau}]\) is mathematically identical to the residual income model \(P_t = y_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^\infty (1+r)^{-\tau} \E_t[x_{t+\tau}^a]\).

Worked Solution

From clean surplus, \(d_t = x_t - (y_t - y_{t-1})\). Substitute \(x_t = x_t^a + r y_{t-1}\) into the dividend expression: \(d_t = x_t^a + (1 + r) y_{t-1} - y_t\). Now discount and sum across \(\tau=1\) to \(T\): \(\sum_{\tau=1}^T (1+r)^{-\tau} d_{t+\tau} = \sum_{\tau=1}^T (1+r)^{-\tau} x_{t+\tau}^a + \sum_{\tau=1}^T (1+r)^{-\tau} [(1+r)y_{t+\tau-1} - y_{t+\tau}]\). The second summation telescopes: \(\sum_{\tau=1}^T [(1+r)^{-(\tau-1)} y_{t+\tau-1} - (1+r)^{-\tau} y_{t+\tau}] = y_t - (1+r)^{-T} y_{t+T}\). Taking conditional expectations and letting \(T \to \infty\), the terminal book value vanishes by transversality (\(\lim_{T \to \infty} (1+r)^{-T} \E_t[y_{t+T}] = 0\)). We obtain \(P_t = y_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^\infty (1+r)^{-\tau} \E_t[x_{t+\tau}^a]\).

Problem P.3. Show that when earnings follow a pure random walk (\(x_{t+1} = x_t + \varepsilon_{t+1}\)) and all earnings are paid as dividends (\(d_t = x_t\), so \(y_t = y_{t-1} = y_0\)), abnormal earnings persistence \(\omega = 1\), and the valuation model simplifies to Gordon-like capitalization \(P_t = x_t / r\).

Worked Solution

When \(y_t = y_0\) is constant, abnormal earnings is \(x_t^a = x_t - r y_0\). Since \(x_t\) follows a random walk, \(\E_t[x_{t+\tau}^a] = \E_t[x_{t+\tau} - r y_0] = x_t - r y_0 = x_t^a\), meaning \(\omega = 1\). Under \(\omega = 1\), \(\alpha_1 = \frac{1}{1 + r - 1} = \frac{1}{r}\). Hence \(P_t = y_0 + \alpha_1 x_t^a = y_0 + \frac{1}{r} (x_t - r y_0) = y_0 + \frac{x_t}{r} - y_0 = \frac{x_t}{r}\). The market price is exactly the capitalized earnings stream.

Empirical Tier

Problem E.1. A quantitative researcher estimates ERC separately for low-accrual and high-accrual portfolios. The low-accrual portfolio has estimated persistence \(\hat{\omega}_{\text{low}} = 0.85\), while the high-accrual portfolio has \(\hat{\omega}_{\text{high}} = 0.45\). Assuming cost of capital \(r = 0.08\), calculate the theoretical ERC ratio \(\alpha_{1,\text{low}} / \alpha_{1,\text{high}}\) and interpret the result in the context of Sloan (1996).

Worked Solution

\(\alpha_{1,\text{low}} = \frac{0.85}{1 + 0.08 - 0.85} = \frac{0.85}{0.23} \approx 3.6957\). \(\alpha_{1,\text{high}} = \frac{0.45}{1 + 0.08 - 0.45} = \frac{0.45}{0.63} \approx 0.7143\). The ratio is \(\frac{3.6957}{0.7143} \approx 5.17\). Cash-flow-dominated earnings are more than 5 times more valuable per dollar of unexpected surprise because operating accruals rapidly reverse, making high-accrual earnings substantially less persistent as established by Sloan (1996).

Problem E.2. In a cross-sectional regression of announcement returns on unexpected earnings, a researcher omits non-accounting information \(v_t\). Under what condition will the estimated OLS slope \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ERC}}\) be upward-biased relative to the true parameter \(\alpha_1\)?

Worked Solution

Under omitted variable bias, \(\E[\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ERC}}] = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 \frac{\Cov(UE, v)}{\Var(UE)}\). Since Theorem 3.1 establishes that \(\alpha_2 > 0\), the estimated ERC will be upward-biased (\(\E[\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ERC}}] > \alpha_1\)) if and only if unexpected earnings and non-accounting information are positively correlated (\(\Cov(UE, v) > 0\)), such as when managers release positive qualitative press releases concurrently with positive earnings beats.

Problem E.3. A corporate event study splits firms into positive-earnings-surprise and negative-earnings-surprise cohorts. Explain why empirical ERC estimates for the loss cohort are systematically attenuated toward zero compared to the profit cohort.

Worked Solution

Equity holders possess a liquidation and abandonment option: if severe operating losses persist, the firm can enter bankruptcy or liquidate assets, capping downside losses at the equity investment. This option convexity makes future equity value insensitive to further negative operating losses. Consequently, the market prices loss announcements as transitory distress signals rather than permanent declines in perpetuity, attenuating the loss ERC.

Research Tier

Problem R.1. How does conservative accounting (e.g., immediate expensing of intangible investments like software and R&D) violate the unbiased clean surplus assumption of Ohlson (1995), and what systematic pattern in market-to-book and future abnormal earnings does it generate?

Worked Solution

Immediate expensing of internally generated intangibles suppresses opening book value \(y_t\) below economic replacement cost. Consequently, unrecorded intangible capital continues to generate operating profits in subsequent periods without being penalized by a capital charge on book assets. This generates persistent positive abnormal earnings (\(\E[x_{t+\tau}^a] > 0\)) and an equilibrium market-to-book ratio permanently exceeding 1.0, requiring the Feltham-Ohlson (1995) conservatism parameter extension.

Problem R.2. Why does the empirical estimate of ERC decline over historical decades in US capital markets, and what role do shifting macroeconomic interest rates and intangible asset intensity play?

Worked Solution

Over recent decades, corporate balance sheets have shifted from tangible, physical capital to intangible, IP-driven capital, where reported GAAP net income has become more volatile and less representative of underlying cash generation due to irregular write-offs, restructuring charges, and share-based compensation. Furthermore, rising dispersion in firm-specific growth options and market sentiment has lowered the signal-to-noise ratio of quarterly accounting earnings, driving empirical ERCs downward.

Problem R.3. Contrast the clean surplus model of Ohlson (1995) with the Modigliani-Miller (1961) dividend irrelevance proposition. Does Ohlson (1995) require dividend irrelevance?

Worked Solution

Yes. In Ohlson (1995), dividend policy is assumed to be value-irrelevant (consistent with Miller & Modigliani 1961). Specifically, dividends \(d_t\) reduce current book value \(y_t\) dollar-for-dollar without altering the stochastic process generating future abnormal earnings. If dividend policy conveyed private signaling information regarding future operational viability, the linear information dynamics would require dividends to enter the \(v_t\) state vector directly.

  3. How does an increase in the firm's cost of equity capital \(r\) affect the earnings response coefficient \(\alpha_1\)?

    Options

    Differentiating \(\alpha_1\) with respect to \(r\) yields \(\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial r} = -\frac{\omega}{(1 + r - \omega)^2} < 0\). Higher discount rates diminish the present value of future abnormal earnings streams.

  4. What empirical finding regarding accounting accruals and earnings persistence was established by Sloan (1996)?

    Options

    Sloan (1996) showed that high-accrual earnings exhibit lower persistence (\(\omega_{\text{accruals}} < \omega_{\text{cash}}\)) because accruals are subject to subjective estimation and aggressive recognition that inevitably reverse.

  5. Why do loss-reporting firms exhibit empirical ERCs close to zero?

    Options

    Equity holders' limited liability gives them an abandonment option. Severe losses do not perpetuate indefinitely into the future; if losses persist, the firm liquidates or reorganizes, capping the downside.

  7. What does the clean surplus accounting relation state?

    Options

    The clean surplus relation mandates that all items affecting equity book value must flow through the income statement, except capital transactions with shareholders (dividends and equity issuances/repurchases).

  8. How does higher financial debt leverage affect the equity ERC?

    Options

    In highly levered firms, debt holders absorb a portion of operational cash flow volatility, dampening the transmission of unexpected earnings shocks to equity prices.
