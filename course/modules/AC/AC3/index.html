Module AC3 · Tier M · 60 hours · Part AC, Accounting

Earnings, Disclosure & Market Reaction

Accounting net income is not cash, yet its quarterly publication moves billions in capital within seconds. This module uncovers how capital markets price corporate disclosures, why price revisions often linger for months after an announcement, how management strategically guides expectations, and when voluntary communication unspools or unravels in competitive equilibrium. You leave able to construct standardized unexpected earnings from filings, model the valuation multipliers of abnormal earnings, test the limits of post-earnings drift net of execution costs, and defend disclosure mechanisms against institutional critics.

  • Prerequisites: AC2 Financial Statement Analysis, S6 Causal Inference & Event Studies
  • Unlocks: advanced asset pricing, corporate finance valuation engines, and quantitative equity anomalies
  • Project: Post-earnings-announcement drift replication, autograded engine and referee note (18 h)

42 h lessons · 18 h project (30.0%)

Objectives

By the end of AC3 you can:

  1. Measure the information content of earnings announcements using high-resolution event studies, separating pre-announcement anticipation from announcement-window price discovery.
  2. Estimate time-series expectations of quarterly earnings under seasonal random walk models with drift, and construct standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) normalized by forecast error volatility.
  3. Derive equity valuation from the Ohlson (1995) clean surplus relation and linear information dynamics, and evaluate the cross-sectional determinants of the earnings response coefficient (ERC).
  4. Test post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) across SUE deciles, examine the Bernard & Thomas (1989) autocorrelation structure of quarterly earnings innovations, and assess post-2000 decay net of execution costs.
  5. Analyze management earnings guidance, measuring the asymmetric timeliness of bad-news pre-announcements versus good-news guidance and the expectation walk-down.
  6. Prove the unraveling result of voluntary disclosure under verifiable information, and determine equilibrium disclosure thresholds under proprietary costs and information uncertainty.

Placement diagnostic

Ten questions: eight testing prerequisite mastery across AC2, S6, AC1, and S2, and two skill checks on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery threshold is 80% (at least 8 correct out of 10). Skip rungs 1 and 2 only if you reach the mastery threshold and answer both skill checks correctly.

  1. Skill check 1 (Rungs 1–2). A firm has historical seasonal earnings changes \(\Delta E_{i,t} = E_{i,t} - E_{i,t-4}\) over the past four quarters of 2.0, 2.5, 1.5, and 2.0 dollars per share. For the current quarter \(T\), actual earnings are 14.0 dollars per share, while earnings four quarters ago \(E_{i,T-4}\) were 11.0 dollars. Historical forecast errors around the seasonal drift have standard deviation \(\hat{\sigma} = 0.50\) dollars. What is the firm's Standardized Unexpected Earnings (SUE)?

    Options

    Average seasonal drift \(\hat{\delta} = (2.0 + 2.5 + 1.5 + 2.0)/4 = 2.0\). Expected earnings \(\hat{E} = 11.0 + 2.0 = 13.0\). Forecast error \(UE = 14.0 - 13.0 = 1.0\). Standardized surprise \(\mathrm{SUE} = 1.0 / 0.50 = 2.00\).

  2. Skill check 2 (Rungs 1–2). A long-short PEAD strategy goes long Decile 10 (average post-announcement CAR of +4.20%) and short Decile 1 (average post-announcement CAR of -2.80%). If trading execution costs are 25 basis points per leg (round-trip), what is the net-of-cost hedge spread?

    Options

    Gross spread \(4.20\% - (-2.80\%) = 7.00\%\). Total transaction cost across both long and short legs is \(2 \times 25\text{ bps} = 50\text{ bps} = 0.50\%\). Net hedge spread is \(7.00\% - 0.50\% = 6.50\%\).

  3. Under an event-study framework (S6), why is the estimation window separated by a buffer from the event window (e.g., using \([-120, -21]\) for an event at day 0)?

    Options

    If the estimation window overlaps with the event or pre-event leakage period, event-related return variance and price movements distort the benchmark \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) estimates.

  4. A firm's stock has market model parameters \(\alpha = 0.00\) and \(\beta = 1.20\). On the earnings announcement day, the market return is +1.00% and the firm's stock return is +2.00%. What is the abnormal return in percent? (give the decimal percent, e.g. 0.80)

    Expected return \(\alpha + \beta R_m = 0.00 + 1.20 \times 1.00\% = 1.20\%\). Abnormal return \(AR = 2.00\% - 1.20\% = 0.80\%\).

  5. Which empirical finding regarding earnings quality is established by Sloan (1996) in AC2?

    Options

    Sloan (1996) showed that earnings driven by high accruals revert faster than earnings driven by cash flows, leading to subsequent negative abnormal returns for high-accrual firms.

  6. Under the clean surplus relation in accounting (AC1/AC2), how is ending book value of equity \(B_t\) related to beginning book value \(B_{t-1}\), net income \(X_t\), and net dividends \(d_t\)?

    Options

    Clean surplus requires all gains and losses affecting book value to pass through the income statement: ending equity equals beginning equity plus profit minus distributions.

  7. In linear regression analysis (S2), what does an omitted variable correlated with unexpected earnings cause?

    Options

    Omitted variable bias occurs whenever an excluded determinant of stock returns is correlated with the regressor (unexpected earnings), violating strict exogeneity.

  8. Why does scaling quarterly unexpected earnings by stock price \(P_{i,t-1}\) introduce potential distortion compared to standardizing by forecast error volatility \(\sigma(UE_i)\)?

    Options

    Deflating by price conflates high earnings variance with low valuation multiples (distressed/value stocks with low price appear to have artificially large surprises).

  9. In an OLS regression with 100 observations, the estimated slope \(\hat{\beta} = 1.50\) has standard error \(\mathrm{se}(\hat{\beta}) = 0.50\). What is the t-statistic for the null hypothesis \(\beta = 0\)?

    \(t = (\hat{\beta} - 0) / \mathrm{se}(\hat{\beta}) = 1.50 / 0.50 = 3.00\).

  10. When an event study evaluates cumulative abnormal returns across multiple event days \([t_1, t_2]\), what property must hold under no autocorrelation?

    Options

    Under independent daily abnormal returns, the variance of the sum equals the sum of the variances.

Lesson map

Six lessons, 7 study hours each, totaling 42 hours:

  1. L01 · Information Content of Earnings & Seminal Event Studies

    The empirical revolution of Ball & Brown (1968): methodology of event studies applied to corporate profit disclosures, abnormal performance indices, and price discovery. 7 h.

  2. L02 · Standardized Unexpected Earnings (SUE) & Time-Series Models

    Quarterly time-series models of earnings, seasonal random walk with drift, forecast error volatility normalization, and consensus analyst surprises. 7 h.

  3. L03 · Earnings Response Coefficient (ERC) & Valuation Multiples

    The Ohlson (1995) clean surplus framework, linear information dynamics, abnormal earnings capitalization, and cross-sectional determinants of ERC. 7 h.

  4. L04 · Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift (PEAD) & Delayed Price Response

    The Bernard & Thomas (1989) delayed price response anomaly, seasonal autocorrelation of forecast errors, quarterly reversal at lag 4, and modern transaction cost decay. 7 h.

  5. L05 · Management Earnings Guidance & Information Conveyance

    Voluntary forward-looking disclosures, asymmetric guidance timeliness, the beat-and-walk-down phenomenon, and the guidance response coefficient. 7 h.

  6. L06 · Voluntary Disclosure Theory & Strategic Market Communication

    Milgrom-Grossman unravelling theorems, breakdown under proprietary costs (Verrecchia 1983) and information uncertainty (Dye 1985 / Jung-Kwon 1988), and adverse selection. 7 h.
