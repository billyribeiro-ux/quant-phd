Financial Accounting

A firm's accounts are a ledger of entries, and the statements in this module are views of that ledger. This module starts with the rules that keep the ledger consistent, then shows how the three statements are drawn from it and why they must agree with one another. It then covers the choices that change the figures: the GAAP and IFRS frameworks, revenue recognition, leases, stock compensation and deferred taxes. The module ends in a three-statement model from raw transactions, graded by an autograder.

  • Prerequisites: P0.1 How to Study This Program
  • Unlocks: AC2 Financial Statement Analysis & Earnings Quality, and modules AC4, CF1, CF7, CF8 and BK1, each of which lists AC1 as a prerequisite
  • Project: Three-statement model from raw transactions, autograded

90 h lessons · 30 h project (25.0%)

Objectives

By the end of AC1 you can:

  1. Post balanced journal entries to a ledger, refuse an entry that does not balance, and state what the balance check does not test.
  2. Compute revenue, expense and the cash they produce, and prove the identities that link revenue and expense to the cash they produce and to the change in the receivable or payable balance.
  3. Sort one ledger into the income statement, the balance sheet and the cash-flow statement, and state which of the three measures a flow and which a stock.
  4. Check the articulation identities that link the three statements, derive operating cash flow by the indirect method, and explain what a passing check does and does not show.
  5. Trace how a generated revenue, lease, stock-compensation or deferred-tax example depends on a rule the lesson leaves open, and name the open question.
  6. Reconcile accumulated depreciation to a straight-line schedule, and argue which of the firm's estimates a reader cannot check from the statements alone.

Placement diagnostic

Nine questions: seven on P0.1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80%, which for nine items means eight correct, since seven is 77.8%. Skip rungs 1 and 2 only if you reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, work through L01 before deciding.

  1. Which evidence grade marks a theorem proved on the page?

    Options

    T marks a theorem proved on the page. The grade legend at the top of each lesson lists all six grades.

  2. Which grade is given to practitioner lore with no systematic evidence?

    Options

    D marks practitioner lore with no systematic evidence. X marks a claim that was refuted, failed replication or was retracted.

  3. What does a prerequisite graph tell a student?

    Options

    The prerequisite graph orders the modules: it shows which modules must be completed before a given module.

  4. What is steelmanning?

    Options

    Steelmanning is the practice of stating the strongest version of a view you dispute.

  5. How many rungs does the project ladder have?

    Four: worked example, completion problem, independent project, stretch.

  6. What must a claim in a lesson cite?

    Options

    A key must exist in the verified citation registry, and a theorem may be proved on the page.

  7. What is identification in an empirical paper?

    Options

    Identification is the variation that pins down an estimate.

  8. Skill check (rung 1). A balanced entry debits cash 500 and credits sales 300. What credit, in the same units, must another account carry for the entry to balance?

    Debits total 500, credits total 300 so far, so the remaining credit is 200 (L01, Definition 1.1).

  9. Skill check (rung 2). Revenue earned in a year is 2,500 and cash received from customers is 1,900. What is the change in accounts receivable over the year?

    Revenue less cash received equals the change in receivables: 2,500 less 1,900 is 600 (L01, Proposition 3.2).

Lessons

Eight lessons. The hours shown are the ones each lesson page declares, and they sum to the 90 lesson hours in the split above. Lessons 1 to 3 cover the ledger and the three statements. Lessons 4 to 8 cover the reporting frameworks and the four topics that change the figures.

L01 · 11 hDouble-Entry Bookkeeping and Accrual AccountingDebits and credits, the T-account ledger, the proof that balanced entries stay balanced, and the identities that separate accrual revenue and expense from cash. L02 · 12 hThe Three StatementsThe income statement as a flow, the balance sheet as a stock, the cash-flow statement as the cash lines of a period, and the proof that the balance sheet balances. L03 · 11 hArticulation and DepreciationThe indirect operating cash flow, the roll-forwards of retained earnings and equipment, the cash identity, and straight-line depreciation checked against the ledger. L04 · 11 hUS GAAP vs. IFRS: Same Transactions, Two Rule SetsOne generated ledger read under US GAAP and under IFRS. Where this module does not state the standard text, the lesson says so. L05 · 12 hRevenue Recognition: Allocating a Price, Timing a DeliveryA generated multi-element contract, its performance obligations, and revenue recognized over time against revenue recognized at a point. L06 · 12 hLease Accounting: A Liability, an Asset and Two Expense PatternsA generated lease schedule, the right-of-use asset and liability roll-forward, and an articulation check against the statements. L07 · 11 hStock-based compensationGenerated grants with vesting, the expense schedule, and the equity and cash-flow entries that follow. L08 · 10 hDeferred tax assets and liabilitiesA generated book-tax difference schedule and the roll-forward of the deferred tax balance.

The project ladder

The module ends in a three-statement model from raw transactions. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. A student skips them only by reaching the placement bar and answering both skill checks.

  1. Worked example

    A fully solved and annotated posting of ten generated transactions to the three statements, with a reason for each step.

  2. Completion problem

    The same posting, with the articulation identity and the cash-flow reconciliation left as blanks. Graded instantly in your browser.

  3. Independent project

    Implement post, the three statements and the articulation check to the specification. A 200-transaction ledger is generated from your workspace seed, and the autograder checks your numbers against it. Project page.

  4. Stretch

    An open-ended question about how the same generated transaction set would present under the other framework, with a stated hypothesis. Not autograded.

Data status

SIMULATED data. Every lab in Lessons 1 to 3 runs on a generated firm, and each lab is labelled SIMULATED in its heading, its hypothesis and its interpretation. In each lab the sales and operating-expense amounts are drawn from a seeded random number generator; in Lessons 2 and 3 a cost percentage of 55 to 65 per cent is drawn too, and cost of sales is that percentage of sales. The opening investment and entry dates (Lesson 1), and the opening position, equipment purchases, borrowing, dividends and depreciation charges (Lessons 2 and 3), are fixed amounts in the lab code. Collections and payments are fixed fractions of balances: four fifths of the receivable, and one half of the payable in Lesson 1 or nine tenths of the payable in Lessons 2 and 3. No figure in Lessons 1 to 3 is a real company's. No dataset is embedded, and no source for this module is listed in tools/data_sources.yaml. The citation registry has no FASB entry, and the FASB Accounting Standards Codification is not in it. Lessons 1 to 3 cite no registry key. Where a lesson makes a claim about what a standard requires and that text is not in the registry, the lesson says the requirement is not stated here.
