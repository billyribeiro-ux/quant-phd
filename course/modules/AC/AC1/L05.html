Revenue Recognition: Allocating a Price, Timing a Delivery

  • Uses: accrual accounting, double-entry bookkeeping, the balance sheet, the income statement, US GAAP vs. IFRS
  • Introduces: revenue recognition
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

Revenue is the first line of most income statements, and its timing decides when a contract's economics appear in net income. A contract can bill before it delivers, deliver before it bills, or do both across its life. This lesson defines the model the unit uses to turn a contract into a schedule of revenue: a transaction price, its allocation to performance obligations, and a timing convention for each obligation. It proves that allocation sums exactly to the price, and that two timing conventions give the same total at different dates. Lab 1 runs both on one SIMULATED contract with four obligations.

1Contracts, obligations and the allocation of a price

Definition 1.1 (revenue recognition, as this unit uses the term)

Revenue recognition is the mapping from a contract to a schedule of revenue by period. In this unit the mapping has three inputs: the transaction price, the performance obligations of the contract, and a timing convention for each obligation. The mapping states how much revenue is reported in each period. It does not state the criteria under which a standard requires an obligation to be identified, a price to be measured, or a timing to be chosen. Those criteria are the open question of Section 4.

Definition 1.2 (performance obligation, this lesson's convention)

A performance obligation is a promised delivery that this lesson treats as separate. Each obligation in this lesson is either a point obligation, delivered in one month, or an over-time obligation, delivered evenly over twelve months. The lesson takes the split between the two kinds as given in the generated contract.

Definition 1.3 (allocation in proportion to stand-alone prices)

Let the obligations have positive integer stand-alone prices \(w_1, \dots, w_n\), with \(W = \sum_i w_i\), and let the transaction price be the integer \(P\) in cents. The allocation is \(a_i = \lfloor P w_i / W \rfloor\) plus one cent for each of the \(L = P - \sum_i \lfloor P w_i / W \rfloor\) obligations with the largest remainders \(r_i = P w_i - W \lfloor P w_i / W \rfloor\), where ties go to the lower index. This is the lesson's convention. It is a rule for splitting an integer, not a claim about how a standard splits a price.

Proposition 1.4 (the allocation sums exactly to the price)

Under Definition 1.3, \(a_i\) is a non-negative integer for every \(i\), and \(\sum_i a_i = P\).

Proof

Let \(f_i = \lfloor P w_i / W \rfloor\), so \(f_i \le P w_i / W\) and each \(f_i\) is a non-negative integer. The remainder \(r_i = P w_i - W f_i\) is an integer with \(0 \le r_i < W\), because \(f_i\) is the largest integer not exceeding \(P w_i / W\). Summing the remainders gives \[ \sum_i r_i = P W - W \sum_i f_i = W L. \] Each \(r_i\) is less than \(W\), so \(W L = \sum_i r_i < n W\), and therefore \(L < n\). Hence \(L\) is a non-negative integer no larger than \(n - 1\), so exactly \(L\) obligations receive one extra cent, and distinct indices receive distinct extras. The result is \(\sum_i a_i = \sum_i f_i + L = P\). Every \(a_i\) is an integer at least \(f_i \ge 0\).

Remark 1.5 (what the allocation does and does not depend on)

The allocation depends only on the ratios \(w_i / W\) and on the price. Scaling every stand-alone price by the same factor leaves it unchanged, which the arithmetic shows directly: \(P (k w_i) / (k W) = P w_i / W\). It does depend on the stand-alone prices themselves, so a change in one price moves revenue between obligations even when the total contract is unchanged.

2Timing: two conventions for the same total

A point obligation is recognized in its delivery month. An over-time obligation is recognized evenly over the twelve months, with the last month taking the remainder so the monthly amounts sum to the allocation. Call this convention 1. Convention 2 is a comparison convention used only in this lesson: every over-time obligation is recognized in month 12, and point obligations are unchanged. Neither convention is claimed to be the rule of any framework.

Proposition 2.1 (total revenue equals the price under convention 1)

Under convention 1, cumulative revenue through month 12 equals \(P\). Under convention 2 it equals \(P\) as well.

Proof

Each point obligation contributes its allocation \(a_i\) once, in its delivery month. Each over-time obligation contributes \(a_i\) in total, because its monthly shares are \(\lfloor a_i / 12 \rfloor\) in months 1 to 11 and \(a_i - 11 \lfloor a_i / 12 \rfloor\) in month 12, which sum to \(a_i\). Under convention 2 the over-time amounts are placed in month 12 in full, which also gives \(a_i\). Summing over all obligations and applying Proposition 1.4 gives \(\sum_i a_i = P\) under both conventions.

Definition 2.2 (the contract balance)

Let \(B_m\) be cumulative billing through month \(m\), including the upfront billing at month 0, and \(V_m\) cumulative revenue through month \(m\). The contract balance is \(D_m = B_m - V_m\). A positive balance is revenue billed ahead of delivery, which this lesson calls a contract liability. A negative balance is revenue recognized ahead of billing, which it calls a contract asset. The lesson models no receivables, so a negative balance here is an unbilled amount only.

Proposition 2.3 (the balance closes at the price)

If total billing equals \(P\) and total revenue equals \(P\), then \(D_{12} = 0\). For every month \(m\), \(D_m\) is the sum of the billing and the negative of the revenue through \(m\), so it moves only with those two flows.

Proof

By Definition 2.2, \(D_{12} = B_{12} - V_{12} = P - P = 0\), using Proposition 2.1 for \(V_{12}\). The second statement is the definition itself: \(D_m - D_{m-1} = (B_m - B_{m-1}) - (V_m - V_{m-1})\), so the balance changes by billing less revenue in each month.

Remark 2.4 (a timing wedge, not a measure of performance)

The contract balance is the cumulative gap between cash-linked billing and delivery. It is zero at the end of the contract under Proposition 2.3, and it can be large in between. A large liability in month 3 says that billing has run ahead of delivery in that month. It says nothing about whether the contract was profitable.

Convention 1 and convention 2 agree at month 12 (Proposition 2.1) and differ before it. Because convention 2 places no over-time revenue before month 12, the difference at any month \(m < 12\) equals the convention-1 revenue recognized by month \(m\) from the over-time obligations. That is a direct consequence of the two definitions, and Lab 1 checks it at month 6.

Lab 1 · One SIMULATED contract: allocation, two timing conventions and the balance

The lab generates a contract from seed 2605 with two to four obligations. Stated before running: the allocated amounts sum to the transaction price (Proposition 1.4); total revenue equals the price under convention 1 (Proposition 2.1); the balance identity holds in every month under both billing scenarios, and the balance closes at zero (Proposition 2.3); and under convention 2 the total is the same while month-6 revenue is lower, by the convention-1 revenue of the over-time obligations through month 6. The lab runs two billing scenarios: 40 percent billed at signing, which is the contract's billing, and 0 percent billed at signing, which shows a contract asset. All amounts are integer cents.

"""AC1 L05 lab 1: one SIMULATED multi-element contract, its allocation, and two timing conventions.

SIMULATED: the contract is generated from a seeded generator. No customer, filing or company is represented.
Units: integer cents for every money amount. Percentages printed below are unitless shares.

The contract has two to four performance obligations. Each obligation is either delivered at a point in time
or delivered over twelve months. The transaction price is allocated to the obligations in proportion to their
stand-alone prices, using whole cents. Revenue for a point obligation is recognized in its delivery month. Revenue
for an over-time obligation is recognized evenly over the twelve months (a straight-line measure of progress).

Two conventions are labelled here for comparison. Convention 1 is the schedule described above. Convention 2
recognizes every over-time obligation in month 12 instead. Neither convention is claimed to be the rule of any
accounting framework. The lab shows only what each convention does to the timing of the same total.

Billing: 40 percent of the price is billed at signing (month 0). The rest is billed in twelve equal monthly
instalments (months 1 to 12). Cash is collected when billed, so the contract balance is cumulative billing less
cumulative revenue.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 2605
MONTHS = 12
UPFRONT_PERCENT = 40


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Obligation:
    """One performance obligation of the SIMULATED contract."""

    name: str
    over_time: bool  # True: recognized evenly over MONTHS; False: recognized in delivery_month
    delivery_month: int  # 1..MONTHS, used only for point obligations
    standalone_cents: int  # stand-alone price in cents, the allocation weight


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Contract:
    """A SIMULATED contract: its obligations and its transaction price in integer cents."""

    obligations: tuple[Obligation, ...]
    price_cents: int


def generate(seed: int) -> Contract:
    """Two to four obligations with seeded stand-alone prices, and a price that is 80 to 95 percent of their sum."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    count = rng.randint(2, 4)
    obligations: list[Obligation] = []
    for i in range(count):
        over_time = rng.random() < 0.5
        delivery = rng.randint(1, MONTHS)
        standalone = rng.randint(50_000_00, 400_000_00)
        name = f"O{i + 1}"
        obligations.append(Obligation(name, over_time, delivery, standalone))
    discount_bps = rng.randint(8000, 9500)
    total = sum(o.standalone_cents for o in obligations)
    price = total * discount_bps // 10000
    return Contract(tuple(obligations), price)


def allocate(total: int, weights: list[int]) -> list[int]:
    """Split total cents in proportion to integer weights, exactly.

    Each share starts at its floor. Each remainder is less than the weight sum, and the leftover cents are fewer than
    the number of shares, so the largest remainders (ties to the lower index) take one cent each and the result sums
    to total.
    """
    weight_sum = sum(weights)
    floors = [total * w // weight_sum for w in weights]
    remainders = [total * w - f * weight_sum for w, f in zip(weights, floors, strict=True)]
    leftover = total - sum(floors)
    order = sorted(range(len(weights)), key=lambda i: (-remainders[i], i))
    for i in order[:leftover]:
        floors[i] += 1
    return floors


def over_time_schedule(amount: int) -> list[int]:
    """Equal monthly shares of an amount over MONTHS months; the last month takes the remainder."""
    base = amount // MONTHS
    return [base] * (MONTHS - 1) + [amount - base * (MONTHS - 1)]


def revenue_schedule(contract: Contract, allocation: list[int], over_time_as_end: bool) -> list[int]:
    """Revenue in cents for months 1..MONTHS. over_time_as_end=True is convention 2 (over-time amounts in month 12)."""
    revenue = [0] * MONTHS
    for obligation, amount in zip(contract.obligations, allocation, strict=True):
        if obligation.over_time and not over_time_as_end:
            for month, share in enumerate(over_time_schedule(amount)):
                revenue[month] += share
        elif obligation.over_time:
            revenue[MONTHS - 1] += amount
        else:
            revenue[obligation.delivery_month - 1] += amount
    return revenue


def billing_schedule(price: int, upfront_percent: int) -> list[int]:
    """Billing in cents for months 0..MONTHS: upfront at month 0, the rest in equal monthly instalments."""
    upfront = price * upfront_percent // 100
    instalments = allocate(price - upfront, [1] * MONTHS)
    return [upfront, *instalments]


def cumulative(values: list[int]) -> list[int]:
    """Running total in cents."""
    total = 0
    out: list[int] = []
    for value in values:
        total += value
        out.append(total)
    return out


def main() -> None:
    contract = generate(SEED)
    allocation = allocate(contract.price_cents, [o.standalone_cents for o in contract.obligations])
    total_standalone = sum(o.standalone_cents for o in contract.obligations)
    print(f"seed {SEED} obligations {len(contract.obligations)} price_cents {contract.price_cents}")
    print(f"standalone_total_cents {total_standalone}")
    for obligation, amount in zip(contract.obligations, allocation, strict=True):
        kind = "over_time" if obligation.over_time else "point"
        share = amount / contract.price_cents
        print(
            f"{obligation.name} kind {kind} delivery_month {obligation.delivery_month} "
            f"standalone_cents {obligation.standalone_cents} allocated_cents {amount} share {share:.4f}"
        )
    print(f"allocated_sum_equals_price {sum(allocation) == contract.price_cents}")

    revenue = revenue_schedule(contract, allocation, over_time_as_end=False)
    revenue_cum = cumulative(revenue)
    recognized_cum = [0, *cumulative(revenue_schedule(contract, allocation, over_time_as_end=False))]
    revenue_cum = recognized_cum[1:]
    print(f"revenue_total_cents {revenue_cum[-1]} equals_price {revenue_cum[-1] == contract.price_cents}")
    print(
        f"revenue_cum_month_6_cents {revenue_cum[5]} "
        f"share_recognized_month_6 {revenue_cum[5] / contract.price_cents:.4f}"
    )

    # Billing scenarios: 40 percent upfront (the contract's billing), and 0 percent upfront (all in instalments).
    for upfront_percent in (UPFRONT_PERCENT, 0):
        billing = billing_schedule(contract.price_cents, upfront_percent)
        billed_cum = cumulative(billing)  # months 0..MONTHS
        balance = [b - r for b, r in zip(billed_cum, recognized_cum, strict=True)]  # positive: contract liability
        print(
            f"upfront_percent {upfront_percent} billing_month_0_cents {billing[0]} billing_total_cents {billed_cum[-1]}"
        )
        print(f"upfront_percent {upfront_percent} balance_end_cents {balance[-1]}")
        print(
            f"upfront_percent {upfront_percent} balance_identity "
            f"{all(balance[m] == billed_cum[m] - recognized_cum[m] for m in range(MONTHS + 1))}"
        )
        print(
            f"upfront_percent {upfront_percent} max_contract_liability_cents {max(balance)} "
            f"max_contract_asset_cents {-min(balance)} months_with_contract_asset {sum(1 for b in balance if b < 0)}"
        )

    # Convention 2: the same allocation with every over-time obligation recognized in month 12.
    revenue_2 = revenue_schedule(contract, allocation, over_time_as_end=True)
    revenue_2_cum = cumulative(revenue_2)
    print(f"convention_2_month_6_cents {revenue_2_cum[5]} convention_2_total_cents {revenue_2_cum[-1]}")
    print(f"totals_equal_under_both_conventions {revenue_2_cum[-1] == revenue_cum[-1]}")
    print(f"month_6_difference_cents {revenue_cum[5] - revenue_2_cum[5]}")


main()

The four allocations sum to the price of 80,495,111 cents, and the printed check is True. The shares of the price are 0.0675, 0.1173, 0.3713 and 0.4439, and they sum to 1.0000 to four decimals. Revenue under convention 1 totals 80,495,111 cents. Cumulative revenue at month 6 is 50,471,362 cents, a share of 0.6270 (50,471,362 divided by 80,495,111, unrounded 0.62701). Under 40 percent upfront billing the balance closes at zero, peaks as a contract liability of 32,792,272 cents, and never becomes a contract asset. 3What the convention cannot settle

The model in Sections 1 and 2 needs four inputs that a reader of a real contract must decide. The first is whether a promised delivery is a separate obligation. The second is whether an obligation is delivered at a point or over time. The third is the measure of progress for an over-time obligation, which here is elapsed time. The fourth is how stand-alone prices are observed. The lesson fixes all four by construction and does not claim that any of them is the required treatment. The requirements of the governing standards for each input are open, because their primary texts are not yet in the registry.

Honest pending. No real contract appears in this lesson. The contracts are generated from seeds and labelled SIMULATED. A worked example from a real company's revenue note is deferred until the primary texts for the governing standards are admitted and read, and until the reuse terms of its source have been checked.

4Where the canon breaks

  • Allocation by stand-alone price moves revenue when prices change. Remark 1.5 shows that a change in one stand-alone price moves revenue between obligations, with the total fixed. Whether a stand-alone price is observable in a given contract is the question the lesson leaves open.
  • Elapsed time is one measure of progress. Convention 1 uses it. A measure based on output or on cost would place the same total at different months. The lesson does not say which measure a reader should use, and convention 2 shows that the choice of timing alone changes month-6 revenue by 20,583,168 cents in Lab 1.
  • A balance is a timing wedge, not a profit or loss. Remark 2.4 shows that the balance can be large in both directions for one contract. Its size reflects the billing schedule as much as the delivery schedule.
  • The four open inputs are the standard-specific questions. Section 3 names them. The identities in this lesson hold whatever the answers are, and they do not decide the answers.

5Misconceptions

  1. “Revenue equals billing.”

    Billing and revenue are different flows, and the contract balance of Definition 2.2 is their cumulative gap. In Lab 1 the contract billed 40 percent at signing, so its balance was a contract liability of up to 32,792,272 cents while delivery was still under way. Under 0 percent upfront billing the same contract's balance became a contract asset in 8 months. Equal billing and revenue would need \(D_m = 0\) in every month. The lab's 40 percent schedule has a peak balance of 32,792,272 cents, so that does not hold here.

  2. “Point and over-time recognition give different total revenue for the same contract.”

    Proposition 2.1 shows that both conventions total the price. The conventions differ in timing only. Lab 1 gives the same total of 80,495,111 cents under both, and a month-6 difference of 20,583,168 cents, which is the timing effect and not a change in the amount.

  3. “A contract asset is simply an amount owed by the customer.”

    In this lesson a negative contract balance is revenue recognized ahead of billing (Definition 2.2). The lesson models no receivables, so the negative balance is an unbilled amount only. Whether an unbilled amount is owed by the customer depends on the contract terms, which the lesson does not model. Lab 1's 0 percent case shows a contract asset of 23,333,398 cents at its largest, with no receivable in the model.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. All amounts are in cents.

Mechanics

M1 · Allocate a price of 100,000 cents

Allocate a transaction price of 100,000 cents across three obligations with stand-alone prices 1, 2 and 3 (any common unit), using Definition 1.3. Show the floors, the remainders and the leftover cents.

Solution

The weight sum is \(W = 6\). The floors are \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 1 / 6 \rfloor = 16{,}666\), \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 2 / 6 \rfloor = 33{,}333\) and \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 3 / 6 \rfloor = 50{,}000\). They sum to 99,999, so \(L = 1\). The remainders are \(100{,}000 - 6 \cdot 16{,}666 = 4\), \(200{,}000 - 6 \cdot 33{,}333 = 2\) and \(300{,}000 - 6 \cdot 50{,}000 = 0\). The largest remainder belongs to the first obligation, which receives the extra cent. The allocation is 16,667, 33,333 and 50,000, which sums to 100,000.

M2 · Schedule of a point and an over-time obligation

Obligation A is a point obligation of 60,000 cents delivered in month 3. Obligation B is an over-time obligation of 24,000 cents. Under convention 1, compute revenue in months 3 and 6 and the cumulative revenue through month 6.

Solution

Obligation A contributes 60,000 in month 3 and nothing else. Obligation B contributes \(24{,}000 / 12 = 2{,}000\) in each month from 1 to 12, with no remainder because 24,000 divides by 12. Revenue in month 3 is \(60{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 62{,}000\). Cumulative revenue through month 6 is \(60{,}000 + 6 \cdot 2{,}000 = 72{,}000\).

M3 · A contract asset in month 6

The contract of M2 has the two obligations' allocations as its price, 84,000 cents (60,000 plus 24,000). It is billed 40 percent at signing and the rest in twelve equal instalments. Compute the contract balance at month 6 under Definition 2.2 and say whether it is a contract asset or a liability.

Solution

Billing at month 0 is \(84{,}000 \cdot 40 / 100 = 33{,}600\) cents. The remaining \(50{,}400\) cents are billed in instalments of \(50{,}400 / 12 = 4{,}200\) cents. Cumulative billing through month 6 is \(33{,}600 + 6 \cdot 4{,}200 = 58{,}800\) cents. Cumulative revenue through month 6 is 72,000 cents, from M2. The balance is \(58{,}800 - 72{,}000 = -13{,}200\). A negative balance is a contract asset of 13,200 cents.

M4 · Scaling the stand-alone prices

Multiply every stand-alone price in M1 by 10. Show that the allocation is unchanged.

Solution

The new weights are 10, 20 and 30, with \(W = 60\). The floors are \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 10 / 60 \rfloor = 16{,}666\), \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 20 / 60 \rfloor = 33{,}333\) and \(\lfloor 100{,}000 \cdot 30 / 60 \rfloor = 50{,}000\). These are the M1 floors, and the remainders are \(1{,}000{,}000 - 60 \cdot 16{,}666 = 40\), \(2{,}000{,}000 - 60 \cdot 33{,}333 = 20\) and 0. The same obligation receives the extra cent, so the allocation is 16,667, 33,333 and 50,000, as in M1, as Remark 1.5 requires.

Proof

P1 · The allocation sums exactly to the price

Prove Proposition 1.4 without using the proof given in Section 1. Explain where the bound \(L < n\) is used.

Solution

Write \(f_i\) for the floors and \(r_i\) for the remainders. The sum of the \(r_i\) equals \(W\) times the number of extra cents \(L\), since \(\sum r_i = PW - W\sum f_i = WL\). Each \(r_i\) is strictly less than \(W\), because \(f_i\) is the largest integer not exceeding \(P w_i / W\). Summing the strict inequalities gives \(WL < nW\), so \(L < n\). The bound is used to show that the leftover can be given out one cent to each of \(L\) distinct obligations, since there are only \(n\) obligations to receive it. Without the bound the procedure would need to give some obligation two cents. Under Definition 1.3, \(\sum a_i = \sum f_i + L = P\).

P2 · Convention 1 totals the price

Prove Proposition 2.1 for convention 1 only. State which fact about the monthly shares of an over-time obligation is used.

Solution

An over-time obligation with allocation \(a\) has monthly shares \(\lfloor a / 12 \rfloor\) in months 1 to 11 and \(a - 11\lfloor a / 12 \rfloor\) in month 12. Their sum is \(11\lfloor a / 12 \rfloor + a - 11\lfloor a / 12 \rfloor = a\). The fact used is that the last share is defined as the remainder, so the shares sum to \(a\) exactly. A point obligation contributes \(a\) once. Summing over the obligations gives \(\sum a_i = P\) by Proposition 1.4.

P3 · The month-m difference between the conventions

Show that for \(m < 12\), the difference between convention 1 and convention 2 cumulative revenue at month \(m\) equals the convention-1 revenue recognized by month \(m\) from over-time obligations.

Solution

Under convention 2, no over-time amount is recognized before month 12, and the point obligations are the same under both conventions. Cumulative revenue at month \(m\) under convention 2 is therefore the point revenue through \(m\). Under convention 1 it is the point revenue through \(m\) plus the over-time revenue through \(m\). Subtracting, the difference is the over-time revenue through \(m\) under convention 1, as claimed. For Lab 1 at \(m = 6\): the first over-time obligation gives \(6 \cdot 452{,}837 = 2{,}717{,}022\) cents and the second gives \(6 \cdot 2{,}977{,}691 = 17{,}866{,}146\) cents, a total of 20,583,168 cents, which the lab prints.

E1 · Revenue share at month 6

Run Lab 1. Report the share of the price recognized by month 6 under convention 1, to four decimals, and show the division.

Solution

The lab prints cumulative revenue through month 6 of 50,471,362 cents and the share 0.6270. The division is \(50{,}471{,}362 / 80{,}495{,}111 = 0.627012\), which rounds to 0.6270 at four decimals. The share depends on the seed, the obligation mix and the delivery months, so it describes this contract only.

E2 · Peak contract liability as a share of price

Run Lab 1. Report the peak contract liability under 40 percent upfront billing as a share of the price, to four decimals, and say at what sign of the balance it occurs.

Solution

The printed peak is 32,792,272 cents. The division is \(32{,}792{,}272 / 80{,}495{,}111 = 0.407382\), which is 0.4074 at four decimals. The peak is a positive balance, so it is a contract liability. The lab prints only the peak value and not its month, so the month is not stated here.

E3 · Contract asset months with no upfront billing

Run Lab 1. Report the number of months with a contract asset under 0 percent upfront billing and the largest contract asset as a share of the price.

Solution

The lab prints 8 months with a contract asset and a largest asset of 23,333,398 cents. The share is \(23{,}333{,}398 / 80{,}495{,}111 = 0.289873\), or 0.2899 at four decimals. The asset arises in the months where cumulative revenue exceeds cumulative billing, which by Definition 2.2 is where the balance is negative. The lab prints the count of those months and the largest value.

Research

R1 · Testing the allocation on a real contract

Describe, in full, a test of Definition 1.3 on one real contract. Name every input, its source, the assumptions to check first, and the result that would show the allocation convention is not what the contract records. Do not run it.

Solution

Inputs: the contract's price, the list of promised goods and services, the stand-alone price the contract or the entity's own records state for each, and the revenue schedule the entity reports. Assumptions to check first: that the list of obligations is the list the entity itself identifies, and that the stand-alone prices are observed rather than assumed. The test computes the allocation of Definition 1.3 and compares it with the entity's reported split. A difference that remains after the entity's own stated split is used is evidence against the convention for that contract, and the reading is refuted if the entity's split is reproduced exactly by the convention. The source of the rules for identifying obligations is the primary text, which the unit has not yet admitted.

R2 · The four open inputs and their sources

For each of the four open inputs in Section 3, name the question a reader must answer from the primary text and the kind of evidence that would decide it.

Solution

Separate obligations: the question is when a promised delivery is separable from the others, and the evidence is the contract's terms and the primary text's criteria. Point or over time: the question is whether the customer receives and consumes the benefit as the entity performs, and the evidence is the delivery terms and the text's criteria. Measure of progress: the question is which measure depicts the transfer of the good or service, and the evidence is the method the entity discloses. Stand-alone price: the question is what observable price exists for each obligation, and the evidence is the entity's own pricing records. None of these four questions is answered by the identities of this lesson. Each answer needs a cited text, which the unit does not yet hold.

R3 · Is timing a measurement choice?

Argue whether the choice between convention 1 and convention 2 is a choice of measurement or a choice of recognition. Use Proposition 2.1 and the month-6 difference in your argument.

Solution

The choice moves the amount recognized at each date and leaves the total unchanged. Proposition 2.1 shows the total is the price under both, so the choice does not change the measurement of the contract's price. It changes when the price enters revenue, so it is a choice of recognition timing. The month-6 difference of 20,583,168 cents in Lab 1 is the size of that timing choice for one contract. A reader who compares two entities at month 6 must therefore check the timing convention each one uses before comparing their revenue.

  1. Under Definition 1.3, what is the allocation of 100,000 cents across stand-alone prices 1, 2 and 3?

  2. A point obligation of 60,000 cents is delivered in month 3, and an over-time obligation of 24,000 cents is spread over twelve months. What is revenue in month 3 under convention 1, in cents?

  3. A contract with a price of 84,000 cents is billed 40 percent at signing and the rest in twelve equal instalments. At month 6, cumulative billing is 58,800 cents and cumulative revenue is 72,000 cents. What is the contract balance in cents, with the Definition 2.2 sign convention (positive means a liability)?

    \(58{,}800 - 72{,}000 = -13{,}200\). A negative balance is a contract asset (M3).

  4. Under convention 2, which revenue is different from convention 1 at month 6?

    Options

  6. If a stand-alone price changes and the contract price does not, what happens under Definition 1.3?
