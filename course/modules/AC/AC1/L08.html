Deferred tax assets and liabilities

  • Uses: the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash-flow statement, statement articulation, depreciation, accrual accounting
  • Introduces: deferred taxes (defined in §1)
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or lab
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

Tax expense and tax paid in cash can differ in timing. The gap is carried as a deferred tax balance, defined in Section 1. This lesson fixes one model: one asset, a book schedule and a tax schedule that differ in timing, and one flat rate. In the model, the current tax (Section 2) and the total tax expense are exact in integer cents when pretax book income (\(P_t\), defined in Section 2) is a multiple of 100 cents. The deferred expense and the deferred balance are exact for every \(P_t\), because Definition 1.1 makes the depreciation amounts and the cumulative difference multiples of 100 cents. The model states no rule for when a deferred tax asset may be recognized.

1Carrying amounts, tax bases and the temporary difference

Two amounts for the same asset can differ. The carrying amount is the book value on the balance sheet: cost less book depreciation to date. The tax base is the amount that the model's tax schedule leaves to be deducted: cost less the tax depreciation to date (Definition 1.1). Their difference is the temporary difference, and in this model it is zero at the end of the asset's life (the proof of Theorem 2.1 (c) gives this). Amounts are in SCU (simulated currency units), in integer cents, because Definition 1.1 makes \(C\), \(b_t\) and \(\tau_t\) whole numbers of cents.

Definition 1.1 (Model asset and schedules)

An asset has cost \(C\), a multiple of 10,000 cents, and a book life of \(L\) years, where \(C / L\) is a multiple of 100 cents. Book depreciation is straight-line, \(b = C / L\) in each year \(t = 1, \dots, L\). Tax depreciation in year \(t\) is \(\tau_t = C\,p_t / 100\), where the integers \(p_1, \dots, p_L\) are the tax percentages and sum to 100. The model's flat tax rate is a whole number of percent, \(r\), the same in every year. The tax schedule is an input to the model. This lesson does not describe any tax code.

Definition 1.2 (Temporary difference, deferred tax balance)

Let \(K_t = \sum_{s \le t} (\tau_s - b_s)\), with \(K_0 = 0\). This is the cumulative temporary difference, which Theorem 1.3 identifies with carrying amount minus tax base. The deferred tax balance at the end of year \(t\) is \(\mathrm{DTL}_t = r K_t / 100\). In this model, a positive balance is a liability, and a negative balance is an asset. The schedule in Example 1.4 gives no negative balance, so it has no asset: its cumulative tax percentages, 40, 70, 90, 100 and 100, never fall below the cumulative book percentages, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100.

Theorem 1.3 (Difference of the two carrying amounts)

Under Definition 1.1, at the end of every year \(t\), carrying amount minus tax base equals \(K_t\).

Proof

Carrying amount is \(C - \sum_{s \le t} b_s\) and tax base is \(C - \sum_{s \le t} \tau_s\). Their difference is \(\sum_{s \le t} \tau_s - \sum_{s \le t} b_s = \sum_{s \le t}(\tau_s - b_s) = K_t\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.4 (A worked asset)

Let \(C = \$100{,}000\), \(L = 5\), tax percentages \((40, 30, 20, 10, 0)\), and \(r = 21\). Then \(b = \$20{,}000\) each year. The tax depreciation is $40,000, $30,000, $20,000, $10,000 and $0. The yearly differences \(\tau_t - b_t\) are $20,000, $10,000, $0, \(-\$10{,}000\) and \(-\$20{,}000\). So \(K_t\) is $20,000, $30,000, $30,000, $20,000 and $0. At \(r = 21\), the deferred balance \(\mathrm{DTL}_t\) is $4,200, $6,300, $6,300, $4,200 and $0.

2Splitting tax expense into current and deferred parts

Let \(P_t\) be pretax book income, that is income before tax and after book depreciation. Taxable income in the model is \(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t)\), where \(\tau_t - b_t\) is the year's change in the temporary difference. Define:

\[ \mathrm{CT}_t = \frac{r\,(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t))}{100}, \qquad \mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}, \qquad \mathrm{TE}_t = \mathrm{CT}_t + \mathrm{DE}_t, \]

where \(\mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\). \(\mathrm{CT}_t\) is the current tax, the tax on taxable income. \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) is the deferred tax expense, and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\) is total tax expense. For \(\mathrm{CT}_t\) and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\) to be integer numbers of cents, \(r\,P_t\) must be a multiple of 100; a \(P_t\) that is a multiple of 100 cents is sufficient for every whole-number \(r\). \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) and \(\mathrm{DTL}_t\) need no condition on \(P_t\): under Definition 1.1, \(b_t\), \(\tau_t\) and \(\tau_t - b_t\) are multiples of 100 cents, so \(r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\) and \(r K_t/100\) are whole numbers of cents. The model's cash convention is that the cash tax paid in year \(t\) is \(\mathrm{CT}_t\), so deferred tax expense moves no cash.

Theorem 2.1 (Tax identities under a flat rate)

Under Definition 1.1 (straight-line book depreciation, tax percentages summing to 100, one flat rate \(r\)) and the definitions of Section 2, for every year \(t\) of the schedule: (a) \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\); (b) \(\mathrm{TE}_t = r P_t / 100\); (c) \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\); (d) \(\sum_{t=1}^{L} \mathrm{DE}_t = 0\).

Proof

(a) By Theorem 1.3, \(K_t - K_{t-1} = \tau_t - b_t\). Then \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(K_t - K_{t-1})/100 = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\). (b) \(\mathrm{TE}_t = r(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t))/100 + r(\tau_t - b_t)/100 = rP_t/100\), by (a). (c) \(K_L = \sum_{s=1}^{L} \tau_s - \sum_{s=1}^{L} b_s = C - C = 0\), because \(\sum_s p_s = 100\) gives \(\sum_s \tau_s = C\), and \(L\,b = C\). Hence \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\). (d) The differences telescope: \(\sum_t \mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_L - \mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.2 (Example 1.4 with pretax income of $50,000)

Take \(P_t = \$50{,}000\) in each year. Year 1: \(\mathrm{CT}_1 = 0.21 \times (50{,}000 - 20{,}000) = \$6{,}300\), \(\mathrm{DE}_1 = \$4{,}200\), so \(\mathrm{TE}_1 = \$10{,}500\). Year 2: \(\mathrm{CT}_2 = 0.21 \times 40{,}000 = \$8{,}400\), \(\mathrm{DE}_2 = \$2{,}100\), so \(\mathrm{TE}_2 = \$10{,}500\). Theorem 2.1 (b) gives \(0.21 \times 50{,}000 = \$10{,}500\) in every year, and (d) gives deferred expense summing to zero over the five years.

The lab runs one generated asset and pretax income for five years, and checks parts (a) and (b) of Theorem 2.1 in every year, and the balance at the end of year 5 that part (c) requires; it does not check part (d). The asset, the income and the rate of 21 percent are SIMULATED.

Lab 1 · Book-tax difference and deferred tax roll-forward on a SIMULATED asset

Stated before running: for one SIMULATED asset and generated pretax income, book depreciation and tax depreciation each total the cost, the deferred tax balance is zero at the end of year 5, and in every year the deferred expense equals the rate times that year's difference and the total tax expense equals the rate times pretax income. Expected result: each of the three year-by-year checks (pretax income times the rate is exact, deferred expense equals the rate times the year's difference, and tax expense equals the rate times pretax income) gives 5 of 5. The asset and income are SIMULATED, and the 21 percent rate is a model parameter, not a statement about any jurisdiction.

"""Lab AC1-L08-1: a book-tax difference schedule and the deferred tax roll-forward.

Every asset, every pretax income figure and the tax rate are SIMULATED: a seeded
generator draws the asset and income, and the rate is a model parameter chosen for
the arithmetic, not a statement about any jurisdiction. No company is represented.
Money is in integer cents of simulated currency units (SCU). The model has one
depreciable asset, straight-line book depreciation over LIFE years, a fixed tax
depreciation schedule that totals the cost, and one flat rate applied to every year.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random

SEED = 20260702
LIFE = 5  # book straight-line life in years
TAX_PERCENT = (40, 30, 20, 10, 0)  # tax depreciation as a percent of cost, years 1 to 5; sums to 100
RATE_PERCENT = 21  # the model's flat tax rate in percent (a parameter of the model)


def cents_text(cents: int) -> str:
    """Format integer cents as SCU dollars and cents, for example 1234567 gives 12,345.67."""
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    whole, frac = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
    return f"{sign}{whole:,}.{frac:02d}"


def main() -> None:
    """Draw the asset and pretax income, roll the deferred tax balance, and check the identities."""
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    cost = rng.randrange(1_000_000, 5_000_001, 10_000)  # SCU $10,000 to $50,000, a multiple of $100
    # Pretax income of $40,000 to $90,000 exceeds the largest yearly difference
    # (20% of cost, which is $10,000 at a $50,000 cost), so taxable income stays positive.
    pretax = [rng.randrange(4_000_000, 9_000_001, 100) for _ in range(LIFE)]
    assert cost % LIFE == 0
    assert sum(TAX_PERCENT) == 100

    print(
        f"SIMULATED asset (seed {SEED}): cost={cents_text(cost)} SCU; book life={LIFE} years; "
        f"tax depreciation percent={TAX_PERCENT}; flat rate={RATE_PERCENT}%"
    )
    print("year  pretax  book_dep  tax_dep  cum_temp_diff  DTL  current_tax  deferred_exp  tax_exp  net_income")

    book_total = 0
    tax_total = 0
    cumulative = 0
    previous_dtl = 0
    exact_rate = 0
    deferred_ok = 0
    tax_ok = 0
    for year in range(1, LIFE + 1):
        index = year - 1
        book_dep = cost // LIFE
        tax_dep = cost * TAX_PERCENT[index] // 100
        assert RATE_PERCENT * tax_dep % 100 == 0 and RATE_PERCENT * book_dep % 100 == 0
        difference = tax_dep - book_dep
        cumulative += difference
        dtl = RATE_PERCENT * cumulative // 100
        taxable = pretax[index] - difference
        current = RATE_PERCENT * taxable // 100
        deferred = dtl - previous_dtl
        tax_expense = current + deferred
        net_income = pretax[index] - tax_expense

        exact_rate += RATE_PERCENT * pretax[index] % 100 == 0
        deferred_ok += deferred == RATE_PERCENT * difference // 100
        tax_ok += tax_expense == RATE_PERCENT * pretax[index] // 100
        book_total += book_dep
        tax_total += tax_dep
        previous_dtl = dtl
        print(
            f"{year:>4}  {cents_text(pretax[index]):>10}  {cents_text(book_dep):>10}  {cents_text(tax_dep):>10}  "
            f"{cents_text(cumulative):>13}  {cents_text(dtl):>10}  {cents_text(current):>11}  "
            f"{cents_text(deferred):>12}  {cents_text(tax_expense):>10}  {cents_text(net_income):>12}"
        )

    print(f"book depreciation total equals cost: {book_total == cost}")
    print(f"tax depreciation total equals cost: {tax_total == cost}")
    print(f"deferred tax balance at the end of year {LIFE}: {cents_text(previous_dtl)} SCU")
    print(f"pretax income times the rate is exact in every year: {exact_rate}/{LIFE}")
    print(f"deferred expense equals rate times the year's difference: {deferred_ok}/{LIFE}")
    print(f"tax expense equals rate times pretax income: {tax_ok}/{LIFE}")
    assert book_total == tax_total == cost
    assert previous_dtl == 0
    assert exact_rate == deferred_ok == tax_ok == LIFE


main()

The identities hold by construction, so a passing run tests the code against Theorem 2.1, not the tax position of any firm. The lab's columns show the mechanism. 3Where the canon breaks

The model uses one flat rate for every year, and Theorem 2.1 (b) depends on that. Proof P3 shows that a rate change adds a term, \((r_t - r_{t-1})K_{t-1}/100\), to total tax expense. A model with changing rates therefore needs the rate of each year, and this lesson does not model a change.

Example 1.4 and the lab use the same tax percentages, 40, 30, 20, 10 and 0, so neither has a negative balance and neither shows an asset. A schedule whose cumulative tax percentages fall below the book percentages gives a negative balance, which is an asset in the signed form of Definition 1.2. Whether such an asset may be recognized is outside the model: the model has no income forecast, and this page states no recognition test.

The model has one asset and one tax schedule, so it cannot show how many temporary differences a real firm holds at once. Its balance is zero at year \(L\) by Theorem 2.1 (c).

4Misconceptions

  1. “A deferred tax liability is a debt that will be paid to the tax authority next year.”

    The balance is the tax on differences that reverse later. Under the cash convention of Section 2, cash moves only as current tax is paid. In Example 2.2 the current tax in year 1 is $6,300, below the total expense of $10,500. In Example 1.4 at 21 percent the balance rises to $6,300 at the end of years 2 and 3, falls to $4,200 at the end of year 4 as the difference reverses, and Theorem 2.1 (c) returns it to $0 at the end of year 5.

  2. “Deferred tax expense is a cash payment.”

    Deferred tax expense is the change in the balance, \(\mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}\), and, under the cash convention of Section 2, it moves no cash. Cash tax is \(\mathrm{CT}_t\). In Example 2.2 the year-1 deferred expense is $4,200, and no cash of that amount is paid in year 1.

  3. “Faster tax depreciation raises the tax expense.”

    Theorem 2.1 (b) gives \(\mathrm{TE}_t = rP_t / 100\), which does not depend on the tax schedule \(\tau_t\). Faster tax depreciation in a year, an extra deduction of \(d\), lowers that year's current tax by \(rd/100\) and raises the deferred balance at that year end by \(rd/100\), the same amount, as Theorem 2.1 (a) gives. Lab 1 shows total tax expense equal to 21 percent of pretax income in every year of its one tax schedule. Theorem 2.1 (b) gives the same result for any tax percentages that sum to 100, at a flat rate.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The balance at a different rate

For Example 1.4, find \(\mathrm{DTL}_2\) at a rate of 25 percent.

Solution

\(K_2 = \$30{,}000\), from Example 1.4. Then \(\mathrm{DTL}_2 = 25 \times 30{,}000 / 100 = \$7{,}500\).

M2 · A three-year asset

Cost $60,000, book life 3 years, tax percentages \((50, 30, 20)\), rate 21 percent. Give \(b_t\), \(\tau_t\), \(K_t\), \(\mathrm{DTL}_t\) and \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) for \(t = 1, 2, 3\), and check Theorem 2.1 (c) and (d).

Solution

\(b_t = \$20{,}000\) each year. \(\tau_t = \$30{,}000\), $18,000 and $12,000. The differences are $10,000, \(-\$2{,}000\) and \(-\$8{,}000\). So \(K_t = \$10{,}000\), $8,000 and $0. The balances are \(\mathrm{DTL}_t = \$2{,}100\), $1,680 and $0. The deferred expenses are $2,100, \(-\$420\) and \(-\$1{,}680\), which sum to zero. The balance is zero at the end of year 3, as (c) requires.

M3 · Split the expense

For the asset in M2 with \(P_t = \$50{,}000\) in each year, give the current tax, deferred expense and total tax expense for each year.

Solution

Year 1: \(\mathrm{CT}_1 = 0.21 \times (50{,}000 - 10{,}000) = \$8{,}400\), \(\mathrm{DE}_1 = \$2{,}100\), total $10,500. Year 2: \(\mathrm{CT}_2 = 0.21 \times 52{,}000 = \$10{,}920\), \(\mathrm{DE}_2 = -\$420\), total $10,500. Year 3: \(\mathrm{CT}_3 = 0.21 \times 58{,}000 = \$12{,}180\), \(\mathrm{DE}_3 = -\$1{,}680\), total $10,500. Each total equals \(0.21 \times 50{,}000 = \$10{,}500\), as Theorem 2.1 (b) requires.

Proof

P1 · Carrying amount minus tax base

Prove Theorem 1.3 without the sum notation, for \(L = 2\).

Solution

Year 1: carrying amount \(C - b_1\), tax base \(C - \tau_1\), difference \(\tau_1 - b_1 = K_1\). Year 2: carrying amount \(C - 2b_1\), tax base \(C - \tau_1 - \tau_2\), difference \(\tau_1 + \tau_2 - 2b_1 = (\tau_1 - b_1) + (\tau_2 - b_1) = K_2\). Each step adds the year's difference.

P2 · The balance sums to zero

Show that Theorem 2.1 (d) holds without using the values of \(\tau_t\) or \(b_t\).

Solution

\(\mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}\) for each \(t\), so the sum over \(t = 1\) to \(L\) telescopes to \(\mathrm{DTL}_L - \mathrm{DTL}_0\). Here \(\mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\) by definition, and \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\) by Theorem 2.1 (c). The sum is zero whatever the schedule, provided (c) holds.

P3 · A rate that changes

Let the rate be \(r_t\) in year \(t\), with \(\mathrm{DTL}_t = r_t K_t / 100\). Show that \(\mathrm{TE}_t = \big(r_t P_t + (r_t - r_{t-1}) K_{t-1}\big) / 100\).

Solution

\(\mathrm{TE}_t = r_t(P_t - d_t)/100 + (r_t K_t - r_{t-1} K_{t-1})/100\), where \(d_t = \tau_t - b_t\). Substitute \(K_t = K_{t-1} + d_t\): the \(d_t\) terms are \(-r_t d_t/100 + r_t d_t/100\), which cancel, leaving \((r_t P_t + r_t K_{t-1} - r_{t-1} K_{t-1})/100\). The extra term is \((r_t - r_{t-1})K_{t-1}/100\), which is zero when the rate does not change or when \(K_{t-1} = 0\).

Research

R1 · Recognize a deferred tax asset

Add a deductible accrual to the model: an amount \(A\) expensed in year 1 and deducted for tax in year 3. Give the temporary difference, the deferred tax asset at each year end, and the identity in Theorem 2.1 that changes sign. Then state the recognition rule the model would need, and say why the lesson does not supply it.

Solution

In the signed form, the accrual gives \(\tau_t - b_t = -A\) in year 1 (book expense, no deduction), \(0\) in year 2 and \(+A\) in year 3 (the deduction, with no book expense). So \(K_t = -A\) in years 1 and 2, and \(K_3 = 0\). The signed balance \(rK_t/100\) is \(-rA/100\) in years 1 and 2, which is an asset of \(rA/100\), and zero after year 3. Identities (a) and (b) hold with the sign as written: their proofs in Theorem 2.1 use only \(K_t - K_{t-1} = \tau_t - b_t\), which is the definition of \(K_t\) here, and the definitions of \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\). Identity (a), \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\), is the one that changes sign: its right side is \(-rA/100\) in year 1 and \(+rA/100\) in year 3. Identity (d) follows from (c) by telescoping. Identity (c) holds here. Its proof in Theorem 2.1 rests on total tax deductions equal to total book expense, \(\sum_t \tau_t = \sum_t b_t\); in this accrual both sums equal \(A\), so the same condition holds. Whether the asset may be recognized is outside the model: it has no income forecast, and the lesson states no recognition test.

R2 · A rate change in year 3

For Example 1.4 with \(P_3 = \$50{,}000\), the rate is 21 percent in years 1 and 2 and 25 percent in year 3. Compute \(\mathrm{TE}_3\), and explain why it differs from \(0.25 \times 50{,}000\).

Solution

\(K_2 = \$30{,}000\) and \(\tau_3 - b_3 = 0\), so \(\mathrm{CT}_3 = 0.25 \times 50{,}000 = \$12{,}500\). The balance at the end of year 3 is \(0.25 \times 30{,}000 = \$7{,}500\), and at the end of year 2 it was \(0.21 \times 30{,}000 = \$6{,}300\). So \(\mathrm{DE}_3 = \$1{,}200\) and \(\mathrm{TE}_3 = \$13{,}700\). The difference from \(0.25 \times 50{,}000 = \$12{,}500\) is \(\$1{,}200\), which is the term \((r_3 - r_2) K_2 / 100 = 4 \times 30{,}000 / 100\) from P3.

R3 · What evidence would test the timing

State the evidence that would show whether a model with a flat tax schedule matches the timing of a real firm's tax, and name the input the model would need that it does not have. Make no claim about any firm.

Solution

The test needs the firm's tax-depreciation schedule for each asset, its book schedule, its pretax income and the rate in each year, which together give the temporary differences and the balances of Definition 1.2. The model lacks the per-asset tax schedules and the year-by-year rates, and it has one asset. This lesson cites no registry key for those inputs, so it makes no claim about the timing of any firm.
