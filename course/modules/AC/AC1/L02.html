The Three Statements

  • Uses: double-entry bookkeeping, accrual accounting
  • Introduces: the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash-flow statement
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore

A firm reports three statements, and each answers a different question: what it earned over a period, what it owned and owed at one date, and where the period's cash came from and went. This lesson defines each statement from the same ledger, proves that the balance sheet balances from the ledger's own arithmetic, and states which statement measures which quantity. Lesson 3 then tests whether the three agree with one another.

1A flow and a stock

The three statements differ in kind before they differ in content. Two of them report an amount accumulated over an interval. One reports an amount held at a date. Definition 1.1 gives the names. Reading one as the other is an error, and the rest of the lesson depends on keeping them apart.

Definition 1.1 (flow and stock)

A flow is an amount measured over a period, such as a year: revenue, expense or cash received. A stock is an amount measured at a date, such as cash held or a debt outstanding. A flow has a start and an end; a stock has a single date.

Definition 1.2 (the three statements)

The income statement reports flows over a fiscal year: revenue, the costs that match it, and net income. The balance sheet reports stocks at a fiscal year end: assets, liabilities and equity. The cash-flow statement reports flows over the year that move the cash account, grouped by the activity that caused each movement.

Lab 2 generates three fiscal years and an opening position at the end of 2024. The income statement is printed for 2025, 2026 and 2027, the balance sheet at the four year ends from 2024 to 2027, and the cash-flow statement for the three years. Amounts are integer cents, printed in USD.

2The income statement

Definition 2.1 (net income)

Net income for a fiscal year is revenue less the costs recognized in that year. In the ledger the revenue and expense accounts are flow accounts: their period balance is the net of their debits and credits within the year. Writing \(S\) for sales, \(K\) for cost of sales, \(O\) for operating expense and \(D\) for the depreciation charge of the year, a non-cash expense whose definition Lesson 3 gives, net income is \(N = S - K - O - D\).

Proposition 2.2 (net income is minus the sum of the period's income-account balances)

Let \(n_a\) be the period's debits minus credits on account \(a\), and let \(I\) be the set of revenue and expense accounts, which in this lab are the four accounts of Definition 2.1. Then \(N = -\sum_{a \in I} n_a\).

Proof

Revenue accounts have credit balances, so \(n_{\text{sales}} = -S\). Expense accounts have debit balances, so \(n_a = +x_a\) for each cost \(x_a\). Then \(-\sum_{a \in I} n_a = S - \sum_a x_a = S - K - O - D = N\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2.2 is the reason the income statement can be read straight from the ledger. The sign convention lets one sum produce net income. Lab 2 computes net income directly, as sales less cost of sales, operating expense and depreciation, each read from its own account. Those four are the only income accounts in the lab, so the direct figure is the Proposition 2.2 sum.

3The balance sheet balances

Definition 3.1 (retained earnings)

Retained earnings at a year end is the cumulative net income of all years to date, less the cumulative dividends paid. The lab has no closing entries that move income into a retained-earnings account, so retained earnings is computed as this cumulative figure. Lesson 3 checks that this computed figure rolls forward correctly from one year to the next.

Proposition 3.2 (assets equal liabilities plus equity at every year end)

For any ledger of balanced entries whose only equity accounts are common stock and dividends, and for any year end, total assets equal total liabilities plus total equity, where equity is common stock plus retained earnings as in Definition 3.1.

Proof

Let \(A\) be the net debit balance of the asset accounts, including the contra-asset account for accumulated depreciation with its credit balance. Let \(L\) be the credit balance of the liability accounts, \(C\) the credit balance of common stock, and \(H\) the debit balance of dividends. By Proposition 2.1 the net of all accounts, summed with signs debits minus credits, is zero. Partition the accounts into balance-sheet accounts and income-statement accounts. The balance-sheet accounts net to \(A - L - C + H\). The income-statement accounts net to \(-N\) by Proposition 2.2 applied to the cumulative period. Hence \(A - L - C + H - N = 0\), which gives \(A = L + C + (N - H)\). Since \(N - H\) is retained earnings, \(A = L + (C + \text{retained earnings})\). \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 3.3 (what the proof uses)

The proof uses only that the ledger is balanced (Proposition 2.1), that the accounts are partitioned into balance-sheet and income accounts, and that the equity accounts are the two named in the hypothesis. Dividends are treated as a debit that reduces equity directly. No tax, interest, inventory or other account is needed for the identity, which is why the lab can omit them and still balance.

Lab 2 prints the balance sheet at each year end and one flag that is True when total assets equal total liabilities and equity at every year end.

4The cash-flow statement

Definition 4.1 (direct-method cash-flow statement)

The direct-method cash-flow statement of a period lists every line on the cash account in the period, grouped by memo, and subtotals the lines by activity. The activity of each cash line is set when its entry is posted, by the lab's generator: operating for cash sales, collections of receivables, and payments of operating expense and of payables; investing for equipment purchases; and financing for borrowing, owner investment and dividends. The classification is a convention of the lab and is stated as one.

Proposition 4.2 (the cash flows explain the change in cash)

Hypothesis: every cash line of the ledger carries one of the three activities operating, investing or financing. Let \(\Delta k\) be the change in the cash balance over a year. Then the sum of the year's cash lines, which is the net of the three activity subtotals, equals \(\Delta k\).

Proof

The cash balance is the sum of the debits minus credits on the cash account over all years to the year end. Subtract the same sum taken to the prior year end, and what remains is the sum of the cash lines posted during the year. Every cash line carries an activity, so the three activity subtotals partition those lines, and their sum is the same total. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 4.2 says the cash-flow statement is complete for the cash account. It does not say the subtotals are the right ones for an analyst. A classification is a choice about what an activity is, and two firms can classify the same cash line differently. Lesson 3 derives operating cash flow a second way, from net income, and the two routes agree in the lab.

Lab 2 · Sorting one ledger into three statements (SIMULATED)

The lab generates a SIMULATED firm from a seeded random number generator: an opening position at the end of 2024, then four quarters in each of 2025, 2026 and 2027. Stated before running: the balance sheet balances at all four year ends, so the printed flag is True; net income in each year is the sales less the three cost lines; the printed net change in cash is the sum of the three activity subtotals, and the balance-sheet cash is printed at each year end beside it; and the lowest cash balance is the opening position, because cash rises in each year.

"""AC1 lab L02: sort a posted ledger into the income statement, the balance sheet and the cash-flow statement.

SIMULATED: the quarterly credit sales, cash sales, operating expense and cost percentage (55 to 65 per cent) are drawn
from a seeded random number generator for this lab. Cost of sales is that percentage of the quarter's sales. The
opening position, equipment, borrowing, dividends and depreciation are fixed amounts in the code. Collections are
four fifths of the receivable and payments are nine tenths of the payable, both fixed fractions of balances. It is
not a real company.
Amounts are integer cents and are printed in USD. The three statements are produced from one ledger, one at a
time; this lab does not yet check that they agree with one another. Standard library only; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 20261027
OPENING_YEAR = 2024
YEARS: tuple[int, ...] = (2025, 2026, 2027)

# Account -> type. A debit-normal account grows with debits; a credit-normal account grows with credits.
TYPES: dict[str, str] = {
    "cash": "asset",
    "accounts_receivable": "asset",
    "equipment": "asset",
    "accumulated_depreciation": "contra-asset",
    "accounts_payable": "liability",
    "long_term_debt": "liability",
    "common_stock": "equity",
    "dividends": "equity",
    "sales": "revenue",
    "cost_of_sales": "expense",
    "operating_expense": "expense",
    "depreciation_expense": "expense",
}
DEBIT_NORMAL = frozenset(
    {
        "cash",
        "accounts_receivable",
        "equipment",
        "dividends",
        "cost_of_sales",
        "operating_expense",
        "depreciation_expense",
    }
)
ACTIVITIES = ("operating", "investing", "financing", "noncash")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Line:
    """One side of one account, in integer cents. Exactly one of debit and credit is non-zero."""

    account: str
    debit: int = 0
    credit: int = 0


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Entry:
    """A journal entry dated by fiscal year and quarter. activity classifies its cash effect."""

    year: int
    quarter: int
    memo: str
    activity: str
    lines: tuple[Line, ...]


def check_entry(entry: Entry) -> None:
    """Raise ValueError unless the entry is valid: known accounts, integer amounts, balanced, classified."""
    if entry.activity not in ACTIVITIES:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: activity must be one of {ACTIVITIES}, not {entry.activity!r}")
    if not 1 <= entry.quarter <= 4:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: quarter must be 1 to 4, not {entry.quarter}")
    debits = 0
    credits = 0
    for line in entry.lines:
        if line.account not in TYPES:
            raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: unknown account {line.account!r}")
        for amount in (line.debit, line.credit):
            if isinstance(amount, bool) or not isinstance(amount, int) or amount < 0:
                raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: amounts must be non-negative integer cents, got {amount!r}")
        if line.debit and line.credit:
            raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: one line carries both a debit and a credit on {line.account!r}")
        debits += line.debit
        credits += line.credit
    if debits != credits:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: debits {debits} do not equal credits {credits}")


class Ledger:
    """Holds only entries that pass check_entry."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.entries: list[Entry] = []

    def post(self, entry: Entry) -> None:
        check_entry(entry)
        self.entries.append(entry)

    def net(self, account: str, year: int | None = None, through: int | None = None) -> int:
        """Debits minus credits. year selects one fiscal year; through selects every year up to that one."""
        total = 0
        for entry in self.entries:
            if year is not None and entry.year != year:
                continue
            if through is not None and entry.year > through:
                continue
            for line in entry.lines:
                if line.account == account:
                    total += line.debit - line.credit
        return total

    def balance(self, account: str, through: int) -> int:
        """The balance on the account's normal side at the end of the given year, in integer cents."""
        net = self.net(account, through=through)
        return net if account in DEBIT_NORMAL else -net


def money(cents: int) -> str:
    """Format integer cents as dollars and cents: 1234567 becomes 12,345.67 and -50 becomes -0.50."""
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    dollars, rest = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
    return f"{sign}{dollars:,}.{rest:02d}"


def generate(ledger: Ledger, rng: random.Random) -> None:
    """Post the opening position and four quarters of each year, drawing every amount from rng."""
    ledger.post(
        Entry(
            OPENING_YEAR,
            4,
            "owner invests cash",
            "financing",
            (Line("cash", debit=800_000_000), Line("common_stock", credit=800_000_000)),
        )
    )
    ledger.post(
        Entry(
            OPENING_YEAR,
            4,
            "equipment A bought for cash",
            "investing",
            (Line("equipment", debit=500_000_000), Line("cash", credit=500_000_000)),
        )
    )
    for year in YEARS:
        for quarter in (1, 2, 3, 4):
            credit_sales = rng.randrange(180_000_000, 240_000_000)
            cash_sales = rng.randrange(20_000_000, 40_000_000)
            cost_percent = rng.randrange(55, 66)
            operating = rng.randrange(40_000_000, 50_000_000)
            if year == 2026 and quarter == 1:
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "equipment B bought for cash",
                        "investing",
                        (Line("equipment", debit=150_000_000), Line("cash", credit=150_000_000)),
                    )
                )
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "borrowing",
                        "financing",
                        (Line("cash", debit=100_000_000), Line("long_term_debt", credit=100_000_000)),
                    )
                )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "credit sale",
                    "noncash",
                    (Line("accounts_receivable", debit=credit_sales), Line("sales", credit=credit_sales)),
                )
            )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "cash sale",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("cash", debit=cash_sales), Line("sales", credit=cash_sales)),
                )
            )
            purchases = (credit_sales + cash_sales) * cost_percent // 100
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "cost of sales on account",
                    "noncash",
                    (Line("cost_of_sales", debit=purchases), Line("accounts_payable", credit=purchases)),
                )
            )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "operating expense paid in cash",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("operating_expense", debit=operating), Line("cash", credit=operating)),
                )
            )
            collection = ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year) * 4 // 5
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "collection of receivables",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("cash", debit=collection), Line("accounts_receivable", credit=collection)),
                )
            )
            payment = ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year) * 9 // 10
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "payment of payables",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("accounts_payable", debit=payment), Line("cash", credit=payment)),
                )
            )
            depreciation = 25_000_000 + (7_500_000 if year >= 2026 else 0)
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "depreciation of equipment",
                    "noncash",
                    (
                        Line("depreciation_expense", debit=depreciation),
                        Line("accumulated_depreciation", credit=depreciation),
                    ),
                )
            )
            if quarter == 4:
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "dividends paid",
                        "financing",
                        (Line("dividends", debit=20_000_000), Line("cash", credit=20_000_000)),
                    )
                )


def income_statement(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Revenue, cost lines and net income for one fiscal year, in integer cents (costs positive)."""
    sales = -ledger.net("sales", year=year)
    cost = ledger.net("cost_of_sales", year=year)
    opex = ledger.net("operating_expense", year=year)
    dep = ledger.net("depreciation_expense", year=year)
    return {
        "sales": sales,
        "cost_of_sales": cost,
        "operating_expense": opex,
        "depreciation": dep,
        "net_income": sales - cost - opex - dep,
    }


def balance_sheet(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Balances at the end of one fiscal year (the opening position is year 2024). Retained earnings is the
    cumulative income statement less cumulative dividends, since the ledger has no closing entries."""
    cash = ledger.balance("cash", year)
    receivables = ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year)
    equipment = ledger.balance("equipment", year)
    depreciated = ledger.balance("accumulated_depreciation", year)
    payables = ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year)
    debt = ledger.balance("long_term_debt", year)
    common = ledger.balance("common_stock", year)
    cumulative_income = -(
        ledger.net("sales", through=year)
        + ledger.net("cost_of_sales", through=year)
        + ledger.net("operating_expense", through=year)
        + ledger.net("depreciation_expense", through=year)
    )
    retained = cumulative_income - ledger.balance("dividends", year)
    assets = cash + receivables + equipment - depreciated
    liabilities = payables + debt
    equity = common + retained
    return {
        "cash": cash,
        "accounts_receivable": receivables,
        "equipment_at_cost": equipment,
        "accumulated_depreciation": depreciated,
        "total_assets": assets,
        "accounts_payable": payables,
        "long_term_debt": debt,
        "total_liabilities": liabilities,
        "common_stock": common,
        "retained_earnings": retained,
        "total_equity": equity,
        "total_liabilities_and_equity": liabilities + equity,
    }


def cash_flow_statement(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> tuple[dict[str, int], dict[str, int]]:
    """The direct-method cash-flow statement: cash lines grouped by memo, and subtotals by activity."""
    by_memo: dict[str, int] = {}
    by_activity = {activity: 0 for activity in ACTIVITIES}
    for entry in ledger.entries:
        if entry.year != year:
            continue
        for line in entry.lines:
            if line.account == "cash":
                amount = line.debit - line.credit
                by_memo[entry.memo] = by_memo.get(entry.memo, 0) + amount
                by_activity[entry.activity] += amount
    return by_memo, by_activity


def main() -> None:
    ledger = Ledger()
    generate(ledger, random.Random(SEED))

    print("Income statement, USD (fiscal years)")
    columns = [income_statement(ledger, y) for y in YEARS]
    rows = ("sales", "cost_of_sales", "operating_expense", "depreciation", "net_income")
    print(f"{'':<22}" + "".join(f"{y:>18}" for y in YEARS))
    for row in rows:
        print(f"{row:<22}" + "".join(f"{money(c[row]):>18}" for c in columns))

    print()
    print("Balance sheet at fiscal year end, USD (2024 is the opening position)")
    years_bs = (OPENING_YEAR, *YEARS)
    sheets = [balance_sheet(ledger, y) for y in years_bs]
    print(f"{'':<30}" + "".join(f"{y:>18}" for y in years_bs))
    for row in sheets[0]:
        print(f"{row:<30}" + "".join(f"{money(s[row]):>18}" for s in sheets))
    balanced = [s["total_assets"] == s["total_liabilities_and_equity"] for s in sheets]
    print(f"total assets equal total liabilities and equity at every year end: {all(balanced)}")

    print()
    print("Cash-flow statement, direct method, USD")
    print(f"{'':<36}" + "".join(f"{y:>18}" for y in YEARS))
    memos = list(
        dict.fromkeys(e.memo for e in ledger.entries if e.year in YEARS and any(x.account == "cash" for x in e.lines))
    )
    statements = [cash_flow_statement(ledger, y) for y in YEARS]
    for memo in memos:
        cells = "".join(f"{money(by_memo.get(memo, 0)):>18}" for by_memo, _ in statements)
        print(f"{memo:<36}{cells}")
    for activity in ACTIVITIES[:3]:
        cells = "".join(f"{money(by_activity[activity]):>18}" for _, by_activity in statements)
        print(f"{'subtotal, ' + activity:<36}{cells}")
    net_change = [sum(by_activity.values()) for _, by_activity in statements]
    print(f"{'net change in cash':<36}" + "".join(f"{money(x):>18}" for x in net_change))
    ending = [sheets[i + 1]["cash"] for i in range(len(YEARS))]
    print(f"{'ending cash, balance sheet':<36}" + "".join(f"{money(x):>18}" for x in ending))
    print(f"lowest cash balance at any year end (USD): {money(min(s['cash'] for s in sheets))}")

5Reading the three together

A statement answers one question, and it is read against the others. Net income of 1,003,421.14 in 2025 says the firm earned that much on accrual terms. The change in retained earnings is that net income less dividends of 200,000.00, which is 803,421.14. The change in cash is 1,454,305.29. Its operating part, 1,654,305.29, differs from net income by the depreciation add-back and by the changes in receivables and payables, which Lesson 3 derives. Lesson 3 shows exactly how the cash change is assembled from profit and the balance-sheet changes, and where each piece sits.

6Where the canon breaks

The three statements are a convention for presenting one ledger. Four points mark where a reader relies on them further than they can bear.

  • A balance sheet is a snapshot, not a sum of activity. Proposition 3.2 holds at every date, but the balance sheet at a date says nothing about the path that produced it.
  • Net income is an accrual measure. Proposition 2.2 sums the revenue and cost accounts of the ledger, which record revenue when a sale is made and cost when it is incurred (Lesson 1, Definition 1.3). Net income is not the cash the firm generated, and Lesson 1 gave the identity that links the two.
  • The activity split is a convention. Definition 4.1 classifies each cash line by the generator. A real firm's rules may differ, and a different classification can move cash between operating and investing. Proposition 4.2 holds under any classification into the three activities, so the total is unaffected and the subtotals are not.
  • The lab omits real items. There are no taxes, interest, inventory or intangible assets in this firm. Each omission keeps the identities intact, because they are proved without those accounts, but it means no figure here is a real firm's.

7Misconceptions

  1. “Net income is the cash the firm made in the year.”

    Net income is the accrual result of Proposition 2.2. In Lab 2's 2025 the net income is 1,003,421.14, and the operating cash flow is 1,654,305.29. The two differ by the depreciation add-back and by the changes in receivables and payables, which Lesson 3 derives. The statement that reports cash is the cash-flow statement.

  2. “The balance sheet shows what happened during the year.”

    The balance sheet is a stock (Definition 1.1). In Lab 2 the cash rises from 3,000,000.00 at the opening position to 4,454,305.29 at the end of 2025, but that change is in the cash-flow statement. The balance sheet shows the two dates and nothing between them.

  3. “A balance sheet that balances proves the figures are right.”

    Proposition 3.2 is a consequence of double entry. A wrong amount posted to one side and a matching wrong amount to the other keeps the balance sheet in balance, as Lesson 1's Remark 2.2 notes for a balanced entry on the wrong account.

Mechanics

M1 · Flow or stock

Classify each of these as a flow or a stock: interest paid in 2026, the cash held on 31 December 2026, the equipment at cost on 31 December 2026, and the depreciation charged in 2026.

Solution

Interest paid in 2026 and depreciation charged in 2026 are flows, because each accumulates over the year (Definition 1.1). The cash held on 31 December 2026 and the equipment at cost on that date are stocks, because each is measured at a date.

Proof

P1 · Net income under a reversed sign

Show that if the sign convention for the income accounts were reversed, net income would be \(\sum_{a \in I} n_a\) rather than \(-\sum_{a \in I} n_a\), and explain why the convention in Proposition 2.2 is the one that gives a positive profit.

Solution

Under the ledger's debit-minus-credit reading, the income-account balances sum to \(\sum_{a \in I} n_a = -S + K + O + D = -N\). The sum is the negative of net income, so the minus sign in Proposition 2.2 is what turns the ledger reading into a profit. Reading the sum without that sign would report a firm that earned more than it spent as having a loss of the same size. The convention therefore fixes the sign of the answer, and the proof of Proposition 2.2 is the step that justifies it.

P2 · The balance identity with a non-zero dividend

Show that the identity of Proposition 3.2 holds in a year with a dividend, by writing out the proof's terms for that year.

Solution

In a year with a dividend \(H\), the dividends account carries \(H\) as a debit, and retained earnings is \(N - H\). The proof's sum is \(A - L - C + H - N = 0\), which holds for the ledger. Substituting \(H\) and \(N\) gives \(A = L + C + (N - H)\), so assets equal liabilities plus common stock plus retained earnings after the dividend. The dividend reduces both cash, which is in \(A\), and retained earnings, which is \(N - H\), by the same amount, so the identity is kept.

P3 · A classification change moves no total

Suppose one cash line is reclassified from operating to investing. Show that the net change in cash, and the balance sheet cash, are unchanged.

Solution

Proposition 4.2 sums the cash lines over all activities. A reclassification changes which subtotal the line is added to, and it does not change the line's amount. The sum over the three subtotals is therefore unchanged, and so is the net change. The balance sheet cash depends only on the cash account's balance, which the reclassification does not touch. The operating and investing subtotals change, in opposite directions and by the same amount.

Empirical

E1 · Margins across the three years (SIMULATED)

Compute the gross margin, meaning sales less cost of sales over sales, for 2025, 2026 and 2027, and the net margin, meaning net income over sales. Show the basis and give each figure to two decimal places of a percent.

E2 · Cash from customers as a share of sales (SIMULATED)

Research

R1 · A real firm's three statements

Specify a check that a real firm's three statements agree: which figures are needed, which identity is tested for each, and what a failure would show.

Solution

Take the reported net income, dividends, the opening and closing equity and cash, the operating, investing and financing subtotals, and the reported total assets and total liabilities and equity. Test that assets equal liabilities plus equity at each date. Test that retained earnings rolls forward by net income less dividends. Test that the subtotals sum to the change in cash. A failure in the first test may mean the balance sheet is mis-stated or a component is missing from the extract. A failure in the second may point to other equity movements, such as share issues or other comprehensive items, that the simple roll-forward does not capture. The check needs the filer's own definition of each line, so no real filing is used here. The SIMULATED firm has none of those items.

R2 · Two presentations of one ledger

Two analysts present the same ledger, one with interest in operating expense and one with interest in financing. Describe what changes in the statements and what does not.

Solution

Interest in operating expense reduces net income by the expense and reduces operating cash flow by the cash paid. Moving it to financing leaves net income unchanged, because the expense is still counted in the income statement once, and the cash paid reduces the financing subtotal instead of the operating one. What does not change is the total net change in cash, which is the sum of all cash lines (Proposition 4.2), and the balance sheet, which is the same ledger at the same date. The difference is the activity split, a classification choice (Section 6, third item).

R3 · Retained earnings with share repurchases

A firm repurchases its own shares for 100 cents. Describe the entries, which statement the repurchase enters, and whether Proposition 3.2 is affected.

Solution

The entry debits a treasury or equity account and credits cash for 100 cents. Cash falls by 100 and the equity account falls by 100, so assets and equity fall by the same amount in this example, and the income statement is unchanged. Proposition 3.2 does not cover this case: its hypothesis names common stock and dividends as the only equity accounts, and a treasury account is another equity account, so the general identity is not claimed here. Definition 4.1 does not classify a repurchase, and the lab has no repurchase account, so this page does not assign one: the classification is open.
