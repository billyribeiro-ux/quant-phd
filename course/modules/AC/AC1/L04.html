The same transactions can produce different net income under two accounting rule sets, and the gap can look like a difference in performance. This lesson separates the three kinds of difference a comparison has to keep apart: timing of recognition, classification within the cash-flow statement, and presentation. For timing and classification it proves what must stay the same. Lab 1 applies two recognition rules and two cash-flow presentations to one SIMULATED set of 36 ledger entries over eight quarters. The lesson does not state what US GAAP or IFRS Standards require, because this lesson cites neither primary text.

1Two rule sets and three kinds of difference

Definition 1.1 (US GAAP vs. IFRS)

US GAAP vs. IFRS is the comparison of two accounting frameworks: US GAAP and IFRS Standards, the two frameworks this unit compares. Each framework is a set of rules for recognizing, measuring, presenting and disclosing an entity's transactions. A framework difference is a rule difference that changes a reported number for the same transactions.

Remark 1.2 (what this lesson does not assert)

The lesson states no rule of either framework. A claim about what a standard requires needs that standard's text, and this lesson cites neither primary text. The lesson therefore proves identities that hold whichever rule is applied, and it marks each standard-specific question it raises as open. Problem R2 lists the standard-specific questions this lesson leaves open.

Definition 1.3 (three kinds of difference)

A timing difference is a rule difference that changes the period in which an amount enters net income. A classification difference is a rule difference that moves an amount between the operating, investing and financing sections of the cash-flow statement. A presentation difference is a rule difference that changes a label or a grouping and leaves every amount as it was. Section 2 studies timing and Section 3 studies classification. A presentation difference leaves every amount unchanged, so none of the identities in this lesson is affected by it.

Remark 1.4 (what a comparison needs)

A comparison of two reported numbers needs the same transactions, the same units and the same horizon. Lab 1 holds all three fixed by construction: one generated set, integer cents throughout, and eight quarters. Any difference between the two rules' figures comes from the rules, not from the inputs.

2Timing: recognition rules and cash

The lab compares two recognition rules, and both are conventions of this lab; this page states neither as a framework rule. Under rule A, a service paid for in advance is expensed in full in the quarter it is paid. This is a definition of the lab's convention (the lab code in Lab 1), and the page makes no claim that it is a framework rule. Under rule B, the same payment is expensed in four shares over the four quarters it covers: each of the first three is the payment divided by four, rounded down to whole cents, and the last share takes the remainder so the shares sum to the payment exactly. A payment in quarter \(s\) therefore produces rule-B expense in quarters \(s\) to \(s+3\), truncated at the end of the horizon.

Proposition 2.1 (cash does not depend on the recognition rule)

Let the same receipts and payments occur under rule A and under rule B. Then the cash balance at every quarter end is the same under both rules.

Proof

Cash changes by receipts less payments in each quarter. A recognition rule changes the quarter in which an expense enters net income. It does not change whether a payment is made, when it is made, or how much it is. Both rules take the same payments in the same quarters, so the cash balance is the same at every quarter end.

Definition 2.2 (the balances)

With zero opening balances, no equity contributions and no distributions, let \(C_t\) be cash at the end of quarter \(t\), \(R_t\) the receivable at that date, and \(P_t\) the unexpired prepaid balance under rule B. Let \(N^A_t\) and \(N^B_t\) be cumulative net income from the first quarter through \(t\) under rule A and rule B. A receivable is the credit sale of the quarter, collected one quarter later, so \(R_t\) equals the credit sales of quarter \(t\).

Proposition 2.2 (the balance identity under each rule)

For every quarter \(t\), \(C_t + R_t = N^A_t\) under rule A, and \(C_t + R_t + P_t = N^B_t\) under rule B. Both identities are exact in integer cents.

Proof

Write \(S_t\) for cash sales, \(K_t\) for credit sales, \(Q_t\) for prepaid payments, \(O_t\) for operating expenses, \(I_t\) for interest paid, and \(E_t\) for rule-B expense, all in quarter \(t\). Revenue in quarter \(t\) is \(S_t + K_t\). Collections in quarter \(t\) are \(K_{t-1}\), so cumulative receipts through \(t\) are \(\sum_{s \le t} S_s + \sum_{s < t} K_s\). Cumulative payments through \(t\) are \(\sum_{s \le t}(Q_s + O_s + I_s)\). Therefore \[ C_t = \sum_{s \le t} S_s + \sum_{s < t} K_s - \sum_{s \le t}(Q_s + O_s + I_s). \] Adding \(R_t = K_t\) gives \[ C_t + R_t = \sum_{s \le t}(S_s + K_s) - \sum_{s \le t}(Q_s + O_s + I_s) = N^A_t, \] because under rule A the expense in each quarter is \(Q_s + O_s + I_s\). Under rule B the prepaid balance is \(P_t = \sum_{s \le t} Q_s - \sum_{s \le t} E_s\). Adding it gives \(C_t + R_t + P_t = \sum_{s \le t}(S_s + K_s) - \sum_{s \le t}(O_s + I_s + E_s) = N^B_t\). Every term is an integer, so both identities hold exactly.

Corollary 2.3 (the cumulative difference is the unexpired balance)

For every quarter \(t\), \(N^A_t - N^B_t = -P_t\). A timing difference therefore appears as a difference in cumulative net income equal to minus the unexpired balance, and it reverses as that balance expires.

Proof

Subtract the rule-B identity of Proposition 2.2 from the rule-A identity. The cash and receivable terms cancel, leaving \(N^A_t - N^B_t = -P_t\).

Remark 2.4 (why the horizon matters)

Corollary 2.3 is exact at every quarter, not only at the end. A comparison made at a date when a balance is unexpired is a comparison of cumulative figures that differ by that balance. The difference is zero only when no balance is unexpired at the date compared.

Lab 1 · One SIMULATED set of transactions under two rules and two presentations

The lab generates 36 ledger entries over eight quarters from seed 2604, all in integer cents. Stated before running: the cash balance at the last quarter is identical under both recognition rules (Proposition 2.1), and the lab compares one expression with itself, so that match checks the code and not the rules; cash plus receivables equals cumulative net income under rule A, and cash plus receivables plus the unexpired prepaid balance equals it under rule B (Proposition 2.2); the cumulative net-income difference at the last quarter equals minus the unexpired prepaid balance (Corollary 2.3); and interest paid moves between operating and financing cash flow with the total cash flow unchanged (Proposition 3.1 of Section 3).

"""AC1 L04 lab 1: one SIMULATED transaction set, two recognition rules, two cash-flow presentations.

SIMULATED: every transaction is generated from a seeded generator. No filing is read and no company is represented.
Units: integer cents for every money amount. The ratios printed below are unitless.

Rule A recognizes a prepaid service in full when it is paid. Rule B recognizes the same payment in four shares over
the four quarters the service covers: the first three are the payment divided by four, rounded down, and the last
takes the remainder. These two rules are labelled conventions of this lab. The lab does not claim
that either one is the rule of any accounting framework.

Presentation 1 places interest paid in operating cash flow. Presentation 2 places it in financing cash flow. Both
presentations start from the same cash movements, so the only difference between them is the section that holds
the interest.

Every identity printed as True is an algebraic consequence of the construction, not an empirical result.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 2604
QUARTERS = 8
TERM = 4  # quarters covered by one prepaid service under rule B
PREPAID_PROBABILITY = 1.0 / 3.0


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Quarter:
    """One quarter of SIMULATED activity; every field is an amount in integer cents."""

    cash_sales: int  # sales collected in the quarter of sale
    credit_sales: int  # sales collected one quarter later
    prepaid_paid: int  # service paid in advance in this quarter (zero when none is bought)
    opex: int  # operating expenses, paid in the quarter they are incurred
    interest: int  # interest paid in the quarter


def generate(seed: int) -> list[Quarter]:
    """Eight quarters of seeded activity: one of each item per quarter, and a prepaid purchase with probability 1/3."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    quarters: list[Quarter] = []
    for _ in range(QUARTERS):
        cash_sales = rng.randint(50_000_00, 120_000_00)
        credit_sales = rng.randint(20_000_00, 60_000_00)
        prepaid_paid = rng.randint(40_000_00, 90_000_00) if rng.random() < PREPAID_PROBABILITY else 0
        opex = rng.randint(30_000_00, 70_000_00)
        interest = rng.randint(2_000_00, 5_000_00)
        quarters.append(Quarter(cash_sales, credit_sales, prepaid_paid, opex, interest))
    return quarters


def shares(amount: int) -> list[int]:
    """Split an amount into TERM whole-cent shares that sum to it exactly; the last share takes the remainder."""
    base = amount // TERM
    return [base] * (TERM - 1) + [amount - base * (TERM - 1)]


def expense_rule_b(quarters: list[Quarter]) -> list[int]:
    """Rule B expense in cents for each quarter: each prepaid payment spread over the TERM quarters it covers."""
    expense = [0] * QUARTERS
    for start, item in enumerate(quarters):
        for offset, share in enumerate(shares(item.prepaid_paid)):
            if start + offset < QUARTERS:
                expense[start + offset] += share
    return expense


def cumulative(values: list[int]) -> list[int]:
    """Running total in cents from the first quarter."""
    total = 0
    out: list[int] = []
    for value in values:
        total += value
        out.append(total)
    return out


def main() -> None:
    quarters = generate(SEED)
    entries = sum(1 for q in quarters for amount in (q.cash_sales, q.credit_sales, q.opex, q.interest) if amount)
    entries += sum(1 for q in quarters if q.prepaid_paid)
    print(f"seed {SEED} quarters {QUARTERS} ledger_entries {entries}")

    revenue = [q.cash_sales + q.credit_sales for q in quarters]
    collections = [0] + [quarters[t - 1].credit_sales for t in range(1, QUARTERS)]  # one-quarter collection lag
    receipts = [q.cash_sales + c for q, c in zip(quarters, collections, strict=True)]
    paid = [q.prepaid_paid + q.opex + q.interest for q in quarters]

    # Net income under each rule: revenue less the expenses that rule recognizes in the quarter.
    expense_b = expense_rule_b(quarters)
    ni_a = [r - q.opex - q.interest - q.prepaid_paid for r, q in zip(revenue, quarters, strict=True)]
    ni_b = [r - q.opex - q.interest - e for r, q, e in zip(revenue, quarters, expense_b, strict=True)]

    cash_change = [r - p for r, p in zip(receipts, paid, strict=True)]
    cash_end = cumulative(cash_change)
    receivable_end = [q.credit_sales for q in quarters]
    prepaid_balance_b = [
        c - e for c, e in zip(cumulative([q.prepaid_paid for q in quarters]), cumulative(expense_b), strict=True)
    ]

    for t, q in enumerate(quarters):
        print(
            f"q{t} revenue {revenue[t]} ni_rule_a {ni_a[t]} ni_rule_b {ni_b[t]} "
            f"cash_change {cash_change[t]} prepaid_balance_b {prepaid_balance_b[t]} interest {q.interest}"
        )

    # Identity 1: the cash balance does not depend on the recognition rule.
    cash_rule_a = cumulative(cash_change)[-1]
    cash_rule_b = cash_end[-1]
    print(f"cash_end_cents_rule_a {cash_rule_a} cash_end_cents_rule_b {cash_rule_b}")
    print(f"cash_identical_across_rules {cash_rule_a == cash_rule_b}")

    # Identity 2: at each quarter end, cash plus receivables equals cumulative net income under the rule.
    # Under rule B the unexpired prepaid balance is an asset as well.
    cum_ni_a = cumulative(ni_a)
    cum_ni_b = cumulative(ni_b)
    balance_a = all(c + r == n for c, r, n in zip(cash_end, receivable_end, cum_ni_a, strict=True))
    balance_b = all(
        c + r + p == n for c, r, p, n in zip(cash_end, receivable_end, prepaid_balance_b, cum_ni_b, strict=True)
    )
    print(f"balance_identity_rule_a {balance_a}")
    print(f"balance_identity_rule_b {balance_b}")

    # Identity 3: over the horizon, the cumulative difference in net income is minus the unexpired prepaid balance.
    diff_cum = cum_ni_a[-1] - cum_ni_b[-1]
    print(f"cum_ni_rule_a_cents {cum_ni_a[-1]} cum_ni_rule_b_cents {cum_ni_b[-1]}")
    print(f"cum_difference_cents {diff_cum} unexpired_prepaid_end_rule_b_cents {prepaid_balance_b[-1]}")
    print(f"cumulative_difference_identity {diff_cum == -prepaid_balance_b[-1]}")

    # Presentation 1 (interest in operating) and presentation 2 (interest in financing) of the same cash movements.
    cfo_1 = cash_change
    cfo_2 = [c + q.interest for c, q in zip(cash_change, quarters, strict=True)]
    cff_2 = [-q.interest for q in quarters]
    presentation_ok = all(a - b == -q.interest for a, b, q in zip(cfo_1, cfo_2, quarters, strict=True))
    totals_ok = all(a == b + c for a, b, c in zip(cash_change, cfo_2, cff_2, strict=True))
    print(f"presentation_identity {presentation_ok and totals_ok}")
    print(f"interest_moved_total_cents {sum(q.interest for q in quarters)}")
    print(f"cfo_presentation_1_total_cents {sum(cfo_1)} cfo_presentation_2_total_cents {sum(cfo_2)}")
    print(f"cff_presentation_2_total_cents {sum(cff_2)}")

3Classification: interest paid in two presentations

Presentation 1 places interest paid in operating cash flow. Presentation 2 places it in financing cash flow. Both presentations use the same cash movements, so what changes is the section that holds the interest.

Proposition 3.1 (a classification difference moves operating cash flow by the amount moved)

Let \(\mathrm{CFO}_1\) and \(\mathrm{CFF}_1\) be operating and financing cash flow under presentation 1, and let \(\mathrm{CFO}_2\) and \(\mathrm{CFF}_2\) be the same under presentation 2. If interest paid \(I_t\) is in operating cash flow under presentation 1 and in financing cash flow under presentation 2, then \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 = \mathrm{CFO}_1 + I_t\), \(\mathrm{CFF}_2 = \mathrm{CFF}_1 - I_t\), and \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 + \mathrm{CFF}_2 = \mathrm{CFO}_1 + \mathrm{CFF}_1\).

Proof

Presentation 2 removes the outflow \(I_t\) from operating cash flow, so operating cash flow rises by \(I_t\). It adds the same outflow to financing cash flow, so financing cash flow falls by \(I_t\). The sum of the two sections is unchanged, because the amount is removed from one section and added to the other.

Remark 3.2 (what a classification difference leaves unchanged)

Net income, the cash balance and the change in cash are the same under both presentations. Only the operating and financing sections differ. A figure built from operating cash flow therefore changes with the presentation, while the statements as a whole do not.

Lab 1 (Section 2) prints the quarterly interest and the section totals. Its totals are \(\mathrm{CFO}_1 = 24{,}513{,}189\) and \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 = 27{,}832{,}482\) cents, a difference of 3,319,293 cents, which is the total interest paid. That difference is 0.1193 of the presentation-2 operating total (3,319,293 divided by 27,832,482, unrounded 0.11926). The printed totals and the identity in Proposition 3.1 come from the same seeded set.

5Where the canon breaks

  • The identities hold under both rules, so they do not rank the rules. Proposition 2.2 holds under rule A and under rule B. Corollary 2.3 says that the two differ by the unexpired balance, and says nothing about which one reports the economics better. A ranking needs a decision use, which this lesson does not state.
  • A single-date comparison is a comparison of an unexpired balance. Corollary 2.3 is exact at every date. A reader who compares one quarter under two timing rules is comparing \(-P_t\) with zero. Whether that difference is zero depends on when the purchases were made: Lab 1 shows it is zero at quarter 0 and non-zero at quarters 1 to 7.
  • Operating cash flow is not a rule-invariant figure. Proposition 3.1 shows that a classification choice changes operating cash flow by the amount moved. Net income and the change in cash are unchanged, so a comparison of operating cash flow across presentations needs the classification to be stated first.
  • Standard-specific treatment is the open question. The identities here are exact, but which framework requires which timing or classification for a given transaction is a question about its text. The lesson has no claim on that question, and Problem R2 names the evidence that would settle each question.

6Misconceptions

  1. “If two frameworks report different operating cash flow, one of them must have the cash wrong.”

    Cash is the same under any two recognition rules applied to the same payments and receipts (Proposition 2.1). A difference in operating cash flow between two presentations comes from a classification choice that moves an amount between sections (Proposition 3.1). In Lab 1 the two presentations differ by 3,319,293 cents of operating cash flow while the cash balance is the same in both.

  2. “A timing difference in net income is a difference in profit.”

    Corollary 2.3 shows that the cumulative difference equals minus the unexpired balance. Over the full life of a payment the two rules recognize the same total, because the balance expires to zero. Lab 1 shows the cumulative difference reaching \(-4{,}974{,}286\) cents at quarter 7 and not zero, because the quarter-7 purchase of 6,632,381 cents has only its first share expensed by the horizon, so 4,974,286 cents of it is still unexpired. The earlier purchases, in quarters 1, 3 and 4, have fully expired by quarter 7. A difference that reverses later is not a difference in total profit.

  3. “If net income and cash are unchanged, the statements have not changed.”

    Net income and cash can be unchanged while operating cash flow and financing cash flow change by the same amount in opposite directions (Remark 3.2). Proposition 3.1 gives the size of the move exactly: the interest paid. A reader who ignores the section totals misses a change of 3,319,293 cents in the lab's operating figure.

7Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. All amounts are in cents.

Mechanics

M1 · One prepaid payment under both rules

A service worth 120,000 cents is paid in advance in quarter 1 and covers quarters 1 to 4. Under rule A it is expensed in full in quarter 1. Under rule B it is expensed in four equal shares. Compute the rule-B expense in each quarter, the cumulative net-income difference \(N^A_1 - N^B_1\), and the unexpired rule-B balance at the end of quarter 1. Check Corollary 2.3.

Solution

Under rule B each share is \(120{,}000 / 4 = 30{,}000\) cents, so the expense is 30,000 in each of quarters 1 to 4. Under rule A the expense in quarter 1 is 120,000. Net income under rule A is lower than under rule B by \(120{,}000 - 30{,}000 = 90{,}000\) cents in quarter 1, so \(N^A_1 - N^B_1 = -90{,}000\). The unexpired rule-B balance at the end of quarter 1 is \(120{,}000 - 30{,}000 = 90{,}000\). Corollary 2.3 gives \(N^A_1 - N^B_1 = -P_1 = -90{,}000\), which agrees.

M2 · Moving interest between sections

Under presentation 1, operating cash flow is 200,000 cents and financing cash flow is \(-50{,}000\) cents in a quarter in which interest paid is 5,000 cents. Compute both sections under presentation 2, and the sum of the two sections under each presentation.

Solution

Proposition 3.1 gives \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 = 200{,}000 + 5{,}000 = 205{,}000\) cents and \(\mathrm{CFF}_2 = -50{,}000 - 5{,}000 = -55{,}000\) cents. The sum under presentation 1 is \(200{,}000 - 50{,}000 = 150{,}000\) cents. The sum under presentation 2 is \(205{,}000 - 55{,}000 = 150{,}000\) cents, the same, as the proposition requires.

M3 · Unexpired balance after two purchases

A payment of 120,000 cents is made in quarter 1 and another of 60,000 cents in quarter 2. Both cover four quarters, and rule B spreads each in equal shares. Compute the rule-B expense in quarters 1 to 3, the unexpired balance at the end of quarter 3, and the cumulative difference \(N^A_3 - N^B_3\).

Solution

The first payment gives 30,000 of expense in each of quarters 1 to 4. The second gives \(60{,}000 / 4 = 15{,}000\) in each of quarters 2 to 5. Rule-B expense is therefore 30,000 in quarter 1, 45,000 in quarter 2 and 45,000 in quarter 3. Total payments through quarter 3 are 180,000 cents, and total rule-B expense through quarter 3 is 120,000 cents, so the unexpired balance is 60,000 cents. Corollary 2.3 gives \(N^A_3 - N^B_3 = -60{,}000\), since rule A has expensed all 180,000 cents of payments by quarter 3.

M4 · Cash after a reclassification

Under presentation 2, operating cash flow is 1,500,000 cents, financing cash flow is 400,000 cents and the change in cash is 1,900,000 cents. Under presentation 1 interest paid is 80,000 cents, which sits in financing under presentation 2. Compute the operating and financing sections under presentation 1 and check that the change in cash is unchanged.

Solution

Proposition 3.1 gives \(\mathrm{CFO}_1 = \mathrm{CFO}_2 - I = 1{,}500{,}000 - 80{,}000 = 1{,}420{,}000\) cents, and \(\mathrm{CFF}_1 = \mathrm{CFF}_2 + I = 400{,}000 + 80{,}000 = 480{,}000\) cents. The sum is \(1{,}420{,}000 + 480{,}000 = 1{,}900{,}000\) cents, which equals the change in cash under presentation 2, as required.

Proof

P1 · The balance identity under rule B, with a payment that expires

Prove Proposition 2.2 for rule B at a quarter \(t\) after which some prepaid payment has fully expired. State every step that uses the fact that rule B's shares sum to the payment.

Solution

The proof of Proposition 2.2 uses \(P_t = \sum_{s \le t} Q_s - \sum_{s \le t} E_s\), which is the definition of the unexpired balance. Each payment \(Q_s\) enters \(E\) as its four shares, and the shares sum to \(Q_s\) exactly by construction: the first three are \(\lfloor Q_s/4 \rfloor\) and the last is the remainder. So once a payment has fully expired, the shares it contributed to \(E\) sum to \(Q_s\), and its contribution to \(P_t\) is zero. The identity \(C_t + R_t + P_t = N^B_t\) then holds at every \(t\), with expired payments contributing nothing to either side. The step that uses the exact sum is the expiry: without it, \(P_t\) would not return to zero, and the identity would not describe a balance that expires.

P2 · When the two rules agree at a quarter

Show that \(N^A_t = N^B_t\) if and only if \(P_t = 0\). Then give an example in which \(P_t = 0\) at a quarter with no purchase in that quarter, and show why \(P_t = 0\) cannot hold at a quarter with a positive purchase.

Solution

By Corollary 2.3, \(N^A_t - N^B_t = -P_t\), so the two are equal exactly when \(P_t = 0\). For the example, take a single purchase of \(Q_1 = 40{,}000\) cents and a horizon of \(t = 4\). Four shares of 10,000 cents have been expensed by the end of quarter 4, and the unexpired balance is 0. Quarter 4 has no purchase, and the two rules agree at quarter 4, although a purchase was made in quarter 1 and the rules differed in quarters 1 to 3. The agreement is at one date only, which is why a single-date comparison can mislead. At a quarter with a positive purchase \(Q_t\), the purchase's own unexpired balance after its first share is \(Q_t - \lfloor Q_t / 4 \rfloor \ge 1\), and every other unexpired balance is non-negative, so \(P_t \ge 1\) and the two rules cannot agree there.

P3 · A classification difference cannot change cash

Show from the cash-flow identity that a classification difference of amount \(x\) leaves the change in cash unchanged, using only that the total of the sections is the change in cash under each presentation.

Solution

Under each presentation the change in cash is the sum of the operating, investing and financing sections. Under presentation 2, \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 + \mathrm{CFF}_2 = (\mathrm{CFO}_1 + x) + (\mathrm{CFF}_1 - x) = \mathrm{CFO}_1 + \mathrm{CFF}_1\). Since presentation 1 also sums to the change in cash, the change in cash is the same under both. The argument uses only that the moved amount \(x\) is added to one section and subtracted from another. A reclassification of zero leaves both sections as they were.

Research

R1 · A test on a real reporting entity

Describe, in full, a test of whether a reported difference between two reporting entities is a framework difference. Name every input, the source of each input, the assumptions you would have to check first, and the result that would refute your reading. Do not run it.

Solution

The inputs are the two statements of each entity for the same periods, the framework each entity states it uses, the notes that disclose recognition timing and classification, and the transactions the two entities share if any. The first assumption to check is that the transactions are the same, in amount and in period, since Remark 1.4 requires it. Next, decompose each difference into timing, classification and presentation as in Definition 1.3. A reported difference in cumulative net income that is explained by an unexpired balance (Corollary 2.3) is a timing difference. A difference in operating cash flow with equal net income and equal change in cash is a classification difference (Proposition 3.1). The reading is refuted if a residual remains after both decompositions, because then a difference in amounts or in periods is present and the framework is not the only cause. The source of each rule is the primary text, which this lesson does not cite.

R2 · Which question the primary texts must answer

List the questions a reader must answer from the primary texts before a standard-specific statement about a transaction type is allowed on a page of this unit. For each question, say which proposition of this lesson it would test.

Solution

First, which timing rule applies to a given transaction type, which tests Corollary 2.3 once the rule is known. Second, whether a given cash item is operating, investing or financing, which tests Proposition 3.1. Third, whether a rule changes an amount or only a label, which decides whether the presentation test of Definition 1.3 applies. Fourth, the horizon over which the comparison is made, which tests Remark 2.4. None of these questions can be answered from the identities alone, because the identities hold under both rules of this lesson. Each answer must cite the text that states the rule, with its locator.

R3 · Is a timing difference a measurement difference?

Argue whether a timing difference between two rules is a difference in measurement, a difference in recognition, or both. Use Proposition 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 in the argument, and state what would have to be true of the economics for the two rules to give the same answer at every date.

Solution

The two rules differ in the quarter in which an expense is recognized, so the difference is one of recognition. The amounts that are eventually recognized are the same, so it is not a difference in the total measurement of the payment. Corollary 2.3 shows the difference at a date is exactly the unexpired balance. For the two rules to agree at every date, that balance would have to be zero at every date, which means every payment would have to be expensed in the quarter it is made. Rule B is then identical to rule A, and the two rules cannot disagree. A timing difference that never reverses within the horizon is a real difference in the cumulative figure at that date, and the argument depends on whether a reader compares dates before or after reversal.

9Retrieval quiz

Answer from memory, then check. Finishing the quiz adds this lesson's cards to your review deck.

  1. Under rule A a payment is expensed in full when paid, and under rule B it is spread over four quarters. Which identity links the cumulative net-income difference to the unexpired balance?

    Options

    Subtracting the rule-B balance identity from the rule-A identity leaves \(N^A_t - N^B_t = -P_t\).

  2. A 120,000-cent service is paid in quarter 1 and covers quarters 1 to 4. What is the cumulative net-income difference \(N^A_1 - N^B_1\), in cents?

    Rule A expenses 120,000 in quarter 1 and rule B expenses 30,000, so the difference is \(-90{,}000\), which equals minus the unexpired balance (M1).

  3. Which change leaves net income and the change in cash unchanged, but changes operating cash flow?

    Options

    A classification difference moves an amount between sections (Proposition 3.1). Recognition changes net income, and a collection timing change changes the cash balance at a date.

  4. Under presentation 1, operating cash flow is 200,000 cents and interest paid is 5,000 cents. What is operating cash flow under presentation 2, in cents?

    Moving the interest out of operating outflows adds 5,000 cents to operating cash flow: \(200{,}000 + 5{,}000 = 205{,}000\) (M2).

  5. Proposition 2.1 says the cash balance is the same under both recognition rules. What is the reason?

    Options

    Cash depends on the receipts and payments, which the two rules take in the same quarters (proof of Proposition 2.1).

  6. Does this lesson establish what US GAAP or IFRS Standards require for a given transaction?

    Options

    The identities hold under both rules of this lesson. Lab 1 uses two labelled conventions and does not claim either is a framework rule.

  7. A 120,000-cent payment is made in quarter 1 and covers four quarters. Rule B spreads it in equal shares. What is the unexpired rule-B balance at the end of quarter 3, in cents?

    Three shares of 30,000 are expensed by the end of quarter 3, leaving \(120{,}000 - 90{,}000 = 30{,}000\).

  8. In Lab 1, rule A's cumulative net income is lower than rule B's at quarter 7. What does that show about the two rules?

    Options

    The difference equals minus the unexpired balance. A positive unexpired balance makes the difference negative. Neither rule is wrong; they recognize the payment at different times.

  9. Which statement about the presentations in Lab 1 is correct?

    Options

    Proposition 3.1: the operating section rises by the interest and the financing section falls by the same amount, so their sum is unchanged.
