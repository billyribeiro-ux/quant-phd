Articulation and Depreciation

  • Uses: the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash-flow statement, accrual accounting
  • Introduces: the articulation of the three statements, depreciation and amortization
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study or convention
  • Dlore
  • Xrefuted

The three statements are separate tables, and they are useful when they agree. A profit that does not reach retained earnings, or a cash change that does not reach the cash balance, may show that a statement was mis-stated or an item is missing. This lesson states the identities that link the statements, proves them from the ledger, and then tests them on a SIMULATED firm. It also treats depreciation, the charge with no cash line that operating cash flow adds back (Definition 2.1), as a schedule that the ledger must reproduce, and it computes the gap that omitting depreciation leaves in the operating identity and the gap that omitting dividends leaves in the retained-earnings identity.

1What articulation means

Definition 1.1 (articulation)

Articulation of the three statements is, in the lab's ledger, the set of identities that connect them: the cash-flow statement ends at the balance-sheet cash; the change in retained earnings equals net income less dividends; the change in each non-cash balance-sheet item is explained by the cash-flow lines and the income statement; and operating cash flow can be computed from net income by the indirect method and gives the same figure as the direct method.

Articulation is a property of the ledger, not of any one statement. A statement drawn from the ledger can be correct on its own and still fail to articulate if an entry is posted to one statement and not to the other. Each identity below therefore names the entries it depends on.

2Operating cash flow by the indirect method

Definition 2.1 (indirect operating cash flow)

The indirect operating cash flow for a year is net income, plus depreciation (Definition 6.1 below), less the increase in accounts receivable, plus the increase in accounts payable. The last three terms convert accrual income to cash, one item at a time.

Proposition 2.2 (indirect equals direct for the lab's operating activity)

In the lab's ledger, the indirect operating cash flow of Definition 2.1 equals the sum of the operating cash lines of the year, which are cash sales, collections of receivables, operating expense paid in cash, and payments of payables.

Proof

Write \(S_c\) and \(S_k\) for credit and cash sales, \(Q\) for collections, \(P_a\) for cost of sales on account, \(V\) for payments of payables, \(O\) for operating expense, which the lab pays in cash (its ledger has no operating-expense entry on account), and \(D\) for depreciation. Net income is \(N = S_c + S_k - P_a - O - D\). The receivable rises by \(S_c\) and falls by \(Q\), so \(\Delta A = S_c - Q\). The payable rises by \(P_a\) and falls by \(V\), so \(\Delta L = P_a - V\). The indirect figure is \(N + D - \Delta A + \Delta L = (S_c + S_k - P_a - O - D) + D - (S_c - Q) + (P_a - V) = S_k + Q - O - V\). The direct operating cash lines are \(S_k + Q - O - V\), since depreciation and the credit and cost entries carry no cash line. The two are equal. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 2.3 (what the proof assumes)

The proof assumes that operating expense is paid in cash in the period it is incurred, so that \(O\) enters both as an accrual and as a cash line. A firm that accrues expenses unpaid would need an additional term for the change in accrued liabilities. The lab has no such account, and that is why the proof has no such term.

3Investing and financing cash flows

Proposition 3.1 (the other two activities are balance changes)

In the lab's ledger, the investing cash flow of a year equals minus the change in equipment at cost. The financing cash flow equals the change in long-term debt plus the change in common stock, minus dividends paid in the year.

Proof

The only investing cash lines are payments for equipment, each posted as a debit to equipment and a credit to cash, so the investing cash flow is \(-\Delta E\), where \(E\) is equipment at cost. The financing cash lines are borrowings, which credit long-term debt, owner investments, which credit common stock, and dividends, which debit dividends and credit cash. Their sum is \(\Delta B + \Delta C - \text{dividends}\), where \(B\) is debt and \(C\) is common stock. \(\blacksquare\)

4The roll-forwards

Proposition 4.1 (retained earnings rolls forward)

Retained earnings at the end of a year equals retained earnings at the end of the prior year, plus net income of the year, less dividends of the year.

Proof

By Definition 3.1 of Lesson 2, retained earnings at a year end is the cumulative net income less cumulative dividends. The difference between two consecutive year ends is the net income and the dividends of the later year. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 4.2 (net equipment rolls forward)

In a ledger with no disposals, meaning no entry that credits equipment at cost, net equipment, meaning equipment at cost less accumulated depreciation, at the end of a year equals net equipment at the end of the prior year, plus additions, less depreciation of the year.

Proof

Additions are the debits to equipment in the year, and depreciation is the debit to depreciation expense, which the credit to accumulated depreciation balances. Net equipment changes by the additions less the depreciation credit, and the depreciation credit equals the depreciation expense of the year by the depreciation entry of Definition 6.1. \(\blacksquare\)

5The cash identity

Theorem 5.1 (articulation of the three statements)

In the lab's ledger, for every fiscal year: the indirect operating cash flow equals the direct operating cash flow; the investing and financing cash flows equal the balance changes of Proposition 3.1; the cash-flow total equals the change in balance-sheet cash; retained earnings and net equipment roll forward as in Propositions 4.1 and 4.2; and the balance sheet balances.

Proof

The first claim is Proposition 2.2. The second is Proposition 3.1. For the third, the cash-flow total is the sum of the three activity subtotals, which by Proposition 4.2 of Lesson 2 equals the change in cash. The fourth is Propositions 4.1 and 4.2. The fifth is Proposition 3.2 of Lesson 2. Each is proved above from the posting rules, so the theorem holds for any ledger that the lab's rules generate. \(\blacksquare\)

Theorem 5.1 is a theorem about the ledger. The lab checks four of its five parts on one generated firm, and the checks print True: Check 2 tests indirect against direct operating cash flow, Check 4 tests the cash total against balance-sheet cash, Check 3 tests the roll-forwards, and Check 1 tests the balance sheet. The investing and financing equalities of Proposition 3.1 are proved on this page and are not printed by the lab. A printed True is evidence that the code and the theorem agree on that firm. It is not an independent test of the theorem, which the proof already gives.

6Depreciation and amortization

Definition 6.1 (depreciation, straight-line)

Depreciation and amortization allocate the cost of a long-lived asset over its useful life. Under the straight-line method used in the lab, the charge is an equal amount for each quarter of the life, so the asset's cost, less any residual value, is spread evenly. The lab's assets have no residual value. Depreciation is an expense, but it is not a payment: its entry debits depreciation expense and credits accumulated depreciation, and no line touches cash. In this lesson, amortization applies the same arithmetic to an intangible asset with a finite life. The lab has no intangible asset, so it does not compute amortization.

Proposition 6.2 (depreciation reduces income and not cash)

A depreciation entry changes net income by minus the charge and changes cash by nothing. It therefore reduces net income and leaves the cash balance and the indirect operating cash flow unchanged, since the add-back restores the charge.

Proof

The depreciation entry has one debit to depreciation expense and one credit to accumulated depreciation, and no line on the cash account. Depreciation expense is an income-statement account, so net income falls by the charge. Cash is not a line of the entry, so it does not change. Operating cash flow by the indirect method starts from net income, which has fallen by the charge, and adds the charge back, so the indirect figure is unchanged. \(\blacksquare\)

Remark 6.3 (the schedule is an assumption)

The lab fixes each asset's cost, life and first quarter, and it also fixes the straight-line method and zero residual value, which are choices as well. A different life or a residual value changes the charge in every quarter, including the first. The lab's schedule rejects a cost that does not divide exactly into quarters, because a remainder would need a rule for the last period, and the lab does not state one.

Lab 3 · Linking the three statements and checking the printed identities (SIMULATED)

The lab uses the SIMULATED firm of Lab 2. Stated before running: the balance sheet balances at every date; the indirect operating cash flow equals the direct figure in each year, with the depreciation add-back of 1,000,000.00 in 2025 and 1,300,000.00 in 2026 and 2027; the retained-earnings and net-equipment roll-forwards hold; the cash-flow total reproduces balance-sheet cash; the ledger's accumulated depreciation equals a straight-line schedule of 1,000,000.00, 2,300,000.00 and 3,600,000.00 at the three year ends; and the two omitted terms leave gaps equal to the depreciation charge and to the dividends.

"""AC1 lab L03: link the three statements and check the articulation identities that it prints.

SIMULATED: the firm is generated from a seeded random number generator for this lab. It is not a real company.
The same ledger as Lab 2 is used. Amounts are integer cents and are printed in USD. The indirect cash-flow
statement is derived from the income statement and the balance sheet, and each printed identity linking the statements
is checked against the ledger. The investing and financing equalities of Proposition 3.1 are not printed.
Depreciation follows a straight-line schedule computed here. Standard library
only; no network.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 20261027
OPENING_YEAR = 2024
YEARS: tuple[int, ...] = (2025, 2026, 2027)

# Account -> type. A debit-normal account grows with debits; a credit-normal account grows with credits.
TYPES: dict[str, str] = {
    "cash": "asset",
    "accounts_receivable": "asset",
    "equipment": "asset",
    "accumulated_depreciation": "contra-asset",
    "accounts_payable": "liability",
    "long_term_debt": "liability",
    "common_stock": "equity",
    "dividends": "equity",
    "sales": "revenue",
    "cost_of_sales": "expense",
    "operating_expense": "expense",
    "depreciation_expense": "expense",
}
DEBIT_NORMAL = frozenset(
    {
        "cash",
        "accounts_receivable",
        "equipment",
        "dividends",
        "cost_of_sales",
        "operating_expense",
        "depreciation_expense",
    }
)
ACTIVITIES = ("operating", "investing", "financing", "noncash")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Line:
    """One side of one account, in integer cents. Exactly one of debit and credit is non-zero."""

    account: str
    debit: int = 0
    credit: int = 0


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Entry:
    """A journal entry dated by fiscal year and quarter. activity classifies its cash effect."""

    year: int
    quarter: int
    memo: str
    activity: str
    lines: tuple[Line, ...]


def check_entry(entry: Entry) -> None:
    """Raise ValueError unless the entry is valid: known accounts, integer amounts, balanced, classified."""
    if entry.activity not in ACTIVITIES:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: activity must be one of {ACTIVITIES}, not {entry.activity!r}")
    if not 1 <= entry.quarter <= 4:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: quarter must be 1 to 4, not {entry.quarter}")
    debits = 0
    credits = 0
    for line in entry.lines:
        if line.account not in TYPES:
            raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: unknown account {line.account!r}")
        for amount in (line.debit, line.credit):
            if isinstance(amount, bool) or not isinstance(amount, int) or amount < 0:
                raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: amounts must be non-negative integer cents, got {amount!r}")
        if line.debit and line.credit:
            raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: one line carries both a debit and a credit on {line.account!r}")
        debits += line.debit
        credits += line.credit
    if debits != credits:
        raise ValueError(f"{entry.memo!r}: debits {debits} do not equal credits {credits}")


class Ledger:
    """Holds only entries that pass check_entry."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.entries: list[Entry] = []

    def post(self, entry: Entry) -> None:
        check_entry(entry)
        self.entries.append(entry)

    def net(self, account: str, year: int | None = None, through: int | None = None) -> int:
        """Debits minus credits. year selects one fiscal year; through selects every year up to that one."""
        total = 0
        for entry in self.entries:
            if year is not None and entry.year != year:
                continue
            if through is not None and entry.year > through:
                continue
            for line in entry.lines:
                if line.account == account:
                    total += line.debit - line.credit
        return total

    def balance(self, account: str, through: int) -> int:
        """The balance on the account's normal side at the end of the given year, in integer cents."""
        net = self.net(account, through=through)
        return net if account in DEBIT_NORMAL else -net


def money(cents: int) -> str:
    """Format integer cents as dollars and cents: 1234567 becomes 12,345.67 and -50 becomes -0.50."""
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    dollars, rest = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
    return f"{sign}{dollars:,}.{rest:02d}"


def generate(ledger: Ledger, rng: random.Random) -> None:
    """Post the opening position and four quarters of each year, drawing every amount from rng."""
    ledger.post(
        Entry(
            OPENING_YEAR,
            4,
            "owner invests cash",
            "financing",
            (Line("cash", debit=800_000_000), Line("common_stock", credit=800_000_000)),
        )
    )
    ledger.post(
        Entry(
            OPENING_YEAR,
            4,
            "equipment A bought for cash",
            "investing",
            (Line("equipment", debit=500_000_000), Line("cash", credit=500_000_000)),
        )
    )
    for year in YEARS:
        for quarter in (1, 2, 3, 4):
            credit_sales = rng.randrange(180_000_000, 240_000_000)
            cash_sales = rng.randrange(20_000_000, 40_000_000)
            cost_percent = rng.randrange(55, 66)
            operating = rng.randrange(40_000_000, 50_000_000)
            if year == 2026 and quarter == 1:
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "equipment B bought for cash",
                        "investing",
                        (Line("equipment", debit=150_000_000), Line("cash", credit=150_000_000)),
                    )
                )
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "borrowing",
                        "financing",
                        (Line("cash", debit=100_000_000), Line("long_term_debt", credit=100_000_000)),
                    )
                )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "credit sale",
                    "noncash",
                    (Line("accounts_receivable", debit=credit_sales), Line("sales", credit=credit_sales)),
                )
            )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "cash sale",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("cash", debit=cash_sales), Line("sales", credit=cash_sales)),
                )
            )
            purchases = (credit_sales + cash_sales) * cost_percent // 100
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "cost of sales on account",
                    "noncash",
                    (Line("cost_of_sales", debit=purchases), Line("accounts_payable", credit=purchases)),
                )
            )
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "operating expense paid in cash",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("operating_expense", debit=operating), Line("cash", credit=operating)),
                )
            )
            collection = ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year) * 4 // 5
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "collection of receivables",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("cash", debit=collection), Line("accounts_receivable", credit=collection)),
                )
            )
            payment = ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year) * 9 // 10
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "payment of payables",
                    "operating",
                    (Line("accounts_payable", debit=payment), Line("cash", credit=payment)),
                )
            )
            depreciation = 25_000_000 + (7_500_000 if year >= 2026 else 0)
            ledger.post(
                Entry(
                    year,
                    quarter,
                    "depreciation of equipment",
                    "noncash",
                    (
                        Line("depreciation_expense", debit=depreciation),
                        Line("accumulated_depreciation", credit=depreciation),
                    ),
                )
            )
            if quarter == 4:
                ledger.post(
                    Entry(
                        year,
                        quarter,
                        "dividends paid",
                        "financing",
                        (Line("dividends", debit=20_000_000), Line("cash", credit=20_000_000)),
                    )
                )


def income_statement(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Revenue, cost lines and net income for one fiscal year, in integer cents (costs positive)."""
    sales = -ledger.net("sales", year=year)
    cost = ledger.net("cost_of_sales", year=year)
    opex = ledger.net("operating_expense", year=year)
    dep = ledger.net("depreciation_expense", year=year)
    return {
        "sales": sales,
        "cost_of_sales": cost,
        "operating_expense": opex,
        "depreciation": dep,
        "net_income": sales - cost - opex - dep,
    }


def balance_sheet(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Balances at the end of one fiscal year (the opening position is year 2024). Retained earnings is the
    cumulative income statement less cumulative dividends, since the ledger has no closing entries."""
    cash = ledger.balance("cash", year)
    receivables = ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year)
    equipment = ledger.balance("equipment", year)
    depreciated = ledger.balance("accumulated_depreciation", year)
    payables = ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year)
    debt = ledger.balance("long_term_debt", year)
    common = ledger.balance("common_stock", year)
    cumulative_income = -(
        ledger.net("sales", through=year)
        + ledger.net("cost_of_sales", through=year)
        + ledger.net("operating_expense", through=year)
        + ledger.net("depreciation_expense", through=year)
    )
    retained = cumulative_income - ledger.balance("dividends", year)
    assets = cash + receivables + equipment - depreciated
    liabilities = payables + debt
    equity = common + retained
    return {
        "cash": cash,
        "accounts_receivable": receivables,
        "equipment_at_cost": equipment,
        "accumulated_depreciation": depreciated,
        "total_assets": assets,
        "accounts_payable": payables,
        "long_term_debt": debt,
        "total_liabilities": liabilities,
        "common_stock": common,
        "retained_earnings": retained,
        "total_equity": equity,
        "total_liabilities_and_equity": liabilities + equity,
    }


def cash_flow_statement(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> tuple[dict[str, int], dict[str, int]]:
    """The direct-method cash-flow statement: cash lines grouped by memo, and subtotals by activity."""
    by_memo: dict[str, int] = {}
    by_activity = {activity: 0 for activity in ACTIVITIES}
    for entry in ledger.entries:
        if entry.year != year:
            continue
        for line in entry.lines:
            if line.account == "cash":
                amount = line.debit - line.credit
                by_memo[entry.memo] = by_memo.get(entry.memo, 0) + amount
                by_activity[entry.activity] += amount
    return by_memo, by_activity


def indirect_operating(ledger: Ledger, year: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Operating cash flow by the indirect method: net income, plus depreciation, less the increase in
    receivables, plus the increase in payables. Every input comes from the statements or the balance sheet."""
    income = income_statement(ledger, year)
    receivables_change = ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year) - ledger.balance("accounts_receivable", year - 1)
    payables_change = ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year) - ledger.balance("accounts_payable", year - 1)
    return {
        "net_income": income["net_income"],
        "depreciation_add_back": income["depreciation"],
        "less_increase_in_receivables": -receivables_change,
        "plus_increase_in_payables": payables_change,
        "operating": income["net_income"] + income["depreciation"] - receivables_change + payables_change,
    }


# Straight-line schedule for the two pieces of equipment in the ledger. Cost in integer cents; life in years;
# first_quarter counts quarters from 1 (2025 Q1 is 1). The costs divide exactly into quarters, so no remainder rule
# is needed; depreciation_schedule rejects a cost that does not.
ASSETS: tuple[tuple[str, int, int, int], ...] = (
    ("equipment A", 500_000_000, 5, 1),
    ("equipment B", 150_000_000, 5, 5),
)


def depreciation_schedule(year: int) -> int:
    """Accumulated straight-line depreciation at the end of a fiscal year, in integer cents."""
    quarters_through = (year - YEARS[0] + 1) * 4
    total = 0
    for name, cost, life_years, first_quarter in ASSETS:
        quarters_of_life = life_years * 4
        if cost % quarters_of_life:
            raise ValueError(f"{name}: cost {cost} does not divide into {quarters_of_life} quarters exactly")
        per_quarter = cost // quarters_of_life
        in_service = max(0, min(quarters_through, quarters_of_life) - first_quarter + 1)
        total += per_quarter * in_service
    return total


def main() -> None:
    ledger = Ledger()
    generate(ledger, random.Random(SEED))
    sheets = {y: balance_sheet(ledger, y) for y in (OPENING_YEAR, *YEARS)}

    print("Check 1: the balance sheet balances at every year end (USD)")
    for year in (OPENING_YEAR, *YEARS):
        gap = sheets[year]["total_assets"] - sheets[year]["total_liabilities_and_equity"]
        print(f"{year} year end: assets less liabilities and equity = {money(gap)}")

    print()
    print("Check 2: operating cash flow, indirect method against direct method (USD)")
    print(f"{'':<34}" + "".join(f"{y:>16}" for y in YEARS))
    indirect = {y: indirect_operating(ledger, y) for y in YEARS}
    direct = {y: cash_flow_statement(ledger, y)[1]["operating"] for y in YEARS}
    for key in ("net_income", "depreciation_add_back", "less_increase_in_receivables", "plus_increase_in_payables"):
        print(f"{key:<34}" + "".join(f"{money(indirect[y][key]):>16}" for y in YEARS))
    print(f"{'operating, indirect':<34}" + "".join(f"{money(indirect[y]['operating']):>16}" for y in YEARS))
    print(f"{'operating, direct (cash lines)':<34}" + "".join(f"{money(direct[y]):>16}" for y in YEARS))
    print(f"indirect equals direct: {all(indirect[y]['operating'] == direct[y] for y in YEARS)}")

    print()
    print("Check 3: retained earnings and net equipment roll forward (USD)")
    for year in YEARS:
        income = income_statement(ledger, year)["net_income"]
        dividends = ledger.net("dividends", year=year)
        opening_re = sheets[year - 1]["retained_earnings"]
        closing_re = sheets[year]["retained_earnings"]
        capex = ledger.net("equipment", year=year)
        dep = income_statement(ledger, year)["depreciation"]
        net_before = sheets[year - 1]["equipment_at_cost"] - sheets[year - 1]["accumulated_depreciation"]
        net_after = sheets[year]["equipment_at_cost"] - sheets[year]["accumulated_depreciation"]
        print(
            f"{year}: retained earnings {money(opening_re)} + net income {money(income)} "
            f"- dividends {money(dividends)} = {money(closing_re)}: {opening_re + income - dividends == closing_re}"
        )
        print(
            f"      net equipment {money(net_before)} + additions {money(capex)} "
            f"- depreciation {money(dep)} = {money(net_after)}: {net_before + capex - dep == net_after}"
        )

    print()
    print("Check 4: cash flows to the balance sheet cash (USD)")
    for year in YEARS:
        _, by_activity = cash_flow_statement(ledger, year)
        opening_cash = sheets[year - 1]["cash"]
        ending_cash = sheets[year]["cash"]
        net = by_activity["operating"] + by_activity["investing"] + by_activity["financing"]
        print(
            f"{year}: opening {money(opening_cash)} + operating {money(by_activity['operating'])} "
            f"+ investing {money(by_activity['investing'])} + financing {money(by_activity['financing'])} "
            f"= {money(opening_cash + net)}; balance sheet cash {money(ending_cash)}: "
            f"{opening_cash + net == ending_cash}"
        )

    print()
    print("Check 5: accumulated depreciation in the ledger against the straight-line schedule (USD)")
    for year in (OPENING_YEAR, *YEARS):
        ledger_value = sheets[year]["accumulated_depreciation"]
        schedule_value = depreciation_schedule(year) if year >= YEARS[0] else 0
        print(
            f"{year} year end: ledger {money(ledger_value)}, "
            f"schedule {money(schedule_value)}: {ledger_value == schedule_value}"
        )

    print()
    print("Check 6: the gap that each omitted term leaves in an identity (USD)")
    for year in YEARS:
        missing_add_back = indirect[year]["operating"] - indirect[year]["depreciation_add_back"]
        difference = direct[year] - missing_add_back
        print(
            f"{year}: operating without the depreciation add-back is {money(missing_add_back)}; "
            f"the gap to the ledger is {money(difference)}, equal to depreciation: "
            f"{difference == indirect[year]['depreciation_add_back']}"
        )
    for year in YEARS:
        opening_re = sheets[year - 1]["retained_earnings"]
        closing_re = sheets[year]["retained_earnings"]
        income = income_statement(ledger, year)["net_income"]
        dividends = ledger.net("dividends", year=year)
        without_deduction = opening_re + income
        print(
            f"{year}: retained earnings with no dividend deduction would be {money(without_deduction)}, "
            f"balance sheet {money(closing_re)}; the gap {money(without_deduction - closing_re)} equals dividends: "
            f"{without_deduction - closing_re == dividends}"
        )

    print()

7Where the canon breaks

Articulation is a property the ledger must have, and it is also a property that a reader cannot test by reading the statements alone. Four limits follow.

  • Articulation holds by construction in a generated ledger. Theorem 5.1 is a consequence of the posting rules. A real firm's statements can fail to articulate because of a restatement, a reclassification, an acquisition or an item the extract omitted. The identities test the extract that the statements were built from, and a consistent error in the extract passes them.
  • Depreciation is an estimate. The life, the residual value and the method are judgements that the firm makes. Proposition 6.2 holds for any of them, but the amount of each charge depends on the judgement, and two firms with identical assets can report different depreciation.
  • The indirect method is a reconciliation, not a forecast. Definition 2.1 explains the gap between profit and operating cash. It does not say whether the gap will persist, and the receivable and payable changes can reverse in the next year.
  • The lab omits items that a real statement carries. Taxes, interest, leases, intangible assets and impairments do not appear. None of these items is in the lab, and the lab's checks test no term for any of them.

8Misconceptions

  1. “Depreciation is a cash cost that the firm pays each year.”

    Proposition 6.2 shows that the depreciation entry has no cash line. The cash was paid when each asset was bought. For equipment B, bought in 2026, that payment is the investing line of Lab 3 (1,500,000.00). Depreciation spreads that payment over the life as an expense, and the indirect method adds it back for that reason.

  2. “Adding depreciation back to net income increases cash.”

    The add-back offsets a charge that reduced net income without a cash payment. Net income is lower by the charge, and the add-back restores the figure to the operating cash it would have had. In Lab 3 the 2026 add-back is 1,300,000.00 and the operating cash flow is 2,191,615.64, which is what the direct lines sum to.

  3. “Statements that articulate are correct.”

    Theorem 5.1 says the statements agree with one another. It does not say each number is right. A wrong depreciation schedule would still articulate, because the schedule enters the income statement and the balance sheet together. Lab 3's Check 5 is a separate test against a schedule, which the identities do not give.

9Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · The indirect figure (SIMULATED)

For 2026 Lab 3 prints net income of 945,769.70, depreciation of 1,300,000.00, a change in receivables of 65,302.46 (an increase) and a change in payables of 11,148.40 (an increase). Compute the indirect operating cash flow.

Solution

\(945{,}769.70 + 1{,}300{,}000.00 - 65{,}302.46 + 11{,}148.40 = 2{,}191{,}615.64\). The receivable increase reduces cash and the payable increase raises it, so the signs are minus and plus. The result matches the printed figure, and the direct lines for 2026 also sum to 2,191,615.64.

M2 · Net equipment (SIMULATED)

Net equipment at the end of 2025 is 4,000,000.00. The 2026 additions are 1,500,000.00 and the 2026 depreciation is 1,300,000.00. Compute net equipment at the end of 2026 and check it against Lab 3.

Solution

\(4{,}000{,}000.00 + 1{,}500{,}000.00 - 1{,}300{,}000.00 = 4{,}200{,}000.00\). Lab 3 prints net equipment of 4,200,000.00 for 2026.

M3 · A straight-line charge

An asset costs 2,400,000.00 with a five-year life and no residual value, bought at the start of a year. What is the quarterly charge, and what is the accumulated depreciation after six quarters?

Solution

The life is \(5 \times 4 = 20\) quarters, so the quarterly charge is \(2{,}400{,}000.00 / 20 = 120{,}000.00\). After six quarters the accumulated depreciation is \(6 \times 120{,}000.00 = 720{,}000.00\). The cost divides exactly, so no remainder rule is needed.

Proof

P1 · Depreciation leaves cash unchanged

Show that a depreciation entry does not change operating cash flow when that flow is computed directly from the cash lines.

Solution

The direct operating flow is the sum of the operating cash lines. A depreciation entry has no cash line, so it contributes nothing to that sum. Adding a depreciation entry to the ledger therefore leaves the direct figure unchanged. This is the direct-method half of Proposition 6.2.

P2 · The retained-earnings roll-forward with a dividend

Show that Proposition 4.1 holds in a year with dividends, by writing the cumulative definition at two year ends and subtracting.

Solution

At year end \(t\), retained earnings is \(R_t = \sum_{s \le t} N_s - \sum_{s \le t} D_s\), with \(N_s\) the net income and \(D_s\) the dividends of year \(s\). Then \(R_t - R_{t-1} = N_t - D_t\), since every earlier term appears in both sums and cancels. This is Proposition 4.1 with the dividend included.

P3 · Omitting the add-back

Show that omitting the depreciation add-back from the indirect figure leaves a gap to the direct figure equal to the year's depreciation, for any ledger in which depreciation has no cash line.

Solution

The indirect figure with the add-back is \(N + D - \Delta A + \Delta L\), and the direct figure is equal to it by Proposition 2.2. Without the add-back the figure is \(N - \Delta A + \Delta L\), which is smaller by \(D\). The gap is therefore exactly the depreciation of the year, for any year in which \(D\) is the depreciation charge.

Research

R1 · A test of articulation on a real filing

Specify the identities a reader can test on one real firm's statements, which figures each needs, and which of them the reader cannot test without the notes.

Solution

The reader can test the retained-earnings roll-forward, using net income, dividends and the two year-end balances. The reader can test the cash identity, using the three subtotals and the two cash balances. The reader can test the indirect figure only if the statements disclose depreciation and the working-capital changes. The reader cannot test the net-equipment roll-forward without the additions and the depreciation, which the notes may disclose. A failure in the first two may point to a missing equity or cash item, or to a misstated balance. The tests do not establish that any single number is right.

R2 · A longer life

Suppose the equipment in Lab 3 had a life of ten years instead of five, with the same costs. Describe the effect on the depreciation charge, on net income and on operating cash flow.

Solution

Equipment A's annual charge halves, from 1,000,000.00 to 500,000.00; equipment B is set aside here. Net income rises by the saving, and the add-back falls by the same amount, so the indirect figure does not change. The direct figure does not change either, since no cash line moves. So a longer life raises net income and leaves operating cash flow unchanged, which is the content of Proposition 6.2 read in the other direction. The reported profit and the cash figure separate further, and the reader should ask which life the firm chose and why.

R3 · Amortization of an intangible

A firm pays 1,200,000.00 for a customer list with a four-year life and no residual value. Describe the entries for the purchase and the first year's charge, and say which statement each entry reaches.

Solution

The purchase debits an intangible asset and credits cash for 1,200,000.00, so it is an asset on the balance sheet. Lesson 2, Definition 4.1 names equipment as an investing item and does not name intangibles, so the activity of this purchase is not stated here and is open. The first year's charge is 300,000.00 under the same straight-line arithmetic. It debits amortization expense, which reduces net income, and credits accumulated amortization, which reduces the asset. The charge has no cash line. Definition 2.1 names depreciation as the add-back, and an amortization charge with no cash line would be added back in the same way, but Proposition 2.2 is not proved for it here. The lab has no intangible asset, so this case is described and not computed.
