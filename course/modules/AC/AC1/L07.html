This lesson studies an equity-settled grant, in which the firm pays the employee in its own shares rather than cash. This lesson fixes one model of an equity grant: a total cost set at the grant date, an expense spread over the years of required service, a matching credit to equity, and no forfeitures. Inside that model, Section 2 proves the identities that link the statements. The model leaves out the measurement and recognition rules for awards, and this lesson does not state them.

1A grant as a schedule

Three terms are used throughout. Vesting is the period in which the employee must remain in service before the award is theirs. The grant-date fair value is the value of one share award on the grant date, which the model takes as an input and does not derive. Additional paid-in capital (APIC) is the equity account that holds amounts contributed by owners, including amounts credited for awards.

Definition 1.1 (Equity-settled grant, model)

A grant \(g\) has a number of shares \(n_g\), a grant-date fair value per share \(f_g\) in integer cents, a whole number of vesting years \(v_g \ge 1\), and a grant year \(y_g\). Its total cost is \(C_g = n_g f_g\). The stock-based compensation expense of \(g\) in fiscal year \(t\) is

\[ e_{g,t} = \begin{cases} \lfloor C_g / v_g \rfloor, & y_g \le t \le y_g + v_g - 2, \\ C_g - (v_g - 1)\lfloor C_g / v_g \rfloor, & t = y_g + v_g - 1, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \]

The first case gives the base amount for each vesting year before the last. The second gives the last vesting year the remainder, so the schedule is exact in cents. Stock-based compensation in year \(t\) is \(S_t = \sum_g e_{g,t}\), summed over all grants.

Definition 1.2 (Model assumptions)

The model has five assumptions. (i) The total cost \(C_g\) is fixed on the grant date. (ii) No award is forfeited. (iii) Vesting has no performance or market conditions. (iv) Income tax is ignored in this lesson; L08 models tax on depreciation differences, not on these grants. (v) Shares issued at vesting are not counted, so the model says nothing about dilution. These are the model's choices. They are not claims about any real award.

Theorem 1.3 (Schedule totals)

For every grant \(g\), \(\sum_{t} e_{g,t} = C_g\), where the sum runs over all fiscal years.

Proof

Let \(v = v_g\), \(b = \lfloor C_g / v \rfloor\) and \(y = y_g\). For \(v = 1\) there are no base years, and the only nonzero term is \(e_{g,y} = C_g - 0 = C_g\). For \(v \ge 2\), the years \(y\) to \(y + v - 2\) are \(v - 1\) years, each with expense \(b\), and year \(y + v - 1\) has \(C_g - (v - 1)b\). Outside these \(v\) years every term is \(0\). The sum is \((v - 1)b + C_g - (v - 1)b = C_g\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 1.4 (One grant)

A grant of \(n_g = 1{,}000\) shares at \(f_g = \$12.00\), that is \(1{,}200\) cents, has \(C_g = 1{,}200{,}000\) cents, or $12,000. With \(y_g = 1\) and \(v_g = 4\), the base amount is \(\lfloor 1{,}200{,}000 / 4 \rfloor = 300{,}000\) cents, or $3,000. The expense is $3,000 in each of years 1 to 4 and $0 after. The four amounts sum to $12,000, as Theorem 1.3 requires.

2Where the expense lands in the statements

Let \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) be operating income before stock-based compensation, in SCU (simulated currency units), let \(\mathrm{NI}_t\) be net income, \(\mathrm{CFO}_t\) be operating cash flow, and \(\mathrm{RE}_t\) be retained earnings. Under the model:

\[ \mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t, \qquad \mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t, \qquad \Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t, \qquad \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t = S_t, \qquad \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t, \]

The cash-flow line is the add-back of a non-cash expense, and the model has no other cash flows, so cash changes only through \(\mathrm{CFO}\). Assets are cash alone in this model, and equity is APIC plus retained earnings, so \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t\).

Theorem 2.1 (Articulation under the model)

For every fiscal year \(t\): (a) \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (b) \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (c) \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (d) \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_t = \Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\).

Proof

(a) \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t = (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t) + S_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\). (b) \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (a). (c) \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = S_t + (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t) = \mathrm{OI}_t\). (d) \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_t = \Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (b), and \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (c). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 2.2 (Expense moves income and APIC, not equity)

For any stock-based compensation \(S_t\), a change in \(S_t\) moves \(\mathrm{NI}_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t\) in opposite directions by the same amount, and leaves \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) unchanged.

Proof

Holding \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) fixed, \(\partial \mathrm{NI}_t / \partial S_t = -1\) and \(\partial \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t / \partial S_t = +1\). Theorem 2.1 (b) and (c) give \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t\) equal to \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) for every value of \(S_t\). \(\blacksquare\)

Example 2.3 (Example 1.4 continued)

Let \(\mathrm{OI}_1 = \$50{,}000\), so \(S_1 = \$3{,}000\) from Example 1.4. Then \(\mathrm{NI}_1 = \$47{,}000\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_1 = \$50{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_1 = \$50{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_1 = \$3{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_1 = \$47{,}000\), and \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_1 = \$50{,}000\), which equals \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_1\).

Lab 1 · Grant schedules and articulation on SIMULATED grants

Stated before running: each of the four SIMULATED grants has an expense schedule that sums to its total, and for every year the CFO, cash, APIC and balance identities of Section 2 hold. Expected result: 4 of 4 schedules and 6 of 6 years for each identity. The lab draws no expense level from data and makes no estimate for any firm. Every grant is SIMULATED.

"""Lab AC1-L07-1: stock-based compensation schedules on generated grants.

Every grant and every operating income figure here is SIMULATED: a seeded generator
draws them, no company is represented, and no output estimates any real firm. Money
is in integer cents of simulated currency units (SCU). The model has no income tax,
no forfeitures, no performance conditions and no share issuance at vesting, so the
only statements it produces are the ones the lesson's identities cover.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 20260701
N_GRANTS = 4
HORIZON = 6  # fiscal years 1 to 6; the latest vesting year of any grant is 3 + 4 - 1 = 6


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Grant:
    """One SIMULATED equity grant. Money in cents; shares as a count."""

    grant_year: int
    shares: int
    fair_value_cents: int  # grant-date fair value per share, in cents
    vest_years: int

    @property
    def value_cents(self) -> int:
        """Total grant value in cents: shares times grant-date fair value per share."""
        return self.shares * self.fair_value_cents


def yearly_expense(grant: Grant, year: int) -> int:
    """Expense recognized for one grant in one fiscal year, in integer cents.

    Straight-line over vest_years, starting in grant_year. Integer division gives the
    base amount for each vesting year; the final vesting year takes the remainder, so
    the schedule sums to value_cents exactly. Years outside the vesting period give 0.
    """
    index = year - grant.grant_year
    if index < 0 or index >= grant.vest_years:
        return 0
    base = grant.value_cents // grant.vest_years
    if index == grant.vest_years - 1:
        return grant.value_cents - base * (grant.vest_years - 1)
    return base


def generate_grants(rng: random.Random) -> list[Grant]:
    """Draw N_GRANTS SIMULATED grants: 100 to 5,000 shares, $5.00 to $40.00 per share, 3 or 4 vesting years."""
    grants: list[Grant] = []
    for _ in range(N_GRANTS):
        grant_year = rng.randint(1, 3)
        shares = rng.randrange(100, 5001, 100)
        fair_value_cents = rng.randint(500, 4000)
        vest_years = rng.choice((3, 4))
        grants.append(Grant(grant_year, shares, fair_value_cents, vest_years))
    return grants


def cents_text(cents: int) -> str:
    """Format integer cents as SCU dollars and cents, for example 1234567 gives 12,345.67."""
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    whole, frac = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
    return f"{sign}{whole:,}.{frac:02d}"


def main() -> None:
    """Draw the grants and operating income, schedule the expense, and check the identities."""
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    grants = generate_grants(rng)
    operating_income = [rng.randrange(40_000_000, 90_000_001, 100) for _ in range(HORIZON)]

    print(f"SIMULATED grants (seed {SEED}): grant year, shares, fair value per share, vest years, total SCU")
    for number, grant in enumerate(grants, start=1):
        print(
            f"G{number}  grant_year={grant.grant_year}  shares={grant.shares:,}  "
            f"fair_value={cents_text(grant.fair_value_cents)}  vest_years={grant.vest_years}  "
            f"total={cents_text(grant.value_cents)}"
        )

    schedule_ok = 0
    for grant in grants:
        expensed = sum(yearly_expense(grant, year) for year in range(1, HORIZON + 1))
        schedule_ok += expensed == grant.value_cents

    sbc = [sum(yearly_expense(g, year) for g in grants) for year in range(1, HORIZON + 1)]
    print("SIMULATED statements, SCU; years 1 to 6 (operating income is before SBC, no tax):")
    print("year  operating_income  sbc_expense  net_income  cfo  apic_change  cash_change")
    apic_ok = 0
    cash_ok = 0
    balance_ok = 0
    cfo_ok = 0
    for index, year in enumerate(range(1, HORIZON + 1)):
        oi = operating_income[index]
        expense = sbc[index]
        net_income = oi - expense
        cfo = net_income + expense
        apic_change = expense
        retained_change = net_income
        cash_change = cfo
        equity_change = apic_change + retained_change
        apic_ok += apic_change == expense
        cash_ok += cash_change == oi
        balance_ok += cash_change == equity_change
        cfo_ok += cfo == net_income + expense
        print(
            f"{year:>4}  {cents_text(oi):>16}  {cents_text(expense):>11}  {cents_text(net_income):>10}  "
            f"{cents_text(cfo):>9}  {cents_text(apic_change):>11}  {cents_text(cash_change):>11}"
        )

    print(f"schedule sums equal grant totals: {schedule_ok}/{N_GRANTS}")
    print(f"CFO equals net income plus SBC in every year: {cfo_ok}/{HORIZON}")
    print(f"cash change equals operating income in every year: {cash_ok}/{HORIZON}")
    print(f"assets change equals equity change in every year: {balance_ok}/{HORIZON}")
    print(f"APIC change equals SBC expense in every year: {apic_ok}/{HORIZON}")
    assert schedule_ok == N_GRANTS
    assert cfo_ok == cash_ok == balance_ok == apic_ok == HORIZON

3Where the canon breaks

The model fixes the total cost on the grant date and spreads it straight-line. It has no forfeitures, no performance or market conditions, and no valuation method for the fair value. Each of these is outside the model, so the schedule above does not describe a plan that has one. Theorem 1.3 uses the fixed-total assumption (Definition 1.2 (i)): its proof takes one \(C_g\) for every year, so a schedule that depends on later share prices falls outside it. This lesson does not state measurement or recognition rules for these features. They are not stated on this page.

The model covers equity-settled grants only. Its credit side is APIC, so Theorem 2.1 (c) makes no claim about a grant that the firm settles in cash. The model also says nothing about dilution, because it does not count the shares issued at vesting.

4Misconceptions

  1. “Stock compensation costs the firm nothing, because no cash leaves the firm.”

    No cash leaves, so \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) is unchanged by \(S_t\). But \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\) falls by the expense, and in the model equity moves from retained earnings to APIC. Corollary 2.2 states both effects. The table in Lab 1 shows net income equal to operating income less the expense in every year.

  2. “Adding stock compensation back in the cash-flow statement means the expense is not a real cost.”

    The add-back reverses a non-cash expense inside operating cash flow, so \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) in Theorem 2.1 (a). Net income still carries the expense. If the add-back were omitted, operating cash flow would equal net income, \(\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), which understates cash generation by the expense (proof P2 in the problem set).

  3. “Once a grant is made, its cost can be recognized in whatever years the firm chooses.”

    Under Definition 1.1 the schedule is fixed by the grant year, the vesting years and the total cost, and Theorem 1.3 gives the total. Example 1.4 and the grant schedules in Lab 1 each sum to the grant's total, and no year can take an extra amount.

5Problem set

Mechanics

M1 · A three-year grant

A grant of 500 shares at $8.00 per share has grant year 2 and vests over 3 years. Give \(e_{g,t}\) for \(t = 1, \dots, 5\), and check the total.

Solution

\(C_g = 500 \times 800 = 400{,}000\) cents, or $4,000. The base amount is \(\lfloor 400{,}000 / 3 \rfloor = 133{,}333\) cents. Years 2 and 3 take 133,333 cents each. Year 4 takes \(400{,}000 - 2 \times 133{,}333 = 133{,}334\) cents. So \(e_{g,1} = 0\), \(e_{g,2} = \$1{,}333.33\), \(e_{g,3} = \$1{,}333.33\), \(e_{g,4} = \$1{,}333.34\), and \(e_{g,5} = 0\). The total is $4,000.00, as Theorem 1.3 requires.

M2 · Change the vesting

Keep M1 except that the grant vests over 4 years. Give the schedule.

Solution

The base amount is \(\lfloor 400{,}000 / 4 \rfloor = 100{,}000\) cents, or $1,000.00. Years 2 to 4 take the base. Year 5 takes \(400{,}000 - 3 \times 100{,}000 = 100{,}000\) cents, which is also $1,000.00. So the schedule is $1,000.00 in each of years 2 to 5, zero otherwise, and the total is $4,000.00.

M3 · Net income and APIC

For the grant in M1 alone, operating income before stock-based compensation is $20,000 in year 2. Give \(\mathrm{NI}_2\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_2\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_2\), and check that they satisfy Theorem 2.1 (c).

Solution

\(S_2 = \$1{,}333.33\). Then \(\mathrm{NI}_2 = 20{,}000.00 - 1{,}333.33 = \$18{,}666.67\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 = \$20{,}000.00\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2 = \$1{,}333.33\), and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_2 = \$18{,}666.67\). The sum \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2 + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_2 = \$20{,}000.00 = \mathrm{OI}_2\), as (c) requires.

Proof

P1 · One-year vesting

Prove Theorem 1.3 for \(v_g = 1\) directly from Definition 1.1, without the general argument.

Solution

With \(v_g = 1\) the first case covers \(y_g \le t \le y_g - 1\), which is empty. The second case covers \(t = y_g\) and gives \(C_g - 0 \cdot \lfloor C_g / 1 \rfloor = C_g\). The third case covers every other year and gives 0. So the only nonzero term is \(e_{g,y_g} = C_g\), and the sum is \(C_g\).

P2 · The omitted add-back

Suppose a firm reports operating cash flow as net income, with no add-back of \(S_t\). Show that its cash flow is lower than \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) by \(S_t\), and that its change in cash is then lower than \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) by the same amount.

Solution

Reported operating cash flow would be \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), which is \(S_t\) below \(\mathrm{OI}_t\). The change in cash follows the reported flow, so it is \(S_t\) below \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) too. But the true cash change is \(\mathrm{OI}_t\), since no cash moves for \(S_t\). So the error is exactly \(S_t\).

P3 · Equity does not depend on the expense

Show that \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\) is the same for every value of \(S_t\), given \(\mathrm{OI}_t\).

Solution

\(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = S_t + (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t)\). The \(S_t\) terms cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{OI}_t\). The expression does not depend on \(S_t\), which is Corollary 2.2.

Research

R1 · Add forfeitures

Extend Definition 1.2 so that a fraction \(\phi\) of each grant is forfeited in its vesting year. State which identity of Theorem 2.1 still holds, which fails, and what you must specify to restore the schedule's total.

Solution

Theorem 2.1 holds for any \(S_t\), so (a) to (d) still hold, because their proofs use the Section 2 display: \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t\), \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t = S_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t\), the statement that assets are cash alone, and the definition of equity as APIC plus RE. The schedule total no longer equals \(C_g\) if the forfeited part is no longer expensed. To restore a total, you must specify the reversal rule: which year absorbs a reversal of expense already recognized, and whether forfeiture reverses prior expense or only reduces future expense. The model does not choose between these, and the lesson does not state a reversal rule.

R2 · Equity grant against a cash bonus

A firm can pay an employee either a grant worth \(C_g\) that vests in one year, or a cash bonus of \(C_g\) paid at the same time. Compare cash, net income, APIC and the share count under each. Say which one is dilutive, meaning it raises the number of shares outstanding.

Solution

Under the grant, the one-year model expenses \(C_g\) in the vesting year, so net income falls by \(C_g\), APIC rises by \(C_g\), and cash does not change, because the model moves cash only through \(\mathrm{CFO}\), which equals the fixed \(\mathrm{OI}\) (Section 2). Under the bonus, cash falls by \(C_g\) and APIC does not change. The model covers equity-settled grants only (Section 3), so the bonus's income effect is not stated here. The grant issues \(n_g\) shares at vesting and the bonus issues none, so the grant is the dilutive one. The model does not count shares, so this follows from the grant's definition and is not computed by the model.

R3 · A schedule that tracks the share price

Design the smallest change to Definition 1.1 that makes the grant's total depend on the later share price. State which assumption of Definition 1.2 you break, and what Theorem 1.3 becomes.

Solution

Let \(C_g\) be replaced by a function of the share price at each year end, so \(C_{g,t}\) changes. This breaks assumption (i), that \(C_g\) is fixed on the grant date. Theorem 1.3 then no longer follows from Definition 1.1, whose proof takes one \(C_g\) for every year. The schedule would have to be restated each year, and the model does not define that restatement, so the lesson does not state a total for it.

7Retrieval quiz

  1. Under Definition 1.1, when does an equity grant have expense in a year?

    Options

    Definition 1.1 gives zero outside \(y_g\) to \(y_g + v_g - 1\), and the second and first cases cover the vesting years.

  2. A grant of 500 shares at $8.00 per share has total cost, in dollars, of how much?

    \(500 \times 800 = 400{,}000\) cents, or $4,000 (M1).

  3. The same grant, with 3 vesting years starting in year 2, has what expense in year 2, in dollars?

    The base amount is \(\lfloor 400{,}000 / 3 \rfloor = 133{,}333\) cents, or $1,333.33 (M1).

  4. Operating income before stock-based compensation is $20,000, and the expense is $1,333.33. What is net income, in dollars?

    \(\mathrm{NI} = \mathrm{OI} - S = 20{,}000.00 - 1{,}333.33 = 18{,}666.67\) (M3).

  5. Under the model, what is operating cash flow when the expense is \(S_t\)?

    Options

    Theorem 2.1 (a): \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t) + S_t\).

  6. Holding operating income fixed, what happens to total equity when the expense rises?

    Options

  7. A $12,000 grant (1,000 shares at $12.00) vests over 4 years from year 1. What is the expense in year 2, in dollars?
