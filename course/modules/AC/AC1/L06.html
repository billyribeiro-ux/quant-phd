Lease Accounting: A Liability, an Asset and Two Expense Patterns

  • Introduces: lease accounting
  • Ttheorem
  • Areplicated
  • Bpublished, not settled
  • Csingle study

A lease that promises fixed payments over several years is a financing of the asset it gives access to, and its schedule has three moving parts: a liability that is the present value of the payments, an asset that is used up over the term, and an expense that combines interest with amortization. This lesson defines that model as the unit uses it, proves that the schedule closes to zero, and proves that the asset less the liability moves with cash paid less expense in every year. Lab 1 runs one SIMULATED lease, a seven-year lease at 6.30 percent, and shows that the two expense patterns total the same payments at different dates.

1The model: liability, asset and two patterns

Definition 1.1 (lease accounting, as this unit uses the term)

Lease accounting is, in this unit, the mapping from a fixed-payment lease to a schedule of a lease liability, a right-of-use asset and the expense each of two patterns recognizes. The lease is a sequence of \(n\) annual payments \(P\) in cents, paid at the end of each year, with the first year beginning at commencement. The mapping is fixed by the three definitions that follow. It does not state the criteria under which a standard requires a lease to be classified, measured or presented in a given way. Those criteria are the open question of Section 4.

Definition 1.2 (liability at commencement)

With an annual discount rate \(r\), the liability at commencement is \(L_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n} P / (1 + r)^t\), the present value of the payments. In the lab this is computed in floating point and rounded once to whole cents. Every later amount is an integer.

Definition 1.3 (the integer schedule)

Write the discount rate in basis points, \(\rho\), so that \(r = \rho / 10{,}000\). Interest in year \(t\) is \(I_t = \lfloor (L_{t-1} \rho + 5{,}000) / 10{,}000 \rfloor\), which is rounded half up in integer arithmetic. The liability closes the year at \(L_t = L_{t-1} + I_t - Y_t\), where \(Y_t\) is the payment. For \(t < n\), \(Y_t = P\). For \(t = n\), \(Y_n = L_{n-1} + I_n\), so that \(L_n = 0\). The difference \(Y_n - P\) is the final adjustment, which is the rounding residual of the schedule.

Definition 1.4 (the right-of-use asset and the two expense patterns)

The right-of-use asset at commencement is \(A_0 = L_0\), because the generated lease has no initial direct costs, prepayments or incentives. It is amortized straight-line over the \(n\) years: the amortization \(a_t\) is \(\lfloor A_0 / n \rfloor\) in years 1 to \(n-1\), and the last year takes the remainder, so the amortization sums to \(A_0\). Pattern F, front-loaded, is expense \(E^F_t = I_t + a_t\). Pattern S, straight-line, spreads the total payments evenly: \(E^S_t = \lfloor \sum_t Y_t / n \rfloor\) in years 1 to \(n-1\), with the last year taking the remainder. Both patterns are conventions of this lab. Neither is claimed to be the required treatment of any framework.

2Identities of the schedule

Proposition 2.1 (the liability closes at zero)

Under Definition 1.3, \(L_n = 0\) in every schedule, and the final adjustment \(Y_n - P\) is an integer.

Proof

Every quantity in the recursion is an integer, because \(I_t\) is the floor of an integer quotient and \(Y_t\) is an integer sum of integers. By the final-payment rule, \(L_n = L_{n-1} + I_n - Y_n = L_{n-1} + I_n - (L_{n-1} + I_n) = 0\). The adjustment \(Y_n - P\) is an integer difference of two integers.

Proposition 2.2 (the asset closes at zero)

Under Definition 1.4, \(A_n = 0\), and \(A_t = A_0 - \sum_{s \le t} a_s\) for every \(t\).

Proof

The amortizations are \(n - 1\) equal shares \(\lfloor A_0 / n \rfloor\) and one final share \(A_0 - (n-1) \lfloor A_0 / n \rfloor\). Their sum is \(A_0\), so \(A_n = A_0 - A_0 = 0\). The formula for \(A_t\) is the running sum.

Proposition 2.3 (the total expense of each pattern equals the total payments)

Under Definitions 1.3 and 1.4, \(\sum_t E^F_t = \sum_t Y_t\) and \(\sum_t E^S_t = \sum_t Y_t\), in integer cents.

Proof

Summing the recursion of Definition 1.3 over all \(n\) years gives \(L_n - L_0 = \sum_t I_t - \sum_t Y_t\). Since \(L_n = 0\), \(\sum_t I_t = \sum_t Y_t - L_0\). Summing the amortization gives \(\sum_t a_t = A_0 = L_0\) by Proposition 2.2. Therefore \(\sum_t E^F_t = \sum_t I_t + \sum_t a_t = \sum_t Y_t - L_0 + L_0 = \sum_t Y_t\). For pattern S, the shares sum to \(\sum_t Y_t\) by the same remainder rule used for the amortization, so \(\sum_t E^S_t = \sum_t Y_t\).

Proposition 2.4 (articulation: asset less liability moves with cash less expense)

For every year \(t\), \((A_t - L_t) - (A_{t-1} - L_{t-1}) = Y_t - E^F_t\). Under Definition 1.4 the expense is pattern F, and \(Y_t\) is the cash paid in year \(t\).

Proof

By Definition 1.4, \(A_t - A_{t-1} = -a_t\). By Definition 1.3, \(L_t - L_{t-1} = I_t - Y_t\). Subtracting the second change from the first gives \((A_t - L_t) - (A_{t-1} - L_{t-1}) = -a_t - I_t + Y_t = Y_t - E^F_t\).

Remark 2.5 (why pattern F is larger early and smaller late)

Interest is charged on the opening liability, which falls each year as payments reduce it. The amortization is constant. Pattern F expense therefore falls over the term. Pattern S is constant by construction. The two sum to the same total (Proposition 2.3), so pattern F is above pattern S early in the term and below it late.

Lab 1 · One SIMULATED lease: liability, asset, two patterns and articulation

The lab generates a lease from seed 2606: a term of four to seven years, an annual payment between 120,000.00 and 300,000.00 CU, and a discount rate between 4.00 and 8.00 percent. Stated before running: the liability and asset close at zero in the last year (Propositions 2.1 and 2.2); the total expense of each pattern equals the total payments (Proposition 2.3); the change in asset less liability equals cash paid less pattern F expense in every year (Proposition 2.4); and the final payment differs from the contractual payment only by a rounding residual of a few cents. All amounts are integer cents.

"""AC1 L06 lab 1: one SIMULATED lease, its liability and right-of-use asset roll-forward, and an articulation check.

SIMULATED: the lease is generated from a seeded generator. No lessor, lessee or filing is represented.
Units: integer cents for every money amount, and the discount rate is in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 percent a year).

The lease has fixed annual payments in arrears over a term of four to seven years, starting at the beginning of
year 1. The liability at commencement is the present value of the payments at the discount rate. The right-of-use
asset at commencement equals that liability, because the generated lease has no initial direct costs, prepayments
or incentives.

Each year the liability accrues interest at the discount rate and is reduced by the payment. The right-of-use asset
is amortized straight-line over the term. Two expense patterns for the same lease are compared:
pattern F (front-loaded) is interest plus amortization, and pattern S (straight-line) spreads the total payments
evenly over the term. Both patterns are conventions of this lab; the lab does not claim either is the required
treatment of any framework.

The only floating-point step is the present value, which is rounded once to whole cents. Every later step is integer
arithmetic. The final payment absorbs the schedule's rounding, and the lab prints the size of that adjustment.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import random
from dataclasses import dataclass

SEED = 2606


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Lease:
    """A SIMULATED fixed-payment lease: annual payments in arrears, all money in integer cents."""

    term_years: int
    payment_cents: int
    rate_bps: int  # annual discount rate in basis points


def generate(seed: int) -> Lease:
    """Seeded term (4 to 7 years), annual payment (1,200.00 to 3,000.00 CU), and rate (4.00 to 8.00 percent)."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    term = rng.randint(4, 7)
    payment = rng.randint(120_000_00, 300_000_00)
    rate = rng.randint(400, 800)
    return Lease(term, payment, rate)


def present_value_cents(lease: Lease) -> int:
    """Present value of the contractual payments at commencement, rounded once to whole cents."""
    rate = lease.rate_bps / 10_000
    pv = sum(lease.payment_cents / (1 + rate) ** t for t in range(1, lease.term_years + 1))
    return round(pv)


def interest_cents(opening: int, rate_bps: int) -> int:
    """One year of interest in cents on an opening balance, rounded half up in integer arithmetic."""
    return (opening * rate_bps + 5_000) // 10_000


def straight_line(total: int, years: int) -> list[int]:
    """Equal yearly shares of a total in cents; the last year takes the remainder so the shares sum to the total."""
    base = total // years
    return [base] * (years - 1) + [total - base * (years - 1)]


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Year:
    """One year of the roll-forward, every amount in integer cents."""

    opening_liability: int
    interest: int
    payment: int
    closing_liability: int
    rou_opening: int
    amortization: int
    rou_closing: int


def roll_forward(lease: Lease, initial_liability: int) -> tuple[list[Year], int]:
    """The liability and right-of-use schedule. Returns the years and the final payment's adjustment in cents.

    The last year's payment is the balance owed plus that year's interest, so the liability ends at exactly zero.
    Its difference from the contractual payment is the schedule's rounding residual, returned as the second value.
    """
    amort = straight_line(initial_liability, lease.term_years)
    years: list[Year] = []
    liability = initial_liability
    rou = initial_liability
    adjustment = 0
    for year in range(lease.term_years):
        interest = interest_cents(liability, lease.rate_bps)
        if year == lease.term_years - 1:
            payment = liability + interest
            adjustment = payment - lease.payment_cents
        else:
            payment = lease.payment_cents
        closing = liability + interest - payment
        rou_closing = rou - amort[year]
        years.append(Year(liability, interest, payment, closing, rou, amort[year], rou_closing))
        liability = closing
        rou = rou_closing
    return years, adjustment


def main() -> None:
    lease = generate(SEED)
    initial = present_value_cents(lease)
    years, adjustment = roll_forward(lease, initial)
    print(
        f"seed {SEED} term_years {lease.term_years} annual_payment_cents {lease.payment_cents} "
        f"rate_bps {lease.rate_bps}"
    )
    print(f"initial_liability_cents {initial} initial_rou_asset_cents {initial}")
    expense_f = [y.interest + y.amortization for y in years]
    total_payments = lease.payment_cents * (lease.term_years - 1) + years[-1].payment
    expense_s = straight_line(total_payments, lease.term_years)
    for t, y in enumerate(years, start=1):
        print(
            f"year {t} opening_liability {y.opening_liability} interest {y.interest} payment {y.payment} "
            f"closing_liability {y.closing_liability} rou_opening {y.rou_opening} amortization {y.amortization} "
            f"rou_closing {y.rou_closing} expense_front_loaded {expense_f[t - 1]} "
            f"expense_straight_line {expense_s[t - 1]}"
        )
    print(f"final_payment_cents {years[-1].payment} contractual_payment_cents {lease.payment_cents}")
    print(f"final_payment_adjustment_cents {adjustment}")
    print(f"liability_ends_at_zero {years[-1].closing_liability == 0} rou_ends_at_zero {years[-1].rou_closing == 0}")

    # Identity 1: the total expense of each pattern equals the total payments.
    print(f"sum_payments_cents {total_payments}")
    print(f"sum_expense_front_loaded_cents {sum(expense_f)} sum_expense_straight_line_cents {sum(expense_s)}")
    print(f"total_expense_identity {sum(expense_f) == total_payments and sum(expense_s) == total_payments}")

    # Identity 2 (articulation): each year, the change in (ROU asset less liability) equals cash paid
    # less the pattern F expense.
    articulation = all(
        (y.rou_closing - y.closing_liability) - (y.rou_opening - y.opening_liability) == y.payment - expense_f[t]
        for t, y in enumerate(years)
    )
    print(f"articulation_every_year {articulation}")
    print(f"net_position_change_total_cents {years[-1].rou_closing - years[-1].closing_liability}")

    # Difference between the patterns, by year: front-loaded expense minus straight-line expense.
    difference = [f - s for f, s in zip(expense_f, expense_s, strict=True)]
    print(
        f"expense_difference_year_1_cents {difference[0]} "
        f"expense_difference_year_{lease.term_years}_cents {difference[-1]}"
    )
    print(f"cumulative_difference_zero_at_end {sum(difference) == 0}")


main()

3What the model cannot settle

The model above takes four decisions as given. It takes the payments as fixed and annual, paid in arrears, and it has no variable payments, renewal options, terminations or modifications. It takes the discount rate as an input and does not say how a reader should choose it. It sets the initial asset equal to the initial liability, with no initial direct costs, prepayments or incentives. It chooses the two expense patterns for comparison and does not say which one a framework requires. Each of these is a standard-specific question. Their answers depend on primary texts that are not yet in the registry, so the lesson states no claim about them.

Honest pending. No real lease appears in this lesson. The lease is generated from a seed and labelled SIMULATED. A worked example from a real lessee's lease note is deferred until the governing texts are admitted and read, and until the reuse terms of its source have been checked.

4Where the canon breaks

  • The discount rate decides the liability. A higher rate lowers the present value, and so the asset, and it raises the interest charged on any given opening liability. The lab takes its rate as an input. The rule for choosing the rate is a standard-specific question and is open.
  • Pattern F and pattern S differ in every year of Lab 1 and agree only in total. Proposition 2.3 shows the totals agree, and Remark 2.5 explains why the years differ. A comparison of one year between two lessees that use different patterns mixes a timing effect with the economics.
  • Integer closing hides a residual, and does not remove it. The final payment absorbs a residual of one cent in Lab 1. That residual is a convention of the schedule. It is not an economic amount, and a reader should not treat it as one.
  • The model omits what the standards most often change. Variable payments, renewal and termination options, and modifications are not modelled here. A lease with any of them is outside the scope of Proposition 2.1 to 2.4 as stated.

5Misconceptions

  1. “A lease expense in each year is the payment made in that year.”

    Pattern F expense in year 1 is 14,820,224 cents, against a payment of 13,034,272 cents (Lab 1). Only the sum over the term equals the payments (Proposition 2.3). Each year's expense is a separate quantity, and under pattern F it is interest plus amortization.

  2. “The asset and the liability are the same number at every date.”

    They are equal at commencement, because \(A_0 = L_0\) under Definition 1.4. After year 1 of Lab 1 they differ: the asset is 61,708,083 cents and the liability is 63,494,035 cents, a difference of \(-1{,}785{,}952\) cents. Proposition 2.4 gives the change in their difference exactly, as cash paid less pattern F expense.

  3. “A one-cent final adjustment means the schedule is wrong.”

    The schedule closes to zero by construction (Proposition 2.1). The one-cent adjustment is the difference between the final payment that closes the liability and the contractual payment. It arises because the present value is rounded once and each year's interest is rounded half up. The lab prints it so that the convention is visible.

6Problem set

Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. All amounts are in cents.

Mechanics

M1 · A three-year lease at 10 percent

A lease has three annual payments of 10,000 cents, paid in arrears, with a discount rate of 1,000 basis points. Compute the liability at commencement, the year-1 interest and the closing liability at the end of year 1, using Definition 1.3.

Solution

The present value is \(10{,}000 / 1.1 + 10{,}000 / 1.1^2 + 10{,}000 / 1.1^3 = 24{,}868.52\) cents, which rounds once to \(L_0 = 24{,}869\). The year-1 interest is \(\lfloor (24{,}869 \cdot 1{,}000 + 5{,}000) / 10{,}000 \rfloor = \lfloor 2{,}487.4 \rfloor = 2{,}487\). The closing liability is \(24{,}869 + 2{,}487 - 10{,}000 = 17{,}356\).

M2 · Amortization of the asset

For the lease of M1, the asset at commencement is 24,869 cents, amortized straight-line over three years. Compute the amortization in each year and check that it sums to the asset.

Solution

\(\lfloor 24{,}869 / 3 \rfloor = 8{,}289\) with remainder 2. Years 1 and 2 each take 8,289, and year 3 takes the remainder \(24{,}869 - 2 \cdot 8{,}289 = 8{,}291\). The three shares are 8,289, 8,289 and 8,291, and they sum to 24,869.

M3 · The final payment and pattern S

For the lease of M1, compute the year-2 closing liability, the year-3 interest and the final payment. Then compute pattern S expense in each year.

Solution

Year 2 interest is \(\lfloor (17{,}356 \cdot 1{,}000 + 5{,}000) / 10{,}000 \rfloor = 1{,}736\), and the year closes at \(17{,}356 + 1{,}736 - 10{,}000 = 9{,}092\). Year 3 interest is \(\lfloor (9{,}092 \cdot 1{,}000 + 5{,}000) / 10{,}000 \rfloor = 909\). The final payment is \(9{,}092 + 909 = 10{,}001\), one cent above the contractual 10,000. The total payments are \(10{,}000 + 10{,}000 + 10{,}001 = 30{,}001\). Pattern S gives 10,000 in years 1 and 2, and the last year takes the remainder \(30{,}001 - 20{,}000 = 10{,}001\).

M4 · Articulation in year 1 of M1

For the lease of M1, compute the asset less the liability at the end of year 1, its change from commencement, and check Proposition 2.4 for year 1.

Solution

At the end of year 1 the asset is \(24{,}869 - 8{,}289 = 16{,}580\) and the liability is 17,356. Their difference is \(16{,}580 - 17{,}356 = -776\). At commencement the difference is \(24{,}869 - 24{,}869 = 0\), so the change is \(-776\). Proposition 2.4 gives \(Y_1 - E^F_1 = 10{,}000 - (2{,}487 + 8{,}289) = 10{,}000 - 10{,}776 = -776\), which agrees.

Proof

P1 · The liability closes at zero

Prove Proposition 2.1 in full, including why every quantity in the recursion is an integer.

Solution

The present value \(L_0\) is an integer because it is rounded once to whole cents. Each interest amount \(I_t = \lfloor (L_{t-1} \rho + 5{,}000) / 10{,}000 \rfloor\) is the floor of a quotient of integers, so it is an integer. Each payment \(Y_t\) is either \(P\) or \(L_{n-1} + I_n\), both integers. By induction every \(L_t = L_{t-1} + I_t - Y_t\) is an integer. The final-payment rule gives \(L_n = L_{n-1} + I_n - (L_{n-1} + I_n) = 0\). The adjustment \(Y_n - P\) is an integer, and the schedule closes exactly, with no tolerance needed.

P2 · Articulation of asset and liability

Prove Proposition 2.4 directly from the definitions of \(A_t\) and \(L_t\), and state which definition supplies the cash payment \(Y_t\).

Solution

By Definition 1.4 the asset falls by the amortization, so \(A_t - A_{t-1} = -a_t\). By Definition 1.3 the liability changes by interest less payment, so \(L_t - L_{t-1} = I_t - Y_t\). Therefore \((A_t - L_t) - (A_{t-1} - L_{t-1}) = (A_t - A_{t-1}) - (L_t - L_{t-1}) = -a_t - I_t + Y_t\). Since pattern F expense is \(E^F_t = I_t + a_t\), this equals \(Y_t - E^F_t\). The payment \(Y_t\) is the cash paid in year \(t\), because each payment is made at year end, as Definition 1.1 states.

P3 · Totals equal the payments under both patterns

Prove Proposition 2.3 for pattern F. Then explain why pattern S also totals the payments, and say which fact of the remainder rule is used.

Solution

Summing \(L_t - L_{t-1} = I_t - Y_t\) over the term gives \(L_n - L_0 = \sum I_t - \sum Y_t\). With \(L_n = 0\), \(\sum I_t = \sum Y_t - L_0\). The amortization sums to \(A_0 = L_0\), so \(\sum E^F_t = \sum I_t + \sum a_t = \sum Y_t\). Pattern S uses shares \(\lfloor \sum Y_t / n \rfloor\) in years 1 to \(n-1\), and the last year takes the remainder \(\sum Y_t - (n-1)\lfloor \sum Y_t / n \rfloor\), so the shares sum to \(\sum Y_t\) exactly. The fact used is that the last share is defined as the remainder.

Empirical

E1 · Pattern F share of total expense in year 1

Run Lab 1. Compute the share of total pattern F expense recognized in year 1, to four decimals, and show the division.

Solution

Year-1 pattern F expense is 14,820,224 cents and the total is 91,239,905 cents. The division is \(14{,}820{,}224 / 91{,}239{,}905 = 0.162431\), which is 0.1624 at four decimals. A straight-line pattern would put \(1/7 = 0.1429\) of the total in year 1, so pattern F places a larger share early. The share describes this lease.

E2 · The interest rate check

Run Lab 1. Confirm from the printed year-1 interest and the initial liability that the interest is the discount rate times the opening liability, to the cent.

Solution

The rate is 630 basis points, or \(0.063\). The opening liability is 71,992,763 cents. Their product is \(4{,}535{,}544.07\) unrounded, and the printed interest is 4,535,544 cents. The printed value is the unrounded product rounded half up to the cent, as Definition 1.3 specifies.

E3 · The year in which the patterns cross

Run Lab 1. Using the printed per-year expenses of the two patterns, find the first year in which pattern F expense is below pattern S expense, and report the difference in that year.

Solution

The printed pattern F expenses are 14,820,224, 14,284,804, 13,715,653, 13,110,645, 12,467,522, 11,783,882 and 11,057,175 cents for years 1 to 7. Pattern S is 13,034,272 for years 1 to 6 and 13,034,273 in year 7. Pattern F is above pattern S in years 1 to 4 (year 4: 13,110,645 against 13,034,272) and below it in years 5 to 7. The first year in which pattern F is below pattern S is year 5, where the difference is \(12{,}467{,}522 - 13{,}034{,}272 = -566{,}750\) cents. The crossing year depends on the lease's term, rate and payment.

Research

R1 · A test on a real lease

Describe, in full, a test of Proposition 2.4 on one real lessee's lease. Name every input, its source, the assumptions to check first, and the result that would refute the identity for that lease. Do not run it.

Solution

Inputs: the payment schedule of each lease, the discount rate the lessee discloses, the opening and closing balances of the asset and the liability for each year, and the cash paid in each year. The first assumption to check is that the asset and the liability are the lessee's own balances and not a reconstruction. Next, check that the lease has no variable payments, options or modifications, since Section 3 says the identity does not cover them. Then compute, for each year, the change in asset less liability, and compare it with cash paid less the lessee's stated expense for the lease. A difference that remains after the lessee's own disclosed expense is used refutes the identity for that lease, unless the difference is traced to an omitted item in Section 3. The rules that set the expense and the discount rate are the primary text, which the unit has not yet admitted.

R2 · The open lease decisions and their evidence

For each of the four decisions in Section 3, name the question a reader must answer from the primary text and the evidence that would decide it.

Solution

Payment structure: whether the payments are fixed, and which of them are variable, which the lessee's lease note states. Discount rate: which rate the standard requires for the measurement, and what the lessee discloses, which the primary text states. Initial asset: which initial costs and incentives enter the asset, which the primary text lists and the lessee's note discloses. Expense pattern: which pattern the standard requires for a given lease type, which only the primary text answers. None of these four questions is answered by Propositions 2.1 to 2.4, which hold for any schedule that follows Definitions 1.3 and 1.4. Each answer needs a cited text, which the unit does not yet hold.

R3 · Is the expense pattern a measurement or a recognition choice?

Argue whether the choice between pattern F and pattern S is a choice of measurement or a choice of recognition. Use Proposition 2.3 and Remark 2.5 in the argument.

Solution

Both patterns recognize the same total expense over the term, by Proposition 2.3. So the choice does not change the measurement of the lease's total cost. It changes the year in which that cost enters net income, which is a choice of recognition timing. Remark 2.5 shows the timing difference is front-loaded and reverses late in the term. A reader comparing two lessees in one year must therefore check which pattern each uses, because the year-1 difference for Lab 1's lease alone is 1,785,952 cents.
