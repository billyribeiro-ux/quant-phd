### Part 0 · Orientation & Learning System (`P0`)

- **P0.1 · How to Study This Program** · F · 6 h · ← none
  - *Core:* Evidence grades (T/A/B/C/D/X); how to read a paper (question, identification, data, inference, robustness); steelmanning; the project ladder; how Atlas compounds; the notation conventions.
  - *Sources:* Dunlosky et al. (2013), Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1); Keshav (2007), 'How to Read a Paper', ACM SIGCOMM CCR 37(3)
  - *Lab:* Navigate the prerequisite DAG; run a first Pyodide cell.
  - *Project — Paper teardown* (`rubric`): Take one assigned classic paper and produce a one-page structured teardown: question, mechanism, data, identification, main result with its locator, the weakest assumption, and the evidence grade it deserves. *Deliverable:* One-page teardown in the in-page template.
- **P0.2 · Diagnostics, Mastery Gating & Spaced Retrieval** · F · 4 h · ← P0.1
  - *Core:* How placement tests let you skip what you already know; mastery thresholds; the spaced-retrieval scheduler; interleaved review; why rereading is a weak strategy and retrieval is a strong one.
  - *Sources:* Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Psychological Science 17(3); Cepeda et al. (2006), Psychological Bulletin 132(3); Rohrer & Taylor (2007), Instructional Science 35; Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003), Educational Psychologist 38(1)
  - *Lab:* Take the program-wide placement diagnostic; the result unlocks test-out paths.
  - *Project — Personal study plan* (`rubric`): From your placement results and the track hour totals, build a dated study plan covering weekly hours, track choice, review cadence and milestones. *Deliverable:* Exported plan JSON plus a half-page rationale.

### Part 1 · Mathematics (`M`)

**Studio · Numerical Mathematics Library** · U · 60 h · ← M7, M8 · `autograder`  
Implement from scratch a root finder (Brent), a QP solver (active-set) and a Monte Carlo engine with antithetic and control variates. Verify each against SciPy to stated tolerances, and include a written proof of convergence for the root finder.  
*Deliverables:* Python package with tests, Proof write-up, Benchmark report

- **M1 · Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning** · F · 100 h · ← P0.1
  - *Core:* Algebra, functions, exponentials and logarithms (continuous compounding), sequences and series (annuities, perpetuities), sets, quantifiers, direct proof, contradiction, induction.
  - *Sources:* Velleman, How to Prove It, 3rd ed. (2019)
  - *Lab:* Compounding-frequency convergence to e^{rt}.
  - *Project — Annuity and mortgage engine* (`both`): Derive the closed forms for annuity, perpetuity and growing-perpetuity values by induction and geometric series, then implement and test them, including a full amortization schedule. *Deliverable:* Proofs and a tested Python module.
- **M2 · Single & Multivariable Calculus** · F · 300 h · ← M1
  - *Core:* Limits, derivatives, Taylor's theorem, integration, partial derivatives, gradients, Jacobians, Hessians, multiple integrals, implicit-function and envelope theorems.
  - *Sources:* Spivak, Calculus, 4th ed. (2008); Hubbard & Hubbard, Vector Calculus, Linear Algebra, and Differential Forms, 5th ed. (2015)
  - *Lab:* Taylor expansion of a bond price: duration and convexity preview.
  - *Project — Sensitivity engine* (`both`): Build first- and second-order sensitivities for any differentiable pricing function, using analytic, finite-difference and automatic-differentiation approaches, and characterize where finite differences break. *Deliverable:* Tested module plus an error-analysis note.
- **M3 · Linear Algebra** · F · 150 h · ← M1
  - *Core:* Vector spaces, linear maps, eigendecomposition, spectral theorem, SVD, positive-semidefinite matrices, projections, least-squares geometry, PCA.
  - *Sources:* Axler, Linear Algebra Done Right, 4th ed. (2024); Strang, Introduction to Linear Algebra, 6th ed. (2023)
  - *Lab:* PCA of the U.S. Treasury curve (embedded data): level, slope, curvature.
  - *Project — Covariance-matrix doctor* (`both`): Diagnose and repair non-PSD empirical correlation matrices (nearest-correlation projection, eigenvalue clipping). Compare the effect of each repair on downstream portfolio weights. *Deliverable:* Tested module plus a comparison report.
- **M4 · Probability** · F · 120 h · ← M2
  - *Core:* Distributions, expectation, MGFs and characteristic functions, the multivariate normal, conditioning, LLN, CLT, heavy tails, and the failure of the CLT under infinite variance.
  - *Sources:* Blitzstein & Hwang, Introduction to Probability, 2nd ed. (2019)
  - *Lab:* Sample-mean behaviour under Gaussian, Student-t(3) and Cauchy draws.
  - *Project — Tail-risk simulator* (`both`): Simulate portfolio losses under multivariate Gaussian vs. multivariate Student-t returns with matched covariance. Quantify how the 99% loss estimate changes, and prove the moment conditions behind the result. *Deliverable:* Simulator plus a proof appendix.
- **M5 · Real Analysis** · U · 130 h · ← M2
  - *Core:* Completeness of R, sequences and series, continuity, uniform convergence, compactness, metric spaces, fixed-point theorems (Banach), Riemann–Stieltjes.
  - *Sources:* Abbott, Understanding Analysis, 2nd ed. (2015); Rudin, Principles of Mathematical Analysis, 3rd ed. (1976)
  - *Lab:* Banach fixed-point iteration solving a Bellman equation.
  - *Project — Contraction-mapping solver* (`both`): Prove the Bellman operator for a discounted savings problem is a contraction, then implement value-function iteration with a certified error bound. *Deliverable:* Proof plus a tested solver.
- **M6 · Measure-Theoretic Probability** · M · 150 h · ← M4, M5
  - *Core:* Sigma-algebras, Lebesgue integral, MCT and DCT, Fubini, Radon–Nikodym, conditional expectation as L2 projection, filtrations, discrete-time martingales, optional stopping, martingale convergence.
  - *Sources:* Williams, Probability with Martingales (1991); Billingsley, Probability and Measure, anniversary ed. (2012)
  - *Lab:* Martingale betting-system simulator: why doubling strategies fail under finite capital.
  - *Project — Proof portfolio: martingales* (`both`): Write complete proofs of optional stopping (bounded case), Doob's maximal inequality, and the martingale convergence theorem, then verify each numerically on simulated paths. *Deliverable:* Typeset proofs plus verification notebook.
- **M7 · Optimization** · F · 110 h · ← M2, M3
  - *Core:* Convex sets and functions, Lagrange multipliers, KKT conditions, duality, quadratic programming, dynamic programming and the Bellman equation.
  - *Sources:* Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization (2004); Stokey & Lucas, Recursive Methods in Economic Dynamics (1989)
  - *Lab:* Efficient frontier via QP with and without a short-sale constraint.
  - *Project — Constrained allocator* (`autograder`): Build a mean-variance allocator supporting box, turnover and sector constraints. Verify the KKT conditions hold at every returned solution. *Deliverable:* Tested module plus KKT-certificate output.
- **M8 · Numerical Methods & Scientific Computing** · U · 110 h · ← M2, M3, CS1
  - *Core:* IEEE 754, conditioning and stability, root finding, linear solvers, interpolation, quadrature, finite differences, random-number generation, Monte Carlo and variance reduction.
  - *Sources:* Goldberg (1991), 'What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic', ACM Computing Surveys 23(1); Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering (2003)
  - *Lab:* Catastrophic cancellation; antithetic and control-variate efficiency.
  - *Project — Robust implied-volatility solver* (`autograder`): Build an implied-vol solver that never fails on valid inputs (deep ITM/OTM, near expiry), using bracketing with Newton acceleration. Stress it on a generated grid of a million inputs. *Deliverable:* Tested solver plus a failure-region report.

*Deliverables:* C++ engine + Python API, Test suite, Benchmark report

- **CS1 · Programming in Python from Zero** · F · 120 h · ← P0.1 · ⚑ A1

  - *Sources:* Python Language Reference (docs.python.org); Ramalho, Fluent Python, 2nd ed. (2022)
  - *Lab:* Vectorized returns computation vs. loops: correctness and speed.
  - *Project — Atlas A1: repository skeleton* (`autograder`): Create the Atlas package with typed modules, a CLI, a pytest suite, formatting and linting config, and CI. Every later Atlas milestone builds on this repository. *Deliverable:* Atlas repository at milestone A1.
- **CS2 · Algorithms & Data Structures** · F · 130 h · ← CS1
  - *Core:* Complexity, arrays, hash maps, heaps, trees, graphs, sorting, searching, dynamic programming, and the data structures behind order books and event queues.
  - *Sources:* Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest & Stein, Introduction to Algorithms, 4th ed. (2022)
  - *Lab:* Heap-based event queue vs. a sorted list at scale.
  - *Project — Streaming statistics library* (`both`): Implement O(1)-update rolling mean, variance (Welford), min/max (monotonic deque) and an approximate quantile sketch. Prove the correctness of Welford's update. *Deliverable:* Tested library plus proof.
- **CS3 · Data, Databases & SQL** · F · 80 h · ← CS1 · ⚑ A2
  - *Core:* The relational model, SQL, joins, window functions, ASOF joins, indexing, columnar storage, Parquet, DuckDB.
