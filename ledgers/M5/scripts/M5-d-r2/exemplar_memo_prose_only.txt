== exemplar-a
h5: Setting
p: The grid has ten points. The operator uses output \(k^{0.3}\) and utility \(\sqrt{c}\) with \(\beta = 0.9\). The iteration starts from zero and stops at a tolerance of \(10^{-10}\).
h5: Contraction
p: The operator is a contraction with modulus \(\beta\), because the maximum of a difference is at most the maximum of the differences.
h5: Certified bound
p: The certified error is \(\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\) times the last change. At the stop it is \(9.226\times 10^{-11}\).
h5: Numerical check
p: The iteration takes about 240 sweeps, and the residual is well inside the bound.
h5: Limitations
p: The grid is a limitation of the model.
== exemplar-b
h5: Setting
p: The state space is a grid \(k_1 < \cdots < k_{10}\) of positive points, with \(k_1 = 0.45 \le 1\), under the sup norm. A saving \(k'\) is feasible from \(k\) when \(k' \le k^{0.3}\), and utility is \(\sqrt{k^{0.3}-k'}\) on consumption \(k^{0.3}-k' \ge 0\). The discount factor is \(\beta = 0.9\), with \(\beta\in(0,1)\), and the production exponent is \(\alpha = 0.3\), with \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). The iteration starts at \(V_0 = 0\), stops at a tolerance of \(10^{-10}\), and the fixed point \(V^{\ast}\) is the one of the grid operator.
h5: Contraction (A1)
p: For a state \(k\), let \(a\) and \(b\) be the maxima of \(\sqrt{k^{0.3}-k'}+\beta V(k')\) and \(\sqrt{k^{0.3}-k'}+\beta W(k')\) over the same feasible set. Each of the two maxima is taken over the same family of summands, which differ only by \(\beta(V(k')-W(k'))\). The maximum of a family of numbers moves by at most the largest move of its members, so \(|a-b| \le \beta\max_{k'}|V(k')-W(k')| = \beta\lVert V-W\rVert_\infty\). Taking the maximum over \(k\) gives \(\lVert\mathcal{B}V-\mathcal{B}W\rVert_\infty \le \beta\lVert V-W\rVert_\infty\).
h5: Fixed point (A2, A3)
p: A2: a vector on the grid is a point of \(\R^{10}\), and \(|V(k_i)-W(k_i)|\le\lVert V-W\rVert_\infty\) for each \(i\). A Cauchy sequence in the sup norm is therefore Cauchy in each of the ten coordinates, each coordinate converges because \(\R\) is complete, and the ten limits form a vector on the grid that is the sup-norm limit, since there are only ten indices. A3: by A1, \(d_{n+1}\le\beta d_n\) for \(d_n = \lVert V_n-V_{n-1}\rVert_\infty\), so \(\sum_n d_n\) is a geometric series and the sweeps are Cauchy. Their limit \(V^{\ast}\) satisfies \(V^{\ast}=\mathcal{B}V^{\ast}\), because \(\mathcal{B}\) is continuous by A1. If \(W\) is another fixed point, \(\lVert V^{\ast}-W\rVert_\infty = \lVert\mathcal{B}V^{\ast}-\mathcal{B}W\rVert_\infty \le \beta\lVert V^{\ast}-W\rVert_\infty\), which with \(\beta<1\) forces \(W=V^{\ast}\).
h5: Certified bound (A4)
p: Let \(d_n\) be as above. By A1 with \(V = V_k\) and \(W = V_{k-1}\), \(d_{k+1} \le \beta d_k\), so by induction on \(j\), \(d_{n+j} \le \beta^{j} d_n\). By A3 the sweeps converge to \(V^{\ast}\), so the triangle inequality and the limit give \(\lVert V_n-V^{\ast}\rVert_\infty \le \sum_{j\ge 1} d_{n+j} \le d_n\sum_{j\ge 1}\beta^{j} = d_n\beta/(1-\beta)\). At the stop, the last change is printed as \(1.025\times 10^{-11}\), and the certified error as \(9.226\times 10^{-11}\).
h5: Numerical check
p: For the instance of Lab 3, with a tolerance of \(10^{-10}\), the printed line sweeps: 236 | last change: 1.025e-11 | certified error: 9.226e-11 gives 236 sweeps, a last change of \(1.025\times 10^{-11}\) and a certified error of \(9.226\times 10^{-11}\). The printed line a priori count: 242 gives the a priori count of 242, and the printed line residual: 9.226e-12 gives a residual of \(9.226\times 10^{-12}\). The residual is at most \((1+\beta)\) times the certified error, as the printed line ending at most (1 + beta) times the certified error: True confirms. The printed line largest observed contraction ratio over 200 pairs: 0.900000 reports a largest ratio of 0.900000, at most \(\beta\) within the stated rounding margin.
h5: Limitations
p: The bound is for the fixed point of the grid operator. It does not bound the error of a continuous model, and the grid's own spacing is part of the error the bound leaves out. The bound does not measure that part, and this implementation costs about \(m^2\) operations per sweep, so the cost grows with the square of the number of points.
