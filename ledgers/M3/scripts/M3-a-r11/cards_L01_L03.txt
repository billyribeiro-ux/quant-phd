M3-c001 L01 | Q: What is a real vector space? | A: A set V with an addition and a scalar multiplication by real numbers that satisfies the axioms of Definition 1.1: addition is commutative and associative; there is a zero vector and each vector has an additive inverse; a(bv) = (ab)v and 1v = v; and scalar multiplication distributes over vector addition and over scalar addition.

M3-c002 L01 | Q: In a real vector space, what are 0v and (-1)v? | A: 0v is the zero vector, and (-1)v equals -v, the additive inverse of v (Lemma 1.3).

M3-c003 L01 | Q: Which three conditions make a subset W of a real vector space V a subspace? | A: W is non-empty, W is closed under addition, and W is closed under multiplication by real scalars. Under these three conditions W is a vector space under the operations of V (Proposition 1.4, the subspace test).

M3-c004 L01 | Q: What is the span of a list v1, …, vk of vectors? | A: The set of all linear combinations c1 v1 + … + ck vk with real coefficients. The span of the empty list is {0} (Definition 2.1).

M3-c005 L01 | Q: In a real vector space, is the span of a finite list the smallest subspace that contains the list? | A: Yes. The span is a subspace containing each vi, and it is contained in every subspace that contains each vi (Proposition 2.2).

M3-c006 L01 | Q: In R^3, for which vectors x is x in span(e1, e2)? | A: Exactly those with third coordinate x3 = 0, because every combination c1 e1 + c2 e2 has third coordinate 0 and every vector (a, b, 0) equals a e1 + b e2 (Example 2.3).

M3-c007 L01 | Q: When is a list v1, …, vk of vectors linearly independent? | A: When c1 v1 + … + ck vk = 0 forces c1 = … = ck = 0. A list that is not independent is dependent, and the empty list is independent (Definition 3.1).

M3-c008 L01 | Q: What is a basis of a vector space V, and what is its dimension? | A: A basis is a linearly independent list that spans V. If V has a basis of finitely many vectors, its dimension is the number of vectors in such a basis (Definition 3.2).

M3-c009 L01 | Q: What is the rank of a real matrix A? | A: The number of pivots in an echelon form of A, reached by elementary row operations, which leave the solutions of Ac = 0 unchanged (Definition 3.3).

M3-c010 L01 | Q: If the echelon form of an m x k real matrix has fewer than k pivots, what follows for Ac = 0? | A: Ac = 0 has a solution c different from 0 in R^k. In particular, if k > m such a solution exists (Lemma 3.4).

M3-c011 L01 | Q: When is a list of vectors in R^n linearly independent, in terms of its matrix? | A: Exactly when the n x k matrix whose columns are the vectors has k pivots, that is, when its rank equals k (Proposition 3.5).

M3-c012 L01 | Q: How many vectors can a linearly independent list in R^n contain? | A: At most n. Every list of more than n vectors in R^n is linearly dependent, so four vectors in R^3 and five vectors in R^4 are dependent (Corollary 3.6).

M3-c013 L01 | Q: If v1, …, vm span V and w1, …, wk lie in V with k > m, what follows for the w's? | A: The list w1, …, wk is linearly dependent (Theorem 3.7).

M3-c014 L01 | Q: Do two finite bases of a real vector space always have the same number of vectors? | A: Yes. If h_1, …, h_m and g_1, …, g_k are both bases of V with finitely many vectors, then m = k (Theorem 3.8, invariance of dimension). So the dimension of a finite-dimensional space does not depend on the basis.

M3-c015 L01 | Q: Is the standard basis e1, …, en a basis of R^n, and what is dim R^n? | A: Yes. The list spans R^n because (x1, …, xn) = sum of xi ei, and it is independent because sum of ci ei has entries c1, …, cn. So dim R^n = n (Example 3.9, using Theorem 3.8).

M3-c020 L01 | Q: Misconception: three distinct non-zero vectors in R^3 form a basis of R^3. True or false? | A: False. The vectors (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1) and (1, 1, 2) are distinct and non-zero, but the third is the sum of the first two. The list is dependent, its span is the plane z = x + y, and it is not a basis of R^3 (Example 3.10).

M3-c021 L01 | Q: Misconception: four vectors in R^3 can be independent, because there is room for them. True or false? | A: False. Every list of more than n vectors in R^n is dependent (Corollary 3.6). A fourth vector cannot add a new independent direction in R^3.

M3-c022 L01 | Q: Misconception: a list is independent when no vector is a multiple of another. True or false? | A: False. In R^2 the list (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1) has no vector that is a multiple of another, yet (1, 1) = (1, 0) + (0, 1), so the list is dependent. Pairwise non-proportionality does not decide the independence of a longer list.

M3-c023 L02 | Q: What is a linear map between real vector spaces V and W? | A: A function L : V -> W with L(u + v) = Lu + Lv and L(av) = a Lv for all u, v in V and all real a (Definition 1.1).

M3-c024 L02 | Q: For a linear map L, what are L(0) and L(av + bw)? | A: L(0) = 0, and L(av + bw) = a Lv + b Lw for all v, w and real a, b (Lemma 1.2).

M3-c025 L02 | Q: What are the kernel and the image of a linear map L : V -> W? | A: The kernel is the set of v in V with Lv = 0. The image is the set of all Lv with v in V (Definition 1.3).

M3-c026 L02 | Q: Are the kernel and the image of a linear map subspaces? | A: Yes. The kernel is a subspace of V and the image is a subspace of W (Proposition 1.4).

M3-c027 L02 | Q: What is the kernel of L(x, y) = (x + y, x - y) on R^2, and what is its image? | A: The kernel is {0}, because x + y = 0 and x - y = 0 force x = y = 0. The image is all of R^2, because (p, q) = L((p + q)/2, (p - q)/2) (Example 1.5).

M3-c028 L02 | Q: What is the coordinate vector [v]_B of v with respect to an ordered basis B = (b1, …, bn)? | A: The unique x in R^n with v = sum of xj bj. It exists because B spans V and is unique because B is independent (Definition 2.1).

M3-c029 L02 | Q: Are coordinate vectors linear in v? | A: Yes. For a basis B, [av + bw]_B = a[v]_B + b[w]_B (Proposition 2.2).

M3-c030 L02 | Q: What is the matrix of a linear map L : V -> W with respect to bases B of V and C of W? | A: The matrix [L]_{C <- B} whose j-th column is the coordinate vector [L b_j]_C of the image of the j-th basis vector of B (Definition 2.3). It depends on both bases.

M3-c031 L02 | Q: Does the matrix of a linear map represent the map in coordinates? | A: Yes. For every v in V, [Lv]_C = [L]_{C <- B} [v]_B (Theorem 2.4).

M3-c032 L02 | Q: Is a matrix determined by the action of x -> Mx on R^n? | A: Yes. If Mx = Nx for every x in R^n, then M = N, because taking x = e_j shows that the j-th columns agree (Remark 2.5).

M3-c033 L02 | Q: In the basis B = (b1, b2) with b1 = (1, 0) and b2 = (1, 1), what is the matrix of L(x, y) = (x + y, x - y)? | A: The matrix with columns (0, 1) and (2, 0), namely [[0, 2], [1, 0]], because L b1 = b2 and L b2 = 2 b1 (Example 2.6).

M3-c034 L02 | Q: What is the matrix of the composition S after L, when L is applied first? | A: The product [S]_{D <- C} [L]_{C <- B}, with the matrix of the outer map S on the left (Proposition 3.1).

M3-c035 L02 | Q: In the change-of-basis theorem, what is the matrix of x -> Ax in a basis whose columns form the invertible matrix P? | A: P^{-1} A P (Theorem 4.1). The proof uses that the columns of P form a basis, so every x has a coordinate vector, and that P is invertible.

M3-c036 L02 | Q: If Ã = P^{-1} A P with P invertible, what is A in terms of Ã? | A: A = P Ã P^{-1} (Corollary 4.2).

M3-c037 L02 | Q: In Example 4.3, what is P^{-1}(AP) for A = [[1, 1], [1, -1]] and P with columns (1, 0) and (1, 1)? | A: The matrix [[0, 2], [1, 0]], which is the matrix of Example 2.6, as Theorem 4.1 predicts (Example 4.3).

M3-c040 L02 | Q: Misconception: changing the basis changes the map. True or false? | A: False. Changing the basis changes the matrix, not the map. M3-c041 L02 | Q: Misconception: the matrix of a composition is the product in either order. True or false? | A: False. The matrix of S after L is [S][L], with L applied first (Proposition 3.1). M3-c042 L02 | Q: Misconception: a vector has the same coordinates in every basis. True or false? | A: False. In the basis (1, 0), (1, 1) the vector (1, 1) is the second basis vector, so its coordinate vector is (0, 1). Coordinates are defined only relative to a named basis (Definition 2.1).

M3-c043 L03 | Q: What is an eigenvalue and an eigenvector of a real n x n matrix A? | A: A real number lambda is an eigenvalue of A when some nonzero vector v satisfies Av = lambda v. Such a v is an eigenvector for lambda. The zero vector is never an eigenvector (Definition 1.1).

M3-c044 L03 | Q: What is the eigenspace E_lambda of A for a real number lambda? | A: The set of all v in R^n with Av = lambda v. It contains the zero vector, and its nonzero elements are the eigenvectors for lambda (Definition 1.2).

M3-c045 L03 | Q: Is the eigenspace E_lambda of a real square matrix a subspace of R^n? | A: Yes, for every real lambda (Proposition 1.3). The proof uses that x -> Ax is linear.

M3-c046 L03 | Q: When is a real number lambda an eigenvalue of a real square matrix A? | A: Exactly when det(A - lambda I) = 0. The hypotheses are that A is real and square, lambda is real, and the invertibility fact F1 holds (Proposition 1.4).

M3-c047 L03 | Q: What is the characteristic polynomial p_A of a real square matrix A? | A: The function p_A(lambda) = det(A - lambda I) of the real variable lambda (Definition 1.5). The real eigenvalues of A are the real zeros of p_A, by Proposition 1.4 under F1.

M3-c048 L03 | Q: What are the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix [[4, 1], [1, 3]]? | A: Under F1, the eigenvalues are (7 + sqrt 5)/2 and (7 - sqrt 5)/2, which are 4.6180 and 2.3820 to four decimals. The characteristic polynomial is lambda^2 - 7 lambda + 11 (Example 1.6).

M3-c049 L03 | Q: Does every real matrix have a real eigenvalue? | A: No. The rotation R = [[0, -1], [1, 0]] has det(R - lambda I) = lambda^2 + 1, which is at least 1 for every real lambda, so under F1 R has no real eigenvalue (Remark 1.7).

M3-c050 L03 | Q: What is an eigendecomposition of a real n x n matrix A, and what is a diagonalisable matrix? | A: A factorisation A = P D P^{-1} with P an invertible real n x n matrix and D a real diagonal matrix. A matrix that has an eigendecomposition is diagonalisable (Definition 2.1).

M3-c051 L03 | Q: If A has n eigenvectors for real eigenvalues that form a linearly independent list, what factorisation follows? | A: Let P have these eigenvectors as columns and D be the diagonal matrix of the eigenvalues in the same order. Under F1, P is invertible and A = P D P^{-1} (Proposition 2.2).

M3-c052 L03 | Q: When does a real n x n matrix have an eigendecomposition? | A: Under F1, exactly when R^n has a basis made of eigenvectors of A (Proposition 2.3). The hypotheses are that A is real and square, and F1.

M3-c053 L03 | Q: If A = P D P^{-1} with P invertible and D diagonal, what is A^k for an integer k of at least 1? | A: A^k = P D^k P^{-1} (Proposition 2.4), proved by induction on k.

M3-c054 L03 | Q: For a real n x n matrix A and a real number lambda, how is dim E_lambda found from the rank of A - lambda I? | A: dim E_lambda = n minus rank(A - lambda I). E_lambda is the null space of A - lambda I (Definition 1.2), and the null space of an n-column matrix has dimension n minus its rank (Lesson 1, Proposition 3.12). For B = [[1, 1], [0, 1]], the rank of B - I is 1, so dim E_1 = 1 (Problem E3).
