Linear Algebra
Covariance matrices and correlation matrices are matrices that act on vectors. This module teaches the structure those matrices carry: vector spaces and linear maps, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, the spectral theorem for symmetric matrices, positive-semidefinite matrices, the singular value decomposition, orthogonal projections and least squares, principal component analysis, and the nearest correlation matrix. It ends in the covariance-matrix doctor, an engine that tests whether a correlation matrix is valid and repairs it when it is not. You leave able to state and prove the results the engine relies on, to check them numerically, and to explain what each repair does and does not guarantee.
Prerequisites: M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning, which provides quantifiers, direct proof, and the finite sums and discounting that the placement items use. Secondary-school algebra is assumed.
Project: Covariance-matrix doctor, graded by an autograder and a rubric
Data: no real data is embedded in M3. Lesson 8 runs on a SIMULATED panel of yield changes, and the autograder's instances are SIMULATED. The project's three worked labs use hand-set matrices labelled SIMULATED. See the data status below.
105 h lessons · 45 h project (30%)
Objectives
By the end of M3 you can:
State the axioms of a real vector space and of a subspace, and decide whether a set is a subspace by the subspace test (Lesson 1).
Decide whether a list of vectors is independent, spans a space, or is a basis, by computing its rank (Lesson 1).
Write the matrix of a linear map in chosen bases, and derive the change-of-basis formula \(P^{-1} A P\) (Lesson 2).
Compute eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and decide when a matrix is diagonalizable (Lesson 3).
State the spectral theorem with its hypotheses, and test whether a symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite (Lessons 4 and 5).
Decompose a matrix by its singular value decomposition, and compute an orthogonal projection and a least-squares fit from the normal equations (Lessons 6 and 7).
Apply principal component analysis to a panel of yield changes, and repair a non-positive-semidefinite correlation matrix by eigenvalue clipping, stating what the repair does not guarantee (Lessons 8 and 9).

Placement diagnostic
Nine questions: seven on M1, and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent of nine items, which is 7.2, so eight correct is needed: seven of nine is 77.8 percent and eight of nine is 88.9 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Skill check 1 uses Lesson 1. Skill check 2 uses the rescaling step of the project's rung 2, which Lesson 9 and the project teach; Lesson 9 and the project come after the placement, so do not skip rung 2 on the strength of this check alone.
What is the negation of "every \(x\) in \(D\) has property \(\pi\)"?
 Options
 Some \(x\) in \(D\) lacks \(\pi\)
 No \(x\) in \(D\) has \(\pi\)
 Every \(x\) in \(D\) lacks \(\pi\)
M1 Lesson 1, Theorem 3.1: the negation of a universal statement is an existential statement with the predicate negated.
What is \(1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18\), as a decimal?
 Answer 
The finite geometric sum with ratio \(\tfrac12\) and four terms is \(\tfrac{1 - (1/2)^4}{1 - 1/2} = \tfrac{15/16}{1/2} = \tfrac{15}{8} = 1.875\) (M1 Lesson 6).
What is the present value of 100 received in two years, at an annual rate of 5 percent compounded annually? Give three decimals.
 Answer 
The present value is \(100 / 1.05^2 = 100 / 1.1025 \approx 90.7029\), which rounds to 90.703 to three decimals (M1 Lesson 5).
For \(q \ne 1\), which expression equals \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} q^k\)?
 Options
 \(\dfrac{1 - q^n}{1 - q}\)
 \(\dfrac{1 - q^{n+1}}{1 - q}\)
 \(n q\)
The closed form of a finite geometric sum with \(q \ne 1\) (M1 Lesson 6). The second option sums one term too many.
In a proof by induction, what must be shown besides the base case?
 Options
 That the claim holds for the largest value of \(k\)
 That if the claim holds for \(k\), it holds for \(k + 1\), for every \(k\) at or beyond the base case
 That the claim is true for a single value of \(k\) chosen at random
The induction step: the claim for \(k\) implies the claim for \(k + 1\) (M1 Lesson 6).
To prove \(\alpha\) implies \(\beta\) directly, what do you assume?
 Options
 \(\alpha\), and then derive \(\beta\)
 \(\beta\), and then derive \(\alpha\)
 Neither, since a direct proof starts from the conclusion
M1 Lesson 2: a direct proof assumes the hypothesis and derives the conclusion.
What is the contrapositive of "\(\alpha\) implies \(\beta\)"?
 Options
 \(\beta\) implies \(\alpha\)
 Not \(\beta\) implies not \(\alpha\)
 Not \(\alpha\) implies not \(\beta\)
The contrapositive has the same truth table as the original: each is false in exactly one of the four cases, namely \(\alpha\) true and \(\beta\) false.
Skill check 1 (rung 1): what is the dimension of the span of \((1, 0, 1)\), \((0, 1, 1)\) and \((1, 1, 2)\)?
 Answer 
The third vector is the sum of the first two, so the span is the plane \(z = x + y\), which two independent vectors span. The dimension is 2 (Lesson 1, Example 3.10).
Skill check 2 (rung 2): the covariance matrix \(S\) has standard deviations \(\sigma_i\) on the diagonal of \(D\), so that \(S = D C D\) for the correlation matrix \(C\). Which expression gives \(C\)?
 Options
 \(D^{-1} S D^{-1}\)
 \(D S D\)
 \(S - D\)
Multiply \(S = D C D\) by \(D^{-1}\) on the left and by \(D^{-1}\) on the right. This is the rescaling step of the project's rung 2, taught in Lesson 9 and in the project.
Lessons
Nine lessons, each declaring its own study hours on its page. Lessons 1 and 2 cover vector spaces and linear maps. Lessons 3 to 5 cover eigendecomposition, the spectral theorem and positive-semidefinite matrices. Lessons 6 and 7 cover the singular value decomposition, projections and least squares. Lesson 8 applies principal component analysis to a SIMULATED panel of yield changes, and Lesson 9 covers nearest correlation matrices and eigenvalue clipping. The lesson pages share one structure: graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.
 L01 · 12 hVector Spaces, Spans, Bases and DimensionReal vector spaces, the subspace test, span, linear independence, bases, and the invariance of dimension for finite bases, with exact rank computations.
 L02 · 11 hLinear Maps and Their MatricesLinear maps, kernel and image, the matrix of a map in chosen bases, composition as matrix multiplication, and the change-of-basis formula.
 L03 · 12 hEigendecompositionEigenvalues, eigenvectors and the characteristic polynomial, diagonalization when a basis of eigenvectors exists, and residuals as numerical checks.
 L04 · 12 hThe Spectral Theorem for Symmetric MatricesThe spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices with its hypotheses stated, orthogonal eigenvectors, and the factorization \(A = Q\Lambda Q^{\top}\) checked on seeded matrices.
 L05 · 12 hPositive-Semidefinite MatricesThe quadratic-form definition, the eigenvalue test for symmetric matrices, sampled quadratic forms, and a counterexample matrix.
 L06 · 11 hThe Singular Value DecompositionThe decomposition \(A = U \Sigma V^{\top}\), singular values as square roots of the eigenvalues of \(A^{\top}A\), and the lab that checks the relation.
 L07 · 11 hOrthogonal Projections and Least SquaresThe projection matrix \(A(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}\), the normal equations, and the residual orthogonal to the columns, checked against a direct solver.
 L08 · 12 hPrincipal Component Analysis and the Yield-Curve FactorsPrincipal component analysis of a centred SIMULATED panel of yield changes, with explained shares computed from unrounded eigenvalues, and level, slope and curvature read from the loadings.
 L09 · 12 hNearest Correlation Matrices and Eigenvalue ClippingA non-positive-semidefinite matrix repaired by eigenvalue clipping and rescaling to unit diagonal, and what that repair does and does not guarantee.
The project ladder
The module ends in the covariance-matrix doctor. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the steps that the placement skill checks test. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.
Worked example
A fully solved and annotated run of the reference repair on one SIMULATED covariance matrix, with the reason for every step: centring, forming the matrix, the eigenvalues, the positive-semidefinite check, the clipping and the rescaling.
Completion problem
The same run with the rescaling step and the positive-semidefinite check removed. Graded instantly in your browser.
Independent project
Implement covariance_doctor.py with the functions min_eigenvalue, is_psd, clip_repair, projection_repair and comparison_weights, then write the comparison report of the repairs. Graded by the autograder against generated instances and by the rubric. Project page.
Stretch
An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a repair that measures distance with a weighted norm, suitable for your portfolio.
Data status
No real data is embedded in M3, and no lab in M3 reads a data file. Lessons 1 and 2 and Lessons 4 to 9 draw their inputs from a seeded generator or fix them in the code, and Lesson 3 writes its matrices out by hand. Each lab says which of its inputs are generated. Lesson 8's lab runs on a SIMULATED six-tenor yield-change panel (0.25 to 10 years), labelled SIMULATED on the page that uses it. The curriculum's own lab entry for this module asks for the U.S. Treasury curve, and the embedded snapshot has only two series (3-month and 10-year), so curvature cannot be read from it. Lesson 8 therefore does not claim a curvature result for real yields. The course has not embedded a cleared multi-tenor yield series; Lesson 8 uses the generated panel. The autograder's instances are SIMULATED and generated from a seed. The project's three worked labs use matrices set by hand, labelled SIMULATED on their pages. This module has no figure that plots real market data.
