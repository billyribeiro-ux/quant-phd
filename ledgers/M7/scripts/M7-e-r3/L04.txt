0012 Dlore
0013 Xrefuted
0014 Each multiplier pair with nonnegative inequality multipliers and a finite dual value gives a lower bound on the optimal value of a constrained minimization, and the dual problem searches for the best such bound. When the best bound equals the primal optimal value, the gap between them is zero. This lesson proves the bound (weak duality), proves the zero gap at a KKT point of a problem with convex, differentiable objective and inequality constraints and affine equality constraints, and checks the dual of a two-asset problem in exact arithmetic in Lab 1. A zero gap under the further condition of Section 3 is not proved here.
0015 1The dual function and weak duality
0016 Use the problem (P), its feasible set \(F\), its optimal value \(p^\ast\) and its Lagrangian \(L\), as defined in Lesson 3, Definition 1.1 and Definition 1.2. Assume throughout that \(F\) is non-empty.
0017 Definition 1.1 (the dual function and the dual problem)
0018 For \(\lambda \in \R^m\) and \(\nu \in \R^p\), the dual function is \(g(\lambda, \nu) = \inf_{x \in \R^n} L(x, \lambda, \nu)\), a number or \(-\infty\). A pair \((\lambda, \nu)\) is dual feasible when \(\lambda_i \ge 0\) for every \(i\) and \(g(\lambda, \nu)\) is finite. The Lagrangian duality of (P) is the problem of maximizing \(g(\lambda, \nu)\) over dual feasible pairs. Its optimal value is \(d^\ast\). The problem (P) is the primal problem, and \(p^\ast\) is its optimal value.
0019 Proposition 1.2 (the dual function is concave)
0020 For fixed \(x\), the map \((\lambda, \nu) \mapsto L(x, \lambda, \nu)\) is affine. Hence \(g\) is concave on the set of \((\lambda, \nu)\) where it is finite.
0021 Proof
0022 Affineness holds because \(L(x, \lambda, \nu) = f(x) + \sum_i \lambda_i g_i(x) + \sum_j \nu_j h_j(x)\) is a constant plus a linear function of \((\lambda, \nu)\) for fixed \(x\). Let \(a = (\lambda_a, \nu_a)\) and \(b = (\lambda_b, \nu_b)\) be points where \(g\) is finite, and let \(\theta \in [0, 1]\). For every \(x\), affineness gives \(L(x, \theta a + (1 - \theta) b) = \theta L(x, a) + (1 - \theta) L(x, b)\). The infimum of a sum is at least the sum of the infima, so \[\begin{aligned} g(\theta a + (1 - \theta) b) &= \inf_x \big[\theta L(x, a) + (1 - \theta) L(x, b)\big] \\ &\ge \theta g(a) + (1 - \theta) g(b). \end{aligned}\] This is the concavity inequality. \(\blacksquare\)
0023 Proposition 1.3 (weak duality)
0024 For every dual feasible \((\lambda, \nu)\), \(g(\lambda, \nu) \le p^\ast\). Hence \(d^\ast \le p^\ast\). This needs no convexity assumption.
0025 Proof
0026 Let \(x \in F\). Since \(\lambda_i \ge 0\) and \(g_i(x) \le 0\), each term \(\lambda_i g_i(x)\) is at most zero. Since \(h_j(x) = 0\), the equality terms vanish. Hence \[\begin{aligned} g(\lambda, \nu) &\le L(x, \lambda, \nu) = f(x) + \sum_i \lambda_i g_i(x) + \sum_j \nu_j h_j(x) \\ &\le f(x). \end{aligned}\] The first inequality is the definition of the infimum. Since this holds for every \(x \in F\), \(g(\lambda, \nu) \le p^\ast\). Taking the supremum over dual feasible \((\lambda, \nu)\) gives \(d^\ast \le p^\ast\). \(\blacksquare\)
0027 Proposition 1.4 (a grid bounds the duality gap)
0028 Let \(\tilde F \subseteq F\) and let \(\tilde D\) be a set of dual feasible pairs, both finite and non-empty. Then \[\begin{aligned} \min_{x \in \tilde F} f(x) - \max_{(\lambda, \nu) \in \tilde D} g(\lambda, \nu) &\ge p^\ast - d^\ast \\ &\ge 0. \end{aligned}\] In particular, if the left side is zero, then \(p^\ast = d^\ast\).
0029 Proof
0030 The minimum over \(\tilde F\) is at least \(p^\ast\), because \(\tilde F \subseteq F\). The maximum over \(\tilde D\) is at most \(d^\ast\), because each point of \(\tilde D\) is dual feasible. Subtracting, the left side is at least \(p^\ast - d^\ast\). The last inequality is Proposition 1.3. If the left side is zero, then \(0 \ge p^\ast - d^\ast \ge 0\), so \(p^\ast = d^\ast\). \(\blacksquare\)
0031 Remark 1.5 (the grid is an inner set on both sides)
0032 A grid of primal points gives an upper bound on \(p^\ast\), and a grid of dual points gives a lower bound on \(d^\ast\). The grid gap is the smallest value of \(f\) on the primal grid minus the largest value of \(g\) on the dual grid. It is therefore an upper bound on the true gap. A grid can miss the dual optimum, and then its gap can be positive even when the true gap is zero. Problem R3 gives a case. The printed grid gap in Lab 1 is zero, so Proposition 1.4 settles the true gap there.
0033 2A zero gap from a KKT point
0034 Proposition 2.1 (a KKT point gives zero gap)
0035 Assume that \(f\) and each \(g_i\) are convex and differentiable on \(\R^n\), and that each \(h_j\) is affine. Let \((\hat{x}, (\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast))\) be a KKT point of (P). Then \(g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = f(\hat{x})\), and \(p^\ast = d^\ast = f(\hat{x})\). The dual optimum is attained at \((\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast)\), and the primal optimum at \(\hat{x}\).
0036 Proof
0037 Let \(\varphi(x) = L(x, \lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast)\). It is convex and differentiable: \(f\) is convex by hypothesis, each \(\lambda_i^\ast g_i\) is convex because \(\lambda_i^\ast \ge 0\) by (K3) and \(g_i\) is convex, and each \(\nu_j^\ast h_j\) is affine. This is the argument of the proof of Theorem 2.1 of Lesson 3. By (K1), \(\nabla \varphi(\hat{x}) = 0\). Lemma 1.4 of Lesson 3 then gives that \(\hat{x}\) minimizes \(\varphi\) over \(\R^n\). So \[\begin{aligned} g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) &= \inf_x \varphi(x) = \varphi(\hat{x}) \\ &= f(\hat{x}) + \sum_i \lambda_i^\ast g_i(\hat{x}) + \sum_j \nu_j^\ast h_j(\hat{x}) = f(\hat{x}). \end{aligned}\] where the last equality uses (K4) and (K2). Next, \((\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast)\) is dual feasible, so \(d^\ast \ge g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = f(\hat{x})\). By Theorem 2.1 of Lesson 3, \(\hat{x}\) is optimal, so \(p^\ast = f(\hat{x})\). Thus \(d^\ast \ge p^\ast\). Weak duality, Proposition 1.3, gives \(d^\ast \le p^\ast\). Hence \(d^\ast = p^\ast = f(\hat{x})\), and both optima are attained. \(\blacksquare\)
0038 Example 2.2 (the dual of the active instance)
0039 Take the active instance of Lesson 3, Example 4.1: \(\mu = (\tfrac32, \tfrac15)\), \(\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\), and the constraints \(g_i(w) = -w_i\), \(h(w) = \mathbf{1}^{\top} w - 1\). Expanding the Lagrangian gives \[\begin{aligned} L(w, \lambda, \nu) &= \tfrac12 w^{\top}\Sigma w - r^{\top} w - \nu, \\ r &= \mu + \lambda - \nu \mathbf{1}. \end{aligned}\] The matrix \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, since \((a, b)\Sigma(a, b)^{\top} = a^2 + b^2 + (a + b)^2\) is zero only at \(a = b = 0\). So the infimum over \(w\) is attained where \(\Sigma w = r\), that is at \(w = \Sigma^{-1} r\). Substituting gives \(g(\lambda, \nu) = -\tfrac12 r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1} r - \nu\). With \(\Sigma^{-1} = \tfrac13 \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\), this is a closed form. At the KKT point \(\lambda^\ast = (0, \tfrac{3}{10})\), \(\nu^\ast = -\tfrac12\), we get \(r = (2, 1)\), \(\Sigma^{-1} r = (1, 0)\), and \(r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1} r = 2\). So \(g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = -1 + \tfrac12 = -\tfrac12\), which equals \(f(1, 0) = -\tfrac12\). Proposition 2.1 gives the zero gap for this instance with no tolerance.

0045 3Slater's condition and strong duality
0046 Definition 3.1 (Slater's condition and a Slater point)
0047 A Slater point of (P) is a point \(\bar x\) that satisfies each inequality strictly, \(g_i(\bar x) < 0\), and each equality exactly, \(h_j(\bar x) = 0\). Slater's condition is the statement that a Slater point exists. It is the hypothesis of the converse direction of the KKT conditions (existence of multipliers at an optimum), which Lesson 3 says it does not treat and which this lesson does not use.
0048 Strong duality means the zero gap \(d^\ast = p^\ast\). Strong duality under Slater's condition is not stated as a numbered result on this page, and no result here depends on it. The zero gap for the active instance comes from Proposition 2.1, which does not use Slater's condition.
0049 Remark 3.3 (the instance satisfies Slater's condition)
0050 For the active instance, \(\bar w = (\tfrac12, \tfrac12)\) is a Slater point. It satisfies \(\bar w_1 > 0\) and \(\bar w_2 > 0\), so \(g_1(\bar w) = -\tfrac12 < 0\) and \(g_2(\bar w) = -\tfrac12 < 0\), and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bar w = 1\). Proposition 2.1 gives the zero gap with a complete proof, and it does not need this Slater point.
0051 Example 3.4 (zero gap, with no Slater point and no dual maximum)
0052 Take \(n = 1\), \(f(x) = x\), and \(g_1(x) = x^2\), with no equality constraints. As in Lesson 3, Example 3.1, \(F = \{0\}\), so \(p^\ast = 0\). Slater's condition fails. The Lagrangian is \(L(x, \lambda) = x + \lambda x^2\). For \(\lambda = 0\), \(L\) is linear in \(x\) and unbounded below, so \(g(0) = -\infty\). For \(\lambda > 0\), \(L\) is minimized at \(x = -1/(2\lambda)\), where it equals \(-\tfrac{1}{2\lambda} + \tfrac{1}{4\lambda} = -\tfrac{1}{4\lambda}\). So \(g(\lambda) = -\tfrac{1}{4\lambda}\), which is negative for every finite \(\lambda > 0\) and tends to \(0\) as \(\lambda \to \infty\). Hence \(d^\ast = \sup_{\lambda \ge 0} g(\lambda) = 0 = p^\ast\). The gap is zero. The supremum is not attained, so the dual maximum does not exist. The example shows that a zero gap can hold when Slater's condition fails, and that the dual maximum need not be attained in that case.
0053 4Where the canon breaks
0054 The lesson has four limits, and each is shown on the page.
0055 Weak duality is a bound, not an equality. Proposition 1.3 holds with no convexity. Equality needs a separate argument, such as Proposition 2.1.
0056 The grid can mislead in one direction. A grid of dual points can miss the dual optimum, and then its gap can be positive although the true gap is zero. Problem R3 gives the numbers. A zero grid gap, in contrast, settles the gap by Proposition 1.4.
0057 Strong duality under Slater's condition is not proved here. The page proves zero gap at a KKT point (Proposition 2.1) and in Example 3.4, and it makes no general claim about when strong duality holds.
0058 The closed form for the dual is special to this instance. The formula \(g(\lambda, \nu) = -\tfrac12 r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1} r - \nu\) holds because the Lagrangian is a quadratic in \(w\) with positive definite \(\Sigma\), so its infimum is at \(\Sigma^{-1} r\). The page makes no claim about the dual of a general problem.
0059 5Misconceptions
0060 “Weak duality shows that the primal and dual optimal values are equal.”
0061 Weak duality gives only \(d^\ast \le p^\ast\). The dual value at \(\lambda = 0\), \(\nu = 0\) in Lab 1 is \(-\tfrac{199}{300}\), which is below \(p^\ast = -\tfrac12\) by \(\tfrac{49}{300}\). A dual value strictly below the primal optimum is allowed by Proposition 1.3, and it is not a statement that the bound is tight. Equality needs an argument such as Proposition 2.1.
0062 “The dual problem minimizes the dual function.”
0063 Each dual feasible value \(g(\lambda, \nu)\) is a lower bound on \(p^\ast\), by Proposition 1.3. The best lower bound is the largest one, so the dual problem maximizes \(g\). Lab 1 shows the largest dual value on its grid, \(-\tfrac12\), and the smaller value \(-\tfrac{199}{300}\) at the origin.
0064 “A grid gap is only a tolerance check, so a zero grid gap says nothing about the true gap.”
0065 Proposition 1.4 says the grid gap is at least \(p^\ast - d^\ast\), and weak duality says \(p^\ast - d^\ast\) is at least zero. A grid gap of zero therefore forces the true gap to be zero. The implication uses only Propositions 1.3 and 1.4. Exact arithmetic makes the printed zero exact, so no tolerance is needed. Lab 1 prints a grid gap of 0.

0068 Mechanics
0069 M1 · The dual of a one-variable problem
0070 Find the dual function, the dual optimum and the primal optimum for minimizing \(x^2\) subject to \(1 - x \le 0\).
0071 Solution
0072 The Lagrangian is \(L(x, \lambda) = x^2 + \lambda(1 - x)\). Minimizing over \(x\), \(2x - \lambda = 0\) gives \(x = \lambda/2\). Substituting, \(g(\lambda) = \tfrac{\lambda^2}{4} + \lambda - \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2} = \lambda - \tfrac{\lambda^2}{4}\). Maximizing over \(\lambda \ge 0\), \(1 - \tfrac{\lambda}{2} = 0\) gives \(\lambda = 2\), with \(g(2) = 1\). The primal optimum is \(x = 1\), with value \(1\), so the gap is zero. The multiplier \(\lambda = 2\) and \(x = 1\) form a KKT point, as Proposition 2.1 predicts.
0073 M2 · The dual value at an intermediate multiplier
0074 For the problem of M1, compute \(g(1)\) and the gap that \(g(1)\) leaves against \(p^\ast = 1\).
0075 Solution
0076 From M1, \(g(1) = 1 - \tfrac14 = \tfrac34\). The gap that this multiplier leaves is \(1 - \tfrac34 = \tfrac14\), which is positive because \(\lambda = 1\) is not the dual optimum. The value \(\tfrac34\) is a lower bound on \(p^\ast\), as Proposition 1.3 requires.
0077 M3 · The dual value at the origin for the two-asset instance
0078 Show by hand that \(g(0, 0) = -\tfrac{199}{300}\) for the active instance.
0079 Solution
0080 At \(\lambda = 0\) and \(\nu = 0\), \(r = \mu = (\tfrac32, \tfrac15)\). We have \(\Sigma^{-1}\mu = \tfrac13 (2 \cdot \tfrac32 - \tfrac15, -\tfrac32 + 2 \cdot \tfrac15) = \tfrac13(\tfrac{14}{5}, -\tfrac{11}{10}) = (\tfrac{14}{15}, -\tfrac{11}{30})\). Then \(\mu^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mu = \tfrac32 \cdot \tfrac{14}{15} - \tfrac15 \cdot \tfrac{11}{30} = \tfrac{210}{150} - \tfrac{11}{150} = \tfrac{199}{150}\). So \(g(0, 0) = -\tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac{199}{150} = -\tfrac{199}{300}\), which is the value Lab 1 prints.
0081 Proof
0082 P1 · Weak duality for the one-variable problem
0083 For the problem of M1, prove \(g(\lambda) \le 1\) for every \(\lambda \ge 0\) directly from the formula for \(g\), without using the zero gap.
0084 Solution
0085 We have \(1 - g(\lambda) = 1 - \lambda + \tfrac{\lambda^2}{4} = \left(1 - \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^2 \ge 0\), by expanding the square. So \(g(\lambda) \le 1 = p^\ast\), which is the weak duality bound, with equality exactly at \(\lambda = 2\).
0086 P2 · Proof of the grid bound
0087 Prove Proposition 1.4, and say which step uses finiteness of \(\tilde F\) and \(\tilde D\).
0088 Solution
0089 Since \(\tilde F\) is finite and non-empty, its minimum of \(f\) exists and is attained at some point \(\tilde x\) of \(\tilde F\). Since \(\tilde F \subseteq F\), \(f(\tilde x) \ge p^\ast\). Since \(\tilde D\) is finite and non-empty, its maximum of \(g\) is attained at some dual feasible pair, and that value is at most \(d^\ast\). Subtracting gives the inequality. Finiteness is used to say that the minimum and maximum exist, so the left side is a number. Without finiteness the same bounds hold for the infimum and supremum.
0090 P3 · The dual value at the KKT point
0091 Let \((\hat{x}, (\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast))\) be a KKT point with convex data. Prove \(g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = f(\hat{x})\), using Lemma 1.4 of Lesson 3.
0092 Solution
0093 The function \(\varphi(x) = L(x, \lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast)\) is convex and differentiable, because \(\lambda^\ast \ge 0\) and the \(g_i\) are convex, while the \(h_j\) are affine. Its gradient at \(\hat{x}\) is zero by (K1). By Lemma 1.4 of Lesson 3, \(\varphi(x) \ge \varphi(\hat{x})\) for every \(x\), so \(g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = \varphi(\hat{x})\). By (K4) and (K2), \(\varphi(\hat{x}) = f(\hat{x})\). Hence \(g(\lambda^\ast, \nu^\ast) = f(\hat{x})\).

0103 E3 · Why the printed zero grid gap is a proof (formula input)
0104 Lab 1 prints a grid duality gap of 0. Explain in one paragraph why this settles the true gap, citing the proposition that gives the argument.
0105 Solution
0106 The printed gap is the smallest primal objective on the grid minus the largest dual value on the grid. Proposition 1.4 says this quantity is at least \(p^\ast - d^\ast\). Weak duality, Proposition 1.3, says \(p^\ast - d^\ast \ge 0\). The printed value is exactly zero, so \(0 \ge p^\ast - d^\ast \ge 0\), and the true gap is zero. The lab's arithmetic is exact, so no rounding enters this argument.

0110 Solution

0112 R2 · Attainment in Example 3.4
0113 In Example 3.4, the dual supremum is zero and not attained. Explain why Proposition 2.1 cannot be applied to that example to produce a dual optimum.
0114 Solution
0115 Proposition 2.1 needs a KKT point. In Example 3.4, \(x = 0\) is the only feasible point. Its stationarity condition reads \(1 + 2\lambda \cdot 0 = 1 \neq 0\) for every \(\lambda\), so no KKT point exists and Proposition 2.1 has no point to use. The page does not claim a general attainment result.
0116 R3 · A grid that misses the dual optimum
0117 Restrict the dual grid of Lab 1 to \(\lambda = (0, 0)\) and \(\nu\) in steps of \(\tfrac{1}{10}\) on \([-1, 0]\), and keep the primal grid. Show that the grid gap is \(\tfrac{7}{300}\), although the true gap is zero.
0118 Solution
0119 With \(\lambda = 0\), \(r = (\tfrac32 - \nu, \tfrac15 - \nu)\). The value is \(g(0, \nu) = -\tfrac13(a^2 - ab + b^2) - \nu\), where \(a = \tfrac32 - \nu\) and \(b = \tfrac15 - \nu\). Evaluating at the grid points \(\nu = -\tfrac35\) and \(\nu = -\tfrac{7}{10}\), each gives \(-\tfrac{157}{300}\). The continuous maximum over \(\nu\) is at \(\nu = -\tfrac{13}{20}\), with value \(-\tfrac{209}{400}\), but \(-\tfrac{13}{20}\) is not on the grid. Since \(g(0, \nu)\) is concave in \(\nu\) by Proposition 1.2, the two neighbours of \(-\tfrac{13}{20}\) on the grid give the largest grid value, which is \(-\tfrac{157}{300}\), and the smallest primal value is \(-\tfrac12\), at \(w = (1, 0)\), which is on the primal grid. The grid gap is \(-\tfrac12 + \tfrac{157}{300} = \tfrac{7}{300}\), which is positive, while the true gap is zero by Proposition 2.1. This is the case Remark 1.5 describes.
