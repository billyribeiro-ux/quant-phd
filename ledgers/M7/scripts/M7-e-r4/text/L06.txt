0020 The mean-variance problem of this lesson is a quadratic program: a quadratic risk term, a linear reward for return, and linear constraints. This lesson writes the problem in that form, solves its equality-constrained case in closed form, and proves how its solution moves with the parameter \(\gamma\) of Definition 1.1, which weights the risk term against return. It then adds linear constraints (Section 3). One of them limits total trading, a sum of absolute values; Section 3 writes it as linear inequalities (Proposition 3.4). Lab 1 computes a frontier on simulated inputs with and without a nonnegativity constraint. Its numbers describe that one sample only.
0021 1The mean-variance objective
0022 Let \(n\) assets have one-period returns. Take as given a vector \(\mu \in \R^{n}\) of returns for the period, and a symmetric positive definite \(n \times n\) matrix \(\Sigma\). Both are inputs, and this unit does not estimate them: estimating a covariance matrix is outside M7. A weight vector \(w\) lists the fraction of wealth in each asset, and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) means the weights sum to one.
0023 Definition 1.1 (the mean-variance objective)
0024 For a risk-aversion parameter \(\gamma > 0\), the objective is \(f_{\gamma}(w) = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\,w^{\top}\Sigma w - \mu^{\top}w\). The first term is \(\gamma/2\) times the quadratic form \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\), which this unit calls the risk term and does not identify with any risk measure. The second term is the return \(\mu^{\top}w\). The parameter \(\gamma\) sets their relative weight.
0025 Definition 1.2 (mean-variance optimization)
0026 The mean-variance optimization problem at \(\gamma\) is \(\min f_{\gamma}(w)\) over the feasible set \(\{w : \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\). It is the quadratic program of L05 with \(Q = \gamma\Sigma\), \(c = -\mu\), \(A = \mathbf{1}^{\top}\) and \(b = 1\). Lab 1 adds the constraint \(w \ge 0\) in its second case.
0027 Theorem 1.3 (the minimiser in closed form)
0028 Let \(\gamma > 0\) and let \(\Sigma\) be symmetric positive definite, as in Section 1. The mean-variance problem with \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) has a unique minimiser \(w(\gamma)\). With \(y = \Sigma^{-1}\mu\) and \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}\), it is
0029 \[w(\gamma) = \frac{y - \lambda z}{\gamma}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}y - \gamma}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}z}, \]
0030 and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\). The multiplier \(\lambda\) is the one in the KKT system of L05, with the sign used there.
0031 Proof
0032 The matrix \(\gamma\Sigma\) is positive definite, because \(\Sigma\) is and \(\gamma > 0\). The constraint matrix \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\) has full row rank, since it is a nonzero row. So Theorem 2.3 of L05 gives a unique minimiser, and it is the solution of \(\gamma\Sigma w + \lambda\mathbf{1} = \mu\), \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\). The first equation gives \(w = (\Sigma^{-1}\mu - \lambda\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1})/\gamma = (y - \lambda z)/\gamma\). Multiplying by \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\) and using \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) gives \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}y - \lambda\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = \gamma\), which is the formula for \(\lambda\). For positivity, \(z\) is not zero, since \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1} \ne 0\). Then \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = z^{\top}\Sigma z > 0\), using \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1}\) and the positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\).
0033 Remark 1.4 (positive definiteness of the simulated Sigma)
0034 Lab 1 constructs \(\Sigma = M^{\top}M/16 + I/10\) for an integer matrix \(M\). For any \(x\), \(x^{\top}\Sigma x = \|Mx\|^{2}/16 + \|x\|^{2}/10\), which is positive unless \(x = 0\). So the matrix is positive definite, and Theorem 1.3 applies to it.
0035 2How the solution moves with risk aversion
0036 Call the points \(\bigl(w(\gamma)^{\top}\Sigma w(\gamma),\; \mu^{\top}w(\gamma)\bigr)\), for \(\gamma > 0\), the trade-off curve of the problem. The lab's title calls this curve the efficient frontier, in an informal sense. This lesson does not prove that the curve traced by \(\gamma\) is the whole efficient set.
0037 Proposition 2.1 (the risk term and the return do not rise as \(\gamma\) rises)
0038 Let \(F'\) be any set of weight vectors, and for \(\gamma > 0\) let \(w(\gamma)\) minimise \(f_{\gamma}\) over \(F'\). Write \(V(\gamma) = w(\gamma)^{\top}\Sigma w(\gamma)\) and \(R(\gamma) = \mu^{\top}w(\gamma)\). If \(0 < \gamma_{2} < \gamma_{1}\), then \(V(\gamma_{1}) \le V(\gamma_{2})\) and \(R(\gamma_{1}) \le R(\gamma_{2})\).
0039 Proof
0040 Write \(w_{1} = w(\gamma_{1})\) and \(w_{2} = w(\gamma_{2})\). Both lie in \(F'\), so minimality of each against the other gives
0041 \[\tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}V(\gamma_{1}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}V(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{2}), \qquad \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}V(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{2}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}V(\gamma_{1}) - R(\gamma_{1}).\]
0042 Adding the two inequalities, the \(R\) terms cancel, leaving \(\tfrac{\gamma_{1} - \gamma_{2}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{1}) - V(\gamma_{2})\big) \le 0\). Since \(\gamma_{1} > \gamma_{2}\), this gives \(V(\gamma_{1}) \le V(\gamma_{2})\). For the return, the second inequality gives \(R(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \ge \tfrac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1})\big)\), and the first gives \(R(\gamma_{2}) - R(\gamma_{1}) \le \tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\big(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1})\big)\). Since \(V(\gamma_{2}) - V(\gamma_{1}) \ge 0\) and \(\gamma_{2} > 0\), the lower bound is nonnegative, so \(R(\gamma_{2}) \ge R(\gamma_{1})\).
0043 Remark 2.2 (the same objective, so the same argument)
0044 Proposition 2.1 needs no property of \(\Sigma\) beyond the definition of \(V\), and no property of \(F'\) beyond the minimisation. So it covers the equality-only problem and the problem with \(w \ge 0\) used in Proposition 2.3 and Part B of Lab 1.
0045 Proposition 2.3 (a constraint cannot lower the optimal objective)
0046 Let \(F_{0} = \{w : \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\) and \(F_{+} = \{w \in F_{0} : w \ge 0\}\). For each \(\gamma > 0\), the infimum of \(f_{\gamma}\) over \(F_{+}\) is at least the infimum over \(F_{0}\).
0047 Proof
0048 \(F_{+} \subseteq F_{0}\), so every point of \(F_{+}\) is a point of \(F_{0}\). The infimum over the larger set is no larger than the infimum over the smaller one.
0049 3Linear portfolio constraints
0050 Definition 3.1 (linear portfolio constraint)
0051 A linear portfolio constraint is an inequality \(a^{\top}w \le \beta\) or an equality \(a^{\top}w = \beta\), with \(a\) and \(\beta\) given. Section 1 and Part A of Lab 1 use the budget constraint alone. Proposition 2.3 and Part B of Lab 1 add the nonnegativity constraint. The sector constraint is defined here and not used on this page. The turnover constraint of Definition 3.2 is written in linear form in Proposition 3.4 and checked in Part D of Lab 1. The budget constraint \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) is an equality. A box constraint \(l_{i} \le w_{i} \le u_{i}\) is two inequalities for each asset. A sector constraint \(\sum_{i \in S_{k}} w_{i} \le c_{k}\) caps the total weight of a set \(S_{k}\) of assets, with \(c_{k}\) given. The nonnegativity constraint \(w \ge 0\) is the box with \(l = 0\) and no upper bound.
0052 Definition 3.2 (turnover)
0053 Let \(w_{0}\) be a given starting portfolio. The turnover of \(w\) relative to \(w_{0}\) is \(\sum_{i} |w_{i} - w_{0,i}|\). The turnover constraint is that this quantity is at most a given \(\tau \ge 0\).
0054 Remark 3.3 (an absolute value is not a linear inequality)
0055 The constraint \(|w_{i} - w_{0,i}| \le s\) is not one linear inequality in \(w\) alone. It is a pair of linear inequalities, \(w_{i} - w_{0,i} \le s\) and \(w_{0,i} - w_{i} \le s\). Proposition 3.4 uses this to write the turnover constraint with auxiliary variables.
0056 Proposition 3.4 (the turnover constraint with auxiliary variables)
0057 Let \(w_{0}\) and \(\tau\) be given real vectors and a real number, with no sign assumption. Then \(\sum_{i}|w_{i} - w_{0,i}| \le \tau\) holds if and only if there is a vector \(t\) with \(t_{i} \ge w_{i} - w_{0,i}\), \(t_{i} \ge w_{0,i} - w_{i}\) for every \(i\), and \(\sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\).
0058 Proof
0059 Put \(d_{i} = w_{i} - w_{0,i}\). If the turnover is at most \(\tau\), take \(t_{i} = |d_{i}|\). Then \(t_{i} = |d_{i}| \ge d_{i}\) and \(t_{i} = |d_{i}| \ge -d_{i}\), and \(\sum_{i}t_{i} = \sum_{i}|d_{i}| \le \tau\). Conversely, let \(t\) satisfy the inequalities. Then \(t_{i} \ge d_{i}\) and \(t_{i} \ge -d_{i}\), so \(t_{i} \ge |d_{i}|\), and \(\sum_{i}|d_{i}| \le \sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\).
0060 Remark 3.5 (the linear form is still a QP)
0061 The variables \((w, t)\) enter linearly in the constraints of Proposition 3.4, and the objective \(f_{\gamma}\) depends on \(w\) only. The problem with the turnover constraint is therefore a quadratic program in \((w, t)\) with a positive semidefinite Hessian on the \(w\) block and zero on the \(t\) block. Lab 1 does not solve this problem. Its Part D checks only the linearization.
0062 Lab 1 · The efficient frontier with and without the short-sale constraint, on simulated inputs
0063 SIMULATED: the four assets A1 to A4, the return vector \(\mu\) and the matrix \(\Sigma\) are generated from a fixed seed by an integer recurrence, and no data are used. Stated before running. Part A solves the mean-variance problem with the budget alone, for \(\gamma\) in 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16, by the KKT system and by the closed form of Theorem 1.3. It predicts an exact match and an exact budget. Part B adds \(w \ge 0\), enumerates the 15 sets of assets fixed at zero that leave at least one asset free, and predicts one passing candidate for each \(\gamma\). Part C predicts that the risk term \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\) does not rise as \(\gamma\) rises in both cases, and that the constrained objective is at least the unconstrained one at each of the five values of \(\gamma\). Part D checks the linearization of Proposition 3.4 at the \(\gamma = 4\) solution of Part B. The return column is expected to fall as \(\gamma\) rises, as Proposition 2.1 requires.

0094 """A linear congruential generator with fixed constants; integer output, so every run is identical."""
0095 def __init__(self, seed: int) -> None:
0096 self.state = seed
0097 def draw(self, modulus: int) -> int:
0098 """The next integer in 0 to modulus minus 1."""

0100 return self.state % modulus
0101 def simulate_inputs() -> tuple[list[list[Fraction]], list[Fraction], list[list[Fraction]]]:
0102 """Return (M, mu, Sigma) generated from SEED: M has integers in -3..3, mu is in 0.02..0.14 per period."""
0103 gen = Lcg(SEED)
0104 m = [[Fraction(gen.draw(7) - 3) for _ in range(N_ASSETS)] for _ in range(N_ASSETS)]

0106 sigma = [
0107 [
0108 sum((m[k][i] * m[k][j] for k in range(N_ASSETS)), Fraction(0)) / 16
0109 + (Fraction(1, 10) if i == j else Fraction(0))
0110 for j in range(N_ASSETS)
0111 ]
0112 for i in range(N_ASSETS)
0113 ]
0114 return m, mu, sigma
0115 def solve(matrix: list[list[Fraction]], rhs: list[Fraction]) -> list[Fraction]:
0116 """Solve matrix x = rhs exactly by Gaussian elimination with row pivoting; raise ValueError if singular."""
0117 n = len(rhs)
0118 rows = [[*row, rhs[i]] for i, row in enumerate(matrix)]
0119 for col in range(n):
0120 pivot = next((r for r in range(col, n) if rows[r][col] != 0), None)
0121 if pivot is None:
0122 raise ValueError("the matrix is singular")
0123 rows[col], rows[pivot] = rows[pivot], rows[col]
0124 for r in range(n):
0125 if r != col and rows[r][col] != 0:
0126 factor = rows[r][col] / rows[col][col]
0127 rows[r] = [a - factor * b for a, b in zip(rows[r], rows[col], strict=True)]
0128 return [rows[i][n] / rows[i][i] for i in range(n)]
0129 def dot(u: list[Fraction], v: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
0130 """The inner product u'v, exact."""
0131 return sum((a * b for a, b in zip(u, v, strict=True)), Fraction(0))
0132 def matvec(a: list[list[Fraction]], v: list[Fraction]) -> list[Fraction]:
0133 """The product a v, exact."""
0134 return [dot(row, v) for row in a]
0135 def submatrix(a: list[list[Fraction]], idx: list[int]) -> list[list[Fraction]]:
0136 """The rows and columns of a with indices in idx."""
0137 return [[a[i][j] for j in idx] for i in idx]
0138 def equality_frontier_point(
0139 sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction, idx: list[int]
0140 ) -> tuple[list[Fraction], Fraction]:
0141 """Minimiser over the assets in idx with budget 1'w = 1, and its multiplier lambda, from the KKT system."""
0142 k = len(idx)
0143 sub_s = submatrix(sigma, idx)
0144 kkt = [[gamma * sub_s[i][j] for j in range(k)] + [Fraction(1)] for i in range(k)]
0145 kkt.append([Fraction(1)] * k + [Fraction(0)])
0146 rhs = [mu[i] for i in idx] + [Fraction(1)]
0147 sol = solve(kkt, rhs)
0148 return sol[:k], sol[k]
0149 def closed_form(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction) -> list[Fraction]:
0150 """The same minimiser from the closed form (y - lambda z)/gamma, with y = Sigma^{-1} mu and z = Sigma^{-1} 1."""
0151 ones = [Fraction(1)] * N_ASSETS
0152 y = solve(sigma, mu)
0153 z = solve(sigma, ones)
0154 lam = (sum(y, Fraction(0)) - gamma) / sum(z, Fraction(0))
0155 return [(y[i] - lam * z[i]) / gamma for i in range(N_ASSETS)]
0156 def risk(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], w: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
0157 """w'Sigma w, exact."""
0158 return dot(w, matvec(sigma, w))
0159 def objective(sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction, w: list[Fraction]) -> Fraction:
0160 """(gamma/2) w'Sigma w - mu'w, exact."""
0161 return gamma / 2 * risk(sigma, w) - dot(mu, w)
0162 def nonnegative_candidates(
0163 sigma: list[list[Fraction]], mu: list[Fraction], gamma: Fraction
0164 ) -> list[tuple[tuple[int, ...], list[Fraction]]]:
0165 """Every active set fixed at zero whose candidate passes primal and dual checks, as (zero set, weights)."""
0166 kept: list[tuple[tuple[int, ...], list[Fraction]]] = []
0167 for mask in product((0, 1), repeat=N_ASSETS):
0168 zeros = [i for i in range(N_ASSETS) if mask[i]]
0169 free = [i for i in range(N_ASSETS) if not mask[i]]
0170 if not free:
0171 continue
0172 w_free, lam = equality_frontier_point(sigma, mu, gamma, free)
0173 w = [Fraction(0)] * N_ASSETS
0174 for pos, value in zip(free, w_free, strict=True):
0175 w[pos] = value
0176 sw = matvec(sigma, w)
0177 nu = [gamma * sw[i] - mu[i] + lam for i in range(N_ASSETS)]
0178 if all(v >= 0 for v in w) and all(nu[i] >= 0 for i in zeros):
0179 kept.append((tuple(zeros), w))
0180 return kept

0275 4Where the canon breaks
0276 The solution depends on the inputs. \(w(\gamma)\) is a function of \(\mu\) and \(\Sigma\), which this unit takes as given. This lesson does not measure how the weights move when the inputs change, and it does not estimate \(\mu\) or \(\Sigma\).
0277 One period, one risk term. The objective uses one period and one quadratic risk term, \(w^{\top}\Sigma w\), which the unit does not identify with any risk measure. Lab 1 uses no costs, and it does not annualize its inputs.
0278 \(\gamma\) is a parameter, not a calibrated quantity. Proposition 2.1 describes how the solution moves with \(\gamma\). It does not say which \(\gamma\) an investor should choose.
0279 Negative weights are a model output. The equality-only solution is a legal point of the stated problem. Whether a short position is allowed is a constraint of the mandate. Misconception 2 shows that at \(\gamma = 1\) the constraint changes all four weights, not only the short one.
0280 The turnover constraint is not used in the solver. Proposition 3.4 makes the constraint linear. Lab 1 checks the linearization, and does not solve a problem with the constraint.
0281 5Misconceptions
0282 “A more risk-averse investor, with a larger \(\gamma\), earns a higher return.”
0283 Proposition 2.1 says the return \(R(\gamma)\) does not rise as \(\gamma\) rises. A larger \(\gamma\) puts more weight on the risk term and less on \(\mu^{\top}w\). In Lab 1 the return column of Part A falls from 0.113798 at \(\gamma = 1\) to 0.106241 at \(\gamma = 16\).
0284 “Adding \(w \ge 0\) changes only the weight of the asset that was short.”
0285 In Lab 1 at \(\gamma = 1\) the constraint changes all four weights. The first asset moves from 0.434783 to 0.388425, the second from 0.526836 to 0.203679, the third from 0.562535 to 0.407895, and the fourth from \(-0.524154\) to 0.000000. The constrained problem is solved jointly. The weights of A1 to A3 all change, and in the constrained solution none of them is zero.
0286 “The turnover constraint cannot be written in a quadratic program, because it contains an absolute value.”
0287 Proposition 3.4 writes it with auxiliary variables \(t_{i}\) as linear inequalities plus \(\sum_{i}t_{i} \le \tau\). Part D of Lab 1 checks the linearization at a solution. It prints True for the two linearization checks and 20 of 20 for the perturbation check.

0290 Mechanics
0291 M1 · A two-asset closed form
0292 Let \(\Sigma = 2I\), \(\mu = (1/10, 1/20)\), and \(\gamma = 1\). Find \(w(\gamma)\) from Theorem 1.3.
0293 Solution
0294 Here \(\Sigma^{-1} = I/2\), so \(y = \Sigma^{-1}\mu = (1/20, 1/40)\) and \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1} = (1/2, 1/2)\). Then \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}y = 3/40\) and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = 1\), so \(\lambda = (3/40 - 1)/1 = -37/40 = -0.925\). The weights are \(w = y - \lambda z = (1/20 + 0.4625, 1/40 + 0.4625) = (0.5125, 0.4875)\). They sum to one, and \(2w_{1} - 0.1 + \lambda = 1.025 - 1.025 = 0\), which is the first stationarity equation.
0295 M2 · The objective at an equal-weight portfolio
0296 With the data of M1 and \(\gamma = 1\), compute \(f_{1}(w)\) at \(w = (1/2, 1/2)\).
0297 Solution
0298 The risk term is \(w^{\top}\Sigma w = 2(1/4 + 1/4) = 1\). The return is \(\mu^{\top}w = (1/10)(1/2) + (1/20)(1/2) = 3/40\). So \(f_{1}(w) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 - 3/40 = 17/40 = 0.425\).
0299 M3 · A single-asset turnover limit
0300 Write \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le 0.1\) with auxiliary variables, as in Proposition 3.4.
0301 Solution
0302 Take \(w_{0,1} = 0.3\) and \(\tau = 0.1\). The linear form is \(t \ge w_{1} - 0.3\), \(t \ge 0.3 - w_{1}\), and \(t \le 0.1\). By Proposition 3.4 a value \(t\) exists exactly when \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le 0.1\). Feasibility requires \(|w_{1} - 0.3| \le t \le 0.1\), which holds exactly when \(w_{1}\) is in \([0.2, 0.4]\).
0303 Proof
0304 P1 · The exact expansion of the mean-variance objective
0305 Show that \(f_{\gamma}(w + d) - f_{\gamma}(w) - (\gamma\Sigma w - \mu)^{\top}d = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}d^{\top}\Sigma d\) for all \(w\) and \(d\).
0306 Solution
0307 This is Lemma 1.3 of L05 with \(Q = \gamma\Sigma\) and \(c = -\mu\). Its gradient is \(\gamma\Sigma w - \mu\), and its quadratic term is \(\tfrac{1}{2}d^{\top}(\gamma\Sigma)d = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}d^{\top}\Sigma d\). Lemma 1.3 gives the identity directly.
0308 P2 · The budget multiplier denominator is positive
0309 Let \(z = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}\). Show that \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\).
0310 Solution
0311 From \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1}\), we get \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = (\Sigma z)^{\top}z = z^{\top}\Sigma z\). The vector \(z\) is not zero, because \(\Sigma z = \mathbf{1} \ne 0\). Since \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, \(z^{\top}\Sigma z > 0\). So \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z > 0\).
0312 P3 · Closed form with the identity matrix
0313 Let \(\Sigma = I\) on \(\R^{n}\). Show that the minimiser of \(f_{\gamma}\) with \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\) is \(w = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\).
0314 Solution
0315 Here \(y = \mu\) and \(z = \mathbf{1}\), so \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}z = n\) and \(\lambda = (\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu - \gamma)/n\). Then \(w = (\mu - \lambda\mathbf{1})/\gamma = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu - \gamma}{n\gamma}\mathbf{1}\). The second term equals \(\tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\), which is the stated form.

0329 Research
0330 R1 · The large-risk-aversion limit with \(\Sigma\) equal to the identity
0331 With \(\Sigma = I\), show that \(w(\gamma) \to \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\) as \(\gamma \to \infty\).
0332 Solution
0333 By P3, \(w(\gamma) = \mu/\gamma - \tfrac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma - 1}{n}\mathbf{1}\). Each term has a limit as \(\gamma \to \infty\): \(\mu/\gamma \to 0\), and \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mu/\gamma \to 0\), so the second term tends to \(\tfrac{-1}{n}\cdot(-1)\mathbf{1} = \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\). The limit is the equal-weight portfolio. With \(\mu = 0\) in P3, the same formula gives \(w = \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\), the unique minimiser of \(\tfrac12 w^{\top}w\) under the budget.
0334 R2 · Nonnegative weights as a projection
0335 With \(\Sigma = I\), \(\mu = (0.1, 0.02)\), \(\gamma = 1\), and \(w \ge 0\), find the minimiser.
0336 Solution
0337 Complete the square: \(f_{\gamma}(w) = \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|w - \mu/\gamma\|^{2} - \|\mu\|^{2}/(2\gamma)\). So the minimiser over \(\{w \ge 0, \mathbf{1}^{\top}w = 1\}\) is the point of that set nearest to \(v = \mu/\gamma = (0.1, 0.02)\). Among points with the budget, the nearest to \(v\) is \(v + t\mathbf{1}\) with \(t = (1 - \mathbf{1}^{\top}v)/2 = 0.44\), which is \((0.54, 0.46)\). Both coordinates are nonnegative, so this point is the minimiser.
0338 R3 · The turnover region for two assets
0339 Let \(w_{0} = (1/2, 1/2)\) and \(\tau = 0.2\). With \(w_{1} + w_{2} = 1\), find the set of \(w_{1}\) that satisfies the turnover constraint.
0340 Solution
0341 With the budget, \(w_{2} - 1/2 = 1/2 - w_{1}\), so the turnover is \(|w_{1} - 1/2| + |1/2 - w_{1}| = 2|w_{1} - 1/2|\). The constraint is \(2|w_{1} - 1/2| \le 0.2\), that is \(|w_{1} - 1/2| \le 0.1\). So \(w_{1}\) lies in \([0.4, 0.6]\).
