E5-c001 L01 None | What is gross domestic product (GDP) in this lesson? | The market value of all final goods and services produced within the economy in the period. A good is final when it is sold to its last user in the period and is not used up as an input to another good in that period (Definition 1.1). | None

E5-c002 L01 None | What are the national accounts? | The system of GDP definitions, applied so that each transaction in the period is counted once (Definition 1.1). | None

E5-c003 L01 None | How are net exports defined? | NX = X - M, where X is exports and M is imports, all in one period and one currency. Consumption, investment, government purchases, exports and imports are the expenditure components (Definition 1.2). | None

E5-c004 L01 T | State the expenditure identity and the hypothesis it needs. | Under Assumption 1.3 (C, I and G are total final purchases, every import is contained in C + I + G and subtracted once, through M, and X is the total value of exports), Y = C + I + G + NX (Theorem 1.4). The identity is a property of the definitions and says nothing about the size of any component. | Theorem 1.4 (proof on page)

E5-c005 L01 None | What is the operating surplus on the income side? | The balancing entry: expenditure-side GDP minus compensation of employees, taxes less subsidies on production, and consumption of fixed capital (Definition 1.5). The income identity therefore holds by construction and is a check on the arithmetic of a table, not an economic result. | None

E5-c006 L01 None | What is nominal GDP in period t? | The sum over goods of p(i,t) times q(i,t), valued at the prices of that period (Definition 1.6). | None

E5-c007 L01 None | What is real GDP at base period 0? | The sum over goods of p(i,0) times q(i,t), which holds prices at their base-period values (Definition 1.6). The lesson uses the deflator form of real GDP in Proposition 2.6 and does not use this base-price form. | None

E5-c008 L01 None | What is a deflator? | A price index D(t) > 0 with D(0) = 1. Real GDP can be defined as nominal GDP divided by the deflator (Definition 1.6). | None

E5-c009 L01 T | Under what hypotheses does closed-economy saving equal investment? | (A0) Assumption 1.3 holds. (A1) The economy is closed, so X = M = 0 and NX = 0. (A2) Taxes net of transfers are Tax, private saving is S_p = Y - Tax - C, and public saving is S_g = Tax - G. Then S_p + S_g = I (Proposition 1.7). The tax cancels, so the result does not depend on its size. | Proposition 1.7 (proof on page)

E5-c010 L01 None | What is the Laspeyres price index of period 1 on base period 0? | The sum of p1 times q0 over the sum of p0 times q0. It fixes the quantities at their base-period values (Definition 2.1). | None

E5-c011 L01 None | What is the Paasche price index of period 1 on base period 0? | The sum of p1 times q1 over the sum of p0 times q1. It uses the period-1 quantities (Definition 2.1). | None

E5-c012 L01 None | What is measured inflation between periods t-1 and t? | The rate of change of a positive price index. Its simple form is P(t)/P(t-1) - 1, a fraction per period, and its log form is ln(P(t)/P(t-1)). Every inflation figure states which form it uses and the period it covers (Definition 2.2). | None

E5-c013 L01 None | What is a chain-linked index? | The product of the link ratios P(t)/P(t-1) across successive periods, so the index at horizon H relative to period 0 is the product for t = 1 to H. When all the links come from one sequence, the product telescopes to P(H)/P(0) (Definition 2.3). | None

E5-c024 L02 None | What is the Cobb-Douglas production function in this lesson? | Y = A K^alpha L^(1 - alpha), where A is a positive productivity level, K and L are positive capital and labour inputs, and 0 < alpha < 1 is the exponent on capital (Definition 1.1). The lesson takes alpha as an input and derives no value for it. | None

E5-c025 L02 None | What are log growth and simple growth of a positive series? | Log growth in period t is ln x(t) - ln x(t-1). Simple growth is x(t)/x(t-1) - 1 (Definition 1.2). The decomposition in Theorem 1.4 uses log growth. | None

E5-c026 L02 T | State the exact log-growth decomposition of Cobb-Douglas output. | Under Assumption 1.3 (a Cobb-Douglas economy over periods 0 to H with the same alpha, 0 < alpha < 1, in every period), Δln Y(t) = Δln A(t) + alpha Δln K(t) + (1 - alpha) Δln L(t) for every t from 1 to H (Theorem 1.4). | Theorem 1.4 (proof on page)

E5-c027 L02 T | What does constant returns to scale mean for the Cobb-Douglas function? | Under (A1) A > 0, K > 0, L > 0, 0 < alpha < 1 and lambda > 0, A(lambda K)^alpha (lambda L)^(1 - alpha) = lambda A K^alpha L^(1 - alpha). Doubling both inputs doubles output (Proposition 1.5). | Proposition 1.5 (proof on page)

E5-c028 L02 None | What does growth accounting compute? | From measured Y, K and L and a chosen capital share alpha in (0, 1): the capital contribution alpha Δln K(t), the labour contribution (1 - alpha) Δln L(t), and the residual Res(t) = Δln Y(t) - alpha Δln K(t) - (1 - alpha) Δln L(t) (Definition 2.1). | None

E5-c029 L02 None | What is the Solow residual? | The residual of Definition 2.1. The name records how the quantity is computed, and the lesson uses it with no attribution to any paper (Definition 2.2). | None

E5-c030 L02 None | What is total factor productivity in this lesson? | The level A of Definition 1.1. Its log growth is the quantity that the residual is meant to measure (Definition 2.2). | None

E5-c031 L02 T | When does the residual equal the growth of technology? | Under Assumption 1.3, the residual computed with the economy's own alpha satisfies Res(t) = ΔlnA(t) for every t from 1 to H (Theorem 2.3). E5-c032 L02 T | How does the residual change when the capital share changes? | For alpha and alpha' in (0, 1), Res(t; alpha) - Res(t; alpha') = -(alpha - alpha')(Δln K(t) - Δln L(t)) for every t. The average residual therefore changes by -(alpha - alpha') times the average of Δln K(t) - Δln L(t) (Proposition 2.4). The decomposition is exact for every alpha. | Proposition 2.4 (proof on page)

E5-c037 L03 None | What is the Solow growth model in this lesson? | The per-worker recursion k(t+1) = g(k(t)) with g(k) = (s k^alpha + (1 - delta) k)/(1 + n), where k(t) = K(t)/L(t) is capital per worker and f(k) = k^alpha is output per worker (Definition 1.1). Technology is fixed at 1 and the saving rate is constant. | None

E5-c038 L03 T | How does the stock accumulation become the per-worker recursion? | Divide K(t+1) = (1 - delta) K(t) + s K(t)^alpha L(t)^(1 - alpha) by L(t+1) = (1 + n) L(t). This gives k(t+1) = (s k(t)^alpha + (1 - delta) k(t))/(1 + n). The step uses only the production function, the labour growth rule and the definitions (Derivation 1.2). | Derivation 1.2 (proof on page)

E5-c039 L03 None | What is a steady state of the recursion? | A number k* > 0 with g(k*) = k* (Definition 2.1). | None

E5-c040 L03 T | Is zero a steady state, and why does A4 exclude it? | Yes. Under A1, g(0) = 0, so zero is a fixed point. Proposition 2.2 concerns positive fixed points only. A4 (k(0) > 0) excludes zero because the recursion keeps capital at zero once capital is zero (Remark 2.3). | Remark 2.3 (arithmetic on the page)

E5-c041 L03 T | Under which hypotheses does the recursion have exactly one positive steady state, and what is it? | Under A1 to A3 (0 < alpha < 1 with technology 1; 0 < s < 1; 0 < delta < 1 and n + delta > 0), the recursion has exactly one positive steady state, k* = (s/(n + delta))^(1/(1 - alpha)) (Proposition 2.2). Every step of the proof is an equivalence. | Proposition 2.2 (proof on page)

E5-c042 L03 T | At the steady state, is output per worker constant? | Under A1 to A3, yes. Output per worker, f(k*) = (k*)^alpha, is constant, because k(t) = k* implies k(t+1) = g(k*) = k* (Remark 2.5 (i), derived from Proposition 2.2). | Remark 2.5 (proof on page); key result on output per worker

E5-c043 L03 T | Does a higher saving rate raise the long-run growth rate of output per worker? | No. A higher s raises the level of the steady state, because the derivative of k* with respect to s is positive (Proposition 4.2, under A1 to A3 and assuming Assumption 4.1). Output per worker at the steady state is constant, so its long-run growth rate is zero for every saving rate in A2 (Remark 2.5). | Proposition 4.2; Remark 2.5 (i)

E5-c044 L03 T | Does aggregate output stop growing at the steady state? | No. Output per worker stops changing, but aggregate output does not.
