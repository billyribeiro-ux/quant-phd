AC4-c082 L07 None | Define transfer pricing. | Transfer pricing is the choice of the price t, in SCU per unit, at which one division of a firm supplies another division of that firm. The transfer price is an internal amount. It moves money between the two divisions and leaves the firm's total gain from the sale unchanged, a fact that Section 3 proves.
AC4-c083 L07 None | What is the opportunity cost per internal unit of an internal order? | D(p_m - v)/q, where D is the number of displaced outside units and each gives up p_m - v. It is the total contribution of the displaced units spread over the q internal units. Internal units that spare capacity absorbs give up nothing (Section 1; Section 2, Lemma 7.2).
AC4-c084 L07 None | How many outside sales does an internal order of q units displace? | D = max(0, q + E - K) units, where E is the units the supplier sells outside and K is its capacity. The order requires q <= K (Section 2).
AC4-c085 L07 None | What is the firm-wide surplus of a transfer? | Sigma = q(r - v) - D(p_m - v), where r is the buyer's net contribution per unit before the transfer price, v is the variable cost per unit, and p_m is the outside market price. Sigma does not contain the transfer price t (Section 3).
AC4-c086 L07 None | What are the two bounds of the feasible transfer-price range? | The supplier's floor t_min = v + D(p_m - v)/q, and the buyer's ceiling t_max = r (Lemma 7.2).
AC4-c087 L07 T | True or false: a transfer price at full cost is fair, so the supplier should accept it. | False. Full cost includes fixed overhead, which does not change with the sale. In the worked case of Section 4, a transfer at 46 SCU, which is 40 variable plus 6 of fixed overhead, gives the supplier a gain of 5,000 x 6 - 60,000 = -30,000 SCU, so the supplier refuses. The full-cost price of 46 is below the floor of 52.
AC4-c088 L07 T | True or false: the transfer price should equal the market price, because the market price is the true value of the good. | False. The market price is the price of the supplier's outside sale, not the buyer's willingness to pay. With v = 40, p_m = 60, K = 10,000, E = 8,000, q = 2,000 and r = 55, spare capacity gives D = 0 and t_min = 40. A transfer at 60 gives the buyer 2,000 x (55 - 60) = -10,000 SCU, so the buyer refuses, although the firm-wide surplus of 30,000 SCU is positive.
AC4-c089 L07 T | True or false: the transfer price changes the firm's profit. | False. The two gains sum to the firm-wide surplus for every price: Delta_S(t) + Delta_B(t) = Sigma (Section 3 and P1; Theorem 7.3, part 3 states it for t in the range). The price moves profit between the two divisions, and the firm's total from the sale is Sigma whichever price is set.
AC4-c090 L07 T | What are the supplier's gain and the buyer's gain at transfer price t? | Delta_S(t) = q(t - v) - D(p_m - v), which is nonnegative exactly when t is at least t_min. Delta_B(t) = q(r - t), which is nonnegative exactly when t is at most t_max = r (Lemma 7.2).
AC4-c091 L07 T | Which transfer prices leave both divisions no worse off? | Exactly the prices t in the interval [t_min, t_max]. On that interval both gains are nonnegative, and outside it at least one gain is negative (Theorem 7.3, part 1).
AC4-c092 L07 T | When is the transfer-price range nonempty? | Exactly when the firm-wide surplus Sigma is at least zero, which is the same as t_min at most t_max (Theorem 7.3, part 2).
AC4-c093 L07 T | Can the lowest transfer price exceed the outside market price? | No. Since q > 0, q <= K, E <= K and p_m >= v, the floor t_min is at most p_m (Problem P2).
AC4-c094 L07 T | In the worked case with supplier capacity K = 10,000, what are the transfer-price range and the firm-wide surplus? | The range is [52, 80] SCU and the surplus is 140,000 SCU (Section 4).

AC4-c097 L08 None | Define residual income for a division. | RI = OI - rC, where OI is the division's operating income for the period in SCU, C is its invested capital, a stated base, and r is the capital-charge rate, an input (Definition 8.1).
AC4-c098 L08 None | Define economic value added (EVA) for a division. | EVA = OI(1 - tau) - rC, where tau is the tax rate applied to operating income. OI(1 - tau) is net operating profit after tax, and rC is the capital charge (Definition 8.1).
AC4-c099 L08 None | How is the ratio OI/C presented? | Operating income divided by the capital base C, which is the operating income earned per unit of capital. It is a plain ratio, not a named measure.
AC4-c100 L08 T | True or false: a positive residual income proves that a division creates value. | False. With C positive on the stated base, RI is nonnegative exactly when OI/C is at least r (Lemma 8.3). The base and the rate are inputs. In Section 4 the capitalization case moves residual income from 30 to 102.8 SCU on the same cash outlay, because the base and operating income change.
AC4-c101 L08 C | True or false: residual income and EVA always agree in sign. | False. Lemma 8.2 gives EVA = RI - tau x OI. A division with a large operating income and a small margin over the charge can have positive residual income and negative EVA. In Lab 1, SIM-G has RI of 2250.00 cents and EVA of -486.00 cents.
AC4-c102 L08 T | True or false: the division with the largest residual income is the best performer. | False as a general rule. Residual income is an amount, so it depends on the size of the division. For two divisions with the same return on capital above the rate, the larger one has the larger residual income, because RI = (OI/C - r)C. Which division is better depends on the question asked.
AC4-c103 L08 T | What is EVA minus RI, in terms of the tax rate and operating income? | -tau x OI, for every division, because the capital charges cancel (Lemma 8.2).
AC4-c104 L08 T | When is residual income nonnegative? | When C is positive, RI >= 0 if and only if OI/C >= r (Lemma 8.3).
AC4-c105 L08 T | When an accounting choice changes operating income by delta OI and the capital base by delta C, how do RI and EVA change? | delta RI = delta OI - r x delta C, and delta EVA = (1 - tau) x delta OI - r x delta C, with the rate and the tax rate unchanged (Proposition 8.4).
AC4-c106 L08 T | When two divisions have the same capital base, do residual income and OI/C order them the same way? | Yes. With a common base C, the difference in RI equals the difference in operating income, and the difference in OI/C has the same sign (Problem P3).
