   51: A fall in the price level raises the real value of a debt that was fixed in nominal terms, and that rise can weaken the borrower's balance sheet and raise the price of further credit. This lesson states that chain as a model, with three stated assumptions, and proves its closed form. The model has no data behind it and describes no historical economy. The lesson also defines the lender of last resort (Definition 4.1), which the model does not contain. Lab 1 prints the output depth (Assumption 1.3) of two model economies, one that stays on parity for longer than the other. Its ordering follows from that assumption and is not evidence about any economy.
   53: 1A stylised model of parity, debt and output
   54: The model has one good, one borrower and a nominal debt. It includes a fixed-parity regime, the gold standard of Lesson 2 in its simplest form, and in the model that regime lowers the price index while it lasts, by assumption. The regime is an assumption of the model and not a description of any actual regime. Section 3 proves its results under the three assumptions below. Sections 2 and 4 give definitions.
   56: Assumption 1.1 (parity and deflation, A1)
   58: Let \(0 < \delta < 1\) be the fraction by which the price index falls in each period on parity. Let \(H \ge 1\) be an integer horizon and let \(m\) be an integer with \(0 \le m \le H\), the number of periods on parity. Set \(P_0 = 1\) and
   59: \[ P_t = (1 - \delta)^{\min(t, m)}, \qquad t = 0, 1, \ldots, H. \]
   60: The economy deflates in periods 1 to \(m\) and its price index is flat in the periods after \(m\).
   63: Assumption 1.2 (fixed nominal debt, net worth and no default, A2)
   65: The nominal debt \(D > 0\) is a whole number of cents, fixed for the whole horizon. Its real value is \(V_t = D / P_t\), so \(V_0 = D\). Borrower net worth is \(N_t = N_0 - (V_t - V_0)\), where \(N_0 > 0\) is the baseline net worth in cents. The whole rise in the real debt falls on net worth. There is no default, no repricing of assets and no other change to the balance sheet. Assume
   66: \[ V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m} < N_0 + D. \]
   67: Proposition 3.2 (Section 3) shows that \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\), so this one inequality keeps \(N_t > 0\) for every period.
   70: Assumption 1.3 (premium and output, A3)
   72: The slope \(\varphi > 0\) and the output coefficient \(\beta > 0\) are fixed. Leverage is \(L_t = V_t / N_t\), with baseline \(L_0 = D / N_0\). The premium above its baseline is \(\psi_t = \varphi\,(L_t - L_0)\), and the fall in output as a fraction of baseline output is \(f_t = \beta\,\psi_t\). The depth for parity duration \(m\) is
   73: \[ \operatorname{depth}(m) = \max_{0 \le t \le H} f_t. \]
   76: Honest-pending note. The course's curriculum names embedded data for this lab. No redistributable historical series for gold-standard adherence or for output depth is embedded, and the registry holds no admitted source for one. The lab therefore runs the stylised model above and is labelled SIMULATED. Its numbers are the model's, and no claim about any historical economy follows from them. No redistributable historical series for these quantities is embedded, and no source for one is admitted to the registry. The lab stays SIMULATED until one is.
   78: 2Definitions
   80: Definition 2.1 (leverage and the external-finance premium)
   82: Under Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, the leverage at period \(t\) is \(L_t = V_t / N_t\), the ratio of the real debt to the borrower's net worth. The external-finance premium is the extra cost a borrower pays for outside funds over the cost of its own funds. In the model it is the rise in that extra cost above its baseline, \(\psi_t\).
   85: Definition 2.2 (credit channel of a depression, in the model)
   87: In the model of Section 1, the credit channel of a depression is the following chain. A fall in the price index raises the real value of the fixed nominal debt (A1 and A2). The rise in the real value lowers borrower net worth one for one (A2). The rise in leverage raises the external-finance premium (A3). The premium lowers output by \(f_t\) as a fraction of baseline output (A3). The chain describes the model. It makes no claim about any economy outside it.
   90: The first step of the chain is the real-debt mechanism of Lesson 2, the debt deflation defined there. The model adds the balance-sheet and premium steps. Its balance sheet is the borrower's. The model has no intermediary whose net worth is modelled, so a claim about banks is outside it.
   92: 3Closed form, ordering and the ratio of depths
   94: Lemma 3.1 (leverage is strictly increasing in real debt)
   96: Let \(C = N_0 + D\) and, for real \(x\) with \(D \le x < C\), let \(\ell(x) = x / (C - x)\). Then \(\ell\) is strictly increasing on \([D, C)\). Under Assumptions 1.2 and 1.3, \(L_t = \ell(V_t)\) whenever \(V_t < C\), and \(L_0 = \ell(D) = D / N_0\).
   99: Proof
  101: Let \(D \le x < y < C\). Then both \(C - x\) and \(C - y\) are positive, and
  102: \[ \ell(y) - \ell(x) = \frac{y}{C - y} - \frac{x}{C - x} = \frac{y(C - x) - x(C - y)}{(C - x)(C - y)} = \frac{C\,(y - x)}{(C - x)(C - y)} > 0. \]
  103: Since \(N_t = N_0 - (V_t - D) = C - V_t\), the leverage is \(L_t = V_t / (C - V_t) = \ell(V_t)\) whenever \(N_t > 0\). At \(t = 0\), \(V_0 = D\) and \(C - D = N_0\), so \(L_0 = D / N_0\). \(\blacksquare\)
  106: Proposition 3.2 (closed form of the depth)
  108: Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3. Let \(V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m}\). Then
  109: (a) \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\) with \(0 \le t \le H\). For \(t \le m\), \(V_t = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-t}\), which is strictly increasing when \(m \ge 1\). For \(t \ge m\), \(V_t = V_m\).
  110: (b) \(\operatorname{depth}(m) = \beta\varphi\,\bigl(\ell(V_m) - \ell(D)\bigr) = \beta\varphi\Bigl(\dfrac{V_m}{N_0 + D - V_m} - \dfrac{D}{N_0}\Bigr)\).
  111: (c) The maximum defining the depth is first attained at \(t = m\). In particular \(\operatorname{depth}(0) = 0\).
  114: Proof
  116: (a) For \(t \le m\), Assumption 1.1 gives \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^{t}\), so \(V_t = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-t}\). Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\), the factor \((1 - \delta)^{-1}\) exceeds 1, so \(t \mapsto V_t\) is strictly increasing on \(0, \ldots, m\) when \(m \ge 1\). For \(t \ge m\), \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^{m}\) and \(V_t = V_m\). Together these give \(V_t \le V_m\) for every \(t\).
  117: (b) By Assumption 1.2, \(V_m < C\), so \(V_t \le V_m < C\) for every \(t\). Lemma 3.1 then gives \(L_t = \ell(V_t)\) for every \(t\), and \(f_t = \beta\varphi\,(\ell(V_t) - \ell(D))\). Because \(\ell\) is strictly increasing on \([D, C)\), \(\ell(V_t) \le \ell(V_m)\) for every \(t\), and \(\beta\varphi > 0\), so \(f_t \le f_m\) for every \(t\). Since \(m\) is one of the periods \(0, \ldots, H\), the maximum equals \(f_m\), which is the displayed formula.
  118: (c) If \(m = 0\), then \(V_t = D\) for every \(t\) by (a), so \(f_t = 0\) for every \(t\) and the first maximising period is \(t = 0 = m\). If \(m \ge 1\), then for every \(t < m\), (a) gives \(V_t < V_m\), so \(f_t < f_m\) by the strict monotonicity of \(\ell\). No period before \(m\) attains the maximum, and \(t = m\) does. \(\blacksquare\)
  121: Proposition 3.2 is an identity about the model. Part (b) is the formula that Lab 1 evaluates, and part (c) says where the depth occurs. Under Assumption 1.1 the depth rises with the number of periods on parity, which is the next result.
  123: Corollary 3.3 (a longer parity duration gives a deeper fall)
  125: Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 for two parity durations \(m'\) and \(m\) with \(0 \le m' < m \le H\). Then \(\operatorname{depth}(m') < \operatorname{depth}(m)\). Economy X in Lab 1 has \(m = 12\) and economy Y has \(m' = 4\), so under these assumptions X is deeper than Y.
  128: Proof
  130: Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\) and \(m' < m\), we have \(V_{m'} = D(1 - \delta)^{-m'} < D(1 - \delta)^{-m} = V_m\). Also \(V_m < C\) by Assumption 1.2, so \(D \le V_{m'} < V_m < C\). Lemma 3.1 gives \(\ell(V_{m'}) < \ell(V_m)\). Proposition 3.2(b) applies to both durations with the same \(\beta\varphi > 0\), so \(\operatorname{depth}(m') = \beta\varphi\,(\ell(V_{m'}) - \ell(D)) < \beta\varphi\,(\ell(V_m) - \ell(D)) = \operatorname{depth}(m)\). \(\blacksquare\)
  133: Corollary 3.4 (the ratio of depths does not depend on the product \(\beta\varphi\))
  135: Assume Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 for two parity durations \(m'\) and \(m\) with \(1 \le m' < m \le H\). Then
  136: \[ \frac{\operatorname{depth}(m)}{\operatorname{depth}(m')} = \frac{\ell(V_m) - \ell(D)}{\ell(V_{m'}) - \ell(D)} \]
  137: and the right-hand side does not depend on \(\beta\) or \(\varphi\).
  140: Proof
  142: Since \(m' \ge 1\), \(V_{m'} > D\), so \(\ell(V_{m'}) - \ell(D) > 0\) by Lemma 3.1, and \(\operatorname{depth}(m') > 0\) by Proposition 3.2(b). Divide the two formulas of Proposition 3.2(b). The common factor \(\beta\varphi\) cancels, which gives the displayed ratio. \(\blacksquare\)

  386: 4The lender of last resort
  388: Definition 4.1 (lender of last resort)
  390: A central bank is the public institution that issues the monetary base. A lender of last resort is a central bank that lends against collateral to institutions that are solvent but illiquid. Solvent means that the institution's assets, valued as the lender values them, cover its liabilities. Illiquid means that the institution cannot meet its payments from its own resources in the time available.
  393: Remark 4.2 (the model has no lender of last resort)
  395: Assumption 1.2 has no term for lending, and Assumption 1.3 has no term through which a lending facility changes the premium of a solvent borrower. Proposition 3.2 therefore says nothing about what such a facility would do. This lesson makes no claim about its effect. The model's net-worth condition \(N_t > 0\) (Assumption 1.2) is a solvency condition in the balance-sheet sense, and Proposition 3.2(a) shows that \(V_t \le V_m\) in every period, and the inequality of Assumption 1.2 then gives \(V_m < N_0 + D\), so \(N_t > 0\) in every period. What the model lacks is any illiquidity, collateral or lending term, so the lender's role in Definition 4.1 has no counterpart.
  399: 5Where the canon breaks
  400: The model is exact inside its assumptions and silent outside them. Four places where the reader must act:
  402: One good, no default, no fiscal response. Assumption 1.2 sets the change in net worth equal to minus the change in the real debt. A default, a bailout or a change in the value of the assets would change that identity, and Proposition 3.2 would no longer apply.
  403: The parity duration is an input. Assumption 1.1 fixes \(m\) for each economy. In the model, when an economy leaves parity is a choice of the modeller. The lesson gives no rule for that choice, and the ordering in Corollary 3.3 depends on the durations being different.
  404: The functional form is a choice. The premium is linear in leverage with slope \(\varphi\), and the output fall is linear in the premium. Corollary 3.3 is proved for the linear premium, and Problem R2 shows that the ordering needs only that the premium is strictly increasing in the real debt. Corollary 3.4's ratio depends on the shape of the premium, not on its scale. A different shape can change the ratio: if the premium is \(\varphi\) times the square of \((L_t - L_0)\), each depth is the square of the linear bracket in Corollary 3.4, so the ratio is the square of the ratio of Corollary 3.4, which differs from it whenever that ratio is not 1. A change of slope cannot change the ratio.
  405: The credit channel is not measured here. The model makes no empirical claim about the credit channel and no claim about its size. The registry holds no admitted source for an empirical credit-channel claim, so a reader who wants one needs a source with a locator, and a counter-account, which this lesson does not supply. The registry holds the monetary history cited in Section 10. This lesson attributes no account to it, because no passage of that book has been read for this page, and it does not identify the model's chain with any account in it.
  410: 6Misconceptions
  413: “The model shows that bank failures caused the depth.”
  414: The model has no bank, no failure and no default, since Assumption 1.2 excludes them. Proposition 3.2 is proved from Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, and the proof uses none of these events. A failure would change the balance-sheet identity of Assumption 1.2, which is the reason it is excluded.
  417: “Economy X's larger depth shows that holding parity makes output fall further.”
  418: The ordering follows from the parity durations of 12 and 4 that Assumption 1.1 sets. The proof of Corollary 3.3 uses those durations, the closed form \(V_m = D\,(1 - \delta)^{-m}\) from Assumption 1.1, the bound \(V_m < N_0 + D\) of Assumption 1.2, Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2(b), and none of the bank failures or defaults that the first misconception rules out. The model cannot say whether holding parity causes a deeper fall. Lab 1 prints the ordering the assumptions imply.
  421: “A lender of last resort lends to any firm that asks, so panics end.”
  422: Definition 4.1 requires collateral, and it limits the lending to institutions that are solvent but illiquid. A lender that lent to insolvent firms would not fit the definition. Remark 4.2 adds that the model contains no such facility, so no panic result follows from it.
  425: “The 9.20 percent depth in Lab 1 is a measured fall in output.”
  426: Lab 1 is SIMULATED. Its depth is the model's value under Assumption 1.3 with \(\beta = 1\), and no output series enters the lab. The printed figure is a property of the model's parameters, and changing \(\beta\) changes it in proportion, as the factor \(\beta\varphi\) in Proposition 3.2(b) shows.

  461: Proof
  462: P1 · Monotone prices and real debt
  464: Under Assumption 1.1, show that \(P_t\) is non-increasing in \(t\) and that \(V_t\) is non-decreasing in \(t\).
  464: Solution
  466: Since \(0 < 1 - \delta < 1\), the map \(k \mapsto (1 - \delta)^{k}\) is strictly decreasing on the integers. The exponent \(\min(t, m)\) is non-decreasing in \(t\), so \(P_t\) is non-increasing in \(t\). Because \(D > 0\), \(V_t = D / P_t\) is then non-decreasing in \(t\). This is Proposition 3.2(a) with the same argument.
  469: P2 · No depth without a parity duration
  471: Show that \(\operatorname{depth}(0) = 0\) for every \(\varphi > 0\) and \(\beta > 0\).
  471: Solution
  473: If \(m = 0\), then \(P_t = (1 - \delta)^0 = 1\) for every \(t\), so \(V_t = D\) and \(N_t = N_0\). Hence \(L_t = L_0\) and \(\psi_t = \varphi(L_t - L_0) = 0\), and \(f_t = \beta \cdot 0 = 0\) for every \(t\). The maximum of a constant zero sequence is zero.
  476: P3 · The strict step in Lemma 3.1
  478: Prove that \(\ell(y) - \ell(x) = C(y - x) / ((C - x)(C - y))\) for \(x, y\) with \(x, y < C\), and deduce Lemma 3.1 for \(D \le x < y < C\).
  478: Solution
  480: Put the two fractions over the common denominator: \(\frac{y}{C-y} - \frac{x}{C-x} = \frac{y(C-x) - x(C-y)}{(C-x)(C-y)}\). The numerator expands to \(yC - xy - xC + xy = C(y - x)\), which gives the identity. If \(D \le x < y < C\), then \(y - x > 0\), \(C - x > 0\) and \(C - y > 0\), so the difference is positive and \(\ell\) is strictly increasing.

  511: Research
  512: R1 · A lending facility in the model
  514: Describe the change to the model that a lender of last resort would require, and name the assumption it extends or replaces.
  514: Solution
  516: The facility must lower the premium for solvent borrowers while leaving insolvent borrowers unaffected. That needs a case in which some borrower is insolvent, which Assumption 1.2 excludes by requiring \(N_t > 0\). It also needs a term in Assumption 1.3 for the facility's effect on \(\psi_t\). Proposition 3.2 is proved for the model without that term, so its statements do not carry over to the extended model without a new proof. Lemma 3.1, a fact about the function \(\ell\) alone, is unaffected.
  519: R2 · The ordering under any strictly increasing premium
  521: Under Assumptions 1.1 to 1.3, let \(G\) be any strictly increasing function on \([D, C)\), and define \(\operatorname{depth}_G(m) = \beta \max_{0 \le t \le H} G(V_t)\). Show that \(\operatorname{depth}_G(m') < \operatorname{depth}_G(m)\) for \(0 \le m' < m \le H\).
  521: Solution
  523: By Proposition 3.2(a), the largest real debt on the horizon is \(V_m\), attained at \(t = m\), and the largest real debt for \(m'\) is \(V_{m'}\). Since \(G\) is strictly increasing, \(\max_t G(V_t) = G(V_m)\) for duration \(m\), and \(G(V_{m'})\) for \(m'\). Corollary 3.3's proof gives \(V_{m'} < V_m\), so \(G(V_{m'}) < G(V_m)\), and multiplying by \(\beta > 0\) gives the claim. The ordering needs only that the premium is a strictly increasing function of the real debt.
  526: R3 · The data that a test of Corollary 3.3 would need
  528: Name the series a test of Corollary 3.3 against an economy would need, and say which of them this course embeds.
  528: Solution
  530: A test needs a price index path with the dates on which an economy left parity, a measured external-finance premium, a measured output series with the baseline it is compared with, and a measure of borrower net worth or leverage. The course embeds none of these for this lesson. The test therefore cannot be run here, and the lab is a model check only.
