=== exemplar-a (essay text only) ===
Episode and condition
Episode one is the interwar gold-standard episode. Its period is the period in the title of Eichengreen (1992), Golden fetters: the gold standard and the Great Depression, 1919-1939 (registry key eichengreen1992golden , locator: the title field). The condition is the exchange rate of sterling in US dollars, from the G.5/H.10 snapshot, over window three of Lab 3, 2025-10 to 2026-09, the twelve months to the snapshot's last month. That last month is 2026-09 (MANIFEST, field last ; retrieved 2026-10-19). The essay describes no other period and no other condition.
Mechanism, episode one
In the model of Lesson 2, the real value of a fixed nominal debt \(D\) is \(V_t = D P_0 / P_t\), and a fall in the price level from \(P_{t-1}\) to \(P_t\) multiplies it by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\), which exceeds one (Proposition 2.1(a) of Lesson 2, under its assumptions (A2) and (A3)). The chain has three steps: (1) the nominal debt is fixed, which is the model's assumption and not a claim about the episode; (2) the price level falls; (3) the real value is multiplied by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\). The analogy holds at step 3: the claim the episode's chain makes there, that the real value is multiplied by \(P_{t-1} / P_t\), is an exact computation from the step before, and so is the condition's step-3 claim below, that the dispersion is the sample standard deviation of its 12 changes. The analogy fails at step 2. Remark 3.3 of Lesson 2 says the corollary gives no cause for a fall, so the model cannot say why the price level fell in the episode. The essay asserts no price path for the episode. The chain applies only if a fall occurred, and the essay does not claim one.
Mechanism, condition
This chain runs from the data to the measured quantity and claims no cause. Step 1: the rate \(S_m\) is the last value of month \(m\) in the snapshot (MANIFEST transform field). Step 2: the monthly log change \(\ln(S_m / S_{m-1})\) measures the move. Step 3: the dispersion of window three is the sample standard deviation of its 12 changes, 1.90 percent per month (Lab 3). The analogy holds at step 3: the claim this chain makes there, that the dispersion is the sample standard deviation of the 12 changes of step 2, is an exact computation from the step before, as the episode's step-3 claim is. The analogy fails at step 2. Under a fixed exchange rate every log change would be zero, because a constant rate has \(\ln 1 = 0\) as its change. Window three has 0 exact zero changes in 12 (Lab 3). The file therefore rules out a fixed exchange rate held over all of window three. The essay makes no claim about any exchange-rate regime.
Numbers
Lab 1 is SIMULATED and is not data. A fall in the price level from 1.00 to 0.85 raises the real value by \(100\,(1/0.85 - 1)\), which is 17.65 percent of the period-0 real value, computed from unrounded values and rounded half up to two decimals (Lab 1, Part B). Lab 3 gives 2.47 percent per month for window two, 2.79 percent per month for window one and 1.90 percent per month for window three, each from an unrounded standard deviation rounded half up to two decimals. Their ratio, window two over window one, is taken from the unrounded values and printed as 0.88 (Lab 3). The essay does not divide the printed figures.
Limits
Limit one: the snapshot fx_monthly holds exchange rates only, with no interest-rate column (MANIFEST columns), so it cannot test any relation between interest rates and exchange rates. Lesson 4 says the lab cannot test any such relation, because the file has no interest-rate column (Lesson 4, Section 4, item &quot;Exchange rates only&quot;). A test would need an interest-rate series for each country over the same months. The essay names no source for them, and so it runs no such test. Limit two: the start month is a choice. Window one, from 1971-02, contains the largest absolute monthly log change of the GBP column, 13.58 percent in 1985-03 (Lab 3, window one: the largest-change line, rounded half up to two decimals from the unrounded change), and window two does not. The difference between the dispersions, window one minus window two, is 0.32 percent per month (Lab 3, difference line, rounded half up to two decimals from the unrounded dispersions). Limit three: the essay does not test the causal role of the gold standard, and it makes no causal claim about it, so it needs no counter-evidence for one. The essay makes no claim graded B or C, so no claim needs counter-evidence, and every factual claim above carries its locator or is the essay's own inference.

=== exemplar-b (essay text only) ===
Summary
Sterling today is nothing like the gold standard. The pound has been volatile, which the gold standard was meant to stop, so the analogy holds for the system as a whole.
Data
The episode is the gold standard, 1919-1939, as the title field of Eichengreen (1992) names it. The condition is sterling against the dollar in the Federal Reserve's H.10 data, which is monthly. The window is recent.
Numbers
The monthly volatility is 2.47 percent per year. The later window's volatility divided by the earlier window's is 2.47 divided by 2.79, which is 0.89, so volatility fell by about 11 percent.
Mechanism
Both periods have a currency tied to a standard, and both show falls in prices. One difference: the gold standard fixed the price of gold, and the spot rate does not. The analogy is therefore weak.
Limits
The data have caveats, and the essay could be wrong about some of them.
