32: Payments, Market Infrastructure & Fintech
33: A trade is settled when the money for it has moved, and the money moves through payment systems and clearing houses. This module describes those systems in the order a payment experiences them. You will compare two settlement designs on one day of payments, follow how a central counterparty turns many bilateral claims into one net obligation per member, trace how a default is absorbed by the layers of a clearing house, and check the reserve arithmetic of a stablecoin. The module ends in a clearing-house simulator, graded by an autograder, and a memo, graded by a rubric.
35: Prerequisites: FC1 The Financial System, Markets & Instruments, which uses E1, M1, M2 and P0.1 in turn. P0.1 is not yet released in this program.
36: Project: Clearing-house simulator, with a memo. Graded by an autograder and a rubric
37: Data: none. The labs and the project use generated inputs, labelled SIMULATED where they appear. No lab or figure reads a data file.
42: 35 h lessons · 15 h project (30% of the 50 module hours)
46: Objectives
47: By the end of BK4 you can:
49: Compute the peak intraday liquidity need and the net debit of each bank for a day of payments, in integer cents, and prove that the need is never below the net debit (Lesson 1).
50: Derive the net obligation of each member after novation, prove that the net obligations sum to zero, and state when the netted amount equals the gross notional (Lesson 2).
51: Compute the layers of a CCP default waterfall in cents, and prove the order statistic that sizes a margin for a target cover (Lesson 3).
52: Derive the coverage of a stablecoin's reserves after redemptions, and state the condition under which that coverage falls below one (Lesson 4).
53: Enumerate the fail patterns of settlement failures exactly, and check the expected cost against its closed form (Lesson 5).
54: Implement a clearing-house simulator to a published interface, and pass the autograder's tests on seeded instances (project).
56: The module's scope is stated once here, and each lesson keeps to it. One member defaults at a time. The scenarios are a fixed, finite set. The waterfall order is an assumed rule of the model, and the settlement period is a parameter.

72: Placement diagnostic
73: Eight questions: six on the prerequisites (M1, M2, E1, P0.1 and FC1), and two skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project ladder teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent. With eight items the bar needs seven correct: six of eight is 75 percent, and seven of eight is 87.5 percent. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. Below the bar, revisit the prerequisites first.
76: What is the present value at 10 percent of 110 received at the end of one period?
77: Options
78: 99.00
79: 100.00
80: 121.00
82: \(110/1.1 = 100\) (M1, present value).
85: What is the sum \(1 + 1/2 + 1/4\)?
86: Answer
87: The first three terms of a geometric series with ratio \(1/2\) sum to \((1-(1/2)^3)/(1/2) = 1.75\) (M1, geometric series).
90: What is \(\lim_{x \to 3} (2x)\)?
91: Answer
92: The limit of \(2x\) as \(x\) approaches 3 is \(2 \cdot 3 = 6\) (M2, limit).
95: In Bertrand competition between two firms with identical products and constant marginal cost \(c\), what is the equilibrium price?
96: Options
97: \(c\), the marginal cost
98: The monopoly price
99: Twice \(c\)
101: The Bertrand outcome sets the price equal to marginal cost (E1, Bertrand competition).
104: Which grade does a finding from a single sample carry under the evidence standard?
105: Options
106: A
107: C
108: T
110: Grade C is the grade for a working paper, practitioner research or single-sample evidence (P0.1, evidence grade).
113: In the trade lifecycle of FC1, which state comes immediately before settlement?
114: Options
115: Matching
116: Clearing
117: Order entry
119: The FC1 lifecycle runs order entry, matching, trade capture, clearing, settlement and position update, in that order (FC1, trade lifecycle).
122: Skill check (rung 1). A defaulter has an initial margin of 100 cents and a default fund of 50 cents. A loss of 120 cents falls on the clearing house. How many cents does the defaulter's default fund absorb?
123: Answer
124: The margin absorbs the first 100 cents. The default fund absorbs the next \(\min(120 - 100, 50) = 20\) cents. The layer order is the one the project specification states, and the worked example in rung 1 applies it.
127: Skill check (rung 2). After the defaulter's layers, a residual of 1 cent is shared among three surviving members whose default funds are 1 cent each. Each member's pro rata share rounds down to 0 cents. Which member receives the 1 cent of remainder under the remainder rule?
128: Options
129: The first surviving member, in ascending member order
130: The last surviving member, in ascending member order
131: Each member pays one third of a cent
133: The remainder rule gives the leftover cents, one each, to the first surviving members with a positive default fund, in ascending member order (the project specification). With a residual of 1 cent, the first surviving member pays it.
139: Lessons
140: Five lessons of seven study hours each, so the lessons sum to 35 hours. Each lesson has graded claims, a runnable lab, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck. The lessons follow one chain from payment to loss: the day's payments and the liquidity they need, the trades that a central counterparty nets, the default waterfall that absorbs a loss, the stablecoin whose reserves back a claim, and the operational losses that a failed settlement causes.
142: L01 · 7 hPayment Systems and the Settlement CycleGross real-time and deferred net settlement compared on one seeded SIMULATED day, the peak intraday liquidity need and the net debit, and the proof that the need is never below the net debit.
143: L02 · 7 hCentral Counterparties and Multilateral NettingNovation and the central counterparty, net obligations that sum to zero, and the netted amount compared with the gross notional, checked in Lab 1 on seeded SIMULATED trades.
144: L03 · 7 hThe CCP Default Waterfall and Margin CoverThe layers that absorb a member's default in a stated order, the survivors' shares with their remainder rule, and the margin for a target cover, sized by an order statistic.
145: L04 · 7 hStablecoins and TokenizationA stablecoin's reserve-to-claims ratio, the coverage after a redemption share, and the condition under which coverage falls below one, checked in exact arithmetic.
146: L05 · 7 hOperational Risk in Market InfrastructureOperational loss defined, a settlement-failure cost model with three trades, and its expected cost by exact enumeration of every fail pattern against the closed form.
151: The project ladder
152: The module ends in the clearing-house simulator. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.
154: Worked exampleA fully solved and annotated default on a four-member clearing house, with a seeded SIMULATED set of 40 scenario losses. Each step gives its reason: the layer order, the survivors' shares and the cover sizing.
155: Completion problemThe same default with the survivor-share line and the sizing-index line left blank. Graded instantly in your browser against the completed solution.
156: Independent projectImplement clearing_house.py to the published interface, and write the memo. The autograder checks your code on seeded SIMULATED instances, and the rubric grades the memo. Project page.
157: StretchAn extension of your own design, for example a second default in the same scenario set, or a re-margining after one netting cycle. It is not graded, and it is suitable for your portfolio.

183: Data status
184: No data is used in BK4. The labs and the project compute from formulas and from generated inputs, and each generated input is labelled SIMULATED where it appears. The curriculum names no dataset for this module, and the program's embedded market data holds no payment, settlement, clearing or stablecoin series. The settlement period in Lesson 1 is a parameter, and the lesson states no settlement rule as a fact.
