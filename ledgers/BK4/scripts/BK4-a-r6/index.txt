32	Payments, Market Infrastructure & Fintech
33	In this unit's model, a trade settles when its payment has moved between accounts. The payment systems that move it are defined in Lesson 1, and the clearing houses of the model are central counterparties, defined in Lesson 2. This module describes those systems in the order a payment experiences them. You will compare two settlement designs on one day of payments, follow how a central counterparty turns many bilateral claims into one net obligation per member, trace how a default is absorbed by the layers of a clearing house, and check the reserve arithmetic of a stablecoin. The module ends in a clearing-house simulator, graded by an autograder, and a memo, graded by a rubric.

46	Objectives
47	By the end of BK4 you can:
49	Compute the peak intraday liquidity need and the net debit of each bank for a day of payments, in integer cents, and prove that the need is never below the net debit (Lesson 1).
50	Derive the net obligation of each member after novation, prove that the net obligations sum to zero, and state when the netted amount equals the gross notional (Lesson 2).
51	Compute the layers of a CCP default waterfall in cents, and prove the order statistic that sizes a margin for a target cover (Lesson 3).
52	Derive the coverage of a stablecoin's reserves after redemptions, and state the condition under which that coverage falls below one (Lesson 4).
53	Enumerate the fail patterns of settlement failures exactly, and check the expected cost against its closed form (Lesson 5).
54	Implement a clearing-house simulator to a published interface, and pass the autograder's tests on seeded instances (project).
56	The module's scope is stated once here, and each lesson states its own limits in its canon-breaks section. One member defaults at a time. The scenarios are a fixed, finite set. The waterfall order is an assumed rule of the model, and the settlement period is a parameter.

75	Options
76	99.00
77	100.00

83	What is the sum \(1 + 1/2 + 1/4\)?
84	Answer
85	The first three terms of a geometric series with ratio \(1/2\) sum to \((1-(1/2)^3)/(1/2) = 1.75\) (M1, geometric series).
88	What is \(\lim_{x \to 3} (2x)\)?
89	Answer
90	The limit of \(2x\) as \(x\) approaches 3 is \(2 \cdot 3 = 6\) (M2, limit).
93	In Bertrand competition between two firms with identical products and constant marginal cost \(c\), what is the equilibrium price?
94	Options
95	\(c\), the marginal cost
96	The monopoly price
97	Twice \(c\)

112	Options

120	Skill check (rung 1). A defaulter has an initial margin of 100 cents and a default fund of 50 cents. A loss of 120 cents falls on the clearing house. How many cents does the defaulter's default fund absorb? (Lesson 3 defines the layers.)
121	Answer
122	The margin absorbs the first 100 cents. The default fund absorbs the next \(\min(120 - 100, 50) = 20\) cents. The layer order is the one the project specification states, and the worked example in rung 1 applies it.
125	Skill check (rung 2). After the defaulter's layers, a residual of 1 cent is shared among three surviving members whose default funds are 1 cent each. Each member's pro rata share rounds down to 0 cents. Which member receives the 1 cent of remainder under the remainder rule? (Lesson 3 defines the survivor shares.)
126	Options
127	The first surviving member, in ascending member order
128	The last surviving member, in ascending member order
129	Each member pays one third of a cent
131	The remainder rule gives the leftover cents, one each, to the first surviving members with a positive default fund, in ascending member order (the project specification). With a residual of 1 cent, the first surviving member pays it.
137	Lessons
138	Five lessons of seven study hours each, so the lessons sum to 35 hours. Each lesson has graded claims, a runnable lab, a problem set with worked solutions and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck. The lessons follow one chain from payment to loss: the day's payments and the liquidity they need, the trades that a central counterparty nets, the default waterfall that absorbs a loss, the stablecoin whose reserves back a claim, and the operational losses that a failed settlement causes.
140	L01 · 7 hPayment Systems and the Settlement CycleGross real-time and deferred net settlement compared on one seeded SIMULATED day, the peak intraday liquidity need and the net debit, and the proof that the need is never below the net debit.
141	L02 · 7 hCentral Counterparties and Multilateral NettingNovation and the central counterparty, net obligations that sum to zero, and the netted amount compared with the gross notional, checked in Lab 1 on seeded SIMULATED trades.
142	L03 · 7 hThe CCP Default Waterfall and Margin CoverThe layers that absorb a member's default in a stated order, the survivors' shares with their remainder rule, and the margin for a target cover, sized by an order statistic.
143	L04 · 7 hStablecoins and TokenizationA stablecoin's reserve-to-claims ratio, the coverage after a redemption share, and the condition under which coverage falls below one, checked in exact arithmetic.
144	L05 · 7 hOperational Risk in Market InfrastructureOperational loss defined, a settlement-failure cost model with three trades, and its expected cost by exact enumeration of every fail pattern against the closed form.
149	The project ladder
150	The module ends in the clearing-house simulator. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, a student must reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.
152	Worked exampleA fully solved and annotated default on a four-member clearing house, with a seeded SIMULATED set of 40 scenario losses. Each step gives its reason: the layer order, the survivors' shares and the cover sizing.
153	Completion problemThe same default with the survivor-share line and the sizing-index line left blank. Graded instantly in your browser against the completed solution.
154	Independent projectImplement clearing_house.py to the published interface, and write the memo. The autograder checks your code on seeded SIMULATED instances, and the rubric grades the memo. Project page.
155	StretchAn extension of your own design, for example a second default in the same scenario set, or a re-margining after one netting cycle. It is not graded, and it is suitable for your portfolio.
