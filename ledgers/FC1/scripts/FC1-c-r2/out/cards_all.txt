FC1-c001 L01 <NOGRADE> | What is a financial intermediary? || A firm that issues claims on itself to savers and holds claims on other parties, such as loans, as its assets. Its equity is its assets minus its liabilities (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c002 L01 <NOGRADE> | Does assets equal liabilities plus equity for every firm, and is that a finding? || It holds by definition, because equity is defined as assets minus liabilities. It states no finding about any firm. A negative equity means the liabilities exceed the assets (Remark 1.2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c003 L01 <NOGRADE> | What does Assumption A1 of the stylised bank require? || A constant reserve ratio: reserves X_t equal ρ times deposits D_t at every stage, for a constant ρ with 0 < ρ ≤ 1 (Assumption 2.0). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c004 L01 <NOGRADE> | What is bank money creation in the stylised model? || The increase in total deposits that results when the bank makes a loan and the proceeds are deposited back at the same bank. In the model it is the sum of the redeposits made after stage 0 (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c005 L01 T | In the stylised bank, what is the new deposit received at stage t, given an initial deposit d and a reserve ratio ρ? || D_t = (1 − ρ)^t d, the deposit received at stage t and not the balance. Each stage's loan is (1 − ρ) times the deposit, and under A1 and A2 it is redeposited at stage t + 1 (Section 2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c006 L01 T | Does the stylised bank's balance sheet balance at every stage? || Yes. Under Assumption 2.0 (A1 constant reserve ratio, A2 full redeposit, A3 closed system), X_t + ℓ_t = D_t at every stage t (Proposition 2.2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c007 L01 T | What is the closed form of total deposits S_n after stages 0 to n? || Under Assumption 2.0 with d > 0 and 0 < ρ ≤ 1, S_n = d(1 − (1 − ρ)^(n+1))/ρ for every n ≥ 0, so S_n/d = (1 − (1 − ρ)^(n+1))/ρ (Proposition 3.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c008 L01 T | Does total deposits have a finite limit when the reserve ratio is zero? || No. With ρ = 0 each deposit equals d, so S_n = (n + 1)d, which grows without bound (Remark 3.2; Problem P2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c009 L01 T | Under leakage, what is the limit of total deposits, and how does it compare with d/ρ? || Under A2′ (a share φ, with 0 ≤ φ ≤ 1, of each loan is redeposited and the rest is held as cash), with A1, 0 < ρ ≤ 1, and the no-withdrawal and no-repayment parts of A3, total deposits converge to d/(1 − φ(1 − ρ)). This is at most d/ρ, with equality if and only if φ = 1 or ρ = 1 (Proposition 3.5). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c010 L01 T | With an initial deposit of 1,000 dollars and a reserve ratio of 1/10, what are total deposits after stage 10 to the cent? || 6,861.89 dollars (686189 cents), from the exact value 1000 × (1 − 0.9^11)/0.1 = 6861.8940391, rounded half up (Example 3.3). || <NOBASIS>

FC1-c019 L02 <NOGRADE> | What is an exchange-traded trade, and what is an over-the-counter trade? || A trade is exchange-traded when it is executed on a venue that fixes one set of trading rules and the standard terms of the instruments it lists. A trade is over-the-counter (OTC) when the two parties negotiate its terms directly and no venue fixes them (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c020 L02 <NOGRADE> | What does Assumption 1.3 require of each trade? || Each trade is executed either on a venue of Definition 1.1 or bilaterally, and not both. FC1-c021 L02 T | For n trades, each classified by Assumption 1.3, what do the exchange count and the OTC count sum to? || n. Each trade lies in exactly one of two disjoint sets, so the exchange count plus the OTC count equals n (Proposition 1.4). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c022 L02 <NOGRADE> | Does Proposition 1.4 say which venue type carries more trades? || No. It is bookkeeping that holds for any set of trades each classified by Assumption 1.3. It says nothing about how many trades take each venue in any market (Remark 1.5). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c023 L02 <NOGRADE> | What are the three axes of the instrument taxonomy? || Exposure, the economic risk that the payoff depends on, with five values: equities, rates, credit, FX and commodities. Form, which is cash or derivative. Venue, which is exchange or OTC (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c024 L02 <NOGRADE> | How many values may an instrument take on each axis of the taxonomy? || Exactly one on each axis. An entry with two values on one axis, or with none, is not a classification under Definition 2.1. || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c025 L02 <NOGRADE> | Why is form a separate axis from exposure? || A derivative has an exposure as well. A futures contract on a government bond is a derivative with rates exposure, so one heading would hide either the form or the exposure (Remark 2.2). || <NOBASIS>

FC1-c035 L03 <NOGRADE> | What is a dealer? || An agent that posts a bid, at which it will buy, and an ask, at which it will sell, and that trades with customers from its own inventory (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c036 L03 <NOGRADE> | For a bid b and an ask a, how are the quoted spread, the mid and the half-spread defined? || The quoted spread is s = a − b, the mid is m = (a + b)/2, and the half-spread is h = s/2. With a = m + h and b = m − h, where m and h are whole numbers of cents (the lesson's convention), the quoted spread is even: s = 2h (Definition 1.2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c037 L03 T | At a mid of 5000 cents with a half-spread of 15 cents, what is the relative spread? || The bid is 4985 cents and the ask is 5015 cents, so the quoted spread is 30 cents. The relative spread is 30/5000 = 0.006, which is 0.6 percent of the mid, on the basis of the quoted spread over the mid (Example 1.4). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c038 L03 <NOGRADE> | What does Assumption A1 of the dealer model fix? || Each customer trade happens at the quote in force, a customer buy at the ask m + h and a customer sell at the bid m − h, with no size limit. The half-spread h is the same at every trade of the sequence, so the quote is not revised within it (Assumption A1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c039 L03 <NOGRADE> | What is a closed sequence of trades? || A sequence whose inventory I = −Σ σ_t q_t is zero, so customers buy and sell the same number of shares (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c040 L03 <NOGRADE> | How is the mark-to-mid profit of a sequence defined? || P = C + I·m_n, where C = Σ σ_t q_t p_t is the dealer's cash, p_t = m_t + σ_t h is the price of trade t, I is the inventory and m_n is the terminal mid (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c041 L03 T | What does the mark-to-mid decomposition say for any finite sequence of trades under A1 to A3, with one half-spread h for the whole sequence? || P = hV + Σ_t σ_t q_t (m_t − m_n). The first term is half the quoted spread times the total shares traded, and the sum is the mid-move term. The identity holds whether or not the sequence is closed (Proposition 2.3). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c042 L03 T | Under A1 to A3, what is the mark-to-mid profit of a closed sequence in which every trade happens at one mid? || P = C = s × (shares customers buy), the quoted spread times the shares that customers buy. Every trade is at the same mid, so the mid-move term is zero (Corollary 2.4). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c043 L03 T | In Example 2.5, with mids 10000 and 9990 and h = 2, what are the spread term, the mid-move term and the profit? || The spread term is 400 cents, the mid-move term is −1000 cents and the profit is −600 cents. The dealer earns the spread and still loses, because the mid fell after it bought (Example 2.5). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c044 L03 <NOGRADE> | Is the spread profit on every share? || No. The spread earns h per share against the mid of the same trade, and the mid-move term of Proposition 2.3 is added to it. Example 2.5 gives a spread term of 400 cents and a profit of −600 cents (misconception 1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c045 L03 <NOGRADE> | Does a dealer's spread income scale with the number of trades? || No. The spread term is h times the total shares traded V, not the trade count. A 100-share trade contributes 100 times h, and a 1-share trade contributes h (Proposition 2.3; misconception 2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c046 L03 <NOGRADE> | Does the mid-move term of Proposition 2.3 have a fixed sign? || No. Under A1 to A3, Proposition 2.3 is an identity for given trades and mids, not a bound. The sign of the mid-move term follows from the trade sides and the path of the mid relative to the terminal mid (Remark 2.6). || <NOBASIS>

FC1-c056 L04 <NOGRADE> | What is a market order, and what happens to its unfilled shares? || A market order trades at the best prices available on the opposite side now, taking resting orders in price-time priority. Shares it cannot fill are cancelled, and it never rests in the book (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c057 L04 <NOGRADE> | What is a limit order? || An order with a price limit p. A buy limit trades only at prices at most p, a sell limit only at prices at least p, and unfilled shares rest in the book at p (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c058 L04 <NOGRADE> | When does a buy stop activate, and when does a sell stop activate? || A buy stop activates when the last trade price is at or above its stop price, and a sell stop when the last trade price is at or below its stop price. On activation it becomes a market order for its full quantity (Definition 1.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c059 L04 <NOGRADE> | At what price does a fill occur? || At the resting order's price, not the incoming order's (Remark 1.2). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c060 L04 <NOGRADE> | How are pending stops activated when more than one could trigger? || Stops are checked only after the arriving order has finished matching. Only the last trade price at that check counts. Pending stops that trigger are activated in arrival order, one at a time. Each activation can move the last trade price, so the check repeats until no pending stop triggers (Remark 1.2). || <NOBASIS>

FC1-c063 L04 <NOGRADE> | What is the limit order book? || The set of resting limit orders on each side. The bid side is ordered from highest price to lowest, and the ask side from lowest to highest (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c064 L04 <NOGRADE> | What is price-time priority? || An incoming order meets the best price on the opposite side first. Among resting orders at that price it meets the earliest arrival first. Share size does not affect priority (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
FC1-c065 L04 <NOGRADE> | When is the book crossed? || The book is crossed when the best bid is at least the best ask (Definition 2.1). || <NOBASIS>
