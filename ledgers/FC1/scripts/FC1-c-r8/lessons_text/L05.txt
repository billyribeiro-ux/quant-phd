  21: A trade is not finished when the two sides agree on a price. Between execution and the transfer of cash and instruments, the terms are confirmed, the obligations are computed, and, where a central counterparty (CCP, defined in Definition 1.1) is used, the trade is replaced by two trades with that counterparty. This lesson defines clearing and settlement and the trade lifecycle. It proves two results about netting and novation: what they conserve, and the bound they give on payments. Lab 1 traces one seeded trade through the lifecycle and nets a seeded batch. The lab's settlement lag is a parameter, and the lesson states no settlement period for any market.
  22: 1Clearing, settlement and the lifecycle
  23: Definition 1.1 (clearing and settlement)
  24: Clearing and settlement are the two steps after a trade is executed. Clearing fixes the trade's terms and computes what each party owes. It confirms the quantity, price, instrument and counterparties. Where a central counterparty (CCP) is used, clearing also includes novation: the trade between the two original parties is replaced by two trades, one between the buyer and the CCP and one between the seller and the CCP. In this lesson the CCP is a financial intermediary that becomes the buyer to the seller and the seller to the buyer for each trade it clears. Settlement is the transfer of cash and instruments that discharges the obligations clearing has fixed. Netting, the subject of Section 2 of this lesson, replaces the gross amounts of a batch with each participant's net position.
  25: Definition 1.2 (trade lifecycle)
  26: The trade lifecycle is the ordered sequence of states a trade passes through from order entry to settlement. This lesson uses six states, in this order: order entry, when an order arrives; matching, when a buy order is paired with a sell order at a price; trade capture, when both sides record the same terms; clearing; settlement; and position update, when each side's holding changes. A business day is a Monday to Friday, and no holiday calendar is used. Each state carries a business-day offset: the number of business days after the trade date, which has offset 0.
  27: Matching follows the price-time rule of Lesson L04: the best price is filled first, and among orders at one price the earliest arrival is filled first. The trade takes the resting order's price, not the incoming order's limit. Offsets are fixed by the model, not measured. The model takes the offsets to be non-decreasing along the six states. The lab prints offsets_nondecreasing to check this for its parameters, and a clearing offset larger than the settlement lag makes that check print False.
  28: Remark 1.3 (the six states are a model)
  29: The six states are the lesson's model of a trade. They are not a description of any venue or clearing house, and the lesson states no timing fact for any venue. The clearing offset and the settlement lag used below are parameters that the lab sets.
  30: 2What netting and novation conserve
  31: Fix a finite batch of \(n\) trades. Trade \(i\) has one buyer and one seller, both among a finite set \(J\) of participants. Its quantity is a positive integer \(q_i\) of shares, its price is a positive integer \(p_i\) of cents per share, and its consideration is \(c_i = q_i p_i\) cents. Participant \(j\) has receipts \(\mathrm{Rec}_j\), the sum of the \(c_i\) for trades in which \(j\) is the seller, and payments \(\mathrm{Pay}_j\), the sum of the \(c_i\) for trades in which \(j\) is the buyer. Its net position is \(x_j = \mathrm{Rec}_j - \mathrm{Pay}_j\). The gross payments of the batch are \(G = \sum_i c_i\), and the net payments are \(\mathrm{NP} = \sum_{j} \max(-x_j, 0)\).
  32: Proposition 2.1 (netting conserves cash and bounds net payments)
  33: Assumptions. A1: in each trade the buyer and the seller are different participants, and \(q_i\) and \(p_i\) are positive integers. Under A1, (i) \(\sum_{j} x_j = 0\), and (ii) \(0 \le \mathrm{NP} \le G\).
  34: Proof
  35: (i) Trade \(i\) adds \(c_i\) to the seller's receipts and \(c_i\) to the buyer's payments. Its effect on the sum of the net positions is \(c_i - c_i = 0\). The sum starts at zero and each trade adds zero, so \(\sum_j x_j = \sum_i (c_i - c_i) = 0\).
  36: (ii) The term \(\max(-x_j, 0)\) is non-negative, so \(\mathrm{NP} \ge 0\). By A1 every \(c_i\) is non-negative, so \(\mathrm{Rec}_j \ge 0\) and \(\mathrm{Pay}_j \ge 0\). For each \(j\), \(-x_j = \mathrm{Pay}_j - \mathrm{Rec}_j \le \mathrm{Pay}_j\), and since \(\mathrm{Pay}_j \ge 0\), \(\max(-x_j, 0) \le \mathrm{Pay}_j\). Summing over \(j\) gives \(\mathrm{NP} \le \sum_j \mathrm{Pay}_j\). Each trade's consideration is paid by exactly one buyer, so \(\sum_j \mathrm{Pay}_j = \sum_i c_i = G\). \(\blacksquare\)
  37: Proposition 2.2 (novation gives the CCP a zero net cash position)
  38: Assumptions. A1 as in Proposition 2.1, and A2: every trade of the batch is novated to the CCP, so that the CCP is the buyer in one leg and the seller in another, with the same quantity and consideration; the CCP charges no fee; and the CCP is party to no trade outside the batch. Under A1 and A2, the CCP's net cash over the batch is zero, and participant \(j\)'s net position with the CCP is \(x_j\): the CCP owes \(j\) the amount \(x_j\) when \(x_j > 0\), and \(j\) owes the CCP \(-x_j\) when \(x_j < 0\).
  39: Proof
  40: Under A2 the CCP receives \(c_i\) from the buyer of trade \(i\) and pays \(c_i\) to its seller. Summed over the batch, the CCP receives \(G\) and pays \(G\), so its net cash is \(G - G = 0\). Participant \(j\)'s legs with the CCP are the same amounts it paid or received in the original trades, so its net position with the CCP is \(\mathrm{Rec}_j - \mathrm{Pay}_j = x_j\), the net amount the CCP owes \(j\). \(\blacksquare\)
  41: Remark 2.3 (what the two propositions do not say)
  42: Proposition 2.1(i) and Proposition 2.2 are identities about obligations, and Proposition 2.1(ii) is a bound on them. Neither says that a participant makes its payment. Proposition 2.1 bounds the net payments between zero and the gross payments, and Proposition 2.2 gives the CCP's net cash in the model as zero. Whether each payment is made is a separate question. The model has no fees, no margin and no default, and it states no rule for what happens when a payment fails.

  86: seller: str

  89: @property
  90: def consideration_cents(self) -> int:

  93: def business_day_after(start: datetime.date, offset: int) -> datetime.date:
  94: """The date offset business days after start, counting Monday to Friday. An offset of 0 returns start.
  95: Raises ValueError for a negative offset, because no state of a trade precedes its trade date.
  96: """
  97: if offset < 0:

 106: def match(incoming: Order, resting: Sequence[Order]) -> list[tuple[Order, int]]:
 107: """Match an incoming buy order against resting sell orders: best price first, then earliest arrival.
 108: Returns (resting order, quantity) pairs in fill order. A trade takes the resting order's price, not the
 109: incoming limit. Matching stops when the incoming quantity is filled or no eligible resting order is left.
 110: """
 111: if incoming.side != "buy":
 112: raise ValueError("this lab matches an incoming buy order")
 113: eligible = sorted(
 114: (o for o in resting if o.side == "sell" and o.price_cents <= incoming.price_cents),
 115: key=lambda o: (o.price_cents, o.time),
 116: )
 117: fills: list[tuple[Order, int]] = []
 118: remaining = incoming.quantity
 119: for order in eligible:
 120: if remaining == 0:
 121: break
 122: filled = min(remaining, order.quantity)
 123: fills.append((order, filled))
 124: remaining -= filled
 125: return fills

 151: def seeded_batch(rng: random.Random) -> list[Trade]:
 152: """BATCH_SIZE trades, each between two different participants at a seeded price and quantity.

 167: def net_positions(trades: Sequence[Trade]) -> dict[str, int]:
 168: """Each participant's net cash in integer cents: consideration received as seller minus paid as buyer.
 169: Every trade adds its consideration to the seller and subtracts the same amount from the buyer, so the
 170: positions sum to zero.
 171: """
 172: net = dict.fromkeys(PARTICIPANTS, 0)
 173: for trade in trades:
 174: net[trade.seller] += trade.consideration_cents
 175: net[trade.buyer] -= trade.consideration_cents
 176: return net
 177: def ratio_milli_half_up(numerator: int, denominator: int) -> int:
 178: """The quotient numerator / denominator in thousandths, rounded half up, computed in integers.
 179: The result is floor(1000 * numerator / denominator + 1/2). Raises ValueError for a non-positive denominator
 180: or a negative numerator.
 181: """

 183: raise ValueError("the ratio needs a positive denominator and a non-negative numerator")

 186: """A thousandths integer as a decimal with three digits after the point."""

 287: 4Where the canon breaks
 288: The settlement lag is not a fact. The lab's lag of 2 business days is a parameter. This lesson states no settlement period for any market, because no settlement-cycle source is yet admitted to the course's reference list.
 289: Netting identities concern obligations. Proposition 2.1 is an identity and a bound about obligations. Remark 2.3 explains why it says nothing about payments that fail.
 290: A zero CCP position is conditional. Proposition 2.2 needs A2, which excludes fees and trades outside the batch. The model has no margin and no default. This lesson states no finding on whether central clearing reduces counterparty risk, and it reads no evidence on that question.
 291: Matching at the resting price is one rule. The price-time rule of Lesson L04 is one convention. The lesson states no rule for any other venue.
 292: The curriculum lists Harris, Trading and Exchanges, among the sources of this unit, and the registry holds that book under the year 2002. This lesson quotes no passage from it and states no finding from it.
 293: 5Misconceptions
 294: “Settlement happens when the trade is matched.”
 295: In Lab 1, matching is at offset 0 and settlement is at offset 2, Friday 2026-10-30. Matching fixes the price and the quantity. Settlement moves the cash and the instruments later. The gap is the lab's parameter SETTLEMENT_LAG, not a fact about any market.
 296: “Netting cancels the payments, so a batch nets to zero cash.”
 297: Proposition 2.1 proves that the net positions sum to zero. It does not prove that net payments are zero. Lab 1 prints 14282414 cents of net payments against 68398886 cents of gross payments. The zero sum is a property of the positions, not of the payments.
 298: “A CCP with a zero net position carries no risk.”
 299: Proposition 2.2 gives the CCP a zero net cash position under A2, which assumes no fee and no trade outside the batch. The model has no margin and no default. The zero is an identity of the model, and it says nothing about whether a participant pays. A zero net position is not a measure of exposure.
 300: “A trade is struck at the buyer's limit price.”
 301: In Lab 1 the incoming buy has a limit of 10117 cents, and the one trade is at 10060 cents, the resting sell's price. The limit is a ceiling the buyer will accept. It is not the price paid.

 304: Mechanics
 305: M1 · One fill at the resting price
 306: A buy order for 100 shares arrives with a limit of 10120 cents per share. It meets one resting sell order of 300 shares at 10090 cents per share, the only resting order at or below the limit. Under the matching rule of this lesson, what are the trade quantity, the trade price and the consideration?
 307: Solution
 308: The incoming quantity is 100 and the resting quantity is 300, so the fill is 100 shares. The trade takes the resting price, 10090 cents, not the limit of 10120 cents. The consideration is \(100 \times 10090 = 1009000\) cents, which is 10090.00 currency units.
 309: M2 · Settlement across a weekend
 310: A trade is executed on Friday 2026-10-30. The settlement lag is 2 business days, counting Monday to Friday with no holidays. On what date is settlement due?
 311: Solution
 312: Saturday and Sunday are not business days. The first business day after Friday is Monday 2026-11-02, which is offset 1, and the second is Tuesday 2026-11-03, which is offset 2. Settlement is due on Tuesday 2026-11-03. The same rule gives Friday 2026-10-30 for Lab 1's trade of Wednesday 2026-10-28, because Thursday is offset 1 and Friday is offset 2.
 313: M3 · Netting two linked trades
 314: Participant A buys 100 shares from B at 10000 cents per share. Participant B buys 100 shares from C at 10000 cents per share. Compute the gross payments, the net payments and the net-to-gross ratio, and state the basis of the ratio.
 315: Solution
 316: Each trade has consideration \(100 \times 10000 = 1000000\) cents, so \(G = 2000000\) cents. A pays 1000000 and receives nothing, so \(x_A = -1000000\). B receives 1000000 from A and pays 1000000 to C, so \(x_B = 0\). C receives 1000000, so \(x_C = +1000000\). The positions sum to zero, as Proposition 2.1 requires. The net payments are \(\mathrm{NP} = 1000000\) cents. The ratio is \(1000000 / 2000000 = 0.500\), on the gross basis.
 317: Proof
 318: P1 · Netting saves nothing when no one nets
 319: Under A1, suppose each participant is either only a buyer or only a seller in the batch. Prove that \(\mathrm{NP} = G\).
 320: Solution
 321: A buyer \(j\) has \(\mathrm{Rec}_j = 0\), so \(x_j = -\mathrm{Pay}_j \le 0\) and \(\max(-x_j, 0) = \mathrm{Pay}_j\). A seller has \(\mathrm{Pay}_j = 0\), so \(x_j = \mathrm{Rec}_j \ge 0\) and \(\max(-x_j, 0) = 0\). Summing over \(j\), \(\mathrm{NP} = \sum_{\text{buyers}} \mathrm{Pay}_j\). Each trade's consideration is paid by its buyer, so this sum is \(G\). Under this condition the net payments equal the gross payments.
 322: P2 · Removing one trade moves net payments by at most its consideration
 323: Under A1, let a batch contain a trade \(t\) with consideration \(c_t\), and let \(B'\) be the batch without \(t\). Prove that \(|\mathrm{NP}(B) - \mathrm{NP}(B')| \le c_t\).
 324: Solution
 325: Removing \(t\) raises the buyer's position by \(c_t\) and lowers the seller's position by \(c_t\), and leaves every other position unchanged. Let \(f(x) = \max(-x, 0)\). The change in net payments is \([f(x_b + c_t) - f(x_b)] + [f(x_s - c_t) - f(x_s)]\), where \(b\) is the buyer and \(s\) the seller. The function \(f\) is non-increasing and changes by at most the size of its argument's change. So the first bracket lies in \([-c_t, 0]\) and the second lies in \([0, c_t]\). Their sum therefore lies in \([-c_t, c_t]\).
 326: P3 · One novated trade
 327: A single trade of consideration \(c\) is novated to a CCP under A2. Show that the CCP's net cash is zero, and that the buyer's and the seller's net positions with the CCP are \(-c\) and \(+c\), with positive meaning the CCP owes the participant.
 328: Solution
 329: The CCP receives \(c\) from the buyer and pays \(c\) to the seller, so its net cash is \(c - c = 0\). The buyer's leg with the CCP has the buyer paying \(c\), so its net position with the CCP is \(-c\). The seller's leg has the CCP paying \(c\) to the seller, so the seller's net position with the CCP is \(+c\). These are the values Proposition 2.2 gives for a batch of one trade.

 348: R2 · Where the sign assumption fails
 349: Proposition 2.1 uses A1, which makes every consideration non-negative. Show by example what happens to the bound \(\mathrm{NP} \le G\) if one trade is allowed a negative price.
 350: Solution
 351: Let one trade have buyer A, seller B and consideration \(c = -100\) cents. Then A's position is \(x_A = -c = +100\) and B's is \(x_B = c = -100\). The net payments are \(\max(-x_B, 0) = 100\), while the gross payments are \(G = c = -100\). The bound then reads \(100 \le -100\), which is false. The argument breaks at the step \(\mathrm{Pay}_j \ge 0\), which needs the sign of each consideration.
