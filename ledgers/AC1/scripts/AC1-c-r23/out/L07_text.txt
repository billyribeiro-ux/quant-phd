20       This lesson studies an equity-settled grant, in which the firm pays the employee in its own shares rather than cash. This lesson fixes one model of an equity grant: a total cost set at the grant date, an expense spread over the years of required service, a matching credit to equity, and no forfeitures (an award the employee loses before the required service is complete). Inside that model, Section 2 proves the identities that link the statements. The model leaves out the measurement and recognition rules for awards, and this lesson does not state them.
21       1A grant as a schedule
22       Amounts are in integer cents of SCU (simulated currency units), and the worked examples write them with a dollar sign, which here means SCU. Three terms are used in this section and the next. Vesting is the period in which the employee must remain in service before the award is theirs. The grant-date fair value is the value of one share award on the grant date, which the model takes as an input and does not derive. Additional paid-in capital (APIC) is the equity account that this model credits for awards.
23         Definition 1.1 (Equity-settled grant, model)
24         A grant \(g\) has a whole number of shares \(n_g \ge 0\), a grant-date fair value per share \(f_g \ge 0\) in integer cents, a whole number of vesting years \(v_g \ge 1\), and a grant year \(y_g\). Its total cost is \(C_g = n_g f_g\). The stock-based compensation expense of \(g\) in fiscal year \(t\) is
25         \[
26           e_{g,t} =
27           \begin{cases}
28             \lfloor C_g / v_g \rfloor, & y_g \le t \le y_g + v_g - 2, \\
29             C_g - (v_g - 1)\lfloor C_g / v_g \rfloor, & t = y_g + v_g - 1, \\
30             0, & \text{otherwise.}
31           \end{cases}
32         \]
33         The first case gives the base amount for each vesting year before the last. The second gives the last vesting year the remainder, so the schedule is exact in cents. Stock-based compensation in year \(t\) is \(S_t = \sum_g e_{g,t}\), summed over all grants.
34         Definition 1.2 (Model assumptions)
35         The model has five assumptions. (i) The total cost \(C_g\) is fixed on the grant date. (ii) No award is forfeited, that is, lost by the employee before it vests. (iii) Vesting has no performance or market conditions, meaning no vesting test tied to a company target or to a share price. (iv) Income tax is ignored in this lesson; L08 models tax on depreciation differences, not on these grants. (v) Shares issued at vesting are not counted, so the model says nothing about dilution, which is a fall in each existing holder's proportionate ownership when new shares are issued. These are the model's choices. They are not claims about any real award.
36         Theorem 1.3 (Schedule totals)
37         Under Definition 1.1, for every grant \(g\), \(\sum_{t} e_{g,t} = C_g\), where the sum runs over all fiscal years.
38       Proof
39         Let \(v = v_g\), \(b = \lfloor C_g / v \rfloor\) and \(y = y_g\). For \(v = 1\) there are no base years, and the only nonzero term is \(e_{g,y} = C_g - 0 = C_g\). For \(v \ge 2\), the years \(y\) to \(y + v - 2\) are \(v - 1\) years, each with expense \(b\), and year \(y + v - 1\) has \(C_g - (v - 1)b\). Outside these \(v\) years every term is \(0\). The sum is \((v - 1)b + C_g - (v - 1)b = C_g\). \(\blacksquare\)
40         Example 1.4 (One grant)
41         A grant of \(n_g = 1{,}000\) shares at \(f_g = \$12.00\), that is \(1{,}200\) cents, has \(C_g = 1{,}200{,}000\) cents, or $12,000. With \(y_g = 1\) and \(v_g = 4\), the base amount is \(\lfloor 1{,}200{,}000 / 4 \rfloor = 300{,}000\) cents, or $3,000. The expense is $3,000 in each of years 1 to 4 and $0 after. The four amounts sum to $12,000, as Theorem 1.3 requires.
42       2Where the expense lands in the statements
43       Let \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) be operating income before stock-based compensation, in SCU (simulated currency units), let \(\mathrm{NI}_t\) be net income, \(\mathrm{CFO}_t\) be operating cash flow, and \(\mathrm{RE}_t\) be retained earnings. Under the model:
44       \[
45         \mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t, \qquad
46         \mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t, \qquad
47         \Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t, \qquad
48         \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t = S_t, \qquad
49         \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t,
50       \]
51       The cash-flow line is the add-back of a non-cash expense, and the model has no other cash flows, so cash changes only through \(\mathrm{CFO}\). Assets are cash alone in this model, and equity is APIC plus retained earnings, so \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t\).
52         Theorem 2.1 (Articulation under the model)
53         Under the model (Definition 1.2 and the display above, with \(S_t\) the sum of the schedules of Definition 1.1), for every fiscal year \(t\): (a) \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (b) \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (c) \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\); (d) \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_t = \Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\).
54       Proof
55         (a) \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t = (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t) + S_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\). (b) \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (a). (c) \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = S_t + (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t) = \mathrm{OI}_t\). (d) \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_t = \Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (b), and \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) by (c). \(\blacksquare\)
56         Corollary 2.2 (Expense moves income and APIC, not equity)
57         Under the model, holding \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) fixed, a change in \(S_t\) moves \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_t\) (which equals \(\mathrm{NI}_t\)) and \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t\) in opposite directions by the same amount, and leaves \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) unchanged.
58       Proof
59         Holding \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) fixed, \(\partial \mathrm{NI}_t / \partial S_t = -1\) and \(\partial \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t / \partial S_t = +1\). Theorem 2.1 (b) and (c) give \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t\) equal to \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) for every value of \(S_t\). \(\blacksquare\)
60         Example 2.3 (Example 1.4 continued)
61         Let \(\mathrm{OI}_1 = \$50{,}000\), so \(S_1 = \$3{,}000\) from Example 1.4. Then \(\mathrm{NI}_1 = \$47{,}000\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_1 = \$50{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_1 = \$50{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_1 = \$3{,}000\), \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_1 = \$47{,}000\), and \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_1 = \$50{,}000\), which equals \(\Delta\mathrm{Assets}_1\).

65         """Lab AC1-L07-1: stock-based compensation schedules on generated grants.
66 Every grant and every operating income figure here is SIMULATED: a seeded generator
67 draws them, no company is represented, and no output estimates any real firm. Money
68 is in integer cents of simulated currency units (SCU). The model has no income tax,
69 no forfeitures, no performance conditions and no share issuance at vesting, so the
70 lab's checks are the schedule sums and the Section 2 identities.
71 """
72 from __future__ import annotations
73 import random
74 from dataclasses import dataclass
75 SEED = 20260701
76 N_GRANTS = 4
77 HORIZON = 6  # fiscal years 1 to 6; the latest vesting year any grant can have is 3 + 4 - 1 = 6
78 @dataclass(frozen=True)
79 class Grant:
80     """One SIMULATED equity grant. Money in cents; shares as a count."""
81     grant_year: int
82     shares: int
83     fair_value_cents: int  # grant-date fair value per share, in cents
84     vest_years: int
85     @property
86     def value_cents(self) -> int:
87         """Total grant value in cents: shares times grant-date fair value per share."""
88         return self.shares * self.fair_value_cents
89 def yearly_expense(grant: Grant, year: int) -> int:
90     """Expense recognized for one grant in one fiscal year, in integer cents.
91     Straight-line over vest_years, starting in grant_year. Integer division gives the
92     base amount for each vesting year; the final vesting year takes the remainder, so
93     the schedule sums to value_cents exactly. Years outside the vesting period give 0.
94     """
95     index = year - grant.grant_year
96     if index < 0 or index >= grant.vest_years:
97         return 0
98     base = grant.value_cents // grant.vest_years
99     if index == grant.vest_years - 1:
100         return grant.value_cents - base * (grant.vest_years - 1)
101     return base
102 def generate_grants(rng: random.Random) -> list[Grant]:
103     """Draw N_GRANTS SIMULATED grants: 100 to 5,000 shares, $5.00 to $40.00 per share, 3 or 4 vesting years."""
104     grants: list[Grant] = []
105     for _ in range(N_GRANTS):
106         grant_year = rng.randint(1, 3)
107         shares = rng.randrange(100, 5001, 100)
108         fair_value_cents = rng.randint(500, 4000)
109         vest_years = rng.choice((3, 4))
110         grants.append(Grant(grant_year, shares, fair_value_cents, vest_years))
111     return grants
112 def cents_text(cents: int) -> str:
113     """Format integer cents as SCU dollars and cents, for example 1234567 gives 12,345.67."""
114     sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
115     whole, frac = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
116     return f"{sign}{whole:,}.{frac:02d}"
117 def main() -> None:
118     """Draw the grants and operating income, schedule the expense, and check the identities."""
119     rng = random.Random(SEED)
120     grants = generate_grants(rng)
121     operating_income = [rng.randrange(40_000_000, 90_000_001, 100) for _ in range(HORIZON)]
122     print(f"SIMULATED grants (seed {SEED}): grant year, shares, fair value per share, vest years, total SCU")
123     for number, grant in enumerate(grants, start=1):
124         print(
125             f"G{number}  grant_year={grant.grant_year}  shares={grant.shares:,}  "
126             f"fair_value={cents_text(grant.fair_value_cents)}  vest_years={grant.vest_years}  "
127             f"total={cents_text(grant.value_cents)}"
128         )
129     schedule_ok = 0
130     for grant in grants:
131         expensed = sum(yearly_expense(grant, year) for year in range(1, HORIZON + 1))
132         schedule_ok += expensed == grant.value_cents
133     sbc = [sum(yearly_expense(g, year) for g in grants) for year in range(1, HORIZON + 1)]
134     print("SIMULATED statements, SCU; years 1 to 6 (operating income is before SBC, no tax):")
135     print("year  operating_income  sbc_expense  net_income  cfo  apic_change  cash_change")
136     apic_ok = 0
137     cash_ok = 0
138     balance_ok = 0
139     cfo_ok = 0
140     for index, year in enumerate(range(1, HORIZON + 1)):
141         oi = operating_income[index]
142         expense = sbc[index]
143         net_income = oi - expense
144         cfo = net_income + expense
145         apic_change = expense
146         retained_change = net_income
147         cash_change = cfo
148         equity_change = apic_change + retained_change
149         apic_ok += apic_change == expense
150         cash_ok += cash_change == oi
151         balance_ok += cash_change == equity_change
152         cfo_ok += cfo == net_income + expense
153         print(
154             f"{year:>4}  {cents_text(oi):>16}  {cents_text(expense):>11}  {cents_text(net_income):>10}  "
155             f"{cents_text(cfo):>9}  {cents_text(apic_change):>11}  {cents_text(cash_change):>11}"
156         )
157     print(f"schedule sums equal grant totals: {schedule_ok}/{N_GRANTS}")
158     print(f"CFO equals net income plus SBC in every year: {cfo_ok}/{HORIZON}")
159     print(f"cash change equals operating income in every year: {cash_ok}/{HORIZON}")
160     print(f"assets change equals equity change in every year: {balance_ok}/{HORIZON}")
161     print(f"APIC change equals SBC expense in every year: {apic_ok}/{HORIZON}")
162     assert schedule_ok == N_GRANTS
163     assert cfo_ok == cash_ok == balance_ok == apic_ok == HORIZON

184         3Where the canon breaks
185         The model fixes the total cost on the grant date and spreads it straight-line. It has no forfeitures, no performance or market conditions, and no valuation method for the fair value. Each of these is outside the model, so the schedule above does not describe a plan that has one. Theorem 1.3 uses the fixed-total assumption (Definition 1.2 (i)): its proof takes one \(C_g\) for every year, so a schedule that depends on later share prices falls outside it. This lesson does not state measurement or recognition rules for these features. They are not stated on this page.
186         The model covers equity-settled grants only. Its credit side is APIC, so Theorem 2.1 (c) makes no claim about a grant that the firm settles in cash. The model also says nothing about dilution, because it does not count the shares issued at vesting.
187         4Misconceptions
188           “Stock compensation costs the firm nothing, because no cash leaves the firm.”
189             No cash leaves, so \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t\) is unchanged by \(S_t\). But \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\) falls by the expense, and the credit to APIC offsets that fall, so total equity does not change. Holding operating income fixed, Corollary 2.2 states that offset and that cash and equity are unchanged. The table in Lab 1 shows net income equal to operating income less the expense in every year.
190           “Adding stock compensation back in the cash-flow statement means the expense is not a real cost.”
191             The add-back reverses a non-cash expense inside operating cash flow, so \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t\) in Theorem 2.1 (a). Net income still carries the expense. If the add-back were omitted, operating cash flow would equal net income, \(\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), which understates cash generation by the expense (proof P2 in the problem set).
192           “Once a grant is made, its cost can be recognized in whatever years the firm chooses.”
193             Under Definition 1.1 the schedule is fixed by the grant year, the vesting years and the total cost, and Theorem 1.3 gives the total. Example 1.4 and the grant schedules in Lab 1 each sum to the grant's total. Every yearly amount is nonnegative, and the amounts sum to the total (Theorem 1.3), so no year takes more than the total. The base \(\lfloor C_g / v_g \rfloor\) is nonnegative, and the last year's remainder is at least \(C_g / v_g\), so both are nonnegative when \(n_g, f_g \ge 0\).
194         5Problem set
195         Mechanics
196           M1 · A three-year grant
197             A grant of 500 shares at $8.00 per share has grant year 2 and vests over 3 years. Give \(e_{g,t}\) for \(t = 1, \dots, 5\), and check the total.
198             Solution
199               \(C_g = 500 \times 800 = 400{,}000\) cents, or $4,000. The base amount is \(\lfloor 400{,}000 / 3 \rfloor = 133{,}333\) cents. Years 2 and 3 take 133,333 cents each. Year 4 takes \(400{,}000 - 2 \times 133{,}333 = 133{,}334\) cents. So \(e_{g,1} = 0\), \(e_{g,2} = \$1{,}333.33\), \(e_{g,3} = \$1{,}333.33\), \(e_{g,4} = \$1{,}333.34\), and \(e_{g,5} = 0\). The total is $4,000.00, as Theorem 1.3 requires.
200           M2 · Change the vesting
201             Keep M1 except that the grant vests over 4 years. Give the schedule.
202             Solution
203               The base amount is \(\lfloor 400{,}000 / 4 \rfloor = 100{,}000\) cents, or $1,000.00. Years 2 to 4 take the base. Year 5 takes \(400{,}000 - 3 \times 100{,}000 = 100{,}000\) cents, which is also $1,000.00. So the schedule is $1,000.00 in each of years 2 to 5, zero otherwise, and the total is $4,000.00.
204           M3 · Net income and APIC
205             For the grant in M1 alone, operating income before stock-based compensation is $20,000 in year 2. Give \(\mathrm{NI}_2\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_2\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_2\), and check that they satisfy Theorem 2.1 (c).
206             Solution
207               \(S_2 = \$1{,}333.33\). Then \(\mathrm{NI}_2 = 20{,}000.00 - 1{,}333.33 = \$18{,}666.67\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_2 = \$20{,}000.00\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2 = \$1{,}333.33\), and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_2 = \$18{,}666.67\). The sum \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_2 + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_2 = \$20{,}000.00 = \mathrm{OI}_2\), as (c) requires.
208         Proof
209           P1 · One-year vesting
210             Prove Theorem 1.3 for \(v_g = 1\) directly from Definition 1.1, without the general argument.
211             Solution
212               With \(v_g = 1\) the first case covers \(y_g \le t \le y_g - 1\), which is empty. The second case covers \(t = y_g\) and gives \(C_g - 0 \cdot \lfloor C_g / 1 \rfloor = C_g\). The third case covers every other year and gives 0. So the only nonzero term is \(e_{g,y_g} = C_g\), and the sum is \(C_g\).
213           P2 · The omitted add-back
214             Suppose a firm reports operating cash flow as net income, with no add-back of \(S_t\). Show that its cash flow is lower than \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) by \(S_t\), and that its change in cash is then lower than \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) by the same amount.
215             Solution
216               Reported operating cash flow would be \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), which is \(S_t\) below \(\mathrm{OI}_t\). The change in cash follows the reported flow, so it is \(S_t\) below \(\mathrm{OI}_t\) too. But the true cash change is \(\mathrm{OI}_t\), since no cash moves for \(S_t\). So the error is exactly \(S_t\).
217           P3 · Equity does not depend on the expense
218             Show that \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t\) is the same for every value of \(S_t\), given \(\mathrm{OI}_t\).
219             Solution
220               \(\Delta\mathrm{Equity}_t = \Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t + \Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = S_t + (\mathrm{OI}_t - S_t)\). The \(S_t\) terms cancel, leaving \(\mathrm{OI}_t\). The expression does not depend on \(S_t\), which is Corollary 2.2.

234         Research
235           R1 · Add forfeitures
236             Extend Definition 1.2 so that a fraction \(\phi\) of each grant is forfeited in its vesting year. State which identity of Theorem 2.1 still holds, which fails, and what you must specify to restore the schedule's total.
237             Solution
238               Theorem 2.1 holds for any \(S_t\), so (a) to (d) still hold, because their proofs use the Section 2 display: \(\mathrm{NI}_t = \mathrm{OI}_t - S_t\), \(\mathrm{CFO}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t + S_t\), \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_t = \mathrm{CFO}_t\), \(\Delta\mathrm{APIC}_t = S_t\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{RE}_t = \mathrm{NI}_t\), the statement that assets are cash alone, and the definition of equity as APIC plus RE. The schedule total no longer equals \(C_g\) if the forfeited part is no longer expensed. To restore a total, you must specify the reversal rule: which year absorbs a reversal of expense already recognized, and whether forfeiture reverses prior expense or only reduces future expense. The model does not choose between these, and the lesson does not state a reversal rule.
239           R2 · Equity grant against a cash bonus
240             An equity-settled grant worth \(C_g\) vests in one year, in year \(y\). Under the model, give the effect of the grant on cash, net income and APIC in year \(y\), and say whether the model computes the number of shares the grant issues.
241             Solution
242               The one-year grant is expensed in full in year \(y\): Definition 1.1 with \(v_g = 1\) gives \(e_{g,y} = C_g\) (proof P1). So \(S_y\) rises by \(C_g\), and by the Section 2 display net income falls by \(C_g\) and APIC rises by \(C_g\). Cash does not change. Corollary 2.2 states that a change in \(S_y\) leaves \(\Delta\mathrm{Cash}_y\) unchanged, because cash moves only through \(\mathrm{CFO}_y = \mathrm{OI}_y\). The model does not compute the share count. Definition 1.2 (v) leaves the shares issued at vesting out of the model, so this lesson gives no number of shares for the grant and makes no dilution comparison.
243           R3 · A schedule that tracks the share price
244             Design the smallest change to Definition 1.1 that makes the grant's total depend on the later share price. State which assumption of Definition 1.2 you break, and what Theorem 1.3 becomes.
245             Solution
246               Let \(C_g\) be replaced by a function of the share price at each year end, so \(C_{g,t}\) changes. This breaks assumption (i), that \(C_g\) is fixed on the grant date. Theorem 1.3 then no longer follows from Definition 1.1, whose proof takes one \(C_g\) for every year. The schedule would have to be restated each year, and the model does not define that restatement, so the lesson does not state a total for it.
