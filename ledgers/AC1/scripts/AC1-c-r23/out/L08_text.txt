20       Tax expense and tax paid in cash can differ in timing. The gap is carried as a deferred tax balance, defined in Section 1. The model has one asset, one book schedule, one tax schedule and one flat rate. The current tax (Section 2) and the total tax expense are exact in integer cents when pretax book income (\(P_t\), defined in Section 2) is a multiple of 100 cents. The deferred expense and the deferred balance are exact for every \(P_t\), because Definition 1.1 makes the depreciation amounts multiples of 100 cents, and the cumulative difference of Definition 1.2 is a sum of them. The model states no rule for when a deferred tax asset may be recognized.
21       1Carrying amounts, tax bases and the temporary difference
22       Two amounts for the same asset can differ. The carrying amount is the book value on the balance sheet: cost less book depreciation to date. The tax base is the amount that the model's tax schedule leaves to be deducted: cost less the tax depreciation to date (Definition 1.1). Their difference is the temporary difference, and in this model it is zero at the end of the asset's life (the proof of Theorem 2.1 (c) gives this). Amounts are in integer cents of SCU (simulated currency units). The worked examples write the same amounts with a dollar sign, which here means SCU. Definition 1.1 makes \(C\), \(b_t\) and \(\tau_t\) whole numbers of cents.
23         Definition 1.1 (Model asset and schedules)
24         An asset has cost \(C\), a multiple of 10,000 cents, and a book life of \(L\) years, where \(C / L\) is a multiple of 100 cents. Book depreciation is straight-line, \(b = C / L\) in each year \(t = 1, \dots, L\). Tax depreciation in year \(t\) is \(\tau_t = C\,p_t / 100\), where the integers \(p_1, \dots, p_L\) are the tax percentages and sum to 100. The model's flat tax rate is a whole number of percent, \(r\), the same in every year. The tax schedule is an input to the model. This lesson does not describe any tax code.
25         Definition 1.2 (Temporary difference, deferred tax balance)
26         Let \(K_t = \sum_{s \le t} (\tau_s - b_s)\), with \(K_0 = 0\). This is the cumulative temporary difference, which Theorem 1.3 identifies with carrying amount minus tax base. The deferred tax balance at the end of year \(t\) is \(\mathrm{DTL}_t = r K_t / 100\). In this model, a positive balance is a liability, and a negative balance is an asset. The schedule in Example 1.4 gives no negative balance, so it has no asset: its cumulative tax percentages, 40, 70, 90, 100 and 100, never fall below the cumulative book percentages, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100.
27         Theorem 1.3 (Difference of the two carrying amounts)
28         Under Definition 1.1, at the end of every year \(t\), carrying amount minus tax base equals \(K_t\).
29       Proof
30         Carrying amount is \(C - \sum_{s \le t} b_s\) and tax base is \(C - \sum_{s \le t} \tau_s\). Their difference is \(\sum_{s \le t} \tau_s - \sum_{s \le t} b_s = \sum_{s \le t}(\tau_s - b_s) = K_t\). \(\blacksquare\)
31         Example 1.4 (A worked asset)
32         Let \(C = \$100{,}000\), \(L = 5\), tax percentages \((40, 30, 20, 10, 0)\), and \(r = 21\). Then \(b = \$20{,}000\) each year. The tax depreciation is $40,000, $30,000, $20,000, $10,000 and $0. The yearly differences \(\tau_t - b_t\) are $20,000, $10,000, $0, \(-\$10{,}000\) and \(-\$20{,}000\). So \(K_t\) is $20,000, $30,000, $30,000, $20,000 and $0. At \(r = 21\), the deferred balance \(\mathrm{DTL}_t\) is $4,200, $6,300, $6,300, $4,200 and $0.
33       2Splitting tax expense into current and deferred parts
34       Let \(P_t\) be pretax book income, that is income before tax and after book depreciation. Taxable income in the model is \(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t)\), where \(\tau_t - b_t\) is the year's change in the temporary difference. Define:
35       \[
36         \mathrm{CT}_t = \frac{r\,(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t))}{100}, \qquad
37         \mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}, \qquad
38         \mathrm{TE}_t = \mathrm{CT}_t + \mathrm{DE}_t,
39       \]
40       where \(\mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\). \(\mathrm{CT}_t\) is the current tax, the tax on taxable income. \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) is the deferred tax expense, and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\) is total tax expense. For \(\mathrm{CT}_t\) and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\) to be integer numbers of cents, \(r\,P_t\) must be a multiple of 100; a \(P_t\) that is a multiple of 100 cents is sufficient for every whole-number \(r\). \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) and \(\mathrm{DTL}_t\) need no condition on \(P_t\): under Definition 1.1, \(b_t\), \(\tau_t\) and \(\tau_t - b_t\) are multiples of 100 cents, so \(r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\) and \(r K_t/100\) are whole numbers of cents. The model's cash convention is that the cash tax paid in year \(t\) is \(\mathrm{CT}_t\), so deferred tax expense moves no cash.
41         Theorem 2.1 (Tax identities under a flat rate)
42         Under Definition 1.1 (straight-line book depreciation, tax percentages summing to 100, one flat rate \(r\)) and the definitions of Section 2, for every year \(t\) of the schedule: (a) \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\); (b) \(\mathrm{TE}_t = r P_t / 100\); (c) \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\); (d) \(\sum_{t=1}^{L} \mathrm{DE}_t = 0\).
43       Proof
44         (a) By Theorem 1.3, \(K_t - K_{t-1} = \tau_t - b_t\). Then \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(K_t - K_{t-1})/100 = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\). (b) \(\mathrm{TE}_t = r(P_t - (\tau_t - b_t))/100 + r(\tau_t - b_t)/100 = rP_t/100\), by (a). (c) \(K_L = \sum_{s=1}^{L} \tau_s - \sum_{s=1}^{L} b_s = C - C = 0\), because \(\sum_s p_s = 100\) gives \(\sum_s \tau_s = C\), and \(L\,b = C\). Hence \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\). (d) The differences telescope: \(\sum_t \mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_L - \mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)
45         Example 2.2 (Example 1.4 with pretax income of $50,000)
46         Take \(P_t = \$50{,}000\) in each year. Year 1: \(\mathrm{CT}_1 = 0.21 \times (50{,}000 - 20{,}000) = \$6{,}300\), \(\mathrm{DE}_1 = \$4{,}200\), so \(\mathrm{TE}_1 = \$10{,}500\). Year 2: \(\mathrm{CT}_2 = 0.21 \times 40{,}000 = \$8{,}400\), \(\mathrm{DE}_2 = \$2{,}100\), so \(\mathrm{TE}_2 = \$10{,}500\). Theorem 2.1 (b) gives \(0.21 \times 50{,}000 = \$10{,}500\) in every year, and (d) gives deferred expense summing to zero over the five years.
47       The lab runs one generated asset and pretax income for five years, and checks parts (a) and (b) of Theorem 2.1 in every year, and the balance at the end of year 5 that part (c) requires; it does not check part (d). The asset, the income and the rate of 21 percent are SIMULATED.
48         Lab 1 · Book-tax difference and deferred tax roll-forward on a SIMULATED asset
49         Stated before running: for one SIMULATED asset and generated pretax income, book depreciation and tax depreciation each total the cost, the deferred tax balance is zero at the end of year 5, and in every year the deferred expense equals the rate times that year's difference and the total tax expense equals the rate times pretax income. Expected result: each of the three year-by-year checks (pretax income times the rate is exact, deferred expense equals the rate times the year's difference, and tax expense equals the rate times pretax income) gives 5 of 5. The asset and income are SIMULATED, and the 21 percent rate is a model parameter, not a statement about any jurisdiction.

138         3Where the canon breaks
139         The model uses one flat rate for every year, and Theorem 2.1 (b) depends on that. Proof P3 shows that a rate change adds a term, \((r_t - r_{t-1})K_{t-1}/100\), to total tax expense. A model with changing rates therefore needs the rate of each year, and this lesson does not model a change.
140         Example 1.4 and the lab use the same tax percentages, 40, 30, 20, 10 and 0, so neither has a negative balance and neither shows an asset. A schedule whose cumulative tax percentages fall below the book percentages gives a negative balance, which is an asset in the signed form of Definition 1.2. Whether such an asset may be recognized is outside the model: the model has no income forecast, and this page states no recognition test.
141         The model has one asset and one tax schedule, so it cannot show how many temporary differences a real firm holds at once. Its balance is zero at year \(L\) by Theorem 2.1 (c).
142         4Misconceptions
143           “A deferred tax liability is a debt that will be paid to the tax authority next year.”
144             The balance is the tax on differences that reverse later. Under the cash convention of Section 2, cash moves only as current tax is paid. In Example 2.2 the current tax in year 1 is $6,300, below the total expense of $10,500. In Example 1.4 at 21 percent the balance rises to $6,300 at the end of years 2 and 3, falls to $4,200 at the end of year 4 as the difference reverses, and Theorem 2.1 (c) returns it to $0 at the end of year 5.
145           “Deferred tax expense is a cash payment.”
146             Deferred tax expense is the change in the balance, \(\mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}\), and, under the cash convention of Section 2, it moves no cash. Cash tax is \(\mathrm{CT}_t\). In Example 2.2 the year-1 deferred expense is $4,200, and no cash of that amount is paid in year 1.
147           “Faster tax depreciation raises the tax expense.”
148             Theorem 2.1 (b) gives \(\mathrm{TE}_t = rP_t / 100\), which does not depend on the tax schedule \(\tau_t\). Faster tax depreciation in a year, an extra deduction of \(d\), lowers that year's current tax by \(rd/100\) and raises the deferred balance at that year end by \(rd/100\), the same amount, as Theorem 2.1 (a) gives. Lab 1 shows total tax expense equal to 21 percent of pretax income in every year of its one tax schedule. Theorem 2.1 (b) gives the same result for any tax percentages that sum to 100, at a flat rate.

163         Proof
164           P1 · Carrying amount minus tax base
165             Prove Theorem 1.3 without the sum notation, for \(L = 2\).
166             Solution
167               Year 1: carrying amount \(C - b_1\), tax base \(C - \tau_1\), difference \(\tau_1 - b_1 = K_1\). Year 2: carrying amount \(C - 2b_1\), tax base \(C - \tau_1 - \tau_2\), difference \(\tau_1 + \tau_2 - 2b_1 = (\tau_1 - b_1) + (\tau_2 - b_1) = K_2\). Each step adds the year's difference.
168           P2 · The balance sums to zero
169             Show that Theorem 2.1 (d) holds without using the values of \(\tau_t\) or \(b_t\).
170             Solution
171               \(\mathrm{DE}_t = \mathrm{DTL}_t - \mathrm{DTL}_{t-1}\) for each \(t\), so the sum over \(t = 1\) to \(L\) telescopes to \(\mathrm{DTL}_L - \mathrm{DTL}_0\). Here \(\mathrm{DTL}_0 = 0\) by definition, and \(\mathrm{DTL}_L = 0\) by Theorem 2.1 (c). The sum is zero whatever the schedule, provided (c) holds.
172           P3 · A rate that changes
173             Let the rate be \(r_t\) in year \(t\), with \(\mathrm{DTL}_t = r_t K_t / 100\). Show that \(\mathrm{TE}_t = \big(r_t P_t + (r_t - r_{t-1}) K_{t-1}\big) / 100\).
174             Solution
175               \(\mathrm{TE}_t = r_t(P_t - d_t)/100 + (r_t K_t - r_{t-1} K_{t-1})/100\), where \(d_t = \tau_t - b_t\). Substitute \(K_t = K_{t-1} + d_t\): the \(d_t\) terms are \(-r_t d_t/100 + r_t d_t/100\), which cancel, leaving \((r_t P_t + r_t K_{t-1} - r_{t-1} K_{t-1})/100\). The extra term is \((r_t - r_{t-1})K_{t-1}/100\), which is zero when the rate does not change or when \(K_{t-1} = 0\).

189         Research
190           R1 · Recognize a deferred tax asset
191             Add a deductible accrual to the model: an amount \(A\) expensed in year 1 and deducted for tax in year 3. Give the temporary difference, the deferred tax asset at each year end, and the identity in Theorem 2.1 that changes sign. Then state the recognition rule the model would need, and say why the lesson does not supply it.
192             Solution
193               In the signed form, the accrual gives \(\tau_t - b_t = -A\) in year 1 (book expense, no deduction), \(0\) in year 2 and \(+A\) in year 3 (the deduction, with no book expense). So \(K_t = -A\) in years 1 and 2, and \(K_3 = 0\). The signed balance \(rK_t/100\) is \(-rA/100\) in years 1 and 2, which is an asset of \(rA/100\), and zero after year 3. Identities (a) and (b) hold with the sign as written: their proofs in Theorem 2.1 use only \(K_t - K_{t-1} = \tau_t - b_t\), which is the definition of \(K_t\) here, and the definitions of \(\mathrm{DE}_t\) and \(\mathrm{TE}_t\). Identity (a), \(\mathrm{DE}_t = r(\tau_t - b_t)/100\), is the one that changes sign: its right side is \(-rA/100\) in year 1 and \(+rA/100\) in year 3. Identity (d) follows from (c) by telescoping. Identity (c) holds here. Its proof in Theorem 2.1 rests on total tax deductions equal to total book expense, \(\sum_t \tau_t = \sum_t b_t\); in this accrual both sums equal \(A\), so the same condition holds. Whether the asset may be recognized is outside the model: it has no income forecast, and the lesson states no recognition test.

195             For Example 1.4 with \(P_3 = \$50{,}000\), the rate is 21 percent in years 1 and 2 and 25 percent in year 3. Compute \(\mathrm{TE}_3\), and explain why it differs from \(0.25 \times 50{,}000\).
196             Solution
197               \(K_2 = \$30{,}000\) and \(\tau_3 - b_3 = 0\), so \(\mathrm{CT}_3 = 0.25 \times 50{,}000 = \$12{,}500\). The difference from \(0.25 \times 50{,}000 = \$12{,}500\) is \(\$1{,}200\), which is the term \((r_3 - r_2) K_2 / 100 = 4 \times 30{,}000 / 100\) from P3.
198           R3 · What evidence would test the timing
199             State the evidence that would show whether a model with a flat tax schedule matches the timing of a real firm's tax, and name the input the model would need that it does not have. Make no claim about any firm.
200             Solution
