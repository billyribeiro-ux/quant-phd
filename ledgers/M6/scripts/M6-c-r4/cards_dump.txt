  B: Assumptions: (Ω, F, P) is a probability space; (F_k) is a filtration; X is an integer-valued process adapted to (F_k), so that {X_k = b} is in F_k for each k and each integer b; B is a set of integers; τ_B = inf{k ≥ 0 : X_k in B}, with inf of the empty set equal to ∞. Then τ_B is a stopping time (Proposition 9.5).

M6-c097 L10 None  
  F: What is the doubling strategy with capital K? 
  B: With K ≥ 1 an integer number of cents, bet k is a fair ±1 coin and stakes 2^(k−1) cents. The strategy places bet k only if no earlier bet has won and the stake does not exceed the wealth on hand. Ruin means stopping without a win (Definition 10.5).
M6-c098 L10 T  
  F: How many bets does the doubling strategy place with capital K? 
  B: Let m satisfy 2^m ≤ K + 1 < 2^(m+1). The number of bets is min(w, m), where w is the index of the first win. D is a martingale (under Assumption CE), τ is a stopping time, and τ ≤ m (Lemma 10.6).
M6-c099 L10 T  
  F: With capital K, what are the ruin probability and the final wealth? 
  B: Assumptions: K ≥ 1 is an integer and m is as in Lemma 10.6. Ruin, meaning no win by bet m, has probability 2^(−m). The wealth is K + 1 after a win and K + 1 − 2^m after ruin, and the expected final wealth is K (Proposition 10.7).

  F: What is a nonnegative submartingale? 
  B: An adapted process X with X_k ≥ 0 and E[X_k] < ∞ for each k, and E[X_(k+1) 1_A] ≥ E[X_k 1_A] for each k ≥ 0 and each event A in F_k. Equivalently, E[X_(k+1) | F_k] ≥ X_k (Definition 11.1).
M6-c107 L11 T  
  F: Is |S_k| a nonnegative submartingale for the fair walk?
