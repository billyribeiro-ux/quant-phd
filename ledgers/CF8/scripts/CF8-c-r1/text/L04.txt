      A committed credit line lets a company pay its bills in a week when cash falls short, and a covenant is a condition the company must meet on a stated date. Together they decide whether a cash plan is financed or in breach. This lesson defines the facility and the covenant, proves that a draw does not change liquidity (Definition 1.2 and Theorem 2.1), locates the pinch week (Definition 1.2), proves the smallest commitment with no shortfall, and works out the effect of a DSO slip. Lab 1 runs these on the Lesson 3 forecast, on a SIMULATED case.
      1The facility, the covenant and their measures
        Definition 1.1 (revolving credit facility)
        A revolving credit facility is a committed line with commitment \(K\), in integer cents. The borrower may draw up to \(K\) in total, repay, and draw again up to the commitment. Its drawn balance is \(B_w\), and its undrawn availability is \(A_w = K - B_w\). The drawn balance is a borrowing, so it is a liability entered on the balance sheet. The credit line of Lesson 3 is this facility, and its draw and repayment rule is the rule of L03 Definition 2.1.
        Definition 1.2 (liquidity, headroom and the pinch week)
        Liquidity at the end of week \(w\) is \(L_w = C_w + A_w\): the ending cash plus the undrawn availability. Given a threshold \(\theta\), in integer cents, the headroom is \(U_w = L_w - \theta\). The pinch week is the week with the least headroom, and when two weeks tie, it is the earlier one.
        Definition 1.3 (financial covenant, minimum liquidity)
        A financial covenant is a condition in a loan agreement that the borrower must meet on a stated date. This lesson uses one covenant, minimum liquidity with threshold \(\theta\): the covenant is met at the end of week \(w\) when \(U_w \ge 0\), and a week with \(U_w
        Theorem 2.1 (a draw leaves liquidity unchanged)
        Assumptions. (1) The flows and the rule of L03 (Theorem 1.3 and Definition 2.1 there) apply in every week. (2) \(0 \le B_0 \le K\). (3) No interest, fee or other cash cost is paid on the facility.
        Statement. Let \(\mathrm{Cum}_w = \sum_{u=1}^{w} \nu_u\) be the cumulative net flow. For every week \(w\),
        \[ L_w = C_0 - B_0 + K + \mathrm{Cum}_w. \]
        A draw or a repayment in any week changes \(L_w\) by nothing.
      Proof
        By Theorem 1.3 of L03, \(C_w = C_0 + \mathrm{Cum}_w + \sum_{u \le w}(\mathrm{Dr}_u - \mathrm{Rp}_u)\). By Definition 2.1 of L03, \(B_w = B_0 + \sum_{u \le w}(\mathrm{Dr}_u - \mathrm{Rp}_u)\). Subtracting the second from the first gives \(C_w - B_w = C_0 - B_0 + \mathrm{Cum}_w\), because the draws and repayments cancel. Since \(L_w = C_w + K - B_w\), the formula follows. A draw of \(x\) raises \(C_w\) and \(B_w\) by \(x\), so \(C_w - B_w\) and \(L_w\) do not move. \(\blacksquare\)
        Remark 2.2 (liquidity sees operations, not financing)
        Under Assumption (3), liquidity moves only with the weekly net flows, which are operating receipts less disbursements. The financing choices of the rule are invisible to it. Assumption (3) matters. A one-time cash cost of the facility paid in week 1 lowers \(C_w\) in every week without changing \(B_w\), so it lowers \(L_w\) by the same amount in every week. A cash cost that depends on the drawn balance, such as interest paid in cash in the same week, behaves differently: a draw of \(x\) raises \(B_w\) by \(x\) and raises \(C_w\) by \(x - \Delta\), where \(\Delta\) is the cash cost that the draw causes in that week, so \(L_w\) changes by \(-\Delta\). This lesson does not model either kind of cost.
        Corollary 2.2 (the pinch week follows the cumulative net flow)
        Assumptions. Those of Theorem 2.1.
        Statement. \(U_w = (C_0 - B_0 + K - \theta) + \mathrm{Cum}_w\) for every week. The pinch week is therefore the week of least cumulative net flow, earlier week on a tie.
      Proof
        Subtract \(\theta\) from Theorem 2.1. The constant \(C_0 - B_0 + K - \theta\) is the same in every week, so \(U_w - U_v = \mathrm{Cum}_w - \mathrm{Cum}_v\) for any two weeks. The week of least \(U_w\) is the week of least \(\mathrm{Cum}_w\), and a tie in one is a tie in the other, so the earlier week is chosen by the same rule. \(\blacksquare\)
        Example 2.3 (the Lab 1 case in the identity)
        In Lab 1 the opening cash is 1,200,000 cents, the opening drawn balance is zero and the commitment is 1,200,000 cents, so the constant in Corollary 2.2 is \(1{,}200{,}000 + 1{,}200{,}000 - 1{,}800{,}000 = 600{,}000\). The cumulative net flow after week 11 is \(-1{,}488{,}640\) cents, so \(U_{11} = 600{,}000 - 1{,}488{,}640 = -888{,}640\) cents. Lab 1 prints the same pinch headroom, in the same week.
        Lab 1 · The SIMULATED case: availability, headroom, sizing and a DSO slip
        The lab uses the seed-7 flows of L03 Lab 1, with a smaller commitment of 1,200,000 cents, a minimum-liquidity threshold of 1,800,000 cents, and a DSO slip of 5 days on weekly sales of 1,234,567 cents. Before running, the expected results are: liquidity equals opening cash less opening drawn plus the commitment plus cumulative net flow in every week (Theorem 2.1); the pinch week is the week of least headroom and also the week of least cumulative net flow (Corollary 2.2); the commitment is too small, so one week shows a shortfall, and the smallest commitment with no shortfall is the peak of the unconstrained drawn balance, one cent below it leaves a shortfall (Theorem 2.3); and the slip lowers liquidity by the same amount in every week and leaves the pinch week unchanged (Theorem 2.4).
        """CF8 L04 lab 1: revolver availability, minimum-liquidity covenant headroom, the pinch week, and a DSO slip.
SIMULATED: the receipts and disbursements are drawn from random.Random(SEED) in this file, and the commitment, the
covenant threshold and the weekly sales are fixed inputs written here. None is any firm's data.
Units: integer cents throughout. A week is 7 days and the horizon is 13 weeks. Interest is not modelled.
Liquidity at week end is ending cash plus undrawn availability, where availability is commitment less drawn balance.
Headroom is liquidity less the covenant threshold. The DSO slip is computed in Fraction and rounded once, to the nearest
cent, with halves rounded up: the amount is 5 days times weekly sales divided by 7, because daily sales are weekly
sales divided by 7 under the 7-day week.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
import math
import random
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction
HORIZON_WEEKS = 13
SEED = 7
OPENING_CASH = 1_200_000
OPENING_DRAWN = 0
MINIMUM_CASH = 1_000_000
COMMITMENT = 1_200_000
COVENANT_THRESHOLD = 1_800_000
WEEKLY_SALES = 1_234_567
SLIP_DAYS = 5
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Week:
    """One row of the forecast. Every money field is an int in cents."""
    week: int
    receipts: int
    disbursements: int
    draw: int
    repay: int
    ending_cash: int
    drawn: int
    availability: int
    liquidity: int
    headroom: int
    shortfall: int
def simulate_inputs(seed: int) -> tuple[list[int], list[int]]:
    """Weekly receipts and disbursements in cents for the horizon, from random.Random(seed). SIMULATED."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    receipts: list[int] = []
    disbursements: list[int] = []
    for week in range(1, HORIZON_WEEKS + 1):
        slump = -350_000 if 5 <= week <= 8 else 0
        receipts.append(1_000_000 + rng.randint(-150_000, 150_000) + slump)
        disbursements.append(1_020_000 + rng.randint(-120_000, 120_000))
    return receipts, disbursements
def forecast(receipts: list[int], disbursements: list[int], commitment: int) -> list[Week]:
    """Weekly rows in cents. A draw covers the shortfall below minimum cash, capped at availability; a repayment
    sweeps cash above minimum cash to the drawn balance, capped at the balance."""
    cash = OPENING_CASH
    drawn = OPENING_DRAWN
    rows: list[Week] = []
    for week, (receipt, payment) in enumerate(zip(receipts, disbursements, strict=True), start=1):
        before = cash + receipt - payment
        draw = 0
        repay = 0
        if before < MINIMUM_CASH:
            draw = min(MINIMUM_CASH - before, commitment - drawn)
        elif before > MINIMUM_CASH and drawn > 0:
            repay = min(before - MINIMUM_CASH, drawn)
        ending = before + draw - repay
        drawn += draw - repay
        availability = commitment - drawn
        liquidity = ending + availability
        rows.append(
            Week(
                week,
                receipt,
                payment,
                draw,
                repay,
                ending,
                drawn,
                availability,
                liquidity,
                liquidity - COVENANT_THRESHOLD,
                max(0, MINIMUM_CASH - ending),
            )
        )
        cash = ending
    return rows
def half_up(amount: Fraction) -> int:
    """The nearest integer, with halves rounded up, for a non-negative Fraction."""
    return math.floor(amount + Fraction(1, 2))
def pinch_week(rows: list[Week]) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """The week of least headroom (earliest on a tie) and its headroom in cents."""
    best = min(rows, key=lambda row: row.headroom)
    return best.week, best.headroom
def required_commitment(receipts: list[int], disbursements: list[int]) -> int:
    """The smallest commitment with no shortfall: the peak drawn balance when no draw is capped."""
    unconstrained = forecast(receipts, disbursements, commitment=10**12)
    return max([OPENING_DRAWN] + [row.drawn for row in unconstrained])
def main() -> None:
    receipts, disbursements = simulate_inputs(SEED)
    rows = forecast(receipts, disbursements, COMMITMENT)
    print(f"SIMULATED case, seed {SEED}, {HORIZON_WEEKS} weeks, integer cents")
    print(
        f"commitment {COMMITMENT}, minimum cash {MINIMUM_CASH}, covenant threshold {COVENANT_THRESHOLD}, "
        f"opening cash {OPENING_CASH}, opening drawn {OPENING_DRAWN}"
    )
    print("week  draw  repay  ending_cash  drawn  availability  liquidity  headroom  shortfall")
    for row in rows:
        print(
            f"{row.week:>4}  {row.draw:>4}  {row.repay:>5}  {row.ending_cash:>11}  {row.drawn:>5}  "
            f"{row.availability:>12}  {row.liquidity:>9}  {row.headroom:>8}  {row.shortfall:>9}"
        )
    cumulative = 0
    identity_ok = True
    net_ok = True
    for row, receipt, payment in zip(rows, receipts, disbursements, strict=True):
        cumulative += receipt - payment
        identity_ok = identity_ok and row.liquidity == OPENING_CASH - OPENING_DRAWN + COMMITMENT + cumulative
        net_ok = net_ok and row.headroom == row.liquidity - COVENANT_THRESHOLD
    print(f"liquidity equals opening cash less opening drawn, plus commitment, plus cumulative net flow: {identity_ok}")
    print(f"headroom equals liquidity less the covenant threshold in every week: {net_ok}")
    week, headroom = pinch_week(rows)
    print(f"pinch week {week}, headroom {headroom} cents")
    path: list[int] = []
    running = 0
    for receipt, payment in zip(receipts, disbursements, strict=True):
        running += receipt - payment
        path.append(running)
    least_net_week = path.index(min(path)) + 1
    print(f"week of least cumulative net flow {least_net_week}; it is the pinch week: {least_net_week == week}")
    print(
        f"least availability {min(row.availability for row in rows)} cents, in week "
        f"{min(rows, key=lambda row: row.availability).week}"
    )
    print(f"largest weekly shortfall {max(row.shortfall for row in rows)} cents")
    needed = required_commitment(receipts, disbursements)
    at_needed = forecast(receipts, disbursements, needed)
    just_below = forecast(receipts, disbursements, needed - 1)
    print(f"required commitment {needed} cents")
    print(f"no shortfall at the required commitment: {all(row.shortfall == 0 for row in at_needed)}")
    print(f"shortfall one cent below it: {max(row.shortfall for row in just_below)} cents")
    slip = half_up(Fraction(SLIP_DAYS * WEEKLY_SALES, 7))
    slipped = list(receipts)
    slipped[0] -= slip
    shifted = forecast(slipped, disbursements, COMMITMENT)
    liquidity_drop = {base.liquidity - alt.liquidity for base, alt in zip(rows, shifted, strict=True)}
    print(f"DSO slip of {SLIP_DAYS} days on weekly sales {WEEKLY_SALES}: receipts fall by {slip} cents in week 1")
    print(f"liquidity falls by the same amount in every week: {liquidity_drop == {slip}}")
    slipped_week, slipped_headroom = pinch_week(shifted)
    print(f"pinch week after the slip {slipped_week}, headroom {slipped_headroom} cents")
    print(f"pinch week unchanged by the slip: {slipped_week == week}")
main()

        Theorem 2.3 (the smallest commitment with no shortfall)
        Assumptions. (1) The flows and the rule of L03 apply. (2) \(B_0 \le K\). Let \(B^\infty_w\) be the drawn balance that the rule produces with no cap on the draw (the same flows and the same rules, with \(K\) removed from rule 1), and let \(K^\ast = \max\bigl(B_0,\ \max_{1 \le w \le H} B^\infty_w\bigr)\).
        Statement. The shortfall is zero in every week if and only if \(K \ge K^\ast\). If \(K Proof
        Sufficiency. Let \(K \ge K^\ast\). Induct on \(w\) to show that the capped path \(B_w\) equals \(B^\infty_w\) and that no week is capped. For \(w = 0\), \(B_0 = B^\infty_0\). Suppose \(B_{w-1} = B^\infty_{w-1} \le K^\ast \le K\). If rule 1 applies, the unconstrained draw is \(m - P_w\), which gives \(B^\infty_w = B_{w-1} + m - P_w \le K^\ast \le K\), so \(m - P_w \le K - B_{w-1}\). The week is not capped, the draws coincide, and the induction continues. Rules 2 and 3 do not depend on \(K\). By Theorem 2.2(b) of L03, every uncapped week ends with cash at least \(m\), so the shortfall is zero.
        Necessity. Let \(K  K\). Let \(w\) be the first such week. Before \(w\), the paths coincide, so \(B_{w-1} = B^\infty_{w-1} \le K\). If week \(w\) were under rule 2 or rule 3, then \(B^\infty_w \le B^\infty_{w-1} \le K\), which is false. So rule 1 applies, and \(B^\infty_w = B_{w-1} + m - P_w > K\). This gives \(m - P_w > K - B_{w-1}\), so week \(w\) is capped. By Theorem 2.2(c) of L03 its shortfall is positive. \(\blacksquare\)

        Theorem 2.4 (a DSO slip lowers liquidity by its amount, and leaves the pinch week)
        Assumptions. (1) Weekly sales are \(S\) cents in every week, so daily sales are \(S/7\) under the 7-day week. Annual revenue is \(\mathrm{Rev} = 365S/7\), and \(\mathrm{Rev}/365 = S/7\). (2) DSO, as defined in L01 with a 365-day year, is applied to the receivable at each week end, so \(\mathrm{AR} = \mathrm{DSO} \times\) daily sales. L01 defines DSO on year-end balances; applying it at week ends is this lesson's convention. DSO rises by \(d\) days at the start of week 1, and the opening receivable is unchanged. (3) Every receipt is a collection of receivables. (4) The collections given up in week 1 stay uncollected through week \(H\), and no other input changes. (5) The assumptions of Theorem 2.1 hold: the rule of L03 applies in every week, \(0 \le B_0 \le K\), and no interest, fee or other cash cost is paid on the facility.
        Statement. The cumulative receipts through each week \(w \ge 1\) fall by exactly \(dS/7\) cents. Liquidity falls by the same amount in every week, and the pinch week does not change. The lab prints the amount to the nearest cent, with halves going up, which is the one rounding step of this lesson.
      Proof
        Assumption (5) gives the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1, which the argument below uses. Receivables satisfy opening plus sales less collections equals closing, so the collections through week \(w\) are \(\mathrm{Coll}_w = \mathrm{AR}_0 + \mathrm{Sales}_w - \mathrm{AR}_w\). Sales and the opening receivable are unchanged by the slip, so the change in cumulative collections through \(w\) is \(-(\mathrm{AR}_w^{\text{new}} - \mathrm{AR}_w^{\text{old}})\). By Assumption (2) and the definition of DSO, the new closing receivable exceeds the old one by \(d \cdot S/7\), the extra days times daily sales. Assumption (4) keeps this excess in place for every \(w \ge 1\). By Assumption (3) receipts equal collections, so cumulative receipts through every week \(w \ge 1\) fall by \(dS/7\). By Theorem 2.1, a fall of \(dS/7\) in cumulative receipts is a fall of \(dS/7\) in liquidity in every week. Corollary 2.2 says the pinch week follows the cumulative flow, and a uniform fall shifts every week's headroom by the same amount, so the pinch week does not change. \(\blacksquare\)
        Example 2.5 (the slip in Lab 1)
        With \(d = 5\) and \(S = 1{,}234{,}567\), the exact fall is \(5 \times 1{,}234{,}567 / 7 = 881{,}833.571\ldots\) cents. To the nearest cent the fall is 881,834 cents, and the fraction .571 is not a half, so the rule for halves does not apply. Lab 1 moves 881,834 cents out of week 1 receipts, and its pinch headroom falls from \(-888{,}640\) to \(-1{,}770{,}474\) cents in week 11.
        Definition 2.6 (the weekly grid)
        All flows and balances in this lesson are measured at week end, as in L03. A breach that begins and ends inside a week is not seen, and the pinch week is the least headroom on that grid.
        3Where the canon breaks
        The covenant is minimum liquidity only. A different covenant, for example one tied to a ratio, has its own measure, and its pinch week is not given by Corollary 2.2. The lesson does not claim anything about covenant design in practice.
        Theorem 2.1 needs Assumption (3). When a facility costs cash, a draw is no longer liquidity-neutral if the cost depends on the balance drawn, and the pinch week can then move. A one-time cost paid in week 1 lowers every week's liquidity by the same amount and leaves the pinch week unchanged. A recurring cost of \(f\) cents in each week lowers \(L_w\) by \(wf\) cents, an amount that grows with the week, and Corollary 2.2 does not cover it. The forecast of Lesson 3 excludes that cost, so these results apply to that forecast and not to one that includes the cost.
        Theorem 2.4 is a one-time slip that stays uncollected through the horizon. A slip that is partly recovered, or that phases in over several weeks, changes the cumulative flows week by week, and the pinch week can move. Research problem R1 shows one such case.
        Theorem 2.3 fixes the repayment rule, the minimum and the flows. A treasurer who holds a higher minimum or repays on a different schedule has a different unconstrained path, and therefore can have a different required commitment.
        4Misconceptions
            “Drawing on the facility improves liquidity.”
            A draw raises cash and the drawn balance by the same amount, so availability falls by that amount and liquidity is unchanged (Theorem 2.1). In Lab 1 the week 6 liquidity of 1,660,095 cents equals \(1{,}200{,}000 + 1{,}200{,}000 - 739{,}905\), which is the identity with no draw term, even though the week's draw is 539,905 cents.
            “The pinch week is the week with the largest outflow.”
            The pinch week is the week of least cumulative net flow (Corollary 2.2). In Lab 1 the largest single-week net outflow is week 6, at \(530{,}408 - 1{,}138{,}473 = -608{,}065\) cents, but the cumulative flow is least in week 11, at \(-1{,}488{,}640\) cents, and week 11 is the pinch week.
            “A one-time DSO slip moves the pinch week.”
            A one-time slip lowers every week's liquidity by the same amount (Theorem 2.4), so the least-headroom week does not change. Lab 1 prints pinch week 11 before and after the slip.
            “A commitment that covers the opening drawn balance is enough.”
            The smallest commitment with no shortfall is the peak of the unconstrained drawn balance, which can exceed the opening balance (Theorem 2.3). In Lab 1 the opening drawn balance is zero, but a commitment of 1,200,000 cents leaves a shortfall of 88,640 cents in week 11, because the peak is 1,288,640 cents.
        5Problem set
        Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. All amounts are integer cents unless a problem says otherwise.
          Mechanics
            M1 · Availability, liquidity and headroom
            A facility has a commitment of 2,000,000 cents and a drawn balance of 500,000 cents. Ending cash is 300,000 cents and the covenant threshold is 1,600,000 cents. Find the availability, the liquidity and the headroom.
            Solution
              Availability is \(A = 2{,}000{,}000 - 500{,}000 = 1{,}500{,}000\) cents. Liquidity is \(L = 300{,}000 + 1{,}500{,}000 = 1{,}800{,}000\) cents. Headroom is \(U = 1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}600{,}000 = 200{,}000\) cents, so the covenant is met with 200,000 cents to spare.
            M2 · A slip in cents
            Weekly sales are 700,000 cents, and DSO rises by 3 days. Find the fall in cumulative receipts, under the assumptions of Theorem 2.4.
            Solution
              The fall is \(dS/7 = 3 \times 700{,}000 / 7 = 300{,}000\) cents. Since 700,000 is divisible by 7, the amount is a whole number of cents and no rounding is needed. Liquidity falls by 300,000 cents in every week from week 1, by Theorem 2.4.
            M3 · The earliest pinch week
            Headroom in weeks 1 to 5 is 100, −50, 200, −50 and 30 thousand cents. Find the pinch week.
            Solution
              The least headroom is −50, which occurs in weeks 2 and 4. By Definition 1.2 the tie goes to the earlier week, so the pinch week is week 2. The headroom figure is in thousands here, and the choice of week does not depend on the unit.
          Proof
            P1 · Theorem 2.1 for two weeks
            Prove that \(L_2 = C_0 - B_0 + K + \nu_1 + \nu_2\) directly, from the rule of L03 and without citing the general formula.
            Solution
              Week 1 gives \(C_1 - B_1 = C_0 + \nu_1 - B_0\), because the draws and repayments of week 1 change \(C_1\) and \(B_1\) by the same amount. Week 2 gives \(C_2 - B_2 = C_1 + \nu_2 - B_1\), by the same argument. Substituting the first into the second gives \(C_2 - B_2 = C_0 - B_0 + \nu_1 + \nu_2\). Adding the availability \(K - B_2\) and using \(L_2 = C_2 + K - B_2\) gives the formula.
            P2 · The pinch week is an argmin of the cumulative flow
            Show that for any two weeks \(v\) and \(w\), \(U_w - U_v = \mathrm{Cum}_w - \mathrm{Cum}_v\), and conclude that the pinch week is the earliest week of least \(\mathrm{Cum}_w\).
            Solution
              Corollary 2.2 writes \(U_w = c + \mathrm{Cum}_w\) with a constant \(c = C_0 - B_0 + K - \theta\). Subtracting the two expressions cancels \(c\). So \(U_w\) is minimal exactly when \(\mathrm{Cum}_w\) is minimal, and a tie in one is a tie in the other, so the earliest-week rule selects the same week.

            E2 · Availability and cash in week 13
            In week 13, Lab 1 prints a liquidity of 1,282,442 cents and a drawn balance of 917,558 cents. Find the availability and check liquidity against the cash.
            Solution
              Availability is \(1{,}200{,}000 - 917{,}558 = 282{,}442\) cents.           Research
            R1 · A phased slip that moves the pinch week
            Suppose the baseline headroom is \(U_1 = 100\) and \(U_2 = 50\), in thousands of cents, so week 2 is the pinch week. Suppose a slip cuts week 1 receipts by 60 and week 2 receipts are 50 above baseline, so cumulative receipts fall by 60 in week 1 and by 10 in week 2. Find the new pinch week.
            Solution
              The cumulative fall is 60 in week 1 and 10 in week 2, so the new headroom is \(U_1 = 40\) and \(U_2 = 40\). The two weeks tie, and Definition 1.2 chooses the earlier one, so the pinch week moves from week 2 to week 1. Theorem 2.4 does not cover this case, because a uniform fall would shift both weeks by the same amount. The example shows that a phased or partly recovered slip can move the pinch week.
            R2 · Raising the threshold
            Show that raising the covenant threshold by \(\eta\) lowers headroom by \(\eta\) in every week, and that the pinch week does not change.
            Solution
              Headroom is \(U_w = L_w - \theta\). With \(\theta + \eta\), the headroom in week \(w\) is \(L_w - \theta - \eta = U_w - \eta\). The same amount is subtracted in every week, so the ordering of weeks by headroom is unchanged, and the least week and its earliest tie are the same. The breach weeks can change, because a week with headroom between 0 and \(\eta\) becomes a breach.
            R3 · Liquidity in a fully drawn week
            Show that in a week with \(A_w = 0\), liquidity equals ending cash, and use Lab 1 week 11 to check it.
            Solution
              Liquidity is \(L_w = C_w + A_w = C_w + 0 = C_w\). Lab 1 prints availability zero in week 11, with ending cash 911,360 cents, and its liquidity for that week is also 911,360 cents. In a fully drawn week, the covenant therefore tests cash alone, and an extra draw is no longer available to meet the threshold.
