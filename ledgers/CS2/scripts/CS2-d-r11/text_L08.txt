21: In this lesson a book is a list of standing orders, grouped by price, that buyers and sellers add to and take from. In this model each message either adds an order, which may trade at once, or cancels an order. Two data-structure questions decide what the book can answer quickly: where the best price is, and in what order orders at one price are filled. This lesson defines the structure in integer cents and integer shares, fixes the matching rule of Section 2 (Definition 2.1), proves that the book never keeps a buy price at or above a sell price, and counts the operations. Lab 1 checks the structure against a plain reference on a SIMULATED stream.
22: 1 Orders, sides and price levels
23: A limit order instructs the book to buy or sell a quantity at a stated price or better. A buy order is a bid , and a sell order is an ask . An order that is not filled at once rests in the book until it is filled or cancelled. A first-in first-out (FIFO) queue hands out its items in the order they were added. Every price in this lesson is an integer number of cents, so 10000 is 100.00 currency units (CU) and one tick is one cent. Every quantity is an integer number of shares. The code in Lab 1 raises an error for any other type. Every order enters the book through its submit method, which checks the types of the price and the quantity before the book changes. Part A passes a float price, and the code refuses it with a TypeError.
24: Definition 1.1 (order-book data structure)
25: An order-book data structure stores resting orders on two sides, bids and asks. An order is live while it is unfilled and not cancelled. For each side, the structure keeps (a) a sorted list of the prices that have at least one live order, and (b) for each such price, a FIFO queue of the orders at that price in arrival order. The pair (price, queue) is a price level . Each queue has a head , its first entry that the structure has not yet passed. An order that is filled or cancelled stays in its queue as a dead entry , that is, an entry that is not live, until the head passes it. The head passes only dead entries, and it never passes a live one. The structure also keeps an index from order identifiers to live orders.
26: Definition 1.2 (best bid, best ask and an uncrossed book)
27: The best bid is the highest price with a live bid, and the best ask is the lowest price with a live ask. Either is undefined when its side has no live order. A bid at price \(p\) crosses an ask at price \(a\) when \(p \ge a\). The book is uncrossed when best bid < best ask whenever both are defined, so that no bid crosses the best ask.
28: The matching rule that fills orders is in Section 2. The next proposition shows that the matching rule never leaves the book crossed, so the best bid and best ask always describe a book in which no bid reaches an ask.
29: Proposition 1.3 (a book never rests a crossed quote)
30: Hypotheses.
31: The book starts with no orders.
32: Each message is either a submission, matched by the rule of Definition 2.1, or a cancellation of a live order.
33: Then after every message the book is uncrossed.
34: Proof
35: Induction on the number of messages. Before the first message the book is empty, so it is uncrossed. Assume it is uncrossed before a message.
36: Cancellation. Removing a live order can only remove a price from a side. The best bid can only fall and the best ask can only rise, so the inequality still holds.
37: Submission of a buy at price \(p\). Matching stops when no live ask remains or when the best live ask is above \(p\). Let \(B\) and \(A\) be the best bid and best ask before the submission, when defined. If no ask exists after matching, the book has no ask and the claim is vacuous, so assume one exists after matching. Matching only removes asks, so the best ask after matching is some \(A'\) with \(A' \ge A\) when defined. If the remainder of the buy is zero, the bids are unchanged. If \(B\) is defined, \(\text{best bid} = B < A \le A'\); if \(B\) is undefined, the bid side is empty and the claim is vacuous. If a remainder rests at \(p\), the new best bid is \(\max\{B, p\}\). The case \(B\) is undefined is trivial, since then the new best bid is \(p\), and it is below \(A'\) whenever \(A'\) is defined because matching stopped with \(A' > p\). When \(B\) is defined, \(B < A \le A'\), and \(p < A'\) by the same stopping condition, so the new best bid is below \(A'\).
38: Submission of a sell. Symmetric, with the roles of bids and asks exchanged.
39: 2 The matching rule: price-time priority
40: Definition 2.1 (price-time priority)
41: An incoming buy at price \(p\) is matched against live asks in this order: the lowest ask price first, and at one price the earliest arrival first. A match with an ask at price \(a \le p\) executes the smaller of the remaining quantity and the resting quantity, in shares, at price \(a\), which is the price of the resting order. The remaining quantity after all matches, if positive, rests as a bid at \(p\). A sell is symmetric: the highest bid first, then the earliest arrival, with bids at prices \(b \ge p\), and the remainder rests as an ask at \(p\).
42: The rule is written as pseudocode below. It is not runnable. Lab 1 contains the runnable version, which is checked against a second implementation on seeded messages.
43: Pseudocode: submit a buy of quantity q at price p, identifier id
44: while q > 0:
45: if no live ask exists, stop
46: a := the lowest price with a live ask
47: if a > p, stop
48: take the earliest live order o at price a
49: fill := min(q, quantity of o)
50: reduce q and the quantity of o by fill; record a trade of fill shares at price a
51: if o is filled, mark it not live; if price a has no live order, remove price a
52: if q > 0, rest q shares at price p as the newest order at that price
53: Proposition 2.2 (the rule is price-time priority)
54: Hypotheses.
55: Hypothesis: each price level's queue holds its orders in arrival order, because an order is appended when it rests and never moved.
56: Assumption 1 (not derived from the pseudocode): the head of a queue passes only dead entries, as Definition 1.1 requires.
57: Assumption 2 (not derived from the pseudocode): every price in the sorted list has at least one live order, the invariant of Definition 1.1.
58: Assuming Assumptions 1 and 2, the trades produced by the pseudocode are the trades produced by the sorted-scan rule: sort the live crossing orders by (price best first, then arrival number), and fill them in that order until the incoming quantity is used up or no crossing order remains.
59: Proof
60: Let \(C_1, C_2, \ldots\) be the sorted sequence of live crossing orders at the start of the submission. The claim is that the pseudocode fills exactly \(C_1, C_2, \ldots\), in that order, with the same amounts. Induct on the number of fills. Suppose the first \(t\) fills agree, and let \(C\) be the next order of the sorted sequence, not yet fully filled. The lowest live ask price \(a\) among the remaining crossing orders is the price of \(C\), because the sorted sequence orders by price first. Assumption 2 makes the level at \(a\) exist with a live order. By Assumption 1 the head passes only dead entries, so after the head moves, it rests on the earliest live order at \(a\). By the hypothesis of arrival order, that is the earliest arrival among live orders at \(a\). So the pseudocode's next fill is \(C\), with the same amount, since both take the minimum of the two quantities. If \(C\) is filled completely, both procedures move on to \(C_{t+2}\); otherwise both keep \(C\) first. The two procedures stop together, when the quantity is used or when no live crossing order remains.
61: Example 2.3 (price before time)
62: Let an ask of 1 share at 10010 arrive first, and an ask of 1 share at 10000 arrive second. A buy of 1 share at 10010 arrives third. Price-time priority fills the second ask, at 10000, even though it arrived later. The first ask, at 10010, is not touched, because the lower price is filled first.
63: 3 What the operations cost
64: The counts in this section are the operations the proofs use. The lab keeps four kinds of count: orders added, queue steps, level creations and removals, and binary-search tests, which it logs. A queue step is one move of a queue's head past an entry. The binary search is the halving search below, which keeps the sorted list of prices in order.
65: Lemma 3.1 (each queue entry is passed at most once)
66: Hypotheses. (1) Entries are appended to a queue only when their orders rest, and (2) a queue's head moves only forward. Then the total number of queue steps over the whole run is at most the number of orders that rested.
67: Proof
68: A queue step moves the head one index forward past one entry. The head starts at the first entry and never moves back, so it passes each index at most once. Entries are appended at the end and are never removed from the middle, so every index in a queue is an entry that rested. Summing over all queues, the number of steps is at most the number of entries that rested over the run.
69: Proposition 3.2 (the halving search)
70: Hypotheses.
71: A list of \(L\) distinct prices is sorted in increasing order.
72: The search looks for the first index whose price is at least a given price \(p\), by testing the middle entry of the current interval and keeping the half that can contain the answer.
73: Then the search makes at most \(\lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor + 1\) tests when \(L \ge 1\), and no test when \(L = 0\). The bound equals the number of binary digits of \(L\).
74: Proof
75: Let the current interval have length \(n\). The middle test is at \(\mathrm{lo} + \lfloor n/2 \rfloor\). If that price is below \(p\), the new interval starts after it, and its length is \(n - \lfloor n/2 \rfloor - 1\), which equals \(\lceil n/2 \rceil - 1\). If it is not below \(p\), the new interval ends at it and has length \(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor\). In both cases the new length is at most \(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor\). Starting from length \(L\), after \(k\) tests the length is at most \(\lfloor L / 2^k \rfloor\). A test is made only when the length is at least 1, so the \(k\)th test needs \(\lfloor L / 2^{k-1} \rfloor \ge 1\), that is \(2^{k-1} \le L\), that is \(k - 1 \le \log_2 L\). So \(k \le \lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor + 1\). For \(L \ge 1\), \(\lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor + 1\) is the number of binary digits of \(L\).
76: Proposition 3.3 (cost of each book operation)
77: Hypotheses. Assuming Assumption 2 of Proposition 2.2, the book of Definition 1.1 with \(L\) prices on the side affected, and the counts of this section.
78: Reading the best bid or best ask is one index access into the sorted list, with no comparison.
79: Joining an existing price level is one lookup of the price in a hash map from prices to levels ( Lesson 2 ), then one append.
80: Creating a price level is one halving search of at most \(\lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor + 1\) tests, then one insertion that moves at most \(L\) entries.
81: Removing a price level is one halving search and one deletion that moves at most \(L\) entries.
82: Proof
83: Each item follows from the operation as written. The best bid is the last entry of the sorted list, and the best ask is the first, so each is one index access. A new level needs one search by Proposition 3.2 and one insertion, which moves the entries after the insertion point, at most \(L\) of them. Removal is the same search and a deletion. Joining a level finds the existing queue by its price and appends to it. The lookup of a price is counted as one operation in this model.
84: Lab 1 · The book against a sorted scan
85: Expected result: a hand-built book shows price-time priority, a cancellation and the refusal of a non-integer price. For 400 seeded messages, the trades, the best bid and the best ask of the book match a reference that sorts the crossing candidates on every match, after every message. Every binary search makes no more comparisons than the number of binary digits of its list length, and the queue steps do not exceed the orders that were added.

117: class Lcg:
118: """A linear congruential generator on integers, so every run draws the same numbers."""
119: def __init__(self, seed: int) -> None:
120: self.state = seed % LCG_MOD
121: def below(self, n: int) -> int:
122: """Return an integer k with 0 <= k < n."""
123: if n < 1:
124: raise ValueError("below needs n >= 1")
125: self.state = (LCG_MULT * self.state + LCG_INC) % LCG_MOD
126: return (self.state >> 16) % n

147: @dataclass
148: class Counts:
149: """Operation counts. queue_steps counts entries the head passed. Binary-search tests go to Book.search_log."""
150: orders_added: int = 0
151: queue_steps: int = 0
152: levels_created: int = 0
153: levels_removed: int = 0
154: def check_order(price: object, qty: object) -> None:
155: """Refuse anything but integer cents and integer shares, with an error that says why."""
156: if type(price) is not int or type(qty) is not int:
157: raise TypeError("prices must be integer cents and quantities integer shares")
158: if qty < 1:
159: raise ValueError("a quantity must be at least 1 share")

162: class Book:
163: """Two sides, each a sorted price list and a dict of FIFO levels, plus an index of live orders by id."""
164: def __init__(self) -> None:
165: self.prices: dict[str, list[int]] = {"B": [], "S": []}
166: self.levels: dict[str, dict[int, Level]] = {"B": {}, "S": {}}
167: self.live: dict[int, Order] = {}
168: self.counts = Counts()
169: self.search_log: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
170: def best(self, side: str) -> int | None:
171: """Best bid ("B": highest price) or best ask ("S": lowest price), or None when that side is empty."""
172: prices = self.prices[side]
173: if not prices:
174: return None
175: return prices[-1] if side == "B" else prices[0]
176: def _search(self, prices: list[int], price: int) -> int:
177: """Binary search for the index of price in a sorted list: the first index whose entry is not below price."""
178: lo, hi, comparisons = 0, len(prices), 0
179: while lo < hi:
180: mid = (lo + hi) // 2
181: comparisons += 1
182: if prices[mid] < price:
183: lo = mid + 1
184: else:
185: hi = mid
186: self.search_log.append((len(prices), comparisons))
187: return lo
188: def _front(self, level: Level) -> Order | None:
189: """The earliest live entry at a level. The head passes each entry it finds with qty 0, once."""
190: while level.head < len(level.queue) and level.queue[level.head].qty == 0:
191: level.head += 1
192: self.counts.queue_steps += 1
193: if level.head < len(level.queue):
194: return level.queue[level.head]
195: return None
196: def _rest(self, order: Order) -> None:
197: prices = self.prices[order.side]
198: levels = self.levels[order.side]
199: level = levels.get(order.price)
200: if level is None:
201: index = self._search(prices, order.price)
202: prices.insert(index, order.price)
203: level = Level()
204: levels[order.price] = level
205: self.counts.levels_created += 1
206: level.queue.append(order)
207: level.live += 1
208: self.live[order.oid] = order
209: def _drop_if_empty(self, side: str, price: int) -> None:
210: level = self.levels[side][price]
211: if level.live != 0:
212: return
213: prices = self.prices[side]
214: index = self._search(prices, price)
215: if index >= len(prices) or prices[index] != price:
216: raise RuntimeError("a price level is missing from its sorted list")
217: del prices[index]
218: del self.levels[side][price]
219: self.counts.levels_removed += 1
220: def submit(self, side: str, price: int, qty: int, oid: int) -> list[Trade]:
221: """Match an incoming limit order against the other side, then rest any remainder. Returns its trades."""
222: check_order(price, qty)
223: self.counts.orders_added += 1
224: opposite = "S" if side == "B" else "B"
225: incoming = Order(oid, side, price, qty)
226: trades: list[Trade] = []
227: while incoming.qty > 0:
228: best = self.best(opposite)
229: if best is None or (side == "B" and best > price) or (side == "S" and best < price):
230: break
231: level = self.levels[opposite][best]
232: resting = self._front(level)
233: if resting is None:
234: raise RuntimeError("a level in the price index has no live order")
235: fill = min(incoming.qty, resting.qty)
236: resting.qty -= fill
237: incoming.qty -= fill
238: trades.append(Trade(best, fill, resting.oid, oid))
239: if resting.qty == 0:
240: level.live -= 1
241: del self.live[resting.oid]
242: self._drop_if_empty(opposite, best)
243: if incoming.qty > 0:
244: self._rest(incoming)
245: return trades
246: def cancel(self, oid: int) -> bool:
247: """Remove a live order by id. Returns False when no live order has that id."""
248: order = self.live.pop(oid, None)
249: if order is None:
250: return False
251: level = self.levels[order.side][order.price]
252: order.qty = 0
253: level.live -= 1
254: self._drop_if_empty(order.side, order.price)
255: return True
256: @dataclass
257: class Resting:
258: oid: int
259: side: str
260: price: int
261: qty: int
262: class Reference:
263: """A flat list of resting orders. Every match sorts the candidates by (price, arrival number)."""
264: def __init__(self) -> None:
265: self.resting: list[Resting] = []
266: def submit(self, side: str, price: int, qty: int, oid: int) -> list[Trade]:
267: opposite = "S" if side == "B" else "B"
268: if side == "B":
269: candidates = [r for r in self.resting if r.side == opposite and r.price <= price]
270: candidates.sort(key=lambda r: (r.price, r.oid))
271: else:
272: candidates = [r for r in self.resting if r.side == opposite and r.price >= price]
273: candidates.sort(key=lambda r: (-r.price, r.oid))
274: trades: list[Trade] = []
275: remaining = qty
276: for r in candidates:
277: if remaining == 0:
278: break
279: fill = min(remaining, r.qty)
280: trades.append(Trade(r.price, fill, r.oid, oid))
281: r.qty -= fill
282: remaining -= fill
283: self.resting = [r for r in self.resting if r.qty > 0]
284: if remaining > 0:
285: self.resting.append(Resting(oid, side, price, remaining))
286: return trades
287: def cancel(self, oid: int) -> bool:
288: before = len(self.resting)
289: self.resting = [r for r in self.resting if r.oid != oid]
290: return len(self.resting) < before
291: def best(self, side: str) -> int | None:
292: """A sorted scan: sort the prices on this side, then take the first (ask) or last (bid)."""
293: prices = sorted(r.price for r in self.resting if r.side == side)
294: if not prices:
295: return None
296: return prices[-1] if side == "B" else prices[0]
297: def describe(trades: list[Trade]) -> str:
298: if not trades:
299: return "none"
300: return "; ".join(f"{t.qty} at {t.price} from order {t.maker}" for t in trades)

406: 4 Where the canon breaks
407: Price-time priority is one rule. This lesson defines it exactly and proves its structure, and it studies no other allocation rule. No claim is made here about which rule any venue applies.
408: Cancellation is lazy. A cancelled order stays in its queue as a dead entry until the head passes it. A queue can therefore hold more entries than live orders. Lemma 3.1 bounds the passes by the orders that rested, not by the live orders.
409: The model is simulated. Prices lie on a one-cent grid, the stream has two message types, limit orders and cancellations, and there is no latency, no hidden quantity and no order type beyond the limit order. The lab's stream is SIMULATED and carries no market information.
410: Counts are not times. Proposition 3.3 counts operations. It does not say how long any operation takes on any machine.
411: 5 Misconceptions
412: “Time priority applies across prices: the oldest resting order on the book fills first.”
413: Price comes first. In Example 2.3 the ask at 10010 arrived first, but a buy of 1 share at 10010 fills the later ask at 10000, because Definition 2.1 takes the lowest price before the arrival number. The oldest order on the book is the one at 10010, and it is not filled.
414: “A bid above the best ask can rest in the book, because the buyer is willing to pay more.”
415: An incoming buy that is above the best ask trades first. Only the remainder rests, and Proposition 1.3 shows that it rests below the best ask. Lab 1, part B ends with a best bid of 9980 and a best ask of 9989, which is uncrossed.
416: “A price stays in the book as a level after its last order is cancelled.”
417: A level is removed when it has no live order. Lab 1, part C creates 185 levels and removes 177, so 8 levels remain at the end, which is the difference of the two printed counts. Part C replays the stream of Part B, which ends with 10 resting orders. Ten orders on 8 levels means that at least one level holds two orders.

420: Mechanics
421: M1 · A buy across two asks at one price
422: The asks are: order 1, 3 shares at 10020; order 2, 2 shares at 10010; order 3, 4 shares at 10010, arriving in that order. A buy of 5 shares at 10020 arrives. Which trades occur, and what rests?
423: Solution
424: The lowest ask is 10010, which is at most 10020, so the buy trades there first. At 10010 the earlier order 2 fills 2 shares, then order 3 fills 3 shares, and the buy's quantity is used up. The trades are 2 shares at 10010 from order 2 and 3 shares at 10010 from order 3. Order 3 keeps 1 share resting at 10010, and order 1 is untouched at 3 shares at 10020. The resting asks are therefore 1 share at 10010 and 3 shares at 10020. Nothing rests as a bid.
425: M2 · The halving bound for a list of 7 prices
426: Using Proposition 3.2, what is the most tests a search can make on a list of 7 prices, and on a list of 8 prices?
427: Solution
428: For \(L = 7\), \(\lfloor \log_2 7 \rfloor + 1 = 2 + 1 = 3\), and 7 has three binary digits (111). For \(L = 8\), \(\lfloor \log_2 8 \rfloor + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4\), and 8 has four binary digits (1000). So the bounds are 3 and 4.
429: M3 · A partial fill and a rest
430: The asks are 1 share at 10000 and 5 shares at 10005, and the bids are empty. A buy of 3 shares at 10001 arrives. What trades occur, and what is the best bid and best ask afterwards?
431: Solution
432: The lowest ask 10000 is at most 10001, so 1 share trades at 10000. The buy has 2 shares left. The next ask, 10005, is above 10001, so matching stops. The 2 shares rest as a bid at 10001. The best bid is 10001 and the best ask is 10005, so the book is uncrossed, as Proposition 1.3 requires.
433: Proof
434: P1 · Insertion cost for a run of new prices
435: Suppose \(n\) orders arrive at \(n\) distinct new prices on one side, so each creates a level, and no level is removed. Prove that the total number of binary-search tests over the run is at most \(n(\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1)\).
436: Solution
437: The \(k\)th arrival searches a list of \(k - 1\) prices, so by Proposition 3.2 it makes at most \(\lfloor \log_2 (k-1) \rfloor + 1\) tests when \(k \ge 2\), and no test when \(k = 1\). Each term is at most \(\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1\), because \(k - 1 \le n\) and \(\log_2\) is increasing. There are \(n\) arrivals, so the total is at most \(n(\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1)\).
438: P2 · The loop never trades past its price
439: Prove that every trade of a buy at price \(p\) under Definition 2.1 is at a price \(a \le p\).
440: Solution
441: The pseudocode records a trade only after the test that stops the loop when the lowest live ask \(a\) is above \(p\). So a trade happens only when \(a \le p\), and the trade is at price \(a\). Since the lowest live ask is the price used, every trade price satisfies \(a \le p\).
442: P3 · The traded quantity
443: Let \(q\) be the buy's quantity and \(Q\) the total live quantity on the ask side at prices at most \(p\). Prove that the total traded by the buy is \(\min\{q, Q\}\).
444: Solution
445: Each pass of the loop trades the smaller of the remaining and resting quantities, and reduces the remaining quantity by that amount, and it reduces the live quantity at crossing prices by the same amount. The loop stops in one of two ways. Either the remaining quantity reaches 0, so the total traded is \(q\), and then \(q \le Q\) because the traded amount never exceeds the live quantity. Or no live crossing quantity remains while the remaining quantity is still positive, so the total traded is \(Q\), and then \(Q < q\). In both cases the total is \(\min\{q, Q\}\).
446: Empirical
447: E1 · The cancellation share
448: Lab 1, part B reports 102 cancellations among 400 messages. Compute the share, and say what rule in the code sets it.
449: Solution
450: The share is \(102/400 = 25.5\%\) of all 400 messages. The code cancels at a step when a live order exists and a seeded draw from 0 to 99 is below 25, so the expected share is 25% of the steps that have a live order. Those steps number 398, because the first and third messages have no live order, and \(102/398 = 25.6\%\) of them are cancellations. The lab does not test the share against 25%; it reports the count.
451: E2 · Why the queue steps are few
452: Lab 1, part C reports 7 queue steps against 298 orders added. Explain why the count is small.
453: Solution
454: A queue step passes one dead entry at the head of a level. The head moves only inside a fill, when the fill examines the front of the best level. A level that empties by cancellation is deleted with the dead entries it still holds, which are never passed; entries that an earlier fill had already passed were counted at that fill. A level that empties by a fill passes its dead entries that come before the final live order, because the head reaches them before that order is filled. The lab's 7 steps are dead entries at the head of levels that a fill examined, and they are far below the 298 orders added.
455: E3 · What a comparison count of 4 implies
456: Lab 1, part C reports a largest comparison count of 4 over all binary searches. What does Proposition 3.2 imply about the list that produced it?
457: Solution
458: A search that makes 4 tests satisfies \(4 \le \lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor + 1\), so \(\lfloor \log_2 L \rfloor \ge 3\), so \(L \ge 8\). The list had at least 8 prices. The lab does not print the list lengths, so this is a bound, not a measured length.
459: Research
460: R1 · Last-in first-out is not price-time priority
461: Two asks of 1 share each rest at 10000, the first arriving as order 1 and the second as order 2. A buy of 1 share at 10000 arrives. Show that a rule that fills the newest order first at a price gives a different trade from Definition 2.1.
462: Solution
463: Definition 2.1 fills the earliest arrival at the price, so the buy trades 1 share with order 1. The newest-first rule fills order 2. The two trades differ in the resting order's identifier, so the rules are different, and Proposition 2.2 does not hold for the newest-first rule.
464: R2 · Levels bounded by live orders
465: Assuming Assumption 2 of Proposition 2.2, prove that at any time the number of price levels on one side is at most the number of live orders on that side.
466: Solution
467: By Assumption 2, which is the invariant of Definition 1.1, every price in the sorted list has at least one live order, and a price with a live order is listed on exactly one level. Mapping each listed price to one of its live orders is injective, since two different prices have different orders. So the number of levels is at most the number of live orders on the side.
468: R3 · Why the best price must be recomputed
469: Explain why the matching loop must read the best ask again after each fill. Say what a stale read of the old best ask raises in the lab's code.
470: Solution
471: After a level is filled completely and removed, the best ask is a different price. Suppose the asks are 1 share at 10000 and 5 shares at 10005, and a buy of 3 shares at 10010 arrives. The first read gives 10000. After that fill the remaining buy is 2 shares, and the next best ask is 10005, which is at most 10010, so it must trade. If the loop used the stale price 10000 again, it would look up the removed level in the line that reads self.levels[opposite][best], and that dictionary lookup raises a KeyError. By reading the code, the stale read stops with an error and produces no trade. The lab never makes this read, so this answer is a reading of the code and is not a printed result.
