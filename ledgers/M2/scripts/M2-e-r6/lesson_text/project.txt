Sensitivity Engine
A desk reports how a bond price moves when its yield moves, in cents, at first and second order. Each sensitivity can be computed by a formula, by a finite difference, or by automatic differentiation (AD, Lesson 14), and the finite difference loses accuracy at small steps. You will write the module that computes all three, the Hessian of a function of two variables and the price in integer cents, and then write a note that shows where each numerical method is accurate and where it breaks.

Lab 1 · Worked example: one bond, step by step
The bond has face value 1000000 cents, a 5.00 % annual coupon, ten annual payments, and a yield of 4.00 %. Each year pays 50000 cents of coupon, and the last year adds the face value. Stated before running: the unrounded price is 1081108.96 cents, which rounds to 1081109 cents; the analytic \(dP/dy\) is about \(-8514667.40\) cents per unit of yield, and its central difference with step \(10^{-5}\) differs from it by about \(1.8\times10^{-9}\) of its size, relative to the analytic value; the analytic \(d^2P/dy^2\) is about 83766485.12, and its central difference differs from it by about \(10^{-7}\) of its size, relative to the analytic value; the duration, the ratio \(-P'/P\) in years, is 7.88. The derivatives use the chain rule of Lesson 5 on each discount factor \((1+y)^{-t}\), and the central differences are the finite-difference derivative of Lesson 13.
"""Rung 1 worked example: price and yield sensitivities of one SIMULATED bond, analytic and by central difference.
The bond is SIMULATED, not market data: face value 1000000 cents (10000.00), an annual coupon of 5.00 %, ten annual
payments, and a yield of 4.00 % compounded once a year. The program prints the unrounded price in cents, the rounded
price, the first and second derivatives in the yield by their cash-flow formulas and by central differences with
step 1e-5, the differences between each formula and its central difference, and the duration -P'/P in years.
Standard library only.
"""
Flow = tuple[int, float]
def price_cents(flows: list[Flow], y: float) -> float:
    """The unrounded price in cents at the annual yield y: the sum of cash_t / (1 + y)**t over the flows."""
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    return sum(cash * v**t for t, cash in flows)
def main() -> None:
    face_cents = 1_000_000  # SIMULATED face value, 10000.00 in cents
    coupon = face_cents * 0.05  # cents paid at the end of each year
    years = 10
    y = 0.04
    # Step 1. The cash flow of year t: the coupon, and the face value added in the last year.
    flows = [(t, coupon + (face_cents if t == years else 0)) for t in range(1, years + 1)]
    # Step 2. The discount factor of year t is v**t with v = 1 / (1 + y); the price is the sum of the discounted flows.
    price = price_cents(flows, y)
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    # Step 3. The derivative of v**t in y is -t * v**(t+1), so dP/dy = -sum t * cash * v**(t+1). The second derivative
    # of v**t is t(t+1) * v**(t+2), so d2P/dy2 = sum t(t+1) * cash * v**(t+2).
    first = -sum(t * cash * v ** (t + 1) for t, cash in flows)
    second = sum(t * (t + 1) * cash * v ** (t + 2) for t, cash in flows)
    # Step 4. The same two derivatives by central differences of the unrounded price, with step h = 1e-5.
    h = 1e-5
    up, down = price_cents(flows, y + h), price_cents(flows, y - h)
    first_numeric = (up - down) / (2 * h)
    second_numeric = (up - 2 * price + down) / (h * h)
    print("unrounded price (cents):", f"{price:.4f}")
    print("rounded price (cents, half to even):", round(price))
    print("dP/dy analytic (cents per unit yield):", f"{first:.4f}")
    print("dP/dy central difference:", f"{first_numeric:.4f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(first - first_numeric):.2e}")
    print("d2P/dy2 analytic (cents per squared unit yield):", f"{second:.4f}")
    print("d2P/dy2 central difference:", f"{second_numeric:.4f}")
    print("difference:", f"{abs(second - second_numeric):.2e}")
    print("duration -P'/P (years):", f"{-first / price:.6f}")
main()

Lab 2 · Completion problem: the second difference and the dual product
Replace each ___ with the expression below. The central second difference is \((f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h))/h^2\), and adding the expansions of \(f(x+h)\) and \(f(x-h)\) in Lesson 13 and dividing by \(h^2\) gives it up to the remainder terms; the product rule of Lesson 14 says that the dot part of a product of two dual numbers (a value with its derivative, Lesson 14) is \(a\,\dot b + \dot a\,b\). The printed difference of 4.44e-16 is the floating-point error of the sum; the derivative is exact up to that error. The test cubic is written in Horner form, so the same function runs on a float and on a dual number.
Rung 3 · Specification
          Context
          A desk reports the price of each bond in integer cents and the sensitivity of that price to its yield, first and second order, so that a reader can see how far the price moves for a given move in the yield. The formulas are exact, but the desk also computes the same sensitivities numerically, and it needs to know where each numerical method is accurate. You write the module to the interface below, check it against the analytic values, and show in a note where each numerical method breaks.
          Inputs
          No data file. The autograder's instance comes from grader/instance.py, seeded by your workspace ID. It holds a SIMULATED bond (a face value in whole cents, a coupon in whole basis points, where one basis point is 0.0001, a maturity in whole years, and a yield in whole basis points), a cubic test function of one variable with a point x0, and a test function of two variables, a quadratic with a cubic cross term, with a point. All of it is SIMULATED. The note uses the bond of Lab 3: 2500000 cents face, a 6.00 % coupon, 12 years and a 5.00 % yield. It is an illustrative input and not market data.
          Required outputs
          In a repository of your own: sensitivity_engine.py at its root, with the five functions below, and a report/ folder beside it. Start from the starter file, whose signatures are complete. The functions are fd_derivative(f, x, h, scheme), returning a float; ad_derivative(f, x), returning a float; hessian_fd(f, x, h), returning a square list of lists of floats; bond_price_cents(instance), returning an int; and sensitivities(instance), returning a dictionary of floats with the keys dprice_dy, d2price_dy2, duration_analytic and convexity_analytic. For the note, commit three more files: report/run_report.py, which is Lab 3 with its calls to the difference function replaced by calls to fd_derivative, its analytic derivative replaced by sensitivities(instance)["dprice_dy"], where instance is a dictionary holding the Lab 3 bond (face_cents 2500000, coupon_bps 600, maturity_years 12 and yield_bps 500), and its unrounded price function kept, because bond_price_cents returns only the rounded integer, and which prints the same lines (the backward quotient in its kink line is fd_derivative(f, x - h, h, "forward"), which is the same quotient in exact arithmetic); report/error_analysis.md, the note described in the brief with its appendix; and report/self_assessment.md, the summary that the form in the self-assessment section produces.
          Constraints
          The standard library, or NumPy, only. Three conditions raise ValueError, and the conventions below name them: a step that is not above zero, a scheme other than "forward" or "central", and a yield at or below -1. A step above zero is accepted by fd_derivative at the sizes the suite tests, from \(10^{-300}\) to \(10^{6}\), and by hessian_fd at the sizes it tests, from \(10^{-4}\) to 1. No other exception is required, and no other input is specified. No function prints or warns. A run in your browser stops after 120 seconds. The conventions in the next subsection fix the quantities, their units and the three refusals. Where a convention names a formula for an estimate or an entry, the suite checks that estimate's value and order (the power of \(h\) in its error, as in the truncation errors of Lesson 13), or that entry's value, and the last subsection of this part lists what it does not check.
          Grading mode
          Both modes apply. The code is autograded by grader/test_project.py, which runs 87 tests with warnings as errors. The tests cover the orders of the two finite differences from two steps, the value of the forward estimate (at a step of 10-9 and at larger steps), the forward-mode derivative (including a sum and a difference of two quantities that depend on x, a plain number beside them, a constant, and a point far from zero) and the Hessian against their analytic values, the symmetry of the Hessian, the price in integer cents against an exact rational calculation (including prices just either side of a half cent, and a price at a negative yield above -1), the sensitivities against central differences of the unrounded price at a positive and at a negative yield, and against their exact formulas at a yield of -0.99 for a 30-year bond, the three named ValueError conditions in both directions, with the scheme given as None, an integer, a bool, a float, bytes, or a string other than forward or central, and the purity of the module, which covers each of the five named functions. The same file runs in your browser and on your machine. It is not secret: its integrity comes from your seeded instance and from a second instance. Conventions
Rates and yields. A rate is a decimal per period: 0.04 is 4 %. The yield is an annual rate compounded once a year. The instance gives it in whole basis points, so the yield is yield_bps / 10000, and the coupon rate is coupon_bps / 10000.
Cash flows. A bond with face value \(F\) cents, a coupon rate \(c\) and \(H\) whole years pays \(C_t = Fc\) cents at the end of each year \(t < H\), and \(C_H = Fc + F\) at the end of year \(H\). With \(F > 0\) and \(c \ge 0\), every cash flow is non-negative and the last one is positive.
Unrounded price. The price is \(P(y) = \sum_{t=1}^{H} C_t\,(1+y)^{-t}\) in cents. It is not rounded. The derivatives are taken of this unrounded price.
Price in integer cents. bond_price_cents returns \(P(y)\) rounded to the nearest whole cent, an exact half cent going to the even cent, as an int. This is half-to-even rounding, and no other rounding rule is used.
Sensitivities. \(P'(y) = -\sum_{t} t\,C_t\,(1+y)^{-t-1}\) is dprice_dy, in cents per unit of yield. \(P''(y) = \sum_{t} t(t+1)\,C_t\,(1+y)^{-t-2}\) is d2price_dy2, in cents per squared unit of yield. Both are floats, not money.
Named ratios. duration_analytic is \(-P'(y)/P(y)\), in years, and convexity_analytic is \(P''(y)/P(y)\), in years squared. The two names are previews. The module computes these formulas, and their later development is not part of this project.
Forward and central differences. For a step \(h > 0\), the forward estimate of \(f'(x)\) is \((f(x+h)-f(x))/h\), and the reference's central estimate is the central quotient \((f(x+h)-f(x-h))/(2h)\). Both are finite-difference derivatives of Lesson 13. The suite checks the value and the order of each estimate, and it requires no particular formula for the central estimate beyond those two properties (see the last subsection of this part).
Hessian. For a step \(h > 0\), the diagonal entry \(i\) is \((f(x+he_i)-2f(x)+f(x-he_i))/h^2\), and the off-diagonal entry \((i,j)\) is \((f(x+he_i+he_j)-f(x+he_i-he_j)-f(x-he_i+he_j)+f(x-he_i-he_j))/(4h^2)\). The result is a list of lists, the matrix of second partial derivatives, the Hessian of Lesson 19. The reference uses these formulas. At step \(10^{-4}\) the suite checks each entry's value against its tolerance below, so another formula that meets that tolerance is accepted at that step. At steps \(10^{-3}\) and \(1\) the suite checks the entries of these formulas themselves, so the step is not changed.
Automatic differentiation. ad_derivative(f, x) evaluates \(f\) on an object of your own design that carries a value and a derivative together (the reference uses a dual number), and returns the derivative at \(x\). The function \(f\) is written with numbers and the operators +, - and *. In exact arithmetic the result is the derivative of the function, and in floating-point arithmetic it differs from that derivative by a rounding error. The suite checks this at the instance's point and at \(x = 10^{5}\) for \(f(x) = x^{3}\), whose derivative is \(3\times10^{10}\).
The three ValueError conditions. (a) A step \(h\) that is not above zero raises ValueError in fd_derivative and in hessian_fd. That covers zero, negative zero, NaN, and negative steps from \(-10^{-300}\) to negative infinity; a step above zero is accepted by fd_derivative at the sizes from \(10^{-300}\) to \(10^{6}\). For hessian_fd the suite accepts steps from \(10^{-4}\) to 1, and a step so small that its square is not a usable float is not tested. (b) A scheme other than "forward" or "central" raises ValueError in fd_derivative, whatever its type: None, the integer 1, True, the float 1.0 and the bytes b"central" are not the two names. (c) A yield at or below -1, that is yield_bps at or below -10000, raises ValueError in bond_price_cents and in sensitivities. A yield above -1 is accepted, and the price at a negative yield above -1 is rounded half to even like any other price.
Tolerances. A comparison passes when its error is at most the tolerance times the larger of 1 and the magnitude of the expected value. The tolerances are: \(10^{-6}\) for the central difference of the cubic at step \(10^{-5}\); \(10^{-9}\) for the forward-mode derivative of the cubic, and \(10^{-9}\) for the forward-mode derivative of \(x^{3}\) at \(x = 10^{5}\) (the expected value is \(3\times10^{10}\)); \(10^{-5}\) for each Hessian entry at step \(10^{-4}\); at steps \(10^{-3}\) and \(1\), \(10^{-9}\) for each Hessian entry against the formula of this part at that step, with an allowance of \(10^{-12}/h^{2}\) for rounding error; \(10^{-9}\) for the forward estimate at steps \(10^{-9}\), \(1\), \(5\) and \(10^{6}\) against \((f(x+h)-f(x))/h\) at that step; \(10^{-6}\) for the forward estimate of the cubic at step \(10^{-3}\) against \(f'(x) + \tfrac{h}{2}f''(x) + h^2 p_3\), which is the exact forward quotient of a cubic, where \(p_3\) is the coefficient of \(x^3\); \(10^{-6}\) for symmetry, as the difference of the two off-diagonal entries against one plus the larger of their absolute values; \(10^{-6}\) for dprice_dy against the central difference of the unrounded price at step \(10^{-5}\); \(10^{-4}\) for d2price_dy2 against the second central difference at step \(10^{-5}\); \(10^{-9}\) for the two named ratios; and \(10^{-9}\) for dprice_dy and d2price_dy2 at a yield of \(-0.99\) (a 30-year bond of 1000000 cents face and a coupon of 500 basis points) against the exact rational values of their stated formulas. The order checks take the errors at steps \(10^{-2}\) and \(5 \times 10^{-3}\). For the forward estimate of the cubic, the ratio of the two errors must lie from 1.9 to 2.1. For the central estimate of \(\sin x\) at the instance's point \(x_0\), the ratio must be at least 3.8: the plain central quotient gives a ratio between 3.9999842 and 3.9999851, rounded to seven decimal places, at the points \(x_0\) of the two instances of each of seeds 0 to 49 (computed from the unrounded \(\sin\) values), and a larger ratio, such as a Richardson extrapolation (defined under What the suite does not check, below) gives, is accepted.
What the suite does not check
The suite does not fix a default step, a particular algorithm, or an internal representation. An implementation may use a Richardson extrapolation (a combination of the central quotients at steps \(h\) and \(h/2\) that cancels the leading \(h^2\) term of the error), a complex-step derivative in ad_derivative (the imaginary part of \(f(x+ih)\) divided by \(h\), for a function that is real on the real line), reverse-mode automatic differentiation (Lesson 14), a NumPy vector sum, or an exact rational cash-flow sum. The suite accepts a Richardson-extrapolated central estimate, a complex-step ad_derivative, and an exact rational price. A failing test names the property it checks and the idea to revisit, and it never prints the expected value.

Stated before running: the unrounded price is 2721581.29 cents, which rounds to 2721581 cents; the analytic \(dP/dy\) is \(-23407826.81\) cents per unit of yield; the smallest error of the central difference over the sweep is at step \(10^{-6}\), where it is 7.1e-4 cents per unit of yield; the observed order of each scheme, which is the log10 of the ratio of its errors at steps \(10^{-2}\) and \(10^{-3}\), is about 1 for the forward difference and 2 for the central difference; the kink \(|x-0.3|\), a corner where the slopes on its two sides differ, gives +1, -1 and 0 for the forward, backward and central differences at \(x = 0.3\); and the cancellation case \(10^{8}+\sin x\), where the difference of two nearly equal floats loses the derivative as the step shrinks. Copy this file into report/run_report.py, replace its calls to difference with calls to fd_derivative and its analytic exact with sensitivities(instance)["dprice_dy"], and keep its unrounded price function, because bond_price_cents returns only the integer. It must print the same lines.
"""
The bond is SIMULATED, not market data: face value 2500000 cents (25000.00), an annual coupon of 6.00 %, twelve
annual payments, and a yield of 5.00 %. The program prints the unrounded price, the analytic dP/dy with its forward
and central differences over a sweep of steps from 1e-1 to 1e-12, the step with the smallest central error, the
observed orders from a tenfold cut in the step, a kink case |x - 0.3| at x = 0.3 by forward, backward and central
differences, and a cancellation case 1e8 + sin(x) at x = 0.5 by the central difference. Standard library only.
"""
import math
from collections.abc import Callable
Flow = tuple[int, float]
def bond_price(flows: list[Flow], y: float) -> float:
    """The unrounded price in cents at the annual yield y."""
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    return sum(cash * v**t for t, cash in flows)
def difference(f: Callable[[float], float], x: float, h: float, scheme: str) -> float:
    """A forward, backward or central difference estimate of f'(x) with step h."""
    if scheme == "forward":
        return (f(x + h) - f(x)) / h
    if scheme == "backward":
        return (f(x) - f(x - h)) / h
    return (f(x + h) - f(x - h)) / (2 * h)
def main() -> None:
    face_cents = 2_500_000  # SIMULATED face value, 25000.00 in cents
    coupon = face_cents * 0.06  # cents paid at the end of each year
    years = 12
    y = 0.05
    flows = [(t, coupon + (face_cents if t == years else 0)) for t in range(1, years + 1)]
    price = bond_price(flows, y)
    v = 1 / (1 + y)
    exact = -sum(t * cash * v ** (t + 1) for t, cash in flows)
    print("unrounded price (cents):", f"{price:.4f}")
    print("rounded price (cents, half to even):", round(price))
    print("dP/dy analytic (cents per unit yield):", f"{exact:.6f}")
    def price_of(yield_rate: float) -> float:
        return bond_price(flows, yield_rate)
    steps = [10.0**-k for k in range(1, 13)]
    errors: dict[float, tuple[float, float]] = {}
    print("step h | forward error | central error")
    for h in steps:
        fwd = abs(difference(price_of, y, h, "forward") - exact)
        cen = abs(difference(price_of, y, h, "central") - exact)
        errors[h] = (fwd, cen)
        print(f"{h:.0e} | {fwd:.6e} | {cen:.6e}")
    best = min(steps, key=lambda h: errors[h][1])

              Truncation and floating-point error
By Taylor's theorem with the remainder in Lagrange form, \(f(x+h) = f(x) + hf'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(\xi)\) for some \(\xi\) between \(x\) and \(x+h\). So the forward error is \(\tfrac{h}{2}f''(\xi)\), of order \(h\) (A1). To the third order, \(f(x\pm h) = f(x) \pm hf'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x) \pm \tfrac{h^3}{6}f'''(\xi_\pm)\), so the central error is of order \(h^2\) (A2). The sweep over steps from 1e-1 to 1e-12 shows the forward error at 1e-6 is 1.302673e+02, and the central error at 1e-6 is 7.111207e-04, the smallest central error printed. Below 1e-6 the central error is larger than its minimum at every printed step: 2.329569e-02 at 1e-7 and 5.004462e+02 at 1e-12. Each price is a float near 2.7 million, whose spacing is 2^-31, about 4.7e-10 cents; the difference of two prices divided by 2h then magnifies that error by 1/(2h), which is 5e11 at h = 1e-12 (A3). The lab prints the errors, not the spacing, so this is the stated mechanism and not a measured one.
