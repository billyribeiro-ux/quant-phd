M2-c115 | L09 | grade=None | basis=-
  F: What is a partition of [a, b], and what is its mesh?
  B: A finite list a = x_0 < x_1 < x_2 and so on up to x_n = b. The subintervals are [x_(i-1), x_i] with widths Delta x_i = x_i - x_(i-1), and the mesh is the largest width max_i Delta x_i (Section 1).
M2-c116 | L09 | grade=None | basis=-
  F: What are the upper and lower sums of a bounded f for a partition P?
  B: U(f, P) = sum over i of M_i Delta x_i and L(f, P) = sum over i of m_i Delta x_i, where M_i and m_i are the supremum and infimum of f on the i-th subinterval. Since m_i <= M_i, L(f, P) <= U(f, P) always.

M2-c120 | L09 | grade=None | basis=-
  F: Definition 1.3: when is f integrable and what is the definite integral?
  B: For bounded f on [a, b], put I_* = sup over P of L(f, P) and I^* = inf over Q of U(f, Q). f is integrable when I_* = I^*, and the common value is the definite integral, written as the integral from a to b of f(x) dx. The convention for reversed limits is the integral from b to a equal to minus the integral from a to b, and the integral from a to a is 0.
