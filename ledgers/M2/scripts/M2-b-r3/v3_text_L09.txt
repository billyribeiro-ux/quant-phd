   52 An integral is the area under a curve, and a sum of many thin rectangles is the way to define it without assuming what it is. Two sums bound it from above and below, and a function is integrable when the best upper bound and the best lower bound agree. This lesson defines the integral this way, proves that it exists for every monotone function (Section 2), and bounds the error of a Riemann sum (Section 1) of a monotone function by the mesh (the width of the widest subinterval, defined in Section 1) times the total change of the function (Corollary 2.3). The common value of the sums is what Lesson 10 turns into a formula.
   54 1
   54 Partitions, sums and the definite integral
   56 Let \([a, b]\) be a closed interval with \(a < b\). A partition \(P\) of \([a, b]\) is a finite list \(a = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n = b\). Its subintervals are \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), with widths \(\Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}\), and its mesh is \(\delta(P) = \max_i \Delta x_i\), the width of the widest subinterval.
   58 Let \(f\) be bounded on \([a, b]\). On each subinterval set \(M_i = \sup\{f(x) : x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\}\) and \(m_i = \inf\{f(x) : x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\}\). The upper sum and the lower sum of \(f\) for \(P\) are \[ U(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} M_i\,\Delta x_i, \qquad L(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i\,\Delta x_i. \] Since \(m_i \le M_i\), always \(L(f, P) \le U(f, P)\).
   62 For tags \(t_i \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\), the sum \(R = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(t_i)\,\Delta x_i\) is a Riemann sum of \(f\) for \(P\). Since \(m_i \le f(t_i) \le M_i\) for each \(i\), every Riemann sum lies between the lower and upper sums of its partition.
   65 Lemma 1.1
   65 (refinement)
   66 Let \(Q\) contain every point of \(P\). Then \(L(f, P) \le L(f, Q)\) and \(U(f, Q) \le U(f, P)\).
   70 Proof
   71 It suffices to add one point \(t\) to \(P\), and then induct on the number of added points. Let \(t\) lie strictly inside the subinterval \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), so that it splits it into \([x_{i-1}, t]\) and \([t, x_i]\). The supremum over a smaller set is at most the supremum over the larger, so \(M' \le M_i\) and \(M'' \le M_i\), where \(M'\) and \(M''\) are the suprema on the two pieces. Hence \(M'(t - x_{i-1}) + M''(x_i - t) \le M_i (x_i - x_{i-1})\), and the upper sum does not increase. The same argument with infima shows that the lower sum does not decrease.
   75 Lemma 1.2
   75 (every lower sum is below every upper sum)
   76 For any two partitions \(P\) and \(Q\) of \([a, b]\), \(L(f, P) \le U(f, Q)\).
   80 Proof
   81 Let \(S\) be the partition formed by all the points of \(P\) and \(Q\). Then \(S\) contains \(P\) and \(Q\), so Lemma 1.1 gives \(L(f, P) \le L(f, S)\) and \(U(f, S) \le U(f, Q)\). The middle inequality \(L(f, S) \le U(f, S)\) holds because \(m_i \le M_i\) on each subinterval of \(S\). Chaining the three gives \(L(f, P) \le U(f, Q)\).
   85 Definition 1.3
   85 (integrable function and the definite integral)
   86 Let \(f\) be bounded on \([a, b]\). Set \(I_* = \sup_P L(f, P)\) and \(I^* = \inf_Q U(f, Q)\), over all partitions. By Lemma 1.2, \(I_* \le I^*\). The function \(f\) is integrable on \([a, b]\) when \(I_* = I^*\), and the common value is the definite integral, written \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\). Thus \(L(f, P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f, P)\) for every partition \(P\). The convention \(\int_b^a f = -\int_a^b f\) is used when the limits are reversed, and \(\int_a^a f = 0\).
   90 Remark 1.4
   90 (the definition is the Darboux form)
   91 Definition 1.3 uses upper and lower sums only. The Riemann sums of Section 1 are the other common form, and Corollary 2.3 shows that for monotone \(f\) the two forms agree in the limit of small mesh. The integrability results proved on this page are for monotone functions only (monotone functions are defined in Section 2). Misconception 2 uses the non-monotone integrand \(x(1-x)\) and cites later lessons for its integrability, and those citations are not proved here.
   94 2
   94 Monotone functions are integrable
   96 A function \(f\) is nondecreasing on \([a, b]\) when \(s \le t\) implies \(f(s) \le f(t)\), and nonincreasing when \(s \le t\) implies \(f(s) \ge f(t)\). A monotone function is one of these two. A monotone function on \([a, b]\) is bounded, because \(\min(f(a), f(b)) \le f(x) \le \max(f(a), f(b))\) for every \(x\).
   99 Theorem 2.2
   99 (monotone integrands)
  100 Let \(f\) be monotone on \([a, b]\). Then \(f\) is integrable on \([a, b]\), and for every partition \(P\) with mesh \(\delta\), \[ 0 \le U(f, P) - L(f, P) \le \delta\,|f(b) - f(a)|. \]
  105 Proof
  106 Suppose \(f\) is nondecreasing. On \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\) the supremum is at the right endpoint and the infimum at the left, so \(M_i = f(x_i)\) and \(m_i = f(x_{i-1})\). Therefore \[ U(f,P) - L(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\bigr)\Delta x_i \le \delta \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\bigr) = \delta\bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr), \] since each increment \(f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})\) is nonnegative and the increments telescope. If \(f\) is nonincreasing, the roles of the endpoints swap, and the same computation gives the bound \(\delta(f(a) - f(b)) = \delta|f(b) - f(a)|\). For integrability, let \(\varepsilon > 0\). Take the uniform partition into \(n\) equal parts, with \(n > (b - a)|f(b) - f(a)|/\varepsilon\). Its mesh is \((b-a)/n\), so \(0 \le I^* - I_* \le U(f, P) - L(f, P) \le \varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, \(I^* = I_*\).
  114 Corollary 2.3
  114 (Riemann sums converge at the mesh rate)
  115 Let \(f\) be monotone on \([a, b]\), let \(P\) be a partition with mesh \(\delta\), and let \(R\) be any Riemann sum of \(f\) for \(P\). Then \(\bigl|R - \int_a^b f\bigr| \le \delta\,|f(b) - f(a)|\).
  119 Proof
  120 We have \(L(f, P) \le R \le U(f, P)\) because the tags satisfy \(m_i \le f(t_i) \le M_i\). We also have \(L(f, P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f, P)\) by Definition 1.3. Two numbers that both lie between \(L(f,P)\) and \(U(f,P)\) differ by at most \(U(f,P) - L(f,P)\), which Theorem 2.2 bounds by \(\delta|f(b) - f(a)|\).
  123 3
  123 Example: the integral of \(x^2\)
  126 Lemma 3.1
  126 (sum of squares)
  127 For every integer \(n \ge 1\), \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} i^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6\).
  131 Proof
  132 For \(n = 1\) both sides equal 1. If the formula holds for \(n\), then \(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} i^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 + (n+1)^2 = (n+1)\bigl(n(2n+1) + 6(n+1)\bigr)/6 = (n+1)(2n^2 + 7n + 6)/6 = (n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)/6\), which is the formula for \(n+1\).
  136 Example 3.2
  136 (\(x^2\) on \([0, 1]\))
  137 Let \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0, 1]\), which is nondecreasing. For the uniform partition into \(n\) parts, \(U_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (i/n)^2 (1/n) = (n+1)(2n+1)/(6n^2)\) and \(L_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n}((i-1)/n)^2 (1/n) = (n-1)(2n-1)/(6n^2)\), by Lemma 3.1 applied with \(n - 1\) in place of \(n\), since \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} (i-1)^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} j^2\). Then \(U_n - L_n = 6n/(6n^2) = 1/n\), and \(U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2)\), \(1/3 - L_n = (3n-1)/(6n^2)\). Both differences tend to 0 by the limit laws of Lesson 2 and \(1/n \to 0\) (Lesson 1, Problem M3). Since \(L_n \le \int_0^1 x^2\,dx \le U_n\) for every \(n\), the two differences above give \(|\int_0^1 x^2\,dx - 1/3| \le 1/n\) for every \(n\), so the integral is \(1/3\).
  141 Lab 1 · Upper and lower sums of \(x^2\), a nonuniform partition, and Riemann sums
  142 Stated before running: for \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0, 1]\) and the uniform partitions with \(n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128\) subintervals, \(U_n - L_n = 1/n\) exactly, and the integral \(1/3\) lies between \(L_n\) and \(U_n\) (part A). For a nonuniform partition with points \((i/10)^2\), \(U - L\) is at most the mesh times \(f(1) - f(0)\) (part B). For the uniform partitions with \(n = 8, 16, 32\), midpoint and right-endpoint Riemann sums lie within \(1/n\) of \(1/3\) (part C).
  143 """M2 L09 lab 1: upper and lower Darboux sums for x^2 on [0, 1], and the bound for monotone integrands.
  143 
  143 Part A takes f(x) = x^2 on [0, 1], which is nondecreasing, and the uniform partitions with n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32,
  143 64, 128 subintervals. For a nondecreasing f the supremum on each subinterval is the value at its right endpoint and
  143 the infimum is the value at its left endpoint, so U_n = sum of f(i/n)/n and L_n = sum of f((i-1)/n)/n. The program
  143 checks in exact fractions that U_n - L_n = 1/n, that L_n <= 1/3 <= U_n, and that U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2) and L_n -
  143 1/3 = -(3n-1)/(6n^2). Part B takes a nonuniform partition with points (i/n)^2 and checks that U - L is at most the
  143 mesh times f(1) - f(0). Part C takes the uniform partitions with n = 8, 16 and 32, with midpoint tags and
  143 right-endpoint tags, and checks |R - 1/3| <= 1/n for each Riemann sum R. Exact fractions throughout; standard
  143 library only; no randomness and no network access.
  143 """
  143 
  143 from __future__ import annotations
  143 
  143 from fractions import Fraction
  143 
  143 N_VALUES = (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128)
  143 TARGET = Fraction(1, 3)
  143 
  143 
  143 def f(x: Fraction) -> Fraction:
  143     """f(x) = x^2, nondecreasing on [0, 1]."""
  143     return x * x
  143 
  143 
  143 def upper_uniform(n: int) -> Fraction:
  143     """U_n for f on [0, 1] with n equal subintervals; the supremum on [(i-1)/n, i/n] is f(i/n)."""
  143     return sum((f(Fraction(i, n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
  143 
  143 
  143 def lower_uniform(n: int) -> Fraction:
  143     """L_n for f on [0, 1] with n equal subintervals; the infimum on [(i-1)/n, i/n] is f((i-1)/n)."""
  143     return sum((f(Fraction(i - 1, n)) / n for i in range(1, n + 1)), Fraction(0))
  143 
  143 
  143 def part_a() -> None:
  143     print("part A: f(x) = x^2 on [0, 1], uniform partitions into n subintervals")
  143     print("part A:    n         L_n          U_n        U_n - L_n")
  143     for n in N_VALUES:
  143         lo, up = lower_uniform(n), upper_uniform(n)
  143         if up - lo != Fraction(1, n):
  143             raise AssertionError(f"U - L is not 1/n at n = {n}")
  143         if not lo <= TARGET <= up:
  143             raise AssertionError(f"the integral 1/3 is not between L and U at n = {n}")
  143         if up - TARGET != Fraction(3 * n + 1, 6 * n * n):
  143             raise AssertionError(f"U - 1/3 formula fails at n = {n}")
  143         if lo - TARGET != -Fraction(3 * n - 1, 6 * n * n):
  143             raise AssertionError(f"L - 1/3 formula fails at n = {n}")
  143         print(f"part A: {n:4d}  {float(lo):12.8f}  {float(up):12.8f}  {float(up - lo):12.8f}")

  143     n = 10
  143     points = [Fraction(i * i, n * n) for i in range(n + 1)]
  143     points_are_x_squared = [Fraction(i, n) ** 2 for i in range(n + 1)]

  261 Where the canon breaks
  265 - Upper and lower sums bracket the integral. For \(x^2\) on \([0, 1]\) they do not equal it at any finite \(n\) (Example 3.2). At \(n = 128\), \(U_{128} - 1/3 = (3 \cdot 128 + 1)/(6 \cdot 128^2) = 385/98304\), and Lab 1, part A asserts this formula and prints \(U_{128} = 0.33724976\).
  266 - The error bound uses the mesh, not the count. Corollary 2.3 bounds the error by \(\delta|f(b) - f(a)|\). A partition with few points can have a large mesh, and a partition with many points can have a small one. Lab 1, part B has 10 subintervals (11 points) and mesh \(0.19\), which is larger than \(1/10\).
  267 - Monotonicity is a sufficient condition. Theorem 2.2 proves integrability for monotone functions, and this lesson proves no integrability result for the others. A function that is neither nondecreasing nor nonincreasing is not covered here; Lesson 11, Lemma 1.1, treats sums of integrable functions.
  272 5
  272 Misconceptions
  274 -
  275 “The upper sum with \(n\) rectangles is the integral.”
  276 For \(x^2\), \(U_n - 1/3 = (3n+1)/(6n^2) > 0\) for every \(n\) (Example 3.2). At \(n = 128\), Lab 1 part A prints \(U_{128} = 0.33724976\), not \(0.33333333\). The upper sum is an upper bound, and it converges to the integral as \(n\) grows.
  278 -
  279 “The left and right endpoint sums always bracket the integral.”
  280 For \(f(x) = x(1-x)\) on \([0, 1]\) with \(n = 2\), the left-endpoint sum is \((0 + 1/4)/2 = 1/8\) and the right-endpoint sum is \((1/4 + 0)/2 = 1/8\). Both equal \(1/8\), and both lie below the integral \(1/6\). That value is a forward reference, not derived here: Lesson 10, Theorem 2.1, under Assumption 2.1 of Lesson 6, applied to \(F(x) = x^2/2 - x^3/3\), whose derivative is \(x - x^2\), gives \(F(1) - F(0) = 1/2 - 1/3 = 1/6\). The integrability of \(x - x^2\) follows from Theorem 2.2 for \(x\) and \(x^2\), both nondecreasing on \([0, 1]\), together with Lesson 11, Lemma 1.1 (sums and scalar multiples). For this function the two endpoint sums lie on the same side of the integral. The bracket in Lemma 1.2 uses the suprema and infima, not the endpoint values.
  282 -
  283 “The error of a Riemann sum depends only on the number of subintervals.”
  284 Corollary 2.3 bounds the error by the mesh, so with a nonuniform partition the count does not control the error. Lab 1, part B uses 10 subintervals whose widest one has width \(0.19\), so the bound is \(0.19\), and it is larger than the \(0.1\) that a uniform partition with 10 subintervals would give.

  294 Mechanics
  296 M2-L09 · Upper and lower sums of \(x\) on \([0, 2]\)
  297 For \(f(x) = x\) on \([0, 2]\) and the uniform partition with \(n = 4\), compute \(U\), \(L\), and \(U - L\).
  298 Solution
  299 The width is \(\Delta x = 1/2\), and \(f\) is nondecreasing, so the upper sum uses the right endpoints \(1/2, 1, 3/2, 2\) and the lower sum uses \(0, 1/2, 1, 3/2\). So \(U = (1/2 + 1 + 3/2 + 2)(1/2) = 5/2\) and \(L = (0 + 1/2 + 1 + 3/2)(1/2) = 3/2\). The difference is \(U - L = 1 = (f(2) - f(0))\,\Delta x = 2 \cdot 1/2\), as Theorem 2.2 gives with equality.
  303 M2-L09 · Two-interval sums for \(x^3\)
  304 For \(f(x) = x^3\) on \([0, 1]\) and \(n = 2\), compute \(U\), \(L\), and \(U - L\).
  305 Solution
  306 The subinterval width is \(1/2\), and \(f\) is nondecreasing. The upper sum is \((f(1/2) + f(1))/2 = (1/8 + 1)/2 = 9/16\), and the lower sum is \((f(0) + f(1/2))/2 = (0 + 1/8)/2 = 1/16\). The difference is \(1/2 = (f(1) - f(0))/2\), which matches the bound of Theorem 2.2 with equality.
  310 M2-L09 · A decreasing integrand
  311 For \(f(x) = 1/(1+x)\) on \([0, 1]\) and \(n = 2\), compute \(U\) and \(L\), and find the interval in which the integral lies.
  312 Solution
  313 The function is nonincreasing, so \(M_i\) is the value at the left endpoint and \(m_i\) at the right one. The values at \(0, 1/2, 1\) are \(1, 2/3, 1/2\). So \(U = (1 + 2/3)/2 = 5/6\) and \(L = (2/3 + 1/2)/2 = 7/12\). The integral lies in \([7/12, 5/6]\), and \(U - L = 1/4 = (f(0) - f(1))/2\), as Theorem 2.2 gives.

  321 M2-L09 · The refinement lemma for one point
  322 Prove that adding one point \(t\) to a partition does not decrease the lower sum and does not increase the upper sum, for any bounded \(f\).
  323 Solution
  324 Let \(t\) lie strictly inside \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\). The pieces \([x_{i-1}, t]\) and \([t, x_i]\) are subsets of \([x_{i-1}, x_i]\), so their infima \(m'\) and \(m''\) are at least \(m_i\), and their suprema are at most \(M_i\). Then \(m'(t - x_{i-1}) + m''(x_i - t) \ge m_i(x_i - x_{i-1})\), so the lower sum does not decrease. The same argument with suprema shows the upper sum does not increase. Other subintervals are unchanged.
  328 M2-L09 · The integrability argument
  329 Let \(f\) be nondecreasing on \([a,b]\). Show that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a partition \(P\) with \(U(f,P) - L(f,P) \le \varepsilon\), and deduce that \(I_* = I^*\).
  330 Solution
  331 Choose an integer \(n > (b-a)(f(b) - f(a))/\varepsilon\) and the uniform partition \(P\) into \(n\) parts, with mesh \((b-a)/n\). Theorem 2.2 gives \(U(f,P) - L(f,P) \le (b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n < \varepsilon\). Since \(L(f,P) \le I_*\) and \(I^* \le U(f,P)\), we get \(0 \le I^* - I_* \le \varepsilon\). As \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, \(I^* = I_*\).
  335 M2-L09 · The sum of squares by induction
  336 Prove Lemma 3.1 without citing it, by induction on \(n\), and check the algebra in the step.
  337 Solution
  338 For \(n = 1\), the left side is 1 and the right side is \(1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3/6 = 1\). Assume the formula for \(n\). Adding \((n+1)^2\) gives \(\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1) + 6(n+1)^2}{6} = \frac{(n+1)\bigl(2n^2 + n + 6n + 6\bigr)}{6} = \frac{(n+1)(2n^2 + 7n + 6)}{6}\). Since \(2n^2 + 7n + 6 = (n+2)(2n+3)\), this is \((n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)/6\), the formula for \(n+1\).

  346 M2-L09 · The right-endpoint error at \(n = 8\)
  347 Lab 1, part C prints a right-endpoint error of \(+0.06510417\) at \(n = 8\). Recompute it from Example 3.2.
  348 Solution

  353 M2-L09 · The midpoint error at \(n = 16\)
  354 Lab 1, part C prints a midpoint error of \(-0.00032552\) at \(n = 16\). Compare it with \(-1/(12 \cdot 16^2)\) and state what this comparison does and does not establish.
  355 Solution
  356 \(1/(12 \cdot 256) = 1/3072 = 0.00032552\) to eight decimals, so the printed value equals \(-1/(12n^2)\) at \(n = 16\) to the digits shown. This lesson does not prove that formula for the midpoint sums, so the match is an observation about one function at the three values \(n = 8, 16, 32\) printed in Lab 1, part C, and not a proof. It shows that the midpoint error is negative and of order \(1/n^2\) in this sample, and no more.
  360 M2-L09 · How many parts for a tolerance
  361 For \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0,1]\), how many uniform parts are needed so that the bound of Corollary 2.3 guarantees a Riemann sum within 0.01 of the integral?
  362 Solution
  363 For a uniform partition with \(n\) parts, the mesh is \(1/n\) and \(|f(1) - f(0)| = 1\). Corollary 2.3 gives the bound \(1/n\), which is at most 0.01 when \(n \ge 100\). So 100 uniform parts suffice. The bound is sufficient, not necessary. Every Riemann sum \(R\) of the uniform partition lies in \([L_n, U_n]\), and so does \(1/3\) (Section 1 and Example 3.2). Hence \(|R - 1/3| \le \max(U_n - 1/3, 1/3 - L_n) = (3n+1)/(6n^2)\), which is below 0.01 for \(n = 51\) (at \(n = 50\) it is \(151/15000 = 0.010067\), and at \(n = 51\) it is \(154/15606 = 0.009868\)). So 51 uniform parts keep every Riemann sum within 0.01, while the bound of Corollary 2.3 needs 100.

  371 M2-L09 · A two-sided bound from a trivial partition
  372 Let \(f\) be nondecreasing on \([a,b]\). Prove that \((b-a)f(a) \le \int_a^b f \le (b-a)f(b)\).
  373 Solution
  374 Take the trivial partition \(P = \{a, b\}\), with one subinterval. Its lower sum is \(L(f,P) = f(a)(b-a)\), because the infimum of a nondecreasing function on \([a,b]\) is \(f(a)\), and its upper sum is \(U(f,P) = f(b)(b-a)\). By Definition 1.3, \(L(f,P) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f,P)\), which is the claim.
  378 M2-L09 · Every Riemann sum converges to the integral
  379 For \(f\) monotone on \([a,b]\), prove that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that every Riemann sum with mesh less than \(\delta\) lies within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\int_a^b f\).
  380 Solution
  381 Let \(\delta = \varepsilon/(|f(b) - f(a)| + 1)\). If a partition \(P\) has mesh below \(\delta\), then Corollary 2.3 gives \(|R - \int_a^b f| \le \delta|f(b) - f(a)| < \varepsilon\) for every Riemann sum \(R\) of \(P\), since \(\delta|f(b)-f(a)| \le \varepsilon|f(b)-f(a)|/(|f(b)-f(a)|+1) < \varepsilon\).
  385 M2-L09 · The upper-sum error of the uniform partition
  386 For \(f\) nondecreasing on \([a,b]\) and the uniform partition into \(n\) parts, show that \(0 \le U_n - \int_a^b f \le (b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n\).
  387 Solution
  388 The inequality \(\int_a^b f \le U_n\) is Definition 1.3, and \(\int_a^b f \ge L_n\) gives \(U_n - \int_a^b f \le U_n - L_n\). Theorem 2.2 bounds \(U_n - L_n\) by \((b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/n\) because the mesh is \((b-a)/n\). Both bounds together give the claim.
