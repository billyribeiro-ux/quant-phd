Probability
A risk number is a probability statement. A 99% loss figure says that a loss at or below a stated amount has a stated probability, and the figure can change when the assumed law changes. This module develops the probability you need to read such a statement exactly: random variables and their quantiles, expectation and variance, skewness and kurtosis, independence and covariance, the normal and Student-t laws, the multivariate normal, the laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem, and what happens to sample means when the variance is infinite. It ends in a tail-risk simulator that compares a Gaussian and a Student-t model of portfolio losses with matched covariance, and a proof appendix that states the finiteness conditions behind the comparison.
Prerequisites: M2 Single & Multivariable Calculus (limits and the definite integral) and M1 Precalculus & Mathematical Reasoning (the exponential and the logarithm, and quantifiers). Linear algebra is not a prerequisite. The matrix facts M4 needs are derived from the quadratic form in Lessons 5 and 8.
Project: Tail-risk simulator, graded by an autograder and a rubric for the proof appendix
Data: none. Every seeded sample and generated input is SIMULATED: it is computed from a formula or a seeded simulation, and no real market data is read. The exact labs compute stated tables. This module has no debate brief and no Atlas milestone.
90 h lessons · 30 h project (25% of 120 h)
Objectives
By the end of M4 you can:
Compute the distribution function and the quantile of a finite law from its table, using the left-endpoint convention, and state which convention a reported quantile uses (Lesson 1).
Derive the shortcut \(\operatorname{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (EX)^2\) and the scale rule for variance, and apply both to a loss in integer cents (Lesson 2).
Compute the skewness and kurtosis of a finite law, and state what a zero skewness does not show (Lesson 3).
Decide whether two variables are independent from a joint table, show that zero covariance does not imply independence, and compute a covariance matrix and a correlation (Lessons 4 and 5).
Derive the variance of a Student-t variable for \(\nu > 2\), and state the hypothesis under which the variance is infinite (Lesson 6).
State the hypotheses of the law of large numbers and of the central limit theorem, and decide from them whether a sample mean settles, citing the lab output for the case where it does not (Lessons 9 and 10).
Prerequisites
M4 assumes M2 and, through M2, M1. It uses these concepts without redefining them:
From M2: the limit, used for the definition of an integral over the whole line in Lesson 2, and the definite integral, used in the same place. The multivariate and matrix results of Lessons 5 and 8 use no M3 concept: they are proved from the quadratic form \(w^{\top} \Sigma w\) of the covariance matrix \(\Sigma\), which Lesson 5 defines.
From M1: the exponential and the logarithm, used in Lesson 7, and quantifiers, used for the statements of the limit theorems in Lesson 9.
M4 does not use the matrix concepts of M3. A determinant, a matrix inverse and a factor \(A\) with \(AA^{\top} = \Sigma\) are described in prose where they first appear, and their use is limited to the statements that the pages prove.
Placement diagnostic
Ten questions. Eight are on M1 and M2 prerequisites, and two are skill checks, marked as such, on what rungs 1 and 2 of the project teach. The mastery bar is 80 percent, which for ten items means eight correct. To skip rungs 1 and 2, you must reach the bar and answer both skill checks. A result below the bar means that you should work through the worked example in rung 1 before the completion problem in rung 2.
What is the negation of "every student in the class has passed"?
            Options
               Every student in the class has failed
               Some student in the class has not passed
               No student in the class has passed, or some student has
M1 Theorem 3.1: the negation of "for every \(x\), \(P(x)\)" is "for some \(x\), not \(P(x)\)", so the answer is "some student has not passed".
What is \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \tfrac{1}{n}\)?
            Options
               1
               0
               It does not exist
The sequence \(1/n\) decreases to 0 (M2, limit).
What is \(\int_0^1 x \, dx\), as a decimal?
            Answer
\(\int_0^1 x \, dx = \tfrac12 x^2 \big|_0^1 = \tfrac12 = 0.5\) (M2, definite integral).
What is the derivative of \(e^{rt}\) with respect to \(t\)?
            Options
               \(e^{rt}\)
               \(r\, e^{rt}\)
               \(t\, e^{rt - 1}\)
The chain rule with the exponential (M1, exponential and logarithm; M2, derivative).
What is \(\ln(e^3)\)?
            Answer
The logarithm is the inverse of the exponential, so \(\ln(e^3) = 3\) (M1).
What is \(1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18\), as a decimal?
            Answer
The geometric sum is \(\tfrac{1 - (1/2)^4}{1 - 1/2} = \tfrac{15/16}{1/2} = \tfrac{15}{8} = 1.875\) (M1, geometric series).
Under annual compounding at rate \(r\), what is the one-year discount factor?
            Options
               \(1/(1 + r)\)
               \(1 + r\)
               \(e^{-r}\)
The discount factor is the reciprocal of the one-year growth factor (M1, discount factor).
What is \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl(x^2 y\bigr)\)?
            Options
               \(2xy\)
               \(x^2\)
               \(2x\)
Differentiate in \(x\) with \(y\) held fixed (M2, partial derivative).
Skill check (rung 1): a variable takes the value 0 with probability \(\tfrac14\), the value 1 with probability \(\tfrac12\), and the value 2 with probability \(\tfrac14\). What is its 0.5 quantile, using the left-endpoint convention?
            Answer
The distribution function is \(F(0) = \tfrac14 < \tfrac12\) and \(F(1) = \tfrac34 \ge \tfrac12\). The least value whose distribution function reaches \(\tfrac12\) is 1 (Lesson 1, Proposition 3.2). This is the worked example of rung 1.
Skill check (rung 2): a Student-t variable with \(\nu = 5\) degrees of freedom has variance \(\tfrac{\nu}{\nu - 2}\) (Lesson 6). Taking the scale rule for Student-t variables as a hypothesis (not proved here), multiplying the variable by a constant \(s\) multiplies its variance by \(s^2\). Which value of \(s^2\) makes the variance equal to 1? Give it as a decimal.
            Answer
Hypothesis of this check (not proved on this page): the scale rule \(\operatorname{Var}(sV) = s^2 \operatorname{Var}(V)\) for a constant \(s\) and the Student-t variable \(V\). Lesson 2 Proposition 2.3 proves the scale rule for variables with finitely many values only, and the Student-t variable has infinitely many values. Assuming the scale rule, and with \(\operatorname{Var}(V) = \tfrac53\) from Lesson 6 Proposition 2.6 (\(\nu = 5\)) under Assumption 1 of Lesson 6, \(s^2 \cdot \tfrac53 = 1\) gives \(s^2 = \tfrac35 = 0.6\). This is the scale-factor blank of the rung 2 completion problem.
Lessons
Ten lessons of nine study hours each, which sum to the 90 lesson hours on this page. Lessons 1 to 3 cover the distribution, the mean and the variance, and the shape of a law. Lessons 4 and 5 cover independence, covariance and the covariance matrix. Lessons 6 to 8 cover the normal and Student-t laws, the transforms that summarize a law, and the multivariate normal. Lessons 9 and 10 cover the limit theorems and what happens when the variance is infinite. Each lesson page has graded claims, runnable labs, a problem set with worked solutions, and a retrieval quiz that feeds your review deck.
          L01 · 9 hRandom Variables, Distributions and QuantilesFinite probability spaces, random variables, the distribution function, and the quantile function with a stated left-endpoint convention.
          L02 · 9 hExpectation and VarianceExpectation as a probability-weighted sum, variance and standard deviation, the shortcut formula checked on a die and a loss in cents, and the scale rule checked on a die.
          L03 · 9 hSkewness and KurtosisStandardized third and fourth expectations, skewness and kurtosis, their behaviour under affine maps, exact values for a skewed law and a two-point law, and the normal kurtosis of 3 under a stated Gaussian-integral assumption.
          L04 · 9 hIndependence and Conditional Expectation (Elementary)Independence of events and of variables on a finite joint table, zero covariance without independence, and conditional expectation from a table.
          L05 · 9 hCovariance, Correlation and the Covariance MatrixCovariance, the bound on correlation, and the covariance matrix, which is symmetric and positive semidefinite.
          L06 · 9 hNormal and Student-t DistributionsThe normal density and its moments, the Student-t as a normal over an independent chi-square variable, and its variance for \(\nu > 2\).
          L07 · 9 hMoment-Generating and Characteristic FunctionsThe moment-generating function of the normal law by completing the square, and the characteristic function of a two-point law.
          L08 · 9 hThe Multivariate NormalA linear image of a standard normal vector, its covariance \(AA^{\top}\), and that a singular covariance matrix has no inverse.
          L09 · 9 hLaws of Large Numbers and the Central Limit TheoremThe weak law by Chebyshev's inequality, the characteristic-function limit of the standardized sample mean, with the distribution-function limit stated and not established here, and the limits stated with M2's limit.
          L10 · 9 hHeavy Tails and Infinite VarianceHeavy-tailed laws defined by tail decay, the Cauchy and Student-t(3) sample means in Lab 1, and the failure of the CLT under infinite variance.
The project ladder
The module ends in the tail-risk simulator. Rungs 1 and 2 scaffold the skills the placement check tests. To skip them, reach the placement bar and answer both skill checks.
Worked example
A fully solved and annotated computation for one seeded instance: the Gaussian portfolio loss distribution, its 99% quantile under the page's convention, and the matched Student-t scale factor, with each step explained.
Completion problem
The same task with two lines blanked: the matched-covariance scale factor and the quantile index. Graded instantly in your browser.
Independent project
Implement tail_risk.py with matched_scale, simulate_losses and quantile_99, and write the proof appendix on the finiteness conditions of the matched Student-t. Graded by the autograder and by the rubric. Project page.
Stretch
An open-ended extension with no ceiling, for example a skew-t margin, or a proof of when the variance of a sum of Student-t variables is additive, with the hypothesis checked. Suitable for your portfolio.
