L01 | Definition 1.1 (random variable) | Let \(\Omega\) be a finite probability space. A random variable on \(\Omega\) is a function \(X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\). Its values are the real numbers \(X(\omega)\), one for each outcome.
L01 | Definition 1.2 (distribution and support) | The distribution of \(X\) assigns to each real \(x\) the probability \(P(X = x) = \sum_{\omega \,:\, X(\omega) = x} p(\omega)\). The support of \(X\) is the set of values that \(X\) takes on \(\Omega\). A value \(x\) is in the support exactly when \(P(X = x) > 0\), because every outcome has positive weight.

L02 | Definition 1.1 (expectation of a finite law) | The expectation of \(X\) is \(E[X] = \sum_{k=1}^m x_k\, p_k\). It is the average of the values of \(X\), weighted by their probabilities. The expectation is a real number, and it need not be one of the values of \(X\).
L02 | Proposition 1.2 (expectation over outcomes) | For \(X\) as above, \(\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} X(\omega)\, p(\omega) = \sum_{k=1}^m x_k\, p_k\).
L02 | Proposition 1.3 (linearity on a finite space) | Let \(U\) and \(V\) be random variables on the same finite probability space, and let \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) be real constants. Then \(E[\alpha U + \beta V + \gamma] = \alpha E[U] + \beta E[V] + \gamma\).

L02 | Definition 2.1 (variance and standard deviation) | Let \(\mu = E[X]\). The variance of \(X\) is \(\operatorname{Var}(X) = E[(X - \mu)^2]\), and the standard deviation is \(\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}\). The variance is in squared units of \(X\), and the standard deviation is in the units of \(X\). A variance is never negative, because it is the expectation of a square.
L02 | Proposition 2.2 (shortcut formula) | For \(X\) on a finite probability space with positive weights, \(\operatorname{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2\).
L02 | Proposition 2.3 (scale and shift) | For constants \(a, b\), \(\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}(X)\). In particular \(\operatorname{Var}(X + b) = \operatorname{Var}(X)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(aX)\) is \(a^2\) times the variance of \(X\).
L02 | Proposition 2.4 (zero variance means a single value) | For \(X\) on a finite probability space with positive weights, \(\operatorname{Var}(X) = 0\) if and only if \(X\) takes exactly one value.

L04 | Definition 1.1 (joint table and marginals) | A joint table for X and Y is the list of values \(p(x,y)\) over the pairs of values, each \(p(x,y) \ge 0\), with \(\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) = 1\). Each pair \((x,y)\) of values is a cell of the table. Its marginal probabilities are \(P(X = x) = \sum_{y} p(x,y)\) and \(P(Y = y) = \sum_{x} p(x,y)\). The marginals are the distributions of X and Y alone, as defined in L01.
L04 | Definition 1.2 (independence) | Independence of two events \(A\) and \(B\) means \(P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B)\). Discrete random variables X and Y are independent when \(p(x,y) = P(X = x)\,P(Y = y)\) for every pair \((x,y)\) of values. In words, every cell of the joint table is the product of its two marginals.
L04 | Remark 1.3 (the test is cell by cell) | Independence is a property of the whole joint table. One cell that fails the product rule makes the pair dependent. Lab 1 applies the test to every cell of two tables and prints the verdict.
L04 | Proposition 1.4 (disjoint events are dependent) | Let \(A\) and \(B\) be events with \(A \cap B = \varnothing\), \(P(A) > 0\) and \(P(B) > 0\). Then \(A\) and \(B\) are not independent.
L04 | Proposition 1.5 (the expectation of a product under independence) | Let X and Y be independent random variables with finitely many values. Then \(E[XY] = E[X]\,E[Y]\).
L04 | Definition 1.6 (covariance, defined here and studied in L05) | The covariance of X and Y is \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = E[(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])]\). Only its formula is used in this lesson. L05 defines the correlation from it and proves its properties.
L04 | Proposition 1.7 (the shortcut formula for covariance) | For random variables X and Y with finitely many values, \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = E[XY] - E[X]\,E[Y]\).
L04 | Proposition 1.8 (independence implies zero covariance) | If X and Y are independent with finitely many values, then \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = 0\).

Its modulus is \(|e^{ia}| = \sqrt{\cos^{2}a + \sin^{2}a} = 1\). The expectation of a complex-valued variable is the expectation of its real part plus \(i\) times the expectation of its imaginary part.

L09 | Proposition 1.2 (mean and variance of the sample mean) | Let \(X_1, X_2, \ldots\) be identically distributed with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), and suppose that \(X_i\) and \(X_j\) are independent whenever \(i \ne j\). L09 | Definition 1.4 (law of large numbers) | The law of large numbers holds for a sequence of identically distributed variables with mean \(\mu\) when \(P(|\bar X_n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\), for every \(\varepsilon > 0\). Theorem 1.3 proves it under the hypotheses of Proposition 1.2, for finitely valued laws.
L09 | Theorem 1.3 (weak law of large numbers) | Under the hypotheses of Proposition 1.2, with every law taking finitely many values, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) and every \(n \ge 1\), \[ P(|\bar X_n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}, \] and therefore \(P(|\bar X_n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\).
