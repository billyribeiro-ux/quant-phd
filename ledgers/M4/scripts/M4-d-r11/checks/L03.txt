[proposition] Proposition 1.1 (the first two central moments) grade=T :: For a random variable \(X\) on a finite probability space with positive weights, \(m_1 = 0\) and \(m_2 = \operatorname{Var}(X)\).
[proof] Proof grade=- :: By linearity of expectation (Lesson 2, Proposition 1.3), \(m_1 = E[X] - \mu = \mu - \mu = 0\). The second claim is Definition 2.1 of Lesson 2 with \(k = 2\). \(\blacksquare\)
[definition] Definition 2.1 (standardized variable) grade=- :: Let \(X\) take at least two values on a finite probability space with positive weights, so that \(\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)} > 0\) by Lesson 2, Proposition 2.4. The standardized variable is \(Z = (X - \mu)/\sigma\). It has mean 0 and variance 1, which Proposition 2.3 checks.
[definition] Definition 2.2 (skewness and kurtosis) grade=- :: The skewness of \(X\) is \(\gamma_3 = E[Z^3]\), the third standardized moment. The kurtosis of \(X\) is \(\gamma_4 = E[Z^4]\), the fourth standardized moment. This lesson uses the kurtosis without subtracting 3. Excess kurtosis, the kurtosis minus 3, is a different quantity and is not used on this page. Every kurtosis computed on this page is \(E[Z^4]\) in this sense.
[proposition] Proposition 2.3 (standardized moments in central moments) grade=T :: Let \(X\) take at least two values on a finite probability space with positive weights. Then \(E[Z] = 0\), \(E[Z^2] = 1\), \(\gamma_3 = m_3 / \sigma^3\), and \(\gamma_4 = m_4 / \sigma^4 = m_4 / m_2^2\).
[proof] Proof grade=- :: Write \(Z = (X - \mu)/\sigma\), where \(\sigma\) is a positive constant. By linearity (Lesson 2, Proposition 1.3), \(E[Z^j] = E[(X - \mu)^j]/\sigma^j\) for \(j = 1, 2, 3, 4\), because \((X - \mu)^j / \sigma^j\) is the \(j\)-th power of \(Z\) and dividing by the constant \(\sigma^j\) pulls out of the sum. So \(E[Z] = m_1/\sigma = 0\) and \(E[Z^2] = m_2/\sigma^2 = 1\). Also \(\gamma_3 = E[Z^3] = m_3/\sigma^3\) and \(\gamma_4 = E[Z^4] = m_4/\sigma^4\). Since \(\sigma^4 = m_2^2\), the last form follows. \(\blacksquare\)
