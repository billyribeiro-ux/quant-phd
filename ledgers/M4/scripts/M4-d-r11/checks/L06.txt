[assumption] Assumption 1 (calculus and measure facts used without proof) grade=- :: The page uses the following standard facts without proof. (a) The substitution rule, integration by parts, the fundamental theorem of calculus for improper integrals of continuous functions over the real line, and the derivative of \(e^{u}\), which gives \(\varphi'(z) = -z\varphi(z)\). (b) Polar coordinates in the plane, with Jacobian \(r\). (c) Tonelli's theorem for nonnegative integrands, which lets a double integral equal an iterated integral. (d) For independent variables X and Y, the joint law of the pair is the product of their two laws. Functions of independent variables are independent: if X and Y are independent, then f(X) and g(Y) are independent for the functions f and g that the page applies, and a function of a finite family of independent variables is independent of any variable that is independent of the whole family. Each result that uses Assumption 1 says so in its statement.
[definition] Definition 1.1 (normal distribution) grade=- :: For a real \(\mu\) and \(\sigma > 0\), the normal distribution \(N(\mu, \sigma^{2})\) is the law with density \(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big(-\dfrac{(x - \mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)\) on the real line. The standard normal law is \(N(0,1)\), with density \(\varphi(z) = e^{-z^{2}/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\). A variable Z with this law is a standard normal variable.
[theorem] Proposition 1.2 (the Gaussian integral) grade=T :: Assuming Assumption 1, the integral \(I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^{2}/2}\,dx\) converges and equals \(\sqrt{2\pi}\).
[proof] Proof grade=- :: The integrand is positive and is at most \(e^{-|x|}\) for \(|x| \ge 2\), since \(x^{2}/2 \ge |x|\) there. So the integral converges. Let \(J = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-(x^{2} + y^{2})/2}\,dx\,dy\) over the plane. Since the integrand is nonnegative, Tonelli's theorem (Assumption 1(c)) lets the double integral equal \(I^{2}\), because \(e^{-(x^{2}+y^{2})/2} = e^{-x^{2}/2}e^{-y^{2}/2}\). Change to polar coordinates (Assumption 1(b)) \(x = r\cos\theta\), \(y = r\sin\theta\), with Jacobian r and \(r \ge 0\), \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi)\): \[ I^{2} = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-r^{2}/2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\Big[-e^{-r^{2}/2}\Big]_{0}^{\infty} = 2\pi. \] Since \(I > 0\), \(I = \sqrt{2\pi}\).

[theorem] Proposition 1.4 (standardization) grade=T :: Assuming Assumption 1, let \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^{2})\) with \(\sigma > 0\). Then \(Z = (X - \mu)/\sigma\) has the standard normal law \(N(0,1)\). Conversely, if Z is standard normal, then \(\mu + \sigma Z \sim N(\mu, \sigma^{2})\).
[proof] Proof grade=- :: For real z, \(P(Z \le z) = P(X \le \mu + \sigma z) = \int_{-\infty}^{\mu + \sigma z} f(x)\,dx\). Substituting \(x = \mu + \sigma u\) as in Proposition 1.3 gives \(\int_{-\infty}^{z} \varphi(u)\,du\), which is the distribution function of N(0,1). The converse follows by reversing the substitution.

[definition] Definition 2.1 (chi-square variable, defined by its sum) grade=- :: For a positive integer \(\nu\), a chi-square variable with \(\nu\) degrees of freedom is \(W_{\nu} = Z_{1}^{2} + \dots + Z_{\nu}^{2}\), where \(Z_{1}, \dots, Z_{\nu}\) are independent standard normal variables. Its law is fixed by this sum. The page uses the sum as the definition.
[remark] Remark 2.2 (independence for variables with densities) grade=- :: Variables X and Y are independent when \(P(X \in A,\ Y \in B) = P(X \in A)\,P(Y \in B)\) for all sets A and B. Assuming Assumption 1(d), two consequences are used below. For independent variables and functions g and h that are nonnegative, \(E[g(X)h(Y)] = E[g(X)]\,E[h(Y)]\). This is Tonelli's theorem (Assumption 1(c)) applied to the product of the two laws. Functions of independent variables are independent (Assumption 1(d)). The page uses both facts in the proofs below.
[theorem] Lemma 2.3 (a Laplace transform of a squared normal) grade=T :: Assuming Assumption 1, for \(s \ge 0\), \(E[e^{-sZ^{2}}] = (1 + 2s)^{-1/2}\) for a standard normal Z. [proof] Proof grade=- :: First, \(E[e^{-sZ^{2}}] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-sz^{2}} e^{-z^{2}/2}\,dz = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-(1 + 2s)z^{2}/2}\,dz\). Substitute \(u = \sqrt{1 + 2s}\,z\), so \(dz = du/\sqrt{1 + 2s}\). The integral is \((1 + 2s)^{-1/2}\) times \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-u^{2}/2}\,du = 1\), by Proposition 1.2 and Proposition 1.3. Second, W is zero only if every \(Z_i\) is zero, and \(P(Z_1 = 0) = 0\) because a single point has zero length under a density. So \(W > 0\) with probability one. For \(w > 0\), \(1/w = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-sw}\,ds\). Substitute \(w = W\) and use Tonelli's theorem, since the integrand is nonnegative: \[ E[1/W] = \int_{0}^{\infty} E[e^{-sW}]\,ds. \] By independence (Remark 2.2), \(E[e^{-sW}] = \prod_{i=1}^{\nu} E[e^{-sZ_i^{2}}] = (1 + 2s)^{-\nu/2}\), using the first step for each \(Z_i\). This gives the formula.
