  51|       The tree of Lesson 6 prices a claim by arithmetic at its nodes. Under assumptions A7 and A7′ (Section 1), a call has the closed-form price of Definition 1.3, for a model in which the stock's volatility \(\sigma\) (the standard deviation of its log return per square root of a year) is constant and the riskless rate \(r\) is continuously compounded. This lesson states the formula as assumption A7′, under A7, and does not derive it. It then compares tree prices, whose parameters depend on the step count, with the closed form. The comparison is numerical: Section 3 says what Lab 1 shows and what it does not. Lesson 8 inverts the formula for volatility.
  52| 
  53|       1The closed form under assumption A7
  54| 
  55|       The lesson uses the standing assumptions A1 to A5 of Lesson 4, as they are used here: A1 (no income on the stock over the life of the option), A2 (no transaction costs, and lending and borrowing at the riskless rate), A3 (no arbitrage), A4 (the riskless rate \(r\) is constant over the life \(\tau\) and continuously compounded, so the discount factor to expiry is \(e^{-r\tau}\)), A5 (European exercise, at expiry only), and A7 and A7′ (Section 1). The maturity is written \(\tau\), in years, and not \(T\).
  56| 
  57| 
  58|         Assumption A7 (Constant-volatility lognormal model)
  59|         The terminal stock price is lognormal: its logarithm is normally distributed, with a volatility \(\sigma > 0\) that is constant over the life of the option, and the same \(\sigma\) applies to every strike. The rate \(r\) is constant as in A4. A7 is a model assumption. The closed form is stated as A7′ below and used; the lesson does not derive it from A7.
  60| 
  61| 
  62| 
  63|         Lemma 1.1 (The normal distribution function by the error function)
  64|         The standard normal distribution function \(\Phi(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} \varphi(t)\,dt\), where \(\varphi(t) = e^{-t^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\), satisfies \(\Phi(x) = \tfrac12\big(1 + \operatorname{erf}(x/\sqrt{2})\big)\), with \(\operatorname{erf}(y) = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0^{y} e^{-s^2}\,ds\).
  65| 
  66|       Proof
  67|         Substitute \(t = \sqrt{2}\,s\), so \(dt = \sqrt{2}\,ds\): \(\Phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x/\sqrt{2}} e^{-s^2}\,ds\). Since \(\int_{-\infty}^{0} e^{-s^2}\,ds = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-s^2}\,ds = \tfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\), the integral over \((-\infty, y]\) equals \(\tfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\big(1 + \operatorname{erf}(y)\big)\) with \(y = x/\sqrt{2}\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{\pi}\) gives the claim. \(\blacksquare\)
  68| 
  69| 
  70| 
  71|         Lemma 1.2 (Symmetry of the normal distribution function)
  72|         For every real \(x\), \(\Phi(-x) = 1 - \Phi(x)\).
  73| 
  74|       Proof
  75|         Since \(\varphi(-t) = \varphi(t)\), the substitution \(t \mapsto -t\) gives \(\Phi(-x) = \int_{-\infty}^{-x}\varphi(t)\,dt = \int_{x}^{\infty}\varphi(t)\,dt\). The total integral of \(\varphi\) is 1, so the last integral equals \(1 - \Phi(x)\). \(\blacksquare\)
  76| 
  77| 
  78| 
  79|         Definition 1.3 (Black–Scholes call value)
  80|         The Black–Scholes formula gives the value of a European call with spot \(S\), strike \(K\), rate \(r\), maturity \(\tau\) and volatility \(\sigma\) as
  81|         \[
  82|           C = S\,\Phi(d_1) - K e^{-r\tau}\,\Phi(d_2), \qquad
  83|           d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r + \sigma^2/2)\,\tau}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}}, \qquad
  84|           d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{\tau}.
  85|         \]
  86|         The closed form is attributed to Black and Scholes (1973), registry key black1973pricing, and this lesson does not check the paper's text. Assumption A7′ below takes this formula to be the call value of the model under A7. The lesson uses the formula, and it does not derive it (Section 4).
  87| 
  88| 
  89| 
  90|         Assumption A7′ (Closed form)
  91|         Under A7, the value of the European call is the value \(C\) of Definition 1.3, for every spot \(S > 0\), strike \(K > 0\), rate \(r\), maturity \(\tau > 0\) and volatility \(\sigma > 0\). A7′ is a premise of this lesson and is not proved here. Each result that uses it states it.
  92| 
  93| 
  94| 
  95|         Proposition 1.4 (The put from parity)
  96|         Under A1 to A5, A7 and A7′, the European put with the same spot, strike and maturity has value
  97|         \[
  98|           P = C - S + K e^{-r\tau} = K e^{-r\tau}\,\Phi(-d_2) - S\,\Phi(-d_1),
  99|         \]
 100|         where \(C\) is the value of Definition 1.3.
 101| 
 102|       Proof
 103|         Put-call parity, from Lesson 5, states \(C - P = S - Ke^{-r\tau}\) under A1 to A5, which gives the first equality. For the second, use Definition 1.3 and Lemma 1.2:
 104|         \[
 105|           C - S + Ke^{-r\tau} = -S\big(1 - \Phi(d_1)\big) + Ke^{-r\tau}\big(1 - \Phi(d_2)\big) = Ke^{-r\tau}\Phi(-d_2) - S\Phi(-d_1). \quad \blacksquare
 106|         \]
 107| 
 108| 
 109| 
 110|         Proposition 1.5 (What the formula depends on)
 111|         Under Definition 1.3, \(C\) depends on \(S\), \(K\), \(r\tau\) and \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau}\) only. In particular it does not depend on the expected return of the stock.
 112| 
 113|       Proof
 114|         Split the numerator of \(d_1\), using \((r + \sigma^2/2)\tau = r\tau + \sigma^2\tau/2\), and note that \(\dfrac{\sigma^2\tau/2}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}} = \dfrac{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}}{2}\). So \(d_1 = \dfrac{\ln(S/K) + r\tau}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}} + \dfrac{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}}{2}\). This uses only \(\ln(S/K)\), \(r\tau\) and \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau}\). Then \(d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{\tau}\), and \(Ke^{-r\tau}\) depends on \(r\tau\). The formula contains no drift term. \(\blacksquare\)

 117|       2The binomial tree with fixed parameters
 118| 
 119| 
 120|         Definition 2.1 (Tree parameters for n steps)
 121|         For a number of steps \(n \ge 1\), let \(h = \tau/n\), and let \(u^{(n)} = e^{\sigma\sqrt{h}}\), \(d^{(n)} = 1/u^{(n)} = e^{-\sigma\sqrt{h}}\), \(R^{(n)} = e^{rh}\), and \(q^{(n)} = (R^{(n)} - d^{(n)})/(u^{(n)} - d^{(n)})\). The parameters are the same at every node, which is assumption A6′ of Lesson 6, and A6 of Lesson 6 holds at each node whenever \(r > 0\) and \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\) (Lemma 2.2).
 122| 
 123| 
 124| 
 125|         Lemma 2.2 (The binomial condition for these parameters)
 126|         Let \(r > 0\) and \(\sigma > 0\). Then \(0 < d^{(n)} < R^{(n)} < u^{(n)}\) holds if and only if \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\).
 127| 
 128|       Proof
 129|         The inequality \(0 < d^{(n)}\) holds because it is an exponential. Since \(\exp\) is strictly increasing, \(d^{(n)} < R^{(n)}\) is \(-\sigma\sqrt{h} < rh\), which holds because \(rh > 0 > -\sigma\sqrt{h}\). Also \(R^{(n)} < u^{(n)}\) is \(rh < \sigma\sqrt{h}\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{h} > 0\) gives \(r\sqrt{h} < \sigma\), that is \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\). \(\blacksquare\)
 130| 
 131| 
 132| 
 133|         Theorem 2.3 (The n-step binomial call)
 134|         Under A1, A2, A3, A4, A5 and A7, with \(r > 0\) and \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\), the tree of Definition 2.1 prices the call at
 135|         \[
 136|           V_n = e^{-r\tau} \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} \big(q^{(n)}\big)^{k} (1 - q^{(n)})^{n - k}\, \max\!\big(S \big(u^{(n)}\big)^{k} \big(d^{(n)}\big)^{n-k} - K,\ 0\big).
 137|         \]
 138| 
 139|       Proof
 140|         By Lemma 2.2, A6 holds at each node, and the parameters are the same at every node, which is A6′. Theorem 3.3 of Lesson 6 with \(N = n\) and gross return \(R^{(n)}\) gives the sum with the discount factor \(\big(R^{(n)}\big)^{-n} = e^{-rnh} = e^{-r\tau}\), since \(nh = \tau\). The payoff is that of Lesson 4 at expiry. \(\blacksquare\)
 141| 
 142| 
 143|       Assumption A7 concerns the terminal distribution of the stock, and A7′ links the closed form to the model. Theorem 2.3 is an exact statement about a finite tree, and it does not use the closed form of Definition 1.3 or A7′. A link from Theorem 2.3 to the formula would be a limit in the number of steps. Section 3 prints nine step counts and does not prove that limit. The theorem needs \(r > 0\): at \(r = 0\) the condition \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\) is undefined, and this lesson does not state the theorem for \(r = 0\).

 145|       3Comparing the tree with the closed form
 146| 
 147| 
 148|         Definition 3.1 (Pricing error)
 149|         For \(n\) steps, the pricing error is \(e_n = V_n - C\), where \(V_n\) is the tree price of Theorem 2.3 and \(C\) is the value of Definition 1.3, both in currency units.
 150| 
 151| 
 152|       Lab 1 prices the call at nine step counts and prints each price in integer cents. The prices are positive and are rounded half up for display only. The error column is the unrounded difference, rounded half away from zero, because the error can be negative. In general the error column can differ by one cent from the difference of the two printed price columns, because the error is rounded from the unrounded difference. In the nine printed rows it does not differ. The lab prints the closed form, the nine tree prices and the errors. It does not extend the list, and it does not prove a limit.
 153| 
 154| 
 155|         Lab 1 · Binomial prices against the Black–Scholes value
 156|         Stated before running: with spot 100, strike 100, \(r = 0.05\), \(\sigma = 0.20\) and \(\tau = 1\), the closed form is 1045 cents and the tree prices at the stated step counts are printed beside it. The lab checks that \(\Phi(x) + \Phi(-x) = 1\) at four points and that the put from parity agrees with the separate put formula. Expected result: the error column is positive at 1, 5 and 25 steps, negative at 2, 10, 50, 100 and 200 steps, and 0 cents at 400 steps. All inputs are model values; no data are read.
 157|         """Lab FC2-L07-1: binomial call prices against the Black-Scholes closed form.
 158| 
 159| The curriculum lab for FC2: a recombining binomial tree (up factor exp(sigma sqrt h),
 160| down factor its reciprocal, gross return exp(r h) per period, h = tau / n) is priced for
 161| each step count in STEPS, and each price is compared with the closed-form Black-Scholes
 162| call under assumption A7. The inputs are model parameters (spot 100.00, strike 100.00,
 163| r = 0.05 continuously compounded, sigma = 0.20, tau = 1 year), not market data and not
 164| a forecast. Nothing is random and no file is read.
 165| 
 166| Money is printed in integer cents. Each price is the unrounded model value rounded half
 167| up to the cent for display (the prices are positive). The error column is the unrounded
 168| difference, rounded half away from zero, so it can differ by one cent from the difference
 169| of the two printed columns. The
 170| standard normal distribution function is computed from math.erf (standard library only).
 171| The lab shows numbers; it does not prove that the binomial price converges to the
 172| closed form.
 173| """
 174| 
 175| from __future__ import annotations
 176| 
 177| import math
 178| 
 179| SPOT_CENTS = 10_000
 180| STRIKE_CENTS = 10_000
 181| RATE = 0.05  # continuously compounded riskless rate r (A4)
 182| SIGMA = 0.20  # volatility sigma (A7)
 183| TAU = 1.0  # time to expiry in years
 184| STEPS = (1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400)
 185| 
 186| 
 187| def cents(amount: float) -> int:
 188|     """Integer cents of a currency amount, rounded half away from zero."""
 189|     sign = -1 if amount < 0 else 1
 190|     return sign * math.floor(abs(amount) * 100 + 0.5)
 191| 
 192| 
 193| def norm_cdf(x: float) -> float:
 194|     """Standard normal distribution function, (1 + erf(x / sqrt 2)) / 2."""
 195|     return 0.5 * (1.0 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2.0)))
 196| 
 197| 
 198| def d_terms(spot: float, strike: float, rate: float, tau: float, sigma: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
 199|     """The Black-Scholes d1 and d2 for the given inputs (sigma and tau positive)."""
 200|     root = sigma * math.sqrt(tau)
 201|     d1 = (math.log(spot / strike) + (rate + sigma * sigma / 2.0) * tau) / root
 202|     return d1, d1 - root
 203| 
 204| 
 205| def bs_call(spot: float, strike: float, rate: float, tau: float, sigma: float) -> float:
 206|     """Black-Scholes European call value, in currency units: S Phi(d1) - K exp(-r tau) Phi(d2)."""
 207|     d1, d2 = d_terms(spot, strike, rate, tau, sigma)
 208|     return spot * norm_cdf(d1) - strike * math.exp(-rate * tau) * norm_cdf(d2)
 209| 
 210| 
 211| def bs_put(spot: float, strike: float, rate: float, tau: float, sigma: float) -> float:
 212|     """Black-Scholes European put value, from its own formula: K exp(-r tau) Phi(-d2) - S Phi(-d1)."""
 213|     d1, d2 = d_terms(spot, strike, rate, tau, sigma)
 214|     return strike * math.exp(-rate * tau) * norm_cdf(-d2) - spot * norm_cdf(-d1)
 215| 
 216| 
 217| def binomial_call(spot: float, strike: float, rate: float, tau: float, sigma: float, steps: int) -> float:
 218|     """Price of the European call on a recombining tree with steps periods, by backward induction."""
 219|     h = tau / steps
 220|     up = math.exp(sigma * math.sqrt(h))
 221|     down = 1.0 / up
 222|     gross = math.exp(rate * h)
 223|     q = (gross - down) / (up - down)
 224|     values = [max(spot * up**j * down ** (steps - j) - strike, 0.0) for j in range(steps + 1)]
 225|     for _ in range(steps):
 226|         values = [(q * values[j + 1] + (1.0 - q) * values[j]) / gross for j in range(len(values) - 1)]
 227|     return values[0]
 228| 
 229| 
 230| def main() -> None:
 231|     """Price the call on each tree, compare with the closed form, and check the identities used."""
 232|     spot, strike = SPOT_CENTS / 100, STRIKE_CENTS / 100
 233|     d1, d2 = d_terms(spot, strike, RATE, TAU, SIGMA)
 234|     print(
 235|         f"parameters: spot={SPOT_CENTS} cents strike={STRIKE_CENTS} cents r={RATE} sigma={SIGMA} tau={TAU} years "
 236|         "(model inputs, not market data)"
 237|     )
 238|     print(f"Black-Scholes d1={d1:.6f} d2={d2:.6f} Phi(d1)={norm_cdf(d1):.6f} Phi(d2)={norm_cdf(d2):.6f}")
 239|     closed = bs_call(spot, strike, RATE, TAU, SIGMA)
 240|     print(f"Black-Scholes call = {closed:.6f} currency units = {cents(closed)} cents")
 241|     print("   n  binomial_cents  black_scholes_cents  error_cents")
 242|     errors: dict[int, float] = {}
 243|     for n in STEPS:
 244|         tree = binomial_call(spot, strike, RATE, TAU, SIGMA, n)
 245|         errors[n] = tree - closed
 246|         print(f"{n:>4}  {cents(tree):>14}  {cents(closed):>19}  {cents(errors[n]):>11}")
 247| 
 248|     symmetric = all(abs(norm_cdf(x) + norm_cdf(-x) - 1.0) < 1e-15 for x in (0.0, 0.35, 1.7, 3.0))
 249|     parity = abs((closed - spot + strike * math.exp(-RATE * TAU)) - bs_put(spot, strike, RATE, TAU, SIGMA)) < 1e-9
 250|     print(f"Phi(x) + Phi(-x) = 1 at the test points: {symmetric}")
 251|     print(f"put-call parity holds for the closed-form put to 1e-9: {parity}")
 252|     assert norm_cdf(0.0) == 0.5
 253|     assert symmetric
 254|     assert parity
 255|     assert abs(d1 - 0.35) < 1e-12 and abs(d2 - 0.15) < 1e-12
 256| 
 257| 
 258| main()
 259|         parameters: spot=10000 cents strike=10000 cents r=0.05 sigma=0.2 tau=1.0 years (model inputs, not market data)
 260| Black-Scholes d1=0.350000 d2=0.150000 Phi(d1)=0.636831 Phi(d2)=0.559618
 261| Black-Scholes call = 10.450584 currency units = 1045 cents
 262|    n  binomial_cents  black_scholes_cents  error_cents
 263|    1            1216                 1045          171
 264|    2             954                 1045          -91
 265|    5            1081                 1045           36
 266|   10            1025                 1045          -20
 267|   25            1052                 1045            7

 269|  100            1043                 1045           -2
 270|  200            1044                 1045           -1

 272| Phi(x) + Phi(-x) = 1 at the test points: True

 274|         The closed form and the tree prices are computed from the same inputs, so the error column measures the tree's distance from the formula at these step counts. The sign of the error changes five times across the printed step counts, and the absolute error decreases along the list. These are facts about the nine values printed. The lab does not show that the error tends to zero as the number of steps grows without bound, and it does not show what the error is for a step count outside the list.  278|         4Where the canon breaks
 279|         The formula is stated as A7′ and used, not derived. This lesson gives no derivation from a continuous-time model of the stock price and takes no limit of the tree. The concepts that such a derivation uses are not taught in FC2, so no step of Definition 1.3 is proved here. A reader who wants the derivation has to look outside this unit; no source for it is cited here.
 280|         Assumption A7 takes one volatility \(\sigma\) for every strike, constant over the life of each option. The formula inverts to one volatility per quoted price, and Lesson 8 shows that the inversion is unique when it exists. Whether quoted prices for different strikes imply the same volatility is therefore a test of A7, and this lesson makes no claim about any data set.
 281|         Assumption A5 limits the claim to exercise at expiry. A European call's value is given by the formula. An American call can be exercised before expiry. Its tree value needs the call payoff of Lesson 4 (Definition 1.1) at each node, with the backward induction of Lesson 6 (Definition 3.2). This lesson does not apply that recursion to American exercise.

 285|         5Misconceptions
 286| 
 287|           “The binomial price approaches the closed form from one side.”
 288|             Lab 1's error column changes sign. The tree price is above the closed form at 1, 5 and 25 steps, and below it at 2, 10, 50, 100, 200 and 400 steps. At 1 step the printed error is 171 cents, at 2 steps it is \(-91\) cents, at 5 steps it is 36 cents, and at 400 steps the unrounded error is \(-0.4998\) cents, which prints as 0. The sign at 400 steps is visible only in the unrounded value, not in the printed table. The sign pattern of the nine printed values is a fact about those values, and it is not a theorem of this lesson.
 289|           “The Black–Scholes call value rises with the stock's expected return.”
 290|             Definition 1.3 contains the rate \(r\), not the expected return of the stock. Proposition 1.5 shows that \(C\) depends on \(S\), \(K\), \(r\tau\) and \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau}\) only. Two inputs with the same \(S\), \(K\), \(r\tau\) and \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau}\) give the same price, whatever the expected return.
 291|           “The put needs its own formula, and it is a separate result from the call.”
 292|             Proposition 1.4 gives the put from the call and from parity. The two forms of the put in that proposition agree, and Lab 1 checks them against each other. Problem M2 computes the put for the lab's inputs from the call value and parity.

 297|         6Problem set
 298|         Mechanics
 299|           M1 · The arguments \(d_1\) and \(d_2\)
 300|             Find \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) for \(S = K = 100\), \(r = 0.05\), \(\sigma = 0.2\) and \(\tau = 1\).
 301|             Solution
 302|               \(\ln(S/K) = 0\), so \(d_1 = (0 + (0.05 + 0.02) \cdot 1)/(0.2 \cdot 1) = 0.07/0.2 = 0.35\). Then \(d_2 = 0.35 - 0.2 = 0.15\). Lab 1 prints the same values, \(d_1 = 0.350000\) and \(d_2 = 0.150000\).
 303|           M2 · The put for the same inputs
 304|             Using the call value of Lab 1 and Proposition 1.4, find the put value for the inputs of M1, in currency units and in cents.
 305|             Solution
 306|               The call is \(C = 10.450584\) currency units (Lab 1, unrounded value \(10.4505836\)). Also \(Ke^{-r\tau} = 100e^{-0.05} = 95.122942\). Then \(P = C - S + Ke^{-r\tau} = 10.450584 - 100 + 95.122942 = 5.573526\) currency units, which is 557 cents.
 307|           M3 · Tree parameters for four steps
 308|             For \(\tau = 1\), \(\sigma = 0.2\) and \(r = 0.05\), give \(u^{(4)}\), \(d^{(4)}\), \(R^{(4)}\) and \(q^{(4)}\) (Definition 2.1). Check Lemma 2.2's condition.
 309|             Solution
 310|               \(h = 1/4\), so \(\sigma\sqrt{h} = 0.1\) and \(u^{(4)} = e^{0.1} = 1.105171\), \(d^{(4)} = 1/u^{(4)} = 0.904837\), \(R^{(4)} = e^{0.0125} = 1.012578\). Then \(q^{(4)} = (R^{(4)} - d^{(4)})/(u^{(4)} - d^{(4)})\). With the unrounded inputs \(u^{(4)} = 1.10517092\), \(d^{(4)} = 0.90483742\) and \(R^{(4)} = 1.01257845\), this gives \(q^{(4)} = 0.5378084\), which rounds to 0.537808. From the six-place inputs as printed, the same quotient is \(0.107741/0.200334 = 0.537807\), so the sixth digit depends on which inputs are used. The condition \(h = 0.25 < \sigma^2/r^2 = 16\) holds, so \(0 < d^{(4)} < R^{(4)} < u^{(4)}\).
 311| 
 312|         Proof
 313|           P1 · Symmetry
 314|             Prove Lemma 1.2 without the substitution in its proof, using only that \(\varphi\) is even and integrates to 1.
 315|             Solution
 316|               Let \(g(x) = \Phi(x) + \Phi(-x)\). Its derivative is \(\varphi(x) - \varphi(-x) = 0\), because \(\Phi'(x) = \varphi(x)\) by the fundamental theorem of calculus and \(\frac{d}{dx}\Phi(-x) = -\varphi(-x)\). So \(g\) is constant. As \(x \to -\infty\), \(\Phi(x) \to 0\) and \(\Phi(-x) \to 1\), so \(g = 1\). Hence \(\Phi(-x) = 1 - \Phi(x)\).
 317|           P2 · The parity algebra
 318|             Verify the second equality in Proposition 1.4 term by term.
 319|             Solution
 320|               Start from \(C - S + Ke^{-r\tau} = S\Phi(d_1) - Ke^{-r\tau}\Phi(d_2) - S + Ke^{-r\tau}\). Group the \(S\) terms and the \(Ke^{-r\tau}\) terms: \(-S(1 - \Phi(d_1)) + Ke^{-r\tau}(1 - \Phi(d_2))\). Lemma 1.2 turns \(1 - \Phi(d_1)\) into \(\Phi(-d_1)\) and \(1 - \Phi(d_2)\) into \(\Phi(-d_2)\), giving \(Ke^{-r\tau}\Phi(-d_2) - S\Phi(-d_1)\).
 321|           P3 · Where the condition comes from
 322|             Show that \(R^{(n)} < u^{(n)}\) holds exactly when \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\), for \(r > 0\).
 323|             Solution
 324|               \(R^{(n)} < u^{(n)}\) is \(e^{rh} < e^{\sigma\sqrt{h}}\), which is \(rh < \sigma\sqrt{h}\) because \(\exp\) is strictly increasing. Dividing by \(\sqrt{h} > 0\) gives \(r\sqrt{h} < \sigma\). Both sides are positive, so squaring is an equivalence and gives \(r^2 h < \sigma^2\), that is \(h < \sigma^2/r^2\).
 325|

 327|           E1 · The step count with the smallest error

 341|           R1 · What the table would need to show
 342|             State a test of whether the tree price converges to the closed form. Name the sequence of step counts, the error measure, and the outcome that would count against convergence. Make no claim about the outcome.
 343|             Solution
 344|               Use a sequence of step counts that increases without bound, and the error \(|V_n - C|\) in currency units. A result that counts against convergence is a sequence of errors that stays above a fixed positive number at large step counts, or that grows. A result that agrees with convergence is an error that falls below every fixed positive number after some step count. Lab 1 covers only nine step counts, so it can support neither outcome, and this lesson draws no conclusion from it.
 345|           R2 · Two inputs with one price
 346|             Find a second set of inputs \((\sigma, \tau, r)\) with \(\tau\) different from 1 that gives the call value of Lab 1, with \(S = K = 100\) unchanged, using Proposition 1.5.
 347|             Solution
 348|               Take \(\tau = 4\), \(\sigma = 0.1\) and \(r = 0.0125\). Then \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau} = 0.2\) and \(r\tau = 0.05\), the same as in Lab 1. By Proposition 1.5, with \(S = K = 100\) unchanged, the call value is 10.450584, which is 1045 cents, the same value as Lab 1's closed form. The price depends on the inputs only through \(r\tau\) and \(\sigma\sqrt{\tau}\), so changing \(\tau\) while both products stay fixed leaves it unchanged.
 349|           R3 · The evidence a comparison needs
 350|             State what evidence would show whether the Black–Scholes formula matches the prices of traded options, and name one input it would need that this lesson does not have. Make no claim about any market.
 351|             Solution
 352|               The test needs quoted option prices for several strikes and maturities, the spot price and the riskless rate on each quote date, and the dividends paid over the option lives. The formula assumes no dividends (A1), so the dividend record is needed to judge that assumption. This lesson cites no source for any of these inputs, and it makes no claim about any market.
