Registration, Disclosure and the Two Acts

 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=sections 5(a) and 5(c)}} Before a security is offered or sold through interstate commerce, the Securities Act of 1933 makes the status of a registration statement (defined in §2) a condition of the offer and the sale. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=sections 12(a), 12(b) and 13(a)}} The Securities Exchange Act of 1934 sets a separate registration for a security listed on an exchange, and periodic reporting for its issuer. One issuer can fall under both. The statutory words set which duty applies: {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities gpondsecuritiesb loc=sections 5(a), 5(c), 13(a) and 15(d)(1)}} “filed” and “in effect” (defined in §2) govern offers and sales, while “registered pursuant to section 12” and “has become effective” govern the reporting duties. This lesson models the lifecycle as a finite state machine. The model's output is a reading of the text, not a finding about any filing.
This lesson is an educational reading of the cited sources. It is not legal advice, and it states no holding of any court. Each statute statement in a claim span carries its section locator. Sentences that are not in a claim span are this lesson's readings of the sections it quotes.
1The two Acts and their texts
The two statutes are the subject of this lesson. The text read here is the statute compilation that GovInfo publishes for each Act. The two GovInfo records used here state each Act's title and the public law through which its compilation is amended. The record's Title field gives the purpose of each Act in the Act's own words.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=Title field of the GovInfo record COMPS-1884}} The Securities Act of 1933 is titled: “To provide full and fair disclosure of the character of securities sold in interstate and foreign commerce and through the mails, and to prevent frauds in the sale thereof, and for other purposes.”
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=Title field of the GovInfo record COMPS-1885}} The Securities Exchange Act of 1934 is titled: “To provide for the regulation of securities exchanges and of over-the-counter markets operating in interstate and foreign commerce and through the mails, to prevent inequitable and unfair practices on such exchanges and markets, and for other purposes.”
The 1934 title names both exchanges and over-the-counter markets. The distinction between the two is defined in FC1 (see the link in the header). The registration sentences of section 12, quoted in §3, are written for transactions on a national securities exchange (a term the statute uses, which §3 does not define). This lesson does not read the statute's provisions on over-the-counter trading.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=Amended Through field of the GovInfo record COMPS-1884}} The 1933 compilation is amended through Public Law 119-27, enacted July 18, 2025. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=Amended Through field of the GovInfo record COMPS-1885}} The 1934 compilation is amended through Public Law 119-60, enacted December 18, 2025. The section numbers in this lesson are those printed in each compilation's text: sections 2(a)(5) and 2(a)(19), 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 of the 1933 Act (with 5(d), 6(e)(1) and 8(e) in §5), and sections 12, 13 and 15(d) of the 1934 Act.
2Registration under the 1933 Act
A registration statement is the document that an issuer files with the Commission {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 2(a)(5)}} (the term “Commission” means the Securities and Exchange Commission) to register a security under the 1933 Act. A statement has two statuses that matter to this lesson. It is filed {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(c)}} on the Commission's receipt of it, subject to the fee condition below. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=sections 8(a) and 8(d)}} Section 8(a) states that “the effective date of a registration statement shall be the twentieth day after the filing thereof or such earlier date as the Commission may determine”, and section 8(d) lets the Commission “issue a stop order suspending the effectiveness of the registration statement”. This lesson reads in effect as: the statement has taken effect under section 8(a) and has not been suspended under section 8(d). The definitions in section 2 do not define the phrase. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=sections 5(a) and 5(c)}} The status of the statement is what sections 5(a) and 5(c) test, not the document itself.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(c)}} Under section 6(c), the filing “shall be deemed to have taken place upon the receipt thereof”, but the filing “shall not be deemed to have taken place unless it is accompanied” by the fee that section 6(b) requires.
 Definition 2.1 (securities registration and disclosure, 1933 Act)
The securities registration and disclosure under the 1933 Act is the filing of a registration statement with the Commission under section 6(a), together with the statement's taking effect under section 8(a). {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5}} Section 5 makes the status of the statement a condition of offers and of sales. The definition names the steps the text names. It does not state the content of the statement, and the content requirements are not stated here.
The statute's wording for each step is quoted below, with its locator.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(a), first sentence}} Section 6(a): “Any security may be registered with the Commission under the terms and conditions hereinafter provided, by filing a registration statement”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5(a), first sentence}} Section 5(a): “Unless a registration statement is in effect as to a security, it shall be unlawful for any person, directly or indirectly” to “make use of any means or instruments of transportation or communication in interstate commerce or of the mails to sell such security through the use or medium of any prospectus or otherwise”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5(c)}} Section 5(c): it is unlawful to offer to sell or offer to buy a security through interstate means or the mails “unless a registration statement has been filed as to such security, or while the registration statement is the subject of a refusal order or stop order or (prior to the effective date of the registration statement) any public proceeding or examination under section 8”. The sentence names a refusal order and a stop order among its clauses. Reading R5c in §4 takes both as conditions under which an offer is not permitted, and the text read here does not settle how its clauses combine.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(a), first sentence}} Section 8(a), first sentence: “Except as hereinafter provided, the effective date of a registration statement shall be the twentieth day after the filing thereof or such earlier date as the Commission may determine”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(b)}} Section 8(b): if it appears to the Commission that the registration statement is on its face incomplete or inaccurate in any material respect, after notice by personal service or the sending of confirmed telegraphic notice not later than ten days after the filing of the registration statement, and opportunity for hearing (at a time fixed by the Commission) within ten days after such notice, the Commission may, before the effective date, issue an order “refusing to permit such statement to become effective until it has been amended in accordance with such order”. When the statement has been amended, the Commission declares so, and “the registration shall become effective at the time provided in subsection (a) or upon the date of such declaration, whichever date is the later”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(d)}} Section 8(d): if it appears to the Commission at any time that the registration statement includes any untrue statement of a material fact or omits to state any material fact required to be stated therein or necessary to make the statements therein not misleading, the Commission may, after notice by personal service or the sending of confirmed telegraphic notice and after opportunity for hearing (at a time fixed by the Commission) within fifteen days after such notice by personal service or the sending of such telegraphic notice, issue a stop order “suspending the effectiveness of the registration statement”. When the statement has been amended in accordance with the stop order, the Commission declares so, and “the stop order shall cease to be effective”.
Two readings follow from these sentences, and the model in §4 uses both of them. A filed statement is not in effect, so section 5(a) still makes a sale unlawful. Under reading R5c (§4), an offer is permitted once the statement is filed, unless a refusal order or a stop order applies. The default effective date below is not used by the model (§5). {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(a), first sentence}} The default effective date is the twentieth day after filing, “except as hereinafter provided”, and it can be earlier if the Commission so determines. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=sections 8(b) and 8(d)}} Section 8(b) and section 8(d) let the Commission hold a statement before it takes effect, or suspend it afterwards.
3Registration and reporting under the 1934 Act
The sections of the 1934 Act that this lesson reads concern a second registration, for a security on a national securities exchange, and periodic reporting by its issuer. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=sections 12(b) and 13(a)}} These are separate from the 1933 registration, and they have their own filings.
 Definition 3.1 (Securities Exchange Act of 1934)
The Securities Exchange Act of 1934 is the statute whose title is quoted in §1. In this lesson the term refers to sections 12, 13 and 15(d), which govern the registration of a security on a national securities exchange and the reports of its issuer. A national securities exchange is the term the statute uses in those sections; the lesson does not define it further.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(a)}} Section 12(a): it is unlawful for a member, broker or dealer “to effect any transaction in any security (other than an exempted security) on a national securities exchange unless a registration is effective as to such security for such exchange”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(b), first sentence}} Section 12(b): “A security may be registered on a national securities exchange by the issuer filing an application with the exchange”, with duplicate originals filed with the Commission as it requires.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(d), first sentence}} Section 12(d): after the exchange certifies that the security is approved for listing and registration, “the registration shall become effective thirty days after the receipt of such certification by the Commission or within such shorter period of time as the Commission may determine”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(d), second sentence}} Section 12(d), second sentence: a security “may be withdrawn or stricken from listing and registration in accordance with the rules of the exchange”, on application by the issuer or the exchange to the Commission, and the issuer is then relieved of section 12 and section 13 for that security.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 13(a), first sentence}} Section 13(a): “Every issuer of a security registered pursuant to section 12 of this title shall file with the Commission” the information and reports that the Commission prescribes, including “such annual reports (and such copies thereof), certified if required by the rules and regulations of the Commission by independent public accountants, and such quarterly reports (and such copies thereof), as the Commission may prescribe”.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1), first sentence}} Section 15(d)(1), first sentence: an issuer that files a registration statement “which has become effective pursuant to the Securities Act of 1933, as amended” must file the supplementary and periodic reports that section 13 requires for a security registered under section 12.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1), second sentence}} Section 15(d)(1), second sentence: “The duty to file under this subsection shall be automatically suspended if and so long as any issue of securities of such issuer is registered pursuant to section 12 of this title.”
The two reporting duties therefore have different triggers. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=sections 13(a) and 15(d)(1)}} Section 13(a) applies to an issuer whose security is registered under section 12. Section 15(d)(1) applies to an issuer whose 1933 statement has become effective, and it is suspended while any issue is registered under section 12. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1), third sentence}} The third sentence of section 15(d)(1) adds a fiscal-year suspension for the securities of each class, other than any class of asset-backed securities, to which the registration statement relates, held of record by fewer than 300 persons, or, in the case of a bank, a savings and loan holding company or a bank holding company, 1,200 persons, which §5 lists as a limit of the model.
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1), third sentence}} The third sentence of section 15(d)(1) provides that the duty “shall also be automatically suspended as to any fiscal year”, other than the fiscal year in which the registration statement became effective, if at the beginning of that year the securities of each class, other than any class of asset-backed securities, to which the registration statement relates, are “held of record by less than 300 persons”, or, in the case of a bank, a savings and loan holding company or a bank holding company, “1,200 persons”. The statute defines the last two terms by reference to two other Acts, which this lesson does not read.
4The lifecycle as a finite state machine
The model below is the lesson's reading of the sentences quoted in §2 and §3. Its events are the steps those sentences name, each with its locator. A state is a pair of values. The first value is the status of the 1933 registration statement, which takes one of five values: unregistered, filed, refused, effective or stop_order. The second value is the listing, which takes one of two values: not_listed or listed, meaning registered on a national securities exchange under section 12. The model has 5 × 2 = 10 states. Its start state is unregistered with not_listed.
The events are the steps the text names, each with its locator:
1933 events, applied under either listing value: filing a registration statement (section 6(a)); a refusal order before effectiveness (section 8(b)); an amendment after a refusal order (section 8(b)); a declaration of effectiveness (section 8(a)); a stop order (section 8(d)); and a stop order ceasing after amendment (section 8(d)). That is 6 events × 2 listing values = 12 transitions.
1934 events, applied under each status: registration on a national securities exchange (sections 12(b) and 12(d)) and withdrawal from listing and registration (section 12(d)). That is 2 events × 5 statuses = 10 transitions.
The model therefore has 22 transitions. In the model, the 1934 events apply under each of the five statuses, and the 1933 events apply under each of the two listings. The model does not make a listing depend on the status. §5 names that as a limit.
Four readings map states to flags. Each is a definition in the lab's code, and each is the lesson's reading of a quoted sentence.
Reading R13 (section 13(a)): the periodic reporting duty applies when the listing is listed.
Reading R15 (section 15(d)(1)): the duty applies when the status is effective or stop_order and the listing is not_listed. This reading rests on Assumption A1 below.
Reading R5c (section 5(c)): an offer is permitted when the status is filed or effective.
Reading R5a (section 5(a)): a sale through interstate means is permitted when the status is effective.
 Assumption A1 (not read from the text)
A security whose 1933 statement is under a stop order is still one whose 1933 registration has become effective, for the purpose of reading R15. The text read here does not state this. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb gpondsecurities loc=section 15(d)(1); section 8(d)}} Section 15(d)(1) uses the words “has become effective”, and section 8(d) suspends “the effectiveness of the registration statement”. The lesson takes one reading and says so. Proposition 4.2 does not depend on A1, because its proof uses only the listing requirement in R15. §5 gives the consequence of the other choice.
Lab 1 · Enumerating the lifecycle model: its states, transitions and reachable set
The expected result, derived from the definitions in §4 before the run. The lab sets up the 10 states and the 22 transitions of §4, counts the transitions that leave the state set, and computes the states reachable from the start state. The printed result is: 10 candidate states and 22 transitions; 0 transitions that leave the state set; 10 of 10 states reachable from unregistered with not_listed; 0 states where both reporting duties apply; and 0 states where a sale is permitted and an offer is not. The state table has 2 states where a sale is permitted, 4 where an offer is permitted, 5 where R13 applies, and 2 where R15 applies. The lab is a finite enumeration of the model, so it is a check of the model's definitions and not a finding about any filing. Standard library only, no randomness and no data.
"""LX2 lab L01: the registration-to-reporting model as a finite state machine, with the events the sections name.
Standard library only. No randomness, no network and no data. The lab is a model of the lesson's reading of
sections 5, 6 and 8 of the Securities Act of 1933 and sections 12, 13 and 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act
of 1934. It is not a description of any issuer, filing or security.
A state is a pair (status, listing). The status is the status of the registration statement under the 1933 Act:
unregistered, filed, refused, effective or stop_order. The listing is whether the security is registered on a
national securities exchange under section 12 of the 1934 Act: not_listed or listed. The 1933 events apply
under either listing value, and the exchange events apply under every status, because the model does not
condition the listing on the status.
Assumption A1 (not read from the text): a security whose registration is under a stop order is still treated as
one whose 1933 registration has become effective, for the reporting-duty reading in duty_15d. The lab states
this as an assumption and does not claim the statute says it.
The lab enumerates every state and every transition, computes the states reachable from the start state, and
prints how many transitions leave the state set. It does not model the statutory timing (the twentieth day in
section 8(a) and the thirty days in section 12(d)), the exemptions, the prospectus requirement of section 5(b),
or the examinations and proceedings of section 8. Its printed flags are the lesson's reading, not a test the
Commission or a court applies.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
from collections import deque
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Final
STATUSES: Final[tuple[str, ...]] = ("unregistered", "filed", "refused", "effective", "stop_order")
LISTINGS: Final[tuple[str, ...]] = ("not_listed", "listed")
START: Final[tuple[str, str]] = ("unregistered", "not_listed")
# (event, status before, status after, locator in the statute)
EVENTS_1933: Final[tuple[tuple[str, str, str, str], ...]] = (
 ("file a registration statement", "unregistered", "filed", "section 6(a)"),
 ("refusal order before effectiveness", "filed", "refused", "section 8(b)"),
 ("amendment after a refusal order", "refused", "filed", "section 8(b)"),
 ("declare effective", "filed", "effective", "section 8(a)"),
 ("stop order", "effective", "stop_order", "section 8(d)"),
 ("stop order ceases after amendment", "stop_order", "effective", "section 8(d)"),
)
# (event, listing before, listing after, locator in the statute)
EVENTS_1934: Final[tuple[tuple[str, str, str, str], ...]] = (
 ("register on a national securities exchange", "not_listed", "listed", "section 12(b) and 12(d)"),
 ("withdraw from listing and registration", "listed", "not_listed", "section 12(d)"),
)
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Transition:
 """One event of the model: it moves the state from source to target, and cites its locator."""
 event: str
 source: tuple[str, str]
 target: tuple[str, str]
 locator: str
def make_transitions() -> list[Transition]:
 """Every transition of the model: the 1933 events under each listing, and the exchange events under each status."""
 out: list[Transition] = []
 for event, before, after, locator in EVENTS_1933:
 for listing in LISTINGS:
 out.append(Transition(event, (before, listing), (after, listing), locator))
 for event, before, after, locator in EVENTS_1934:
 for status in STATUSES:
 out.append(Transition(event, (status, before), (status, after), locator))
 return out
def reachable(start: tuple[str, str], transitions: list[Transition]) -> list[tuple[str, str]]:
 """The states reachable from start, in the order a breadth-first search first meets them."""
 order = [start]
 seen = {start}
 queue: deque[tuple[str, str]] = deque([start])
 while queue:
 current = queue.popleft()
 for t in transitions:
 if t.source == current and t.target not in seen:
 seen.add(t.target)
 order.append(t.target)
 queue.append(t.target)
 return order
def duty_13(state: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
 """The lesson's reading of section 13(a): the duty applies to an issuer of a section 12 registered security."""
 return state[1] == "listed"
def duty_15d(state: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
 """The lesson's reading of section 15(d)(1) under Assumption A1: the duty applies while the 1933 registration has
 become effective, which under A1 includes a stop order, and no section 12 registration is in force."""
 return state[0] in ("effective", "stop_order") and state[1] == "not_listed"
def offer_permitted(state: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
 """The lesson's reading of section 5(c): an offer is permitted when the statement is filed or in effect, and not
 when it is refused or under a stop order."""
 return state[0] in ("filed", "effective")
def sale_permitted(state: tuple[str, str]) -> bool:
 """The lesson's reading of section 5(a): a sale through interstate means is unlawful unless a statement
 is in effect."""
 return state[0] == "effective"
def main() -> None:
 states = [(status, listing) for status in STATUSES for listing in LISTINGS]
 transitions = make_transitions()
 print(f"candidate states: {len(states)}, transitions: {len(transitions)}")
 leaving = [t.event for t in transitions if t.source not in states or t.target not in states]
 print(f"transitions that leave the state set: {len(leaving)}")
 reached = reachable(START, transitions)
 print(f"reachable from {START[0]}/{START[1]}: {len(reached)} of {len(states)}")
 print("state (status, listing) reachable s13(a) s15(d) offer sale")
 for state in states:
 print(
 f"{state[0]:<13} {state[1]:<11} {'yes' if state in reached else 'no':<10}"
 f"{'yes' if duty_13(state) else 'no':<8}{'yes' if duty_15d(state) else 'no':<8}"
 f"{'yes' if offer_permitted(state) else 'no':<7}{'yes' if sale_permitted(state) else 'no'}"
 )
 both = sum(duty_13(s) and duty_15d(s) for s in states)
 print(f"states where both reporting duties apply: {both}")
 sale_without_offer = sum(sale_permitted(s) and not offer_permitted(s) for s in states)
 print(f"states where a sale is permitted and an offer is not: {sale_without_offer}")
 print("transitions, with the locator each one cites:")
 for t in transitions:
 print(f" {t.event}: {t.source[0]}/{t.source[1]} -> {t.target[0]}/{t.target[1]} [{t.locator}]")
main()

Three propositions state what the lab's counts follow from. Each is proved from the definitions of §4, so each is a theorem of the model. None is a statement about the statute beyond the readings it names.
 {{CLAIM grade=T cite=None loc=None}} Proposition 4.1 (reachability in the model)
(H1) The model has the 10 states of §4, the start state unregistered with not_listed, and the 22 transitions of §4, and nothing else.
Conclusion: every one of the 10 states is reachable from the start state.
 Proof
Write a state as (status, listing), with status abbreviated u, f, r, e, s for unregistered, filed, refused, effective, stop_order, and listing as n or l for not_listed or listed. Each arrow below is one of the 22 transitions: “file” is the 1933 event that takes u to f, “refuse” takes f to r, “declare” takes f to e, “stop” takes e to s, and “register” takes any status with n to the same status with l. Then: (u,n) is the start state. (u,l) is reached by register. (f,n) is reached by file. (f,l) is reached by file, register. (r,n) is reached by file, refuse. (r,l) is reached by file, refuse, register. (e,n) is reached by file, declare. (e,l) is reached by file, declare, register. (s,n) is reached by file, declare, stop. (s,l) is reached by file, declare, stop, register. These ten paths cover the 10 states, so every state is reachable. \(\blacksquare\)
 {{CLAIM grade=T cite=None loc=None}} Proposition 4.2 (the reporting duties do not overlap)
(H1) Readings R13 and R15 of §4 are the definitions used by the lab. R15 requires the listing to be not_listed, whatever its condition on the status.
Conclusion: no state of the model satisfies both R13 and R15.
 Proof
R13 holds only when the listing is listed. R15 holds only when the listing is not_listed. A state has one listing value, so it cannot satisfy both. \(\blacksquare\) This is a consequence of the two readings. It is not a statement about the statute, although the second sentence of section 15(d)(1), quoted in §3, says that the duty is suspended while a section 12 registration applies, which is the same non-overlap for the listed case.
 {{CLAIM grade=T cite=None loc=None}} Proposition 4.3 (a permitted sale implies a permitted offer in the model)
(H1) Readings R5a and R5c of §4 are the definitions used by the lab.
Conclusion: in every state where R5a permits a sale, R5c permits an offer.
 Proof
R5a permits a sale only when the status is effective. R5c permits an offer when the status is filed or effective. So a state where R5a holds has status effective, which is one of the two statuses that R5c names. \(\blacksquare\)
5Where the canon breaks
The model follows the quoted sentences as §2 and §3 read them. It leaves out the following, and each omission changes what the model can say.
Timing. The model has no clock. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(a), first sentence}} Section 8(a) gives the effective date as the twentieth day after filing, “except as hereinafter provided”, or “or such earlier date as the Commission may determine”. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(d), first sentence}} Section 12(d) gives thirty days after the Commission receives the exchange's certification, “or within such shorter period of time as the Commission may determine”. The model treats the declaration of effectiveness as an event with no date. Without the clock, the model cannot say when a statement takes effect, and it cannot test a statutory date.
Exemptions. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=headings before sections 3 and 4}} Sections 3 and 4 of the 1933 Act carry the headings “exempted securities” and “exempted transactions”, as printed in the compilation. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(a)}} Section 12(a) excludes “an exempted security” from its requirement. The model has no exemption. Without one, the offer, sale and listing flags make no distinction for an exempted security or transaction, and the lesson has not read the text of those exemptions. The lesson does not state what the exemptions are.
Independence of the two registrations. The model makes the listing independent of the 1933 status, so it allows a listing for a security with no 1933 statement at all. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(a)}} Section 12(a) does not state that independence, and Research problem R3 asks whether the exemptions make it hold.
Emerging growth companies. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 2(a)(19)}} “Emerging growth company” is a term the statute defines in section 2(a)(19); this lesson does not quote the definition. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(e)(1)}} Section 6(e)(1) allows an emerging growth company to submit a draft registration statement “for confidential nonpublic review by the staff of the Commission prior to public filing”. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5(d)}} Section 5(d) lets an emerging growth company engage in certain oral and written communications with potential investors that are qualified institutional buyers or institutions that are accredited investors. The model has no state for either. It starts the modelled issuer at unregistered.
Proceedings. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5(c)}} Section 5(c) bars offers before the effective date while “any public proceeding or examination under section 8” is pending. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(e)}} Section 8(e) empowers the Commission to make an examination in any case to determine whether a stop order should issue under subsection (d). The model has no proceeding state, so its offer flag is looser than section 5(c) before effectiveness.
The fiscal-year suspension. The third sentence of section 15(d)(1) is quoted in §3. The model has no holder count, so reading R15 cannot apply it.
Assumption A1. The model treats a stop order as leaving the registration “effective” for R15. If a stop order is read to end the duty, then R15 must be changed to require the status effective alone, and the state with a stop order and not_listed would no longer carry the duty. The lab's output would change in one row, in its s15(d) column.
6Misconceptions
“Filing a registration statement permits sales.”
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 5(a), first sentence}} Section 5(a) makes a sale through interstate means unlawful “unless a registration statement is in effect”. A filed statement is not in effect, because {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(a)}} section 8(a) makes effectiveness a separate step that takes place on the effective date. Under reading R5c, a filed statement is the condition that permits an offer, and nothing in the reading makes a filed statement a condition of a sale. The model in §4 gives a filed state with an offer flag and no sale flag, as the state table prints.
“Once a registration takes effect, it cannot be suspended.”
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(d)}} Section 8(d) lets the Commission, if it appears at any time that the registration statement includes an untrue statement of a material fact or omits a material fact required to be stated therein or necessary to make the statements therein not misleading, and after notice by personal service or confirmed telegraphic notice and an opportunity for hearing (at a time fixed by the Commission) within fifteen days after such notice, “issue a stop order suspending the effectiveness of the registration statement”. The model has the transition from effective to stop_order, and its sale and offer flags are off in that state. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 8(d)}} The stop order “shall cease to be effective” after the amendment and declaration that section 8(d) describes.
“Registration on an exchange and the 1933 filing are the same act.”
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(a), first sentence}} Section 6(a) registers a security “by filing a registration statement” with the Commission. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(b), first sentence}} Section 12(b) registers a security on an exchange “by the issuer filing an application with the exchange”, with duplicates to the Commission as it requires. The registration statement goes to the Commission, and the application goes to the exchange. The model keeps them in separate components of the state, so that one can change without the other.
“Every effective 1933 registration brings the section 13 reporting duty.”
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=sections 13(a) and 15(d)(1)}} Section 13(a) applies to an issuer of a security “registered pursuant to section 12”. The duty for an issuer with only an effective 1933 registration comes from section 15(d)(1), which is suspended “if and so long as” a section 12 registration applies. The model's reading R13 is 1934 registration alone, and its reading R15 is the 1933 route, so the two do not both apply in the model (Proposition 4.2).
7Problem set
Each problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1.
Mechanics
 M1 · Filed and effective, unlisted
A statement for a security that is not listed on an exchange is filed, and then declared effective. Give the state after each event, and the offer and sale flags at each state.
 Solution
After filing, the state is filed with not_listed. Reading R5c permits an offer there, because the statement is filed and no refusal or stop order applies. Reading R5a does not permit a sale, because the statement is not in effect. After the declaration, the state is effective with not_listed. Both flags are on there. The lab's table prints these two rows: filed with not_listed has offer yes and sale no, and effective with not_listed has offer yes and sale yes.
 M2 · A stop order and its lifting
From the state effective with not_listed, a stop order is issued and later lifted after the amendment that section 8(d) describes. Give the states and the flags at each step.
 Solution
The stop order moves the state to stop_order with not_listed. In that state reading R5c gives no offer, because a stop order is one of the conditions in section 5(c). Reading R5a gives no sale. Reading R15 gives a reporting duty under Assumption A1, since the status is stop_order and the listing is not_listed. Lifting the stop order moves the state back to effective with not_listed, where the offer and sale flags are on again. The lab's table has the same rows.
 M3 · Listing and withdrawal, and the reporting duty
A security is in the state effective with not_listed. It is then registered on an exchange and later withdrawn. Name the reporting duty in each of the three states, using readings R13 and R15.
 Solution
In effective with not_listed, R15 applies and R13 does not, because the listing is not registered under section 12. After registration on the exchange, the state is effective with listed. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 13(a), first sentence}} R13 applies, because section 13(a) covers an issuer whose security is registered under section 12. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1), second sentence}} R15 is suspended, because section 15(d)(1) suspends the duty “if and so long as” an issue is registered under section 12. After withdrawal, which section 12(d) allows, the state returns to effective with not_listed, and R15 applies again.
Proof
 P1 · The transition count
Show that the model of §4 has 22 transitions.
 Solution
The six 1933 events are each applied under either of the two listing values, which gives 6 × 2 = 12 transitions. The two 1934 events are each applied under each of the five statuses, which gives 2 × 5 = 10 transitions. A 1933 transition changes the status and keeps the listing, and a 1934 transition changes the listing and keeps the status. So no transition is in both families, and the total is 12 + 10 = 22. The lab prints 22.
 P2 · A sale is an offer
Prove Proposition 4.3 and say which readings it uses.
 Solution
Reading R5a permits a sale only in a state whose status is effective. Reading R5c permits an offer in any state whose status is filed or effective. Since effective is one of those two statuses, every state where R5a permits a sale is a state where R5c permits an offer. The proof uses only the two readings, and it does not use the statute, so it is a fact about the model.
 P3 · Which claim the non-overlap depends on
Proposition 4.2 says that no state carries both reporting duties. Say which of its hypotheses carries the assumption, and say what the statute would need to say for the proposition to become a statement about the statute.
 Solution
R13 requires the listing to be listed, and R15 requires it to be not_listed, so the two cannot hold together. The proof uses no hypothesis that carries an assumption. Assumption A1 fixes R15 for states under a stop order, and the proof does not need that part of R15, because it needs only the listing requirement. Without A1 the proof still holds, because R15 would still require not_listed. For the proposition to become a statement about the statute, the text would need to say that the section 15(d)(1) duty is suspended whenever a section 12 registration applies, which the second sentence of section 15(d)(1) does say. The statute's statement covers the case that the proof uses, so the non-overlap is supported by the text as well as by the definitions.
Empirical
 E1 · Sale-permitted states (Lab 1)
From the state table in Lab 1, how many of the 10 states permit a sale, and which are they?
 Solution
Two states permit a sale: effective with not_listed, and effective with listed. Both have status effective, which is the only status for which reading R5a is on. The count is 2 of 10.
 E2 · Transitions by section (Lab 1)
From the transition list that Lab 1 prints, count the transitions that cite section 8 and the transitions that cite section 12.
 Solution
The printed list has a refusal order (section 8(b)) in two lines, an amendment after a refusal order (section 8(b)) in two lines, a declaration of effectiveness (section 8(a)) in two lines, a stop order (section 8(d)) in two lines, and a stop order ceasing after amendment (section 8(d)) in two lines. That is 10 section 8 lines. The section 12 lines are the five registrations on an exchange and the five withdrawals, which is 10. Section 6(a) has 2. The three counts sum to 22, which matches the 22 in hypothesis H1 of Proposition 4.1.
 E3 · What the reachability result shows (Lab 1)
Lab 1 prints “reachable from unregistered/not_listed: 10 of 10”. Say what this result establishes about the model and what it does not establish about the statute.
 Solution
The result establishes that, from the start state, the 22 encoded transitions reach each of the 10 states. Proposition 4.1 proves the same fact by exhibiting a path to each state. The result does not establish that any issuer can move through these states in this order, that the statute permits each path, or that the statute contains a state with a given name. It is a fact about the model's transitions. Each transition in the model is taken from a sentence quoted in §2 or §3, and the locator is printed next to it in the lab output.
Research
 R1 · Does a stop order end the reporting duty?
Decide whether a stop order under section 8(d) ends the reporting duty of section 15(d)(1). List the text you would need and say why this lesson cannot decide the question.
 Solution
The text read here does not answer the question. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 15(d)(1); section 8(d)}} Section 15(d)(1) uses the words “has become effective”, and section 8(d) says the stop order suspends “the effectiveness of the registration statement”. The first uses the perfect tense of become effective, and the second describes a suspension of effectiveness, not an end to the registration. Whether the duty survives a stop order is therefore a reading of the two sections together, and Assumption A1 takes one side of it. To decide, a student would need any rule or interpretation of section 15(d) that bears on the question. None is read here, so the question is not stated here, and the lesson states no answer.
 R2 · Adding the emerging growth company branch
Extend the model of §4 with the confidential draft submission of section 6(e)(1). Name the new states and events, and cite the sentence that sets the public filing date.
 Solution
A new state, a draft submitted confidentially, starts from unregistered with the listing not_listed. A new event moves it to filed when the statement is publicly filed. {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecurities loc=section 6(e)(1)}} Section 6(e)(1) says that “the initial confidential submission and all amendments thereto shall be publicly filed with the Commission not later than 15 days before the date on which the issuer conducts a road show”. The extension adds a state and a transition, so the count of states and transitions that Lab 1 prints would change, and so would the reachability proof of Proposition 4.1, which would need a path to the new state. The sections 5(d) communications would need their own flag, because section 5(d) permits certain communications that the offer flag of §4 does not model.
 R3 · Should listing depend on the 1933 status?
The model makes the listing independent of the 1933 status. Argue whether the statute requires that independence, using the sentence of section 12(a) and the exemption headings of sections 3 and 4.
 Solution
 {{CLAIM grade=C cite=gpondsecuritiesb loc=section 12(a)}} Section 12(a) makes an exchange transaction unlawful unless a registration “is effective as to such security for such exchange”, and it excludes “an exempted security”. The sentence refers to a registration effective for the exchange, not to the 1933 statement, so it does not, on its face, make the listing depend on the 1933 status. Whether the exemptions in sections 3 and 4 make the two independent in every case requires the text of those sections, which this lesson does not read. The independence is therefore a reading to test, and the lesson does not state it as a fact.
