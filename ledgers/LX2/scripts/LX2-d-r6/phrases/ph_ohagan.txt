Under the traditional or classical theory of insider trading liability, 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 are violated when a corporate insider trades
not only to officers, directors, and other permanent insiders of a corporation, but also to attorneys, accountants, consultants, and others who temporarily become fiduciaries of a corporation
The misappropriation theory holds that a person commits fraud
when he misappropriates confidential information for securities trading purposes, in breach of a duty owed to the source of the information
if the fiduciary discloses to the source that he plans to trade on the nonpublic information, there is no deceptive device
the fiduciary uses the information to purchase or sell securities
The theory does not catch all conceivable forms of fraud involving confidential information
the trader may still be liable under the misappropriation theory
a duty to disclose under 10(b) does not arise from the mere possession of nonpublic market information
Deception through nondisclosure is central to the theory of liability
The securities transaction and the breach of duty thus coincide
