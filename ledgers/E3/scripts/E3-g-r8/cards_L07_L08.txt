E3-c118 L07 None 
  F: What is a weighting function?
  B: A map omega from [0, 1] to [0, 1] that is nondecreasing, with omega(0) = 0 and omega(1) = 1. omega(p) is the decision weight of an outcome with probability p (Definition 1.1).
E3-c119 L07 None 
  F: What is the non-cumulative weighted value of a lottery on gains?
  B: V_omega(L) = sum of omega(p_i) v(x_i). Each outcome's own probability is weighted, not the probability of reaching it. With omega(p) = p and v = u it is expected utility (Definition 1.2).
E3-c120 L07 T 
  F: What does the identity weighting give?
  B: V_omega(L) = sum of p_i v(x_i), which is the expected value of v(X) (Remark 1.3).
E3-c121 L07 T 
  F: What is the weighting function with eta = 1/2 in closed form, and what are its values at 3/10, 3/5 and 1/2?
  B: omega(p) = sqrt(p)/(1 + 2 sqrt(p(1 - p))), with omega(0) = 0 and omega(1) = 1. To eight decimals, omega(3/10) = 0.28579088, omega(3/5) = 0.39125077 and omega(1/2) = 0.35355339 (Example 1.4).

E3-c136 L08 None 
  F: What is a prior set?
  B: A finite nonempty set of probability distributions over the outcomes of a gamble. For a two-outcome gamble a prior is a number p in [0, 1], the probability of the first outcome (Definition 1.1).
E3-c137 L08 None 
  F: What is the alpha-MEU value with weight tau?
  B: V_tau(f) = tau min over P of E_P u(f) + (1 - tau) max over P of E_P u(f). The certainty equivalent is u^(-1)(V_tau(f)) (Definition 1.2).
E3-c138 L08 T 
  F: What does a one-element prior set give for every tau?
  B: Expected utility under that prior, because the minimum and the maximum coincide (Proposition 1.3).
E3-c139 L08 T

  B: P(red) = 1/2. The prior set has P_k(red) = k/90 for k = 30 to 60, and the uniform average of those priors is 45/90 = 1/2 (Example 1.4).
E3-c140 L08 None

  B: When CE(f) <= CE_Pbar(f), where Pbar is the uniform average of the prior set and CE_Pbar(f) = u^(-1)(E_Pbar u(f)) (Definition 2.1).
E3-c141 L08 T

  B: Under A1 (a finite nonempty prior set with uniform average), A2 (u strictly increasing) and A3 (finitely many outcomes), the maxmin certainty equivalent is at most the certainty equivalent at the average prior (Theorem 2.2).

E3-c145 L08 None 
  F: What timing convention is used for a one-shot gamble?
  B: The gamble pays at date 1 only. Date 0 consumes a sure c0, and date 1 consumes c1, which may be random. The value uses the exponent a = 1 - 1/psi (Definition 3.1).
E3-c146 L08 T 
  F: What is the Epstein-Zin certainty equivalent of a last-date gamble?
  B: Under A1 to A5 of Proposition 3.2 (psi > 0 and psi not 1, gamma not 1, beta in (0, 1), c0 > 0, and a finite gamble with positive outcomes), the Epstein-Zin certainty equivalent is (E c1^(1 - gamma))^(1/(1 - gamma)), the CRRA certainty equivalent with the same gamma. The formula contains neither psi, nor beta, nor c0. So the two criteria give the same certainty equivalent for every psi and beta (section 6, third misconception).
E3-c147 L08 None 
  F: What changes when the gamble resolves before a later consumption date?
  B: The date-1 value becomes random. The certainty equivalent is not computed in that case, and a dependence on psi and beta is an untested hypothesis (Remark 3.3).
