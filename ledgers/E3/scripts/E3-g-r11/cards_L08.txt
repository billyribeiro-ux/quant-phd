### E3-c136 | L08 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a prior set?
B: A finite nonempty set of probability distributions over the outcomes of a gamble. For a two-outcome gamble a prior is a number p in [0, 1], the probability of the first outcome (Definition 1.1).
### E3-c137 | L08 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is the alpha-MEU value with weight tau?
B: V_tau(f) = tau min over P of E_P u(f) + (1 - tau) max over P of E_P u(f). The certainty equivalent is u^(-1)(V_tau(f)) (Definition 1.2).
### E3-c138 | L08 | grade=T | basis=None

B: Expected utility under that prior, because the minimum and the maximum coincide (Proposition 1.3).

### E3-c140 | L08 | grade=None | basis=None

### E3-c146 | L08 | grade=T | basis=None
F: What is the Epstein-Zin certainty equivalent of a last-date gamble?
B: Under A1 to A5 of Proposition 3.2 (psi > 0 and psi not 1, gamma not 1, beta in (0, 1), c0 > 0, and a finite gamble with positive outcomes), the Epstein-Zin certainty equivalent is (E c1^(1 - gamma))^(1/(1 - gamma)), the CRRA certainty equivalent with the same gamma. The formula contains neither psi, nor beta, nor c0. So the two criteria give the same certainty equivalent for every psi and beta (section 6, third misconception).

### E3-c148 | L08 | grade=T | basis=None
F: Why does alpha-MEU at tau = 1/2 equal expected utility for the gamble of Section 4?
B: With u(w) = -1/w (CRRA, gamma = 2, Model 1), for a two-outcome gamble E_P[1/w] is affine in p.
