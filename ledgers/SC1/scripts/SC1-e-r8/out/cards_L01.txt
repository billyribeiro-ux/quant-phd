  F: What is a process (B_t)_(t≥ 0)?
  B: A family of random variables indexed by time, all defined on one probability space.
SC1-c002 [L01] grade=- cite=[] basis=-
  F: What is a path of a process (B_t)?
  B: For a fixed outcome ω, the function t↦ B_t(ω).
SC1-c003 [L01] grade=- cite=[] basis=-
  F: What is the finite-dimensional law of a process at times t_1,…,t_r?
  B: The joint law of (B_(t_1),…,B_(t_r)).
SC1-c004 [L01] grade=- cite=[] basis=-
  F: State the three conditions of Definition 1.1 (standard Brownian motion).
  B: (D1) B_0=0. (D2) For 0≤ s<t, B_t-B_s∼N(0,t-s). (D3) For every 0≤ t_0<…<t_r, the increments over the consecutive intervals are mutually independent.
SC1-c005 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Definition 1.1 (D1) and (D2), Section 1
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, what is Var(B_t)?
  B: t, for every t≥ 0. (D1) gives the case t=0, and (D2) with s=0 gives variance t for t>0.
SC1-c006 [L01] grade=- cite=[] basis=-
  F: What is a Gaussian process?
  B: A process for which every vector (B_(t_1),…,B_(t_r)) with 0<t_1<…<t_r is multivariate normal.
SC1-c007 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Proof in Section 2 (Proposition 1.2), assuming A1(a)
  F: Proposition 1.2 (covariance of Brownian motion). Hypotheses: (H1) (B_t) is a standard Brownian motion; (H2) assuming A1(a), the expectation of a product of independent integrable variables is the product of their expectations. What is the conclusion?
  B: Cov(B_s,B_t)= min(s,t) for all s,t≥ 0.
SC1-c008 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Proof in Section 2 (Remark 1.3), assuming A1(a) and A1(b)
  F: Remark 1.3. Hypotheses: (H1) (B_t) is a standard Brownian motion; (H2) assuming A1(b) and A1(a). For 0<t_1<…<t_r, what is the law of (B_(t_1),…,B_(t_r))?
  B: Multivariate normal with mean 0 and covariance matrix ( min(t_i,t_j)). Assuming A1(a) and A1(b), Remark 1.3 says that a standard Brownian motion is a Gaussian process, with covariance min(s,t) for times s,t>0.
SC1-c009 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Proof in Section 3 (Example 1.4), from (D2)
  F: Example 1.4. Hypotheses: (B_t) is a standard Brownian motion, t≥ 0, h>0. What is the standard deviation of B_(t+h)-B_t?
  B: √h. Its ratio to h is 1/√h, which grows without bound as h→ 0.
SC1-c010 [L01] grade=C cite=[] basis=Lab 1 (exact Fraction check, no simulation)
  F: What does Lab 1 check on the grid t_i=i/8, i=0,…,8, and how many of its cases agree?
  B: That Cov(B_(t_i),B_(t_j))= min(t_i,t_j) for each of the 81 ordered pairs; 81 of 81 agree in exact Fraction arithmetic. The lab simulates no path.
SC1-c011 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Section 4 (Where the canon breaks), assuming a continuous-path B exists
  F: Assuming a standard Brownian motion B with continuous paths exists, and U is uniform on (0,1) and independent of B, what does B̃_t=B_t+1{t=U} satisfy?
  B: (D1), (D2) and (D3), and the same finite-dimensional laws as B, because P(U∈ F)=0 for every finite set of times F.
SC1-c012 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Section 4 (Where the canon breaks), assuming a continuous-path B exists
  F: Assuming a standard Brownian motion B with continuous paths exists, and U uniform on (0,1) independent of B, why is B̃ discontinuous?
  B: B̃_U=B_U+1, while B̃_t=B_t for t≠ U. So B̃_t→ B_U as t→ U on every outcome, and B̃ is discontinuous at U.
SC1-c013 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Section 4 (Where the canon breaks), assuming a continuous-path B exists
  F: Assuming a standard Brownian motion B with continuous paths exists, and U uniform on (0,1) independent of B, is the event that the path is continuous determined by the finite-dimensional laws?
  B: No. The continuity event holds for B on every outcome and for B̃ on no outcome, although both have the same finite-dimensional laws.
SC1-c014 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Proof of Proposition 1.2 (Section 2) and Section 4
  F: Does Proposition 1.2 need the increments to be normal? Assume A1(a).
  B: No. Its proof uses B_0=0 (D1), the mean 0 and variance t-s of each increment (D2), the independence of increments (D3), and Assumption A1(a). Remark 1.3 needs normality, through A1(b).
SC1-c015 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Proposition 1.2 and A1(a), Section 5
  F: Misconception: B_s and B_t are independent, because the increments are. Assuming A1(a), what is the correct statement?
  B: Increments over disjoint intervals are independent (D3), but they say nothing about B_s and B_t themselves. Cov(B_(1/2),B_1)=1/2 by Proposition 1.2, and independent variables have covariance 0 by A1(a), so B_(1/2) and B_1 are not independent.
SC1-c016 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Example 1.4, Section 5
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, misconception: an increment over a short interval of length h is of order h. What is the correct order?
  B: Its standard deviation is √h (Example 1.4). For h=1/100 it is 1/10, ten times h. The ratio 1/√h grows without bound as h→ 0.
SC1-c017 [L01] grade=T cite=[] basis=Section 4, assuming a continuous-path B exists
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, misconception: Definition 1.1 fixes the paths, so Brownian motion is one function. What does Definition 1.1 fix?
  B: Finite-dimensional laws, not the set of paths. The process B̃ of Section 4 satisfies (D1)–(D3) and has the laws of B, yet its paths differ from those of B.
SC1-c186 [L01] grade=- cite=[] basis=-
  F: What is a dyadic partition of depth n of [0,t]?
  B: The partition that cuts [0,t] into 2^n pieces of length t/2^n, for an integer n≥ 0.
