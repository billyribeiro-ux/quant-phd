  basis: None
SC1-c004 [L01] grade=None cite=[]
  F: State the three conditions of Definition 1.1 (standard Brownian motion).
  B: (D1) B_0=0. (D2) For 0≤ s<t, B_t-B_s∼N(0,t-s). (D3) For every 0≤ t_0<…<t_r, the increments over the consecutive intervals are mutually independent.
  basis: None
SC1-c005 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, what is Var(B_t)?
  B: t, for every t≥ 0. (D1) gives the case t=0, and (D2) with s=0 gives variance t for t>0.
  basis: Definition 1.1 (D1) and (D2), Section 1
SC1-c006 [L01] grade=None cite=[]
  F: What is a Gaussian process?
  B: A process for which every vector (B_(t_1),…,B_(t_r)) with 0<t_1<…<t_r is multivariate normal.
  basis: None
SC1-c007 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: Proposition 1.2 (covariance of Brownian motion). Hypotheses: (H1) (B_t) is a standard Brownian motion; (H2) assuming A1(a), the expectation of a product of independent integrable variables is the product of their expectations. What is the conclusion?
  B: Cov(B_s,B_t)= min(s,t) for all s,t≥ 0.
  basis: Proof in Section 2 (Proposition 1.2), assuming A1(a)

SC1-c012 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: Assuming A1(a), A1(b), A1(e), A2 and Assumption 2.1 of Lesson 2, let B be the continuous-path standard Brownian motion of Remark 2.6 of Lesson 2, and let U be uniform on (0,1) and independent of B. Why is B̃_t=B_t+1{t=U} discontinuous?
  B: B̃_U=B_U+1, while B̃_t=B_t for t≠ U. So B̃_t→ B_U as t→ U on every outcome, and B̃ is discontinuous at U.
  basis: Section 4 (Where the canon breaks), with Remark 2.6 of Lesson 2 and A1(a), A1(b), A1(e), A2, Assumption 2.1 of Lesson 2
SC1-c013 [L01] grade=T cite=[]

  B: No. Its proof uses B_0=0 (D1), the mean 0 and variance t-s of each increment (D2), the independence of increments (D3), and Assumption A1(a). Remark 1.3 needs normality, through A1(b).

SC1-c015 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: Misconception: B_s and B_t are independent, because the increments are. Assuming A1(a), what is the correct statement?
  B: Increments over disjoint intervals are independent (D3), but they say nothing about B_s and B_t themselves. Cov(B_(1/2),B_1)=1/2 by Proposition 1.2, and independent variables have covariance 0 by A1(a), so B_(1/2) and B_1 are not independent.
  basis: Proposition 1.2 and A1(a), Section 5
SC1-c016 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, misconception: an increment over a short interval of length h is of order h. What is the correct order?
  B: Its standard deviation is √h (Example 1.4). For h=1/100 it is 1/10, ten times h. The ratio 1/√h grows without bound as h→ 0.
  basis: Example 1.4, Section 5
SC1-c017 [L01] grade=T cite=[]
  F: For a standard Brownian motion, misconception: Definition 1.1 fixes the paths, so Brownian motion is one function. What does Definition 1.1 fix?
  B: Finite-dimensional laws, not the set of paths. The process B̃ of Section 4 satisfies (D1)–(D3) and has the laws of B, yet its paths differ from those of B.
  basis: Section 4, assuming a continuous-path B exists
