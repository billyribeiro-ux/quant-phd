  36: The running maximum up to horizon \(H\), \(M_H=\max_{0\le s\le H}B_s\), is the highest value a Brownian path reaches by time \(H\). Lesson 6 gives its tail in closed form, through the reflection identity: for \(a>0\), \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function, under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5. A computer sees a path at finitely many times. On a continuous path, the discretely monitored maximum (Definition 2.1 of this lesson) never exceeds \(M_H\), so the tail of the grid maximum, \(\Prob(M^{(N)}_H\ge a)\), is at most \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\), as Section 2 proves. Lab 1 checks the discrete tail identity of Lesson 6 exactly and compares seeded grid maxima with the closed form.

  38: 1The running maximum and its tail

  40: Let \(B\) be a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, as in Lesson 2. Lesson 6 proves the reflection identity for it, under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5. This lesson uses that identity and does not prove it again. Lesson 5 states Assumptions A3 and A4 without proof, so every result below that uses them says so.

  42: Definition 1.1 (running maximum)

  44: For \(H\ge 0\), the running maximum of \(B\) up to the horizon \(H\) is \(M_H=\max_{0\le s\le H}B_s\). Lesson 6 writes the same horizon as \(t\). This unit reserves \(T\) for the number of return observations, a meaning fixed in module S1, so the horizon is \(H\) throughout. The maximum exists because \(s\mapsto B_s\) is continuous on the compact interval \([0,H]\). Since \(B_0=0\) is one of the values in the maximum, \(M_H\ge 0\).

  46: Definition 1.2 (distribution of the running maximum)

  48: For fixed \(H>0\), the [DFN id=def-running-maximum-distribution concept=running-maximum-distribution]distribution of the running maximum is the law of \(M_H\). Its tail function is \(a\mapsto\Prob(M_H\ge a)\). Section 1 computes this function in closed form under H2 of Theorem 1.3.

  50: Theorem 1.3 (tail of the running maximum)

  52: (H1) \(B\) is a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, and \(B_0=0\).

  54: (H2) Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, used through Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6.

  56: (H3) \(H>0\) and \(a>0\) are fixed.

  58: Conclusion. Assuming H2, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))=\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(a/\sqrt{2H}\bigr)\), where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function and \(\operatorname{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_x^\infty e^{-w^2}\,dw\) is the complementary error function, the definition used in Corollary 2.6 of Lesson 6.

  60: Proof

  62: By Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6, under H2, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\). By Definition 1.1 of Lesson 1 with \(s=0\), \(B_H-B_0=B_H\) has the normal law with mean 0 and variance \(H\). So \(Z=B_H/\sqrt H\) is standard normal, and \(\{B_H\ge a\}=\{Z\ge a/\sqrt H\}\). The standard normal law has no atoms, so \(\Prob(Z\ge x)=\Prob(Z>x)=1-\Phi(x)\) for every real \(x\). Taking \(x=a/\sqrt H\) gives \(\Prob(B_H\ge a)=1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H)\). The second form is Corollary 2.6 of Lesson 6, which states \(2\,\Prob(B_H>a)=\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{2H})\) with only \(H>0\) and \(a>0\) as hypotheses. \(\blacksquare\)

  64: Remark 1.4 (limits in the horizon)

  66: Fix \(a>0\). The argument \(a/\sqrt H\) tends to \(\infty\) as \(H\downarrow 0\) and to 0 as \(H\to\infty\). Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, and assuming H2 of Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) tends to \(2(1-1)=0\) as \(H\downarrow 0\) and to \(2(1-\Phi(0))=1\) as \(H\to\infty\). The value \(\Phi(0)=\tfrac12\) holds because the standard normal density is even, so \(\Prob(Z\ge 0)=\Prob(Z\le 0)\) for the standard normal variable \(Z=B_H/\sqrt H\) of the proof of Theorem 1.3. These two events add to 1, because \(\Prob(Z\ge 0)+\Prob(Z\le 0)=1+\Prob(Z=0)\) and \(\Prob(Z=0)=0\), since the standard normal law has no atoms. Under H2 these limits follow from the closed form, because \(\Phi\) is continuous.

  68: Corollary 1.5 (distribution function and mean)

  70: Assumptions. Those of Theorem 1.3 for part (a). For part (b), additionally Assumption A5 of this lesson, stated below.

  72: (a) Assuming H2 of Theorem 1.3 (Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5), and under H1 and H3 of Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=2\,\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\) for \(m\ge 0\).

  74: (b) Assuming A5 and H2 of Theorem 1.3 (Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5), and under H1 and H3 of Theorem 1.3, \(\E[M_H]=\sqrt{2H/\pi}\).

  76: Assumption A5 (tail formula for a nonnegative variable)

  78: For a random variable \(X\ge 0\), \(\E[X]=\int_0^\infty \Prob(X>m)\,dm\), with the value in \([0,\infty]\). This page takes it as an assumption and does not prove it.

  80: Proof

  82: (a). Let \(m>0\). As \(b\downarrow m\) the events \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase, because a larger \(b\) gives a smaller event. Their union over \(b>m\) is \(\{M_H>m\}\): if \(M_H>m\), any \(b\in(m,M_H]\) works, and if \(M_H\ge b>m\) then \(M_H>m\). By continuity of probability from below and Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H>m)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}\Prob(M_H\ge b)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}2(1-\Phi(b/\sqrt H))=2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\). Hence \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=1-\Prob(M_H>m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). For \(m=0\), the same limit gives \(\Prob(M_H>0)=2(1-\Phi(0))=1\), so \(\Prob(M_H\le 0)=0=2\Phi(0)-1\).

  84: (b). Let \(\varphi(u)=e^{-u^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\), so \(\Phi'=\varphi\) and \(\varphi'(u)=-u\varphi(u)\). For \(R>0\), integrate by parts:

  86: \[\int_0^R\bigl(1-\Phi(u)\bigr)\,du=\bigl[u(1-\Phi(u))\bigr]_0^R+\int_0^R u\,\varphi(u)\,du=R\bigl(1-\Phi(R)\bigr)+\varphi(0)-\varphi(R).\]

  88: The last step uses \(\int_0^R u\varphi(u)\,du=-\int_0^R\varphi'(u)\,du=\varphi(0)-\varphi(R)\). Next, \(1-\Phi(R)=\int_R^\infty\varphi(v)\,dv\le\int_R^\infty (v/R)\varphi(v)\,dv=\varphi(R)/R\), because \(v/R\ge 1\) on \([R,\infty)\), and \(\int_R^\infty v\varphi(v)\,dv=\varphi(R)\). So \(0\le R(1-\Phi(R))\le\varphi(R)\), which tends to 0 as \(R\to\infty\). Letting \(R\to\infty\) gives \(\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=\varphi(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).

  90: By Assumption A5 and part (a), \(\E[M_H]=\int_0^\infty\Prob(M_H>m)\,dm=\int_0^\infty 2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\,dm\). Substituting \(m=\sqrt H\,u\) gives \(2\sqrt H\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=2\sqrt H/\sqrt{2\pi}=\sqrt{2H/\pi}\). \(\blacksquare\)

  92: 2The discretely monitored maximum

  94: A simulation or a monitoring system observes the path at finitely many times. The next definition gives the maximum that such an observer sees.

  96: Definition 2.1 (grid and discretely monitored maximum)

  98: Fix \(H>0\) and an integer \(N\ge 1\). The equal grid of \([0,H]\) with \(N\) steps has times \(s_k=kH/N\) for \(k=0,\dots,N\). The [DFN id=None concept=None]discretely monitored maximum of \(B\) on this grid is \(M^{(N)}_H=\max_{0\le k\le N}B_{s_k}\).

 100: Proposition 2.2 (the grid maximum never exceeds the running maximum)

 102: (H1) For a fixed outcome, the path \(s\mapsto B_s\) is continuous on \([0,H]\).

 104: (H2) \(N\) and \(N'\) are integers with \(N\ge 1\) and \(N'\ge 1\), and \(N\) divides \(N'\).

 106: Conclusion: (a) \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\). (b) \(M^{(N)}_H\le M^{(N')}_H\).

 108: Proof

 110: (a) The grid times \(s_k\) are points of \([0,H]\), so the maximum of the path over the grid is at most its maximum over \([0,H]\). This is \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\) for the outcome in question. (b) Write \(N'=cN\) with \(c\) a positive integer. Then \(s_k=kH/N=(kc)H/N'\), so every grid time of the \(N\)-step grid is a grid time of the \(N'\)-step grid. The maximum over a subset of a finite set of values is at most the maximum over the whole set. \(\blacksquare\)

 112: Corollary 2.3 (the grid tail is bounded by the running tail)

 114: Under H1 of Proposition 2.2, for every real \(a\) and every grid, \(\{M^{(N)}_H\ge a\}\subseteq\{M_H\ge a\}\). Hence \(\Prob(M^{(N)}_H\ge a)\le\Prob(M_H\ge a)\). Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the right-hand side equals the closed form of Theorem 1.3 for \(a>0\).

 116: Proof

 118: If \(M^{(N)}_H\ge a\), then \(M_H\ge M^{(N)}_H\ge a\) by Proposition 2.2(a). This is the inclusion, and monotonicity of probability gives the inequality. The closed form is Theorem 1.3. \(\blacksquare\)

 120: Remark 2.4 (one step)

 122: With \(N=1\), the grid is \(\{0,H\}\), and \(M^{(1)}_H=\max(B_0,B_H)=\max(0,B_H)\). For \(a>0\), \(\{\max(0,B_H)\ge a\}=\{B_H\ge a\}\), so \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_H\ge a)=\Prob(B_H\ge a)\) with no probability assumption. Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the factor 2 of Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\). Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the one-step grid tail is therefore exactly half the continuous tail.

 154: from __future__ import annotations

 156: import math
 157: import random
 158: from collections.abc import Callable
 159: from itertools import accumulate, pairwise, product

 161: SEED = 20260507
 162: PATHS = 400
 163: FINE = 256
 164: GRID_STEPS = (4, 16, 64, 256)
 165: LEVEL = 1.0
 166: MAX_ENUM = 12
 167: EXACT_N = (4, 16, 64, 256, 1024)
 168: SIMPSON_INTERVALS = 2000

 170: def end_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
 171: """The number of length-n step sequences whose endpoint S_n is at least m, as an exact integer.

 173: The endpoint is S_n = 2u - n, where u is the number of +1 steps, so the count is the sum of C(n, u) over the u
 174: for which 2u - n is at least m.
 175: """
 176: return sum(math.comb(n, (n + s) // 2) for s in range(-n, n + 1, 2) if s >= m)

 178: def max_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
 179: """The number of length-n step sequences whose running maximum over S_0 to S_n is at least m.

 181: By the discrete tail identity of Lesson 6, this equals the count with endpoint at least m plus the count with
 182: endpoint above m.
 183: """
 184: return end_at_least(n, m) + end_at_least(n, m + 1)

 186: def enumerated_pairs(n: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
 187: """For each of the 2**n step sequences of length n, the pair (running maximum, endpoint) of the walk S_0 = 0."""
 188: pairs: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
 189: for steps in product((1, -1), repeat=n):
 190: position = 0
 191: highest = 0
 192: for step in steps:
 193: position += step
 194: highest = max(highest, position)
 195: pairs.append((highest, position))
 196: return pairs

 198: def part_a() -> None:
 199: """Check the discrete tail identity of Lesson 6 by enumeration for N <= 12, then print exact tails for larger N."""
 200: checked = 0
 201: identity_holds = 0
 202: formula_agrees = 0

 204: pairs = enumerated_pairs(n)

 224: def std_normal_cdf(x: float) -> float:
 225: """The standard normal distribution function Phi(x) = erfc(-x / sqrt(2)) / 2, a probability."""
 226: return 0.5 * math.erfc(-x / math.sqrt(2.0))

 228: def fine_path(rng: random.Random) -> list[float]:
 229: """The values of a Brownian path on [0, 1] at the times j / FINE for j = 0 to FINE, in length units.

 231: The path is the partial sum of FINE independent Gaussian increments with mean 0 and variance 1 / FINE.
 232: """
 233: scale = math.sqrt(1.0 / FINE)
 234: increments = [rng.gauss(0.0, scale) for _ in range(FINE)]
 235: return [0.0, *accumulate(increments)]

 237: def grid_maximum(path: list[float], n: int) -> float:
 238: """The maximum of the path over the n + 1 grid times of the equal partition of [0, 1] into n steps."""
 239: stride = FINE // n
 240: return max(path[::stride])

 242: def mean_and_standard_error(values: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
 243: """The sample mean, and the standard error sd / sqrt(n) with sd computed by divisor n - 1."""
 244: n = len(values)
 245: mean = sum(values) / n
 246: variance = sum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in values) / (n - 1)
 247: return mean, math.sqrt(variance / n)

 249: def simulate_maxima(rng: random.Random) -> dict[int, list[float]]:
 250: """For each of the PATHS seeded paths, the grid maximum on each grid in GRID_STEPS, using the same path."""
 251: maxima: dict[int, list[float]] = {n: [] for n in GRID_STEPS}
 252: for _ in range(PATHS):
 253: path = fine_path(rng)
 254: for n in GRID_STEPS:
 255: maxima[n].append(grid_maximum(path, n))
 256: return maxima

 258: def nesting_violations(maxima: dict[int, list[float]]) -> int:
 259: """The number of (path, pair of consecutive grids) with a coarse maximum above the finer maximum. Exact count."""
 260: violations = 0
 261: for coarse, fine in pairwise(GRID_STEPS):
 262: for coarse_max, fine_max in zip(maxima[coarse], maxima[fine], strict=True):
 263: violations += coarse_max > fine_max
 264: return violations

 285: mean, se = mean_and_standard_error(maxima[n])
 286: print(
 287: f"Part C: N = {n:3d} steps: mean grid maximum {mean:.4f}, se {se:.4f}; "
 288: f"difference from sqrt(2 / pi) {mean - target:+.4f}"
 289: )

 291: def simpson(g: Callable[[float], float], a: float, b: float, intervals: int) -> float:
 292: """Composite Simpson rule for the integral of g on [a, b] with an even number of intervals."""
 293: h = (b - a) / intervals
 294: total = g(a) + g(b)
 295: for i in range(1, intervals):
 296: total += (4.0 if i % 2 else 2.0) * g(a + i * h)
 297: return total * h / 3.0

 299: def standard_normal_density(x: float) -> float:
 300: """The standard normal density exp(-x^2 / 2) / sqrt(2 pi), a density per unit of x."""
 301: return math.exp(-x * x / 2.0) / math.sqrt(2.0 * math.pi)

 303: def part_d() -> None:

 309: print(f"Part D: the two values agree to 1e-9: {abs(by_erfc - by_simpson) < 1e-9}")

Assuming A3 and A4, the four proportions lie below the closed form 0.3173105 by 0.0898, 0.0498, 0.0348 and 0.0148, so in this sample the gap narrows as the grid refines. Part C gives mean grid maxima 0.5546, 0.6639, 0.7232 and 0.7586, all below the closed-form mean \(\sqrt{2/\pi}=0.797885\), which holds under A3, A4 and A5. The finest gap is 0.0393, which is 1.24 printed standard errors, so this sample does not separate that gap from sampling error at 256 steps. Each printed difference is computed from unrounded values and rounded once, and no printed value is rounded again in this paragraph.

 346: [SECTION class=canon-breaks id=canon-breaks]

 348: 3Where the canon breaks

 350: Continuity of the paths is needed. Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6 uses continuity to identify \(\{M_H\ge a\}\) with \(\{\tau_a\le H\}\), where \(\tau_a\) is the first-passage time of Lesson 6, Section 2. A path that jumps over the level \(a\) can satisfy \(M_H\ge a\) without visiting \(a\). The tail formula is not asserted for the process \(\tilde B\) of Lesson 1, Section 4, whose paths are discontinuous.

 352: The closed form is conditional. Theorem 1.3 holds under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, which are stated there, with a sketch for A3 only, and not proved. Every result on this page that uses the closed form either carries the phrase "Assuming A3 and A4" or names H2 of Theorem 1.3, which is that assumption.

 354: The grid maximum is a different random variable. Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, Remark 2.4 makes the one-step grid tail exactly half the continuous tail at every level \(a>0\). By Corollary 2.3, no grid tail exceeds the continuous tail. Lab 1 shows a gap that narrows with \(N\) in one sample. This page gives no closed form for the grid tail at \(N>1\).

 356: [SECTION class=misconceptions id=misconceptions]

 358: 4Misconceptions

 360: “The running maximum has the same law as \(B_H\).”

 362: Assuming A3 and A4, Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\) for \(a>0\), which is twice the tail of \(B_H\). At \(H=1\) and \(a=1\), the tail of \(M_1\) is 0.3173 and the tail of \(B_1\) is 0.1587, each rounded to four decimals (to seven decimals, 0.3173105 and 0.1586553). Lab 1, Part D prints \(1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\), and the difference between the two tails is the probability of the event that the path reaches 1 and ends below 1.

 364: “A grid maximum can overstate the running maximum, so the grid tail sits above the closed form.”

 366: Proposition 2.2(a) gives \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\) on every continuous path, so Corollary 2.3 bounds the grid tail above by the running tail. Assuming A3 and A4, Lab 1, Part B prints grid estimates of 0.2275 at 4 steps and 0.3025 at 256 steps, both below the closed form 0.3173105 (H2 of Theorem 1.3, to seven decimals).

 368: “Any process with normal one-time marginals has the closed-form tail of \(M_H\).”

 370: The tail identity depends on the whole path. Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6 needs continuous paths, and Theorem 1.3 needs Assumptions A3 and A4. The process \(\tilde B\) of Lesson 1, Section 4, has the same one-time laws as \(B\) and discontinuous paths, and this page asserts no tail formula for it.

 374: 5Problem set

 376: Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1. Problems that use the closed form assume A3 and A4.

 378: Mechanics

 380: M1 · The tail at a larger horizon

 382: Let \(H=4\) and \(a=2\). Compute \(\Prob(M_4\ge 2)\), assuming A3 and A4.

 384: Solution

 386: Here \(a/\sqrt H=2/\sqrt 4=1\), so Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_4\ge 2)=2(1-\Phi(1))\). Part D of Lab 1 prints \(1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\), so the value is \(2(1-\Phi(1))=0.3173105079\) (unrounded 0.31731050786; \(2\times 0.1586552539=0.3173105078\) on the printed digits). The same value is \(\operatorname{erfc}(2/\sqrt 8)=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt 2)\).

 388: M2 · Scaling

 390: Assuming A3 and A4, show that \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) depends on \((a,H)\) only through \(a/\sqrt H\), and state the value of \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) when \(a/\sqrt H=1\).

 392: Solution

 394: By Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), a function of \(a/\sqrt H\) alone. When \(a/\sqrt H=1\), the value is \(2(1-\Phi(1))=0.3173105079\), by the same calculation as Problem M1.

 396: M3 · A one-step grid

 398: Let \(H=1\). Compute \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_1\ge 1)\) for the one-step grid, and compare it with \(\Prob(M_1\ge 1)\), assuming A3 and A4.

 400: Solution

 402: By Remark 2.4, \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_1\ge 1)=\Prob(B_1\ge 1)=1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\). By Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_1\ge 1)=0.3173105079\). The grid value is exactly half the continuous value.

 404: Proof

 406: P1 · The distribution function of the running maximum

 408: Assuming A3 and A4, prove Corollary 1.5(a): for \(m\ge 0\), \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). State each assumption used.

 410: Solution

 412: Let \(m>0\). The sets \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase as \(b\) decreases to \(m\), and their union over \(b>m\) is \(\{M_H>m\}\). By continuity of probability from below, \(\Prob(M_H>m)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}\Prob(M_H\ge b)\). Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge b)=2(1-\Phi(b/\sqrt H))\) for \(b>0\), and \(\Phi\) is continuous, so the limit is \(2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\). Then \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=1-\Prob(M_H>m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). The case \(m=0\) follows by the same limit. Assumptions used: H1, H2 and H3 of Theorem 1.3, with H3 applied at each level \(b>0\) and \(H>0\) fixed.

 414: P2 · The integral of the normal tail

 416: Prove that \(\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).

 418: Solution

 420: Integrating by parts on \([0,R]\) with \(\varphi'(u)=-u\varphi(u)\) gives \(\int_0^R(1-\Phi(u))\,du=R(1-\Phi(R))+\varphi(0)-\varphi(R)\). The tail satisfies \(1-\Phi(R)=\int_R^\infty\varphi(v)\,dv\le\varphi(R)/R\), because \(v/R\ge 1\) on \([R,\infty)\). So \(R(1-\Phi(R))\le\varphi(R)\to 0\). Letting \(R\to\infty\) leaves \(\varphi(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).

 422: P3 · The grid tail is nested

 424: Let \(N\) and \(N'\) be integers with \(N\ge 1\) and \(N'\ge 1\), and let \(N\) divide \(N'\). Prove that \(\Prob(M^{(N)}_H\ge a)\le\Prob(M^{(N')}_H\ge a)\) for every \(a\), assuming H1 of Proposition 2.2.

 426: Solution

 428: By Proposition 2.2(b), \(M^{(N)}_H\le M^{(N')}_H\) on every outcome with a continuous path. So \(\{M^{(N)}_H\ge a\}\subseteq\{M^{(N')}_H\ge a\}\), and monotonicity of probability gives the inequality. The proof uses the subset relation between the two events and monotonicity of probability.

 430: Empirical

 432: E1 · Reading the grid counts

 434: Lab 1, Part B prints the proportions 0.2275 at 4 steps and 0.3025 at 256 steps. Convert them to counts out of 400, and state what the counts show.

 436: Solution

 438: The counts are \(0.2275\times 400=91\) and \(0.3025\times 400=121\). Both are integers, as they must be for 400 paths. In this sample the count is 91 at 4 steps and 121 at 256 steps, and it does not decrease across the four grids. Proposition 2.2(b) implies that the counts on nested grids are nondecreasing for the same paths, and this sample satisfies that. Assuming A3 and A4, the sample does not show that the difference from 0.3173105 tends to zero, because four grid sizes from one seed cannot establish a limit.

 440: E2 · Recomputing an exact tail

 442: Lab 1, Part A prints \(\Prob(M_{16}\ge 4)=0.332306\). Recompute it from the walk counts.

 444: Solution

 446: The discrete tail identity gives the count \(\#\{S_{16}\ge 4\}+\#\{S_{16}>4\}\). Here \(S_{16}=2u-16\), where \(u\) is the number of up steps, so \(S_{16}\ge 4\) means \(u\ge 10\), and \(S_{16}>4\) means \(u\ge 11\). The binomial coefficients give \(\#\{S_{16}\ge 4\}=8008+4368+1820+560+120+16+1=14893\) and \(\#\{S_{16}>4\}=6885\). The total is 21778, and \(21778/2^{16}=0.332306\).

 448: E3 · The printed standard error

 450: Lab 1, Part B prints a standard error of 0.0230 for the 256-step grid, from 121 of 400 paths. Show that the sample standard error with divisor \(n-1\) of a proportion \(k/n\) is \(\sqrt{k(n-k)/(n^2(n-1))}\), and check the printed value.

 452: Solution

 454: The indicators have sample mean \(p=k/n\). Their sum of squared deviations is \(k(1-p)^2+(n-k)p^2=k(n-k)/n\), so the sample variance with divisor \(n-1\) is \(k(n-k)/(n(n-1))\). The standard error is that variance divided by \(n\), under the square root: \(\sqrt{k(n-k)/(n^2(n-1))}\). With \(k=121\) and \(n=400\), this is \(\sqrt{33759/63840000}=0.0230\), which matches the printed value.

 456: Research

 458: R1 · The restriction to positive levels

 460: Show that \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\) for \(a\le 0\), and that the expression \(2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\) exceeds 1 for \(a<0\). Explain why Theorem 1.3 is stated for \(a>0\).

 462: Solution

 464: For \(a\le 0\), \(M_H\ge 0\ge a\) by Definition 1.1, so \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\). For \(a<0\), \(-a/\sqrt H>0\), so \(\Phi(a/\sqrt H)<\tfrac12\) by the evenness of the normal density. Then \(2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))>1\), which cannot be a probability. Since \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\) for \(a\le 0\), the closed form does not give \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) for \(a<0\), and Theorem 1.3 is stated for \(a>0\).

 466: R2 · No atoms at positive levels

 468: For fixed \(H>0\) and \(a>0\), show that \(\Prob(M_H=a)=0\), assuming A3 and A4.

 470: Solution

 472: The events \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase as \(b\downarrow a\), with union \(\{M_H>a\}\). By continuity of probability from below, \(\Prob(M_H>a)=\lim_{b\downarrow a}\Prob(M_H\ge b)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), using Theorem 1.3 and the continuity of \(\Phi\). Also \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\). Subtracting, \(\Prob(M_H=a)=\Prob(M_H\ge a)-\Prob(M_H>a)=0\).

 474: R3 · Short horizons

 476: For fixed \(a>0\), find \(\lim_{H\downarrow 0}\Prob(\tau_a\le H)\), where \(\tau_a\) is the first time the path reaches \(a\), and say what the limit means for the running maximum over short times, assuming A3 and A4.

 478: Solution

 480: By Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6, \(\{\tau_a\le H\}=\{M_H\ge a\}\) on continuous paths, so \(\Prob(\tau_a\le H)=\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\). As \(H\downarrow 0\), \(a/\sqrt H\to\infty\), so \(\Phi(a/\sqrt H)\to 1\) and the probability tends to 0 (Remark 1.4). Over short horizons the running maximum has a vanishing chance of reaching a given positive level \(a\).
