## The Reflection Principle
        
          
- Uses: Brownian motion, stopping time, normal distribution; Lesson 5, Assumptions A3 and A4
          
- Introduces: reflection principle

A Brownian path that reaches a level \(a\) and then returns has a mirror image: reflect the path after the first visit to \(a\), and the reflected value at a fixed time has the same law as the original (Proposition 2.3, under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5). The reflection turns "how far does the path rise" into "where does it end", a Gaussian answer. The lesson proves the discrete version exactly for the symmetric simple random walk, including the grid's correction term. It then proves the continuous identity \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a)\), where \(M_t\) is the running maximum of \(B\) on \([0,t]\), under the strong Markov property of Lesson 5 (Assumption A3) and the facts of Assumption A4.
      
## 1The discrete reflection principle, exactly
      
Let \(N\ge 1\) be an integer. A step sequence is \((e_1,\dots,e_N)\in\{-1,1\}^N\). The walk is \(S_0=0\) and \(S_k=e_1+\dots+e_k\) for \(1\le k\le N\). This is the simple random walk of length \(N\). Its \(2^N\) step sequences have equal weight \(2^{-N}\), so each probability below is a count divided by \(2^N\). The running maximum is \(M_N=\max_{0\le k\le N}S_k\). For an integer level \(a\ge 1\), let \(\kappa=\min\{k : S_k=a\}\) be the first visit to \(a\), when it occurs at some \(k\le N\). Because the walk moves by \(\pm 1\), it visits every integer between \(0\) and \(S_N\).
      
        Definition 1.1 (reflected walk)
Let \(a\ge 1\) and let the step sequence satisfy \(\kappa\le N\). The reflected walk has steps \(e'_i=e_i\) for \(i\le\kappa\) and \(e'_i=-e_i\) for \(\kappa<i\le N\). Its walk is \(S'_k\).
      
      
        Lemma 1.2 (the reflected walk)
Assumptions. \(N\ge 1\), \(a\ge 1\), and a step sequence with \(\kappa\le N\).
        
Statement. \(S'_k=S_k\) for \(k\le\kappa\), and \(S'_k=2a-S_k\) for \(\kappa\le k\le N\). In particular \(S'_\kappa=a\), \(\kappa'=\kappa\), and \(S'_N=2a-S_N\). Reflection is an involution on the step sequences with \(\kappa\le N\).
      
      
        Proof
For \(k\le\kappa\) the steps agree, so \(S'_k=S_k\). For \(k\ge\kappa\), \(S'_k=S'_\kappa+\sum_{\kappa<i\le k}(-e_i)=a-(S_k-S_\kappa)=a-(S_k-a)=2a-S_k\), using \(S_\kappa=a\) and \(S'_\kappa=S_\kappa\). The prefix up to \(\kappa\) is unchanged, so \(\kappa'=\kappa\), because \(\kappa\) depends only on the prefix. Applying the reflection to \((e'_i)\) flips the steps after \(\kappa\) back, so it returns \((e_i)\). \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Proposition 1.3 (discrete reflection identity)
Assumptions. \(N\ge 1\) and \(a\ge 1\) are integers, and \(b<a\) is an integer. The walk is the simple random walk of length \(N\).
        
Statement. The number of step sequences with \(M_N\ge a\) and \(S_N=b\) equals the number of step sequences with \(S_N=2a-b\).
      
      
        Proof
Since \(S_0=0<a\) and the walk moves by \(\pm 1\), \(M_N\ge a\) holds exactly when \(\kappa\le N\). So the left side counts the step sequences with \(\kappa\le N\) and \(S_N=b\). By Lemma 1.2, reflection maps this set into the set of step sequences with \(\kappa\le N\) and \(S'_N=2a-b\), and it is an involution, so it is a bijection between the two sets.
        
The last set is the set of all step sequences with \(S_N=2a-b\). Each has \(\kappa\le N\), because \(S_0=0<a<2a-b=S_N\), and the walk moves by \(\pm 1\), so it visits \(a\) at some \(k\le N\). So the set of sequences with \(\kappa\le N\) and \(S_N=2a-b\) is the set of all sequences with \(S_N=2a-b\). The two counts are equal. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Corollary 1.4 (discrete tail identity)
Assumptions. Those of Proposition 1.3, for every integer \(a\ge 1\).
        
Statement. \(\#\{M_N\ge a\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\#\{S_N>a\}\), where \(\#\) counts step sequences. Hence
        \[
          \Prob(M_N\ge a)=\Prob(S_N\ge a)+\Prob(S_N>a)=2\,\Prob(S_N>a)+\Prob(S_N=a).
        \]
      
        Proof
Split \(\{M_N\ge a\}\) by the value of \(S_N\). Every sequence with \(S_N\ge a\) has \(M_N\ge a\). For \(b<a\), the sequences with \(M_N\ge a\) and \(S_N=b\) are counted by Proposition 1.3. So \(\#\{M_N\ge a\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\sum_{b<a}\#\{S_N=2a-b\}\). The map \(b\mapsto 2a-b\) is a bijection from the integers below \(a\) onto the integers above \(a\), so the sum is \(\#\{S_N>a\}\). The probability form divides by \(2^N\), and \(\Prob(S_N\ge a)=\Prob(S_N>a)+\Prob(S_N=a)\). \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
## 2The Brownian reflection, under Assumptions A3 and A4
      
Let \(B\) be a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, with the laws of Definition 1.1 of Lesson 1. Lesson 2 gives such a process on \([0,1]\), under Assumption A2 and Assumption 2.1 of Lesson 2 (Theorem 2.5 there). Remark 2.6 of Lesson 2 extends it to \([0,\infty)\), with a proof under Assumptions A1(a), A1(b) and A1(e) of Lesson 1, and Assumption A2 and Assumption 2.1 of Lesson 2; this page does not reprove it. The results below need the path only on \([0,t]\) for a fixed \(t>0\). For \(a>0\), write \(\tau_a=\inf\{s\ge 0 : B_s=a\}\), with \(\inf\varnothing=\infty\), for the first time the path reaches \(a\). Lesson 8 studies the law of this time. Write \(\mathcal F_t=\sigma(B_r : 0\le r\le t)\) for the information the path has revealed by time \(t\), as in Lesson 5, Section 1, and let \(\mathcal F_\rho\) be the \(\sigma\)-field of events known at a stopping time \(\rho\), as in Lesson 5, Section 3. The results use the events \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\) and the value \(B_t\), and Proposition 2.3 also uses the random time \(\rho=\tau_a\wedge t\) and the reflected value \(W_t\) defined in its statement.
      
        Lemma 2.1 (running maximum and first visit)
Assumptions. \(B\) has continuous paths, \(a>0\), \(t>0\), and \(B_0=0\).
        
Statement. (i) \(\{M_t\ge a\}=\{\tau_a\le t\}\), where \(M_t=\max_{0\le s\le t}B_s\). (ii) \(\{B_t\ge a\}\subseteq\{\tau_a\le t\}\). (iii) On \(\{\tau_a>t\}\), \(B_t<a\).
      
      
        Proof
(i) Suppose \(M_t\ge a\). The continuous function \(s\mapsto B_s\) attains its maximum on \([0,t]\) at some \(s^\ast\le t\), so \(B_{s^\ast}\ge a>0=B_0\). By the intermediate value theorem there is \(s\le s^\ast\) with \(B_s=a\), so \(\tau_a\le s\le t\). Conversely, if \(\tau_a\le t\), the set \(\{s : B_s=a\}\) is closed by continuity and nonempty, so \(B_{\tau_a}=a\) and \(M_t\ge B_{\tau_a}=a\).
        
(ii) If \(B_t\ge a\), the same intermediate value argument on \([0,t]\) gives \(\tau_a\le t\).
        
(iii) Let \(\tau_a>t\). If \(B_s>a\) for some \(s\le t\), then the intermediate value theorem gives a time in \((0,s)\) where \(B=a\), which contradicts \(\tau_a>t\). So \(B_s\le a\) on \([0,t]\). Since \(B_s\neq a\) for \(s<\tau_a\), we get \(B_t<a\). \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Definition 2.2 (reflection principle)
The reflection principle for \(B\) at \(\tau_a\) is the statement that, assuming A3, the path reflected after \(\tau_a\), namely \(W_s=B_s\) for \(s\le\tau_a\) and \(W_s=2a-B_s\) for \(s>\tau_a\), is again a standard Brownian motion. On this page the path-level statement is not proved. Only its fixed-time form is proved, under the assumptions of Proposition 2.3.
      
      
        Proposition 2.3 (the reflected value has the law of B at a fixed time)
Assumptions.
        
          
- \(B\) is a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, and \(a>0\), \(t>0\).
          
- Assumption A3 of Lesson 5 (strong Markov property), applied to the bounded stopping time \(\rho=\tau_a\wedge t\).
          
- Assumptions A4(b), A4(c), A4(g) and A4(h) of Lesson 5 (freezing; hitting times are stopping times; joint measurability; measurability at a stopping time).
        
        
Statement. Assuming A3 and A4(b), A4(c), A4(g) and A4(h) of Lesson 5, define \(W_t=2B_\rho-B_t\) with \(\rho=\tau_a\wedge t\). Then \(W_t\) has the same law as \(B_t\). Explicitly, \(W_t=B_t\) on \(\{\tau_a>t\}\) and \(W_t=2a-B_t\) on \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\).
      
      
        Proof
By A4(c), \(\tau_a\) is a stopping time, because \(\{a\}\) is closed and \(B\) is continuous and adapted (each \(B_r\) is \(\mathcal F_r\)-measurable). So \(\rho=\tau_a\wedge t\) is a stopping time with \(\rho\le t\): \(\{\rho\le r\}=\{\tau_a\le r\}\in\mathcal F_r\) for \(r<t\), and \(\{\rho\le r\}=\Omega\) for \(r\ge t\). On \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\), \(B_\rho=B_{\tau_a}=a\), so \(W_t=2a-B_t\). On \(\{\tau_a>t\}\), \(\rho=t\) and \(W_t=B_t\). This gives the explicit form.
        
Let \(X_r=B_{\rho+r}-B_\rho\) for \(r\ge 0\). By A3, \(X\) is a standard Brownian motion, independent of \(\mathcal F_\rho\). Since \(\rho+(t-\rho)=t\), we have \(B_t-B_\rho=X_{t-\rho}\), and therefore
        \[
          W_t=2B_\rho-B_t=B_\rho-X_{t-\rho},\qquad B_t=B_\rho+X_{t-\rho}.
        \]
Let \(g\) be bounded and Borel. By Assumption A4(h) of Lesson 5, applied to \(\rho\le t\), the variables \(B_\rho\) and \(\rho\) are \(\mathcal F_\rho\)-measurable, so \(t-\rho\in[0,t]\) is too. Also \(X\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_\rho\). Assumption A4(g) makes \((u,\omega)\mapsto X_u(\omega)\) jointly measurable on \([0,t]\times\Omega\), so \(X_{t-\rho}\) is a random variable. By A4(b), applied with \(\mathcal G=\mathcal F_\rho\), \(Z=(B_\rho,t-\rho)\), \(Y=X\) (a random continuous path, as A4(b) allows), and the bounded jointly measurable functions \(f_\mp((x,u),y)=g(x\mp y_u)\),
        \[
          \E[g(W_t)\mid\mathcal F_\rho]=h_-(B_\rho,t-\rho),\qquad \E[g(B_t)\mid\mathcal F_\rho]=h_+(B_\rho,t-\rho),
        \]
where \(h_\mp(x,u)=\E[g(x\mp X_u)]\). Since \(X_u\) has the law \(\Normal(0,u)\) by (D1) and (D2) for \(X\), and this law is symmetric, \(-X_u\) has the same law as \(X_u\). So \(h_-(x,u)=h_+(x,u)\) for all \(x\) and \(u\in[0,t]\). Hence \(\E[g(W_t)\mid\mathcal F_\rho]=\E[g(B_t)\mid\mathcal F_\rho]\) almost surely (that is, with probability one). Taking expectations gives \(\E g(W_t)=\E g(B_t)\) for every bounded Borel \(g\), so \(W_t\) and \(B_t\) have the same law. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Remark 2.4 (what is not proved)
Proposition 2.3 is a statement about one time \(t\) at a time. It does not show that the whole reflected path \(W\) is a standard Brownian motion, which is the reflection principle of Definition 2.2. That path-level statement is not used below, and this page does not claim it.
      
      
        Theorem 2.5 (the reflection identity for Brownian motion)
Assumptions.
        
          
- \(B\) is a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, and \(a>0\), \(t>0\).
          
- Assumption A3 of Lesson 5 (applied in Proposition 2.3) and Assumption A4 of Lesson 5.
        
        
Statement. Assuming A3 and A4,
        \[
          \Prob(M_t\ge a)=\Prob(B_t\ge a)+\Prob(B_t>a)=2\,\Prob(B_t>a)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a).
        \]
      
        Proof
Let \(\rho\) and \(W_t\) be as in Proposition 2.3. Write \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\) for the event of Lemma 2.1(i), so \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=\Prob(\tau_a\le t)\).
        
By Lemma 2.1(ii), \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\) contains \(\{B_t\ge a\}\). Split \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\) into \(\{B_t\ge a\}\) and \(E=\{\tau_a\le t, B_t<a\}\). So \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=\Prob(B_t\ge a)+\Prob(E)\).
        
We identify \(E\). On \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\), \(W_t=2a-B_t\), so \(W_t>a\) exactly when \(B_t<a\). Hence \(E=\{\tau_a\le t,\,W_t>a\}\). The event \(\{W_t>a\}\) lies inside \(\{\tau_a\le t\}\): on \(\{\tau_a>t\}\), \(W_t=B_t<a\) by Lemma 2.1(iii). So \(E=\{W_t>a\}\).
        
By Proposition 2.3, \(\Prob(W_t>a)=\Prob(B_t>a)\). Therefore \(\Prob(\tau_a\le t)=\Prob(B_t\ge a)+\Prob(B_t>a)\). Since \(B_t\) has the normal law \(\Normal(0,t)\) with \(t>0\), it has a density, so \(\Prob(B_t=a)=0\). The two tail probabilities are equal, and the result is \(2\,\Prob(B_t>a)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a)\). \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Corollary 2.6 (closed form of the tail)
Assumptions. \(t>0\), \(a>0\), and the standard definition \(\operatorname{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_x^\infty e^{-w^2}\,dw\).
        
Statement. \(2\,\Prob(B_t>a)=\operatorname{erfc}\big(a/\sqrt{2t}\big)\). Assuming A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, this is \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)\).
      
      
        Proof
By (D2), \(B_t\) has the law \(\Normal(0,t)\), so \(B_t/\sqrt t\) is standard normal \(Z\). Then \(\Prob(B_t>a)=\Prob(Z>a/\sqrt t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{a/\sqrt t}^\infty e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\). Substitute \(z=\sqrt2\,w\), so \(dz=\sqrt2\,dw\) and \(z^2/2=w^2\). The lower limit becomes \(a/\sqrt{2t}\), and the integral becomes \(\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{a/\sqrt{2t}}^\infty e^{-w^2}\,dw=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\int_{a/\sqrt{2t}}^\infty e^{-w^2}\,dw=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{2t})\). Multiply by 2. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        
## Lab 1 · Exact enumeration of the discrete reflection identity, and the closed form
        
The lab enumerates all \(2^N\) step sequences for each \(N\) from 1 to 12, and counts exactly with integers and fractions. Prediction: Part A finds no unequal triple, so the reflection identity of Proposition 1.3 holds for every \((N,a,b)\) it checks; Part B finds no unequal pair for the tail identity of Corollary 1.4. Part C prints the counts for \(N=12\) and \(a=3\). Part D prints the exact values \(P(M_{10}\ge 2)\), \(2P(S_{10}>2)\) and \(P(S_{10}=2)\), and shows that their difference is \(P(S_{10}=2)\). Part E prints \(2\,\Prob(B_1>1)=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)\), from the closed form of Corollary 2.6. The lab uses no random draws.
        
"""SC1 lab L06: exact enumeration of the discrete reflection identity for the symmetric simple random walk.
Standard library only. Nothing is random: the lab enumerates all 2**N step sequences for each N from 1 to 12,
so every count is an exact integer and every probability in Parts A to D is an exact fraction; Part E evaluates a
closed form in floating point. The walk starts at S_0 = 0, and
S_k is the sum of the steps e_1 to e_k, where each step e_i is +1 or -1. For an integer level a >= 1, M_N is
the largest of S_0 to S_N. Proposition 1.3 of the page proves, for every N, every integer a >= 1 and every
integer b < a, that the number of paths with M_N >= a and S_N = b equals the number of paths with S_N = 2a - b.
The lab checks that identity for every N from 1 to 12, every integer a with 1 <= a <= N, and every integer b < a
with b >= -N, and checks the tail identity of Corollary 1.4 for the same (N, a). Part D shows the term that the
discrete identity keeps and the continuous identity drops. Part E evaluates the continuous tail 2 P(B_1 > 1) in
closed form.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
import math
from collections import Counter
from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import product
MAX_N = 12
def path_summary(steps: tuple[int, ...]) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """Return (maximum of S_0 .. S_N, S_N) for the walk with the given +1 and -1 steps."""
    position = 0
    highest = 0
    for step in steps:
        position += step
        highest = max(highest, position)
    return highest, position
def summaries(n: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """The (maximum, endpoint) pair of each of the 2**n paths of length n."""
    return [path_summary(steps) for steps in product((1, -1), repeat=n)]
def main() -> None:
    print("Part A: reflection identity #{M_N >= a, S_N = b} = #{S_N = 2a - b} for b < a")
    triples = 0
    equal = 0
    for n in range(1, MAX_N + 1):
        pairs = summaries(n)
        ends = Counter(end for _, end in pairs)
        for a in range(1, n + 1):
            hits_a = Counter(end for top, end in pairs if top >= a)
            for b in range(-n, a):
                triples += 1
                equal += hits_a[b] == ends[2 * a - b]
    print(f"triples (N, a, b) checked: {triples}; equal: {equal}")
    print(f"Part A holds for every triple: {triples == equal}")
    print("Part B: tail identity #{M_N >= a} = #{S_N >= a} + #{S_N > a}")
    checks = 0
    tail_equal = 0
    for n in range(1, MAX_N + 1):
        pairs = summaries(n)
        for a in range(1, n + 1):
            checks += 1
            maxima = sum(1 for top, _ in pairs if top >= a)
            at_least = sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end >= a)
            above = sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end > a)
            tail_equal += maxima == at_least + above
    print(f"pairs (N, a) checked: {checks}; equal: {tail_equal}")
    print("Part C: the example N = 12, a = 3")
    pairs = summaries(12)
    maxima_12 = sum(1 for top, _ in pairs if top >= 3)
    ends_12 = Counter(end for _, end in pairs)
    print(f"paths with M_12 >= 3: {maxima_12} of {2**12}")
    print(f"paths with M_12 >= 3 and S_12 = 2: {sum(1 for top, end in pairs if top >= 3 and end == 2)}")
    print(f"paths with S_12 = 4 (the reflected endpoint 2 * 3 - 2): {ends_12[4]}")
    print("Part D: the discrete correction, N = 10 and a = 2")
    pairs = summaries(10)
    total = 2**10
    tail = Fraction(sum(1 for top, _ in pairs if top >= 2), total)
    above_exact = Fraction(sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end > 2), total)
    at_level = Fraction(sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end == 2), total)
    print(f"P(M_10 >= 2) = {tail} = {float(tail):.6f}")
    print(f"2 P(S_10 > 2) = {2 * above_exact} = {float(2 * above_exact):.6f}")
    print(f"P(S_10 = 2) = {at_level} = {float(at_level):.6f}")
    print(f"P(M_10 >= 2) - 2 P(S_10 > 2) equals P(S_10 = 2): {tail - 2 * above_exact == at_level}")
    print("Part E: continuous tail at t = 1, a = 1")
    two_tail = math.erfc(1.0 / math.sqrt(2.0))
    print(f"2 P(B_1 > 1) = erfc(1 / sqrt(2)) = {two_tail:.10f}")
    print(f"P(B_1 > 1) = {two_tail / 2:.10f}")
main()

## 3Where the canon breaks
        
        
- Discrete grid. Corollary 1.4 has the extra term \(\Prob(S_N=a)\) that Theorem 2.5 does not have. Part D of Lab 1 shows the term at \(N=10\), \(a=2\). This page does not compute how the term behaves as \(N\) grows.
        
- Path continuity. Lemma 2.1 uses the intermediate value theorem, which needs continuous paths. A path that jumps from below \(a\) to above \(a\) has \(M_t\ge a\) without ever taking the value \(a\), so \(\{M_t\ge a\}\neq\{\tau_a\le t\}\) for such a path. The process \(\tilde B\) of Lesson 1, Section 4, has discontinuous paths, and nothing on this page applies it.
        
- Infinite hitting times. Proposition 2.3 uses \(\rho=\tau_a\wedge t\), which is bounded. The page does not establish that \(\tau_a\) is finite, and it does not state the law of \(\tau_a\). Lesson 8 does.
        
- Symmetry. The factor 2 comes from adding two events of equal probability in the proof of Theorem 2.5. Proposition 2.3 gives \(\Prob(E)=\Prob(B_t>a)\), and it uses the symmetry of \(\Normal(0,u)\) at the step \(h_-=h_+\). The no-atom fact \(\Prob(B_t=a)=0\), from the normal density, is also needed to make \(\Prob(B_t\ge a)=\Prob(B_t>a)\). The page makes no claim about the tail of a process whose increments are not symmetric.
        
- Assumptions. Theorem 2.5 is conditional on A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, which are stated and not proved there. The path-level reflection principle is not proved, only its fixed-time form.
      
      
      
        
## 4Misconceptions
        
          
- 
“The running maximum has the same tail as the endpoint: \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=\Prob(B_t\ge a)\).”
            
Assuming A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, Theorem 2.5 gives \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a)\). At \(t=1\) and \(a=1\), Corollary 2.6 gives \(2\,\Prob(B_1>1)=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)=0.3173\), so under that assumption \(\Prob(M_1\ge 1)=0.3173\), while \(\Prob(B_1\ge 1)=0.1587\) (both values rounded to four decimal places). Part E of Lab 1 prints the first value to ten decimal places.
          
          
- 
“The discrete identity \(\Prob(M_N\ge a)=2\,\Prob(S_N>a)\) holds exactly, so the continuum has the same form.”
            
Corollary 1.4 has the extra term \(\Prob(S_N=a)\). At \(N=10\) and \(a=2\), Part D of Lab 1 prints \(\Prob(M_{10}\ge 2)=281/512\) and \(2\,\Prob(S_{10}>2)=11/32\). The difference \(105/512\) is \(\Prob(S_{10}=2)\), which is not zero.
          
          
- 
“Reflecting after the first visit to a level always gives an equal-law path, with no path assumption.”
            
The identity \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=\Prob(\tau_a\le t)\) needs continuity (Lemma 2.1), and the fixed-time law of the reflected value needs the strong Markov property (Assumption A3 of Lesson 5) and the facts of Assumption A4 of Lesson 5. Section 3 gives the failure for a jump.

## Mechanics
          
            M1 · Count the paths of length 3 that reach 1
For \(N=3\) and \(a=1\), list the step sequences with \(M_3\ge 1\), count them, and check Corollary 1.4.
            
Solution
              
The sequences with \(e_1=+1\) reach 1 at \(k=1\): there are 4 of them. The sequence \((-1,+1,+1)\) has \(S=(0,-1,0,1)\), which reaches 1, so it counts. The other three sequences starting with \(-1\), namely \((-1,-1,-1)\), \((-1,+1,-1)\) and \((-1,-1,+1)\), have maxima 0, 0 and 0, so they do not count. The count is \(4+1=5\) of \(8\). For Corollary 1.4: \(\#\{S_3\ge 1\}=3+1=4\), from \(S_3=1\) (three sequences) and \(S_3=3\) (one), and \(\#\{S_3>1\}=1\). The sum is \(5\), which agrees.
            
          
          
            M2 · The probability of reaching 2 in two steps
For \(N=2\) and \(a=2\), find \(\Prob(M_2\ge 2)\), and check Corollary 1.4.
            
Solution
              
Only the sequence \((+1,+1)\) reaches 2, so \(\Prob(M_2\ge 2)=1/4\). Corollary 1.4 gives \(\#\{S_2\ge 2\}+\#\{S_2>2\}=1+0=1\), since \(S_2\ge 2\) only for \((+1,+1)\) and \(S_2\) cannot exceed 2. The probability is \(1/4\), as before.
            
          
          
            M3 · Reflect a specific path
Take \(N=5\), \(a=2\), and steps \((+1,+1,-1,-1,+1)\). Find \(\kappa\), the reflected steps, the reflected path and its endpoint, and check Lemma 1.2.
            
Solution
              
The path is \(S=(0,1,2,1,0,1)\), so \(\kappa=2\). The steps after \(\kappa\) are \((-1,-1,+1)\), which reflect to \((+1,+1,-1)\). The reflected steps are \((+1,+1,+1,+1,-1)\), with path \(S'=(0,1,2,3,4,3)\). Lemma 1.2 says \(S'_k=2a-S_k=4-S_k\) for \(k\ge 2\): \(4-2=2\), \(4-1=3\), \(4-0=4\), \(4-1=3\). These equal \(S'_2,\dots,S'_5=2,3,4,3\). The endpoint is \(S'_5=3=2a-S_5\).
            
          
        
        
          
## Proof
          
            P1 · Reflection is an involution
Show that applying the reflection of Definition 1.1 twice returns the original step sequence, for every step sequence with \(\kappa\le N\).
            
Solution
              
Let \(e'\) be the reflected steps of \(e\). Then \(e'_i=e_i\) for \(i\le\kappa\) and \(e'_i=-e_i\) for \(\kappa<i\le N\). The first time the walk built from \(e'\) reaches \(a\) is \(\kappa'=\kappa\), by Lemma 1.2, so the reflection of \(e'\) negates the steps after the same \(\kappa\). Negating twice gives \(e''_i=e'_i\) for \(i\le\kappa\) and \(e''_i=-e'_i=e_i\) for \(\kappa<i\le N\). So \(e''=e\).
            
          
          
            P2 · Parts (ii) and (iii) of Lemma 2.1 from continuity
Prove Lemma 2.1 (ii) and (iii) from continuity of \(B\) and \(B_0=0\), without using part (i).
            
Solution
              
(ii) If \(B_t\ge a\) with \(a>0=B_0\), then the continuous function \(s\mapsto B_s\) on \([0,t]\) takes the values 0 at time 0 and at least \(a\) at time \(t\). The intermediate value theorem gives some \(s\in[0,t]\) with \(B_s=a\), so \(\tau_a\le s\le t\). (iii) Suppose \(\tau_a>t\), so \(B_s\neq a\) for all \(s\le t\) by the definition of \(\tau_a\). If \(B_s>a\) for some \(s\le t\), the intermediate value theorem on \([0,s]\) gives a time in \((0,s)\) where \(B=a\), which would make \(\tau_a<s\le t\). So \(B_s<a\) for all \(s\le t\), and in particular \(B_t<a\).
            
          
          
            P3 · Corollary 1.4 from Proposition 1.3
Derive \(\#\{M_N\ge a\}=\#\{S_N\ge a\}+\#\{S_N>a\}\) from Proposition 1.3 for a single integer \(a\ge 1\), without repeating the bijection.
            
Solution
              
Split the sequences with \(M_N\ge a\) by \(S_N\). Those with \(S_N\ge a\) all have \(M_N\ge a\), and there are \(\#\{S_N\ge a\}\) of them. Those with \(S_N=b<a\) and \(M_N\ge a\) are counted, for each \(b\), by \(\#\{S_N=2a-b\}\), from Proposition 1.3. As \(b\) runs over the integers below \(a\), \(2a-b\) runs over the integers above \(a\), so the sum is \(\#\{S_N>a\}\).
            
          
        
        
          
## Empirical
          
            E1 · How many cases does Part B check?
Part B of Lab 1 prints \(78\) pairs \((N,a)\) checked, and all equal. Explain why the number is 78, and why the check covers every pair with \(1\le a\le N\le 12\).
            
Solution
              
For each \(N\), the code takes \(a\) from 1 to \(N\), so there are \(N\) pairs. Summing over \(N=1,\dots,12\) gives \(1+2+\dots+12=\tfrac{12\cdot 13}{2}=78\). All 78 pairs print as equal, so the tail identity holds on every pair the lab checks. The lab does not check \(a>N\), where \(\#\{M_N\ge a\}\) is zero.
            
          
          
            E2 · The discrete correction at N = 10
From Part D of Lab 1, verify that \(\Prob(M_{10}\ge 2)-2\,\Prob(S_{10}>2)=\Prob(S_{10}=2)\), using the printed fractions.
            
Solution
              
The printed values are \(281/512\), \(11/32=352/1024\) and \(105/512=210/1024\). Then \(562/1024-352/1024=210/1024\), which is \(\Prob(S_{10}=2)\). The check agrees with Corollary 1.4 in the form \(\Prob(M_N\ge a)=2\,\Prob(S_N>a)+\Prob(S_N=a)\). The printed value \(\Prob(S_{10}=2)=\binom{10}{6}/1024=210/1024\) also matches the binomial count of sequences with six up-steps.
            
          
          
            E3 · Check the closed form at t = 1 and a = 1
From Part E of Lab 1, the printed values are \(2\,\Prob(B_1>1)=0.3173105079\) and \(\Prob(B_1>1)=0.1586552539\). Check that the second is half the first, and that the first equals \(\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)\).
            
Solution
              
The lab computes \(\Prob(B_1>1)\) as \(\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)/2\), so the two printed values are the same closed form at two scales, and their ratio is 2 by construction. Halving the printed \(0.3173105079\) gives \(0.15865525395\), which differs from the printed \(0.1586552539\) only because the first value is itself rounded: the unrounded half is \(0.15865525393\ldots\), which prints as \(0.1586552539\). Corollary 2.6 with \(t=1\) and \(a=1\) gives \(2\,\Prob(B_1>1)=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)\).
            
          
        
        
          
## Research
          
            R1 · The identity without a parity condition
Show that Proposition 1.3 needs no parity assumption on \(b\), and explain why both sides vanish when \(b\) and \(N\) have different parity.
            
Solution
              
Every \(S_N\) has the parity of \(N\), since it is a sum of \(N\) terms \(\pm 1\). If \(b\not\equiv N\pmod 2\), then no sequence has \(S_N=b\), so the left side is zero. Also \(2a-b\equiv b\pmod 2\), so \(2a-b\not\equiv N\pmod 2\), and no sequence has \(S_N=2a-b\), so the right side is zero. The identity holds with both sides zero, and the proof of Proposition 1.3 does not use parity anywhere.
            
          
          
            R2 · The minimum of B, assuming A3 for −B
Let \(a>0\). Assuming A3 and A4 hold for \(-B\) as well, show that \(\Prob\big(\min_{s\le t}B_s\le -a\big)=2\,\Prob(B_t<-a)\).
            
Solution
              
Let \(B'=-B\). It is a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, since (D1) to (D3) are preserved under sign change: \(B'_t-B'_s=-(B_t-B_s)\) has the law \(\Normal(0,t-s)\), and independence is preserved. Then \(\min_{s\le t}B_s\le -a\) holds exactly when \(\max_{s\le t}B'_s\ge a\). By Theorem 2.5 applied to \(B'\), \(\Prob(\max_{s\le t}B'_s\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B'_t>a)=2\,\Prob(-B_t>a)=2\,\Prob(B_t<-a)\).
            
          
          
            R3 · A strict inequality in the continuum
Assuming A3 and A4, show that \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)>\Prob(B_t\ge a)>0\) for \(a>0\) and \(t>0\).
            
Solution
              
Theorem 2.5 gives \(\Prob(M_t\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_t\ge a)\). Since \(\Prob(B_t\ge a)>0\), the first inequality is \(2x>x\) for \(x=\Prob(B_t\ge a)>0\). Positivity holds because \(B_t\) has the normal law \(\Normal(0,t)\), which gives positive probability to every half-line. So both inequalities hold.
