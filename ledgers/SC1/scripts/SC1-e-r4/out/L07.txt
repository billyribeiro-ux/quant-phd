## The Distribution of the Running Maximum
        
          
- Uses: Brownian motion as defined in Lesson 1, with continuous paths as in Lesson 2; the reflection identity of Lesson 6; the normal distribution, random variable and expectation from M4 and M6
          
- Introduces: distribution of the running maximum (defined in §1)

- Areplicated
        
- Bpublished, not settled
        
- Csingle study or lab result
        
- Dlore

The running maximum up to a horizon \(H\), \(M_H=\max_{0\le s\le H}B_s\), is the highest value a Brownian path reaches by time \(H\). Its tail is the reflection identity of Lesson 6 in closed form: for \(a>0\), \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function, under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5. A computer sees a path only at finitely many times. On a continuous path, the maximum over a grid (Definition 2.1 of this lesson) never exceeds \(M_H\), so a grid-based estimate of the tail is a one-sided underestimate, and Section 2 proves that bound. Lab 1 checks the walk identity exactly and compares seeded grid maxima with the closed form.
      
## 1The running maximum and its tail
      
Let \(B\) be a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, as in Lesson 2. Lesson 6 proves the reflection identity for it, under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5. This lesson uses that identity and does not prove it again. Lesson 5 states Assumptions A3 and A4 without proof, so every result below that uses them says so.
      
        Definition 1.1 (running maximum)
For \(H\ge 0\), the running maximum of \(B\) up to the horizon \(H\) is \(M_H=\max_{0\le s\le H}B_s\). Lesson 6 writes the same horizon as \(t\). The maximum exists because \(s\mapsto B_s\) is continuous on the compact interval \([0,H]\). Since \(B_0=0\) is one of the values in the maximum, \(M_H\ge 0\).
      
      
        Definition 1.2 (distribution of the running maximum)
For fixed \(H>0\), the distribution of the running maximum is the law of \(M_H\). Its tail function is \(a\mapsto\Prob(M_H\ge a)\). Section 1 computes this function in closed form.
      
      
        Theorem 1.3 (tail of the running maximum)
          
- (H1) \(B\) is a standard Brownian motion with continuous paths, and \(B_0=0\).
          
- (H2) Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, used through Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6.
          
- (H3) \(H>0\) and \(a>0\) are fixed.
        
        
Conclusion. Assuming H2, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))=\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(a/\sqrt{2H}\bigr)\), where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function and \(\operatorname{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_x^\infty e^{-w^2}\,dw\) is the complementary error function, the definition used in Corollary 2.6 of Lesson 6.
      
      
        Proof
By Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6, under H2, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\). By Definition 1.1 of Lesson 1 with \(s=0\), \(B_H-B_0=B_H\) has the normal law with mean 0 and variance \(H\). So \(Z=B_H/\sqrt H\) is standard normal, and \(\{B_H\ge a\}=\{Z\ge a/\sqrt H\}\). The standard normal law has no atoms, so \(\Prob(Z\ge x)=\Prob(Z>x)=1-\Phi(x)\) for every real \(x\). Taking \(x=a/\sqrt H\) gives \(\Prob(B_H\ge a)=1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H)\). The second form is Corollary 2.6 of Lesson 6, which states \(2\,\Prob(B_H>a)=\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{2H})\) under the same assumptions. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Remark 1.4 (limits in the horizon and the level)
Fix \(a>0\). The argument \(a/\sqrt H\) tends to \(\infty\) as \(H\downarrow 0\) and to 0 as \(H\to\infty\). Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) tends to \(2(1-1)=0\) as \(H\downarrow 0\) and to \(2(1-\Phi(0))=1\) as \(H\to\infty\). The value \(\Phi(0)=\tfrac12\) holds because the standard normal density is even, so \(\Prob(Z\ge 0)=\Prob(Z\le 0)\) for the standard normal variable \(Z=B_H/\sqrt H\) of the proof of Theorem 1.3. These two events add to 1, because \(\Prob(Z\ge 0)+\Prob(Z\le 0)=1+\Prob(Z=0)\) and \(\Prob(Z=0)=0\), since the standard normal law has no atoms. Under H2 these limits follow from the closed form, because \(\Phi\) is continuous.
      
      
        Corollary 1.5 (distribution function and mean)
Assumptions. Those of Theorem 1.3 for part (a). For part (b), additionally Assumption A5 of this lesson.
        
          
- (a) For \(m\ge 0\), \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=2\,\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\), under H1, H2 and H3 of Theorem 1.3 (H2 is Assumptions A3 and A4).
          
- (b) Assuming A5, and under H1, H2 and H3 of Theorem 1.3, \(\E[M_H]=\sqrt{2H/\pi}\).
        
      
      
        Assumption A5 (tail formula for a nonnegative variable)
For a random variable \(X\ge 0\), \(\E[X]=\int_0^\infty \Prob(X>m)\,dm\), with the value in \([0,\infty]\). This page takes it as an assumption and does not prove it.
      
      
        Proof
(a). Let \(m>0\). As \(b\downarrow m\) the events \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase, because a larger \(b\) gives a smaller event. Their union over \(b>m\) is \(\{M_H>m\}\): if \(M_H>m\), any \(b\in(m,M_H]\) works, and if \(M_H\ge b>m\) then \(M_H>m\). By continuity of probability from below and Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H>m)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}\Prob(M_H\ge b)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}2(1-\Phi(b/\sqrt H))=2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\). Hence \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=1-\Prob(M_H>m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). For \(m=0\), the same limit gives \(\Prob(M_H>0)=2(1-\Phi(0))=1\), so \(\Prob(M_H\le 0)=0=2\Phi(0)-1\).
        
(b). Let \(\varphi(u)=e^{-u^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\), so \(\Phi'=\varphi\) and \(\varphi'(u)=-u\varphi(u)\). For \(R>0\), integrate by parts:
        
\[\int_0^R\bigl(1-\Phi(u)\bigr)\,du=\bigl[u(1-\Phi(u))\bigr]_0^R+\int_0^R u\,\varphi(u)\,du=R\bigl(1-\Phi(R)\bigr)+\varphi(0)-\varphi(R).\]
        
The last step uses \(\int_0^R u\varphi(u)\,du=-\int_0^R\varphi'(u)\,du=\varphi(0)-\varphi(R)\). Next, \(1-\Phi(R)=\int_R^\infty\varphi(v)\,dv\le\int_R^\infty (v/R)\varphi(v)\,dv=\varphi(R)/R\), because \(v/R\ge 1\) on \([R,\infty)\), and \(\int_R^\infty v\varphi(v)\,dv=\varphi(R)\). So \(0\le R(1-\Phi(R))\le\varphi(R)\), which tends to 0 as \(R\to\infty\). Letting \(R\to\infty\) gives \(\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=\varphi(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).
        
By Assumption A5 and part (a), \(\E[M_H]=\int_0^\infty\Prob(M_H>m)\,dm=\int_0^\infty 2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\,dm\). Substituting \(m=\sqrt H\,u\) gives \(2\sqrt H\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=2\sqrt H/\sqrt{2\pi}=\sqrt{2H/\pi}\). \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
## 2The discretely monitored maximum
      
A simulation or a monitoring system observes the path at finitely many times. The next definition gives the maximum that such an observer sees.
      
        Definition 2.1 (grid and discretely monitored maximum)
Fix \(H>0\) and an integer \(N\ge 1\). The equal grid of \([0,H]\) with \(N\) steps has times \(s_k=kH/N\) for \(k=0,\dots,N\). The discretely monitored maximum of \(B\) on this grid is \(M^{(N)}_H=\max_{0\le k\le N}B_{H_k}\).
      
      
        Proposition 2.2 (the grid maximum never exceeds the running maximum)
          
- (H1) For a fixed outcome, the path \(s\mapsto B_s\) is continuous on \([0,H]\).
          
- (H2) \(N\) and \(N'\) are integers with \(N\ge 1\), and \(N\) divides \(N'\).
        
        
Conclusion: (a) \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\). (b) \(M^{(N)}_H\le M^{(N')}_H\).
      
      
        Proof
(a) The grid times \(s_k\) are points of \([0,H]\), so the maximum of the path over the grid is at most its maximum over \([0,H]\). This is \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\) for the outcome in question. (b) Write \(N'=cN\) with \(c\) a positive integer. Then \(s_k=kH/N=(kc)H/N'\), so every grid time of the \(N\)-step grid is a grid time of the \(N'\)-step grid. The maximum over a subset of a finite set of values is at most the maximum over the whole set. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Corollary 2.3 (the grid tail is bounded by the running tail)
Under H1 of Proposition 2.2, for every real \(a\) and every grid, \(\{M^{(N)}_H\ge a\}\subseteq\{M_H\ge a\}\). Hence \(\Prob(M^{(N)}_H\ge a)\le\Prob(M_H\ge a)\). Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the right-hand side equals the closed form of Theorem 1.3 for \(a>0\).
      
      
        Proof
If \(M^{(N)}_H\ge a\), then \(M_H\ge M^{(N)}_H\ge a\) by Proposition 2.2(a). This is the inclusion, and monotonicity of probability gives the inequality. The closed form is Theorem 1.3. \(\blacksquare\)
      
      
        Remark 2.4 (one step)
With \(N=1\), the grid is \(\{0,H\}\), and \(M^{(1)}_H=\max(B_0,B_H)=\max(0,B_H)\). For \(a>0\), \(\{\max(0,B_H)\ge a\}=\{B_H\ge a\}\), so \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_H\ge a)=\Prob(B_H\ge a)\) with no probability assumption. Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the factor 2 of Theorem 2.5 of Lesson 6 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\). Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, the one-step grid tail is therefore exactly half the continuous tail.
      
      
        
## Lab 1 · Exact walk counts, grid maxima of seeded Brownian paths, and the closed form (Parts B and C SIMULATED)
        
Predicted before running. Part A enumerates, for \(N\le 12\), all \(2^N\) step sequences of the simple random walk (partial sums of \(\pm 1\) steps from 0) and checks the walk identity of Lesson 6 for every pair. It then prints the exact tails \(\Prob(M_N\ge m)\) at \(m=\sqrt N\) for \(N\) up to 1024; these should decrease toward the closed form \(2(1-\Phi(1))=0.317311\), which holds under A3 and A4. Part B simulates 400 seeded Brownian paths on \([0,1]\) with 256 fine steps and reads the grid maxima for 4, 16, 64 and 256 steps from the same paths. By Proposition 2.2, the counts of paths reaching level 1 should not decrease in \(N\). Part C prints the mean grid maximum against the closed-form mean \(\sqrt{2/\pi}=0.797885\), which holds under A3, A4 and A5, and Part D evaluates \(1-\Phi(1)\) by erfc and by Simpson integration of the density.
        
"""SC1 lab L07: exact walk counts for the running maximum, grid maxima of seeded Brownian paths, and the closed form.
Standard library only. Part A and Part D use no randomness. Parts B and C are SIMULATED: they draw PATHS Brownian
paths on [0, 1] from random.Random(SEED), so every run prints the same lines.
Part A enumerates all 2**N step sequences for N = 1 to 12 and checks, for every level a from 1 to N, the walk identity
of Lesson 6: the number of sequences with running maximum at least a equals the number with endpoint at least a plus
the number with endpoint above a. It then evaluates the same counts with math.comb for N = 4, 16, 64, 256 and 1024, at
the level m = sqrt(N), so that m / sqrt(N) = 1 on the scale of the Brownian path. The walk identity holds for all
pairs in the enumerated range. The probabilities are exact integer counts divided by 2**N, computed as floats
and printed to six decimals.
Part B uses FINE = 256 equal steps on [0, 1]. The grids with N = 4, 16, 64 and 256 steps are subsets of the fine grid,
so the lab counts pathwise violations of the nested inequality between consecutive grids. It then prints, for each
grid, the proportion of paths whose grid maximum reaches level 1, with the standard error computed with divisor
PATHS - 1, next to the closed form 2 (1 - Phi(1)) of the running-maximum tail.
Part C prints the mean of the grid maximum on each grid, with its standard error, next to sqrt(2 / pi), the closed-form
mean of the running maximum on [0, 1].
Part D evaluates 1 - Phi(1) two ways: by erfc, and by composite Simpson integration of the standard normal density on
[1, 13] with SIMPSON_INTERVALS intervals. The Simpson value is a numerical rule; the erfc value is a library function.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
import math
import random
from collections.abc import Callable
from itertools import accumulate, pairwise, product
SEED = 20260507
PATHS = 400
FINE = 256
GRID_STEPS = (4, 16, 64, 256)
LEVEL = 1.0
MAX_ENUM = 12
EXACT_N = (4, 16, 64, 256, 1024)
SIMPSON_INTERVALS = 2000
def end_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
    """The number of length-n step sequences whose endpoint S_n is at least m, as an exact integer.
    The endpoint is S_n = 2u - n, where u is the number of +1 steps, so the count is the sum of C(n, u) over the u
    for which 2u - n is at least m.
    """
    return sum(math.comb(n, (n + s) // 2) for s in range(-n, n + 1, 2) if s >= m)
def max_at_least(n: int, m: int) -> int:
    """The number of length-n step sequences whose running maximum over S_0 to S_n is at least m.
    By the walk identity, this equals the count with endpoint at least m plus the count with endpoint above m.
    """
    return end_at_least(n, m) + end_at_least(n, m + 1)
def enumerated_pairs(n: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """For each of the 2**n step sequences of length n, the pair (running maximum, endpoint) of the walk S_0 = 0."""
    pairs: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    for steps in product((1, -1), repeat=n):
        position = 0
        highest = 0
        for step in steps:
            position += step
            highest = max(highest, position)
        pairs.append((highest, position))
    return pairs
def part_a() -> None:
    """Check the walk identity by enumeration for N <= 12, then print exact tails for the larger N."""
    checked = 0
    identity_holds = 0
    formula_agrees = 0
    for n in range(1, MAX_ENUM + 1):
        pairs = enumerated_pairs(n)
        for m in range(1, n + 1):
            reached = sum(1 for top, _ in pairs if top >= m)
            at_least = sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end >= m)
            above = sum(1 for _, end in pairs if end > m)
            checked += 1
            identity_holds += reached == at_least + above
            formula_agrees += reached == max_at_least(n, m)
    print(f"Part A: walk identity (maximum >= a) = (endpoint >= a) + (endpoint > a): {identity_holds} of {checked}")
    print(f"Part A: counting formula agrees with the enumeration: {formula_agrees} of {checked}")
    print(f"Part A: pairs (N, a), N = 1 to {MAX_ENUM} and a = 1 to N; each count is exact")
    print("Part A: exact tails P(M_N >= m) at m = sqrt(N), so that m / sqrt(N) = 1")
    for n in EXACT_N:
        m = math.isqrt(n)
        probability = max_at_least(n, m) / 2**n
        print(f"  N = {n:4d}, m = {m:2d}: P(M_N >= m) = {probability:.6f}")
def std_normal_cdf(x: float) -> float:
    """The standard normal distribution function Phi(x) = erfc(-x / sqrt(2)) / 2, a probability."""
    return 0.5 * math.erfc(-x / math.sqrt(2.0))
def fine_path(rng: random.Random) -> list[float]:
    """The values of a Brownian path on [0, 1] at the times j / FINE for j = 0 to FINE, in length units.
    The path is the partial sum of FINE independent Gaussian increments with mean 0 and variance 1 / FINE.
    """
    scale = math.sqrt(1.0 / FINE)
    increments = [rng.gauss(0.0, scale) for _ in range(FINE)]
    return [0.0, *accumulate(increments)]
def grid_maximum(path: list[float], n: int) -> float:
    """The maximum of the path over the n + 1 grid times of the equal partition of [0, 1] into n steps."""
    stride = FINE // n
    return max(path[::stride])
def mean_and_standard_error(values: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """The sample mean, and the standard error sd / sqrt(n) with sd computed by divisor n - 1."""
    n = len(values)
    mean = sum(values) / n
    variance = sum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in values) / (n - 1)
    return mean, math.sqrt(variance / n)
def simulate_maxima(rng: random.Random) -> dict[int, list[float]]:
    """For each of the PATHS seeded paths, the grid maximum on each grid in GRID_STEPS, using the same path."""
    maxima: dict[int, list[float]] = {n: [] for n in GRID_STEPS}
    for _ in range(PATHS):
        path = fine_path(rng)
        for n in GRID_STEPS:
            maxima[n].append(grid_maximum(path, n))
    return maxima
def nesting_violations(maxima: dict[int, list[float]]) -> int:
    """The number of (path, pair of consecutive grids) with a coarse maximum above the finer maximum. Exact count."""
    violations = 0
    for coarse, fine in pairwise(GRID_STEPS):
        for coarse_max, fine_max in zip(maxima[coarse], maxima[fine], strict=True):
            violations += coarse_max > fine_max
    return violations
def part_b(maxima: dict[int, list[float]]) -> None:
    """Print the nesting check and the grid tail estimates against the closed form at level 1 on [0, 1]."""
    closed = 2.0 * (1.0 - std_normal_cdf(LEVEL))
    print(f"Part B: {PATHS} seeded Brownian paths on [0, 1] (SIMULATED), fine grid of {FINE} steps")
    violations = nesting_violations(maxima)
    print(f"Part B: pathwise violations of nesting (coarse maximum above fine maximum): {violations}")
    for n in GRID_STEPS:
        indicators = [1.0 if m >= LEVEL else 0.0 for m in maxima[n]]
        estimate, se = mean_and_standard_error(indicators)
        print(
            f"Part B: N = {n:3d} steps: P(grid max >= 1) = {estimate:.4f}, se {se:.4f}; "
            f"closed form P(M_1 >= 1) = {closed:.4f}; difference {estimate - closed:+.4f}"
        )
def part_c(maxima: dict[int, list[float]]) -> None:
    """Print the mean of the grid maximum on each grid, with its standard error, against sqrt(2 / pi)."""
    target = math.sqrt(2.0 / math.pi)
    print(f"Part C: closed-form mean of the running maximum on [0, 1], sqrt(2 / pi) = {target:.6f}")
    for n in GRID_STEPS:
        mean, se = mean_and_standard_error(maxima[n])
        print(
            f"Part C: N = {n:3d} steps: mean grid maximum {mean:.4f}, se {se:.4f}; "
            f"difference from sqrt(2 / pi) {mean - target:+.4f}"
        )
def simpson(g: Callable[[float], float], a: float, b: float, intervals: int) -> float:
    """Composite Simpson rule for the integral of g on [a, b] with an even number of intervals."""
    h = (b - a) / intervals
    total = g(a) + g(b)
    for i in range(1, intervals):
        total += (4.0 if i % 2 else 2.0) * g(a + i * h)
    return total * h / 3.0
def standard_normal_density(x: float) -> float:
    """The standard normal density exp(-x^2 / 2) / sqrt(2 pi), a density per unit of x."""
    return math.exp(-x * x / 2.0) / math.sqrt(2.0 * math.pi)
def part_d() -> None:
    """Evaluate 1 - Phi(1) by erfc and by Simpson integration of the density, and compare them."""
    by_erfc = 0.5 * math.erfc(1.0 / math.sqrt(2.0))
    by_simpson = simpson(standard_normal_density, 1.0, 13.0, SIMPSON_INTERVALS)
    print(f"Part D: 1 - Phi(1) by erfc = {by_erfc:.10f}")
    print(f"Part D: 1 - Phi(1) by Simpson on [1, 13] with {SIMPSON_INTERVALS} intervals = {by_simpson:.10f}")
    print(f"Part D: the two values agree to 1e-9: {abs(by_erfc - by_simpson) < 1e-9}")
def main() -> None:
    part_a()
    maxima = simulate_maxima(random.Random(SEED))
    part_b(maxima)
    part_c(maxima)
    part_d()
main()

## 3Where the canon breaks
        
          
- Continuity of the paths is needed. Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6 uses continuity to identify \(\{M_H\ge a\}\) with \(\{\tau_a\le H\}\). A path that jumps over the level \(a\) can satisfy \(M_H\ge a\) without visiting \(a\). The tail formula is not asserted for the process \(\tilde B\) of Lesson 1, Section 4, whose paths are discontinuous.
          
- The closed form is conditional. Theorem 1.3 holds under Assumptions A3 and A4 of Lesson 5, which are stated and sketched there but not proved. Every result on this page that uses the closed form either carries the phrase "Assuming A3 and A4" or names H2 of Theorem 1.3, which is that assumption.
          
- The grid maximum is a different random variable. Under H2 of Theorem 1.3, Remark 2.4 makes the one-step grid tail exactly half the continuous tail at every level \(a>0\). By Corollary 2.3, no grid tail exceeds the continuous tail. Lab 1 shows a gap that narrows with \(N\) in one sample. This page gives no closed form for the grid tail at \(N>1\).
        
      
      
        
## 4Misconceptions
        
          
- 
“The running maximum has the same law as \(B_H\).”
            
Assuming A3 and A4, Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2\,\Prob(B_H\ge a)\) for \(a>0\), which is twice the tail of \(B_H\). At \(H=1\) and \(a=1\), the tail of \(M_1\) is 0.3173 and the tail of \(B_1\) is 0.1587, each rounded to four decimals (unrounded 0.3173105 and 0.1586553). Lab 1, Part D prints \(1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\), and the difference between the two tails is the probability of the event that the path reaches 1 and ends below 1.
          
          
- 
“A grid maximum can overstate the running maximum, so the grid tail sits above the closed form.”
            
Proposition 2.2(a) gives \(M^{(N)}_H\le M_H\) on every continuous path, so Corollary 2.3 bounds the grid tail above by the running tail. Assuming A3 and A4, Lab 1, Part B prints grid estimates of 0.2275 at 4 steps and 0.3025 at 256 steps, both below the closed form 0.3173105 (H2 of Theorem 1.3, to seven decimals).
          
          
- 
“Any process with normal one-time marginals has the closed-form tail of \(M_H\).”
            
The tail identity depends on the whole path. Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6 needs continuous paths, and Theorem 1.3 needs Assumptions A3 and A4. The process \(\tilde B\) of Lesson 1, Section 4, has the same one-time laws as \(B\) and discontinuous paths, and this page asserts no tail formula for it.
          
        
      
      
        
## 5Problem set
        
Every problem has a full worked solution. Attempt each one before opening it. Problems marked E use the output of Lab 1. Problems that use the closed form assume A3 and A4.
        
          
## Mechanics
          
            M1 · The tail at a larger horizon
Let \(H=4\) and \(a=2\). Compute \(\Prob(M_4\ge 2)\), assuming A3 and A4.
            
Solution
              
Here \(a/\sqrt H=2/\sqrt 4=1\), so Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_4\ge 2)=2(1-\Phi(1))\). Part D of Lab 1 prints \(1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\), so the value is \(2(1-\Phi(1))=0.3173105079\) (unrounded 0.31731050786; \(2\times 0.1586552539=0.3173105078\) on the printed digits). The same value is \(\operatorname{erfc}(2/\sqrt 8)=\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt 2)\).
            
          
          
            M2 · Scaling
Assuming A3 and A4, show that \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) depends on \((a,H)\) only through \(a/\sqrt H\), and state the value of \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) when \(a/\sqrt H=1\).
            
Solution
              
By Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), a function of \(a/\sqrt H\) alone. When \(a/\sqrt H=1\), the value is \(2(1-\Phi(1))=0.3173105079\), by the same calculation as Problem M1.
            
          
          
            M3 · A one-step grid
Let \(H=1\). Compute \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_1\ge 1)\) for the one-step grid, and compare it with \(\Prob(M_1\ge 1)\), assuming A3 and A4.
            
Solution
              
By Remark 2.4, \(\Prob(M^{(1)}_1\ge 1)=\Prob(B_1\ge 1)=1-\Phi(1)=0.1586552539\). By Theorem 1.3, \(\Prob(M_1\ge 1)=0.3173105079\). The grid value is exactly half the continuous value.
            
          
        
        
          
## Proof
          
            P1 · The distribution function of the running maximum
Prove Corollary 1.5(a): for \(m\ge 0\), \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). State each assumption used.
            
Solution
              
Let \(m>0\). The sets \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase as \(b\) decreases to \(m\), and their union over \(b>m\) is \(\{M_H>m\}\). By continuity of probability from below, \(\Prob(M_H>m)=\lim_{b\downarrow m}\Prob(M_H\ge b)\). Theorem 1.3 gives \(\Prob(M_H\ge b)=2(1-\Phi(b/\sqrt H))\) for \(b>0\), and \(\Phi\) is continuous, so the limit is \(2(1-\Phi(m/\sqrt H))\). Then \(\Prob(M_H\le m)=1-\Prob(M_H>m)=2\Phi(m/\sqrt H)-1\). The case \(m=0\) follows by the same limit. Assumptions used: H1, H2 and H3 of Theorem 1.3, with H3 applied at each level \(b>0\) and \(H>0\) fixed.
            
          
          
            P2 · The integral of the normal tail
Prove that \(\int_0^\infty(1-\Phi(u))\,du=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).
            
Solution
              
Integrating by parts on \([0,R]\) with \(\varphi'(u)=-u\varphi(u)\) gives \(\int_0^R(1-\Phi(u))\,du=R(1-\Phi(R))+\varphi(0)-\varphi(R)\). The tail satisfies \(1-\Phi(R)=\int_R^\infty\varphi(v)\,dv\le\varphi(R)/R\), because \(v/R\ge 1\) on \([R,\infty)\). So \(R(1-\Phi(R))\le\varphi(R)\to 0\). Letting \(R\to\infty\) leaves \(\varphi(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).
            
          
          
            P3 · The grid tail is nested
Let \(N\) divide \(N'\). Prove that \(\Prob(M^{(N)}_H\ge a)\le\Prob(M^{(N')}_H\ge a)\) for every \(a\), assuming H1 of Proposition 2.2.
            
Solution
              
By Proposition 2.2(b), \(M^{(N)}_H\le M^{(N')}_H\) on every outcome with a continuous path. So \(\{M^{(N)}_H\ge a\}\subseteq\{M^{(N')}_H\ge a\}\), and monotonicity of probability gives the inequality. The proof uses the subset relation between the two events and monotonicity of probability.
            
          
        
        
          
## Empirical
          
            E1 · Reading the grid counts
Lab 1, Part B prints the proportions 0.2275 at 4 steps and 0.3025 at 256 steps. Convert them to counts out of 400, and state what the counts show.
            
Solution
              
The counts are \(0.2275\times 400=91\) and \(0.3025\times 400=121\). Both are integers, as they must be for 400 paths. In this sample the count is 91 at 4 steps and 121 at 256 steps, and it does not decrease across the four grids. Proposition 2.2 requires the counts on nested grids to be nondecreasing for the same paths, and this sample satisfies that. The sample does not show that the difference from 0.3173105 tends to zero, because it uses one seed.
            
          
          
            E2 · Recomputing an exact tail
Lab 1, Part A prints \(\Prob(M_{16}\ge 4)=0.332306\). Recompute it from the walk counts.
            
Solution
              
The walk identity gives the count \(\#\{S_{16}\ge 4\}+\#\{S_{16}>4\}\). Here \(S_{16}=2u-16\), where \(u\) is the number of up steps, so \(S_{16}\ge 4\) means \(u\ge 10\), and \(S_{16}>4\) means \(u\ge 11\). The binomial coefficients give \(\#\{S_{16}\ge 4\}=8008+4368+1820+560+120+16+1=14893\) and \(\#\{S_{16}>4\}=6885\). The total is 21778, and \(21778/2^{16}=0.332306\).
            
          
          
            E3 · The printed standard error
Lab 1, Part B prints a standard error of 0.0230 for the 256-step grid, from 121 of 400 paths. Show that the sample standard error with divisor \(n-1\) of a proportion \(k/n\) is \(\sqrt{k(n-k)/(n^2(n-1))}\), and check the printed value.
            
Solution
              
The indicators have sample mean \(p=k/n\). Their sum of squared deviations is \(k(1-p)^2+(n-k)p^2=k(n-k)/n\), so the sample variance with divisor \(n-1\) is \(k(n-k)/(n(n-1))\). The standard error is that variance divided by \(n\), under the square root: \(\sqrt{k(n-k)/(n^2(n-1))}\). With \(k=121\) and \(n=400\), this is \(\sqrt{33759/63840000}=0.0230\), which matches the printed value.
            
          
        
        
          
## Research
          
            R1 · The restriction to positive levels
Show that \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\) for \(a\le 0\), and that the expression \(2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\) exceeds 1 for \(a<0\). Explain why Theorem 1.3 is stated for \(a>0\).
            
Solution
              
For \(a\le 0\), \(M_H\ge 0\ge a\) by Definition 1.1, so \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\). For \(a<0\), \(-a/\sqrt H>0\), so \(\Phi(a/\sqrt H)<\tfrac12\) by the evenness of the normal density. Then \(2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))>1\), which cannot be a probability. Since \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=1\) for \(a\le 0\), the closed form does not give \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)\) for \(a<0\), and Theorem 1.3 is stated for \(a>0\).
            
          
          
            R2 · No atoms at positive levels
For fixed \(H>0\) and \(a>0\), show that \(\Prob(M_H=a)=0\), assuming A3 and A4.
            
Solution
              
The events \(\{M_H\ge b\}\) increase as \(b\downarrow a\), with union \(\{M_H>a\}\). By continuity of probability from below, \(\Prob(M_H>a)=\lim_{b\downarrow a}\Prob(M_H\ge b)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\), using Theorem 1.3 and the continuity of \(\Phi\). Also \(\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\). Subtracting, \(\Prob(M_H=a)=\Prob(M_H\ge a)-\Prob(M_H>a)=0\).
            
          
          
            R3 · Short horizons
For fixed \(a>0\), find \(\lim_{H\downarrow 0}\Prob(\tau_a\le H)\), where \(\tau_a\) is the first time the path reaches \(a\), and say what the limit means for the running maximum over short times, assuming A3 and A4.
            
Solution
              
By Lemma 2.1 of Lesson 6, \(\{\tau_a\le H\}=\{M_H\ge a\}\) on continuous paths, so \(\Prob(\tau_a\le H)=\Prob(M_H\ge a)=2(1-\Phi(a/\sqrt H))\). As \(H\downarrow 0\), \(a/\sqrt H\to\infty\), so \(\Phi(a/\sqrt H)\to 1\) and the probability tends to 0 (Remark 1.4). Over short horizons the running maximum has a vanishing chance of reaching a given positive level \(a\).
