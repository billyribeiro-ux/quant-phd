00020 A price that traders use to forecast the price itself makes the forecast and the price depend on each other. This lesson states a linear-normal one-period model in which that loop closes exactly. The rational-expectations equilibrium (Definition 1.2) is a fixed point of a map on the forecast's coefficients (Proposition 2.2 in Section 2). The fixed point has a closed form. Iterating the map converges from every start only when \(\varsigma < \beta\), that is, when the supply response is smaller than the demand slope (Proposition 3.2; Definition 1.1 defines both). The lab checks the fixed point and the error ratios (Lemma 3.1) exactly. The model is a stated special case, and the lesson makes no claim about any market.
00021 1A linear-normal model with a public signal
00022 Assumptions
00023 A1 (parameters). The constants \(\alpha\) and \(\delta\) are real, \(\beta > 0\), and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).
00024 A2 (affine forecasts). The forecast of the price given the
00025 public signal
00026 \(z\), which every trader observes, is restricted to the family of
00027 affine
00028 functions \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\), with \(c_0, c_1 \in \R\). An affine function is a constant plus a multiple of \(z\).
00029 A3 (independent normal shocks). The public signal \(z\), the demand shock \(u\) and the supply shock \(\upsilon\) are independent. Each has mean zero: \(z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_z^2)\) with \(\sigma_z^2 > 0\), \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\) and \(\upsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\upsilon^2)\) with \(\sigma_u^2, \sigma_\upsilon^2 \ge 0\).
00030 Definition 1.1 (the model's market)
00031 Demand for the risky asset is \(d(p) = \alpha - \beta p + u\), where \(p\) is the price. The public signal \(z\) is observed by every trader. Supply is produced in advance on the basis of the forecast \(m(z)\) of the price, so the total supply is \(q = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\). The market clears when \(d(p) = q\). Given a forecast function \(m\), the clearing price is the unique solution in \(p\) of \(\alpha - \beta p + u = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\), namely
00032 \[ p_m = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\, m(z) - \delta z + u - \upsilon}{\beta}. \]
00033 The solution is unique because \(\beta > 0\). The price \(p_m\) is a function of \((z, u, \upsilon)\). The parameter \(\varsigma\) is the supply response to the forecast, and \(\delta\) is the supply response to the public signal.
00034 Definition 1.2 (rational-expectations equilibrium)
00035 Under A1 to A3, a forecast \(m\) is a
00036 rational-expectations equilibrium
00037 when the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price the market produces: \(m(z) = \E[\,p_m \mid z\,]\) for every real \(z\). The conditional expectation of \(p_m\) given \(z\) is a function of \(z\) alone. Lemma 2.1 computes it for the affine forecasts that Section 2 uses.
00038 2The conditional law of the price and the fixed point
00039 Lemma 2.1 (the forecast of the market price)
00040 Under A1 to A3, for every affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\),
00041 \[ \E[\,p_m \mid z\,] = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\,(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z}{\beta}, \]
00042 and the conditional law of \(p_m\) given \(z\) is \(\Normal\bigl(\E[p_m \mid z],\ (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\bigr)\).
00043 Proof. Write \(p_m = g(z) + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), where \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\) is a function of \(z\) alone. A function of the conditioning variable is known given that variable, so \(\E[g(z) \mid z] = g(z)\). The variable \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\) by A3, so its conditional expectation given \(z\) equals its unconditional expectation, which is zero. This gives the formula. Next, \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\), so its conditional variance is its unconditional variance, \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). Adding the function \(g(z)\), which is fixed given \(z\), shifts the mean and leaves the variance unchanged. Since \(u\) and \(\upsilon\) are normal and independent of \(z\), the conditional law is normal. \(\square\)
00044 Proposition 2.2 (the fixed point of the coefficient map)
00045 Under A1 to A3, an affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\) is a rational-expectations equilibrium if and only if \((c_0, c_1)\) is a fixed point of the coefficient map
00046 \[ \Psi(c_0, c_1) = \Bigl(\frac{\alpha - \varsigma c_0}{\beta},\ -\frac{\varsigma c_1 + \delta}{\beta}\Bigr). \]
00047 The unique fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\) and \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), and the equilibrium price is \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\).
00048 Proof. By Lemma 2.1, the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price exactly when \(c_0 + c_1 z = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0 - \varsigma c_1 z - \delta z)/\beta\) for every real \(z\). Two affine functions agree for every \(z\) exactly when their constant terms and their slopes agree, so this is the pair of equations \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\) and \(\beta c_1 = -(\varsigma c_1 + \delta)\). These say \(\Psi(c_0, c_1) = (c_0, c_1)\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\) by A1, each equation has exactly one solution, namely \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\). Substituting the fixed point into \(p_m\) gives \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). \(\square\)
00049 Corollary 2.3 (the equilibrium forecast and its error)
00050 Under A1 to A3, at the rational-expectations equilibrium of Proposition 2.2, \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), the forecast error \(p - \E[p \mid z]\) has conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\), and the unconditional variance of \(p\) is \((c_1^\ast)^2 \sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).
00051 Proof. The first two statements follow from Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.1, since \(p - \E[p \mid z] = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). For the third, \(\Var(p) = (c_1^\ast)^2 \Var(z) + \Var\bigl((u - \upsilon)/\beta\bigr)\), because \(z\) and \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) are independent and so uncorrelated, and the constant \(c_0^\ast\) adds nothing to the variance. The variance of \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). \(\square\)
00052 3Iterating the coefficient map
00053 Lemma 3.1 (the error recursion)
00054 Under A1 (so that \(\beta > 0\)), let \(c^\ast = (c_0^\ast, c_1^\ast)\) be the fixed point of Proposition 2.2, and let \(e_k = c_k - c^\ast\) be the error of the iterate \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\). Then each component of the error satisfies \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)\, e_k\) exactly, and therefore \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).
00055 Proof. Since \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\), the first component gives \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)\). Since \(c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta\), the second component gives \(c_{1,k+1} - c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_{1,k} + \delta)/\beta + (\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{1,k} - c_1^\ast)\). Induction gives the power formula. \(\square\)
00056 Proposition 3.2 (when iteration converges)
00057 Under A1 to A3, if \(\varsigma < \beta\), the iterates \(c_k\) converge to \(c^\ast\) from every starting point. If \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) and the starting point differs from \(c^\ast\) in some component, the error in that component does not converge to zero.
00058 Proof. By Lemma 3.1 the error in each component has absolute value \((\varsigma/\beta)^k\) times its starting absolute value. If \(\varsigma < \beta\), then \(\varsigma/\beta < 1\), so \((\varsigma/\beta)^k \to 0\). If \(\varsigma = \beta\), the absolute error stays constant, and it is nonzero when the starting error is nonzero. If \(\varsigma > \beta\), it grows. In both cases the error does not converge to zero. \(\square\)
00059 Proposition 3.3 (a damped step can reach the fixed point at once)
00060 Under A1 to A3, consider the damped step \(c \mapsto (1 - \nu) c + \nu\, \Psi(c)\) with weight \(\nu\). Its error factor in each component is \(1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\). With \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), the factor is zero, so one damped step from any starting point gives \(c^\ast\).
00061 Proof. Subtracting \(c^\ast\) from the damped step and using \(\Psi(c) - c^\ast = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c - c^\ast)\) from Lemma 3.1 gives an error \(\bigl[(1 - \nu) - \nu\varsigma/\beta\bigr](c - c^\ast) = \bigl[1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\bigr](c - c^\ast)\). For \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), \(\nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta) = \frac{\beta}{\beta + \varsigma}\cdot\frac{\beta + \varsigma}{\beta} = 1\), so the factor is zero. \(\square\)
00062 Lab 1 · The fixed point and the iteration, in exact arithmetic
00063 At the base parameters \(\alpha = 10\), \(\beta = 2\), \(\varsigma = 1/2\), \(\delta = 3/4\), the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 4\) and \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the map's residual at it is zero. Iterating from \((0, 0)\), each error ratio is \(-1/4\). With \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\), the ratio is \(-1\) and the \(c_0\) path alternates between 0 and 5. With \(\varsigma = 3\), the ratio is \(-3/2\) and the error grows. A damped step with weight \(4/5\) reaches the fixed point in one step, and the fixed-point identity holds at each of five points of \(z\).
00064 """E4 L05 lab 1: the coefficient fixed point of a linear rational-expectations model, solved exactly and iterated.
00065 Inputs are illustrative numbers, not data. The equilibrium price is affine in a public signal z, p = c0 + c1 z + noise,
00066 and the traders' forecast of the price given z is the affine function m(z) = c0 + c1 z. The coefficient map sends a
00067 forecast (c0, c1) to the forecast the market produces:
00068 c0' = (alpha - varsigma * c0) / beta, c1' = -(varsigma * c1 + delta) / beta.
00069 A rational-expectations equilibrium in this family is a fixed point of the map. The fixed point is
00070 c0* = alpha / (beta + varsigma) and c1* = -delta / (beta + varsigma), which is unique in the family because
00071 beta + varsigma > 0. The error e_k = c_k - c* obeys e_{k+1} = -(varsigma / beta) * e_k exactly, so iteration converges
00072 from any start when varsigma < beta and fails to converge when varsigma >= beta and the start is off the fixed point.
00073 Each parameter set that Parts B to D iterate is checked by check_params before the iteration runs: beta must be
00074 positive and varsigma nonnegative, otherwise the lab raises ValueError.
00075 Part A: the closed-form fixed point, and the residual of the map at it, which is exactly zero.
00076 Part B: iteration from (0, 0) at the base parameters; the ratio of successive errors is -varsigma / beta at every step.
00077 Part C: the same ratio when varsigma equals beta: the iteration does not converge.
00078 Part D: the same ratio when varsigma exceeds beta: the error grows.
00079 Part E: a damped step with weight beta / (beta + varsigma) reaches the fixed point in one step.
00080 Part F: the fixed-point identity m(z) = (alpha - varsigma m(z) - delta z) / beta on a five-point grid of z, exact.
00081 All values are exact Fractions, and no random draws are used, so every run prints the same lines.
00082 """
00083 from __future__ import annotations
00084 from fractions import Fraction
00085 Coeffs = tuple[Fraction, Fraction]
00086 Params = tuple[Fraction, Fraction, Fraction, Fraction]
00087 BASE: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(1, 2), Fraction(3, 4))
00088 EQUAL: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(2), Fraction(3, 4))
00089 LARGE: Params = (Fraction(10), Fraction(2), Fraction(3), Fraction(3, 4))
00090 Z_GRID: tuple[Fraction, ...] = tuple(Fraction(z) for z in (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2))
00091 def check_params(params: Params) -> None:
00092 """Raise ValueError unless beta is positive and varsigma is nonnegative."""
00093 _, beta, varsigma, _ = params
00094 if beta <= 0:
00095 raise ValueError("demand slope beta must be positive")
00096 if varsigma < 0:
00097 raise ValueError("supply response varsigma must be nonnegative")
00098 def coefficient_map(c: Coeffs, params: Params) -> Coeffs:
00099 """One step of the map from a forecast (c0, c1) to the forecast the market produces, exact in Fractions."""
00100 alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = params
00101 c0, c1 = c
00102 return ((alpha - varsigma * c0) / beta, -(varsigma * c1 + delta) / beta)
00103 def fixed_point(params: Params) -> Coeffs:
00104 """The closed-form fixed point (alpha / (beta + varsigma), -delta / (beta + varsigma))."""
00105 alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = params
00106 return (alpha / (beta + varsigma), -delta / (beta + varsigma))
00107 def iterate(start: Coeffs, params: Params, steps: int) -> list[Coeffs]:
00108 """The path of the map from the start: the start, then steps further iterates."""
00109 path = [start]
00110 for _ in range(steps):
00111 path.append(coefficient_map(path[-1], params))
00112 return path
00113 def error_ratios(path: list[Coeffs], target: Coeffs, index: int) -> list[str]:
00114 """The ratio of successive errors in coefficient index (0 for c0, 1 for c1), printed exactly.
00115 A zero error is reported as such, because the ratio is then undefined.
00116 """
00117 errors = [c[index] - target[index] for c in path]
00118 out: list[str] = []
00119 for k in range(len(errors) - 1):
00120 if errors[k] == 0:
00121 out.append("zero-error")
00122 else:
00123 out.append(str(errors[k + 1] / errors[k]))
00124 return out
00125 def show(label: str, params: Params, steps: int) -> None:
00126 """Print the fixed point, the iterates from (0, 0) and the exact ratio of successive errors in each coefficient."""
00127 check_params(params)
00128 target = fixed_point(params)
00129 path = iterate((Fraction(0), Fraction(0)), params, steps)
00130 print(f"{label} fixed_point c0 {target[0]} c1 {target[1]}")
00131 print(f"{label} c0_path {[str(c[0]) for c in path]}")
00132 print(f"{label} c1_path {[str(c[1]) for c in path]}")
00133 print(f"{label} c0_error_ratios {error_ratios(path, target, 0)}")
00134 print(f"{label} c1_error_ratios {error_ratios(path, target, 1)}")
00135 def main() -> None:
00136 """Print Parts A to F. Every value is an exact Fraction, so the output is reproducible."""
00137 alpha, beta, varsigma, delta = BASE
00138 print("Part A: closed-form fixed point and residual of the map")
00139 target = fixed_point(BASE)
00140 residual = coefficient_map(target, BASE)
00141 print(f"base alpha {alpha} beta {beta} varsigma {varsigma} delta {delta}")
00142 print(f"fixed_point c0 {target[0]} c1 {target[1]}")
00143 print(f"residual_c0 {residual[0] - target[0]} residual_c1 {residual[1] - target[1]}")
00144 print("Part B: iteration at the base parameters from (0, 0)")
00145 show("base", BASE, 8)
00146 print("Part C: varsigma equal to beta, the iteration does not converge")
00147 show("equal", EQUAL, 6)
00148 print("Part D: varsigma above beta, the error grows")
00149 show("large", LARGE, 6)
00150 print("Part E: damped step with weight beta / (beta + varsigma) at the base parameters")
00151 weight = beta / (beta + varsigma)
00152 start = (Fraction(0), Fraction(0))
00153 image = coefficient_map(start, BASE)
00154 damped = ((1 - weight) * start[0] + weight * image[0], (1 - weight) * start[1] + weight * image[1])
00155 print(f"weight {weight} damped_step c0 {damped[0]} c1 {damped[1]} equals_fixed_point {damped == target}")

00157 c0, c1 = target
00158 checks: list[bool] = []

00191 4Notes on the model
00192 Remark 4.1 (what Proposition 2.2 covers)
00193 The fixed point is found within the affine family of A2. Proposition 2.2 shows that this family contains exactly one rational-expectations equilibrium. It does not show that no non-affine forecast is an equilibrium of the model, because the argument restricts the search to affine forecasts.
00194 5Where the canon breaks
00195 The equilibrium forecast is affine by construction, since A2 restricts the search to affine forecasts. The result is therefore a property of this model, not of rational expectations in general. Three limits apply.
00196 The search is restricted to affine forecasts. A2 is an assumption, not a consequence of rationality. Remark 4.1 records what the restriction does and does not establish.
00197 Existence in the family does not give convergence of naive iteration. Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for all \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\). Proposition 3.2 shows that repeated application of \(\Psi\) converges from every starting point only when \(\varsigma < \beta\). Lab 1 Parts C and D show the two failures. Proposition 3.3 gives a damped step that avoids the failure in this affine case, which decouples into two one-dimensional affine maps.
00198 The forecasts are correct by construction, inside the model. Definition 1.2 takes the traders to know the price rule, the distribution of the shocks and the supply response, because the forecast is the conditional expectation of the price that the model produces. Whether real forecasts have this property is a separate empirical question. This lesson makes no claim about it.
00199 The phrase rational expectations appears in the title of Muth (1961). Only the title is used from that work. The model here is a stated special case, and this lesson uses no result from that paper.
00200 6Misconceptions
00201 “A rational forecast of the price is exact.”
00202 Corollary 2.3 gives a conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\) for the forecast error. This is zero only when both shocks have zero variance, and A3 allows positive variances. The forecast is unbiased, in the sense that its error has mean zero given \(z\), but it is not exact.
00203 “A rational-expectations equilibrium exists only when repeated iteration of the forecast map converges.”
00204 Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for every \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\), with \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Proposition 3.2 is about convergence of one particular procedure. Lab 1 Part C has \(\varsigma = \beta\): the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 5/2\), but the iterates alternate between 0 and 5 and never reach it.
00205 “The equilibrium forecast of the price is the unconditional mean of the price.”
00206 The forecast is \(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), which varies with \(z\) whenever \(\delta \ne 0\), because \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\). At the base parameters of Lab 1 the slope is \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the printed fixed point shows it. The unconditional mean is \(c_0^\ast\), because \(\E z = \E u = \E\upsilon = 0\) by A3 gives \(\E p = c_0^\ast\). It is a different object from the forecast.

00209 Mechanics
00210 M1 · The fixed point at new parameters
00211 Let \(\alpha = 12\), \(\beta = 3\), \(\varsigma = 1\) and \(\delta = 2\). Find \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\), and check that \(\Psi\) maps the fixed point to itself.
00212 Solution
00213 Here \(\beta + \varsigma = 4\), so \(c_0^\ast = 12/4 = 3\) and \(c_1^\ast = -2/4 = -1/2\). Check the first component: \((12 - 1\cdot 3)/3 = 9/3 = 3\). Check the second: \(-(1\cdot(-1/2) + 2)/3 = -(3/2)/3 = -1/2\). Both components are unchanged, so \(\Psi(c^\ast) = c^\ast\).
00214 M2 · One step of the error recursion
00215 With the parameters of M1, start the iteration at \((0, 0)\). Compute the error ratio \(e_1/e_0\) in each component and compare it with \(-\varsigma/\beta\).
00216 Solution
00217 The first iterate is \(\Psi(0,0) = (12/3,\ -2/3) = (4, -2/3)\). The starting error in \(c_0\) is \(0 - 3 = -3\), and the next error is \(4 - 3 = 1\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). In \(c_1\) the starting error is \(0 - (-1/2) = 1/2\), and the next error is \(-2/3 - (-1/2) = -1/6\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). Both equal \(-\varsigma/\beta = -1/3\), as Lemma 3.1 states.
00218 M3 · The conditional law of the price
00219 Let \(\beta = 2\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 4\) and \(\sigma_\upsilon^2 = 1\). Find the conditional variance of the price given \(z\) at the equilibrium of Proposition 2.2.
00220 Solution
00221 By Corollary 2.3 the conditional variance is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (4 + 1)/4 = 5/4\). It does not depend on \(z\), and the conditional law is \(\Normal\bigl(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z,\ 5/4\bigr)\).
00222 Proof
00223 P1 · The conditional expectation in Lemma 2.1
00224 Show that \(\E[\,(u - \upsilon)/\beta \mid z\,] = 0\) under A3, and deduce the formula for \(\E[p_m \mid z]\) in Lemma 2.1.
00225 Solution
00226 Under A3 the vector \((u, \upsilon)\) is independent of \(z\). For an integrable function \(h\) of an independent variable, \(\E[h(u,\upsilon) \mid z] = \E[h(u,\upsilon)]\). With \(h = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), this gives \(\E[(u-\upsilon)/\beta] = (\E u - \E\upsilon)/\beta = 0\). Since \(p_m = g(z) + (u-\upsilon)/\beta\) with \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\), linearity gives \(\E[p_m \mid z] = g(z) + 0\), which is the formula of Lemma 2.1.
00227 P2 · Uniqueness in the affine family
00228 Show that Proposition 2.2 has exactly one fixed point in the affine family, for every \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).
00229 Solution
00230 The first component of the fixed-point condition is \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\), equivalently \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma \ge \beta > 0\), this has exactly one solution \(c_0 = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\). The second is \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which likewise has exactly one solution. So the pair is unique.
00231 P3 · The error recursion
00232 Prove Lemma 3.1 for the first component, starting from \(c_{0,k+1} = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta\) and the identity \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\).
00233 Solution
00234 Subtract the two identities: \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = \bigl[(\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k}) - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)\bigr]/\beta = -\varsigma(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)/\beta\). So \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)e_k\). Induction on \(k\) gives \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).
00235 Empirical
00236 E1 · When the path is within 0.01 of the fixed point
00237 Using Lab 1 Part B at the base parameters, find the first iterate of \(c_0\) that lies within 0.01 of \(c_0^\ast = 4\), and check it against the printed path.
00238 Solution
00239 By Lemma 3.1 the error after \(k\) steps is \(e_k = (-1/4)^k(-4)\), so \(|e_k| = 4/4^k\). We need \(4/4^k < 1/100\), which requires \(4^k > 400\), so \(k \ge 5\). At \(k = 4\), \(|e_4| = 4/256 = 1/64 \approx 0.0156\), which is too large. At \(k = 5\), \(|e_5| = 4/1024 = 1/256 \approx 0.0039\). The printed sixth entry of the \(c_0\) path is \(1025/256\), and \(1025/256 - 4 = 1/256\), which agrees.
00240 E2 · Why the \(\varsigma = \beta\) path does not settle
00241 Lab 1 Part C prints a \(c_0\) path that alternates between 0 and 5 at \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\). Explain the alternation from the error ratio, and state the starting point at which the path would stay put.
00242 Solution
00243 The printed ratio of successive errors in \(c_0\) is \(-1\), which is \(-\varsigma/\beta\) at \(\varsigma = \beta\). Each step flips the sign of the error and keeps its size, so the iterates swap between two values symmetric about the fixed point \(5/2\). The \(c_0\) path stays put at \(c_0 = 5/2\): then \(\Psi(5/2) = (10 - 2\cdot 5/2)/2 = 5/2\), so the starting error is zero, and Lemma 3.1 keeps it at zero.
00244 E3 · Growth of the error at \(\varsigma = 3\)
00245 At \(\varsigma = 3\) and \(\beta = 2\), Lab 1 Part D prints a \(c_0\) path starting \(0, 5, -5/2, 35/4\). Verify the error ratio from the path, and compute the error \(e_3\).
00246 Solution
00247 Here \(c_0^\ast = 10/(2 + 3) = 2\). The errors are \(e_0 = -2\), \(e_1 = 3\), \(e_2 = -9/2\) and \(e_3 = 35/4 - 2 = 27/4\). Each ratio is \(-3/2\), which equals \(-\varsigma/\beta = -3/2\) by Lemma 3.1. The error grows by a factor of \(3/2\) and alternates in sign at each step.
00248 Research
00249 R1 · The damped weight at \(\varsigma = \beta\)
00250 At \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\) and \(\alpha = 10\), find the damping weight of Proposition 3.3, and show that one damped step from \((0,0)\) gives the fixed point in the first component.
00251 Solution
00252 The weight is \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma) = 2/4 = 1/2\). The first entry of \(\Psi(0,0)\) is \(10/2 = 5\). The damped step gives \((1 - 1/2)\cdot 0 + (1/2)\cdot 5 = 5/2\), which is \(c_0^\ast = 10/4 = 5/2\).
00253 R2 · The price with no supply response to the signal
00254 Let \(\delta = 0\). Show that the equilibrium forecast \(\E[p \mid z]\) does not depend on \(z\), and find the unconditional variance of the price.
00255 Solution
00256 With \(\delta = 0\), \(c_1^\ast = -0/(\beta + \varsigma) = 0\), so \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast\) for every \(z\), which does not depend on \(z\). By Corollary 2.3 the unconditional variance is \((c_1^\ast)^2\sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).
00257 R3 · Existence and convergence are different questions
00258 Explain, in one paragraph and with reference to Propositions 2.2 and 3.2, why a rational-expectations equilibrium can exist in the affine family even when repeated iteration of \(\Psi\) does not converge.
00259 Solution
00260 Proposition 2.2 finds the fixed point from the two linear equations \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\) and \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which have a solution whenever \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Existence therefore needs only the algebra of those equations. Proposition 3.2 concerns a different question, namely whether the particular sequence \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\) reaches the fixed point. With \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) the error in each component does not shrink, so this sequence fails to converge, while the fixed point is still there to be found by other means, such as the damped step of Proposition 3.3.
