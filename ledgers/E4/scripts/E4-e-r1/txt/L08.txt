00020 A trader who pays for information gains nothing when the price already reveals the signal. This lesson derives the equilibrium price of a CARA-normal market (Definition 6.2 of Lesson L06) and the gain from information as a cost, \(G(\lambda)\) (Definition 4.3; Lemma 4.5 for \(G(0)\)). With no supply noise, the price is a function of the signal, the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)), and no informed fraction \(\lambda\) (Assumption A3) is an equilibrium informed fraction (Definition 4.6) at a cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\) (Proposition 5.1(b)). With noise, the price stays partly informative, and equilibria exist outside \((G(1), G(0))\). Inside that interval, existence uses Assumption A4 (Section 5). Only Proposition 5.2(c) and Section 5.1's bisection use A4.
00021 1The model
00022 A risky asset pays \(v\) in cash, and a riskless asset has gross return \(1\). The model has three assumptions, A1 to A3. Each is numbered, and each result below states the assumptions it uses. Section 5 adds Assumption A4, which Proposition 5.2(c) uses for existence and which justifies the bisection of Section 5.1.
00023 Assumption A1 (preferences)
00024 Each trader has constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) utility (Definition 6.2 of Lesson L06), \(U(w) = -\exp(-a w)\) with \(a > 0\), over terminal wealth \(w\). Each trader starts with wealth \(W_0\) and no shares of the risky asset. A trader who buys \(x\) shares at price \(p\) and holds the rest in the riskless asset ends with
00025 \[ W = W_0 + x\,(v - p) \quad\text{or}\quad W = W_0 - c + x\,(v - p), \]
00026 where the second form applies to an informed trader, who pays the cost \(c \ge 0\) in wealth. Since \(U'(w) = a\exp(-aw)\) and \(U''(w) = -a^2\exp(-aw)\), the Arrow–Pratt coefficient of absolute risk aversion \(-U''/U'\) equals \(a\) at every \(w\).
00027 Assumption A2 (normal information and noise)
00028 The random variables \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\) are independent, with \(v \sim \Normal(\bar v, \sigma_v^2)\), \(\varepsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\). Here \(\sigma_v^2 > 0\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 \ge 0\). The signal of Lesson L06 is \(s = v + \varepsilon\). The variable \(u\) is the noise-trader supply of Definition 6.1 of Lesson L06: the net number of shares of the risky asset that noise traders bring to the market. Noise traders are not modelled as optimisers. Their supply \(u\) is an exogenous normal shock.
00029 Assumption A3 (price taking and market clearing)
00030 A continuum of traders takes the price \(p\) as given. A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) of them is informed, and each informed trader pays \(c\): each observes the signal \(s\) and the price \(p\). Assumption A2 of Lesson L06 takes \(\lambda\) in \((0, 1)\) as given; this lesson allows the endpoints and asks which fraction is an equilibrium (Definition 4.6). The rest are uninformed: each observes only \(p\). Let \(x_I\) and \(x_U\) be the demands of an informed and an uninformed trader. Market clearing requires
00031 \[ \lambda\, x_I + (1 - \lambda)\, x_U = u \qquad\text{in every state.} \]
00032 Assumption A3 is price taking in the sense of the competitive equilibrium concept linked in the header: no single trader's demand moves the price. The symbols used below are local to this lesson. Write \(\tau_v = 1/\sigma_v^2\) and \(\tau_\varepsilon = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) for precisions. The measure of price informativeness is the squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p)^2\), defined in Definition 7.2 of Lesson L07.
00033 Example 1.1 (the parameters of Lab 1)
00034 Set \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_v^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\), and \(\bar v = 0\). These values satisfy A1 to A3. The prior mean \(\bar v\) does not enter any gain or any informativeness measure, because Corollary 3.2 and Proposition 4.1 express the gain and the informativeness through the variances, the CARA coefficient \(a\) and the informed fraction \(\lambda\). Lemma 4.5 (Section 4) gives \(G(0)\) at these values, and Lab 1, part B prints \(G(0)\) and \(G(1)\).
00035 2Demands and the value of information
00036 Lemma 2.1 (CARA demand)
00037 Assumptions: A1. Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be an information set such that, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(m, V)\) with \(V > 0\), and the price \(p\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable. Let \(W_0'\) be a constant. The conditional expected utility \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}]\) is maximised over \(x\) at
00038 \[ x^\ast = \frac{m - p}{a V}, \]
00039 and its maximal value is \(-\exp(-a W_0')\,\exp\bigl(-(m - p)^2 / (2V)\bigr)\).
00040 Proof
00041 Given \(\mathcal{I}\), the variable \(-a x (v - p)\) is normal with mean \(-a x (m - p)\) and variance \(a^2 x^2 V\). The moment generating function of a normal variable gives
00042 \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-a x (v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}\bigr] = \exp\bigl(-a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\bigr). \]
00043 Hence \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}] = -\exp(-a W_0')\exp(f(x))\), where \(f(x) = -a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\). The prefactor is negative, so maximising the expected utility means minimising \(f\). The function \(f\) is a quadratic in \(x\) with leading coefficient \(a^2 V / 2 > 0\). Its derivative \(f'(x) = -a(m - p) + a^2 x V\) vanishes only at \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV)\), so \(x^\ast\) is the unique minimiser. Substituting gives \(f(x^\ast) = -a x^\ast (m - p) + a^2 (x^\ast)^2 V / 2 = -(m-p)^2/V + (m-p)^2/(2V) = -(m - p)^2/(2V)\).
00044 Lemma 2.2 (a normal moment)
00045 Assumptions: none beyond the statement. Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma^2 \ge 0\), and let \(V > 0\). Then
00046 \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-Z^2/(2V))\bigr] = \bigl(1 + \sigma^2/V\bigr)^{-1/2}. \]
00047 Proof
00048 If \(\sigma^2 = 0\), then \(Z = 0\) almost surely and both sides equal \(1\). If \(\sigma^2 > 0\), write the density of \(Z\) and combine the exponents:
00049 \[ \int_{\R} (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{x^2}{2V}\Bigr)\,dx = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \int_{\R} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2}\bigl(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1}\bigr)\Bigr)\,dx. \]
00050 The Gaussian integral equals \(\sqrt{2\pi/(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})}\). Multiplying gives \(\bigl(\sigma^2(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})\bigr)^{-1/2} = (1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2}\).
00051 Fact 2.3 (normal conditioning)
00052 Assumptions: \((x, y)\) is jointly normal and \(\Var(y) > 0\). Then \(\E[x \mid y] = \E x + \beta_{xy}\,(y - \E y)\) with \(\beta_{xy} = \Cov(x, y)/\Var(y)\), and \(\Var(x \mid y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\).
00053 Proof
00054 Let \(r = x - \E x - \beta_{xy}(y - \E y)\). Then \(\Cov(r, y) = \Cov(x, y) - \beta_{xy}\Var(y) = 0\), so \(r\) and \(y\) are uncorrelated. Since \((r, y)\) is jointly normal, they are independent. Therefore \(\E[r \mid y] = \E r = 0\) and \(\Var(r \mid y) = \Var(r)\). Writing \(x = \E x + \beta_{xy}(y - \E y) + r\) gives the conditional mean, and \(\Var(r) = \Var(x) - \beta_{xy}^2\Var(y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\) gives the conditional variance.
00055 Corollary 2.4 (conditional laws of the two groups)
00056 Assumptions: A1 to A3. The posterior of \(v\) (Lesson L06) given an information set is its conditional law given that set. Write \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).
00057 \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s (s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = V_I\).
00058 Let \(\kappa' \in \R\) and let \(\eta = \varepsilon - \kappa' u\), so that \(y = s - \kappa' u = v + \eta\). Let \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa'^2 \sigma_u^2\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\eta^2\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\E[v \mid y] = \bar v + b_y (y - \bar v)\) with \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\), and \(\Var(v \mid y) = V_U\).
00059 Proof
00060 (a) Here \(\Cov(v, s) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\), so Fact 2.3 gives \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + (\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2))(s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).
00061 (b) The pair \((v, y)\) is jointly normal, because \(y\) is a linear combination of \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\). Here \(\Cov(v, y) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(y) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\) by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\) to get \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\Var(v \mid y) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\eta^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
00062 The uninformed trader observes \(p\), not \(y\). For \(\lambda\) in \((0, 1]\), Section 3 shows that \(p\) is an invertible affine function of \(y\) in equilibrium (Proposition 3.1), so \(\sigma(p) = \sigma(y)\), where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is the information generated by the variable in brackets, and Corollary 2.4(b) applies to \(p\) as well.
00063 Lemma 2.5 (what the informed trader knows)
00064 Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) for constants \(\alpha\), \(\beta_p\) and \(\varpi\). Let \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(s, p)\). Then, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\).
00065 Proof
00066 Let \(m(s) = \E[v \mid s]\) and \(r_v = v - m(s)\). By Fact 2.3 applied to \((v, s)\), \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\). It is also independent of \(u\), because \(u\) is independent of \((v, s)\). Since \(u\) is independent of the pair \((r_v, s)\) and \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\), the three variables are mutually independent, so \(r_v\) is independent of \((s, u)\). The variable \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so \(\sigma(s, p) \subseteq \sigma(s, u)\), and \(r_v\) is independent of \(\mathcal{I}\). Since \(s\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable, \(v = m(s) + r_v\) has conditional law \(\Normal(m(s), \Var(r_v))\) given \(\mathcal{I}\), and \(\Var(r_v) = V_I\) by Corollary 2.4(a).
00067 3The equilibrium price
00068 Proposition 3.1 (the linear equilibrium price)
00069 Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2)\), \(A = \lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A)\).
00070 Suppose \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). If \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) with \(\beta_p \ne 0\) clears the market in every state, then \(\varpi/\beta_p = \kappa\), \(\alpha = (1 - \beta)\bar v\) and \(\beta_p = \beta\).
00071 The price \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\) clears the market in every state.
00072 Proof
00073 Let \(\kappa' = \varpi/\beta_p\), and let \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) be defined from \(\kappa'\) as \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\) are defined from \(\kappa\). By Corollary 2.4(b) with \(\kappa'\), the uninformed trader's posterior mean of \(v\) is \(\bar v + b_y'(y - \bar v)\), where \(y = (p - \alpha)/\beta_p = s - \kappa' u\), and their posterior variance is \(V_U' = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta')\). By Corollary 2.4(a) the informed posterior mean is \(\bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\). Multiply the clearing condition of A3 by \(a\), and use \(x_I = (\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)(\E[v \mid s] - p)/a\) and \(x_U = (\tau_v + \tau_\eta')(\E[v \mid y] - p)/a\). Then use \((\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) b_s = \tau_\varepsilon\), \((\tau_v + \tau_\eta') b_y' = \tau_\eta'\), and \(\lambda(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) + (1 - \lambda)(\tau_v + \tau_\eta') = \tau_v + A'\):
00074 \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa' u = a u, \]
00075 where the term in \(\kappa' u\) comes from \(y - \bar v = (s - \bar v) - \kappa' u\). Rearranged,
00076 \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) = \bigl(a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa'\bigr) u. \qquad(\ast) \]
00077 Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\). Both sides are affine in \((s, u)\). The pair \((s, u)\) has a density that is positive on \(\R^2\), because \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So an affine function that vanishes almost surely is identically zero, and the coefficients match:
00078 constant term: \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha) = A' \bar v\), so \(\alpha = \tau_v \bar v/(\tau_v + A') = (1 - \beta')\bar v\);
00079 coefficient of \(s\): \(A' = (\tau_v + A')\beta_p\), so \(\beta_p = \beta'\);
00080 coefficient of \(u\): \((\tau_v + A')\varpi = a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta'\kappa'\). Since \(\varpi = \kappa'\beta_p = \kappa'\beta'\) and \((\tau_v + A')\beta' = A'\), the left side is \(\kappa' A'\). So \(\kappa'(A' - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta') = a\). The bracket is \(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon\), so \(\kappa' = a/(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = \kappa\).
00081 With \(\kappa' = \kappa\), the quantities \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) equal \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\). This proves (a). Each step of the argument is an equivalence, so running it backwards from \(\kappa' = \kappa\) gives (b).
00082 Corollary 3.2 (price informativeness)
00083 Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\) of Proposition 3.1. Then
00084 \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\tau_\eta}{\tau_v + \tau_\eta} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2 \sigma_u^2}. \]
00085 Proof
00086 The price is \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\). Therefore \(\Cov(v, p^\ast) = \beta\sigma_v^2\), and, because \(s\) and \(u\) are independent,
00087 \[ \Var(p^\ast) = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2 = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \]
00088 where \(\sigma_\eta^2 = 1/\tau_\eta = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\). Hence
00089 \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\beta^2\sigma_v^4}{\sigma_v^2 \cdot \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2}. \]
00090 The last expression equals \(\tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) after multiplying by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\), as in Corollary 2.4(b). The factor \(\beta\) cancels because \(\beta > 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\).
00091 Lemma 3.3 (informativeness in the informed fraction)
00092 Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).
00093 \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\).
00094 If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\lambda \mapsto \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\).
00095 If \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), then \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).
00096 Proof
00097 (a) \(\tau_\eta > 0\) and \(\tau_v > 0\), so \(0 < \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) < 1\). (b) As \(\lambda\) rises, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) falls strictly. If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls strictly, so \(\tau_\eta\) rises strictly. The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), since its derivative is \(\tau_v/(\tau_v + t)^2 > 0\). (c) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = \tau_\varepsilon\) for every \(\lambda\), and \(\tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\).
00098 4The gain from information
00099 Proposition 4.1 (the demand gaps and their variances)
00100 Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\). Define the
00101 demand gaps
00102 \(D_I = \E[v \mid s] - p^\ast\) and \(D_U = \E[v \mid p^\ast] - p^\ast\). Then \(\E D_I = \E D_U = 0\), and
00103 \[ \Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2, \qquad \Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \]
00104 where \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
00105 Proof
00106 Using \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\),
00107 \[ D_I = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v) - (1 - \beta)\bar v - \beta s + \kappa\beta u = (b_s - \beta)(s - \bar v) + \kappa\beta u. \]
00108 Its mean is zero. The variables \(s\) and \(u\) are independent, so \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2\Var(s) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2\), with \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\).
00109 For the uninformed, let \(y = (p^\ast - (1 - \beta)\bar v)/\beta\). Then \(y - \bar v = (p^\ast - \bar v)/\beta\), and \(y - \bar v = (v - \bar v) + \eta\), so \(\Var(y - \bar v) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(\E[v \mid p^\ast] = \bar v + b_y(y - \bar v)\). Therefore \(D_U = b_y(y - \bar v) - \beta(y - \bar v) = (b_y - \beta)(y - \bar v)\), which has mean zero and variance \((b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\).
00110 Proposition 4.2 (expected utilities of the two groups)
00111 Assumptions: A1 to A3 and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(EU_I\) be the expected utility of an informed trader who chooses demand optimally given \((s, p^\ast)\), net of the cost \(c\). Let \(EU_U\) be the expected utility of an uninformed trader who chooses demand optimally given \(p^\ast\). Then
00112 \[ EU_I = -\exp\bigl(-a(W_0 - c)\bigr)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I\bigr)^{-1/2}, \qquad EU_U = -\exp(-aW_0)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U\bigr)^{-1/2}, \]
00113 where \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
00114 Proof
00115 For the informed trader, Lemma 2.5 gives the conditional law of \(v\) given \((s, p^\ast)\) as \(\Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\). Lemma 2.1 with \(m = \E[v \mid s]\), \(V = V_I\) and \(W_0' = W_0 - c\) gives the optimal conditional utility \(-\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-(\E[v \mid s] - p^\ast)^2/(2V_I)) = -\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-D_I^2/(2V_I))\). Taking the expectation over \((s, u)\) and applying Lemma 2.2 to \(D_I \sim \Normal(0, \Var(D_I))\) gives the first formula. The uninformed case is the same with \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(p^\ast)\). Here \(\sigma(p^\ast) = \sigma(y)\), so Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(v \mid p^\ast \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid y], V_U)\), and Lemma 2.2 applies to \(D_U\).
00116 Definition 4.3 (gain from information)
00117 For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), the
00118 gain from information
00119 is
00120 \[ G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\!\left(\frac{1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I}{1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U}\right), \]
00121 and the
00122 net benefit
00123 of information at cost \(c\) is \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\). The gain \(G(\lambda)\) is the largest cost at which an informed trader is at least as well off as an uninformed one. Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0)\) for the case with no informed trader.
00124 Proposition 4.4 (the indifference condition)
00125 Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and \(c \ge 0\). Then \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(\lambda)\). Equality holds exactly when \(c = G(\lambda)\).
00126 Proof
00127 Write \(X = (1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)^{-1/2}\) and \(Y = (1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)^{-1/2}\). By Proposition 4.2, \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) reads \(-\exp(-aW_0)\exp(ac)X \ge -\exp(-aW_0)Y\). Dividing by \(-\exp(-aW_0) < 0\) reverses the inequality: \(\exp(ac)X \le Y\). Taking logarithms, \(ac + \ln X \le \ln Y\). Here \(\ln Y - \ln X = \tfrac12\ln\bigl((1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)/(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)\bigr) = aG(\lambda)\). So the condition is \(ac \le aG(\lambda)\), that is \(c \le G(\lambda)\), because \(a > 0\). Equality is the same computation with equalities.
00128 Lemma 4.5 (no informed trader)
00129 Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\lambda = 0\). Then \(p^\ast = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), and the gain from becoming informed is
00130 \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\Bigl(1 + \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}\Bigr). \]
00131 This value does not depend on \(\sigma_u^2\).
00132 Proof
00133 With \(\lambda = 0\), the uninformed trader's posterior of \(v\) is the prior, because \(p\) is a function of \(u\), which is independent of \(v\). Lemma 2.1 gives demand \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2)\). Market clearing \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2) = u\) gives \(p = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\).
00134 A deviating informed trader's posterior mean is \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\), so \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\) and \(\Var(D_I) = b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed gap is \(D_U = \bar v - p = a\sigma_v^2 u\), so \(\Var(D_U) = a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\), and \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\).
00135 Let \(X_0 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) and \(q = \sigma_v^2/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). Then \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = X_0\). Using \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(1/V_I = (\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), we get \(b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/V_I = \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2) = q\). Also \(a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2/V_I = X_0(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = X_0(1 + q)\). The numerator is therefore \(1 + q + X_0(1 + q) = (1 + q)(1 + X_0)\). Hence
00136 \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\frac{(1 + q)(1 + X_0)}{1 + X_0} = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1 + q). \]
00137 The formula of Definition 4.3 also gives \(G(1)\), the advantage of an informed trader over an uninformed deviator at the price that prevails when every trader is informed. The three cases of Definition 4.6 concern the disjoint sets \(\{0\}\), \(\{1\}\) and \((0, 1)\), so they cannot overlap.
00138 Definition 4.6 (equilibrium informed fraction)
00139 A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an
00140 equilibrium informed fraction
00141 at cost \(c\) when no trader wants to switch:
00142 \(\lambda = 0\) and \(G(0) \le c\), so informing is not worth its cost to a deviator;
00143 \(\lambda = 1\) and \(G(1) \ge c\), so staying informed is worth its cost to each informed trader;
00144 \(0 < \lambda < 1\) and \(G(\lambda) = c\), so informed and uninformed traders are equally well off.
00145 Case (iii) is the indifference condition of Proposition 4.4 with equality. Case (ii) follows from Proposition 4.4 at \(\lambda = 1\), which lies in \((0, 1]\): a trader informed at \(\lambda = 1\) is weakly better off than an uninformed trader when \(c \le G(1)\). Case (i) is not an instance of Proposition 4.4 as stated, because \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) is undefined at \(\lambda = 0\). It follows from Lemma 4.5. Its proof gives the demand gaps at \(\lambda = 0\): the deviator's gap \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\), with variance \(\Var(D_I)\) and posterior variance \(V_I\), and the uninformed gap \(D_U = a\sigma_v^2 u\), with \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\). Lemma 2.5 holds with \(\beta_p = 0\), because its proof uses only that \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so the proof of Proposition 4.2 gives the expected utilities at \(\lambda = 0\). The algebra of Proposition 4.4 then gives \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(0)\), so a deviator at \(\lambda = 0\) is weakly worse off when \(c \ge G(0)\).
00146 5The paradox, and solving for the informed fraction
00147 Definition (the Grossman–Stiglitz paradox)
00148 In a model of this kind, the
00149 Grossman–Stiglitz paradox
00150 is the conflict between two facts. A price that reveals the signal gives the uninformed the value of the information, so no one pays for it. A price that reveals nothing leaves the informed a gain that can cover a positive cost. Proposition 5.1 states the conflict for the noiseless case, and Proposition 5.2 states its resolution with noise.
00151 Proposition 5.1 (the noiseless case)
00152 Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\). Then the following hold.
00153 For every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), the price is the affine function \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\) of the signal, and \(G(\lambda) = 0\).
00154 If \(0 < c < G(0)\), then no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
00155 If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
00156 Proof
00157 (a) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 0\), so \(\tau_\eta = \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(A = \tau_\varepsilon\). Then \(\beta = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\), and \(\kappa\beta u = 0\), so \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\). Also \(b_y = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\) by the formula of Corollary 2.4(b), so \(b_y - \beta = 0\). Then \(\Var(D_U) = 0\), and \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - b_s)^2(\cdot) + 0 = 0\). Hence \(G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1) = 0\). Corollary 3.2 gives the informativeness.
00158 (b) If \(0 < c < G(0)\): case (i) fails, because \(G(0) \le c\) is false. Case (ii) fails, because \(G(1) = 0 < c\). Case (iii) fails for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 \ne c\).
00159 (c) If \(c \ge G(0) > 0\), case (i) holds at \(\lambda = 0\). Case (ii) fails at \(\lambda = 1\), because \(G(1) = 0 < c\), and case (iii) fails for \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\).
00160 Part (a) of Proposition 5.1 is the paradox in this model. When the supply of the risky asset carries no noise, the price \(p^\ast\) is a function of \(s\), so an uninformed trader who observes the price has the signal as well. The information then has no value, so no informed fraction is an equilibrium at a cost between zero and \(G(0)\). Part (b) states this as a theorem: for every cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), no fraction works. Part (c) states that at or above \(G(0)\) the only equilibrium is \(\lambda = 0\), with a price that carries no information.
00161 Proposition 5.2 (the noisy case)
00162 Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\).
00163 \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} G(\lambda) = G(0)\).
00164 If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\). If \(c \le G(1)\), then \(\lambda = 1\) is one.
00165 Assuming A4 (Section 5), if \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), there is an equilibrium informed fraction \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) with \(G(\lambda^\ast) = c\). On the interval \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\) and \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\) (Lemma 3.3).
00166 Assumption A4 (bisection condition: continuity and sign change)
00167 Fix the cost \(c\). Let \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) for \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and let \(N(0) = G(0) - c\), with \(G(0)\) from Lemma 4.5.
00168 \(N\) is continuous on \([0, 1]\).
00169 For every bracket \([\ell, h]\) with \(0 \le \ell < h \le 1\), \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) implies that \(N\) has a zero in \([\ell, h]\).
00170 This is assumed here, in both parts. Part (i) holds on \((0, 1]\) by the closed form of Definition 4.3, and at \(0\) it is the limit in Proposition 5.2(a), which this page proves; the assumption is stated so that Proposition 5.2(c) cites one condition. The page does not prove part (ii) from earlier results. Part (ii) is the invariant of the bisection rule in Section 5.1. Lab 1 tests neither part; it prints the gap at the point the bisection returns.
00171 Proof
00172 (a) Write \(\lambda \downarrow 0\). Then \(\kappa \to \infty\), and \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 \to \infty\) because \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) \to 0\), and \(\tau_\eta \kappa \to 0\). The identity \(\kappa\lambda\tau_\varepsilon = a\) gives \(\kappa A = a + (1 - \lambda)\kappa\tau_\eta \to a\). Therefore \(\kappa\beta = \kappa A/(\tau_v + A) \to a/\tau_v = a\sigma_v^2\), and \(\beta \to 0\). Hence \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + (\kappa\beta)^2\sigma_u^2 \to b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\).
00173 For the uninformed term, \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\), and \(\tau_\eta - A = \lambda(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)\). So
00174 \[ (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta) = \frac{\tau_v^2(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)^2}{(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)^2(\tau_v + A)^2}\bigl(\lambda^2\sigma_v^2 + \lambda^2/\tau_\eta\bigr). \]
00175 Now \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \lambda^2\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2 \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\). Also \(\tau_\eta \to 0\) and \(A \to 0\), so the displayed fraction tends to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4 = \tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^2\). The limit of \(\Var(D_U)\) is therefore \(\tau_\varepsilon^2 a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2/\tau_v^2 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), using \(\tau_\varepsilon\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\). Since \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) \to \sigma_v^2\), \(\Var(D_U)/V_U \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\). Substituting these limits into Definition 4.3 gives the value of Lemma 4.5, which is \(G(0)\).
00176 (b) If \(c \ge G(0)\), case (i) of Definition 4.6 holds. If \(c \le G(1)\), case (ii) holds.
00177 (c) By A4(i), \(N\) is continuous on \([0, 1]\), and by (a) its value at \(0\) is the limit of \(G\) at \(0\). If \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), then \(N(0) = G(0) - c > 0 > G(1) - c = N(1)\), and A4(ii) gives a zero \(\lambda^\ast\) of \(N\) in \([0, 1]\). The zero is not \(0\) or \(1\), because \(N(0) \ne 0\) and \(N(1) \ne 0\). So \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) satisfies case (iii). The informativeness statement is Lemma 3.3.
00178 Proposition 5.2 resolves the paradox within this model. With noise, the price reveals the signal only up to the error \(\kappa u\), whose variance \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls as \(\lambda\) rises. The uninformed therefore learn from the price, but imperfectly. At the parameters of Example 1.1 the gain from information is still positive at \(\lambda = 1\), where \(G(1) = \ln(5/3)/4 \approx 0.127706\) (Problem Emp 3); the page does not show that the gain is positive at every \(\lambda\). The price stays partly informative, and the cost restricts the equilibrium informed fraction. Costs strictly between \(G(1)\) and \(G(0)\) give an interior equilibrium under A4. Costs at or above \(G(0)\) make \(\lambda = 0\) an equilibrium informed fraction, and costs at or below \(G(1)\) make \(\lambda = 1\) one. The interior equilibrium is not claimed to be unique. Lab 1 checks monotonicity only on its grid.
00179 5.1 Solving for the informed fraction
00180 The lab solves \(N(\lambda) = 0\) on the interval \((0, 1)\) by bisection. The boundary rule is applied first. If \(c \ge G(0)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 0\). If \(c \le G(1)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 1\). Otherwise the bracket \([0, 1]\) has \(N(0) > 0 > N(1)\), and the bisection keeps the invariant \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) on the bracket \([\ell, h]\). Each step evaluates \(N\) at the midpoint. It replaces \(\ell\) if \(N\) is positive there and \(h\) otherwise, and it stops when \(h - \ell \le 10^{-12}\). By A4(ii) the bracket contains a zero. At an interior root the printed gap is \(N(\lambda^\ast)\) at the reported point, and a gap that is zero to the printed precision is the evidence that the root satisfies case (iii). At a boundary the printed gap is \(G(0) - c\) or \(G(1) - c\), and the boundary rule is checked by the sign of that gap.

00209 def equilibrium(lam: float, var_u: float = VAR_U) -> dict[str, float]:
00210 """Price coefficients and the gain from information at informed fraction lam in (0, 1].
00211 var_u is the variance of the noise-trader supply. The returned dictionary holds beta (the weight of the signal in
00212 the price), kappa (the noise coefficient divided by beta), corr2 (the squared correlation between payoff and price)
00213 and gain (the cost at which an informed and an uninformed trader are equally well off).
00214 """
00215 if not 0.0 < lam <= 1.0:

00217 tau_v = 1.0 / VAR_V
00218 tau_e = 1.0 / VAR_EPS
00219 kappa = CARA * VAR_EPS / lam
00220 tau_eta = 1.0 / (VAR_EPS + kappa * kappa * var_u)
00221 a_w = lam * tau_e + (1.0 - lam) * tau_eta
00222 beta = a_w / (tau_v + a_w)
00223 b_s = tau_e / (tau_v + tau_e)
00224 b_y = tau_eta / (tau_v + tau_eta)
00225 post_s = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_e)
00226 post_u = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_eta)
00227 noise_term = (kappa * beta) ** 2 * var_u
00228 var_informed = (b_s - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + VAR_EPS) + noise_term
00229 var_uninformed = (b_y - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + 1.0 / tau_eta)
00230 gain = math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + var_uninformed / post_u)) / (2.0 * CARA)
00231 return {
00232 "beta": beta,
00233 "kappa": kappa,
00234 "corr2": b_y,
00235 "gain": gain,

00241 post_s = 1.0 / (1.0 / VAR_V + 1.0 / VAR_EPS)
00242 noise = CARA * CARA * VAR_V * VAR_V * var_u
00243 var_informed = b_s * b_s * total + noise
00244 return math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + noise / VAR_V)) / (2.0 * CARA)

00386 6Where the canon breaks
00387 The closed forms need CARA utility and normal variables. Lemma 2.1 uses the normal moment generating function, and Lemma 2.2 uses the normal density. The formulas of Sections 2 to 4 are derived for CARA utility and normal variables. The page makes no claim for other utilities or distributions.
00388 Noise traders are exogenous. The shock \(u\) is a normal supply that does not respond to prices or information. Proposition 5.1(b) gives no equilibrium informed fraction for \(0 < c < G(0)\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\). Proposition 5.2 gives an equilibrium informed fraction for each such cost when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\): \(\lambda = 1\) for \(c \le G(1)\), and, for \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), an interior fraction under A4, which is needed only for those costs. That contrast holds for this specification, in which \(u\) is exogenous. Models in which noise traders optimise are outside this lesson.
00389 Price taking is an assumption. Assumption A3 says no trader moves the price, and Proposition 3.1 uses that assumption. With a finite number of traders the derivation does not apply, and the page does not derive the equilibrium of that game.
00390 The interior root is assumed to exist and is not shown to be unique. Assumption A4(ii) gives a zero of \(N\) for each bracket with a sign change. Lab 1 checks that \(G\) falls on its grid of 999 points, and nothing on the page proves that \(G\) falls on \((0, 1)\) for every parameter choice.
00391 The cost is in wealth. The cost \(c\) reduces terminal wealth by the same amount for every trader and every state. A cost in utility units, or a cost that depends on the signal, is outside the model of Proposition 4.4, which takes the cost as a reduction of terminal wealth.
00392 7Misconceptions
00393 “Noise traders only hurt the informed, so noise makes information less valuable.”
00394 Proposition 5.1(a) shows the reverse. Without noise, \(G(\lambda) = 0\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), because the price reveals the signal to the uninformed. With noise (Lab 1, part B), \(G(1) = \ln(5/3)/4 > 0\). Noise is what makes the price less informative: with \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\) the term \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) lowers \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) (Corollary 3.2). For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), it is also what gives an informed trader something to gain: without noise the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)).
00395 “Information is never worth buying in this model, so the informed fraction is zero.”
00396 At cost \(c\) below \(G(0) = \ln(2)/4\), Lab 1 gives a positive informed fraction for every cost on its grid from \(0.12\) to \(0.17\), and the value at cost \(0.15\) is \(\lambda^\ast \approx 0.658\). Information is worth buying when its cost is below the gain, and in Lab 1 the bisection root falls as the cost rises.
00397 “In equilibrium the price reveals the payoff, so the informed have no advantage.”
00398 The squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly below one (Lemma 3.3(a)). At the costs where some trader is informed, Lab 1, Part C, prints \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) from \(0.013061\) (cost \(0.17\)) to \(0.166667\) (cost \(0.12\)), rounded to six decimals. Partial informativeness alone does not give the informed an advantage. Without noise the price is also partly informative, with \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), and the gain is zero for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\) (Proposition 5.1(a)). For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), the advantage comes from the noise in the price. At \(\lambda = 0\) the gain \(G(0)\) does not depend on the noise (Lemma 4.5).
00399 “A higher cost should induce more traders to become informed.”
00400 The net benefit \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) falls with \(c\). Lab 1 part C shows the informed fraction falling from \(1\) at cost \(0.12\) to \(0\) at cost \(0.18\), with interior values in between. Across the seven costs of Lab 1, Part C the fraction falls as the cost rises. The page does not prove that it falls for every cost or parameter set (Section 6).

00403 Mechanics
00404 Mech 1 · The noise coefficient at one fraction
00405 With \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\) and \(\lambda = 1/2\), find \(\kappa\), and find \(\tau_\eta\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\).
00406 Solution
00407 From Proposition 3.1, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2 \cdot 1/(1/2) = 4\). Then \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 16\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + 16) = 1/17\). The variance of the noise \(\eta\) in the signal that the uninformed extract is \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 17\).
00408 Mech 2 · Demand of one trader
00409 An informed trader has posterior mean \(m = 1\), posterior variance \(V = 2\), and faces price \(p = 1/2\). Her risk aversion is \(a = 3\). Find her optimal demand and the maximal conditional expected utility when her initial wealth is \(W_0' = W_0\).
00410 Solution
00411 By Lemma 2.1, \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV) = (1 - 1/2)/(3 \cdot 2) = 1/12\). The maximal value is \(-\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-(1/2)^2/(2 \cdot 2)) = -\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-1/16)\). The exponent is \(-(m-p)^2/(2V) = -(1/4)/4 = -1/16\).
00412 Mech 3 · A normal moment
00413 Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, 4)\) and \(V = 1\). Compute \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)]\).
00414 Solution
00415 Lemma 2.2 gives \((1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2} = (1 + 4)^{-1/2} = 1/\sqrt{5} \approx 0.447214\). As a check, Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(t \mapsto e^{-t}\) gives \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)] \ge \exp(-\E[Z^2]/2) = \exp(-2) \approx 0.135\), and \(0.447 \ge 0.135\) is consistent with that bound.
00416 Proof
00417 Proof 1 · The coefficient of the signal
00418 Starting from the identity \((\ast)\) in the proof of Proposition 3.1, show directly that the coefficient of \(s\) on the right side of \((\ast)\) forces \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\).
00419 Solution
00420 Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) into \((\ast)\). The left side is \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha - \beta_p s + \varpi u) + A'(s - \bar v)\). The coefficient of \(s\) on the left is \(-(\tau_v + A')\beta_p + A'\), and the right side has no \(s\) term. Setting this coefficient to zero gives \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\), which is the coefficient \(\beta\) of Proposition 3.1 when \(A' = A\).
00421 Proof 2 · Strict monotonicity of the informativeness
00422 Show that for \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\tau_\eta(\lambda)\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\), and deduce the monotonicity of \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\).
00423 Solution
00424 Let \(0 < \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le 1\). Then \(\kappa(\lambda_1) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda_1 > \kappa(\lambda_2)\), and both are positive. Since \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\kappa(\lambda_1)^2\sigma_u^2 > \kappa(\lambda_2)^2\sigma_u^2\), so \(\sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_1) > \sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_2)\), and \(\tau_\eta(\lambda_1) < \tau_\eta(\lambda_2)\). The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), so \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\).
00425 Proof 3 · The derivative of the gain with respect to the cost
00426 Show that \(N(\lambda)\) is strictly decreasing in \(c\) at each fixed \(\lambda\), and that the set of costs at which \(\lambda\) is an equilibrium interior fraction is a single point.
00427 Solution
00428 For fixed \(\lambda\), \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) is a function of \(c\) with slope \(-1\), so it is strictly decreasing in \(c\). The interior equilibrium condition is \(G(\lambda) = c\). For a fixed \(\lambda\), this fixes \(c\) at the single value \(G(\lambda)\). So each interior fraction is an equilibrium at exactly one cost. This does not show that a given cost has one equilibrium fraction, which requires monotonicity of \(G\) in \(\lambda\), not proved on this page.

00432 Solution
00433 Part C prints the gap \(N(\lambda^\ast) = 1.83\times 10^{-15}\) at the reported point. The cost 0.15 lies between \(G(1) \approx 0.1277\) and \(G(0) \approx 0.1733\), so the boundary rule of Section 5.1 does not apply, and the bisection root is the answer.
00434 Emp 2 · Which cost gives the fraction 0.2316?
00435 Lab 1, part C, prints \(\lambda^\ast = 0.231615819\) at one of the seven costs. Identify the cost and the printed informativeness at that point.
00436 Solution

00438 Emp 3 · The gain at the boundary fractions
00439 Lab 1, part B, prints \(G(0)\) and \(G(1)\) at the parameters of Example 1.1. Check both values against the closed forms, and state which of the two is the larger.
00440 Solution
00441 Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0) = \ln(1 + 1)/(2 \cdot 2) = \ln(2)/4 = 0.173287\), which matches the printed value. For \(G(1)\), at \(\lambda = 1\) with \(a = 2\) and unit variances: \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) = 1/5\). Corollary 2.4 gives \(V_I = 1/(1 + 1) = 1/2\), \(b_y = (1/5)/(1 + 1/5) = 1/6\) and \(V_U = 1/(1 + 1/5) = 5/6\). Proposition 3.1 gives \(A = \tau_\varepsilon = 1\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A) = 1/2\), so \(b_s = \beta = 1/2\). Proposition 4.1 gives \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2 \cdot 2 + \kappa^2\beta^2 = 0 + 4 \cdot \tfrac14 = 1\) and \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(1 + 5) = \tfrac19 \cdot 6 = 2/3\). Definition 4.3 then gives \(G(1) = \tfrac14\ln\bigl((1 + 1/(1/2))/(1 + (2/3)/(5/6))\bigr) = \tfrac14\ln(3/(9/5)) = \tfrac14\ln(5/3) = 0.127706\), which matches. The larger value is \(G(0)\).
00442 Research
00443 Res 1 · The noiseless paradox, by cost range
00444 Prove that with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at any cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), and state which fraction is an equilibrium when \(c \ge G(0)\).
00445 Solution
00446 This is Proposition 5.1(b). For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), Proposition 5.1(a) gives \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\), so \(N(\lambda) < 0\) and case (iii) fails. At \(\lambda = 1\), \(G(1) = 0 < c\), so case (ii) fails. At \(\lambda = 0\), \(G(0) > c\), so case (i) fails. When \(c \ge G(0)\), Proposition 5.1(c) shows that \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium. There the price is \(\bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), a function of \(u\) alone, which is independent of \(v\). Because \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\) makes \(u\) constant, \(\Var(p) = 0\), so the correlation is undefined (Definition 7.1 of Lesson L07 requires positive variances).
00447 Res 2 · The limit of the gain at zero
00448 Show that \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} \Var(D_U)/V_U = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), using the formulas of Proposition 4.1, Corollary 2.4 and the definitions of \(\beta\), \(A\) and \(\tau_\eta\) in Proposition 3.1.
00449 Solution
00450 From Proposition 4.1, \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta)\), and \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\). As \(\lambda \to 0^+\), \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\), and the other factors converge to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4\). So \(\Var(D_U) \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), and \(V_U \to \sigma_v^2\), so the ratio tends to \(a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\).
00451 Res 3 · The gain with vanishing noise
00452 Show that \(G(1) \to 0\) as \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), with \(a\), \(\sigma_v^2\) and \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) fixed.
00453 Solution
00454 At \(\lambda = 1\), \(\beta = b_s\), \(\Var(D_I) = \kappa^2 b_s^2\sigma_u^2\), which tends to zero with \(\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed term is \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = (b_y - b_s)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)/V_U\). As \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), \(\tau_\eta \to \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(b_y \to b_s\), so this term tends to zero. Hence both ratios in \(G(1)\) tend to their limits, the numerator to \(1\) and the denominator to \(1\), and \(G(1) \to 0\).
