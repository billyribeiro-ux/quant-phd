  65| In this model a trader who pays for information gains nothing when the price already reveals the signal. This lesson derives the equilibrium price of a CARA-normal market and the gain from information as a cost, \(G(\lambda)\) (Definition 4.3; Lemma 4.5 for \(G(0)\)). With no noise in the supply of the risky asset, the price is a function of the signal, the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)), and no informed fraction \(\lambda\) (Assumption A3) is an equilibrium at a cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\) (Proposition 5.1(b)). With noise, the price stays partly informative, and equilibria exist outside \((G(1), G(0))\). Inside that interval, existence uses Assumption A4. Only Proposition 5.2(c) and the bisection rule use A4.
  68| 1The model
  71| A risky asset pays \(v\) in cash, and a riskless asset has gross return \(1\). The model has three assumptions, A1 to A3. Each is numbered, and each result below states the assumptions it uses. Section 5 adds Assumption A4, which Proposition 5.2(c) uses for existence and which justifies the bisection of Section 5.1.
  75| Assumption A1 (preferences)
  77| Each trader has constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) utility (Definition 6.2 of Lesson L06), \(U(w) = -\exp(-a w)\) with \(a > 0\), over terminal wealth \(w\). Each trader starts with wealth \(W_0\) and no shares of the risky asset. A trader who buys \(x\) shares at price \(p\) and holds the rest in the riskless asset ends with
  78| \[ W = W_0 + x\,(v - p) \quad\text{or}\quad W = W_0 - c + x\,(v - p), \]
  79| where the second form applies to an informed trader, who pays the cost \(c \ge 0\) in wealth. Since \(U'(w) = a\exp(-aw)\) and \(U''(w) = -a^2\exp(-aw)\), the Arrow–Pratt coefficient of absolute risk aversion \(-U''/U'\) equals \(a\) at every \(w\).
  84| Assumption A2 (normal information and noise)
  86| The random variables \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\) are independent, with \(v \sim \Normal(\bar v, \sigma_v^2)\), \(\varepsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\). Here \(\sigma_v^2 > 0\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 \ge 0\). The signal of Lesson L06 is \(s = v + \varepsilon\). The variable \(u\) is the noise-trader supply: the net number of shares of the risky asset that noise traders (Lesson L06) bring to the market. Noise traders are not modelled as optimisers. Their supply \(u\) is an exogenous normal shock.
  91| Assumption A3 (price taking and market clearing)
  93| A continuum of traders takes the price \(p\) as given. A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) of them is informed: each observes the signal \(s\) and pays \(c\). The rest are uninformed: each observes only \(p\). Let \(x_I\) and \(x_U\) be the demands of an informed and an uninformed trader. Market clearing requires
  94| \[ \lambda\, x_I + (1 - \lambda)\, x_U = u \qquad\text{in every state.} \]
  98| Assumption A3 is price taking in the sense of the competitive equilibrium concept linked in the header: no single trader's demand moves the price. The symbols used below are local to this lesson. Write \(\tau_v = 1/\sigma_v^2\) and \(\tau_\varepsilon = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) for precisions. The measure of price informativeness is the squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p)^2\), defined in Definition 7.2 of Lesson L07.
 102| Example 1.1 (the parameters of Lab 1)
 104| Set \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_v^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\), and \(\bar v = 0\). These values satisfy A1 to A3. The prior mean \(\bar v\) does not enter any gain or any informativeness measure, because Corollary 3.2 and Proposition 4.1 express the gain and the informativeness through variances alone. Lemma 4.5 (Section 4) gives \(G(0)\) at these values, and Lab 1 prints \(G(1)\).
 108| 2Demands and the value of information
 112| Lemma 2.1 (CARA demand)
 114| Assumptions: A1. Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be an information set such that, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(m, V)\) with \(V > 0\), and the price \(p\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable. Let \(W_0'\) be a constant. The conditional expected utility \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}]\) is maximised over \(x\) at
 115| \[ x^\ast = \frac{m - p}{a V}, \]
 116| and its maximal value is \(-\exp(-a W_0')\,\exp\bigl(-(m - p)^2 / (2V)\bigr)\).
 121| Proof
 123| Given \(\mathcal{I}\), the variable \(-a x (v - p)\) is normal with mean \(-a x (m - p)\) and variance \(a^2 x^2 V\). The moment generating function of a normal variable gives
 124| \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-a x (v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}\bigr] = \exp\bigl(-a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\bigr). \]
 125| Hence \(\E[U(W_0' + x(v - p)) \mid \mathcal{I}] = -\exp(-a W_0')\exp(f(x))\), where \(f(x) = -a x (m - p) + a^2 x^2 V / 2\). The prefactor is negative, so maximising the expected utility means minimising \(f\). The function \(f\) is a quadratic in \(x\) with leading coefficient \(a^2 V / 2 > 0\). Its derivative \(f'(x) = -a(m - p) + a^2 x V\) vanishes only at \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV)\), so \(x^\ast\) is the unique minimiser. Substituting gives \(f(x^\ast) = -a x^\ast (m - p) + a^2 (x^\ast)^2 V / 2 = -(m-p)^2/V + (m-p)^2/(2V) = -(m - p)^2/(2V)\).
 130| Lemma 2.2 (a normal moment)
 132| Assumptions: none beyond the statement. Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma^2 \ge 0\), and let \(V > 0\). Then
 133| \[ \E\bigl[\exp(-Z^2/(2V))\bigr] = \bigl(1 + \sigma^2/V\bigr)^{-1/2}. \]
 138| Proof
 140| If \(\sigma^2 = 0\), then \(Z = 0\) almost surely and both sides equal \(1\). If \(\sigma^2 > 0\), write the density of \(Z\) and combine the exponents:
 141| \[ \int_{\R} (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{x^2}{2V}\Bigr)\,dx = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \int_{\R} \exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^2}{2}\bigl(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1}\bigr)\Bigr)\,dx. \]
 142| The Gaussian integral equals \(\sqrt{2\pi/(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})}\). Multiplying gives \(\bigl(\sigma^2(\sigma^{-2} + V^{-1})\bigr)^{-1/2} = (1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2}\).
 147| Fact 2.3 (normal conditioning)
 149| Assumptions: \((x, y)\) is jointly normal and \(\Var(y) > 0\). Then \(\E[x \mid y] = \E x + \beta_{xy}\,(y - \E y)\) with \(\beta_{xy} = \Cov(x, y)/\Var(y)\), and \(\Var(x \mid y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\).
 154| Proof
 156| Let \(r = x - \E x - \beta_{xy}(y - \E y)\). Then \(\Cov(r, y) = \Cov(x, y) - \beta_{xy}\Var(y) = 0\), so \(r\) and \(y\) are uncorrelated. Since \((r, y)\) is jointly normal, they are independent. Therefore \(\E[r \mid y] = \E r = 0\) and \(\Var(r \mid y) = \Var(r)\). Writing \(x = \E x + \beta_{xy}(y - \E y) + r\) gives the conditional mean, and \(\Var(r) = \Var(x) - \beta_{xy}^2\Var(y) = \Var(x) - \Cov(x, y)^2/\Var(y)\) gives the conditional variance.
 161| Corollary 2.4 (conditional laws of the two groups)
 163| Assumptions: A1 to A3. The posterior of \(v\) (Lesson L06) given an information set is its conditional law given that set. Write \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).
 166| \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s (s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = V_I\).
 168| Let \(\kappa' \in \R\) and let \(\eta = \varepsilon - \kappa' u\), so that \(y = s - \kappa' u = v + \eta\). Let \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa'^2 \sigma_u^2\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\eta^2\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\E[v \mid y] = \bar v + b_y (y - \bar v)\) with \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\), and \(\Var(v \mid y) = V_U\).
 174| Proof
 176| (a) Here \(\Cov(v, s) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\), so Fact 2.3 gives \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + (\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2))(s - \bar v)\) and \(\Var(v \mid s) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\).
 178| (b) The pair \((v, y)\) is jointly normal, because \(y\) is a linear combination of \(v\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(u\). Here \(\Cov(v, y) = \sigma_v^2\) and \(\Var(y) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\) by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\) to get \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\). Then \(\Var(v \mid y) = \sigma_v^2 - \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = \sigma_v^2\sigma_\eta^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2) = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
 182| The uninformed trader observes \(p\), not \(y\). For \(\lambda\) in \((0, 1]\), Section 3 shows that \(p\) is an invertible affine function of \(y\) in equilibrium (Proposition 3.1), so \(\sigma(p) = \sigma(y)\), where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is the information generated by the variable in brackets, and Corollary 2.4(b) applies to \(p\) as well.
 186| Lemma 2.5 (what the informed trader knows)
 188| Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) for constants \(\alpha\), \(\beta_p\) and \(\varpi\). Let \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(s, p)\). Then, conditional on \(\mathcal{I}\), \(v \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\).
 193| Proof
 195| Let \(m(s) = \E[v \mid s]\) and \(r_v = v - m(s)\). By Fact 2.3 applied to \((v, s)\), \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\). It is also independent of \(u\), because \(u\) is independent of \((v, s)\). Since \(u\) is independent of the pair \((r_v, s)\) and \(r_v\) is independent of \(s\), the three variables are mutually independent, so \(r_v\) is independent of \((s, u)\). The variable \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so \(\sigma(s, p) \subseteq \sigma(s, u)\), and \(r_v\) is independent of \(\mathcal{I}\). Since \(s\) is \(\mathcal{I}\)-measurable, \(v = m(s) + r_v\) has conditional law \(\Normal(m(s), \Var(r_v))\) given \(\mathcal{I}\), and \(\Var(r_v) = V_I\) by Corollary 2.4(a).
 199| 3The equilibrium price
 203| Proposition 3.1 (the linear equilibrium price)
 205| Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2)\), \(A = \lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A)\).
 208| Suppose \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). If \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) with \(\beta_p \ne 0\) clears the market in every state, then \(\varpi/\beta_p = \kappa\), \(\alpha = (1 - \beta)\bar v\) and \(\beta_p = \beta\).
 210| The price \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\) clears the market in every state.
 216| Proof
 218| Let \(\kappa' = \varpi/\beta_p\), and let \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) be defined from \(\kappa'\) as \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\) are defined from \(\kappa\). By Corollary 2.4(b) with \(\kappa'\), the uninformed trader's posterior mean of \(v\) is \(\bar v + b_y'(y - \bar v)\), where \(y = (p - \alpha)/\beta_p = s - \kappa' u\), and their posterior variance is \(V_U' = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta')\). By Corollary 2.4(a) the informed posterior mean is \(\bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\). Multiply the clearing condition of A3 by \(a\), and use \(x_I = (\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)(\E[v \mid s] - p)/a\) and \(x_U = (\tau_v + \tau_\eta')(\E[v \mid y] - p)/a\). Then use \((\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) b_s = \tau_\varepsilon\), \((\tau_v + \tau_\eta') b_y' = \tau_\eta'\), and \(\lambda(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) + (1 - \lambda)(\tau_v + \tau_\eta') = \tau_v + A'\):
 219| \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa' u = a u, \]
 220| where the term in \(\kappa' u\) comes from \(y - \bar v = (s - \bar v) - \kappa' u\). Rearranged,
 221| \[ (\tau_v + A')(\bar v - p) + A'(s - \bar v) = \bigl(a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta' \kappa'\bigr) u. \qquad(\ast) \]
 222| Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\). Both sides are affine in \((s, u)\). The pair \((s, u)\) has a density that is positive on \(\R^2\), because \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 > 0\) and \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So an affine function that vanishes almost surely is identically zero, and the coefficients match:
 223| constant term: \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha) = A' \bar v\), so \(\alpha = \tau_v \bar v/(\tau_v + A') = (1 - \beta')\bar v\);
 224| coefficient of \(s\): \(A' = (\tau_v + A')\beta_p\), so \(\beta_p = \beta'\);
 225| coefficient of \(u\): \((\tau_v + A')\varpi = a + (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta'\kappa'\). Since \(\varpi = \kappa'\beta_p = \kappa'\beta'\) and \((\tau_v + A')\beta' = A'\), the left side is \(\kappa' A'\). So \(\kappa'(A' - (1 - \lambda)\tau_\eta') = a\). The bracket is \(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon\), so \(\kappa' = a/(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = \kappa\).
 226| With \(\kappa' = \kappa\), the quantities \(\tau_\eta'\), \(A'\) and \(\beta'\) equal \(\tau_\eta\), \(A\) and \(\beta\). This proves (a). Each step of the argument is an equivalence, so running it backwards from \(\kappa' = \kappa\) gives (b).
 231| Corollary 3.2 (price informativeness)
 233| Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\) of Proposition 3.1. Then
 234| \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\tau_\eta}{\tau_v + \tau_\eta} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2 \sigma_u^2}. \]
 239| Proof
 241| The price is \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\). Therefore \(\Cov(v, p^\ast) = \beta\sigma_v^2\), and, because \(s\) and \(u\) are independent,
 242| \[ \Var(p^\ast) = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2 = \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \]
 243| where \(\sigma_\eta^2 = 1/\tau_\eta = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\). Hence
 244| \[ \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \frac{\beta^2\sigma_v^4}{\sigma_v^2 \cdot \beta^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)} = \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2}. \]
 245| The last expression equals \(\tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) after multiplying by \(\tau_v\tau_\eta\), as in Corollary 2.4(b). The factor \(\beta\) cancels because \(\beta > 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\).
 250| Lemma 3.3 (informativeness in the informed fraction)
 252| Assumptions: A1 to A3, and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).
 255| \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\).
 257| If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\lambda \mapsto \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\).
 259| If \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), then \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\).
 265| Proof
 267| (a) \(\tau_\eta > 0\) and \(\tau_v > 0\), so \(0 < \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) < 1\). (b) As \(\lambda\) rises, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) falls strictly. If \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), then \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls strictly, so \(\tau_\eta\) rises strictly. The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), since its derivative is \(\tau_v/(\tau_v + t)^2 > 0\). (c) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\tau_\eta = 1/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = \tau_\varepsilon\) for every \(\lambda\), and \(\tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\).
 271| 4The gain from information
 275| Proposition 4.1 (the demand gaps and their variances)
 277| Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and the price \(p^\ast\). Define the demand gaps \(D_I = \E[v \mid s] - p^\ast\) and \(D_U = \E[v \mid p^\ast] - p^\ast\). Then \(\E D_I = \E D_U = 0\), and
 278| \[ \Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2, \qquad \Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2), \]
 279| where \(b_y = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
 284| Proof
 286| Using \(p^\ast = (1 - \beta)\bar v + \beta s - \kappa\beta u\),
 287| \[ D_I = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v) - (1 - \beta)\bar v - \beta s + \kappa\beta u = (b_s - \beta)(s - \bar v) + \kappa\beta u. \]
 288| Its mean is zero. The variables \(s\) and \(u\) are independent, so \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2\Var(s) + \kappa^2\beta^2\sigma_u^2\), with \(\Var(s) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2\).
 289| For the uninformed, let \(y = (p^\ast - (1 - \beta)\bar v)/\beta\). Then \(y - \bar v = (p^\ast - \bar v)/\beta\), and \(y - \bar v = (v - \bar v) + \eta\), so \(\Var(y - \bar v) = \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\). Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(\E[v \mid p^\ast] = \bar v + b_y(y - \bar v)\). Therefore \(D_U = b_y(y - \bar v) - \beta(y - \bar v) = (b_y - \beta)(y - \bar v)\), which has mean zero and variance \((b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)\).
 294| Proposition 4.2 (expected utilities of the two groups)
 296| Assumptions: A1 to A3 and \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\). Let \(EU_I\) be the expected utility of an informed trader who chooses demand optimally given \((s, p^\ast)\), net of the cost \(c\). Let \(EU_U\) be the expected utility of an uninformed trader who chooses demand optimally given \(p^\ast\). Then
 297| \[ EU_I = -\exp\bigl(-a(W_0 - c)\bigr)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I\bigr)^{-1/2}, \qquad EU_U = -\exp(-aW_0)\bigl(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U\bigr)^{-1/2}, \]
 298| where \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\).
 303| Proof
 305| For the informed trader, Lemma 2.5 gives the conditional law of \(v\) given \((s, p^\ast)\) as \(\Normal(\E[v \mid s], V_I)\). Lemma 2.1 with \(m = \E[v \mid s]\), \(V = V_I\) and \(W_0' = W_0 - c\) gives the optimal conditional utility \(-\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-(\E[v \mid s] - p^\ast)^2/(2V_I)) = -\exp(-a(W_0 - c))\exp(-D_I^2/(2V_I))\). Taking the expectation over \((s, u)\) and applying Lemma 2.2 to \(D_I \sim \Normal(0, \Var(D_I))\) gives the first formula. The uninformed case is the same with \(\mathcal{I} = \sigma(p^\ast)\). Here \(\sigma(p^\ast) = \sigma(y)\), so Corollary 2.4(b) gives \(v \mid p^\ast \sim \Normal(\E[v \mid y], V_U)\), and Lemma 2.2 applies to \(D_U\).
 310| Definition 4.3 (gain from information)
 312| For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), the gain from information is
 313| \[ G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\!\left(\frac{1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I}{1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U}\right), \]
 314| and the net benefit of information at cost \(c\) is \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\). The gain \(G(\lambda)\) is the largest cost at which an informed trader is at least as well off as an uninformed one. Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0)\) for the case with no informed trader.
 319| Proposition 4.4 (the indifference condition)
 321| Assumptions: A1 to A3, \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), and \(c \ge 0\). Then \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(\lambda)\). Equality holds exactly when \(c = G(\lambda)\).
 326| Proof
 328| Write \(X = (1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)^{-1/2}\) and \(Y = (1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)^{-1/2}\). By Proposition 4.2, \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) reads \(-\exp(-aW_0)\exp(ac)X \ge -\exp(-aW_0)Y\). Dividing by \(-\exp(-aW_0) < 0\) reverses the inequality: \(\exp(ac)X \le Y\). Taking logarithms, \(ac + \ln X \le \ln Y\). Here \(\ln Y - \ln X = \tfrac12\ln\bigl((1 + \Var(D_I)/V_I)/(1 + \Var(D_U)/V_U)\bigr) = aG(\lambda)\). So the condition is \(ac \le aG(\lambda)\), that is \(c \le G(\lambda)\), because \(a > 0\). Equality is the same computation with equalities.
 333| Lemma 4.5 (no informed trader)
 335| Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\lambda = 0\). Then \(p^\ast = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), and the gain from becoming informed is
 336| \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\Bigl(1 + \frac{\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}\Bigr). \]
 337| This value does not depend on \(\sigma_u^2\).
 342| Proof
 344| With \(\lambda = 0\), the uninformed trader's posterior of \(v\) is the prior, because \(p\) is a function of \(u\), which is independent of \(v\). Lemma 2.1 gives demand \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2)\). Market clearing \((\bar v - p)/(a\sigma_v^2) = u\) gives \(p = \bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\).
 345| A deviating informed trader's posterior mean is \(\E[v \mid s] = \bar v + b_s(s - \bar v)\), so \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\) and \(\Var(D_I) = b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed gap is \(D_U = \bar v - p = a\sigma_v^2 u\), so \(\Var(D_U) = a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\), and \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\).
 346| Let \(X_0 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) and \(q = \sigma_v^2/\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). Then \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = X_0\). Using \(b_s = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\) and \(1/V_I = (\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), we get \(b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/V_I = \sigma_v^4/(\sigma_v^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2) = q\). Also \(a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2/V_I = X_0(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)/\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = X_0(1 + q)\). The numerator is therefore \(1 + q + X_0(1 + q) = (1 + q)(1 + X_0)\). Hence
 347| \[ G(0) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\frac{(1 + q)(1 + X_0)}{1 + X_0} = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1 + q). \]
 351| The formula of Definition 4.3 also gives \(G(1)\), the advantage of an informed trader over an uninformed deviator at the price that prevails when every trader is informed. The three cases of Definition 4.6 concern the disjoint sets \(\{0\}\), \(\{1\}\) and \((0, 1)\), so they cannot overlap.
 355| Definition 4.6 (equilibrium informed fraction)
 357| A fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\) when no trader wants to switch:
 360| \(\lambda = 0\) and \(G(0) \le c\), so informing is not worth its cost to a deviator;
 362| \(\lambda = 1\) and \(G(1) \ge c\), so staying informed is worth its cost to each informed trader;
 364| \(0 < \lambda < 1\) and \(G(\lambda) = c\), so informed and uninformed traders are equally well off.
 369| Case (iii) is the indifference condition of Proposition 4.4 with equality. Case (ii) follows from Proposition 4.4 at \(\lambda = 1\), which lies in \((0, 1]\): a trader informed at \(\lambda = 1\) is weakly better off than an uninformed trader when \(c \le G(1)\). Case (i) is not an instance of Proposition 4.4 as stated, because \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda\) is undefined at \(\lambda = 0\). It follows from Lemma 4.5. Its proof gives the demand gaps at \(\lambda = 0\): the deviator's gap \(D_I = b_s(s - \bar v) + a\sigma_v^2 u\), with variance \(\Var(D_I)\) and posterior variance \(V_I\), and the uninformed gap \(D_U = a\sigma_v^2 u\), with \(V_U = \sigma_v^2\). Lemma 2.5 holds with \(\beta_p = 0\), because its proof uses only that \(p\) is a function of \((s, u)\), so the proof of Proposition 4.2 gives the expected utilities at \(\lambda = 0\). The algebra of Proposition 4.4 then gives \(EU_I \ge EU_U\) if and only if \(c \le G(0)\), so a deviator at \(\lambda = 0\) is weakly worse off when \(c \ge G(0)\).
 372| 5The paradox, and solving for the informed fraction
 376| Definition (the Grossman–Stiglitz paradox)
 378| In a model of this kind, the Grossman–Stiglitz paradox is the conflict between two facts. A price that reveals the signal gives the uninformed the value of the information, so no one pays for it. A price that reveals nothing leaves the informed a gain that can cover a positive cost. Proposition 5.1 states the conflict for the noiseless case, and Proposition 5.2 states its resolution with noise.
 383| Proposition 5.1 (the noiseless case)
 385| Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\). Then the following hold.
 388| For every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), the price is the affine function \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\) of the signal, and \(G(\lambda) = 0\).
 390| If \(0 < c < G(0)\), then no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
 392| If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\).
 398| Proof
 400| (a) With \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 0\), so \(\tau_\eta = \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(A = \tau_\varepsilon\). Then \(\beta = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\), and \(\kappa\beta u = 0\), so \(p^\ast = (1 - b_s)\bar v + b_s s\). Also \(b_y = \tau_\varepsilon/(\tau_v + \tau_\varepsilon) = b_s\) by the formula of Corollary 2.4(b), so \(b_y - \beta = 0\). Then \(\Var(D_U) = 0\), and \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - b_s)^2(\cdot) + 0 = 0\). Hence \(G(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2a}\ln(1) = 0\). Corollary 3.2 gives the informativeness.
 401| (b) If \(0 < c < G(0)\): case (i) fails, because \(G(0) \le c\) is false. Case (ii) fails, because \(G(1) = 0 < c\). Case (iii) fails for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 \ne c\).
 402| (c) If \(c \ge G(0) > 0\), case (i) holds at \(\lambda = 0\). Case (ii) fails at \(\lambda = 1\), because \(G(1) = 0 < c\), and case (iii) fails for \(\lambda \in (0, 1)\), because \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\).
 406| Part (a) of Proposition 5.1 is the paradox in this model. When the supply of the risky asset carries no noise, the price \(p^\ast\) is a function of \(s\), so an uninformed trader who observes the price has the signal as well. The information then has no value, so no informed fraction is an equilibrium at a cost between zero and \(G(0)\). Part (b) states this as a theorem: for every cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), no fraction works. Part (c) states that at or above \(G(0)\) the only equilibrium is \(\lambda = 0\), with a price that carries no information.
 410| Proposition 5.2 (the noisy case)
 412| Assumptions: A1 to A3 with \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\).
 415| \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} G(\lambda) = G(0)\).
 417| If \(c \ge G(0)\), then \(\lambda = 0\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at cost \(c\). If \(c \le G(1)\), then \(\lambda = 1\) is one.
 419| Assume A4 below. If \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), there is an equilibrium informed fraction \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) with \(G(\lambda^\ast) = c\). On the interval \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), \(0 < \Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 < 1\) and \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\) (Lemma 3.3).
 425| Assumption A4 (sign change, the bisection's invariant)
 427| For every bracket \([\ell, h]\) with \(0 \le \ell < h \le 1\), \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) implies that \(N\) has a zero in \([\ell, h]\). This is assumed here. The page does not prove it from earlier results, and Lab 1 uses it only through the bisection rule below.
 432| Proof
 434| (a) Write \(\lambda \downarrow 0\). Then \(\kappa \to \infty\), and \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 \to \infty\) because \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\). So \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) \to 0\), and \(\tau_\eta \kappa \to 0\). The identity \(\kappa\lambda\tau_\varepsilon = a\) gives \(\kappa A = a + (1 - \lambda)\kappa\tau_\eta \to a\). Therefore \(\kappa\beta = \kappa A/(\tau_v + A) \to a/\tau_v = a\sigma_v^2\), and \(\beta \to 0\). Hence \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + (\kappa\beta)^2\sigma_u^2 \to b_s^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) + a^2\sigma_v^4\sigma_u^2\).
 435| For the uninformed term, \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\), and \(\tau_\eta - A = \lambda(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)\). So
 436| \[ (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta) = \frac{\tau_v^2(\tau_\eta - \tau_\varepsilon)^2}{(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)^2(\tau_v + A)^2}\bigl(\lambda^2\sigma_v^2 + \lambda^2/\tau_\eta\bigr). \]
 437| Now \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \lambda^2\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = \lambda^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2 \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\). Also \(\tau_\eta \to 0\) and \(A \to 0\), so the displayed fraction tends to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4 = \tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^2\). The limit of \(\Var(D_U)\) is therefore \(\tau_\varepsilon^2 a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2/\tau_v^2 = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), using \(\tau_\varepsilon\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\). Since \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta) \to \sigma_v^2\), \(\Var(D_U)/V_U \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\). Substituting these limits into Definition 4.3 gives the value of Lemma 4.5, which is \(G(0)\).
 438| (b) If \(c \ge G(0)\), case (i) of Definition 4.6 holds. If \(c \le G(1)\), case (ii) holds.
 439| (c) The formulas of Section 3 are continuous on \((0, 1]\), so \(G\) is continuous there, and \(N\) is continuous on \((0, 1]\) and extends continuously to \(0\) by (a). If \(G(1) < c < G(0)\), then \(N(0) = G(0) - c > 0 > G(1) - c = N(1)\), and A4 gives a zero \(\lambda^\ast\) of \(N\) in \([0, 1]\). The zero is not \(0\) or \(1\), because \(N(0) \ne 0\) and \(N(1) \ne 0\). So \(\lambda^\ast \in (0, 1)\) satisfies case (iii). The informativeness statement is Lemma 3.3.
 443| Proposition 5.2 resolves the paradox within this model. With noise, the price reveals the signal only up to the error \(\kappa u\), whose variance \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) falls as \(\lambda\) rises. The uninformed therefore learn from the price, but imperfectly, and the informed keep an advantage. The price stays partly informative, and the cost restricts the equilibrium informed fraction. Costs strictly between \(G(1)\) and \(G(0)\) give interior equilibria under A4. Costs at or above \(G(0)\) give \(\lambda = 0\), and costs at or below \(G(1)\) give \(\lambda = 1\). The interior equilibrium is not claimed to be unique. Lab 1 checks monotonicity only on its grid.
 446| 5.1 Solving for the informed fraction
 449| The lab solves \(N(\lambda) = 0\) on the interval \((0, 1)\) by bisection. The boundary rule is applied first. If \(c \ge G(0)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 0\). If \(c \le G(1)\), the answer is \(\lambda = 1\). Otherwise the bracket \([0, 1]\) has \(N(0) > 0 > N(1)\), and the bisection keeps the invariant \(N(\ell) > 0 \ge N(h)\) on the bracket \([\ell, h]\). Each step evaluates \(N\) at the midpoint. It replaces \(\ell\) if \(N\) is positive there and \(h\) otherwise, and it stops when \(h - \ell \le 10^{-12}\). By A4 the bracket contains a zero. The gap printed for each cost is \(N(\lambda^\ast)\) at the reported point, so a small gap is evidence that the reported point is an equilibrium to the printed precision.

 458| """E4 L08 lab 1: the informed fraction in a CARA-normal model with costly information, by bisection.
 460| The parameters are illustrative constants, not data: CARA coefficient a = 2, prior variance of the payoff
 461| sigma_v^2 = 1, signal-noise variance sigma_eps^2 = 1, noise-trader supply variance sigma_u^2 = 1, prior mean 0.
 462| Parts B to D evaluate population formulas in double precision. Part A uses seeded draws and is SIMULATED.
 463| The informed fraction lam lies in [0, 1]. The price is p = (1 - beta) * prior mean + beta * s - kappa * beta * u,
 464| with kappa = a * sigma_eps^2 / lam, where s = v + eps is the signal and u is the noise-trader supply.
 465| The gain from information is the cost at which an informed and an uninformed trader are equally well off.
 466| Part C solves gain(lam) = cost by bisection on (0, 1) to a tolerance of 1e-12 on lam. The boundary rule is:
 467| lam = 0 when gain(0) <= cost, lam = 1 when gain(1) >= cost, and the bisection root otherwise.
 468| The gap printed for each cost is gain(lam) - cost at the reported lam (the net benefit of information).
 469| Standard library only; no network access.
 470| """
 472| from __future__ import annotations
 474| import itertools
 475| import math
 476| import random
 478| CARA = 2.0
 479| VAR_V = 1.0
 480| VAR_EPS = 1.0
 481| VAR_U = 1.0
 482| PRIOR_MEAN = 0.0
 483| COSTS: tuple[float, ...] = (0.12, 0.13, 0.14, 0.15, 0.16, 0.17, 0.18)
 484| TOL = 1e-12
 485| SEED = 20261103
 486| DRAWS = 20000
 487| GRID = 999
 490| def equilibrium(lam: float, var_u: float = VAR_U) -> dict[str, float]:
 491| """Price coefficients and the gain from information at informed fraction lam in (0, 1].
 493| var_u is the variance of the noise-trader supply. The returned dictionary holds beta (the weight of the signal in
 494| the price), kappa (the noise coefficient divided by beta), corr2 (the squared correlation between payoff and price)
 495| and gain (the cost at which an informed and an uninformed trader are equally well off).
 496| """
 497| if not 0.0 < lam <= 1.0:
 498| raise ValueError("lam must lie in (0, 1]")
 499| tau_v = 1.0 / VAR_V
 500| tau_e = 1.0 / VAR_EPS
 501| kappa = CARA * VAR_EPS / lam
 502| tau_eta = 1.0 / (VAR_EPS + kappa * kappa * var_u)
 503| a_w = lam * tau_e + (1.0 - lam) * tau_eta
 504| beta = a_w / (tau_v + a_w)
 505| b_s = tau_e / (tau_v + tau_e)
 506| b_y = tau_eta / (tau_v + tau_eta)
 507| post_s = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_e)
 508| post_u = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_eta)
 509| noise_term = (kappa * beta) ** 2 * var_u
 510| var_informed = (b_s - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + VAR_EPS) + noise_term
 511| var_uninformed = (b_y - beta) ** 2 * (VAR_V + 1.0 / tau_eta)
 512| gain = math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + var_uninformed / post_u)) / (2.0 * CARA)
 513| return {
 514| "beta": beta,
 515| "kappa": kappa,
 516| "corr2": b_y,
 517| "gain": gain,
 518| }
 521| def gain_at_zero(var_u: float = VAR_U) -> float:
 522| """Gain from becoming informed when no trader is informed; the price is prior mean minus CARA VAR_V u."""
 523| total = VAR_V + VAR_EPS
 524| b_s = VAR_V / total
 525| post_s = 1.0 / (1.0 / VAR_V + 1.0 / VAR_EPS)
 526| noise = CARA * CARA * VAR_V * VAR_V * var_u
 527| var_informed = b_s * b_s * total + noise
 528| return math.log((1.0 + var_informed / post_s) / (1.0 + noise / VAR_V)) / (2.0 * CARA)
 531| def informativeness(lam: float) -> float:
 532| """Squared correlation between payoff and price at lam; zero when no trader is informed."""
 533| if lam == 0.0:
 534| return 0.0
 535| return equilibrium(lam)["corr2"]

 542| beta, kappa = eq["beta"], eq["kappa"]

 546| b_s = tau_e / (tau_v + tau_e)
 547| b_y = tau_eta / (tau_v + tau_eta)
 548| post_s = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_e)
 549| post_u = 1.0 / (tau_v + tau_eta)

 693| 6Where the canon breaks
 696| The closed forms need CARA utility and normal variables. Lemma 2.1 uses the normal moment generating function, and Lemma 2.2 uses the normal density. The formulas of Sections 2 to 4 are derived for CARA utility and normal variables. The page makes no claim for other utilities or distributions.
 698| Noise traders are exogenous. The shock \(u\) is a normal supply that does not respond to prices or information. Proposition 5.1(b) gives no equilibrium with informed traders for \(0 < c < G(0)\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), and Proposition 5.2 gives one for each such cost when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), and A4 is needed only for costs strictly between \(G(1)\) and \(G(0)\). That contrast holds for this specification, in which \(u\) is exogenous. Models in which noise traders optimise are outside this lesson.
 700| Price taking is an assumption. Assumption A3 says no trader moves the price, and Proposition 3.1 uses that assumption. With a finite number of traders the derivation does not apply, and the page does not derive the equilibrium of that game.
 702| The interior root is assumed to exist and is not shown to be unique. Assumption A4 gives a zero of \(N\) for each bracket with a sign change. Lab 1 checks that \(G\) falls on its grid of 999 points, and nothing on the page proves that \(G\) falls on \((0, 1)\) for every parameter choice.
 704| The cost is in wealth. The cost \(c\) reduces terminal wealth by the same amount for every trader and every state. A cost in utility units, or a cost that depends on the signal, is outside the model of Proposition 4.4, which takes the cost as a reduction of terminal wealth.
 711| 7Misconceptions
 716| “Noise traders only hurt the informed, so noise makes information less valuable.”
 718| Proposition 5.1(a) shows the reverse. Without noise, \(G(\lambda) = 0\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), because the price reveals the signal to the uninformed. With noise (Lab 1, part B), \(G(1) = \ln(5/3)/4 > 0\). Noise is what makes the price less informative: with \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\) the term \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2\) lowers \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\) (Corollary 3.2). It is also what gives an informed trader something to gain, because without noise the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)).
 723| “Information is never worth buying in this model, so the informed fraction is zero.”
 725| At cost \(c\) below \(G(0) = \ln(2)/4\), Lab 1 gives a positive informed fraction for every cost on its grid from \(0.12\) to \(0.17\), and the value at cost \(0.15\) is \(\lambda^\ast \approx 0.658\). Information is worth buying when its cost is below the gain, and in Lab 1 the bisection root falls as the cost rises.
 730| “In equilibrium the price reveals the payoff, so the informed have no advantage.”
 732| The squared correlation \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly below one (Lemma 3.3(a)). Lab 1 prints values from \(0.013\) to \(0.167\) at the costs where some trader is informed. Partial informativeness alone does not give the informed an advantage. Without noise the price is also partly informative, with \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\), and the gain is zero (Proposition 5.1(a)). The advantage comes from the noise in the price.
 737| “A higher cost should induce more traders to become informed.”
 739| The net benefit \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) falls with \(c\). Lab 1 part C shows the informed fraction falling from \(1\) at cost \(0.12\) to \(0\) at cost \(0.18\), with interior values in between. The fraction falls as the cost rises on the grid, and the grid does not show this for other costs.

 754| Mechanics
 757| Mech 1 · The noise coefficient at one fraction
 759| With \(a = 2\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1\) and \(\lambda = 1/2\), find \(\kappa\), and find \(\tau_\eta\) when \(\sigma_u^2 = 1\).
 761| Solution
 763| From Proposition 3.1, \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2 \cdot 1/(1/2) = 4\). Then \(\kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 16\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + 16) = 1/17\). The variance of the noise \(\eta\) in the signal that the uninformed extract is \(\sigma_\eta^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2 = 17\).
 768| Mech 2 · Demand of one trader
 770| An informed trader has posterior mean \(m = 1\), posterior variance \(V = 2\), and faces price \(p = 1/2\). Her risk aversion is \(a = 3\). Find her optimal demand and the maximal conditional expected utility when her initial wealth is \(W_0' = W_0\).
 772| Solution
 774| By Lemma 2.1, \(x^\ast = (m - p)/(aV) = (1 - 1/2)/(3 \cdot 2) = 1/12\). The maximal value is \(-\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-(1/2)^2/(2 \cdot 2)) = -\exp(-3W_0)\exp(-1/16)\). The exponent is \(-(m-p)^2/(2V) = -(1/4)/4 = -1/16\).
 779| Mech 3 · A normal moment
 781| Let \(Z \sim \Normal(0, 4)\) and \(V = 1\). Compute \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)]\).
 783| Solution
 785| Lemma 2.2 gives \((1 + \sigma^2/V)^{-1/2} = (1 + 4)^{-1/2} = 1/\sqrt{5} \approx 0.447214\). As a check, Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(t \mapsto e^{-t}\) gives \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/2)] \ge \exp(-\E[Z^2]/2) = \exp(-2) \approx 0.135\), and \(0.447 \ge 0.135\) is consistent with that bound.
 793| Proof
 796| Proof 1 · The coefficient of the signal
 798| Starting from the identity \((\ast)\) in the proof of Proposition 3.1, show directly that the coefficient of \(s\) on the right side of \((\ast)\) forces \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\).
 800| Solution
 802| Insert \(p = \alpha + \beta_p s - \varpi u\) into \((\ast)\). The left side is \((\tau_v + A')(\bar v - \alpha - \beta_p s + \varpi u) + A'(s - \bar v)\). The coefficient of \(s\) on the left is \(-(\tau_v + A')\beta_p + A'\), and the right side has no \(s\) term. Setting this coefficient to zero gives \(\beta_p = A'/(\tau_v + A')\), which is the coefficient \(\beta\) of Proposition 3.1 when \(A' = A\).
 807| Proof 2 · Strict monotonicity of the informativeness
 809| Show that for \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\tau_\eta(\lambda)\) is strictly increasing on \((0, 1]\), and deduce the monotonicity of \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2\).
 811| Solution
 813| Let \(0 < \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le 1\). Then \(\kappa(\lambda_1) = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda_1 > \kappa(\lambda_2)\), and both are positive. Since \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), \(\kappa(\lambda_1)^2\sigma_u^2 > \kappa(\lambda_2)^2\sigma_u^2\), so \(\sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_1) > \sigma_\eta^2(\lambda_2)\), and \(\tau_\eta(\lambda_1) < \tau_\eta(\lambda_2)\). The map \(t \mapsto t/(\tau_v + t)\) is strictly increasing on \(t > 0\), so \(\Corr(v, p^\ast)^2 = \tau_\eta/(\tau_v + \tau_\eta)\) is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\).
 818| Proof 3 · The derivative of the gain with respect to the cost
 820| Show that \(N(\lambda)\) is strictly decreasing in \(c\) at each fixed \(\lambda\), and that the set of costs at which \(\lambda\) is an equilibrium interior fraction is a single point.
 822| Solution
 824| For fixed \(\lambda\), \(N(\lambda) = G(\lambda) - c\) is a function of \(c\) with slope \(-1\), so it is strictly decreasing in \(c\). The interior equilibrium condition is \(G(\lambda) = c\). For a fixed \(\lambda\), this fixes \(c\) at the single value \(G(\lambda)\). So each interior fraction is an equilibrium at exactly one cost. This does not show that a given cost has one equilibrium fraction, which requires monotonicity of \(G\) in \(\lambda\), not proved on this page.

 857| Emp 3 · The gain at the boundary fractions
 859| Lab 1, part B, prints \(G(0)\) and \(G(1)\) at the parameters of Example 1.1. Check both values against the closed forms, and state which of the two is the larger.
 861| Solution
 863| Lemma 4.5 gives \(G(0) = \ln(1 + 1)/(2 \cdot 2) = \ln(2)/4 = 0.173287\), which matches the printed value. For \(G(1)\), at \(\lambda = 1\) with \(a = 2\) and unit variances: \(\kappa = a\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\lambda = 2\), so \(\tau_\eta = 1/(\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \kappa^2\sigma_u^2) = 1/5\). Corollary 2.4 gives \(V_I = 1/(1 + 1) = 1/2\), \(b_y = (1/5)/(1 + 1/5) = 1/6\) and \(V_U = 1/(1 + 1/5) = 5/6\). Proposition 3.1 gives \(A = \tau_\varepsilon = 1\) and \(\beta = A/(\tau_v + A) = 1/2\), so \(b_s = \beta = 1/2\). Proposition 4.1 gives \(\Var(D_I) = (b_s - \beta)^2 \cdot 2 + \kappa^2\beta^2 = 0 + 4 \cdot \tfrac14 = 1\) and \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(1 + 5) = \tfrac19 \cdot 6 = 2/3\). Definition 4.3 then gives \(G(1) = \tfrac14\ln\bigl((1 + 1/(1/2))/(1 + (2/3)/(5/6))\bigr) = \tfrac14\ln(3/(9/5)) = \tfrac14\ln(5/3) = 0.127706\), which matches. The larger value is \(G(0)\).
 871| Research
 874| Res 1 · The noiseless paradox, by cost range
 876| Prove that with \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\), no fraction \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) is an equilibrium informed fraction at any cost \(c\) with \(0 < c < G(0)\), and state which fraction is an equilibrium when \(c \ge G(0)\).
 878| Solution
 880| This is Proposition 5.1(b). For \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), Proposition 5.1(a) gives \(G(\lambda) = 0 < c\), so \(N(\lambda) < 0\) and case (iii) fails. At \(\lambda = 1\), \(G(1) = 0 < c\), so case (ii) fails. At \(\lambda = 0\), \(G(0) > c\), so case (i) fails. When \(c \ge G(0)\), Proposition 5.1(c) shows that \(\lambda = 0\) is the only equilibrium. There the price is \(\bar v - a\sigma_v^2 u\), a function of \(u\) alone, which is independent of \(v\). Because \(\sigma_u^2 = 0\) makes \(u\) constant, \(\Var(p) = 0\), so the correlation is undefined (Definition 7.1 of Lesson L07 requires positive variances).
 885| Res 2 · The limit of the gain at zero
 887| Show that \(\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} \Var(D_U)/V_U = a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\) when \(\sigma_u^2 > 0\), using the formulas of Proposition 4.1, Corollary 2.4 and the definitions of \(\beta\), \(A\) and \(\tau_\eta\) in Proposition 3.1.
 889| Solution
 891| From Proposition 4.1, \(\Var(D_U) = (b_y - \beta)^2(\sigma_v^2 + 1/\tau_\eta)\), and \(b_y - \beta = \tau_v(\tau_\eta - A)/((\tau_v + \tau_\eta)(\tau_v + A))\). As \(\lambda \to 0^+\), \(\lambda^2/\tau_\eta \to a^2\sigma_\varepsilon^4\sigma_u^2\), and the other factors converge to \(\tau_v^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/\tau_v^4\). So \(\Var(D_U) \to a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^4\), and \(V_U \to \sigma_v^2\), so the ratio tends to \(a^2\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\).
 896| Res 3 · The gain with vanishing noise
 898| Show that \(G(1) \to 0\) as \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), with \(a\), \(\sigma_v^2\) and \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) fixed.
 900| Solution
 902| At \(\lambda = 1\), \(\beta = b_s\), \(\Var(D_I) = \kappa^2 b_s^2\sigma_u^2\), which tends to zero with \(\sigma_u^2\). The uninformed term is \(\Var(D_U)/V_U = (b_y - b_s)^2(\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\eta^2)/V_U\). As \(\sigma_u^2 \to 0\), \(\tau_\eta \to \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(b_y \to b_s\), so this term tends to zero. Hence both ratios in \(G(1)\) tend to their limits, the numerator to \(1\) and the denominator to \(1\), and \(G(1) \to 0\).
