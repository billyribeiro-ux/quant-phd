0006| Price Informativeness
0007| Some investors pay a cost \(c\) to learn the payoff of a risky asset, and the others learn only from its price. Noise traders, investors who trade for reasons unrelated to the payoff, add a random supply of the asset with standard deviation \(\sigma\), the noise volume, so the price moves for reasons unrelated to the payoff. With the fraction of investors who pay to learn held fixed, more noise makes the price reveal less about the payoff; in the model here, on the interior where that fraction lies strictly between 0 and 1, at a fixed cost the fraction rises with the noise, and there the price's informativeness, the squared correlation of the payoff and the price, does not depend on the noise volume. You write the solver that finds the fraction of investors who pay to learn (the informed fraction, \(\lambda\)), and you report how that fraction and the price's informativeness move with the noise and with the cost of information.

0030| The model and its equilibrium
0031| The model has one risky asset, two dates and a risk-free return of zero. It is the noisy rational-expectations equilibrium of Lesson 6, and its price informativeness is the measure of Lesson 7. Each result below is derived from the assumptions numbered here, and each proposition states the assumptions it uses.
0032| Notation
0033| \(v\) is the payoff, \(p\) the price, \(s\) the signal, \(\varepsilon\) the noise in the signal, \(u\) the noise-trader supply, \(\sigma\) its standard deviation (the noise volume, noise_sd), \(c\) the information cost (info_cost), \(\lambda\) the informed fraction, \(a\) the CARA coefficient, \(x\) a demand, \(\hat x\) the optimal demand of Lemma 1, \(w\) terminal wealth, and \(\tau\) a precision, which is the inverse of a variance. \(\tau_v\) is the prior precision of \(v\), \(\tau_\varepsilon\) the precision of \(\varepsilon\), and \(\tau_y\) the precision of the price signal defined in Proposition 1. \(\Omega = \Var(v-p)\) is the variance of the payoff less the price, and \(V_I\) and \(V_U\) are the conditional variances of \(v\) for an informed and for an uninformed investor. The target precision is \(\bar\tau = (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac}-\tau_v\). This page uses \(\bar\tau\) for this quantity.
0034| Assumptions
0035| A1 · Prior and signal. \(v \sim \Normal(0, 1/\tau_v)\) and \(\varepsilon \sim \Normal(0, 1/\tau_\varepsilon)\), with \(\varepsilon\) independent of \(v\), and \(s = v + \varepsilon\). The prior mean of zero is a normalization: a common prior mean \(\mu\) shifts the payoff, the signal, the conditional means and the price by \(\mu\). For an information set \(\mathcal F\) (Lemma 1), a demand is \((\E[v\mid\mathcal F]-p)/(aV)\), which depends on the shift only through the difference, and an expected utility depends on \(v-p\) alone, so both are unchanged (the economy is the zero-mean one with each variable less \(\mu\)).
0036| A2 · Noise supply. \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma > 0\), independent of \((v, \varepsilon)\). The rational demand absorbs it: \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\).
0037| A3 · Preferences, wealth and cost. Each investor has constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) utility (Definition 6.2 of Lesson 6), \(-\exp(-a w)\), with \(a = 2\). Initial wealth is 0 for every investor, and the risk-free return is 0. An informed investor pays \(c\), in units of terminal wealth, so \(w = x(v-p) - c\). An uninformed investor has \(w = x(v-p)\). A common initial wealth \(w_0\) makes terminal wealth \(w_0+w\), so \(-\exp(-a(w_0+w)) = -\exp(-aw_0)\exp(-aw)\), and it multiplies every expected utility by the same positive factor \(\exp(-aw_0)\), so it changes no comparison below.
0038| A4 · Linear price. The price is \(p = b_s s + b_u u\), with \(b_s \neq 0\) whenever \(\lambda > 0\). The results are for this family. The page does not claim that an equilibrium exists outside it.
0039| A5 · Types and price taking. A fraction \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) of investors is informed. Every informed investor observes the same \(s\). The others observe only \(p\). Each investor takes the price as given, and the average over a type is its expectation, which is the continuum limit.
0040| A6 · Sign change and continuity (bisection). The gap \(G\) of Proposition 3 is continuous on \([0,1]\), with \(G(0)\) taken as its limit \(G(0{+})\), and in the interior case \(G(0{+}) > 0 > G(1)\), which is the case the boundary rule of Proposition 4 leaves open. The page assumes this and does not prove it. It is used only in the interior case, to give a zero of \(G\) in \((0,1)\) that the bisection brackets.
0041| Definition (equilibrium). At an informed fraction \(\lambda\), an equilibrium is a price and two demand functions, \(x_I(s,p)\) for an informed investor and \(x_U(p)\) for an uninformed one, such that each investor maximizes expected utility given the information of its type, and the market clears: \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\) for every realized \((s,u)\).
0042| Three lemmas
0043| Lemma 1 · CARA demand and expected utility. Assume A3. Let \(\mathcal F\) be an information set for which \(p\) is measurable, let \(m = \E[v\mid\mathcal F]\) (a function of the information), and suppose that \(v\) is conditionally normal given \(\mathcal F\) and that \(V = \Var(v\mid\mathcal F) > 0\) is a constant. Then the optimal demand is \(\hat{x} = (m-p)/(aV)\), and the conditional expected utility at that demand is \(\E[-\exp(-aw)\mid\mathcal F] = -\exp(ac)\exp\!\big(-(m-p)^2/(2V)\big)\) for an informed investor, whose wealth carries the cost \(c\). For an uninformed investor the factor \(\exp(ac)\) is absent.
0044| Proof. Given \(\mathcal F\), the payoff \(v-p\) is normal with mean \(m-p\) and variance \(V\), so \(\E[\exp(-a x(v-p))\mid\mathcal F] = \exp\big(-a x(m-p) + a^2x^2V/2\big)\). For an informed investor \(w = x(v-p)-c\), so \(\E[-\exp(-aw)\mid\mathcal F] = -\exp(ac)\exp\big(-a g(x)\big)\) with \(g(x) = x(m-p) - (a/2)x^2V\). The function \(g\) is strictly concave in \(x\), with \(g'(x) = (m-p) - axV\), which vanishes at \(\hat{x} = (m-p)/(aV)\). Then \(g(\hat{x}) = (m-p)^2/(aV) - (m-p)^2/(2aV) = (m-p)^2/(2aV)\), and \(-a\,g(\hat{x}) = -(m-p)^2/(2V)\). An uninformed investor has no cost, so the factor \(\exp(ac)\) is absent for it. \(\square\)
0045| Lemma 2 · The Gaussian mean. If \(Z \sim \Normal(0, S)\) with \(S \ge 0\) and \(V > 0\), then \(\E[\exp(-Z^2/(2V))] = (V/(V+S))^{1/2}\).
0046| Proof. For \(S = 0\) the value is 1. For \(S > 0\), the expectation is \((2\pi S)^{-1/2}\int \exp\big(-z^2(1/S + 1/V)/2\big)\,dz = (2\pi S)^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\pi SV/(S+V)} = (V/(V+S))^{1/2}\). \(\square\)
0047| Lemma 3 · Total variance of the payoff less the price. Let \(\mathcal F\) be as in Lemma 1, and let \(m = \E[v\mid\mathcal F]\) and \(V = \Var(v\mid\mathcal F)\). Then \(\Var(v-p) = V + \Var(m-p)\).
0048| Proof. Let \(X = v-p\). Since \(p\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable, \(\Var(X\mid\mathcal F) = V\) and \(\E[X\mid\mathcal F] = m - p\). Writing \(\Var(X) = \E[X^2] - (\E X)^2\) and \(\E[X^2] = \E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F) + \E[X\mid\mathcal F]^2]\) gives \(\Var(X) = \E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F)] + \Var(\E[X\mid\mathcal F])\). Here \(\E[\Var(X\mid\mathcal F)] = V\), a constant, and \(\Var(\E[X\mid\mathcal F]) = \Var(m-p)\). So \(\Var(v-p) = V + \Var(m-p)\). \(\square\)
0049| Equilibrium, informativeness and the indifference condition
0050| Proposition 1 · The linear equilibrium. Assume A1 to A5 and let \(\lambda \in (0,1]\). The linear equilibrium of A4 exists and is unique. Its coefficients satisfy \[\frac{b_u}{b_s} = -\frac{a}{\lambda\tau_\varepsilon}, \qquad b_s = \frac{\lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y}{\lambda(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)+(1-\lambda)(\tau_v+\tau_y)}, \qquad \tau_y = \frac{\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2}{\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2}.\]
0051| Proof. Let \(\kappa = b_u/b_s\) and \(y = p/b_s = s + \kappa u\). The term \(\varepsilon + \kappa u\) is independent of \(v\), normal with mean 0 and variance \(1/\tau_\varepsilon + \kappa^2\sigma^2\). Define \(\tau_y\) as the reciprocal of that variance; this gives the formula for \(\tau_y\) once \(\kappa\) is known.
0052| (0) Normality. Under A1, A2 and A4, the variables \((v,s,u)\) are jointly normal and \(p\) is a linear function of \((s,u)\), so \(v\) is conditionally normal given \((s,p)\) and given \(p\). This is the hypothesis of Lemma 1 for both investors.
0053| (1) Informed demand. Since \(v\) is independent of \(u\) given \(s\) (A1 and A2), \(\E[v\mid s,u] = \E[v\mid s]\), and the information set generated by \((s,p)\) is contained in the one generated by \((s,u)\), \(\mathcal F_{s,p} \subseteq \mathcal F_{s,u}\), so \(\E[v\mid s,p] = \E[v\mid s] = \tau_\varepsilon s/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(\Var(v\mid s,p) = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\). By Lemma 1, \(x_I = (\tau_\varepsilon s - (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)p)/a\).
0054| (2) Uninformed demand. The uninformed investor observes \(p\), which is equivalent to \(y\) because \(b_s \neq 0\). Since \(y = v + (\varepsilon+\kappa u)\) with \(\varepsilon + \kappa u\) normal and independent of \(v\), the posterior of \(v\) has precision \(\tau_v + \tau_y\) and mean \(\tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). So \(\E[v\mid p] = \tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), \(\Var(v\mid p) = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), and by Lemma 1, \(x_U = (\tau_y y - (\tau_v+\tau_y)p)/a\).
0055| (3) Clearing. Let \(D = \lambda(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)+(1-\lambda)(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). Multiply \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\) by \(a\), substitute \(y = s + \kappa u\) and \(p = b_s s + \kappa b_s u\). The condition becomes \([\lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y - Db_s]\,s + [(1-\lambda)\tau_y\kappa - D b_s\kappa - a]\,u = 0\). Since \(s\) and \(u\) are independent with positive variances, both brackets vanish. The first gives \(b_s = (\lambda\tau_\varepsilon+(1-\lambda)\tau_y)/D\). Substituting \(Db_s = \lambda\tau_\varepsilon + (1-\lambda)\tau_y\) into the second gives \(-\lambda\tau_\varepsilon\kappa = a\), so \(\kappa = -a/(\lambda\tau_\varepsilon)\).
0056| (4) Consistency and uniqueness. The value of \(\kappa\) fixes \(\tau_y = \lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\), and \(b_s\) then follows from (3). Each step is an equivalence, so the coefficients above give the one linear equilibrium. \(D > 0\) because \(\lambda > 0\). \(\square\)
0057| Proposition 2 · Informativeness. Assume A1 to A5. For \(\lambda \in (0,1]\), \(\Corr(v,p)^2 = \tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). At \(\lambda = 0\), \(\Corr(v,p) = 0\).
0058| Proof. With \(p = b_s y\), \(\Cov(v,p) = b_s\Cov(v,y) = b_s/\tau_v\), because \(\Cov(v,y) = \Var(v)\). Also \(\Var(y) = 1/\tau_v + 1/\tau_y\), so \(\Var(p) = b_s^2(1/\tau_v+1/\tau_y)\). Hence \(\Corr(v,p)^2 = (b_s^2/\tau_v^2)/\big((1/\tau_v)\,b_s^2(1/\tau_v+1/\tau_y)\big) = \tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). At \(\lambda = 0\) every investor is uninformed. The regression of \(v\) on \(p\) gives \(\E[v\mid p] = \beta p\) and \(\Var(v\mid p) = V\), with constants \(\beta\) and \(V\), so Lemma 1 gives \(x_U = (\beta-1)p/(aV)\). Clearing, \(x_U = u\), then requires \(p = aVu/(\beta-1)\), a constant times \(u\). So \(b_s = 0\), which A4 allows at \(\lambda = 0\), and \(p\) is independent of \(v\). Then \(\beta = 0\) and \(V = 1/\tau_v\), so \(p = -au/\tau_v\) and \(\Corr(v,p) = 0\). \(\square\)
0059| Proposition 3 · The indifference gap. Assume A1 to A5 and let \(\lambda \in (0,1]\). Let \(V_I = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) and \(V_U = 1/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\). The expected utilities net of cost are \(\E[U_I] = -e^{ac}(V_I/\Omega)^{1/2}\) and \(\E[U_U] = -(V_U/\Omega)^{1/2}\), so the gap \(G(\lambda) = \E[U_I] - \E[U_U] = \Omega^{-1/2}\big(V_U^{1/2} - e^{ac}V_I^{1/2}\big)\). Write \(F_U = (V_U/\Omega)^{1/2}\) and \(F_I = (V_I/\Omega)^{1/2}\), so that \(G = F_U - e^{ac}F_I\). For \(\lambda = 1\) the uninformed term is that of a hypothetical uninformed investor who faces the price of the economy with \(\lambda = 1\). Then \[\operatorname{sign} G(\lambda) = \operatorname{sign}(\bar\tau - \tau_y(\lambda)),\] so \(G(\lambda) > 0\) iff \(\tau_y(\lambda) < \bar\tau\), and \(G\) has at most one zero in \((0,1]\). The limit as \(\lambda\) tends to 0 is \[G(0{+}) = \Big(\tfrac{1}{\tau_v}+\tfrac{a^2\sigma^2}{\tau_v^2}\Big)^{-1/2}\Big(\tfrac{1}{\tau_v^{1/2}} - \tfrac{e^{ac}}{(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)^{1/2}}\Big),\] whose sign is the sign of \(\bar\tau\).
0060| Proof. The conditional normality that Lemma 1 needs holds for both investors, as in step (0) of Proposition 1. For an informed investor, Lemma 1 gives \(\E[U_I\mid s,p] = -e^{ac}\exp(-(m_I-p)^2/(2V_I))\), where \(m_I = \E[v\mid s,p]\) from step (1) of Proposition 1. The variable \(m_I - p\) is a linear function of \((s,u)\) with mean 0 (A1), so Lemma 2 gives \(\E[U_I] = -e^{ac}(V_I/(V_I+S_I))^{1/2}\) with \(S_I = \Var(m_I-p)\). Lemma 3 gives \(V_I + S_I = \Omega\). The uninformed case is identical with \(m_U = \tau_y y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\), \(V_U\) and \(S_U = \Var(m_U-p)\), and Lemma 3 again gives \(V_U+S_U = \Omega\). Since \(\Omega \ge V_I > 0\), the sign of \(G\) is the sign of \(V_U - e^{2ac}V_I\). Multiplying by \((\tau_v+\tau_y)(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} > 0\) gives \((\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} - (\tau_v+\tau_y) = \bar\tau - \tau_y\). Proposition 1 gives \(\tau_y(\lambda) = \lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^2\tau_\varepsilon + a^2\sigma^2)\), which is strictly increasing in \(\lambda\), so \(\bar\tau - \tau_y\) changes sign at most once. As \(\lambda\) tends to 0, \(\tau_y\) tends to 0, \(b_s\) tends to 0 and \(b_u\) tends to \(-a/\tau_v\) (Proposition 1, using \(b_s/\lambda \to \tau_\varepsilon/\tau_v\)), so \(p\) tends to \(-au/\tau_v\), \(\Omega\) tends to \(1/\tau_v + a^2\sigma^2/\tau_v^2\) and \(V_U\) to \(1/\tau_v\). Substituting these limits into the formula for \(G\) gives the displayed \(G(0{+})\). \(\square\)
0061| Proposition 4 · The informed fraction. Assume A1 to A5. Assuming A6, let \(\lambda^*\) be the informed fraction defined by the boundary rule: \(\lambda^* = 0\) when \(G(0{+}) \le 0\), \(\lambda^* = 1\) when \(G(1) \ge 0\), and otherwise the zero of \(G\) in \((0,1)\). Then \[\lambda^* = \begin{cases} 0, & \bar\tau \le 0,\\ 1, & \bar\tau \ge \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2),\\ a\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/\big(\tau_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon-\bar\tau)\big)}, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\] In the interior case the price informativeness is \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau) = 1 - e^{2ac}\tau_v/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\), which does not depend on \(\sigma\).
0062| Proof. In the limit formula of Proposition 3 the bracket \(\tau_v^{-1/2} - e^{ac}(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\) is positive exactly when \((\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac} > \tau_v\), that is, when \(\bar\tau > 0\); so \(G(0{+}) \le 0\) iff \(\bar\tau \le 0\). By the sign rule of Proposition 3, \(G(1) \ge 0\) iff \(\tau_y(1) \le \bar\tau\), where \(\tau_y(1) = \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\). Otherwise, by A6, \(G\) has a zero \(\lambda^*\) in \((0,1)\), and by the sign rule of Proposition 3 it satisfies \(\tau_y(\lambda^*) = \bar\tau\). Solving \(\lambda^{*2}\tau_\varepsilon^2/(\lambda^{*2}\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2) = \bar\tau\) gives \(\lambda^{*2}(\tau_\varepsilon^2 - \bar\tau\tau_\varepsilon) = \bar\tau a^2\sigma^2\). Here \(\bar\tau < \tau_\varepsilon\), because \(\tau_y(1) < \tau_\varepsilon\) and \(\bar\tau < \tau_y(1)\) in this case, so the division is valid, and the formula follows. The informativeness at \(\lambda^*\) is \(\tau_y/(\tau_v+\tau_y)\) at \(\tau_y = \bar\tau\) (Proposition 2), and \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau) = 1 - \tau_v e^{2ac}/(\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)\) by substituting \(\bar\tau\). \(\square\)
0063| Proposition 5 · Comparative statics on the interior. Assume A1 to A5. Assuming A6, suppose \(0 < \bar\tau < \tau_\varepsilon^2/(\tau_\varepsilon+a^2\sigma^2)\), so that \(\lambda^*\) is interior. Then \(\partial\lambda^*/\partial\sigma = \lambda^*/\sigma > 0\); over the costs \(c\) at which the interior holds, \(\lambda^*\) and the price informativeness are strictly decreasing in \(c\); and the price informativeness does not depend on \(\sigma\).
0064| Proof. By Proposition 4, \(\lambda^* = a\sigma k(\bar\tau)\) with \(k(t) = \sqrt{t/(\tau_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon-t))}\), which does not depend on \(\sigma\), so \(\partial\lambda^*/\partial\sigma = \lambda^*/\sigma > 0\). The map \(c \mapsto \bar\tau = (\tau_v+\tau_\varepsilon)e^{-2ac}-\tau_v\) is strictly decreasing because \(a > 0\), and \(k\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\tau_\varepsilon)\), so \(\lambda^*\) is strictly decreasing in \(c\). The informativeness \(\bar\tau/(\tau_v+\bar\tau)\) is strictly increasing in \(\bar\tau\), so it is strictly decreasing in \(c\), and it depends on \(\sigma\) through no term. \(\square\)
0065| With \(\tau_v = \tau_\varepsilon = 1\) and \(a = 2\), the formulas read \(\bar\tau = 2e^{-4c} - 1\), \(\lambda^* = 2\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/(1-\bar\tau)}\) in the interior, and an informativeness of \(1 - e^{4c}/2\). Lab 3 prints these values on its grid, and its last column checks \(\lambda^*\) against the closed form of Proposition 4 at each point. Proposition 5 explains the pattern of the grid: at a fixed cost the informed fraction is proportional to the noise volume, and on the grid the informativeness is the same at every noise volume.
0066| Where the model's limits are. The results need A1 to A5 together. Exponential utility with normal payoffs (A1, A3) is the source of the closed forms, and a utility other than the exponential, or a payoff that is not normal, is outside this page. The noise supply is exogenous (A2), so the model says nothing about why noise traders trade. Investors are price takers in a static, two-date economy (A5), and the price is linear (A4). This page does not estimate how much any of these limits changes the answer.
0067| Rung 3 · Specification
0068| Context
0069| A risky asset pays an uncertain amount \(v\). Some investors pay a cost \(c\) to observe a signal of \(v\), the others learn only from the price, and a noise-trader supply \(u\) moves the price. The question is how informative the price is, and what fraction of investors chooses to pay for information. The fraction solves an indifference condition, and the informativeness follows from it.
0070| Inputs
0071| No dataset. The solver receives two numbers for each instance: noise_sd, the noise volume \(\sigma\), and info_cost, the cost \(c\). The instance generator in grader/instance.py draws both from the seed, and the draws are SIMULATED. The constants \(a = 2\), \(\tau_v = 1\) and \(\tau_\varepsilon = 1\) are fixed in your module and are not arguments of the interface. The report's grid is given in the report section. Under A1 to A5 and assuming A6, every instance the generator draws has one interior root. The generator draws \(c\) from \([0.120, 0.150]\) and \(\sigma\) from \([0.500, 0.750]\), each rounded to three decimals. Here \(\bar\tau = 2e^{-4c}-1\) decreases in \(c\), so \(0.097623 \le \bar\tau \le 0.237567\) on the box (six decimals; \(\bar\tau(0.150) = 0.09762327\ldots\) and \(\bar\tau(0.120) = 0.23756678\ldots\)), and \(\tau_y(1) = 1/(1+4\sigma^2) \ge 1/(1+4\cdot 0.75^2) = 0.30769\ldots\). Therefore \(0 < \bar\tau < \tau_y(1)\) at every point of the box, and Proposition 4 gives an interior \(\lambda^*\) there.

0074| Constraints
0075| Standard library only, with no network. No function prints or warns. The solver works in floating point at full precision, and the gap it reports at fraction 0 agrees with the limit of Proposition 3 to within \(10^{-14}\) relative to the larger of 1 and the absolute value of that limit. At an interior fraction the reported gap agrees with the gap of Proposition 3 at that fraction to the same precision. The bisection on \(\lambda\) brackets the root in \([0,1]\), evaluates the gap at 0 by the limit of Proposition 3, and keeps the gap positive at the lower end of its bracket and negative at the upper end (Assumption A6; the boundary rule of Proposition 4 covers the cases where it does not apply). The bisection's tolerance and stop rule are documented constants of this page: the search stops when the bracket is no wider than \(10^{-12}\) times its upper end, and it takes at most 240 halvings. Both are constants of your module, not arguments of the interface, and the report states them (see the brief). For an interior root, when the noise volume is at least \(10^{-50}\) and \(\bar\tau \ge 10^{-2}\), the reported \(\lambda^*\) agrees with the root of Proposition 4 to within \(10^{-10}\) times the root. The page states no accuracy requirement for smaller \(\bar\tau\) or for a noise volume below \(10^{-50}\), and the suite does not test accuracy there. The boundary rule is part of the answer, and the suite tests it. Invalid input raises the exception named in the exception table, and the message contains the text the table gives. The report displays every number at the precision of the brief and computes each one from unrounded solver output.

0103| Lab 3 · The reference case: the grid of the report
0104| The inputs are a stated grid, SIMULATED: noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750, and information costs 0.120, 0.135 and 0.150. Expected, stated before running: at each noise volume the informed fraction falls as the cost rises, and at each cost it rises in proportion to the noise volume, so at cost 0.120 it is 0.5582, 0.6978 and 0.8373 for noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750; the informativeness is the same at the three noise volumes of each cost, at 0.1920, 0.1420 and 0.0889 for costs 0.120, 0.135 and 0.150; the gap at each reported point has absolute value below \(10^{-13}\); the bisection agrees with the closed form of Proposition 4 to within \(10^{-12}\); and the gap is positive at fraction 0 and negative at fraction 1 at all 484 points of a 22 by 22 grid over the autograder's box.
0105| """Rung 3 reference case: the solver on the 3 by 3 grid that the report uses, with stated SIMULATED inputs. Standard library only. The grid values are stated parameters of the model, not a draw from the generator. The model constants are a = 2, tau_v = 1 and tau_e = 1. The function solve is the reference: copy this file as report/run_case.py and replace the body of solve with a call to your module's solve_model; the file must then print the same lines. Each row gives the informed fraction (bisection on the gap, with TOLERANCE), the price informativeness, the gap at the reported fraction, and the closed-form fraction of Proposition 4. The last line checks the box of the autograder's generator on a 22 by 22 grid, which includes the four corners: the gap must be positive at fraction 0 and negative at fraction 1 at every grid point. """ import math A = 2.0 # the CARA coefficient TAU_V = 1.0 # the prior precision of the payoff v TAU_E = 1.0 # the precision of the signal s = v + eps NOISE_GRID = (0.500, 0.625, 0.750) # the noise volumes the report uses COST_GRID = (0.120, 0.135, 0.150) # the information costs the report uses TOLERANCE = 1e-12 # the bisection stops when the bracket is narrower than this times its upper end MAX_HALVINGS = 240 # a cap on the halvings: 240 halvings reach the tolerance for every root above about 6e-61 def price_precision(lam: float, sd: float) -> float: """tau_y for lam in (0, 1]: lam^2 tau_e^2 / (lam^2 tau_e + a^2 sd^2).""" return lam * lam * TAU_E * TAU_E / (lam * lam * TAU_E + A * A * sd * sd) def indifference_gap(lam: float, sd: float, cost: float) -> float: """F_U(lam) - exp(a c) F_I(lam) for lam in (0, 1]; at lam = 1 the uninformed term is a hypothetical agent's.""" ty = price_precision(lam, sd) denominator = lam * (TAU_V + TAU_E) + (1.0 - lam) * (TAU_V + ty) b_s = (lam * TAU_E + (1.0 - lam) * ty) / denominator b_u = -A * b_s / (lam * TAU_E) r = ty / (TAU_V + ty) - b_s f_u = math.pow(1.0 + r * r * (TAU_V + ty) * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / ty), -0.5) precision = TAU_V + TAU_E slope = TAU_E / precision - b_s var_gap = slope * slope * (1.0 / TAU_V + 1.0 / TAU_E) + b_u * b_u * sd * sd f_i = math.pow(1.0 + precision * var_gap, -0.5) return f_u - math.exp(A * cost) * f_i def gap_at_zero(sd: float, cost: float) -> float: """The limit of the gap as the informed fraction tends to 0.""" uninformed = math.pow(1.0 + A * A * sd * sd / TAU_V, -0.5) informed = math.pow(1.0 + TAU_E / TAU_V + (TAU_V + TAU_E) * A * A * sd * sd / (TAU_V * TAU_V), -0.5) return uninformed - math.exp(A * cost) * informed def closed_form_fraction(sd: float, cost: float) -> float: """The root in closed form: tau_y(lam) = tau_bar = (tau_v + tau_e) exp(-2 a c) - tau_v, solved for lam, with the same boundary rule as solve. Valid as a check of the bisection, not as the solver.""" t = (TAU_V + TAU_E) * math.exp(-2.0 * A * cost) - TAU_V if t <= 0.0: return 0.0 if t >= TAU_E * TAU_E / (TAU_E + A * A * sd * sd): return 1.0 return A * sd * math.sqrt(t / (TAU_E * (TAU_E - t))) def solve(sd: float, cost: float) -> dict[str, float]: """The reference solver: the boundary rule, then bisection on the sign change of the gap.""" g_0, g_1 = gap_at_zero(sd, cost), indifference_gap(1.0, sd, cost) if g_0 <= 0.0: lam = 0.0 elif g_1 >= 0.0: lam = 1.0 else: low, high = 0.0, 1.0 halvings = 0 while high - low > TOLERANCE * high and halvings < MAX_HALVINGS: mid = 0.5 * (low + high) if indifference_gap(mid, sd, cost) > 0.0: low = mid else: high = mid halvings += 1 lam = 0.5 * (low + high) if lam == 0.0: gap, pi = g_0, 0.0 elif lam == 1.0: gap, pi = g_1, price_precision(1.0, sd) / (TAU_V + price_precision(1.0, sd)) else: gap = indifference_gap(lam, sd, cost) ty = price_precision(lam, sd) pi = ty / (TAU_V + ty) return {"informed_fraction": lam, "price_informativeness": pi, "indifference_gap": gap} def main() -> None: print("noise volume | cost | informed fraction | informativeness | gap | closed-form fraction") worst = 0.0 for sd in NOISE_GRID: for cost in COST_GRID: out = solve(sd, cost) closed = closed_form_fraction(sd, cost) worst = max(worst, abs(out["informed_fraction"] - closed)) print( f"{sd:.3f} | {cost:.3f} | {out['informed_fraction']:.4f} | {out['price_informativeness']:.4f} | " f"{out['indifference_gap']:+.2e} | {closed:.4f}" ) print("largest difference between the bisection and the closed form on the grid:", f"{worst:.1e}") inside = 0 for i in range(22): for j in range(22): sd = 0.500 + 0.250 * i / 21.0 cost = 0.120 + 0.030 * j / 21.0 if gap_at_zero(sd, cost) > 0.0 and indifference_gap(1.0, sd, cost) < 0.0: inside += 1 print("box grid points with an interior fraction (gap positive at 0, negative at 1):", inside, "of", 22 * 22) main()

0151| Model
0152| Assumption 1 (prior and signal): the payoff \(v\) is normal with mean 0 and precision 1, and the signal is \(s = v + \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\) normal, independent, of precision 1. Assumption 2 (noise): \(u\) is normal with mean 0 and standard deviation \(\sigma\), independent of \((v,\varepsilon)\), and the market clears, \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\). Assumption 3 (preferences): utility is \(-\exp(-2w)\), initial wealth and the risk-free return are zero, and an informed investor pays \(c\). Assumption 4: the price is linear, \(p = b_s s + b_u u\), with \(b_s \neq 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\). Assumption 5 (types and price taking): a fraction \(\lambda\) of investors is informed and observes \(s\), the others observe only \(p\), and each investor takes the price as given. Assumption 6 (sign change and continuity): the gap is continuous on \([0,1]\), with \(G(0)\) its limit, and it changes sign in the interior case. Assumption 1 is used in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 2 in Propositions 1 to 4, where the market clears in Propositions 1 and 2 (at \(\lambda = 0\) in Proposition 2), the noise is normal in Proposition 3, and the volume \(\sigma\) appears in Propositions 1, 3 and 4; Assumption 3 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3, where the cost enters the gap; Assumption 4 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 5 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3. Propositions 4 and 5 state Assumptions 1 to 5 as a block and name Assumption 6, and are not listed.
0153| Meets C1 level 4: lists each assumption with its number and names the results that use it.
0154| Method
0155| The solver brackets the informed fraction in \([0,1]\). It sets the fraction to 0 when the gap at 0, taken as the limit of Proposition 3, is nonpositive, and to 1 when the gap at 1 is nonnegative. Otherwise it bisects on the sign of the gap, with a tolerance of \(10^{-12}\) times the upper end of the bracket, and it stops after at most 240 halvings. The reported fraction is the midpoint of the final bracket. The table below gives the gap at each reported point.

0157| Results and comparative statics
0158| Each row is one point of the grid printed by Lab 3. At cost 0.120 the informed fraction is 0.5582, 0.6978 and 0.8373 for noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750, and the informativeness is 0.1920 at all three. At cost 0.135 the informativeness is 0.1420 at all three noise volumes, and at cost 0.150 it is 0.0889. So on the stated grid, at each cost the informativeness is the same at every noise volume, and it falls as the cost rises. The informed fraction rises with the noise volume at each cost, and at cost 0.120 the three fractions are proportional to the noise volumes: the ratios of the unrounded fractions are 1.25 = 0.625 / 0.500 and 1.50 = 0.750 / 0.500 (from the displayed values, 0.6978/0.5582 = 1.2501 and 0.8373/0.5582 = 1.5000). Proposition 5 gives this, since \(\lambda^* = 2\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/(1-\bar\tau)}\) is proportional to \(\sigma\) at a fixed cost. At each noise volume the fraction falls as the cost rises. Every printed gap has absolute value below \(4\times10^{-14}\). Display rule: the fraction and the informativeness are shown to four decimals and the gap to three significant digits, each computed from unrounded solver output and rounded once for display.
