===== exemplar-a (prose only) =====
Exemplar A
Model
The payoff is normal and the signal is the payoff plus normal noise. The noise supply is normal. Investors have exponential utility. Some investors are informed, and the price clears the market.
Method
The solver finds the informed fraction by bisection on the gap between the informed and uninformed utilities, starting from the interval from 0 to 1.
Results
At a noise volume of 0.625 and a cost of 0.135, the informed fraction is 0.5567 and the price informativeness is 0.1420.
Comparative statics
A higher noise volume makes prices less informative, so the informativeness should fall as the noise rises.
Limitations
The results depend on the model, which is a simplified description of a market.
===== exemplar-b (prose only) =====
Exemplar B
Model
Assumption 1 (prior and signal): the payoff \(v\) is normal with mean 0 and precision 1, and the signal is \(s = v + \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\) normal, independent, of precision 1. Assumption 2 (noise): \(u\) is normal with mean 0 and standard deviation \(\sigma\), independent of \((v,\varepsilon)\), and the market clears, \(\lambda x_I + (1-\lambda)x_U = u\). Assumption 3 (preferences): utility is \(-\exp(-2w)\), initial wealth and the risk-free return are zero, and an informed investor pays \(c\). Assumption 4: the price is linear, \(p = b_s s + b_u u\), with \(b_s \neq 0\) when \(\lambda > 0\). Assumption 5 (types and price taking): a fraction \(\lambda\) of investors is informed and observes \(s\), the others observe only \(p\), and each investor takes the price as given. Assumption 6 (sign change and continuity): the gap is continuous on \([0,1]\), with \(G(0)\) its limit, and it changes sign in the interior case. Assumption 1 is used in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 2 in Propositions 1 to 4, where the market clears in Propositions 1 and 2 (at \(\lambda = 0\) in Proposition 2), the noise is normal in Proposition 3, and the volume \(\sigma\) appears in Propositions 1, 3 and 4; Assumption 3 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3, where the cost enters the gap; Assumption 4 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3; Assumption 5 in Propositions 1, 2 and 3. Propositions 4 and 5 state Assumptions 1 to 5 as a block and name Assumption 6, and are not listed.
Method
The solver brackets the informed fraction in \([0,1]\). It sets the fraction to 0 when the gap at 0, taken as the limit of Proposition 3, is nonpositive, and to 1 when the gap at 1 is nonnegative. Otherwise it bisects on the sign of the gap, with a tolerance of \(10^{-12}\) times the upper end of the bracket, and it stops after at most 240 halvings. The reported fraction is the midpoint of the final bracket. The table below gives the gap at each reported point.
Results and comparative statics
Each row is one point of the grid printed by Lab 3. At cost 0.120 the informed fraction is 0.5582, 0.6978 and 0.8373 for noise volumes 0.500, 0.625 and 0.750, and the informativeness is 0.1920 at all three. At cost 0.135 the informativeness is 0.1420 at all three noise volumes, and at cost 0.150 it is 0.0889. So on the stated grid, at each cost the informativeness is the same at every noise volume, and it falls as the cost rises. The informed fraction rises with the noise volume at each cost, and at cost 0.120 the three fractions are proportional to the noise volumes: the ratios of the unrounded fractions are 1.25 = 0.625 / 0.500 and 1.50 = 0.750 / 0.500 (from the displayed values, 0.6978/0.5582 = 1.2501 and 0.8373/0.5582 = 1.5000). Proposition 5 gives this, since \(\lambda^* = 2\sigma\sqrt{\bar\tau/(1-\bar\tau)}\) is proportional to \(\sigma\) at a fixed cost. At each noise volume the fraction falls as the cost rises. Every printed gap has absolute value below \(4\times10^{-14}\). Display rule: the fraction and the informativeness are shown to four decimals and the gap to three significant digits, each computed from unrounded solver output and rounded once for display.

Limits and evidence
The model is limited by its exponential utility and its normal payoff, which give the closed forms; by the exogenous noise supply, which the model does not explain; by price taking in a static, two-date economy; and by the linear price, which is the only equilibrium family the page treats. A result for a different utility or a different information structure is outside these propositions. No empirical claim is made. No registry key is attached to any claim, and none is claimed: the results are the derivations of Propositions 1 to 5 and the computed results of Lab 3.
