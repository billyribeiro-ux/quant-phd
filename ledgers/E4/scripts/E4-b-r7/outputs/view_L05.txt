51:       A price that traders use to forecast the price itself makes the forecast and the price depend on each other. This lesson states a linear-normal one-period model in which that loop closes exactly. The rational-expectations equilibrium (Definition 1.2) is a fixed point of a map on the forecast's coefficients (Proposition 2.2 in Section 2). The fixed point has a closed form. Iterating the map from an arbitrary start converges for every start only when the supply response is smaller than the demand slope (Proposition 3.2 in Section 3). The lab checks the fixed point and the error ratios (Lemma 3.1) exactly. The model is a stated special case, and the lesson makes no claim about any market.
53:       1A linear-normal model with a public signal
56:         Assumptions
58:           A1 (parameters). The constants \(\alpha\) and \(\delta\) are real, \(\beta > 0\), and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).
59:           A2 (linear forecasts). The forecast of the price given the public signal \(z\), which every trader observes, is restricted to the family of affine functions \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\), with \(c_0, c_1 \in \R\). An affine function is a constant plus a multiple of \(z\).
60:           A3 (independent normal shocks). The public signal \(z\), the demand shock \(u\) and the supply shock \(\upsilon\) are independent. Each has mean zero: \(z \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_z^2)\) with \(\sigma_z^2 > 0\), \(u \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_u^2)\) and \(\upsilon \sim \Normal(0, \sigma_\upsilon^2)\) with \(\sigma_u^2, \sigma_\upsilon^2 \ge 0\).
65:         Definition 1.1 (the model's market)
66:         Demand for the risky asset is \(d(p) = \alpha - \beta p + u\), where \(p\) is the price. The public signal \(z\) is observed by every trader. Supply is produced in advance on the basis of the forecast \(m(z)\) of the price, so the total supply is \(q = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\). The market clears when \(d(p) = q\). Given a forecast function \(m\), the clearing price is the unique solution in \(p\) of \(\alpha - \beta p + u = \varsigma\, m(z) + \delta z + \upsilon\), namely
67:         \[ p_m = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\, m(z) - \delta z + u - \upsilon}{\beta}. \]
68:         The solution is unique because \(\beta > 0\). The price \(p_m\) is a function of \((z, u, \upsilon)\). The parameter \(\varsigma\) is the supply response to the forecast, and \(\delta\) is the supply response to the public signal.
72:         Definition 1.2 (rational-expectations equilibrium)
73:         Under A1 to A3, a forecast \(m\) is a rational-expectations equilibrium when the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price the market produces: \(m(z) = \E[\,p_m \mid z\,]\) for every real \(z\). The conditional expectation is the version given in Lemma 2.1, which is the one that depends only on \(z\).
76:       2The conditional law of the price and the fixed point
79:         Lemma 2.1 (the forecast of the market price)
80:         Under A1 to A3, for every affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\),
81:         \[ \E[\,p_m \mid z\,] = \frac{\alpha - \varsigma\,(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z}{\beta}, \]
82:         and the conditional law of \(p_m\) given \(z\) is \(\Normal\bigl(\E[p_m \mid z],\ (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\bigr)\).
83:         Proof. Write \(p_m = g(z) + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), where \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\) is a function of \(z\) alone. A function of the conditioning variable is known given that variable, so \(\E[g(z) \mid z] = g(z)\). The variable \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\) by A3, so its conditional expectation given \(z\) equals its unconditional expectation, which is zero. This gives the formula. Next, \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is independent of \(z\), so its conditional variance is its unconditional variance, \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). Adding the function \(g(z)\), which is fixed given \(z\), shifts the mean and leaves the variance unchanged. Since \(u\) and \(\upsilon\) are normal and independent of \(z\), the conditional law is normal. \(\square\)
87:         Proposition 2.2 (the fixed point of the coefficient map)
88:         Under A1 to A3, an affine forecast \(m(z) = c_0 + c_1 z\) is a rational-expectations equilibrium if and only if \((c_0, c_1)\) is a fixed point of the coefficient map
89:         \[ \Psi(c_0, c_1) = \Bigl(\frac{\alpha - \varsigma c_0}{\beta},\ -\frac{\varsigma c_1 + \delta}{\beta}\Bigr). \]
90:         The unique fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\) and \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), and the equilibrium price is \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\).
91:         Proof. By Lemma 2.1, the forecast equals the conditional expectation of the price exactly when \(c_0 + c_1 z = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0 - \varsigma c_1 z - \delta z)/\beta\) for every real \(z\). Two affine functions agree for every \(z\) exactly when their constant terms and their slopes agree, so this is the pair of equations \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\) and \(\beta c_1 = -(\varsigma c_1 + \delta)\). These say \(\Psi(c_0, c_1) = (c_0, c_1)\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\) by A1, each equation has exactly one solution, namely \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\). Substituting the fixed point into \(p_m\) gives \(p = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z + (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). \(\square\)
95:         Corollary 2.3 (the equilibrium forecast and its error)
96:         Under A1 to A3, at the rational-expectations equilibrium of Proposition 2.2, \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), the forecast error \(p - \E[p \mid z]\) has conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\), and the unconditional variance of \(p\) is \((c_1^\ast)^2 \sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).
97:         Proof. The first two statements follow from Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.1, since \(p - \E[p \mid z] = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\). For the third, \(\Var(p) = (c_1^\ast)^2 \Var(z) + \Var\bigl((u - \upsilon)/\beta\bigr)\), because \(z\) and \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) are independent and so uncorrelated, and the constant \(c_0^\ast\) adds nothing to the variance. The variance of \((u - \upsilon)/\beta\) is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\). \(\square\)
100:       3Iterating the coefficient map
103:         Lemma 3.1 (the error recursion)
104:         Let \(c^\ast = (c_0^\ast, c_1^\ast)\) be the fixed point of Proposition 2.2, and let \(e_k = c_k - c^\ast\) be the error of the iterate \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\). Then each component of the error satisfies \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)\, e_k\) exactly, and therefore \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).
105:         Proof. Since \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\), the first component gives \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)\). Since \(c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta\), the second component gives \(c_{1,k+1} - c_1^\ast = -(\varsigma c_{1,k} + \delta)/\beta + (\varsigma c_1^\ast + \delta)/\beta = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c_{1,k} - c_1^\ast)\). Induction gives the power formula. \(\square\)
109:         Proposition 3.2 (when iteration converges)
110:         Under A1 to A3, if \(\varsigma < \beta\), the iterates \(c_k\) converge to \(c^\ast\) from every starting point. If \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) and the starting point differs from \(c^\ast\) in some component, the error in that component does not converge to zero.
111:         Proof. By Lemma 3.1 the error in each component has absolute value \((\varsigma/\beta)^k\) times its starting absolute value. If \(\varsigma < \beta\), then \(\varsigma/\beta < 1\), so \((\varsigma/\beta)^k \to 0\). If \(\varsigma = \beta\), the absolute error stays constant, and it is nonzero when the starting error is nonzero. If \(\varsigma > \beta\), it grows. In both cases the error does not converge to zero. \(\square\)
115:         Proposition 3.3 (a damped step can reach the fixed point at once)
116:         Under A1 to A3, consider the damped step \(c \mapsto (1 - \nu) c + \nu\, \Psi(c)\) with weight \(\nu\). Its error factor in each component is \(1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\). With \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), the factor is zero, so one damped step from any starting point gives \(c^\ast\).
117:         Proof. Subtracting \(c^\ast\) from the damped step and using \(\Psi(c) - c^\ast = -(\varsigma/\beta)(c - c^\ast)\) from Lemma 3.1 gives an error \(\bigl[(1 - \nu) - \nu\varsigma/\beta\bigr](c - c^\ast) = \bigl[1 - \nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta)\bigr](c - c^\ast)\). For \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma)\), \(\nu(1 + \varsigma/\beta) = \frac{\beta}{\beta + \varsigma}\cdot\frac{\beta + \varsigma}{\beta} = 1\), so the factor is zero. \(\square\)
121:         Lab 1 · The fixed point and the iteration, in exact arithmetic
122:         At the base parameters \(\alpha = 10\), \(\beta = 2\), \(\varsigma = 1/2\), \(\delta = 3/4\), the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 4\) and \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the map's residual at it is zero. Iterating from \((0, 0)\), each error ratio is \(-1/4\). With \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\), the ratio is \(-1\) and the \(c_0\) path alternates between 0 and 5. With \(\varsigma = 3\), the ratio is \(-3/2\) and the error grows. A damped step with weight \(4/5\) reaches the fixed point in one step, and the fixed-point identity holds at each of five points of \(z\).

284:       4Notes on the model
286:         Remark 4.1 (what Proposition 2.2 covers)
287:         The fixed point is found within the affine family of A2. Proposition 2.2 shows that this family contains exactly one rational-expectations equilibrium. It does not show that no non-affine forecast is an equilibrium of the model, because the argument restricts the search to affine forecasts.
291:         5Where the canon breaks
292:         The equilibrium forecast is affine by construction, since A2 restricts the search to affine forecasts. The result is therefore a property of this model, not of rational expectations in general. Three limits apply.
293:         The search is restricted to affine forecasts. A2 is an assumption, not a consequence of rationality. Remark 4.1 records what the restriction does and does not establish.
294:         Existence in the family does not give convergence of naive iteration. Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for all \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\). Proposition 3.2 shows that repeated application of \(\Psi\) converges from every starting point only when \(\varsigma < \beta\). Lab 1 Parts C and D show the two failures. Proposition 3.3 gives a damped step that avoids the failure in this affine case, which decouples into two one-dimensional affine maps.
295:         The forecasts are correct by construction, inside the model. Definition 1.2 takes the traders to know the price rule, the distribution of the shocks and the supply response, because the forecast is the conditional expectation of the price that the model produces. Whether real forecasts have this property is a separate empirical question. This lesson makes no claim about it.
296:         The phrase rational expectations appears in the title of Muth (1961). That registry record is used for the title only. The model here is a stated special case, and this lesson uses no result from that paper.
300:         6Misconceptions
303:             “A rational forecast of the price is exact.”
304:             Corollary 2.3 gives a conditional variance \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\) for the forecast error. This is zero only when both shocks have zero variance, and A3 allows positive variances. The forecast is unbiased, in the sense that its error has mean zero given \(z\), but it is not exact.
307:             “A rational-expectations equilibrium exists only when repeated iteration of the forecast map converges.”
308:             Proposition 2.2 gives the fixed point for every \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\), with \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Proposition 3.2 is about convergence of one particular procedure. Lab 1 Part C has \(\varsigma = \beta\): the fixed point is \(c_0^\ast = 5/2\), but the iterates alternate between 0 and 5 and never reach it.
311:             “The equilibrium forecast of the price is the unconditional mean of the price.”
312:             The forecast is \(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z\), which varies with \(z\) whenever \(\delta \ne 0\), because \(c_1^\ast = -\delta/(\beta + \varsigma)\). At the base parameters of Lab 1 the slope is \(c_1^\ast = -3/10\), and the printed fixed point shows it. The unconditional mean is \(c_0^\ast\), because \(\E z = \E u = \E\upsilon = 0\) by A3 gives \(\E p = c_0^\ast\). It is a different object from the forecast.

322:           Mechanics
324:             M1 · The fixed point at new parameters
325:             Let \(\alpha = 12\), \(\beta = 3\), \(\varsigma = 1\) and \(\delta = 2\). Find \(c_0^\ast\) and \(c_1^\ast\), and check that \(\Psi\) maps the fixed point to itself.
326:             Solution
327:               Here \(\beta + \varsigma = 4\), so \(c_0^\ast = 12/4 = 3\) and \(c_1^\ast = -2/4 = -1/2\). Check the first component: \((12 - 1\cdot 3)/3 = 9/3 = 3\). Check the second: \(-(1\cdot(-1/2) + 2)/3 = -(3/2)/3 = -1/2\). Both components are unchanged, so \(\Psi(c^\ast) = c^\ast\).
331:             M2 · One step of the error recursion
332:             With the parameters of M1, start the iteration at \((0, 0)\). Compute the error ratio \(e_1/e_0\) in each component and compare it with \(-\varsigma/\beta\).
333:             Solution
334:               The first iterate is \(\Psi(0,0) = (12/3,\ -2/3) = (4, -2/3)\). The starting error in \(c_0\) is \(0 - 3 = -3\), and the next error is \(4 - 3 = 1\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). In \(c_1\) the starting error is \(0 - (-1/2) = 1/2\), and the next error is \(-2/3 - (-1/2) = -1/6\), so the ratio is \(-1/3\). Both equal \(-\varsigma/\beta = -1/3\), as Lemma 3.1 states.
338:             M3 · The conditional law of the price
339:             Let \(\beta = 2\), \(\sigma_u^2 = 4\) and \(\sigma_\upsilon^2 = 1\). Find the conditional variance of the price given \(z\) at the equilibrium of Proposition 2.2.
340:             Solution
341:               By Corollary 2.3 the conditional variance is \((\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (4 + 1)/4 = 5/4\). It does not depend on \(z\), and the conditional law is \(\Normal\bigl(c_0^\ast + c_1^\ast z,\ 5/4\bigr)\).
347:           Proof
349:             P1 · The conditional expectation in Lemma 2.1
350:             Show that \(\E[\,(u - \upsilon)/\beta \mid z\,] = 0\) under A3, and deduce the formula for \(\E[p_m \mid z]\) in Lemma 2.1.
351:             Solution
352:               Under A3 the vector \((u, \upsilon)\) is independent of \(z\). For an integrable function \(h\) of an independent variable, \(\E[h(u,\upsilon) \mid z] = \E[h(u,\upsilon)]\). With \(h = (u - \upsilon)/\beta\), this gives \(\E[(u-\upsilon)/\beta] = (\E u - \E\upsilon)/\beta = 0\). Since \(p_m = g(z) + (u-\upsilon)/\beta\) with \(g(z) = (\alpha - \varsigma(c_0 + c_1 z) - \delta z)/\beta\), linearity gives \(\E[p_m \mid z] = g(z) + 0\), which is the formula of Lemma 2.1.
356:             P2 · Uniqueness in the affine family
357:             Show that Proposition 2.2 has exactly one fixed point in the affine family, for every \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), \(\beta > 0\) and \(\varsigma \ge 0\).
358:             Solution
359:               The first component of the fixed-point condition is \(\beta c_0 = \alpha - \varsigma c_0\), equivalently \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\). Since \(\beta + \varsigma \ge \beta > 0\), this has exactly one solution \(c_0 = \alpha/(\beta + \varsigma)\). The second is \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which likewise has exactly one solution. So the pair is unique.
363:             P3 · The error recursion
364:             Prove Lemma 3.1 for the first component, starting from \(c_{0,k+1} = (\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k})/\beta\) and the identity \(c_0^\ast = (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)/\beta\).
365:             Solution
366:               Subtract the two identities: \(c_{0,k+1} - c_0^\ast = \bigl[(\alpha - \varsigma c_{0,k}) - (\alpha - \varsigma c_0^\ast)\bigr]/\beta = -\varsigma(c_{0,k} - c_0^\ast)/\beta\). So \(e_{k+1} = -(\varsigma/\beta)e_k\). Induction on \(k\) gives \(e_k = (-\varsigma/\beta)^k e_0\).
372:           Empirical
374:             E1 · When the path is within 0.01 of the fixed point
375:             Using Lab 1 Part B at the base parameters, find the first iterate of \(c_0\) that lies within 0.01 of \(c_0^\ast = 4\), and check it against the printed path.
376:             Solution
377:               By Lemma 3.1 the error after \(k\) steps is \(e_k = (-1/4)^k(-4)\), so \(|e_k| = 4/4^k\). We need \(4/4^k < 1/100\), which requires \(4^k > 400\), so \(k \ge 5\). At \(k = 4\), \(|e_4| = 4/256 = 1/64 \approx 0.0156\), which is too large. At \(k = 5\), \(|e_5| = 4/1024 = 1/256 \approx 0.0039\). The printed sixth entry of the \(c_0\) path is \(1025/256\), and \(1025/256 - 4 = 1/256\), which agrees.
381:             E2 · Why the \(\varsigma = \beta\) path does not settle
382:             Lab 1 Part C prints a \(c_0\) path that alternates between 0 and 5 at \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\). Explain the alternation from the error ratio, and state the starting point at which the path would stay put.
383:             Solution
384:               The printed ratio of successive errors in \(c_0\) is \(-1\), which is \(-\varsigma/\beta\) at \(\varsigma = \beta\). Each step flips the sign of the error and keeps its size, so the iterates swap between two values symmetric about the fixed point \(5/2\). The path stays put at \(c_0 = 5/2\): then \(\Psi(5/2) = (10 - 2\cdot 5/2)/2 = 5/2\), so the starting error is zero, and Lemma 3.1 keeps it at zero.
388:             E3 · Growth of the error at \(\varsigma = 3\)
389:             At \(\varsigma = 3\) and \(\beta = 2\), Lab 1 Part D prints a \(c_0\) path starting \(0, 5, -5/2, 35/4\). Verify the error ratio from the path, and compute the error \(e_3\).
390:             Solution
391:               Here \(c_0^\ast = 10/(2 + 3) = 2\). The errors are \(e_0 = -2\), \(e_1 = 3\), \(e_2 = -9/2\) and \(e_3 = 35/4 - 2 = 27/4\). Each ratio is \(-3/2\), which equals \(-\varsigma/\beta = -3/2\) by Lemma 3.1. The error grows by a factor of \(3/2\) and alternates in sign at each step.
397:           Research
399:             R1 · The damped weight at \(\varsigma = \beta\)
400:             At \(\varsigma = \beta = 2\) and \(\alpha = 10\), find the damping weight of Proposition 3.3, and show that one damped step from \((0,0)\) gives the fixed point in the first component.
401:             Solution
402:               The weight is \(\nu = \beta/(\beta + \varsigma) = 2/4 = 1/2\). The map sends \(0\) to \(\Psi_0(0) = 10/2 = 5\). The damped step gives \((1 - 1/2)\cdot 0 + (1/2)\cdot 5 = 5/2\), which is \(c_0^\ast = 10/4 = 5/2\).
406:             R2 · The price with no supply response to the signal
407:             Let \(\delta = 0\). Show that the equilibrium forecast \(\E[p \mid z]\) does not depend on \(z\), and find the unconditional variance of the price.
408:             Solution
409:               With \(\delta = 0\), \(c_1^\ast = -0/(\beta + \varsigma) = 0\), so \(\E[p \mid z] = c_0^\ast\) for every \(z\), which does not depend on \(z\). By Corollary 2.3 the unconditional variance is \((c_1^\ast)^2\sigma_z^2 + (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2 = (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\upsilon^2)/\beta^2\).
413:             R3 · Existence and convergence are different questions
414:             Explain, in one paragraph and with reference to Propositions 2.2 and 3.2, why a rational-expectations equilibrium can exist in the affine family even when repeated iteration of \(\Psi\) does not converge.
415:             Solution
416:               Proposition 2.2 finds the fixed point from the two linear equations \((\beta + \varsigma)c_0 = \alpha\) and \((\beta + \varsigma)c_1 = -\delta\), which have a solution whenever \(\beta + \varsigma > 0\). Existence therefore needs only the algebra of those equations. Proposition 3.2 concerns a different question, namely whether the particular sequence \(c_{k+1} = \Psi(c_k)\) reaches the fixed point. With \(\varsigma \ge \beta\) the error in each component does not shrink, so this sequence fails to converge, while the fixed point is still there to be found by other means, such as the damped step of Proposition 3.3.
