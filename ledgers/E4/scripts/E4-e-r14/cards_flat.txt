## E4-c001 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a state space (Definition 1.1)?
B: A finite set of states, one of which is realized, with a probability for each state. The probabilities are positive and sum to 1.
## E4-c002 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a claim (Definition 1.2)?
B: A vector of payoffs in cents, one entry per state: entry s is the payoff in state s. As a random variable X on the state space, X(s) = x_s. Payoffs may be negative.
## E4-c003 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is the Arrow–Debreu security of state s (Definition 1.3)?
B: The claim that pays one cent in state s and nothing in every other state.
## E4-c004 | L01 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 1.4, proof in §2 (no probability used)
F: Assume A1 (finite state space). What does Proposition 1.4 say about every claim x?
B: x equals the sum over states of x_s times the Arrow–Debreu security e_s. The coefficient of the security of state s is the claim's payoff in state s.
## E4-c005 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What does Assumption A2 (linear valuation) require of a valuation V?
B: V(ax + by) = aV(x) + bV(y) for all claims x and y and all real a and b. A2 is an assumption about the market and is not derived in L01. It excludes quantity discounts, bid-ask spreads and price impact.
## E4-c006 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is the security price of state s (Definition 1.6)?
B: Under A2, q_s = V(e_s), the value of the Arrow–Debreu security of state s. It is a dimensionless number: cents of value per cent of payoff. The vector of these prices is the Arrow–Debreu security price vector.
## E4-c007 | L01 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 1.7, proof in §3 (uses A2 by induction)
F: Assume A1 and A2. What is the value V(x) of a claim x in terms of the security prices (Proposition 1.7)?
B: V(x) = sum over states of q_s times x_s. The value is fixed by the security prices and the payoffs alone.
## E4-c008 | L01 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 1.8, proof in §3 (q is defined under A2)
F: Assume A1 and A2. Suppose a vector r satisfies V(x) = sum of r_s x_s for every claim x. What follows (Proposition 1.8)?
B: r_s = q_s for every state s. Taking x equal to the security e_s gives V(e_s) = r_s, and Definition 1.6 gives V(e_s) = q_s. The prices are unique.
## E4-c009 | L01 | grade=None | basis=None

B: E x = sum over states of pi_s times x_s, the average payoff weighted by the state probabilities.
## E4-c010 | L01 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 1.10, proof in §3

B: Exactly when the security price of every state equals its probability, q_s = pi_s. Under this condition V(x) = E x for every claim.

## E4-c018 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None

## E4-c019 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: Assumption A4 (utility): what does it require of agent i's utility u_i?
B: u_i is defined on (0, ∞), and it is strictly increasing, strictly concave and differentiable. Expected utility is the sum over states of π_s u_i(x_s) on strictly positive bundles. A bundle with a zero entry has utility minus infinity.
## E4-c020 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What are the wealth and the budget set of agent i at a price vector q in (0, ∞)^S (Definition 2.1)?
B: Wealth is w_i = q · e^i, the value of the endowment at q. The budget set is B_i(q) = {x in R^S_+ : q · x ≤ w_i}.
## E4-c021 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a demand of agent i at prices q (Definition 2.2)?
B: A bundle x^i(q) in the budget set B_i(q) with utility at least that of every bundle in B_i(q). Agents take prices as given.
## E4-c022 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is the excess demand z(q) of the economy?
B: z(q) = x^1 + x^2 − ē, where x^i is the demand chosen for agent i at q: the two demands minus the aggregate endowment. Markets clear at q exactly when z(q) = 0 (Definition 2.3).
## E4-c023 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is an Arrow–Debreu equilibrium (Definition 2.3)?
B: A pair consisting of a strictly positive price vector q* and an allocation (x^1*, x^2*) such that each x^i* is a demand of agent i at q* and markets clear, x^1* + x^2* = ē. Equivalently, z(q*) = 0.
## E4-c024 | L02 | grade=None | basis=None
F: Is the existence of an Arrow–Debreu equilibrium proved in L02 for a general economy?
B: No. Existence is Assumption A5, a hypothesis and not a proved result. The logarithmic example in Section 4 exhibits an equilibrium by construction, so A5 is not assumed there.
## E4-c025 | L02 | grade=T | basis=Lemma 2.4, proof in §2

B: Every demand is strictly positive in every state: x_s > 0 for each state s. A demand with a zero entry has utility minus infinity, and the bundle (w_i / (2 q·ι)) ι, with ι the vector of ones, is affordable and has finite utility.
## E4-c026 | L02 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 2.5, proof in §2
F: Assume A1, A3 and A4. Let q be in (0, ∞)^S and let x^1 and x^2 be demands at q. What does Proposition 2.5 (Walras' law) say?
B: q · (x^1 + x^2 − ē) = 0, that is, q · z(q) = 0. Each budget binds, q · x^i = w_i.
## E4-c027 | L02 | grade=T | basis=Corollary 2.7, proof in §2 from Proposition 2.5

## E4-c028 | L02 | grade=T | basis=Proposition 2.8, proof in §3 (tangent-line inequality for ln)

## E4-c039 | L03 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a feasible allocation (Definition 3.1)?
B: A pair of bundles (y^1, y^2), each in R^S_+, with y^1 + y^2 = ē. The whole aggregate endowment is distributed, and no good is destroyed.
## E4-c040 | L03 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is a Pareto improvement of a feasible allocation (Definition 3.2)?
B: A feasible allocation (y^1, y^2) with U_i(y^i) at least U_i(x^i) for both agents, and strictly greater for at least one. An allocation with no Pareto improvement is Pareto efficient.
## E4-c041 | L03 | grade=None | basis=None
F: What is the marginal rate of substitution of agent i between states s and t at a strictly positive bundle x (x_r > 0 for every state r) (Definition 3.3)?
B: MRS^i_st(x) = (∂U_i/∂x_s) / (∂U_i/∂x_t) = π_s u_i'(x_s) / (π_t u_i'(x_t)). It is the number of units of the state-t good the agent would give up for one more unit in state s, at the same utility.
