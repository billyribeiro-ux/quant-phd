A seller who knows its quality can send a costly, observable action that a low-quality seller would not find worth sending. A worker who knows its productivity can pay for education that a less productive worker would find too expensive. This lesson defines a signal and the threshold wage schedule, states the two incentive constraints, and finds the separating education levels in a two-type model. Lab 1 enumerates the thresholds 0 to 6 with money in whole cents.
1A costly signal and the incentive constraints
Money is in integer cents, with 1 CU = 100 cents. A worker has a type \(\theta \in \{L, H\}\). A type \(\theta\) worker is worth \(y_\theta\) cents a year to the firm. Each whole year of education \(e \ge 0\) costs a type \(\theta\) worker \(c_\theta\) cents, so the cost is \(c_\theta e\). The high type is the more productive one, \(y_L < y_H\). The base case of this lesson uses \(y_L = 4{,}000{,}000\), \(y_H = 6{,}000{,}000\), \(c_L = 1{,}000{,}000\) and \(c_H = 500{,}000\) cents per year. Write \(\Delta = y_H - y_L\), which is \(2{,}000{,}000\) cents in the base case.
 Definition 1.1 (signaling)
Signaling is a game in which a privately informed worker chooses an observable action, the signal, and the firm, which sees the signal and not the type, sets a wage that depends on the signal. Here the signal is a whole number of years of education \(e\), and its cost differs by type. A signal separates the types when the two types choose different signals. In this lesson, "signal" means this action.
 Assumption 1 (risk-neutral workers)
A type \(\theta\) worker who receives wage \(w\) after choosing education \(e\) has utility \(w - c_\theta e\) in cents. The worker's outside option is not used in this lesson.
 Assumption 2 (competitive firms)
A firm pays a wage equal to the expected productivity of the workers who send the signal it observes. The wage at a signal that only one type sends therefore equals that type's productivity.
 Assumption 3 (threshold wage schedule)
For a whole number threshold \(t \ge 0\), the wage is \(w(e) = y_H\) when \(e \ge t\), and \(w(e) = y_L\) when \(e < t\). The wage at each education level is fixed by this rule. The threshold profile is the choice in which the high type chooses \(e = t\) and the low type chooses \(e = 0\). A threshold \(t \ge 1\) is separating when the threshold profile separates the types in the sense of Definition 1.1. An education level is off-path when no type chooses it in the threshold profile, and an education level that is not off-path is on-path. The levels \(0 < e < t\) and \(e > t\) are off-path, and Assumption 2 does not fix the wage there. The rule sets the wage at \(0 < e < t\) to \(y_L\), a belief that the worker is a low type, and the wage at \(e > t\) to \(y_H\). Section 2 returns to other choices of off-path wages.
 Assumption 4 (costs and productivities)
\(0 < c_H \le c_L\) and \(0 < y_L < y_H\). The proof of Proposition 1.3 uses only \(c_H > 0\), \(c_L > 0\) and \(\Delta > 0\). The inequality \(c_H \le c_L\) is used in Remark 1.4.
 Proposition 1.3 (the separating interval)
Under Assumptions 1 to 4, let \(t\) be a whole number with \(t \ge 1\). Let the wage schedule be the threshold schedule of Assumption 3, with the high type choosing \(e = t\) and the low type choosing \(e = 0\). The following two conditions are the incentive constraints of the two types.
(IC-L) the low type's utility at \(0\) is at least its utility at every education level \(e \ge 0\); and
(IC-H) the high type's utility at \(t\) is at least its utility at every education level \(e \ge 0\).
Both hold exactly when \(\Delta / c_L \le t \le \Delta / c_H\).
 Proof
Low type. If \(e < t\), the wage is \(y_L\), so the low type's utility is \(y_L - c_L e \le y_L\), which is its utility at \(0\). If \(e \ge t\), the wage is \(y_H\), so the utility is \(y_H - c_L e\). Since \(c_L > 0\), this is largest at \(e = t\), where it equals \(y_H - c_L t\). Therefore (IC-L) holds exactly when \(y_L \ge y_H - c_L t\), that is, when \(c_L t \ge \Delta\), that is, when \(t \ge \Delta / c_L\). The "if" direction uses the bound for \(e < t\) and the bound at \(e = t\). The "only if" direction holds because if \(c_L t < \Delta\), the education level \(e = t\) gives the low type more than \(e = 0\).
High type. The high type chooses \(t\). If \(e > t\), the wage is \(y_H\) and the utility is \(y_H - c_H e < y_H - c_H t\), because \(c_H > 0\). If \(e < t\), the wage is \(y_L\) and the utility is \(y_L - c_H e \le y_L\), with the supremum over \(e < t\) equal to \(y_L\) at \(e = 0\). So (IC-H) holds exactly when \(y_H - c_H t \ge y_L\), that is, when \(t \le \Delta / c_H\).
Both. Combine the two conditions. The signals differ because \(t \ge 1\), so the profile is separating. The firm's wages equal the productivities of the types who send each signal, so Assumption 2 holds. \(\square\)
 Remark 1.4 (the base case and equal costs)
In the base case, \(\Delta / c_L = 2\) and \(\Delta / c_H = 4\), so the separating thresholds are \(t = 2, 3, 4\). If \(c_H = c_L = c > 0\), the two bounds coincide and the interval is the single point \(t = \Delta / c\). If \(c_H \le c_L\), the lower bound is at most the upper bound, so the real interval is not empty. Lab 1 checks the base case on the grid of whole years 0 to 6.
Lab 1 · Separating thresholds in a two-type education model
The inputs are illustrative, not data: \(y_L = 4{,}000{,}000\) and \(y_H = 6{,}000{,}000\) cents a year, and \(c_L = 1{,}000{,}000\) and \(c_H = 500{,}000\) cents a year. Part A prints the closed-form bounds from Proposition 1.3. Part B checks each threshold \(t\) from 0 to 6 by enumeration over the same grid of whole years, and labels the outcome. Part C compares the enumerated thresholds with the closed-form interval. Part D prints the least-cost separating threshold (defined in Remark 2.1 below). Proposition 1.3 predicts the separating thresholds 2, 3 and 4, and the enumeration should agree.
"""E2 L04 lab 1: separating signals in a two-type education model.
Inputs are illustrative, not data: a low type with productivity 40,000 CU and a high type with productivity
60,000 CU, and education costs of 10,000 CU per year (low type) and 5,000 CU per year (high type). All money is
in integer cents, with 1 CU = 100 cents. Education is measured in whole years on the grid 0..6.
The firm pays the threshold wage of Assumption 3 on the page: it pays the high-type productivity at or above the
threshold t and the low-type productivity below it. For each threshold t on the grid, the lab checks the two weak
incentive constraints by enumeration over the grid: the high type's utility at signal t is at least its utility at
every other grid signal, and the low type's utility at signal 0 is at least its utility at every other grid signal.
A threshold t > 0 with both constraints holding is a separating profile. The closed-form interval
(Y_HIGH - Y_LOW) / COST_LOW <= t <= (Y_HIGH - Y_LOW) / COST_HIGH is printed beside the enumeration.
No random draws are used, so every run prints the same lines.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
from fractions import Fraction
Y_LOW = 4_000_000 # productivity of a low type, in cents per year (40,000 CU)
Y_HIGH = 6_000_000 # productivity of a high type, in cents per year (60,000 CU)
COST_LOW = 1_000_000 # cost of one year of education for a low type, in cents (10,000 CU)
COST_HIGH = 500_000 # cost of one year of education for a high type, in cents (5,000 CU)
GRID = range(0, 7) # whole years of education checked
def wage(e: int, threshold: int) -> int:
 """Wage in cents for e whole years of education under the threshold schedule (Assumption 3)."""
 return Y_HIGH if e >= threshold else Y_LOW
def utility(cost: int, e: int, threshold: int) -> int:
 """Net utility in cents: the wage in cents minus the education cost, cost * e cents."""
 return wage(e, threshold) - cost * e
def classify(threshold: int) -> tuple[str, int, int, int, int]:
 """Return the label and the four utilities in cents for one threshold on the grid."""
 u_high = utility(COST_HIGH, threshold, threshold)
 best_high = max(utility(COST_HIGH, e, threshold) for e in GRID)
 u_low = utility(COST_LOW, 0, threshold)
 best_low = max(utility(COST_LOW, e, threshold) for e in GRID)
 high_ok = u_high == best_high
 low_ok = u_low == best_low
 if threshold == 0:
 label = "same signal for both types"
 elif high_ok and low_ok:
 label = "separating"
 elif not low_ok:
 label = "low type mimics"
 else:
 label = "high type prefers another signal"
 return label, u_high, best_high, u_low, best_low
def main() -> None:
 print("Part A: the closed-form interval, in whole years of education")
 lower = Fraction(Y_HIGH - Y_LOW, COST_LOW)
 upper = Fraction(Y_HIGH - Y_LOW, COST_HIGH)
 print(f"lower bound (Y_HIGH - Y_LOW) / COST_LOW = {lower}")
 print(f"upper bound (Y_HIGH - Y_LOW) / COST_HIGH = {upper}")
 print("Part B: each threshold on the grid 0..6, checked by enumeration (cents)")
 print("threshold | high utility at t | best high utility | low utility at 0 | best low utility | outcome")
 separating: list[int] = []
 for threshold in GRID:
 label, u_high, best_high, u_low, best_low = classify(threshold)
 if label == "separating":
 separating.append(threshold)
 print(f"{threshold:9d} | {u_high:17d} | {best_high:17d} | {u_low:17d} | {best_low:16d} | {label}")
 print("Part C: agreement between the interval and the enumeration")
 interval = [t for t in GRID if lower <= t <= upper and t > 0]
 print(f"separating thresholds on the grid by enumeration: {separating}")
 print(f"thresholds inside the closed-form interval: {interval}")
 print(f"the two lists agree: {separating == interval}")
 print("Part D: the least-cost separating signal")
 least = min(separating)
 print(f"least-cost separating threshold, in whole years: {least}")
 print(f"education cost to the high type at that threshold, in cents: {least * COST_HIGH}")
 print(f"high type utility at that threshold, in cents: {utility(COST_HIGH, least, least)}")
main()

The table shows thresholds 2, 3 and 4 with both weak incentive constraints holding. Threshold 1 fails because the low type gains by sending the high-type signal, and thresholds 5 and 6 fail because the high type prefers education 0. 2The least-cost separating profile and what the model does not select
 Remark 2.1 (the least-cost separating threshold)
The smallest separating threshold is the least costly one for the high type, since the high type's cost \(t c_H\) rises with \(t\). In the base case it is \(t = 2\). At that threshold the high type pays \(2 \times 500{,}000 = 1{,}000{,}000\) cents, or 10,000 CU, and keeps a net utility of \(5{,}000{,}000\) cents. At \(t = 4\) the high type's net utility is \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents (Lab 1, Part B). The lesson calls \(t = 2\) the least-cost separating threshold because it is the minimum of the enumerated set, and it makes no claim that the market selects it.
 Remark 2.2 (the choice of off-path beliefs)
Assumption 3 is one rule for the off-path wages. The necessary part of Proposition 1.3 does not depend on that rule. The two incentive constraints compare the wage at \(e = 0\), which is \(y_L\), and the wage at \(e = t\), which is \(y_H\), by Assumption 2, and these are on-path wages. The constraint at \(e = 0\) for the high type gives \(y_H - c_H t \ge y_L\), and the constraint at \(e = t\) for the low type gives \(y_L \ge y_H - c_L t\). So under any off-path wages, a separating threshold lies in \([\Delta / c_L, \Delta / c_H]\). Under Assumption 3, every whole number in that interval is a separating profile by Proposition 1.3. This lesson does not check other off-path rules, and it does not state which of them an equilibrium concept selects.
4Where the canon breaks
The model has two types, a noiseless signal and a linear education cost. Spence (1973), "Job Market Signaling", QJE 87(3), is cited for the topic of signaling by its title, year and venue only; no page or result of that paper is used. The threshold schedule of Assumption 3 is one choice of off-path wages (Remark 2.2). The model has no noisy signals, no third type and no continuous wages, and Proposition 1.3 says nothing about those cases.
5Misconceptions
“A costly signal always reveals the type.”
Under the threshold schedule of Assumption 3 with two types, a threshold profile separates the types only when \(t \ge 1\) and both incentive constraints of Proposition 1.3 hold. At threshold 1 the low type sends the high-type signal, because its utility at \(e = 1\) is \(5{,}000{,}000\) cents against \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents at \(e = 0\) (Lab 1, Part B, threshold 1). At thresholds 5 and 6 the high type prefers education 0 (Lab 1, Part B).
“The least-cost separating profile is the one the market must choose.”
Lab 1 finds three separating thresholds, 2, 3 and 4. Each is a separating profile under Assumption 3. The least-cost one is the minimum of that set (Remark 2.1), and the lesson does not claim a selection rule.
“The high type pays nothing to separate, because the wage is higher.”
The high type pays the education cost \(t c_H\) to reach the higher wage. At \(t = 2\) the cost is 1,000,000 cents, and the net utility is \(5{,}000{,}000\) cents against the \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents it would receive at \(e = 0\). The wage gain is \(2{,}000{,}000\) cents, and the cost is \(1{,}000{,}000\) cents.
6Problem set
Mechanics
L04 · Interval for a new cost
Take \(y_L = 4{,}000{,}000\), \(y_H = 6{,}000{,}000\), \(c_L = 1{,}000{,}000\) and \(c_H = 250{,}000\) cents a year. Find the whole-year thresholds that Proposition 1.3 allows.
Solution
\(\Delta = 2{,}000{,}000\). The lower bound is \(\Delta / c_L = 2\) and the upper bound is \(\Delta / c_H = 8\). The separating thresholds are \(t = 2, 3, \ldots, 8\), which the lesson writes as the seven whole numbers from 2 to 8.
L04 · Utilities at t = 3 in the new case
In the case of Problem L04-m1, compute the high type's utility at \(t = 3\), the low type's utility at \(0\), and the low type's utility at \(e = 3\).
Solution
The high type's utility is \(y_H - c_H \cdot 3 = 6{,}000{,}000 - 750{,}000 = 5{,}250{,}000\) cents. The low type's utility at \(0\) is \(y_L = 4{,}000{,}000\) cents. At \(e = 3\) the wage is \(y_H\), so the low type's utility is \(6{,}000{,}000 - 3{,}000{,}000 = 3{,}000{,}000\) cents, which is below \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents, so the low type does not mimic.
L04 · A threshold that is not an integer
Take \(\Delta = 1{,}500{,}000\), \(c_L = 1{,}000{,}000\) and \(c_H = 500{,}000\) cents a year. Find the separating whole-year thresholds.
Solution
The lower bound is \(1.5\) and the upper bound is \(3\). The whole numbers in \([1.5, 3]\) are 2 and 3. The threshold 2 is the least-cost separating threshold, and the bound 1.5 is not a threshold because education is a whole number of years.
Proof
L04 · The low type's constraint
Show that for \(\Delta > 0\) and \(c_L > 0\), the low type's constraint (IC-L) in Proposition 1.3 holds if and only if \(c_L t \ge \Delta\).
Solution
Apply the first paragraph of the proof of Proposition 1.3. For \(e < t\) the low type's utility is at most \(y_L\), which is its utility at 0. For \(e \ge t\) the utility is \(y_H - c_L e\), which is largest at \(e = t\) because \(c_L > 0\). So (IC-L) holds exactly when \(y_L \ge y_H - c_L t\), which rearranges to \(c_L t \ge \Delta\).
L04 · Equal costs give one point
Show that if \(c_H = c_L = c > 0\), the separating interval of Proposition 1.3 is the single point \(t = \Delta / c\), provided that point is a whole number.
Solution
With \(c_H = c_L = c\) the two bounds are \(\Delta / c\) and \(\Delta / c\). The condition \(\Delta / c \le t \le \Delta / c\) holds only at \(t = \Delta / c\). The point is a whole number, and so a separating threshold, only when \(\Delta / c\) is an integer.
L04 · The shared signal is not separating
Show that the threshold \(t = 0\) cannot produce a separating profile under Definition 1.1.
Solution
At \(t = 0\) both types choose \(e = 0\), because the high type's choice is the threshold \(t\) and the low type's choice is \(0\). Both types then send the same signal, so the firm cannot tell them apart by the signal. Proposition 1.3 requires \(t \ge 1\) for the signals to differ, and the lesson labels this row as a shared signal in Lab 1.
Empirical
L04 · A lab variant with a different high-type cost
Predict the separating thresholds for the base case with \(c_H = 400{,}000\) cents a year, keeping the other inputs. Then change the constant in Lab 1 and run it. No data are used; the question is about the model's arithmetic.
Solution
The lower bound is \(2{,}000{,}000 / 1{,}000{,}000 = 2\) and the upper bound is \(2{,}000{,}000 / 400{,}000 = 5\). The prediction is the thresholds 2, 3, 4 and 5. The lab variant should print that list with "the two lists agree: True". A different output means that either the prediction or the code needs checking.
L04 · A smaller productivity gap
Set \(y_H = 5{,}000{,}000\) and keep the other base-case inputs. Find the separating thresholds by the closed form and check them with the lab.
Solution
Now \(\Delta = 1{,}000{,}000\). The lower bound is \(1\) and the upper bound is \(2\), so the separating thresholds are 1 and 2. At threshold 1 the high type's net utility is \(5{,}000{,}000 - 500{,}000 = 4{,}500{,}000\) cents, above the \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents at \(e = 0\), and the low type's utility at \(e = 1\) equals its utility at \(0\), \(4{,}000{,}000\) cents, so the low-type check holds with equality. The enumeration should list 1 and 2.
L04 · Net utility across the base-case thresholds
Using only the base-case inputs, tabulate the high type's net utility at each separating threshold and state which threshold gives the most.
Solution
At \(t = 2\): \(6{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000 = 5{,}000{,}000\). At \(t = 3\): \(6{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}500{,}000 = 4{,}500{,}000\). At \(t = 4\): \(6{,}000{,}000 - 2{,}000{,}000 = 4{,}000{,}000\). The most is at \(t = 2\), which matches the rows of Lab 1, Part B.
Research
L04 · What a selection rule must do
Describe what a rule for choosing among the separating thresholds must establish, given Proposition 1.3. Say what the lesson does not establish.
Solution
A rule must pick one threshold from the set \(\{2, 3, 4\}\) in the base case. Proposition 1.3 establishes that the set is exactly the separating thresholds under the threshold schedule. It does not establish which one is selected. An argument that selects \(t = 2\) needs a criterion beyond Proposition 1.3, and this lesson does not state one.
L04 · Monotone high-type utility on the interval
Show that the high type's net utility \(y_H - c_H t\) is decreasing in \(t\), and state what that implies for the choice of \(t\) inside the interval.
Solution
Since \(c_H > 0\), the function \(t \mapsto y_H - c_H t\) is strictly decreasing. On the interval \([\Delta / c_L, \Delta / c_H]\) the high type's utility is therefore largest at the lower bound \(\Delta / c_L\), which is \(t = 2\) in the base case. The rule that picks the least-cost separating threshold would then pick the high type's preferred separating profile among those in the set.
L04 · A third worker type
State what a third worker type would add to the question of separating thresholds, and what Proposition 1.3 would no longer cover.
Solution
With three types, a separating profile must give each type a distinct signal, and the constraints must be checked for each pair of types. Proposition 1.3 covers two types only, so it does not cover this case. The lesson does not derive the three-type conditions, and it claims no result for them.
