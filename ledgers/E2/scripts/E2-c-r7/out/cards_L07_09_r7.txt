  F: What is mechanism design? 
  B: The choice, by a designer, of the rules of a game: the messages each agent may send and the outcome that each message profile produces. The designer's aim is stated in advance, and the rules are chosen so that the agents' own choices of messages serve that aim (Definition 1.1).
E2-c077 L07 None |  
  F: What is a direct mechanism? 
  B: A map from each reported type to an outcome (q, tau), where q is the probability of trade and tau is the payment in cents (Definition 1.2).
E2-c078 L07 None |  
  F: What is incentive compatibility (IC) of a direct mechanism? 
  B: For every type theta and every report r, the utility of truthful reporting is at least the utility of reporting r. Truthful reporting is a best report for every type (Definition 1.2).
E2-c079 L07 None |  
  F: What is individual rationality (IR) of a direct mechanism? 
  B: The utility of each type from truthful reporting is at least 0, so every type does at least as well as the outside option (Definition 1.2).
E2-c080 L07 None |

The direct mechanism x_hat(theta) = x(s(theta)) gives each type its outcome under the equilibrium s (Definition 2.1; Proposition 2.2).

E2-c091 L08 None |  
  F: What is a common-value auction? 
  B: An auction in which every bidder's payoff depends on the same unknown value V, and each bidder has only a signal about it. A private-value auction is a different model and is not analyzed here (Definition 1.1).
E2-c092 L08 None |  
  F: What is the winner's curse? 
  B: In a common-value auction in which each bidder bids its own signal, the bidder who wins has a negative expected profit. The winner is the bidder whose signal is the largest, and its error is the largest error M, with E[M] > 0, so the winning signal is too high on average (Definition 1.2; Proposition 2.1).
E2-c093 L08 None |  
  F: What is bid shading? 
  B: A bid below the bidder's own signal, of the form b = S - c, with c a whole number of cents that does not depend on the signal. It is a fixed offset, and it is not claimed to be an equilibrium (Definition 1.3).
E2-c094 L08 T |

The hypotheses are Assumptions 1 and 2: a common value V; signals S_i = V + ε_i with independent errors uniform on the whole numbers -a to a; a first-price auction with ties to the lower index; and risk neutrality.

E2-c104 L08 T |  
  F: Is winning an auction good news about the value? 
  B: Under naive bidding, winning means that one's signal was the largest, so the signal tends to be too high. The winner pays V + M, which is above V by the largest error, so its expected loss is E[M] cents (Proposition 2.1).

  B: Not every positive shading. The expected profit is c - E[M] (Proposition 3.1), which is nonnegative only when c is at least E[M], so a shading below E[M] leaves it negative. The size of the shading matters, not only its sign.
E2-c106 L08 T |

E2-c107 L09 None |  
  F: What is the uniform-price format? 
  B: A payment rule in which the winners are the k highest bids, with ties to the lower bidder index, and each winner pays the clearing bid b(k+1), the bid of the first loser (Definition 1.1).
E2-c108 L09 None |  
  F: What is the discriminatory format? 
  B: A payment rule for the same winners in which each winner pays its own bid. Only the payments differ from the uniform-price format (Definition 1.1).
E2-c109 L09 None |  
  F: What is the clearing bid? 
  B: The bid of the first loser, b(k+1), when the bids are ranked from highest to lowest with ties to the lower bidder index (Definition 1.1).
E2-c110 L09 None |  
  F: What is the revenue of a format on a bid profile? 
  B: The total cents paid by the winners. Under the uniform-price format it is k times the clearing bid, k b(k+1). Under the discriminatory format it is the sum of the k highest bids, b(1) plus b(2) and so on through b(k), which is the sum of the winners' own bids (Definition 1.2).
E2-c111 L09 T |  
  F: On a fixed bid profile, how do the discriminatory and uniform-price revenues compare? 
  B: The discriminatory revenue is at least the uniform revenue on every bid profile, with equality exactly when the k+1 highest bids are all equal (Proposition 2.1). The hypotheses are Assumption 1: 1 ≤ k < n, each bidder wants at most one unit, and bids are whole numbers of cents.
E2-c112 L09 None |  
  F: What is allocative efficiency of a bid profile? 
  B: The pair (value of the winners, sum of the k highest values), both in cents. When the second entry is positive, its ratio, the first entry divided by the second, is the efficiency; if the k highest values have a sum that is not positive, the ratio is undefined (Definition 3.1).
