E2-c091 L08 None | What is a common-value auction? || An auction in which every bidder's payoff depends on the same unknown value V, and each bidder has only a signal about it. A private-value auction is a different model and is not analyzed here (Definition 1.1). |  | 
E2-c092 L08 None | What is the winner's curse? || In a common-value auction in which each bidder bids its own signal, the bidder who wins has a negative expected profit. The winner is the bidder whose signal is the largest, and its error is the largest error M, with E[M] > 0, so the winning signal is too high on average (Definition 1.2; Proposition 2.1). |  | 
E2-c093 L08 None | What is bid shading? || A bid below the bidder's own signal, of the form b = S - c, with c a whole number of cents that does not depend on the signal. It is a fixed offset, and it is not claimed to be an equilibrium (Definition 1.3). |  | 
E2-c094 L08 T | Under naive bidding in a common-value auction, what is the winner's expected profit? || -E[M], where M is the largest error. It is strictly negative for n at least 2 and a at least 1, because E[M] is positive (Proposition 2.1). The hypotheses are Assumptions 1 and 2: a common value V; signals S_i = V + ε_i with independent errors uniform on the whole numbers -a to a; a first-price auction with ties to the lower index; and risk neutrality. |  | 
E2-c095 L08 T | With two bidders and errors that are -1, 0 or 1 with probability 1/3 each, what is the expected largest error? || 4/9 cent. P(M = -1) = 1/9, P(M = 0) = 3/9 and P(M = 1) = 5/9 (Example 2.4). Lab 1 Part A checks the value by listing the nine pairs of errors. |  | 
E2-c096 L08 T | What is the closed form of E[M] for the largest of n errors that are uniform on the whole numbers -a to a? || E[M] = -a + sum over x from -a to a-1 of (1 - F(x)^n), with F(x) = (x + a + 1) / (2a + 1) (Proposition 2.2). E2-c103 L08 T | Is bidding one's own signal a fair bid when every signal is an unbiased estimate of the value? || No. Each signal is unbiased for V, but the winner is the bidder with the largest error, and that error is what the winner pays for (Proposition 2.1). |  |

E2-c107 L09 None | What is the uniform-price format? || A payment rule in which the winners are the k highest bids, with ties to the lower bidder index, and each winner pays the clearing bid b(k+1), the bid of the first loser (Definition 1.1). |  | 
E2-c108 L09 None | What is the discriminatory format? || A payment rule for the same winners in which each winner pays its own bid. Only the payments differ from the uniform-price format (Definition 1.1). |  | 
E2-c109 L09 None | What is the clearing bid? || The bid of the first loser, b(k+1), when the bids are ranked from highest to lowest with ties to the lower bidder index (Definition 1.1). |  | 
E2-c110 L09 None | What is the revenue of a format on a bid profile? || The total cents paid by the winners. Under the uniform-price format it is k times the clearing bid, k b(k+1). Under the discriminatory format it is the sum of the k highest bids, b(1) plus b(2) and so on through b(k), which is the sum of the winners' own bids (Definition 1.2). |  | 
E2-c111 L09 T | On a fixed bid profile, how do the discriminatory and uniform-price revenues compare? || The discriminatory revenue is at least the uniform revenue on every bid profile, with equality exactly when the k+1 highest bids are all equal (Proposition 2.1). The hypotheses are Assumption 1: 1 ≤ k < n, each bidder wants at most one unit, and bids are whole numbers of cents. |  | 
E2-c112 L09 None | What is allocative efficiency of a bid profile? || The pair (value of the winners, sum of the k highest values), both in cents. When the second entry is positive, its ratio, the first entry divided by the second, is the efficiency; if the k highest values have a sum that is not positive, the ratio is undefined (Definition 3.1). |  | 
E2-c113 L09 T | Under truthful bids, what is the efficiency under either format? || Under Assumption 1 and truthful bids the efficiency is 1 under either format, provided the k highest values have a positive sum. When each bidder bids its value, the winners are the k highest values under both formats, so their value is the sum of the k highest values (Proposition 3.2). |  |
