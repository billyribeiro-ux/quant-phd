## Mathematical specifications

### 1. Ledoit-Wolf Shrinkage Covariance

Given an observation matrix of returns $R \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ where $T \ge 3$ and $N \ge 2$:
The sample covariance matrix is:
$$S = \frac{1}{T - 1} \sum_{t=1}^T (r_t - \bar{r})(r_t - \bar{r})^\top$$
The shrinkage target $F$ is either:
- `"scaled_identity"`: $F = \frac{\mathrm{tr}(S)}{N} I_N$
- `"constant_correlation"`: $F_{ii} = S_{ii}$, and for $i \ne j$, $F_{ij} = \bar{\rho} \sqrt{S_{ii} S_{jj}}$ where $\bar{\rho}$ is the average sample correlation.

The optimal linear shrinkage intensity $\delta^* \in [0, 1]$ is estimated analytically via the Ledoit-Wolf formula:
$$\Sigma^* = (1 - \delta^*) S + \delta^* F$$

### 2. Marginal, Component, and Percent Risk Contributions

For portfolio weights $w \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and positive definite covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, portfolio volatility is $\sigma_p = \sqrt{w^\top \Sigma w}$:
- **Marginal Risk Contribution (MRC):**
  $$\mathrm{MRC}_i = \frac{(\Sigma w)_i}{\sigma_p}$$
- **Total/Component Risk Contribution (TRC):**
  $$\mathrm{TRC}_i = w_i \cdot \mathrm{MRC}_i = \frac{w_i (\Sigma w)_i}{\sigma_p}$$
  Euler's homogeneous function theorem guarantees $\sum_{i=1}^N \mathrm{TRC}_i = \sigma_p$.
- **Percent Risk Contribution (PRC):**
  $$\mathrm{PRC}_i = \frac{\mathrm{TRC}_i}{\sigma_p} = \frac{w_i (\Sigma w)_i}{w^\top \Sigma w}$$
  satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^N \mathrm{PRC}_i = 1$.

### 3. Risk Budgeting & Equal Risk Contribution (ERC) Allocator

Given covariance $\Sigma$ and risk budget vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^N_{>0}$ with $\sum_{i=1}^N b_i = 1$ (default $b_i = 1/N$):
Solves the strictly convex optimization problem:
$$\min_{y \in \mathbb{R}^N_{>0}} \frac{1}{2} y^\top \Sigma y - \sum_{i=1}^N b_i \ln(y_i)$$
The optimal normalized weights are:
$$w^* = \frac{y^*}{\mathbf{1}^\top y^*}$$
At the solution, $\mathrm{PRC}_i(w^*) = b_i$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

### 4. Mean-Variance Allocator

Given expected return vector $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^N$, covariance matrix $\Sigma$, and risk aversion parameter $\gamma > 0$:
Solves:
$$\max_{w} w^\top \mu - \frac{\gamma}{2} w^\top \Sigma w \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top w = 1$$
If `long_only=True`, enforces $w_i \ge 0$ for all $i$.

### 5. Volatility Target Allocator

Given initial weights $w \in \mathbb{R}^N$, covariance matrix $\Sigma$, target annualized volatility $\sigma_{\mathrm{target}} > 0$, and optional `max_leverage`:
Portfolio volatility is $\sigma_p = \sqrt{w^\top \Sigma w}$. The scaling factor is:
$$\lambda = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{target}}}{\sigma_p}$$
Tentative weights are $w_{\mathrm{scaled}} = \lambda w$. If `max_leverage` is provided and $\sum_{i=1}^N |w_{\mathrm{scaled}, i}| > \mathrm{max\_leverage}$:
$$w^* = \frac{\mathrm{max\_leverage}}{\sum_{i=1}^N |w_{\mathrm{scaled}, i}|} w_{\mathrm{scaled}}$$
Otherwise $w^* = w_{\mathrm{scaled}}$.

## Error Handling

All functions raise `ValueError` on:
- Non-finite (NaN, Inf) inputs.
- Empty or ragged input matrices.
- Non-square, asymmetric, or non-positive-definite covariance matrices.
- Mismatched dimensions between mean, weights, budgets, and covariance matrices.
- Risk budgets that do not sum to 1.0 within 1e-5 or contain non-positive values.
- Non-positive target volatility or negative risk aversion.
- Failure of iterative algorithms to converge within `max_iter`.
