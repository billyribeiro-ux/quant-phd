# S7 against the strongest programs: external benchmark and gap analysis

Independent reviewer report, 2026-10-17. The reviewer did not build S7. Scope:
`course/modules/S/S7/` (hub, L01–L08, project, grader, cards.json) and the S7 entry
in `curriculum/01-foundations.yaml`. Every "course X covers Y" rests on a public
page the reviewer fetched; pages that could not be reached are listed and never
counted. Course descriptions understate content, so "not in the description" is
never read as "not taught".

## Verdict

S7 is deeper on finance-specific multiple testing than any public course page the
reviewer could verify at Columbia, Princeton, CMU, MIT, Stanford, Cornell, NYU,
UChicago, Berkeley, Harvard, Yale and Penn:

- **Not found in the public text of any finance master's programme reached:** the
  Reality Check, the SPA, StepM, the deflated Sharpe ratio and PBO. The programmes
  are Princeton MFin, Berkeley MFE, CMU MSCF, Cornell ORIE 5640, NYU Courant,
  MIT 15.450 and UChicago FINM.
- **Exception:** Cornell ORIE 5256 (López de Prado) teaches backtest overfitting,
  but its slides (SSRN) were unreachable.
- **On theory**, S7 matches the PhD statistics and econometrics notes reached:
  CMU 36-705, Berkeley Stat 210A and 212A, and Shaikh's UChicago notes.

What the best sources have and S7 lacked:

1. **Real data and graded judgement.** Every S7 lab was simulated, and no written
   work was assessed.
2. **Estimation after selection.** S7 had no simultaneous intervals and no
   false-coverage-rate intervals.
3. **The neighbouring statistical toolkit:** k-FWER and FDP exceedance, local FDR
   and empirical Bayes, anytime-valid inference, global testing, and the
   Diebold–Mariano test.
