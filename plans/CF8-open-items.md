## Requests for the project spec (Builder D and the architect)

- **Receipts source.** The plan's case inputs (section 5.4) list a "weekly collections lag in days" but no receipts or sales series.
  L03 and L04 take weekly receipts and disbursements as inputs, and L04 Theorem 2.4 uses weekly sales \(S\) and the DSO slip
  \(dS/7\). The project's `make_instance` must provide the series or sales that these results need, and the architect should fix that
  before the spec is frozen (Q4).
- **Shortfall and commitment outputs.** L04 defines the weekly shortfall as \(\max(0, m - C_w)\), a commitment with no shortfall as
  \(K^\ast = \max(B_0, \max_w B^\infty_w)\) (Theorem 2.3), and the peak drawn balance under the actual commitment. The project's
  `revolver_plan` keys (`peak_drawn_cents`, `required_commitment_cents`, `shortfall_cents`) should use these definitions, or the
  lesson's definitions should change with the project's.
- **Stretch hypothesis.** The plan's stretch example says a five-day slip moves the pinch week by at most one week. Under a one-time
  slip the pinch week does not move (L04 Theorem 2.4). A phased or partly recovered slip can move it (L04 research problem R1, a
  case where the pinch week moves from week 2 to week 1 through a tie). The stretch rung must state which slip shape it tests.
- **Interest.** Plan Q7 makes interest a stated input rate. L03 and L04 exclude interest, and L04 Theorem 2.1 needs Assumption (3)
  (no cash cost on the facility). A cash interest cost lowers liquidity, so the project's liquidity and pinch week will differ from the
  lessons' results if it includes interest. The architect should decide, and the project page should say which case applies.
- **Half-up ties.** For a DSO slip \(d \cdot S/7\) with integer \(S\), the fractional part is a multiple of \(1/7\), so an exact half
  never arises and the truncation mutant (plan section 5.8, item 11) does not break a cent-exact check on that formula. The verifier
  needs a construction whose fraction can be exactly one half, such as a 365-day form, to kill that mutant. Noted for Builder D and the
  verifier. Lab 1 of L04 prints 881,834 cents from 5 times 1,234,567 divided by 7, which has fraction .571 and is not a tie.

## Day-count convention

- L01 defines DSO on year-end balances with a 365-day year. L04 Theorem 2.4 applies it at week ends with a 7-day week, so that
  daily sales equal \(S/7\). This extension is stated as Assumption (2) of Theorem 2.4. The project's Q8 convention (365-day year,
  7-day week) must state the same week-end application if its DSO effect is computed on weekly balances.
